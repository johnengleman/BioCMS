# Playbook — The Christian East

For saints of Egypt (Coptic), Syria and Mesopotamia (Syriac), Palestine and Sinai, Georgia, Armenia, and Ethiopia. It covers the Desert Fathers. For Greek saints of the same lands (for example John of Damascus, Sabas), also read the Greek playbook.

All checks below were made on 2026-09-29 with `curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0"`, unless marked "unchecked".

## Catholic, Orthodox, or both

The region holds four groups. Set the tradition type for each saint.

- **Shared (before about 451).** The Desert Fathers (Anthony, Pachomius, Macarius, Arsenius), Ephrem the Syrian, and the martyrs before the Councils of Ephesus and Chalcedon. Catholics and both Orthodox families honor them. Use one Catholic and one Orthodox source, and record both feast days.
- **Oriental Orthodox only.** Coptic, Syriac Orthodox, Armenian Apostolic, and Ethiopian saints after 451 (for example Severus of Antioch, Pope Kyrillos VI, Takla Haymanot). The Catholic Church does not list most of them. Do not set Catholic veneration.
- **Eastern Orthodox (Chalcedonian) and Georgian.** Georgia is Eastern Orthodox. The Georgian Church honors Nino and Gabriel Urgebadze. Catholics list some early Georgian saints (Nino) in martyrologies and in Georgian Catholic use, but the main sources are Orthodox.
- **Eastern Catholic.** Maronite (Charbel, Rafqa, Ni'matullah Kassab), Chaldean, Syro-Malabar, Coptic Catholic, and Armenian Catholic saints. Use Vatican decrees plus the sources of that Church.
- **Disputed.** Some saints of the 5th to 7th centuries are honored by the Oriental Orthodox and viewed critically by Chalcedonian Churches (Dioscorus of Alexandria, Severus of Antioch, Jacob Baradaeus). Some Church Fathers count as heretics in one family (Theodore of Mopsuestia and Nestorius in the Assyrian Church of the East, where they are honored). Present both views fairly. Set `venerated_in` only for the Church that honors the saint.
- Saints of the Assyrian Church of the East (Mar Mari, Mar Aba, Isaac of Nineveh in shared use) are a fifth family. Isaac of Nineveh is honored by the Orthodox and by the Catholic Chaldean and Syro-Malabar Churches.

## Start here

**Shared and early saints (before 600)**

1. **New Advent, Church Fathers (newadvent.org/fathers) and CCEL (ccel.org).** Full English texts of the classic early Lives: Athanasius' *Life of Anthony*, Jerome's *Life of Paulus*, Sozomen's account of Ephrem, Ephrem's own hymns. Works, real text.
2. **Coptic Synaxarium at copticchurch.net.** One entry for each saint, arranged by Coptic date. Works, full English text.
3. **Bibliotheca Hagiographica Orientalis (archive.org, Peeters 1910).** A finder list of every Oriental Life by saint and language. Works, full text file. Use it to learn which Life exists and where it was printed.

**Oriental Orthodox and Georgian saints (after 451)**

1. **Synaxaria:** copticchurch.net (Coptic), Budge's translation of the Ethiopic synaxarium (archive.org), and the Armenian Synaxarium of Ter Israel (archive.org, Armenian text).
2. **Kartlis Tskhovreba** (archive.org, English 2014) for Nino and the Georgian kings, plus Georgian Church sites.
3. **Bedjan, *Acta Martyrum et Sanctorum*** (archive.org, Syriac text, volumes 1 to 7) for Syriac martyrs and saints.

**Catholic sources**

1. **Catholic Encyclopedia (newadvent.org/cathen).** Long articles for Anthony and Ephrem. Older view, so recheck modern scholarship.
2. **Vatican News and the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints,** and **Butler's *Lives of the Saints*** (archive.org, Thurston edition) for calendar entries.
3. **Maronite Heritage (maronite-heritage.com)** for Maronite saints. It answered, but its saint pages are unchecked.

- Coptic: the Synaxarium (compiled in the 13th century), and the Arabic *History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria* (*Patrologia Orientalis* 1, 5, 10, 17, 20, 21). Evetts' text and translation are in *Patrologia Orientalis* volumes on archive.org (identifier for each volume: unchecked).

- Georgian: *Kartlis Tskhovreba* covers the kings and saints (Nino, Shushanik, David the Builder, Tamar). Georgian Lives of Athonite Georgians (Euthymius, George the Athonite) are in the Life by George the Hagiorite. Translation: unchecked.
- Armenian: Ter Israel's synaxarium, checked above.
- Ethiopian: Takla Haymanot, Lalibela, Gebre Menfes Kidus, in Budge.
- The Maronite saints of this period (John Maron): unchecked.

### Early modern (1500–1900)

- Coptic and Ethiopian saints of these centuries: the Synaxarium, and the *History of the Church of Alexandria*. Few sources exist beyond the Synaxarium.
- Georgian and Armenian martyrs under Persian and Ottoman rule: the synaxaria and Brosset's *Histoire de la Géorgie* (1849, French, archive.org `BrossetHistGeo2`, `histoiredelagor01fgoog`).

## Access

- **archive.org text.** Find the identifier with `https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php?q=<terms>&fl[]=identifier&fl[]=title&rows=8&output=json`. Read the exact file names with `https://archive.org/metadata/<id>`. Then read the text file with `https://archive.org/download/<id>/<file>_djvu.txt`. Guessed file names often return 404. Do not save PDFs. Read the text to stdout and search it with `grep`.
- **archive.org `access-restricted-item`.** Items marked `true` (for example Lang's *Lives and Legends*) are borrow-only. Do not use them.
- **New Advent.** Each Father's work has an index page, and long works split into chapter pages (numbers such as `2811.htm`, `26023.htm`). Fetch the index, then follow the link.
- **CCEL.** Book pages are `https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnfNNN`. Full text of a volume: `https://www.ccel.org/ccel/s/schaff/<id>/cache/<id>.txt` (a large file, read only with `grep`).
- **Blocked or browser-only:** `gedsh.bethmardutho.org` (403), `eotc.org` (403), `brepolsonline.net` (bot check), `armenianorthodoxchurch.org` (bot check), `digital.nplg.gov.ge` and `ktb.hmml.org` (no connection), `patrologiaorientalis.brepols.net` (no connection), `patriarchate.ge` and `syriaca.org` (page returns almost no text to `curl`; needs a browser). `ecatholic2000.com` (Butler online) returned 403. `st-takla.org` and `tasbeha.org` deep pages returned 404 with the URLs I tried.
- **Do not trust look-alike domains.** `ephrem.org` now hosts a gambling site.
- **Wikipedia** answers, but some pages return a 404 status with the page body. Check the title. It is a lead, not an authority.

## Language

- **Name forms.** Anthony: Antony, Antonius, Abba Antonios, Anba Antonios, Anthony the Great. Ephrem: Ephraim, Ephraem, Mar Aphrem, Afrem. Nino: Nina, Ninō, Christiana (Rufinus), Theognosta (Byzantines), ნინო. Kyrillos VI: Cyril VI, Abba Kyrillos, Pope Kyrillos VI, Kyrillos the Sixth. Gabriel: Gabriel Urgebadze, Goderdzi, გაბრიელ ურგებაძე, Saint Gabriel the Fool for Christ and Confessor.
- **Coptic calendar.** 13 months. Thirty days each for the first twelve, plus a short month (Nasie). Year 1 is AD 284 (the Era of the Martyrs). New Year is 11 September (12 in the year before a Gregorian leap year). Coptic dates fall 7 or 8 days after the Gregorian date. Coptic Christmas is 7 January. Record the Coptic date and the Gregorian date, and state the year used.
- **Ethiopian calendar.** Also 13 months. Its year runs 7 to 8 years behind the Gregorian year. Takla Haymanot's commemoration dates in Budge follow the Ethiopian month names (for example Hamle).
- **Armenian calendar.** The Armenian Apostolic Church keeps a fixed-feast calendar with many movable feast weeks. The Synaxarium's Armenian month names are Navasard, Hori, and so on (from `NavasardSahmi` and `TreAratz` items on archive.org).
- **Georgian calendar.** The Georgian Orthodox Church uses the Julian calendar. Feast dates in Georgian sources are Julian, so add 13 days for the civil date.
- **Syriac.** Pick a transliteration and keep it. Bedjan's text uses Syriac script. Syriac names: Mar (Lord), Mart (Lady), Abba (father). Add Latin, Greek, Arabic, and Syriac forms when they differ.
- **Arabic transliteration.** Coptic saints appear in Arabic sources under names such as Anba, Abu, Abu Sayfayn (St. Mercurius), and Mari Girgis (St. George).
- **Ge'ez.** Ethiopian saint Lives are called *Gadl* (plural *Gadlat*). Saint names carry prefixes such as Abba, Abuna, and Gebre (servant of).
