# Playbook — Greece, Byzantium, Mount Athos, Cyprus, Constantinople, and Asia Minor

For Greek-speaking saints: the early martyrs of the Greek East, Byzantine saints, Athonite fathers, the New Martyrs under Ottoman rule, and modern Greek saints. For Russian monks on Athos, also read `russia-slavic.md`.

All access notes were tested on 2026-09-29 with `curl -sL -A "Mozilla/5.0"`. Anything not tested is marked "unchecked".

## Start here

### Orthodox sources (most saints of this region)

1. **saint.gr (Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής)** — one page in Greek for each saint. It has a biography, hymns, icons, and related links. Pages run from about 400 to 11,000 words. Works with plain `curl`. Best for the identity, the feast day, and the Greek name forms. It is a private site with unsourced text, so use it to find leads, not as the final authority.
2. **Sanidopoulos: johnsanidopoulos.com and mystagogyresourcecenter.com** — English translations from Greek, with the Greek source usually named. Use the search page and the feed (see Access). His texts are facts-only. Follow the named Greek source to the original.
3. **Pemptousia (pemptousia.gr)** — Greek articles by monks, professors, and bishops, and the Athonite archive of Monk Moses. Search works with `curl` (see Access). Under copyright: facts only.

### Catholic sources (for shared saints and for scholarship on Byzantine saints)

1. **Acta Sanctorum (Bollandists)** — Latin. It has commentary and often the Greek Life with a Latin translation. On archive.org.
2. **Delehaye, *Synaxarium Ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae* (1902)** — the Greek text of the Church of Constantinople's calendar, with a Latin apparatus. On archive.org. Best for early saints (before about 950).
3. **Analecta Bollandiana** — scholarly editions of Greek Lives. Volumes to about 1939 are on archive.org (see below).

## By era

### Early Church and the martyrs (before 600)

Sample saint: **St. Demetrios the Myrrh-streamer** (Thessaloniki, d. about 306; feast 26 October).

- **saint.gr:** `https://www.saint.gr/2649/saint.aspx`. About 1,700 words. It gives the Greek verse, the dates (280–306), and the family background. It works.
- **Delehaye, Synaxarium of Constantinople.** The entry for 26 October begins `Ἄθλησις τοῦ ἁγίου μεγαλομάρτυρος Δημητρίου` under the heading `ΜΗΝΙ ΤΩ ΑΥΤΩ Κς΄`. Found at line 18340 of the archive.org text. The OCR is noisy, so search a short Greek phrase, not a whole line.
- **Migne, Patrologia Graeca vol. 116** (Symeon Metaphrastes, vol. 3). The volume has a section "S. Demetrii Martyris Acta" (found at line 74336 of the archive.org text). Metaphrastes' rewritten Lives are 10th-century and are not eyewitness. Use them to show the tradition, not the history.
- **OCA:** `https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/10/26/103059-holy-glorious-demetrios-the-myrrh-gusher-of-thessaloniki`. A short English Life, and the hymns. Adjust the year in the URL.
- **Sanidopoulos:** a translation, "The Passion and Miracles of St. Demetrios by Anastasius the Librarian" (found by feed search). The *Miracula* of Demetrios are a separate, early text. Ask for them by name.
- **Acta Sanctorum:** 26 October belongs to the volume Oct. XI. The archive.org ordinal for that volume is **unchecked** (see Access).
- Also check: Wikisource and Wikipedia for names of the early Greek texts (the *Passio* and the *Miracula*). Then locate the editions (unchecked).

### Byzantine and medieval (600–1500)

Sample saint: **St. Symeon the New Theologian** (d. 1022; feast 12 October in the Greek calendar).

- **saint.gr:** `https://www.saint.gr/2723/saint.aspx`. About 1,700 words, with the Life summary.
- **Sanidopoulos:** "The Bright Feast and Glorious Celebration of Saint Symeon the New Theologian" by St. Nikodemos the Hagiorite (`https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2022/10/the-bright-feast-and-glorious.html`). Full text comes back with `curl` (about 244 words in the post body).
- **The primary Life:** Niketas Stethatos' *Vita of Symeon*. The edition is Hausherr–Horn (Orientalia Christiana 12, 1928). Access **unchecked**.
- **Migne PG:** Symeon's works are in PG 120 (Latin only in Migne). Not found on archive.org by search (unchecked). Do not rely on it.
- **OrthodoxWiki:** `https://orthodoxwiki.org/Symeon_the_New_Theologian` works. It is good for the outline and for the bibliography leads.
- **Delehaye does not help here.** The Sirmondian Synaxarium is a 10th-century text, so it has no entry for Symeon (tested by searching the text).
- **Metaphrastes** (PG 114–116) covers saints up to the 10th century: PG 114 = archive.org `patrologicursus00migngoog`, PG 115 = `patrologicursus09migngoog`, PG 116 = `patrologicursus06migngoog`.
- For Athonite saints: the *Typika* and Lives on the monasteries' own sites (mostly unchecked; see Athos below).

### Athos (all eras)

- **Pemptousia, Monk Moses archive.** See `references/orthodox-paterika.md` for the Greek books (*Μέγα Γεροντικό*, *Ασκητικές μορφές*, and others). Those are books, and most are not free.
- **Athonite monastery sites.** `impantokratoros.gr` returned 403 to `curl`. `vatopaidi.gr` and `mountathos.gr` did not answer at all. Use a browser, or find the monastery's news pages by search. All **blocked or unchecked**.
- **Nikodemos the Hagiorite's *Synaxaristes*** (18th century) is the base of the modern Greek Orthodox calendars. The full text online was **not found** by search. Sanidopoulos and Pemptousia quote him. Use those quotes and cite Nikodemos by name.
- **Doukakis, *Megas Synaxaristes*** (1889–1896, reprinted). Full digital text **not found**. It sits in university libraries. Leave it as a bibliographic lead.

### Early modern and Ottoman period (1500–1900)

Sample saint: **St. Kosmas Aitolos** (1714–1779, New Martyr and missionary; feast 24 August).

- **saint.gr:** `https://www.saint.gr/835/saint.aspx`. About 3,800 words. It lists the date of his death (1779), the sources, and the teachings.
- **Pemptousia:** the site search returns several articles (for example `https://www.pemptousia.gr/2025/08/agios-kosmas-aitolos-to-kyrio-onoma-tou-theou-mas-einai-i-agapi/`).
- **Sanidopoulos:** "Saint Kosmas the Aitolos and the New Martyrs" and other posts, found by feed search for `Kosmas Aitolos` (4 hits).
- **His own words:** the *Didaches* (Διδαχές). The Greek text is in older print editions. A digital copy was **not found** (the Anemi library was under maintenance and did not answer as `www.anemi.lib.uoc.gr`; `https://anemi.lib.uoc.gr/` answered, but the search was not tested).
- **New Martyrs generally** (Νεομάρτυρες): the *Νέον Μαρτυρολόγιον* (Nikodemos, 1799). Full text online **not found**. Search saint.gr, which has pages for many New Martyrs.
- **Wikisource (el.wikisource.org):** the site search API works (encode Greek with `urllib.parse.quote`), but it found no useful Kosmas or Synaxaristes text.

### Modern (after 1900)

Samples: **St. Nektarios of Aegina** (d. 1920, canonized 1961; feast 9 November) and **St. Paisios the Athonite** (d. 1994, canonized 2015; feast 12 July).

- **saint.gr:** Nektarios `https://www.saint.gr/2960/saint.aspx` (about 5,700 words). Paisios `https://www.saint.gr/3200/saint.aspx` (about 10,800 words, and it is a fixed page shown on every day's list).
- **Pemptousia:** an article on Nektarios (`https://www.pemptousia.gr/2019/11/agios-nektarios/`) and many on Paisios (found by site search).
- **agiospaisios.gr** — the official site of the Holy Monastery of St. Arsenios the Cappadocian, Souroti, which holds Paisios' grave. Answers to `curl` (about 1,500 Greek characters on the front page). Deeper pages unchecked.
- **Sanidopoulos:** four hits each for Nektarios and Paisios by feed search. The Paisios hits are stories, not a full Life.
- **Canonization records:** the Ecumenical Patriarchate decided the canonization of Paisios on 13 January 2015. The patriarchate.org site is a script-driven page: every URL tested returned the same 190 KB home page. Use a browser, or search for the *Tomos* and the Synod minutes on other sites (unchecked).
- **ecclesia.gr (Church of Greece):** the home page answers with about 2 KB. `https://www.ecclesia.gr/greek/holysynod/` is an open directory listing. It holds Holy Synod documents (unchecked for saints).
- Modern Greek Lives are under copyright. Facts only.

## Catholic and other views of this region's saints

- **Shared saints (before 1054):** Demetrios and other early martyrs are honored by both Churches. Give the Catholic date (26 October is the same) and the Orthodox date. Check the Catholic date in the Roman Martyrology.
- **Disputed saints.** These are known from general knowledge and were **not checked** in sources: Gregory Palamas (Orthodox saint; the Catholic view of the hesychast controversy is critical or divided), Mark of Ephesus, Photios the Great, Gennadios Scholarios. For these, present both views with sources. Set `venerated_in` only for the Church that honors the saint.
- **Catholic scholarship:** Acta Sanctorum (see Access), Analecta Bollandiana, and the *Bibliotheca Sanctorum* (unchecked, mostly not free), and the *Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca* (unchecked). The BHG number is the standard way to find the Greek text of any Life.
- **Eastern Catholic (Melkite, Greek Catholic):** unchecked. Start from their own eparchy sites.
- **New Advent, *Catholic Encyclopedia*:** many short articles on Greek saints. Article numbers were not found for Demetrios (three guessed URLs returned 404). Use its own alphabetical index.

## Access

- **saint.gr.** Works with plain `curl` and a browser User-Agent.
  - A saint page: `https://www.saint.gr/<ID>/saint.aspx`. IDs are not sorted by name. **`/1013/saint.aspx` is a different saint (Honorius).**
  - To find the ID, open the day page `https://www.saint.gr/MM/DD/index.aspx` (for example `/10/26/index.aspx`) and read the links. The letter pages (`/Α/searchalphabet.aspx`) returned the same short list, so do not use them.
  - The search form posts back with `__VIEWSTATE` and was not tested.
  - The `/<ID>/downloadpdf.aspx` link returns a PDF file. Do not download it.
  - Every page carries a fixed side panel (Paisios, Nikodemos, Panagia). Ignore those links.
  - Each page has "Biography" and hymns. It does not name its sources. Cross-check the facts.
- **Feast dates: 12 October vs 12 March for Symeon.** The Greek calendar has Symeon the New Theologian on 12 October. The 12 March date belongs to his teacher, Symeon the Pious (Eulabes, saint.gr ID 1921).
- **Sanidopoulos feed search:** `https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/feeds/posts/default?q=<name>&alt=json&max-results=50`. The search is thin: it gave 3 to 5 hits for each name I tried, and "Demetrios Myrrhbearer" gave 0. Try each spelling ("Demetrios", "Demetrius"). The site search page `https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/search?q=<name>` also answers. Full posts come back with `curl`. Take the `post-body` element. The mystagogyresourcecenter.com home page also answers.
- **Pemptousia search:** `https://www.pemptousia.gr/?s=<url-encoded Greek>` works when the Greek is percent-encoded. The English site `pemptousia.com` answers too (63 KB). Skip `pemptousia.gr/tag/...` URLs, which returned 404.
- **Archive.org.** Use `https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php?q=...&fl[]=identifier&fl[]=title&rows=10&output=json`. Then read `https://archive.org/download/<id>/<file>_djvu.txt` or list files at `https://archive.org/metadata/<id>`. Greek queries in the search API returned nothing. Use Latin titles.
  - **Delehaye:** the id is `DelehayeSynaxariumConstantinopolitanum`. The text file is `Delehaye, Synaxarium Constantinopolitanum_djvu.txt` (5.6 MB; URL-encode the spaces and comma). The plain `_djvu.txt` name returned 404.
  - **Migne PG:** the collection `PatrologiaGraeca` on archive.org has about 300 text files, but they stop at about volume 50. For later volumes use the Google scans: PG 114, 115, 116 (see above). Other volumes: search `title:(patrologiae graeca) AND 120` and similar. Also the item series `patrologiae_cursus_completus_gr_vol_0NN`.
  - **Acta Sanctorum:** items named `actasanctorumquo00NNunse` (NN is the running volume number, not the month volume). Some numbers fail (0063 returned 500). Volumes 0062, 0064, 0065 downloaded, and none contained the Demetrios Life. Read the volume front matter before trusting a number. The month-volume (Oct. XI) and its archive.org id are **unchecked**.
  - **Analecta Bollandiana:** items such as `AnalectaBollandiana1900a` and `sim_analecta-bollandiana_1939_57`. Search `Analecta Bollandiana` to list more.
- **Wikisource, el.wikisource.org:** the API needs percent-encoded Greek. The plain form with raw Greek returns 400.
- **Blocked or no answer:** `goarch.org/saints` (403), `impantokratoros.gr` (403), `hsir.org` (406), `synaxarion.gr`, `mountathos.gr`, `vatopaidi.gr`, `orthodoxia.info`, `documentacatholicaomnia.eu`, `migne.fr` (no connection). Do not cite them without checking in a browser.
- **anemi.lib.uoc.gr:** the front page answers (18 KB), and a notice says it is under maintenance. The search and the documents were not tested. `https://pergamos.lib.uoa.gr/` (University of Athens) answers with a Greek front page. Its content for saints is unchecked.
- **patristica.net:** answers, but it is a search form for Migne's Greek and Latin series and needs a browser. Its Greek section did not give links to volumes 114–120.
- **TLG (tlg.uci.edu)** answers with a login page. It needs a subscription.

## Language

- Search every form: Greek (Δημήτριος), the Latin form of Acta Sanctorum (Demetrius), the usual English (Demetrios or Demetrius), and epithets: ὁ Μυροβλύτης, ὁ Μεγαλομάρτυς, ὁ Θαυματουργός, ὁ Νέος Θεολόγος.
- Greek titles: Ὅσιος (venerable monk), Ἅγιος (saint), Ἱερομάρτυς, Νεομάρτυς (New Martyr), Ἰσαπόστολος, Μέγας.
- Transliteration varies: Symeon or Simeon; Kosmas or Cosmas; Nektarios or Nectarius; Paisios or Paisius. Old print uses polytonic Greek with accents and breathings. Search a short root of the word and expect OCR errors.
- **Calendar.** The Church of Greece and the Ecumenical Patriarchate use the New (Revised Julian) Calendar for fixed feasts. Athos and the Old Calendarists use the Julian Calendar (13 days behind in the 20th and 21st centuries). saint.gr's day pages follow the New Calendar; record which calendar each source uses. Easter is always on the Julian date.
- The Greek month name follows the Greek form (Οκτώβριος). Delehaye's text uses the Byzantine year, which starts on 1 September.

## Rights

- **Public domain:** Migne's *Patrologia Graeca*, Acta Sanctorum, Delehaye (1902), Analecta Bollandiana up to about 1939, and Nikodemos and Doukakis (old prints).
- **Modern text is under copyright:** Pemptousia articles, monastery sites, Sanidopoulos' translations, and modern editions. Use facts only, in our own words.
- **saint.gr** states that it is a private, non-profit site and that it will remove infringing text. Treat its text as facts-only.
- Free reading does not mean permission to copy.
