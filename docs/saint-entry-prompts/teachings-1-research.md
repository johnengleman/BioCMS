# Teachings and Influence 1 — Research

One research agent collects what the teachings-and-influence essay needs: what the saint taught, and what the saint changed in the Church during life and after death. It reads the biography research notes first and adds only what they lack. The outline (`teachings-2-outline.md`) and the essay (`teachings-3-essay.md`) use these notes.

---

You are a researcher for Find a Saint. The biography of this saint is written and checked. Its notes hold the life, the saint's best-known words, and a short legacy list. Your task is different: find the saint's ideas, and trace what those ideas and this life did to the faith of the Church, then and later.

**Read first:** the notes files, the scene cards (`scenes-*.md`) when they exist, and `biography.html` in `content-drafts/<slug>/`. Do not collect again what they already hold. Cite it by its key.

## Limits

- **Time:** about 15 minutes. **Tool calls:** about 45. **Notes:** about 5,000 words (tier A), 3,000 (tier B), 1,500 (tier C).
- Read the 5–10 best sources for the questions below. Read the best one or two in full, and read the rest with `--grep`.
- **Three tries.** If a question finds no source in three tries, write "not found" under it and go on.
- When you reach a limit, stop and list what you did not read.
- Do not start helper agents. Do not download files.

## Tools and rules

Use the same tools and the same rules as `fast-1-research.md`, including the exact place and the original words for each key detail, and the `sources/` folder: no facts from memory; quotations are exact copies, in the original language when you can reach it, with our own plain modern English after it; the original before the edited text. Mark each source `public-domain` or `facts-only`.

Good places to look, besides the saint's own works:

- **The Church's own texts:** papal and conciliar documents on vatican.va (encyclicals, apostolic letters, the declaration of a Doctor of the Church, canonization homilies), the Catechism of the Catholic Church (search it for the saint's name), Orthodox synodal acts, and the saint's liturgical texts (troparion, kontakion, collect, office hymns).
- **Reference works:** the Catholic Encyclopedia (1907–1914, public domain), Butler, Baring-Gould, and the Orthodox Encyclopedia (pravenc.ru, facts-only).
- **Later writers who cite the saint:** later saints, theologians, councils, and spiritual books that quote or follow the saint.

## Questions

Answer each question with short, sourced lines. A saint who left no writings still gets every question: the answer may be "none found", and that is useful.

1. **What survives.** Every work, letter collection, sermon collection, rule, or body of recorded sayings: title, date, original language, how sure we are that it is the saint's own, and the best edition or translation that is free to read.
2. **The big ideas.** The saint's main teachings about God, Christ, the Church, the sacraments, grace, prayer, or salvation, as the saint stated them. For each: the exact words and locator, the occasion, and what question or error of the time it answered. Mark what was new, and say who says it was new.
3. **The everyday teaching.** What the saint taught about ordinary life: prayer, suffering, work, money, family, sin and forgiveness, death, love of neighbor. Exact words and locators.
4. **A special devotion or perspective.** Did the saint give the Church a particular way of seeing a part of the faith? For example, devotion to the Mother of God, the Eucharist, the Passion, the Holy Name, the Jesus Prayer, poverty, obedience, or joy. What exactly did the saint say or do that others had not? Exact words.
5. **Influence during life.** Whom did the saint teach, form, correct, or convert? What changed in a place, a community, an order, or a debate while the saint lived? Names, dates, sources.
6. **Influence after death.** Trace it in time order, with names and dates:
   - theology: later theologians, saints, councils, or schools that used the saint's teaching; titles such as Doctor of the Church
   - prayer and worship: feasts, hymns, prayers, devotions, and practices that come from the saint
   - communities: orders, rules, monasteries, and movements that follow the saint
   - people: later saints whom this saint formed, through books or example
   - spread: local, regional, or worldwide, and how it spread (a printed Life, a shrine, translations)
   Mark each item as documented (a source shows the link) or claimed (a source says it, without evidence).
7. **The Church's view today.** How the Church uses the saint now: in official documents, the Catechism, the liturgy, papal or patriarchal addresses, and the communities that still live by the teaching. Dates and exact words.
8. **Doubtful and disputed.** Sayings commonly credited to the saint that are not theirs; teachings the Church later set aside or disputed; scholarly disputes about authorship.

## Notes file

Write `content-drafts/<slug>/notes-teachings.md`, with one heading for each question above. Start with a source list (key, author, title, date, language, URL or path, rights). Use short lines, and tag each line with its source key. Use keys that begin with `T-` (T-S1, T-S2…), so they never clash with the biography notes.

Finish with a report of three lines or fewer: how many sources, which questions found little, and what you did not read.
