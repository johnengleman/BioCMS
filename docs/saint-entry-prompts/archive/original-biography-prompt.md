# Original biography prompt (the user's own, from about 2024)

Kept verbatim for reference. Its ideas are merged into `04-write-biography.md`.

---

Biography Objective:

Using the provided outline, write a detailed, fact-based, and engaging biography of a saint. The biography should make the reader feel familiar with the saint, while strictly adhering to verifiable historical evidence. The goal is to create a compelling narrative, appropriate to the subject's historical significance, that is both informative and captivating, presenting the saint's life and legacy to a modern audience. The level of detail and narrative expansiveness should be proportionate to the saint's historical importance.

General Instructions:

Section-by-Section Process:

Write one full section from the outline at a time.

After completing each section, ask for feedback before proceeding to the next section.

Overall Biography Requirements:

Length: Total biography between 3000–6000 words.

Style: Factual and encyclopedia-like in content, but engaging and narrative in style. Aim for a balance between providing accurate information and telling a compelling story.

Tone: Clear, readable, and at a 10th-grade reading level. Maintain a respectful and objective tone, but don't be afraid to use descriptive language (where supported by sources) to help the reader understand the story.

Citations: Use only information explicitly supported by historical sources.

ABSOLUTELY NO INLINE CITATIONS. UNDER NO CIRCUMSTANCES SHOULD CITATIONS APPEAR WITHIN THE TEXT ITSELF. ONLY A FULL LIST OF CITATIONS AT THE END IS PERMITTED.

THIS IS A CRITICAL REQUIREMENT: NO INLINE CITATIONS. FAILURE TO ADHERE TO THIS WILL RESULT IN REWRITES.

Include full details for each source (author, year, title, publisher, etc.).

For digital sources without a URL, use: "Accessed via [AI Name], [Date]."

Follow either MLA or APA style consistently.

Sentence Structure: Avoid sentences structured as "x was not y, but z." Rephrase these sentences for directness and clarity. Prioritize active voice and straightforward phrasing.

I. Writing the Biography:

Transform the outline into a narrative biography, using only documented historical facts as the foundation for the story.

Avoid any speculation. Present only verifiable historical events, names, dates, locations, and anecdotes.

Do not include meta-commentary. Simply present the information as factual.

Incorporate Authentic Quotes:

Judiciously include up to five (5) verified quotes from the saint. These quotes must be directly sourced to reliable historical documents (letters, journals, writings, etc.).

Purpose: Use quotes to:

Show the saint's inner thoughts, feelings, or motivations.

Show their personality and character.

Add detail and authenticity to the narrative.

Highlight key moments or turning points in their life.

Integration: Weave the quotes seamlessly into the narrative, providing context and ensuring they flow naturally within the text. Do not force quotes into the text; they should enhance, not disrupt, the flow. Introduce the quote with a brief phrase explaining its context (e.g., "Writing to her friend Amabilia Filicchi, she expressed her growing faith, saying, '...'").

Explore Internal Struggles (If Documented): If historical sources reveal documented internal conflicts, doubts, or spiritual struggles, portray these with sensitivity and accuracy. Show how the saint wrestled with these challenges and how they contributed to their growth and development. Avoid speculation; base any depiction of internal struggles solely on evidence from letters, journals, or reliable contemporary accounts.

Develop a Character Arc (Within Factual Boundaries): While adhering strictly to historical facts, strive to present the saint's life as a journey with a discernible character arc. Show how they evolved over time, how they were shaped by their experiences, and how they responded to challenges. This doesn't mean inventing emotions or motivations; it means carefully selecting and arranging the documented facts to reveal a sense of growth, change, and development.

II. Structure:

Headlines: Use concise headlines (4–6 words each).

Subheadlines: Use concise subheadlines (4-8 words each) to break down each section. Each subheadline should introduce a specific aspect of the section's content.

Section Length: Each biography section should be between 500–1000 words. Each section should be composed of subsections, each of at least 3-4 sentences.

Chronology:

Present events in strict chronological order within each section and subsection.

Do not reference events out of sequence; mention dates only when crucial and verifiable.

III. Content, Style, & Readability:

Introduction (First Section):

Begin with a clear and engaging introduction: an interesting and relevant moment. Introduce the reader to the saint.

Consider the Significance of the Figure: For particularly prominent or influential figures, allow for a slightly longer and more detailed introduction. The hook and thesis should reflect the magnitude of their impact, providing sufficient context to establish their significance without sacrificing conciseness. Aim for a balance between brevity and conveying the scope of their contributions.

Provide context if needed: If the figure faced particular societal or circumstantial hardship, hint at that.

Follow with a clear thesis statement explaining the saint's significance and contributions.

Language and Tone:

Use simple, clear language and short sentences (aim for an average of no more than 20 words per sentence).

Prioritize a natural, flowing narrative. Vary sentence lengths and structures to create a dynamic rhythm.

Write in active voice and use concrete language.

Avoid overly dramatic, sentimental, or speculative language.

Narrative and Descriptive Elements:

Follow a chronological account based solely on verifiable historical facts.

Use clear descriptions and relevant details when directly supported by historical accounts to describe the saint's world and experiences.

Present anecdotes exactly as recorded, without embellishment.

Ensure each new section builds upon the previous one, creating a cohesive narrative arc.

Use smooth transitions between paragraphs, sentences, and subsections.

Emphasize Turning Points: Identify and highlight key moments of decision, transformation, or significant impact in the saint's life. Use narrative techniques (e.g., foreshadowing, where appropriate and supported by evidence) to build anticipation and create a sense of the saint's journey.

Subtle, Evidence-Based Foreshadowing (Where Appropriate): If historical sources provide hints of later developments in the saint's life, use subtle foreshadowing to create anticipation and build a sense of destiny. This must be done carefully and only when supported by verifiable evidence. For example, if a childhood anecdote reveals a particular character trait that becomes crucial later, you might briefly highlight that trait in a way that subtly anticipates its future significance. Avoid heavy-handed foreshadowing or anything that feels contrived.

"Show, Don't Just Tell (Impact): Instead of simply stating the saint's impact, show it through concrete examples and anecdotes. Illustrate how their actions directly affected individuals, communities, or institutions. Use accurate language (where supported by sources) to depict the documented consequences of their work. For example, instead of saying 'She helped the poor,' describe a specific instance where she provided aid and the documented outcome of that aid."

"Contextualize Challenges: When presenting challenges, opposition, or controversies, provide sufficient context to help the reader understand the reasons behind them. Explain the prevailing social, religious, or political climate that contributed to these difficulties. This helps the reader appreciate the magnitude of the obstacles the saint faced and the significance of their perseverance."

"Highlight Key Relationships: Portray the saint's interactions with significant individuals—mentors, family members, collaborators, and even adversaries—in a way that illuminates their personality and the dynamics of their relationships. Show these interactions through documented actions and words, revealing how they shaped the saint's life and work."

"**Continuously highlight what made the saint's actions, approach, or contributions unique, innovative, or distinct from their contemporaries, grounding these distinctions in historical evidence."

IV. Sentence Structure & Readability:

Paragraphs: Contain 3–6 sentences, unless a slightly longer or shorter paragraph is needed for narrative flow.

Transitions: Connect ideas smoothly and naturally, creating a seamless flow between sentences and paragraphs. Use a variety of transitional words and phrases, but avoid overusing them.

Variety: Mix sentence lengths and structures to maintain a dynamic yet clear narrative flow.

V. Verification & Accuracy:

Cross-check all details against reputable primary sources.

Do not include unverified or doubtful details.

Clearly distinguish between documented facts and traditional/legendary information.

For extraordinary events or miracles, clearly state the official Church-verified status.

Present all information as factual.

VI. Citation Guidelines:

All citations must appear together at the end of the essay (no inline citations or citation numbers).

Include full details for each source (author, year, title, publisher, etc.).

For digital sources without a URL, use: "Accessed via [AI Name], [Date]."

Follow either MLA or APA style consistently.

VII. Overall Goal:

Craft a biography that is:

Accurate & Fact-Based: Every detail must be grounded in historical sources.

Engaging & Accessible: Tell a clear story that introduces the reader to the saint's life.

Readable: Maintain a clear, concise style suitable for a 10th-grade reading level, with a strong narrative flow and logical progression. Prioritize a natural and engaging style.

Well-Structured: Use headlines and subheadlines to improve the organization and flow. Every subsection must have at least 3-4 sentences, to ensure readability.
