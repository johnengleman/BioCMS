# Original outline prompt (the user's own, from about 2024)

Kept verbatim for reference. Its ideas are merged into `03-evidence-outline.md`.

---

**Objective:**

Create a detailed and *compelling* outline for a fact-based biography of [Saint's Name]. The biography will bring the saint's life and legacy to life for a modern audience, strictly adhering to verifiable historical accounts while crafting a narrative that is both informative and engaging. The outline should be structured to support a clear and logical flow, but also flexible enough to capture the unique aspects of the individual saint's life and contributions. The level of detail and narrative expansiveness should be proportionate to the saint's historical importance.

**Instructions:**

In addition to your internal knowledge, perform online searches to find verifiable and reputable historical, scholarly, and Church sources that detail the saint's life. Incorporate lesser-known facts from reliable websites, academic databases, and church records, *but only if these facts are explicitly stated in the sources and can be directly verified*. Ensure all content is factual, straightforward, and chronologically organized, *but with an emphasis on identifying narrative connections and turning points*. Every point in the outline must be directly supportable by historical evidence.

**Guide:**

**(Note: The main sections (I-IX) provide a general framework. Within each section, use *flexible and specific subheadings* that are tailored to the individual saint. The length and emphasis of each section should also be adjusted based on the specific saint's life. Not all sections will be equally important for every saint.)**

**I. Introduction (Setting the Stage)**

- **Engaging Hook:** Begin with a documented, highly engaging description of a pivotal, dramatic, or transformative moment in the saint's life, OR a concise, impactful summary of their lasting legacy, *specifically highlighting what was unique, groundbreaking, or distinctive about their contributions or approach.* The hook should immediately capture the reader's attention and hint at the saint's significance. It MUST be directly supported by a reputable source. Consider the significance of the figure when deciding on length.
- **Thesis:** A concise statement of the saint's documented significance and contributions, as supported by reputable historical sources, *explicitly stating their unique or distinctive contributions and their impact.* Provide context if needed.

**II. A Promising Beginning (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Early Life and Formation)*

- **Birth and Family Context:**
    - Birthdate and place
    - Parents' names, occupations, and social standing *and their influence on the saint's early life*
    - Siblings and family dynamics, *highlighting any significant relationships or influences*
    - Socioeconomic context of their upbringing *and its impact on their development*
- **Childhood and Education:**
    - Formal education (if any) *and its nature*
    - Religious upbringing and instruction, *emphasizing key formative experiences*
    - Early mentors or influential figures *and the nature of their influence*
    - Key documented childhood events *that foreshadowed later traits or decisions*
    - Documented challenges or turning points in childhood
    - Significant relationships and their documented influence
- *(Use additional, specific subheadings as needed to capture the key elements of this period)*
- ***Throughout this section, look for and highlight any early indications of the traits or tendencies that would later define the saint's unique contributions.***

**III. A Call to Serve (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Path to Religious Life or Service)*

- **Turning Points and Decisions:**
    - Verifiable actions, statements, or events that indicate a commitment to a religious life or path of service, *emphasizing the context and motivations (as documented)*
    - Age, place, and circumstances of these documented actions or decisions
    - Documented reactions of family, community, *and any relevant authorities (religious or secular)* to these actions or decisions.
    - Documented opposition or challenges faced *and how they were initially addressed* (e.g., logistical, financial, theological, personal, etc.)
    - Documented sacrifices made
- *(Use additional, specific subheadings as needed to capture the key elements of this period, focusing on the process of commitment and the challenges encountered)*
- ***Throughout this section, highlight any decisions or actions that were unconventional or that set the saint apart from their contemporaries.***

**IV. A Life of Faith in Action (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Life of Service and Devotion)*

- *(Organize this section using thematically relevant and specific subheadings that capture the major aspects of the saint's active life. Examples include: "Founding the Mission to X," "The Controversy over Y," "A Ministry of Healing," "Preaching and Reform," etc. Maintain an overall chronological flow, but group related events and activities thematically.)*
- **Within each subheading, include:**
    - Specific actions, initiatives, and ministries (as documented)
    - Locations served and the documented impact on those communities
    - Interactions with important historical or religious figures (highlighting the nature and significance of these interactions)
        - Mentors and spiritual guides, and documented influence they had.
    - Challenges, controversies, and opposition faced *in this specific context*, specifying the *type* of challenge (e.g., logistical, financial, theological, personal, internal conflicts within the religious order/community) and the *source* of opposition (specific individuals or groups, and their documented reasons).
    - Documented examples of virtues, resilience, and perseverance
- *(If the saint was part of a religious order, include relevant details about their roles and responsibilities within the order, but integrate these into the thematic subheadings.)*
- ***Throughout this section, consistently emphasize how the saint's actions, methods, or approaches were unique, innovative, or particularly effective. Highlight any deviations from the norm and their consequences.***
- Extraordinary events or reported miracles attributed to them during this period (present these *exactly as they appear in historical sources*, clearly indicating their source and emphasizing that they are *attributed* accounts, not established facts, unless officially recognized by the Church. Maintain a clear distinction between documented historical events and later claims of miraculous occurrences.)

**V. Facing the Storm (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Trials and Tribulations)*

- *(This section can be integrated into Section IV if the trials are directly related to specific ministries or initiatives. If there are significant separate trials (e.g., persecution, illness), use this section.)*
    - Documented personal hardships, illnesses, or persecutions
    - Documented opposition from within the Church or from secular authorities
    - Documented opposition (from specific individuals or groups, and their documented reasons)
    - Documented controversies or misunderstandings (specifically outlining the nature of the disagreement and the parties involved)
    - Internal conflicts within the religious order/community(if any, and as documented.)
    - How they responded to adversity (documented through letters, writings, or accounts from contemporaries)
    - Documented examples of forgiveness, humility, and strength

**VI. Shaping the Future (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Major Contributions and Achievements)*

- *(Use specific subheadings to highlight key areas of impact. Examples: "Writings and Teachings," "Founding of Institutions," "Reforms and Initiatives," "Charitable Works," etc.)*
- **Within each subheading, provide:**
    - Specific examples and details (dates, locations, activities, as documented)
    - Documented impact on specific communities or institutions
    - Documented influence on subsequent religious practices or thought
    - Verifiable long-term consequences of their actions or initiatives
    - Tangible evidence of their influence
- ***Within each subheading, explicitly connect the saint's achievements to their unique qualities or approach. Explain how their distinctiveness contributed to their success or impact.***
- Writings (if applicable):
    - Letters, sermons, treatises, spiritual guides, etc. (Provide specific examples and their dates of composition, if known).
    - Original language of writings, if known
    - If applicable, locations where the original manuscripts are held
    - Key themes and teachings (only those explicitly stated in their writings).
    - Documented impact on theological thought or spiritual practice (as recorded in historical sources).
- Reforms and Initiatives (if applicable):
    - Documented changes in religious practice, liturgy, or Church structure that they were directly involved in (as recorded in historical sources).
    - Founding of institutions (hospitals, schools, orphanages) (dates, locations, and activities as documented in historical records).
    - Establishment of new religious orders or movements (dates, locations, and activities as documented in historical records).
- Charitable Works (if applicable):
    - Care for the poor, sick, and marginalized (only as documented in historical records).
    - Specific projects and their documented impact on the community (as recorded in historical sources).
- Miracles:

    Present any accounts of miracles attributed to the saint as they appear in the sources, clearly indicating their source and whether they are verified by the Church. Do not present them as factual unless officially recognized by the Church.

**VII. A Life Well Lived (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Later Years and Holy Death)*

- Roles and activities in their later years (focus on continued impact or a shift in focus)
- Mentorship of younger generations (if documented, highlighting specific individuals or groups)
- Documented health issues and their impact on their activities
- Circumstances of their death (as documented)
- Date and place of death
- Documented last words and final acts
- Immediate burial and documented initial reactions

**VIII. The Path to Sainthood (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Veneration and Canonization)*

- *(This section can potentially be combined with Section IX if the legacy is closely intertwined with the canonization process.)*
    - Documented initial veneration and growth of their following
    - Present any reports of miracles at their tomb or through their intercession as they appear in the sources, clearly indicating their source. Do not present them as factual unless officially recognized by the Church.
    - The Canonization Process (investigation, verification of miracles – *only include officially recognized miracles*)
    - Formal declaration of beatification and canonization (dates and locations)
    - Establishment of their feast day and liturgical commemoration. Development of liturgical prayers and hymns (mentioning any *significant or unique* aspects of these commemorations, and their documented origins).

**IX. An Enduring Legacy (Or Similar Narrative Title)**

*(Focus: Legacy and Enduring Influence)*

- *(Use specific subheadings to highlight different aspects of their legacy. Examples: "Impact on the Church," "Devotion and Pilgrimage," "Patronage," "Continuing Influence," etc.)*
- **Within each subheading, focus on:**
    - Documented influence on theology, spirituality, and religious practice
    - Documented role in shaping Church history and tradition
    - Spread of their cult, pilgrimage sites, veneration of relics (with documented details)
    - Causes, places, or groups that claim the saint as their patron
- **Modern Relevance:** Focus on documented evidence of the saint's *continued and tangible* influence. This could include: the ongoing activities of orders they founded; institutions named after them that *actively carry on their mission*; scholarly works about their life that offer *new interpretations or insights* (based on evidence); demonstrable ways in which their work *continues to impact specific communities or address contemporary issues*. Avoid vague statements. Prioritize concrete, verifiable examples of lasting impact.
- **(Optional) Brief Concluding Summary:**
    - *If, and only if,* after completing the outline, you feel that the unique aspects haven't been sufficiently emphasized, add a *very short* concluding paragraph *as part of the "Legacy" section* that briefly summarizes their distinctive contributions. This should be a *synthesis*, not a repetition, of previously presented information. This should be a rare occurrence and only for truly exceptional circumstances.
