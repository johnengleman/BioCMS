# Rules card (checkers and copy editors)

A short form of the writing rules, so that checkers and copy editors do not read the writing prompt. Read this card and `citations.md`. When a case is not clear, read `write-slim.md` (short); never the old `04-write-biography.md`.

## Readers and voice

- A bright thirteen-year-old, or an adult who reads English as a second language, must read the text without stopping. Grade 6–7; the check script rejects grade 8 and above.
- The narrator is warm, reverent, and on the saint's side. Plain story prose: no praise words ("holy", "humble", "selfless" about the saint), no sermon, no skeptical or clinical tone. Do not make the text chopped: keep it flowing, with varied sentences.
- The STE rule for messages to the owner never applies to site text.

## Sentences

- **The real actor is the subject.** The person, or God, who really did the action. A thing, place, time, or idea never does what only a person can do. Not "A muddy street had sent the icon through the gate", but "The clergy carried the icon through the yard, because the mud blocked the street." Natural forces may do what they really do.
- Natural English first (the slim prompt, `write-slim.md`, chosen by the owner on 2026-10-06). One short interruption is fine: a short "who" clause or a short phrase in apposition ("Her mother, Hortulana, had been a pilgrim"). Two or three interruptions stacked in one sentence are not.
- Do not glue two unrelated facts together with ", and" ("Saint Clare was the abbess there, and he loved her like a father"). Give each fact the sentence shape it needs.
- No proof sentences in the story ("Two of her sisters were there and heard him", "She told this to Francisca herself"): the numbered notes carry the sources. A witness is named only when that person acts or speaks in the scene, or when two tellers disagree.
- Most sentences 10–22 words; none over 30.
- A paragraph that lists many small facts reads as a list: keep the two or three details that serve the point.
- Every "he", "she", "it", "they" is clear. With two people of the same sex, name the one you mean.
- Plain words: "asked", not "bade"; "robe", not "habit". A Church word the story needs gets its own short sentence of explanation the first time.
- Few names: name only the people the reader follows; a one-time witness is "a neighbor" or "one of the sisters". At most two new names in a paragraph.

## Headings

Each heading says plainly what happens, like a table of contents, in 3–8 words, with only names the reader already knows. No riddles, quotations, or bare categories.

## Quotations

- Plain modern English with the exact meaning; nothing added; cuts shown with an ellipsis. No "thee", "hath", "spake".
- Never change a word inside quotation marks to fix style or to avoid a rule. If a quotation reads badly, list it for the checker.
- Never put a paraphrase in quotation marks.

## Titles

"Saint" (or "Blessed") before every saint's name, every time, in headings and in childhood scenes; never inside a quotation. A name the world knows better is given once, early: "The world still knows him as Padre Pio." When you are not sure someone is a saint, leave the name plain and list it. See `titles-pass.md` for the clashes (Pope Francis, Pope Benedict XVI, Thomas of Celano, Emperor Constantine).

## Reverence (from `reverence-pass.md`)

1. God acts in a miracle, but only where the source names God. Otherwise tell the source's own words with trust ("he was healed").
2. The people's prayer, vows, Communion, and thanks are told when the source gives them.
3. In a biography, the saint's life with God is the center.
4. Holy things have their proper names: Holy Communion, the holy relics, the Mother of God.
5. Trust the tradition: tell the Church's Lives and stories as the Church remembers them. Doubts and scholars' questions go only in "A Note on the Sources".
6. No "allegedly", "reportedly", "is said to", "claims no scientific proof", "does not dismiss" in the body.
7. The Church's own words at death and glory, used once ("reposed in the Lord", "glorified", "canonized").
8. "Saint" before every saint's name.
9. Warm, plain, and simple. Wonder in a plain sentence.

## Honesty and citations

Read `citations.md`, including section 1b: fix errors of fact, not fair word choices. In short: nothing without a source, nothing from memory, write less when a detail is missing, a note after every quotation and every story, borrowed words always in quotation marks, and the fact sheet (`facts.md`) wins over any other wording.
