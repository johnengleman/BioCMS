# Reverence

Only for texts written before 2026-10-05. New texts get these nine points from the writing prompts and the checker; do not run a separate pass on them.

The owner's verdict (2026-10-05): "There was a certain amount of reverence missing from a lot of these, everything from the biographies, the teachings, and the miracles… reverence and respectfulness missing."

Every text on the site is written by someone who stands inside the faith of the Church that venerates the saint, and who loves the saint. Reverence is not praise words. It is the way the text treats God, the saint, and holy things.

## What a reverent text does

1. **God is the one who acts.** A miracle is God's work, done through the saint's prayers or at the saint's tomb. Say so plainly, as the Church believes it: "Through the prayers of Saint Francis, God healed him." "At the tomb of Saint Sergius the boy was healed." Keep the teller named once ("Celano tells this"), but do not let the teller's name stand where God's work should be. Never write "Celano records that he was freed" as the whole of a miracle. But follow the source's own words about who acted. Write "God healed him" only when the source names God. When the source says "he was healed" or "the saint restored him to life", tell it that way, with trust. The checkers of the Saint Augustine and Saint Anthony passes had to undo about ten lines each where the pass put God in as the actor and the source did not.
2. **The faith of the people in the story is part of the story.** When the source says that someone prayed, vowed, went to the tomb, received Communion, wept, or gave thanks and praised God, tell it. That is often the heart of the account. Add only what the source gives.
3. **The saint's life with God is the center.** In a biography, the saint's prayer, love of Christ, and trust in God carry the story, in scenes and in the saint's own words.
4. **Holy things are named with care.** The Eucharist, the Mother of God, the Cross, the relics, the icons, and the sacraments get their proper names: "Holy Communion", "his holy relics", "the Most Holy Mother of God" where the tradition says it. The rule against praise words is about praising the saint, not about naming holy things.
5. **Trust the tradition.** See "Tell the tradition as the Church remembers it" in `04-write-biography.md`. No debunking in the body, no "only a legend", no "doubted". Scholars' questions go only in "A Note on the Sources".
6. **No clinical or legal framing.** Do not write "claims no scientific proof", "does not dismiss", "attributed by people", "we cannot say", "allegedly", "reportedly", or "is said to" in the body. A miracle list opens by presenting the accounts as the Church's memory of God's works through the saint, gathered from the oldest witnesses.
7. **The Church's own words for death and glory.** "He died" is fine. Where the tradition of the saint's Church says it, "he reposed in the Lord" (Orthodox) or "he went to God" may be used once at the death. The Church "glorified" (Orthodox) or "canonized" (Catholic) the saint.
8. **"Saint" before every saint's name.** See `titles-pass.md`.
9. **Warm, plain, and simple.** Reverence does not mean long or ornate sentences. Keep "Straight sentences" and "Easy to read". Wonder is welcome in a plain sentence: "The whole town saw it."

## The pass (for texts written before this rule)

Copy each file to `<file>.before-reverence`. Read the whole text and revise it to the nine points above. Change only tone, framing, and the treatment of God and holy things. Add no fact the sources do not give: where you restore a person's prayer or thanksgiving, it must be in the notes, the cards, or the source text. Keep every sentence straight. Run the check script on each file and keep it free of MUST FIX lines. List every change of substance in `reverence-notes.md`, so the checker can confirm no fact changed.

## Notes from the first pass (Saint Francis, 2026-10-05)

- The check script now judges praise words only in the narrator's words. Words inside quotation marks keep their source's wording: never paraphrase a quotation to avoid "holy" or "saint".
- Status lines in a miracle list: name the teller and the date with trust ("told by Thomas of Celano in his First Life (1228–29)"). Drop labels such as "legendary", "late account", or "the vision rests on one witness"; those belong in the closing note if anywhere.
- Rebuild thin miracle accounts from the source text when the source gives the vow, the prayer at the tomb, or the thanks to God, and check each restored detail in the source.
- Keep the real actor as the subject (see "The real actor is the subject" in `04-write-biography.md`). A reverent rewrite must never make a thing the actor: "A muddy street had sent the icon" was rejected by the owner as backwards.
