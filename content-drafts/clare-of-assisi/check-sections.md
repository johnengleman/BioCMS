# Check of the miracle list: Saint Clare of Assisi (2026-10-06)

File checked: `miracles.html` (47 accounts; no teachings essay yet). Backup: `miracles.html.before-check`. Checked against `biography.html`, `facts.md`, and the saved sources: S1 (Robinson, Legend), S3 (Acta Sanctorum, Latin), S4 (Process, Spanish). Every account and its "Source:" line was compared with the witness paragraphs in S4 and the Legend pages in S1. All 47 `<h3 id>` values are unchanged. The "Source:" lines are unchanged; all places were found as given.

## Fixes

1. **Intro, "three months after her death."** Death 11 August; hearings 24 to 29 November. Now "a little more than three months".
2. **Saracens** (W9.2; Legend pp. 36–37). The list follows Sister Francisca; facts.md follows the Legend. Both are kept, each with its teller. The list now says that Sister Francisca gave this account under oath, and that the Legend gives the prayer and the answer in fuller words and says the voice sounded like a little child's. Order fixed from W9.2: Saint Clare lay on the ground in prayer, and two sisters were holding her ("sostenían a la madonna"). Before, the list named only Francisca as holding her; W9.2 names the other sister as Illuminata of Pisa. Both are now named. "That evening" added from W9.2 ("llamó a las dos aquella tarde").
3. **Two children healed of fever** (W3.15, W9.6). Both witnesses name the father "messer Juan de maese Juan". W3.15 calls him the sisters' procurator. The words "healed at once" are the father's, in both witnesses: Felipa (W3.15, "repentinamente") and Francisca (W9.6, "instantáneamente", in the speaking room). The list gave Francisca's source for the first boy only and joined the two stories. Rewritten as one account under a new heading ("A procurator’s son is healed of fever"), each teller named, the father named Juan, and one sentence that the Process does not say whether it is one boy or two.
4. **Juan Ventura** (W20.9). Heading "1253" removed: the source gives "aquel año" (that year) and "septiembre pasado", not a year in figures. The body now says "that same year, after her death" and that he thought it was in September. The cause "because the man was not from those parts" is in the source ("pues no era de estas partes"), so it stays, and `facts.md` now records it. The heading before: "A raging man is healed, 1253".
5. **Benvenuta di Diambra.** The sentence "was the daughter of Diambra" is removed. The source says "Bienvenida de madonna Diambra de Asís", not "daughter". The "two years before" with "she believed" matches W11.1.
6. **Sister Agnes of Opportulo.** The sentence "was the daughter of Opportulo" is removed (the name explained itself; the tautology is gone).
7. **Cecilia's cough** (W4.9, W6.8). Amada tells the story. Cecilia testifies that she was healed "como dijo sor Amada". The list now names both.
8. **Nun of San Paolo** (Legend pp. 63–64). "The Pope" is now "Pope Innocent IV" at Lyons and "Pope Innocent" at Assisi in 1253. The Legend means the same Pope in both places (the next section names Pope Innocent). Quotation made exact and plain: "Do not weep, daughters, for the one who will conquer." The old wording "for her who will conquer" read badly.
9. **Quotations changed to read plainly, with the same meaning.**
   - Tempter: "He will not be blind who will see God" is now "Whoever will see God will not be blind" (Latin: "Caecus non erit, qui Deum videbit").
   - Giacobello: "and you will be healed" is now "so that you may be healed" (Latin "ut libereris"). "for you have been healed" is now "because you have been healed" (Latin "quia liberatus es").
   - Cannara: "will you dare to carry me any further, after I have been commended" is now "Will you carry me any further, thief, when I have been commended to so great a virgin?" The word "dare" is Robinson's addition. The Latin has "Portabis me tu latro ulterius, tantae Virgini commendatam?"
   - Monte Galliano: "O holy and glorious Clare, give me back my poor boy" is now "Holy and glorious virgin Clare, give me back my poor son" (Latin "Sancta et gloriosa virgo Clara, redde mihi miserum filium meum").
   - Sister Andrea: "for Sister Andrea is very ill" is now "because ..."; "Make her a soft-boiled egg ... when she can speak again" is now "Prepare a soft-boiled egg ... when she has her speech back" (W3.16: "prepara un huevo pasado por agua ... cuando haya recuperado el habla").
10. **Vitalis** (W3.19, W9.3, Legend p. 39). Felipa says Vitalis left "the next day, at night". Francisca says "the next day, in the morning". The Legend says "the following morning". The list said "the next night", which follows one witness only. Now "the next day", which all three give.
11. **French youth** (Legend p. 79). The list said the frenzy "made him violent". The Legend says it made his body restless; he could not be held and bruised himself in the hands of those who held him. Now: "made his body wildly restless... he hurt himself in the hands of those who tried."

## Checked and left (not errors)

- **Sister Andrea, "herself".** The Legend (p. 87) says the Lord would heal "what thou wished to heal thyself", so "cure it herself" has a source. W3.16: "muy tentada por querer sanar de ellas".
- **Communion quotation** (W9.10). It is a different occasion from Pope Innocent IV's visit (W3.24; facts.md item 14): about three years before the inquiry, around Saint Martin's day, after Mass, and different words. They are kept apart. Not in facts.md; the wording follows W9.10.
- **"The church of Saint Clare"** (French youth). The Latin Legend has "ad ecclesiam S. Clarae". The list tells the Legend's wording. Robinson (p. 73) has the body at San Giorgio. No source says the two are the same, so the list keeps the Legend's words.
- **Dropsy and procurator explanations.** Both are plain and right. Dropsy is a swelling of the body with water. A procurator is the man who handled the sisters' business.
- **Pope in "Nun of San Paolo".** Fixed above.
- **"Three months".** Fixed above.
- **Names:** Sister Benvenuta di Diambra and Sister Benvenuta of Perugia kept apart in every account; Saint Agnes (Clare's sister) at Panzo; Sister Agnes of Opportulo; Blessed Hortulana outside quotations, "Sister Hortulana" inside the quotation (Clare's own words, W4.11). All agree with `facts.md`.
- **Numbers:** twelve years (W11.1, W3.10); thirteen months (W4.7); fifty slices (W6.16); five sisters (W1.16); seven years, July (W5.5); twelve men, sixteen days (Legend pp. 41–42); four years, five demons (W4.20, W7.14); "more than twenty-two years" (W16.4). All agree.
- **Private visions** (Francisca's child and wings, Benvenuta di Diambra's virgins, Agnes of Opportulo's child, the brightness): each is attributed to the sister who saw it, with her "alone" statements kept.
- **Titles:** "Saint" before every saint's name outside quotations; none inside.
