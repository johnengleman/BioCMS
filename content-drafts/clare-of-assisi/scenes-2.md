# Saint Clare of Assisi — scene cards, group 2 (SC-06 to SC-10)

Source keys as in notes.md. S4 is Spanish and facts-only: our own words, short quotations marked "(our translation from the Spanish)". Page numbers of S1 are the book's own (OCR; small OCR errors in quotations are marked). Saved files are in `sources/`; nothing new was fetched.

Corrections to the outline, found in this pass:
- SC-06: Agnes of Opportulo borrowed the HORSEHAIR shirt, not the pig-skin one (S4 W10.1; S1 p.30-31). The pig-skin garment was worn in secret.
- SC-07: the pope-offer paragraph is S3 section 14 by OCR count, not 13 (see card).

## SC-06 — The hair shirt lent for three days (San Damiano)
- Status: full
- When and where: undated; San Damiano, "cuando ella, siendo muy niña, entró en el monasterio" (W10.1). Agnes of Opportulo lived there under Saint Clare "cerca de treinta y tres años" (W10.2).
- Who was there: Saint Clare; Agnes (Inés), daughter of messer Opórtulo di Bernardo of Assisi, witness 10 (W10.1); Pacifica, witness 1; Saint Francis and the Bishop of Assisi (name not given).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Saint Clare wore a hair shirt "de pelos de caballo anudados" (made of knotted horsehair) [S4 W10.1; S3 §17 "de pilis equorum nodose conserto"; S1 p.30]. S1: tied to the body with "little raw cords" (S3 "chordulis rudibus").
  2. Agnes borrowed it once "por espacio de tres días" (for three days); it seemed "tan áspero" (so harsh) she could not bear it [W10.1]. S1 p.31: she "gave it up after three days" (S3 "post triduum resignavit"). Benvenuta of Perugia: Saint Clare lent such garments "de vez en cuando a algunas hermanas" [W2.7].
  3. Saint Clare fasted on bread and water in the two long Lents, and ate nothing on three days a week: "lunes, miércoles y viernes" [W1.8; W2.8; S1 p.31; S3 §18].
  4. The sisters grieved: "se dolían y se lamentaban"; Pacifica "había llorado alguna vez" (had wept at times) [W1.7]. S1 p.32: they "deplored with tears" (S3 "lachrymis deplorabant").
  5. Saint Francis and the Bishop of Assisi ordered her to eat every day: "medio panecillo al día, que era aproximadamente onza y media" [W1.8]; Legend: "at least one ounce and a half of bread" [S1 p.32; S3 §18 "[u]nciam et dimidiam panis", OCR "tinciam"]. Benvenuta of Perugia was present when Saint Francis gave the order; she says she then took "un poco de pan y agua" "por cumplir con la obediencia" [W2.8].
- Details: pig-skin garment, worn secretly, hair turned to the flesh [W2.5; S1 p.30]; a stone for a pillow, "canto rodado" [W1.7]; bed of vine cuttings [W3.4].
- Inner life: the Legend says she kept a joyful face and "seemed either not to feel bodily austerities or to laugh at them" [S1 p.32; S3 §18 "vel non sentire, vel deridere"].
- Testimony (S1 p.30-31, public-domain; OCR fixed in "horses' hair"): "She was accustomed at times to wear a rough shirt made of horses' hair, twisted in a knot, which was fastened to her body on either side by little raw cords. When Cläre had lent this garment to one of the household who had asked for it, and the latter had put it on, she quickly succumbed to such harshness and gave it up after three days with more readiness than she had cheerfulness in asking for it." Witness, S4 W10.1 (facts-only; one short quotation): "la madonna se lo prestó una vez por espacio de tres días, y le pareció tan áspero, que de ninguna manera lo pudo soportar" (our translation: the lady lent it once for three days, and it seemed so rough she could not bear it at all).
  plain: Saint Clare sometimes wore a rough shirt of knotted horsehair, tied on with raw cords. She lent it to a sister who asked. The sister gave it back after three days, quicker than she had asked for it.
- What changed: Saint Francis and the bishop made her eat daily; the sisters' grief ended in obedience.
- Not in the sources: the sister's face or words; the room; the season; what Saint Clare said when it came back; the bishop's name; a date of the order.
- Note: S5-569 §40 (Letter 3 to Agnes of Prague, 1238): "a quadam indiscreta et impossibili abstinentiae austeritate quam te aggressam esse cognovi" (from a certain indiscreet and impossible severity of fasting, which I know you have taken up). This is 1238, fifteen years before her death, so "late in life" in the outline is not supported.
- Sources: S4 W1.7, W1.8, W2.5, W2.7, W2.8, W3.4, W10.1, W10.2; S1 pp.30-32; S3 §§17-18; S5-569 §40.

## SC-07 — The Pope's offer (San Damiano; date not fixed by the sources)
- Status: full (place and date are inferences; see below)
- When and where: the Legend gives neither. Witnesses: Pope Gregory "había ido personalmente al monasterio de San Damián" [S4 W2.22, Benvenuta of Perugia, present]. Robinson (editor, S1 note 116) places the "memorable interview" in May-June 1228, when Gregory IX came to Assisi for the preliminaries of Saint Francis's canonization; this is his inference, not the Legend's. He gives September 1228 for the Privilege of Poverty (S1 note 116; p.96; the bull "Sicut manifestum est", 17 Sept 1228, note 185).
- Who was there: Saint Clare; Pope Gregory IX; Benvenuta of Perugia (witness 2) heard it.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Saint Clare asked Innocent III for the "privilegium paupertatis"; he called it a unique request, and wrote "primam notulam sua manu" (the first lines in his own hand) [S3 §14; S1 p.26]. Robinson: whether it was granted in writing is "not clear" (S1 note 114).
  2. Gregory, who loved her with "paterno affectu", urged her to take some possessions, "propter eventus temporum et pericula saeculorum" (because of the troubles of the times) [S3 §14; S1 p.26]. She "fortissimo animo restitit" (resisted with a very strong spirit).
  3. He answered with release from her vow; she refused (Words spoken).
  4. Pacifica: Pope and Bishop of Ostia "ni... pudieron conseguir nunca" (never could persuade her) [W2.22]; Felipa: she honored the privilege and kept it "temiendo perderlo" (fearing to lose it) [W3.14]; Pacifica: he "le había querido dar muchas cosas" (wanted to give her many things) [W1.13].
- Words spoken (S3 §14, Latin; S1 p.26-27): Gregory: "Si votum formidas, nos te a voto absolvimus" (If you fear your vow, we release you from it). Saint Clare: "Sancte pater, ... nequaquam a Christi sequela in perpetuum absolvi desidero" (Holy Father, I never want to be released from following Christ, for all time).
- Testimony (S1 p.26-27, public-domain): "The Lord Pope Gregory, of happy memory, a man most worthy of the Chair as he was most venerable in merits, loved the Saint most dearly with a paternal affection. As he was seeking to persuade her that, on account of the condition of the times and the dangers of the age, she should consent to have some possessions which he himself liberally offered, she resisted with an unyielding resolve and would in nowise acquiesce. To whom the Pontiff answered: 'If thou fearest thy vow, we release thee from the vow.' 'Holy Father,' she said, 'Never do I wish to be released in anywise from following Christ forever.'"
  plain: Pope Gregory loved her like a father. He urged her to accept property he offered freely, because times were dangerous. She would not give way. He said, "If you fear your vow, we release you from it." She said, "Holy Father, I never want to be released from following Christ, not for all time."
- Bridge (S1 p.60; S3 §37): Gregory forbade any friar to visit the Ladies' monasteries without his leave. Saint Clare sighed: "Omnes nobis auferat de caetero Fratres" (let him take all the friars from us); she sent them all back to the Minister. Gregory then relaxed the order. Robinson ties the ban to the bull Quo elongati, 28 Sept 1230 (S1 note 146).
- What changed: the Pope gave her the right to own nothing (S1 p.96, note 116).
- Not in the sources: the room, the hour, who stood by; the Pope's tone; her tears or gestures; any year in the Legend itself.
- Sources: S3 §14; S1 pp.26-27, 60, 96, notes 114-116, 146, 185; S4 W1.13, W2.22, W3.14. Latin OCR numbering: S3 reads "13 Paupertatem ... 1 i Volens ... 1 5 Unicus erat"; so the offer paragraph is §14 by count, not §13 as in the notes.

## SC-08 — Saracens in the cloister (San Damiano)
- Status: full (no date; one witness gives the month)
- When and where: San Damiano, "durante la guerra de Asís" [W2.20]. Francisca: "el mes de septiembre, un viernes, según creía, como a la hora de tercia" (September, a Friday, she thought, about Terce); she could not say the year [W9.2]. Robinson's note 123 gives 1244 (Cristofani) or 1234 (others); secondary.
- Who was there: Saint Clare, gravely ill; the sisters; Francisca di messer Capitaneo of Col di Mezzo (witness 9), who held her up with Sister Iluminada of Pisa (dead by 1253) [W9.2].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Saracens "escalaron el muro" (climbed the wall) and came down into the cloister [W3.18; W2.20]. Legend: the army was "close to the gates" [S1 p.36; S3 §21].
  2. The sisters were afraid. Saint Clare "se hizo conducir hasta la puerta del refectorio" (had herself led to the refectory door) and ordered a "cofrecito" with the Sacrament brought [W9.2]. Legend: carried "to the door", a "silver casket enclosed in ivory" before her [S1 pp.36-37; S3 §21].
  3. She lay on the ground and prayed with tears [W9.2; S3 §22].
  4. Francisca heard a voice "de maravillosa suavidad"; Legend: "quasi pueruli" (like a child's) [W9.2; S3 §22].
  5. The Saracens left "sin causar mal ni daño" [W9.2]. Legend: they went down the walls they had climbed.
  6. That evening she called the two sisters and ordered silence: "mientras ella viviese" [W9.2; S3 §22 "dum corpus ago"].
- Words spoken: Saint Clare: "Señor, guarda Tú a estas siervas tuyas, pues yo no las puedo guardar" (Lord, guard these your servants, for I cannot guard them) [W9.2]; Latin: "Custodi Domine, precor, has famulas tuas, quas ipsa in praesenti articulo custodire non possum" [S3 §22]. Voice: "¡Yo te defenderé siempre!" (I will always defend you) [W9.2]; "Ego vos semper custodiam" [S3 §22]. For the city: "La ciudad sufrirá muchos peligros, pero será protegida" [W9.2]. To the sisters: "yo soy fiadora de que no sufriréis mal alguno" (I stand surety that you will suffer no harm) [W9.2]; "Fidejubeo vobis, filiolae" [S3 §22]. Earlier: "ponedme delante de ellos" (put me in front of them) [W3.18].
- Testimony (S1 p.37, public-domain): "And as she wholly prostrated herself before the Lord in prayer she said to her Christ amid tears: 'Doth it please Thee, my Lord, to deliver Thy defenceless handmaids, whom I have nourished with Thy love, into the hands of the pagans? Defend, O Lord, I beseech Thee, these Thy servants whom I in this hour am unable to defend.' Presently He sent her of His special grace a voice as if of a little child which sounded in her ears: 'I will always defend thee.'"
  plain: She lay face down before the Lord and said in tears, "Do you wish, my Lord, to hand over your defenceless servants, whom I raised in your love, to the pagans? Lord, protect these servants. I cannot protect them now." Then a voice like a little child's sounded in her ears: "I will always protect you."
- What changed: the enemy left; only two sisters knew of the voice while she lived.
- Not in the sources: soldiers' number or weapons; blood; the weather; whether Saint Clare stood or was carried the whole way; how she looked.
- Sources: S4 W2.20, W3.18, W9.2; S3 §§21-22; S1 pp.36-37, note 123.

## SC-09 — Christmas night, 1252 (San Damiano, her sickbed)
- Status: full
- When and where: Christmas night of the year before the Process: "la noche de la Navidad del Señor del año pasado" [W3.30]; "próxima pasada" (the one just past) [W7.9, Balbina]. Robinson: "generally referred to Clare's last Christmas on earth, that of 1252" [S1 note 131]. Her sickroom at San Damiano; the Friars' church is San Francesco, "about two kilometers" away (Robinson, note 131).
- Who was there: Saint Clare alone. The sisters had gone to the chapel for Matins. Witnesses heard it from her: Felipa (W3.30), Amada (W4.16), Balbina (W7.9).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Too ill to rise, she was left alone while the sisters went to Matins [W3.30; S1 p.47]. Balbina: "Vosotras me habéis dejado aquí sola" (You left me here alone) [W7.9].
  2. She sighed her words (below).
  3. She heard "los órganos y los responsorios y todo el oficio de los frailes" (the organs, the responsories and the whole office of the friars) at San Francesco, "como si estuviera presente" [W3.30]; Legend: voices chanting the psalter, the organ [S1 p.47; S3 §29].
  4. Amada: she heard Saint Clare say she also saw "el pesebre de nuestro Señor" (the Lord's crib) that night [W4.16]; Legend: "ipsum... Domini praesepe videre" [S3 §29].
  5. Morning: she told the sisters (below).
- Words spoken: "¡Oh Señor Dios! Aquí me han dejado sola contigo, en este lugar" [W3.30]; Latin: "Domine Deus, ecce sola relicta sum tibi in loco isto" [S3 §29]. Morning: "Benedictus Dominus Jesus Christus, qui me, vobis relinquentibus, non reliquit" (Blessed be the Lord Jesus Christ, who did not leave me when you left me) [S3 §29].
- Testimony (S1 pp.47-48, public-domain): "At the hour of the Nativity, when the earth sings with the angels of the new-born Babe, the other Ladies went to the oratory for Matins and left the Mother alone, weighed down with illness." [...] "In the morning when her daughters came to her the Blessed Cläre said: 'Blessed be the Lord Jesus Christ, who when ye left me did not abandon me. By the grace of Christ I have indeed heard all the solemnities which were celebrated last night in the Church of S. Francesco.'"
  plain: On Christmas night the sisters went to Matins and left her alone, weak with illness. In the morning she said, "Blessed be the Lord Jesus Christ, who did not leave me when you left. By his grace I heard all the services in the church of San Francesco last night."
- What changed: loneliness answered; she told it to the sisters.
- Not in the sources: the hour by clock; the room's light or cold; her tears; which sister was told first; the details of the crib.
- Sources: S4 W3.30, W4.16, W7.9; S3 §29; S1 pp.47-48, note 131.

## SC-10 — The Pope at her bed; the bull the day before she died (San Damiano, August 1253)
- Status: full. Witness 3 and the document are settled below.
- Who is witness 3: Sor Felipa, daughter of messer Leonardo di Gislerio, a nun of San Damiano, who entered four years after Saint Clare [S4 III, W3.1]. Pacifica is witness 1 (W1.1). W3.32 is Felipa's, so the notes' "Pacifica" is an error.
- What document: the papal bull confirming the Rule. W3.32: she wished "tener bulada la regla de la Orden y poder besar un día la bula y al día siguiente morir" (to have the Rule of the Order sealed by bull, and to kiss the bull one day and die the next). Robinson names it: bull "Solet annuere", 9 August 1253, Innocent IV, approving the Rule drawn by Cardinal Rainaldo on 16 Sept 1252 [S1 pp.96-97; note 188]. Gilliat-Smith agrees ("Solet annuere sedes apostolica", Part II ch. XIII, p.273); its closing date is "at Assisi on the fifth day from the Ides of August in the eleventh year" (= 9 Aug 1253) [S2 Part II ch. XIII, last lines of the bull text; page not checked]. The bull speaks of "the bond of sublime poverty" [S2 p.274]. It is not the Privilege of Poverty of 1228 (that is SC-07). Conflict: W3.32 says "the day before" she died; S2 p.273 says "two days before"; the bull is dated 9 Aug and she died 11 Aug [S1 p.96; S4 W11.4]. Do not reconcile.
- When and where: San Damiano; Innocent IV visited "pocos días antes" (a few days before) her death [W3.24]. Robinson: "a few days after his arrival in Assisi and again about two days before her death" (S1 note 153).
- What happened, in order:
  1. The Curia came from Perugia to Assisi [S1 p.64]. The Pope came with the cardinals to see her [S1 p.65].
  2. He touched his hand to her lips; she asked to kiss his foot; he stood on a wooden footstool; she kissed it "above and below" [S1 p.66]. Felipa does not tell the foot kiss.
  3. She asked absolution; he said "Would to God that I had no more need of forgiveness" [S1 p.66].
  4. That day she received the Sacrament from the Provincial Minister [S1 p.66]. Afterwards she spoke to the sisters (below).
  5. Near her death, "llegó un fraile con las Letras buladas" (a friar came with the sealed letters), and she took them and kissed the bull [W3.32]. The friar is unnamed.
  6. "Al día siguiente, pasó de esta vida" [W3.32].
- Words spoken: "el cielo y la tierra no son bastantes" (heaven and earth are not enough), said to the sisters after the visit [W3.24]. At the end of her life she "recomendó encarecidamente el Privilegio de la Pobreza" (urged them to keep the Privilege of Poverty) [W3.32].
- Testimony (S1 pp.65-66, public-domain): "Having entered the monastery he approached the bed and touched his hand to the lips of the invalid for her to kiss. She took it most gratefully, and begged, with the utmost reverence, to be allowed to kiss the Pope's foot. The courtly Lord having mounted a wooden footstool condescendingly extended his foot, and Cläre reverently inclining her head impressed a kiss upon it both above and below." Also: "Praise the Lord, my little daughters, that in this day Christ hath vouchsafed me a favor so great that heaven and earth would not suffice to repay it." Back of the parchment, contemporary hand [S1 p.98; S2 p.274]: "Hanc beata Clara tetigit et obsculata est pro devotione pluribus et pluribus vicibus" (Blessed Clare touched this and kissed it from devotion many times).
  plain: He came to her bed and held out his hand; she kissed it, then asked to kiss his foot. He stood on a wooden stool and gave it; she kissed it above and below. She said, "Praise the Lord, little daughters: Christ gave me a favor so great that heaven and earth could not repay it."
- Details: Innocent IV wrote on the top margin "Ad instar fiat S." and "Ex causis manifestis michi et protectori monasterii fiat ad instar" (S for Sinibaldus); Cozza-Luzzi and Oliger say it is his own hand, and Robinson says he wanted to hurry the bull "to the dying Saint" [S1 pp.97-98; S2 p.274]. "It is said" the bull was clasped in her hands at death (S1 p.98 and note 194, secondary).
- What changed: her Rule, built on poverty, was confirmed in her last days.
- Not in the sources: the friar's name or order; the hour; whether the Pope was present when the bull came; what she said to the friar; the Latin of the Pope's visit (S3's Latin lacks the death chapters).
- Sources: S4 III (W3.1, W3.24, W3.32), W11.4; S1 pp.64-66, 96-98, notes 150-153, 188, 194; S2 Part II ch. XIII pp.272-275 (Gilliat-Smith, English, public-domain).
