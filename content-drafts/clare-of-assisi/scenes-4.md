## 1 — SC-05: Saint Francis's death, and Saint Clare
- Status: thin on her own words; full on the date, the place, and the body at San Damiano.
- When and where: Saint Francis died at the Porziuncola, to which "he caused himself to be carried in the last days of his last sickness; there he died" [S2 ch.III pp.46-47, grep line 2581]. Date: "on the evening of the 4th of October, 1226"; "the next day he was buried at Assisi in the Church of Saint George" [S2 p.74]. S9 p.134 says "the 3rd of October, 1226" (conflict; S2 and the Latin do not agree on one date; write "early October 1226" or "the night of 3-4 October"). The Latin: "totam noctem illam, in qua obiit sanctus pater" (all that night in which the holy father died) [S6 Celano 1 §116].
- Who was there: the friars and the people of Assisi with the clergy; Saint Clare "cum reliquis filiabus" (with the other daughters) [S6 §116].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Before he died, she was ill and feared she would die before him. She sent a friar to tell him. The Mirror: she "grieved bitterly and could not be consoled" (S10 ch.X pp.161-162; Latin of this book not seen). He sent his blessing and absolution in a letter [S10 p.162; paraphrase].
  2. His answer: "Go and tell Sister Clare not to grieve and be sad because at present she cannot see me, but let her know she shall verily see me before her death" [S10 p.162].
  3. He died in the night. At dawn "mane autem facto" (when morning came) the crowd of Assisi took the body "cum hymnis et laudibus, clangentibus tubis" (with hymns and praises, with trumpets) toward the city, with olive branches [S6 §116].
  4. The procession stopped at San Damiano: "deponentibus eum in ecclesia Sancti Damiani" (they set him down in the church of San Damiano) [S6 §116]. "aperta est fenestra parvula" (a small window was opened), the one through which the sisters received Communion [S6 §116].
  5. Saint Clare came "ad videndum patrem non loquentem eis" (to see the father who no longer spoke to them) [S6 §116]. The Mirror: the friars lifted the body from the bier and held it in their arms before the window "for a long time" [S10 p.163].
  6. The sisters cried out. Latin, one group lament: "Pater, pater, quid faciemus? Cur nos miseras deseris" (Father, father, what shall we do? Why do you leave us, poor women?) [S6 §117]. Then "virgineus pudor multo fletui imperabat" (a maidenly modesty held back much weeping), for it was unfitting to mourn him whose death the angels attended [S6 §117].
- Details: the small Communion window; the friars carrying the body; the sisters kissing his hands: "deosculabantur splendidissimas manus eius" [S6 §117]. S9 p.134 adds that Clare kissed the stigmata and that a nail could not be removed; that is de Chérancé's retelling and I did not find it in S6 or S10. Do not use it.
- Words spoken by her: none of her own. Careful: S9 p.135 gives "O blessed Father, you were the Founder..." as her cry, citing Celano. In the Latin (S6 §117) the lament belongs to the sisters as a group. Do not give it to her alone.
- Inner life, her own words: in her Testament she calls him "columna nostra, unica consolatio post Deum et firmamentum" (our pillar, our only comfort after God, and our support), and says the sisters feared their weakness "post obitum sancti patris nostri Francisci" (after the death of our holy father Francis) [S3 Testament §43; S1 note 64 quotes the same Latin, "Test. B. Clarae, Seraph. Legislat. p.276"]. Plain: he was her pillar, her only comfort after God, her support. She also writes that he "was ever careful in word and deed to tend us, his little plant" ("sollicitus fuit verbo et opere semper excolere et fovere nos plantulam suam") [S3 §44]. That is the only first-person feeling about his loss.
- Testimony (public-domain; S6 Celano 1 §116, Latin; S10 Mirror, English, ch.X p.163):
  > "perventum esset ad locum in quo religionem et ordinem sacrarum virginum et Dominarum pauperum ipse primo plantavit, deponentibus eum in ecclesia Sancti Damiani, in qua dictae filiae suae quas Domino acquisierat morabantur, aperta est fenestra parvula per quam ancillae Christi constituto tempore communicare solent Dominici corporis sacramento... Et ecce domina Clara... venit cum reliquis filiabus ad videndum patrem non loquentem eis, nec reversurum ad eas, alibi properantem." [S6 §116]
  plain: They came to the place where he first planted the order of the poor ladies, and set him down in the church of San Damiano, where his daughters lived. The small window for Communion was opened. Lady Clare came with the other daughters to see the father who no longer spoke to them and would not come back to them.
  > "the friars lifted the holy body from the bier and held it in their arms before the window for a long time, giving the Lady Clare and her sisters this consolation, though they were full of sorrow and affliction, shedding many tears" [S10 p.163]
- What changed: his promise to see her before her death is kept; the poverty he wrote for her is hers to guard (Rule ch. VI, S1 App. I pp.110-111).
- Not in the sources: a single speech by Clare at the window; what she did in the days after; whether she touched the body (the Latin says only that the sisters kissed the hands); the hour of the procession; the weather. Her private grief beyond "grieved bitterly" before his death (S10) is not told. Invent none.
- Sources: S6 Celano, Vita prima, ed. 1906 (Latin), archive:sfrancisciassis00dogoog, Legenda prima §§116-117, public-domain; S10 The Mirror of Perfection, tr. Steele, 1902, archive:TheMirrorOfPerfection, ch.X pp.161-163, public-domain (author attribution debated; the Mirror is a later compilation); S2 pp.46-47, 74; S9 de Chérancé, St. Clare of Assisi, 1910, archive:StClareOfAssisi, pp.133-135, public-domain; S3 Testament (below, card 2).

## 2 — The Testament (her own account)
- Status: full for the passages; one caution on the text.
- Where the text is: the Latin is in S3 (Acta Sanctorum, Commentarius to Clare, §§41-49, p.748), printed from "antiquo Ms. Memoriali" as Wadding gave it in the Annales Minorum for 1253. Papebroch writes that it "potius ad illa propinqua mortis tempora pertinet" (belongs rather to the time near her death) [S3 Comm., note before §41]. English: S9 Appendix pp.223-229 (a free rendering; I checked key phrases against the Latin). Robinson cites it as hers (S1 notes 8, 64, 9). Caution: Robinson also says the Testament is "not found in any of the early MSS." of the Italian Life and that its authenticity "has been called into question on other grounds" (S1 introduction, in the note on the Magliabechian MS.). So: attribute it ("the Testament that has come down under her name"). The notes call it contested.
- Key passages, Latin [S3 §§41-44, copied; OCR joins fixed only where marked] with plain English:
  1. Opening: "Inter alia beneficia, quae a largitore nostro Patre misericordiarum recepimus, et quotidie recipimus" (among the gifts we have received and keep receiving from the Father of mercies) the first thanks go to her "vocatio" (calling) [S3, note before §41].
  2. Francis at San Damiano: he stood on the wall and spoke in French to poor neighbours: "Venite et adjuvate me in opere monasterii Sancti Damiani, quoniam adhuc erunt dominae ibi, quarum famosa et sancta conversatione glorificabitur Pater noster coelestis in universa Ecclesia sua" [S3 §40-41]. Plain: "Come and help me build the monastery of San Damiano, for ladies will live here whose holy life will bring glory to our Father in heaven in all his Church." She says he foretold this "dum essemus in seculi vanitate" (while we were still in the vanity of the world).
  3. Her conversion: "Postquam altissimus Pater coelestis per misericordiam suam et gratiam cor meum dignatus est illustrare, ut exemplo et doctrina beatissimi patris nostri Francisci poenitentiam facerem, paulo post conversionem ipsius, una cum paucis Sororibus... obedientiam sibi voluntarie promisi" [S3 §42]. Plain: after the Father lit up my heart, so that I did penance by the example and teaching of Saint Francis, a little after his own conversion, I promised him obedience, with a few sisters. (The same words open Rule ch. VI; see card 3.)
  4. His care: he "obligavit se... habere semper de nobis, tanquam de Fratribus suis, curam diligentem, et sollicitudinem specialem" [S3 §42] (bound himself to care for us as for his own friars). He wrote "formam vivendi et maxime ut in sancta paupertate semper perseveraremus" (a form of life, above all that we persevere in holy poverty), and "plura scripta" (many writings) so that "post mortem suam" we would not turn from it [S3 §42].
  5. Her promise: "iterum atque iterum voluntarie nos obligavimus dominae nostrae sanctissimae paupertati" (again and again we bound ourselves freely to our lady, most holy poverty) [S3 §43]. She sought the privileges of Innocent and his successors "ne... ab ipsa ullatenus declinaremus" (so we would never turn from it) [S3 §43].
  6. Christ's poverty: "amore illius Domini, qui pauper positus in praesepio, pauper vixit in seculo, et nudus remansit in patibulo" (for love of the Lord who, poor, was laid in a manger, lived poor in the world, and stayed naked on the gibbet) [S3 §43].
  7. Land: sisters must not acquire land, "nisi quantum extrema necessitas pro horto ad excolenda olera poposcerit" (except what dire need requires for a vegetable garden); more ground must "semper solida et inculta permaneat" (stay firm and untilled) [S3 §44].
  8. On Francis: "fundatorem, plantatorem, et adjutorem nostrum in servitio Christi" (our founder, planter, and helper in Christ's service) [S3 §44]; "columna nostra... unica consolatio post Deum et firmamentum" [S3 §43].
- Testimony: the passage in step 8 and step 3 above is 200 words or less; copied exactly in the steps.
- Not in the sources: dates of the writing; who copied it; any scene of her dictating it.
- Sources: S3 Commentarius pp.747-748 (public-domain, Latin, Papebroch 1737 ed.); S9 Appendix pp.223-229; S1 notes 8, 9, 64, introduction.

## 3 — SC-02: her own voice at the flight
- Status: full on (a), (b), (c); S1 p.25 gives no order for the sale, and S4 gives one order.
- (a) Sale before or after leaving home: S1 p.25, ch. "Of her true and holy poverty": "in the very beginning of her conversion, Cläre caused the paternal inheritance which had come to her to be sold and, reserving nothing of the price for herself, gave it all to the poor" (Latin not found in this session). No sequence with the flight. S4 W12.3 (Beatriz, her sister): "Y vendió toda su herencia y parte de la herencia de la testigo y la dio a los pobres. 4. Y luego, san Francisco la tonsuró" (and she sold all her inheritance, and part of the witness's, and gave it to the poor; and then Saint Francis cut her hair). So the sworn order is: sale, then the hair cut at the Porziuncola. W12.11: her relatives "habían querido dar más cantidad" (wanted to pay more) but she "no había querido vendérsela a ellos, sino a otros, para que no quedasen defraudados los pobres" (would not sell to them, but to others, so the poor would not be cheated). That is the same witness. The "before the flight" reading is fair; no source gives the day. Write "before she left" only as W12.3's order.
- (b) Letter 1 (1234), Latin [S5-560 §§15-17, 30; English there is Mueller, facts-only]:
  "O beata paupertas, quae diligentibus et amplexantibus eam divitias praestat aeternas!" (§15) Plain: blessed poverty, which gives eternal riches to those who love and embrace it.
  "O pia paupertas, quam Dominus Iesus Christus... dignatus est prae ceteris amplexari!" (§17) Plain: loving poverty, which the Lord Jesus Christ chose to embrace before everything else.
  "Magnum quippe ac laudabile commercium: relinquere temporalia pro aeternis, promereri caelestia pro terrenis, recipere centuplum pro uno, beatam vitam habere aeternam." (§30) Plain: a great and praiseworthy exchange: to give up the passing for the lasting, to earn heaven in place of earth, to receive a hundredfold for one, to have a happy, everlasting life. She also names Agnes's choice: "sanctissimam paupertatem et corporis penuriam elegistis" (you chose most holy poverty and bodily want) (§6).
- (c) Rule ch. VI [S1 App. I p.110]: yes. The chapter, headed "Of the Seraphic Institution of the Second Order and of the promised privation of possessions", is in the first person: "After the Heavenly Father Most High deigned to enlighten my heart by His grace to do penance according to the example and teaching of our most blessed Father St. Francis, I, together with my Sisters, voluntarily promised him obedience a little while after his conversion." plain: After the Father lit up my heart to do penance by Saint Francis's example, I and my sisters freely promised him obedience, a little after his own conversion. The Latin of the ending is quoted by de Chérancé: "Paulo ante obitum suum, scripsit nobis ultimam voluntatem suam dicens..." (shortly before his death he wrote us his last will, saying...) [S9 p.133 note]. Robinson marks ch. VI as one where "the impersonal style of the legislator is abandoned" and her own hand shows [S1 App. I pp.96-97]. It contains Saint Francis's last wish (p.111) and her pledge to keep poverty. Not seen: the full Latin of ch. VI (S2 Rule table not searched).
- Not in the sources: the price of the inheritance; who bought it; how many days between sale and flight; what she told her family.
- Sources: S1 p.25, pp.96-97, 110-111; S4 W12.3, W12.11; S5-560 (Latin; English by Joan Mueller, facts-only); S9 p.133.

## 4 — SC-07: the friars sent away
- Status: full.
- When and where: Gregory IX's ban follows Quo elongati of 28 September 1230 [S1 note 146]. No day for the scene. Place: San Damiano.
- What happened, in order:
  1. The Pope forbids any friar to go to the nuns' monasteries without his leave: "prohibuisset, ne aliquis Frater ad monasteria dominarum sine sua licentia pergeret" [S3 §37].
  2. Saint Clare, grieving that her sisters would get the food of the word less often, said with a sigh: "Omnes nobis auferat de caetero Fratres, postquam vitalis nutrimenti nobis abstulit praebitores" [S3 §37]. Plain: Let him take all the friars from us now, since he has taken those who gave us the food of life. (Source for the quote: S3 OCR "cnetero", "vilalis"; fixed here to the printed words.)
  3. She "statim omnes Fratres ad Ministrum remisit, nolens habere eleemosynarios, qui panem corporalem acquirerent, postquam panis spiritalis eleemosynarios non haberet" [S3 §37]: at once she sent all the friars back to the Minister; she would not keep alms-collectors for bodily bread when she had none for spiritual bread.
  4. Gregory "statim prohibitum illud in generalis Ministri manibus relaxavit" [S3 §37]: at once he eased the ban and placed the matter in the hands of the Minister General. S1 p.60 in English: "he immediately mitigated his prohibition, leaving the matter in the hands of the Minister General." The Pope relented in his own act; no source names a messenger.
- Full Latin of S3 §37 (as in S3, line breaks removed): "Cum semel dominus Papa Gregorius prohibuisset, ne aliquis Frater ad monasteria dominarum sine sua licentia pergeret, dolens pia Mater cibum sacrae doctrinae rarius habituras sorores, cum gemitu dixit: Omnes nobis auferat de caetero Fratres, postquam vitalis nutrimenti nobis abstulit praebitores. Et statim omnes Fratres ad Ministrum remisit, nolens habere eleemosynarios, qui panem corporalem acquirerent, postquam panis spiritalis eleemosynarios non haberet. Quod cum audiret Papa Gregorius, statim prohibitum illud in generalis Ministri manibus relaxavit." (S3 prints "doctrinEe", "cnetero", "vilalis", "praebilores", "eleemosyna- rios", "Grego- rius"; here corrected from the OCR.)
- Words spoken: the line above, with a "gemitu" (a groan, a sigh) [S3]; S1 p.60 "with a sigh".
- What changed: the ban was eased, with the Minister General holding the permission (S1 note 147: "to their Provincial").
- Not in the sources: the year; the number of friars; who carried the news to the Pope; whether she or the friars spoke to him; the Minister's name.
- Sources: S3 Legend §37 (Commentarius text, public-domain); S1 p.60, notes 146-147.

## 5 — SC-10: the Pope at her bed
- Status: full; answer is no, not the Pope.
- Answer: in the Legend she asked Cardinal Rainaldo, Bishop of Ostia, not Pope Innocent IV, and she asked him about seven months earlier, when he visited her during the Pope's stay at Perugia [S1 pp.63-64, note 151]. "Cläre with tears besought so great a father that, for the name of Christ, he would ever be mindful of her soul and the souls of the other Ladies. But above all else she begged that he would ask the Lord Pope and the Cardinals to confirm the privilege of Poverty. This the faithful supporter of religion not only promised in word but fulfilled in deed." [S1 pp.63-64]. plain: She asked the Cardinal, with tears, above all to ask the Pope and the Cardinals to confirm the privilege of poverty. He promised it and did it. Date: "8 September, 1252"; on 16 September 1252 he drew up the Rule for her [S1 notes 151, App. I p.96].
- At the Pope's bed (spring 1253, the Pope came 27 April-1 May [S1 note 152]; a first visit a few days after he arrived in Assisi, a second about two days before her death [S1 note 153]): "Having entered the monastery he approached the bed and touched his hand to the lips of the invalid for her to kiss" [S1 pp.65-66]. She asked leave to kiss his foot; she then "asked the Sovereign Pontiff for remission of all her sins." His answer: "Would to God that I had no more need of forgiveness" [S1 p.66]; he gave her "perfect absolution" and "a most ample blessing." The Legend does not say she asked him for the Rule. The Latin of this passage was not read.
- Her words to her sisters afterwards: "that heaven and earth would not suffice to repay it. This day... I have merited to receive the Most High Himself and to behold His Vicar" [S1 p.66].
- The seal: Innocent IV's own-hand notes on the bull: "Ad instar fiat. S." and "Ex causis manifestis michi et protectori mon[asterii?] fiat ad instar" (as Cozza-Luzzi reads them; both said to be in his hand) [S1 App. I p.97]. The bull is dated 9 August 1253; "the Pontiff... wished to hurry the Bull to the dying Saint. After it reached her, Cläre intoned her Nunc dimittis; she died the next day" [S1 pp.97-98].
- Not in the sources: any request from her to the Pope for the Rule; any talk between them beyond the absolution; that she knew of the bull's progress; the room.
- Sources: S1 pp.63-66, 96-98, notes 149-153.

## 6 — The Order today
- Status: full for 1910; the saved sources give no figure for 2026.
- The best saved source: S9 ch. XXXI "Vicissitudes and Growth of the Order" pp.216-221 (de Chérancé, 1910). It says the Order's two branches, the Poor Clares (first Rule) and the Urbanist Poor Clares (the 1263 Constitutions of Urban IV), "remains divided into two branches, both canonically recognized" [p.217]; it was renewed in the fifteenth century by Saint Colette in France and Saint Bernardine in Italy [p.217]; and it states "Of the one hundred thousand nuns France counts to-day, there are from eleven to twelve hundred Poor Clares" [p.221]. It closes with Pope Alexander IV's letter of 1257 to the nuns of San Damiano: "the monastic tree she has planted will be laden with fruits pleasing to the Most High" [S9 pp.222-223].
- S1 shows the Order alive in the Church of 1910: the translator thanks the Abbess of the Franciscan convent at Taunton [note 80], and note 81 names the Poor Clares of the Monastery of St Cläre, Evansville, Indiana (1900) as publishers. S1 App. I p.97: "our present-day Cläres" keep the Rule.
- Use: "today" in a 1910 book means 1910. If the entry says "today", the writer needs a modern source (new research, outside this scope). A safe line from saved sources: her Order kept her Rule's name and spirit through the centuries and had houses in Europe and America by 1910.
- Not in the sources: any modern count of monasteries or sisters.
- Sources: S9 pp.216-223; S1 notes 80, 81, App. I p.97.
