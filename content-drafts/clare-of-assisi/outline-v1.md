# Saint Clare of Assisi — outline (tier B, standard)

Source keys are those of `notes.md`. S4 (the Process) is a modern Spanish translation, facts-only: retell it in our own words, quote only short phrases, marked "(our translation from the Spanish)". S1 pages marked ~ are unchecked.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Clare wanted to follow the poor Christ, as Saint Francis taught her, and to own nothing [S4 W17.3; S1 p.11]. Her noble family stood in the way, then popes who offered her sisters possessions, and at the end her own sick body [S4 W18.2, W19.2; S3 §13; S1 pp.26-27, ~62]. By prayer and by her own refusals she kept that poverty for more than forty years, and a papal confirmation reached her the day before she died [S1 p.64; S4 W3.32, to check].

She is famous for founding the Poor Ladies of San Damiano and keeping their poverty against the offers of popes [S1 pp.17-18, 26-27; S4 W2.22].

Her family wanted to marry her "with great and powerful men"; she would not even listen, and preached contempt of the world to the relative who urged it [S4 W19.2, W18.2]. Popes offered her property, and she asked instead for the right to have none [S1 pp.26-27].

## 2. Portrait

A strong will with a light heart. Her sisters say she was always glad in the Lord and never seen upset (W3.6); the Legend says she seemed to laugh at her penances (S1 p.32). She teased a cat as a "rascal" for dragging her cloth (W9.8). She led by doing: she gave orders "with much fear and humility" and mostly did the task herself (W1.10), covered sleeping sisters in the cold (W2.3), and kissed the foot that struck her (W10.6). In her sickbed she spun linen for fifty pairs of corporals (W9.9).

Her fault was severity to herself: the sisters grieved at her fasts, and Pacifica wept (W1.7), until Saint Francis and the bishop ordered her to eat (W1.8). Late in life she warned Agnes of Prague against "indiscreet and impossible" fasting (S5-569 §40). She wanted martyrdom in Morocco (W6.6), and wept when anyone offended God (W2.10).

Life with God: long night prayer (W1.7); praise God for every tree, person, and creature (W14.9). On her illness: since she knew grace through Saint Francis, "no suffering hath troubled me" (S1 p.~69). To her soul at death: "Blessed be Thou, Lord, who hast created me" (S1 p.~70).

## 3. The opening

Hook: her own words to the Pope.

> Pope Gregory IX wanted to give Saint Clare and her sisters possessions, and she would not take them. If her vow was what held her back, the Pope said, he would release her from it. "Holy Father," Saint Clare answered, "I never want to be released from following Christ, not for all time." She came from a house with seven knights in it, and her family had meant to marry her to great and powerful men. She had left all of it to follow Saint Francis, and she meant to own nothing.

Facts: S3 §13 (Latin; our translation); S1 pp.26-27; S4 W19.1-2. Do not place or date the meeting: the notes give neither.

Thesis: Saint Clare led the sisters of San Damiano for more than forty years [S1 p.18; S4 W1.3], and her Order spread to many cities [S1 pp.21-22; S4 W1.14, W7.11]. She kept the Privilege of Poverty, begun by Pope Innocent III in his own hand, against Pope Gregory IX's offer [S1 pp.26-27]. She taught that poverty in four letters to Agnes of Prague, a king's daughter [S5]. Two years after her death she was declared a saint [S1 canonization chapter]. Chapters 6, 10, and 13 answer it.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** four letters to Agnes of Prague, 1234, 1235-38, 1238, 1253 [S5]; the Rule [S1 App. I; S2 Part II] (ch. 8, 13).
- **What she changed:** the Order of Saint Damian [S2 Part II]; "cities without number" [S1 pp.21-22]; Pacifica to Spello [W1.14], Balbina to Arezzo [W7.11]; the Privilege of Poverty [S1 p.26] (ch. 5, 6, 10, 13).
- **Why famous:** the sworn testimony of 1253 [S4]; tomb cures [S1 pp.76-90]; people ask her for sight, hearing, speech, a sound mind, and their town's protection [S1; S4] (ch. 12, 13).
- **Path to sainthood:** inquiry ordered 18 Oct 1253, held 24-29 Nov 1253 [S4]; canonized at Anagni, 1255, by Alexander IV [S1]; feast 12 August, because 11 August was Saint Rufino's [S1 note 179] (ch. 12, 13).

**Fact needs:**
1. Bull *Clara claris praeclara*: the date (11 Aug or 19 Oct 1255), place, and one line of the Pope's praise. Look: S1 canonization chapter and note 180; S3.
2. The Rule: its poverty chapter in her words, and the bull that approved it (the letter she kissed?). Look: S1 Appendix I, pp.95-125, and notes.
3. The Order's spread: houses named in S1 pp.21-22, and Agnes of Prague's house. Look: S1 pp.21-22; S5-560 context.

## 8. What we leave out

- Oil jar, bread, Brother Stephen, Sister Andrea, the heavy door, the cures: miracles section.
- The Porziuncola meal and fire: legend; S2 says no early writer tells it.
- The nursing vision of Saint Francis (W3.29): teachings or miracles, with care.
- The Holy Thursday trance and the boy at the sermon (W10.8): one line at most.
- Woman of Pisa, Hugolino's son, the foot water: minor or second-hand.
- Testament and Blessing: not read; the story does not need them. Teachings essay, after reading.
- Patron of television, the monstrance, 1240 and 1241, the relics: memory only.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth: "in all probability" 1193, range 1190-1196 [S2 ch.II]. Write "about 1193", never 1194.
- Leaving home: the Legend gives Palm Sunday night [S1 pp.13-14]; Benvenuta of Perugia says Holy Monday [W2.1]. Tell the Legend's account, named in a few words. Give Benvenuta one sentence in "A Note on the Sources". Do not reconcile them.
- 1211 is computed from "forty-two years": write "about 1211" unless S2 states it.
- Sisters: Beatrice testified in Nov 1253 [W12.1]; "sor Inés, hermana de santa Clara" had died by then [W1.15, W6.15]; the Legend's Agnes is at the deathbed [S1 p.67]. The notes point to two sisters. Name each only where her source does. No source says Agnes followed Clare. Agnes of Opportulo and Agnes of Prague are other women.
- Illness 28 years [S1] or 29 [W1.17]; abbess 43 years [W4.17] or 42 at San Damiano: write "nearly thirty" and "more than forty".
- The Legend is "ascribed" to Thomas of Celano and draws on the Process.
- Names: Hortulana (Ortolana), Clara. Always "Saint Clare", "Saint Francis". Witness 3 is unnamed in the notes; W1 is Pacifica.
