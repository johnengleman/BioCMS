# Saint Clare of Assisi — research notes (tier B, standard)

Rule for this file: every fact has a source key and place. Original words are in "quotes" with our English in (parentheses). A line marked `paraphrase` has no quotation marks. Nothing here comes from memory. What I know but did not find is under "Other: to check".

Limit note: I used about 50 tool calls. I did not read the Rule (English in S1 Appendix I), the Testament, the Blessing, or the bull of canonization. See "Not read" at the end.

## 1. Identity

- Name forms: Clare (English). "Clara" (Latin, Process and Legend). "Chiara" (Italian, not in my sources; to check). The OCR of S1 writes "Cläre". Mother: "Hortulana" (S3 AS Vita §1 Latin; S4 W1.4) = "Ortolana" (S1 ch.I). Father "messer Favarone" [S4 W1.4]. Grandfather "messer Offreduccio de Bernardino" [S4 W16.1, W20.2].
- Born at Assisi, Umbria. Year: "in all probability born in 1193" [S2 Part I ch.II, pp.11-13, contents line and p.~13 text]. Gilliat-Smith: she "can hardly have been born earlier than 1190 or later than 1196" [S2 ch.II, end]. The task gave 1194. No source I read gives 1194. Treat the birth year as uncertain.
- Died at San Damiano, Assisi, the 11th of August 1253 [S1 Legend ch.I last chapters: "on the day after that of Blessed Laurence"; S1 note 179 "11 August, the day on which St Cläre died"; S2 contents ch.II "Saint Clare died on the nth of August, 1253"].
- Canonized at Anagni, 1255, by Pope Alexander IV [S1 Legend, "Of the Canonization", and note 180: "on 11 August, 1255" per the Legend's author; bull Clara claris praeclara dated 19 October 1255 per Sbaralea]. See conflict in section 9.
- Tradition: Catholic only (after 1054, Franciscan Second Order).
- Feast: 12 August. Reason: 11 August was the feast of "S. Rufino, the Patron of Assisi", so her feast went to the next day [S1 note 179]. Feast in other calendars: to check.
- Order: the Poor Ladies / Order of Saint Damian (Poor Clares). "Ordo Sancti Damiani" form: [S2 Part II title, "Order of Saint Damian"].
- Research tier: B.

## 3. Timeline

Format: date, event, detail, source.

- c.1193: birth, Assisi. Before delivery her mother prays before the crucifix and hears a voice: "Ne paveas mulier: quia quoddam lumen salva parturies, quod ipsum mundum clarius illustrabit" (Fear not, woman: you will bring forth a light that will light the world more clearly). Named Clara for it. [S3 Vita §1; S1 ch.I, p.7; S4 W3.28 and W6.12 (Cecilia: heard from the mother)]. NEW as a scene of testimony; W6.12 says Cecilia heard it from Clare's mother.
- Childhood, Assisi: she counts prayers with "a heap of pebbles" for lack of beads [S1 ch.I p.9]. She sends her own food to the poor [S1 p.8; S4 W17.1 Bona, W20.3 Giovanni Ventura]. Under rich dress she wears "a little hair shirt" [S1 p.9]; a man-at-arms of her house says she wore a white rough cloth ("estameña") under her clothes [S4 W20.4]. NEW.
- Before 1211 (age about 17-18): family wants a rich marriage; she refuses. Ranieri di Bernardo: "ella jamás accedió" (she never agreed); he himself urged her and "ella no quería ni oírle; antes bien, ella le predicaba a él el desprecio del mundo" (she would not even hear him; she preached contempt of the world to him) [S4 W18.2]. Pietro di Damiano, a neighbour: she was about 17, family wanted "magníficamente, con hombres grandes y poderosos" [S4 W19.2]. House: "siete caballeros" in her family [S4 W19.1].
- Secret visits to Francis: Bona di Guelfuccio went with her "secretamente para no ser vista por los parientes" [S4 W17.3]; Francis and Brother Philip urged her "que se convirtiera a Jesucristo". Legend: "to her his words seemed a flame" [S1 ch.I p.11, "ei verba velut ignis"; Latin not located]. Pacifica adds that Clare and she were neighbours with only the square between the houses [S4 W1.2].
- Pacifica and Clare's mother Hortulana travelled overseas, and to Saint Michael (Monte Gargano) and Rome, as pilgrims [S4 W1.4].
- Night of Palm Sunday, 1211 (date by Gilliat-Smith: "She renounced the world... on the night of Palm Sunday" [S2 contents ch.II]; Legend: Palm Sunday, she stays in her place and the bishop brings her the palm [S1 ch.I p.14]). She leaves by a second door "walled up by a mass of beams and stones" [S1 p.14; Latin not located]. Witness: Cristiana di Bernardo da Suppo lived in the house: door "trancada con unos troncos pesados y con una columna de piedra" (barred with heavy logs and a stone column) and "ella sola, con el auxilio de Jesucristo, los apartó" [S4 W13.1]. She was 18 "según se decía" [S4 W13.1]. Conflict: Benvenuta of Perugia says Clare "ingresó el lunes santo" (Holy Monday) [S4 W2.1]. See section 9.
- Same night: Porziuncola. Friars with torches receive her at the little altar. Her hair is cut [S1 p.14; S4 W12.4 Beatrice, her sister: "san Francisco la tonsuró ante el altar, en la iglesia de la Virgen María, llamada de la Porciúncula"]. Bona di Guelfuccio was in Rome that Lent, so only heard it [S4 W17.5].
- Days after: San Paolo (Bastia) Benedictine nuns. Relatives come to take her. She "agarró los manteles del altar y se descubrió la cabeza, mostrándola rapada" [S4 W12.4]; Legend: "laying hold of the altar cloths and baring her tonsured head" [S1 p.16]. Three witnesses give this (W12, W18.3, W20.6).
- Then Sant'Angelo di Panzo, "poco tiempo", then San Damiano [S4 W12.5; S1 p.17]. Francis, Brother Philip and Brother Bernard took her to Panzo [S4 W12.5].
- Three years later (c.1214): accepts the title of abbess "a ruegos e instancias de san Francisco, que casi la obligó" [S4 W1.6; S1 p.23]. She had refused it for three years.
- Her sister Agnes follows. Agnes (Beatrice in S4) is witness 12 and "hermana carnal" [S4 W12.1]. Name conflict: S4 calls the sister "Beatriz" as witness, and "sor Inés, hermana de santa Clara" elsewhere [S4 W1.15, W6.15]. See section 9.
- Hortulana enters the same Order and dies there [S4 W1.5; S1 ch.I "Of Divine..." p.~53].
- Years at San Damiano: privilege of poverty from Innocent III, who wrote the first lines himself [S1 p.26]. Gregory IX offers possessions; Clare refuses [S1 p.26-27; S3 §13; S4 W1.13, W2.22, W3.14].
- Siege of Vitalis of Aversa (imperial captain), time not given in sources [S3 §23; S1 p.38; S4 W3.19, W9.3].
- Saracens in the cloister, also undated; one witness says September, a Friday, about the hour of Terce [S4 W9.2].
- 1253, Gloriosus Deus bull: Innocent IV gives Bishop Bartholomew of Spoleto the task of the inquiry, 18 October 1253 (S4 §6 "quince días antes de las calendas de noviembre... año onceno"; S1 note 178 gives the same bull Gloriosus Deus dated 18 October 1253).
- Last illness: Clare was ill 28 years (Legend: "twenty-eight years of continual illness" [S1 p.~62]; Pacifica: "hacía veintinueve años" [S4 W1.17]). Total in San Damiano: "forty-two years" [S1 p.18; S4 W1.3, W2.2, W12.4]. Notice: 42 years from 1211 gives 1253.
- Summer 1253: Pope Innocent IV and the Curia move from Lyons to Perugia and then to Assisi. Cardinal of Ostia visits first [S1 p.~64-65]. The Pope comes to her bed, she kisses his foot [S1 p.66; S4 W3.24].
- Death week: Friday evening to Saturday vigil, the "corte celestial" vision of Benvenuta di Diambra [S4 W11.3-4]. Monday (11 August), Clare dies [S4 W11.4: "murió luego, el lunes siguiente"].
- 12 August 1253: funeral. Pope would have recited the Office of Virgins instead of the Office of the Dead; the Cardinal of Ostia says to do the usual. Body taken to San Giorgio, where Francis had first been laid [S1 p.~72-73].
- 24 to 29 November 1253: Bishop Bartholomew questions 20 witnesses at San Damiano and San Paolo/St Paul in Assisi [S4 introduction §7, W15, W16, W20].
- 1255: canonization at Anagni [S1 "Of the Canonization"].

## 4. Character

- Humour and warmth: "Bandido, tú no lo sabes traer. ¿Por qué lo arrastras por el suelo?" (Rascal, you do not know how to carry it. Why do you drag it on the floor?) said to a cat that pulled her cloth; the cat then rolled up the cloth. Told by Francisca, who had it from Clare herself [S4 W9.8]. NEW; this is the only humorous moment I found.
- Joy under hardship: "siempre estaba alegre en el Señor, y nunca se la veía alterada" [S4 W3.6]; Legend: she seemed "either not to feel bodily austerities or to laugh at them" [S1 p.32].
- Fault shown by witnesses: severity on herself. Sisters "se dolían y se lamentaban" at her night prayers and fasts; Pacifica "había llorado alguna vez por este motivo" [S4 W1.7]. Francis and the bishop ordered her to eat half a roll (about an ounce and a half) on her three fast days [S4 W1.8; S1 p.32]. Her hog-skin shirt and horsehair shirt: [S1 p.30; S4 W2.5].
- Gilliat-Smith: Clare herself was "the cause of the severity" in the Ugolino Rule, and she "ruined her health by her excessive austerities, and afterwards counselled prudence" [S2 contents Part II ch.III]. Letter 3 does counsel prudence: "a quadam indiscreta et impossibili abstinentiae austeritate" (from a certain indiscreet and impossible severity of fasting) [S5-569 (40)].
- Gentleness with sisters: "mandaba con mucho temor y humildad, y la mayor parte de las veces prefería hacerlo ella que mandarlo" [S4 W1.10]. In the cold she covered sleeping sisters [S4 W2.3; S1 p.~61].
- Humility, foot-washing: sister strikes Clare's mouth with her foot, Clare kisses the sole. Three witnesses tell it: W2.3, W3.9, W10.6; Legend [S1 p.24; S3 §11].
- Courage: the stand before the Pope, "Sancte pater, ... nequaquam a Christi sequela in perpetuum absolvi desidero" [S3 §13].
- Fear and weakness shared: she wept when others were attacked by the world: "si alguna vez acaecía que alguna persona mundana había hecho algo contra Dios, ella, maravillosamente, lloraba" [S4 W2.10].
- Willingness for martyrdom: when she heard Friars were martyred in Morocco she said she wanted to go; Cecilia wept [S4 W6.6; Balbina W7.2].
- Never idle: she spun linen even on her sickbed, and made corporals sent to churches around Assisi: Francisca counted "cincuenta pares" [S4 W1.11, W2.12, W6.14, W9.9].
- Admirers: the sisters, the Pope, the townspeople. Her witnesses say she was holy before she entered religion [S4 W2.2, W3.2, W4.2].
- Critics: none in the Process. The 1914 scholar attacks later biographers' fables (S2 ch.I, contents). Details to check: Rule disputes with the Friars [S2 Part I ch.VII-IX].

## 5. Quotations (20 usable; items 10 and 13 removed)

Language note: S4 is Spanish from a Latin or Umbrian original, so each Process quotation below is a Spanish copy. The Latin or Umbrian is not read. Our English is our own.

1. Her inner voice before death. Latin: "Vade secura..." not found in S3 OCR (the death chapter OCR is lost). English in S1: "Go forth without fear. For thou hast a good guide for thy journey. Go forth, for He who created thee hath sanctified thee and, protecting thee always, loveth thee with a love as tender as that of a mother for her son. Blessed be Thou, Lord, who hast created me." [S1 Legend, "Of her Last End", p.~70]. Process version, Spanish: "Vete en paz, pues tendrás buena escolta; porque el que te creó previó antes que serías santificada; y, después que te creó, infundió en ti el Espíritu Santo; y luego te ha cuidado como la madre a su hijo pequeñito" [S4 W11.3] and "Vete segura en paz... ¡Bendito seas Tú, Señor, porque me has creado!" [S4 W3.20]. When Anastasia asks to whom she speaks: "Hablo a mi alma bendita" [S4 W11.3] / "Hablo a mi alma" [S4 W3.22]. (My translation: I am speaking to my blessed soul.) Rights: S1 public-domain; S4 facts-only.
2. To the dying Pope's visit: "Hijitas mías, alabad a Dios, porque el cielo y la tierra no son bastantes para tantos beneficios como yo he recibido de Dios, pues le he recibido hoy en el santo Sacramento y he visto también a su Vicario" [S4 W3.24]. S1 p.67 agrees: "heaven and earth would not suffice to repay it".
3. To Brother Rainaldo, who urged patience: "Dearest Brother, ever since I have known the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ through His servant Francis, no suffering hath troubled me, no penance hath been hard, no illness arduous." [S1 p.~69]. Latin not located.
4. To Pope Gregory: "Si votum formidas, nos te a voto absolvimus" (If you fear your vow, we release you from it) and she: "Sancte pater, nequaquam a Christi sequela in perpetuum absolvi desidero" (Holy Father, I never wish to be released from following Christ) [S3 §13; S1 p.26-27].
5. On the night of Christmas, alone and ill: "Domine Deus, ecce sola relicta sum tibi in loco isto" (Lord God, here I am left alone with you in this place). Next morning: "Benedictus Dominus Jesus Christus, qui me, vobis relinquentibus, non reliquit. Audivi revera per gratiam Christi tota illa solemnia, quae in ecclesia Sancti Francisci hac nocte celebrata sunt" [S3 §29; S1 p.50; S4 W3.30, W4.16, W7.9].

Guard them, Lord, I ask, for I cannot guard them now) [S3 §22; S1 p.37]. Voice: "Ego vos semper custodiam" (I will always guard you). Then "Fidejubeo vobis, filiolae, quod nihil patiemini mali; tantum in Christo confidite" (I stand surety for you, little daughters, that you will suffer no evil; only trust in Christ) [S3 §22].
7. Before the Saracens, in the Process: "Hermanas e hijitas mías, no tengáis miedo... Y yo quiero seros fiadora de que no nos harán ningún mal; si vienen, ponedme delante de ellos" [S4 W3.18; W4.14]. (Sisters, little daughters, do not be afraid... I will be your guarantor... if they come, put me in front of them.)
8. At the refectory door, with the Sacrament before her (Francisca's testimony): "Señor, guarda Tú a estas siervas tuyas, pues yo no las puedo guardar" [S4 W9.2]. Heard answer: "¡Yo te defenderé siempre!"; for the city: "La ciudad sufrirá muchos peligros, pero será protegida" [S4 W9.2]. Francisca, who held Clare, heard it. Clare ordered silence "mientras ella viviese" [S4 W9.2].
9. At the siege, to the sisters: "Muchos bienes hemos recibido de esta ciudad, y por ello debemos rogar a Dios que la guarde" [S4 W9.3]. She asked for ashes, uncovered her head, put ashes on her head and on each sister's head [S4 W3.19, W9.3].

## 6. Scenes (20)

Page marks with ~ in this file are approximate: the S1 OCR page numbers were not checked line by line. Confirm before use.

Each: place, people, act, source and place. `testimony` = told at length by a witness.

1. The voice in the church. Hortulana prays before the crucifix; hears "Ne paveas mulier..." [S3 §1; S1 p.7; S4 W3.28]. testimony (Clare told it to the sisters; Cecilia heard it from Hortulana, W6.12).
2. The pebbles. Child Clare counts her prayers on a heap of pebbles [S1 p.9]. Single source, a Legend detail.
3. The refused suitor. Ranieri di Bernardo, relative by marriage, begs her to marry; she preaches contempt of the world back to him [S4 W18.2]. testimony.
4. Secret visits. Bona di Guelfuccio walks with her to Francis, so the family will not see [S4 W17.3]. testimony.
5. Palm Sunday. She stays in her place; the bishop descends and puts the palm in her hands [S1 p.14]. Legend only.
6. The walled door. A door barred with beams and a stone column; she opens it alone; the household is amazed in the morning [S4 W13.1 (witness lived in the house); S1 p.14]. testimony.
7. The altar cloth. At San Paolo, relatives pull; she grips the altar cloths and shows her shaved head [S4 W12.4, W18.3, W20.6; S1 p.16]. testimony (her sister, Beatrice, W12).
8. The unwilling abbess. Francis "casi la obligó" (almost forced her) to take the office after three years [S4 W1.6; S1 p.23].
9. The pig-skin shirt and the horsehair shirt lent for three days. Sister Agnes of Opportulo tried it and could not bear it [S4 W10.1]; Legend [S1 p.30-31]. testimony.
10. The cold-night blankets, and the foot-kiss: sister kicks her mouth; Clare kisses the sole [S4 W2.3, W3.9, W10.6 (Lent, a Thursday); S1 p.24].
11. The oil jar. She washes the jar herself, sets it on the wall by the door; Brother Bentevenga finds it full [S4 W1.15, W2.14, W3.26; S1 p.29; S3 §15]. testimony (Pacifica: "estando ella en casa, había visto que la madonna sacó la vasija vacía y la metió llena").
12. Fifty slices from half a loaf. Sister Cecilia: "sería necesario aquel milagro del Señor de los cinco panes" [S4 W6.16; S1 p.28; S3 §14].
13. Brother Stephen, the madman, sent by Francis, sleeps in her place of prayer and wakes sane [S4 W2.15, W3.12; S1 p.~51].
14. Sister Andrea's throat. Clare knows by revelation that a sister has squeezed her own throat in the night; she wakes Philippa: "Baja pronto al dormitorio... prepara un huevo pasado por agua" [S4 W3.16]. testimony.
15. The Saracens. Clare ill, carried to the refectory door with the Sacrament in an ivory-and-silver box; prays; hears the voice [S3 §22; S1 p.36-37; S4 W9.2 (Francisca held her, tells the Friday, Terce, September), W2.20, W3.18]. testimony.
16. Vitalis of Aversa and the ashes. Clare puts ashes on her shaved head and on each sister; the army leaves at night [S3 §23; S1 p.38; S4 W3.19, W9.3]. testimony.
17. The Christmas hearing. Alone in the sickroom, she hears the Friars' Matins at San Francesco and sees the crib [S3 §29; S1 p.50; S4 W3.30; W4.16 adds "el pesebre"; W7.9]. testimony.
18. The Holy Thursday trance. She sits on her bed from Thursday evening through Friday night; a sister brings a candle; Clare: "What need is there of a candle? Is it not day?" [S1 p.51-52; S4 W3.25].
19. Brother Philip's sermon and the child. Agnes of Opportulo sees a beautiful boy beside Clare during Brother Philip's preaching; a red light then surrounds her [S4 W10.8; S1 p.59 shorter]. testimony (a private vision, attributed; keep per the private-moments rule).
20. Death. Pope Innocent at her bed, she kisses his foot; days later the vision of the white-robed virgins and the tall one with the crown (Benvenuta di Diambra); the Pope wants the Office of Virgins at her funeral [S4 W3.24, W11.3-4; S1 p.65-73]. testimony.

Further scenes from the Process, held in reserve: the heavy door that fell on her at the monastery and she said it felt like a light cloak (W5.5, W14.6, W15.2); the visit of the woman of Pisa freed from five demons (W4.20, W7.14); Hugolino and his wife Guiduzia, a prophecy from Clare (W16.4); the sister who drank the water that washed Clare's feet and found it sweet, and Clare threw out the rest (W10.11); Francis's nursing vision, the "tetilla" dream (W3.29: strong imagery; handle with care).

## 7. Miracles

Statuses: R = recorded in the Process (sworn, 1253); L = recorded in the Legend; P = popular. Locators as above. None is approved as a canonization miracle by a document I read; the bull was not read.

During life (in the Process, named sisters):
- Sister Benvenuta (Bienvenida) di Diambra, fistulas under the arm, 11-12 years: cured by the sign of the cross at night [W3.10 (12 years), W4.8 (11 years), W11.1; L: S1 p.~54; reporter: herself, W11; witnesses W1, W3, W4, W12, W13, W14, W15]. R.
- Sister Amata, dropsy and fever, 13 months [W3.11, W4.7; L S1 p.~54]. R.

## 8. Legacy

- Founded the Poor Ladies at San Damiano; she lived there 42 years [S1 p.17-18; S4 W1.3]. The Order spread: "Cities without number were adorned with monasteries" [S1 p.21-22]; Pacifica at Vallegloria (Spello) "para formar a las hermanas de dicho lugar" for a year [S4 W1.14]; Balbina at Arezzo for one year and five months [S4 W7.11]. Balbina was abbess of Vallegloria [S4 W1.15].
- The Privilege of Poverty: Clare asked Innocent III; Pope wrote the first lines [S1 p.26]. Later Gregory IX tried to make her accept property; she refused [S1 p.26-27; S4 W2.22]. Gilliat-Smith notes Gregory's letter of 9 May 1238 to Agnes of Prague on the Primitive Rule [S2 Part II ch.I, contents]. Her last request was the confirmation of the privilege [S1 p.64]; Pacifica says a Friar brought the sealed letters and she kissed the bull the day before she died [S4 W3.32]. NEW: the bull kissed a day before death.
- The Rule: "the Rule and Life of the Poor Sisters" in S1 Appendix I (not read, pp.95-125 of the book). Gilliat-Smith: three rules in her lifetime (Primitive Rule, Ugolino Rule, her own) [S2 Part II contents].
- Letters to Agnes of Prague: 1234, 1235-38, 1238, 1253 [S5]. Agnes was daughter of the king of Bohemia and had refused Frederick II (S5-560 context).
- After death: canonization by Alexander IV at Anagni, 1255 [S1 canonization chapter]; first feast 12 August [S1 note 179]. Process held 24-29 Nov 1253 [S4].
- Famous for: poverty kept against two popes, deliverance of Assisi from the imperial army, the mirror letters, and healing by the sign of the cross. People asked her for: sight/ears (cures in the Process and Legend: eyes, ears, speech), mental illness, and protection of the town [S1; S4]. Patronage today: to check (I did not find it).

## 9. Other: conflicts, to check, image candidates

Conflicts between sources:
- Birth year: 1193 in S2 (probable), 1194 asked by caller; the Process gives only that she was "unos dieciocho años" when she left (W2.2, W13.1, W17.4; "dieciséis" for Pietro di Damiano: "unos diecisiete años", W19.2), about 42 years before 1253 (W1.3, W12.4, W13.1, W17.3). Subtracting 42 or so gives c.1211 and an age of 18 gives c.1193.
- Day of departure: Legend and Gilliat-Smith: Palm Sunday night [S1 p.13-14; S2 ch.II]. Benvenuta of Perugia: Clare entered on "lunes santo" (Holy Monday) and she in September [S4 W2.1]. The two may mean the same night; do not merge.
- Place of tonsure: Beatrice and Hugolino say Porziuncola [S4 W12.4, W16.6]; Giovanni Ventura says "Santa María de la Porciúncula, o en la de San Pablo" [S4 W20.6]. The Legend says Porziuncola [S1 p.14].
- Sister's name: the Process calls the carnal sister "Beatriz" (W12). The Legend calls her "Agnes" (S1 p.67 and p.~73). W1.15, W6.15 call the sister who recently died "Inés, hermana de santa Clara". A Spanish witness list calls W10 "Inés de Opórtulo"; she is a different woman. Possible two names (religious name Agnes, baptismal name Beatrice); not stated in my sources. Do not merge until checked.
- Years ill: 28 (Legend), 29 (Pacifica) [S1 p.62; S4 W1.17].
- Years of rule: "cuarenta y tres años" (W4.17, W6.1) vs 42 in San Damiano. Cecilia adds that she was abbess about 43 years.
- Canonization date: Legend's author: 11 August 1255 at Anagni; Sbaralea: bull 19 October 1255 [S1 note 180]. Bull text: not read.
- Source order: the Process is sworn testimony; the Legend is built on it and on the companions of Francis ("I had recourse to the companions of the Blessed Francis and to the community of the virgins of Christ" [S1 Prefatory Letter p.4]). So the Legend and the Process are not independent.
- Author: the Legend is "ascribed to Fr. Thomas of Celano" [S1 title]. Not certain (S2 calls Celano the author, S1 title says "ascribed").
- Visions in the Process are private testimony (W2.17, W9.4 the child on Clare's lap and wings; W9.10; W10.8; W11.4). Keep them attributed to the witness. Several say she saw it alone.
