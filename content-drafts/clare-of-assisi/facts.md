# Fact sheet: Saint Clare of Assisi (checked 2026-10-06, draft 5 of the biography)

The teachings essay and the miracle list must use these names, dates, numbers, and wordings exactly. "n." means the note number in the current `biography.html` (102 notes). Where a later writer finds a source that disagrees, list the conflict for the checker. The old facts file (slim draft, 105 notes) is replaced by this one; its note numbers no longer apply.

## Names and titles

- **Saint Clare of Assisi.** Latin "Clara", Italian "Chiara". Born Assisi, about 1193 (Gilliat-Smith: "in all probability"; he allows 1190 to 1196). Father: the knight Favarone; grandfather Offreduccio di Bernardino (W16.1, W20.2). Mother: **Blessed Hortulana** (Robinson writes "Ortolana"; the Process and the Latin write "Hortulana"; use "Hortulana"). Hortulana entered San Damiano as a widow (Robinson note 137; Legend p. 54) and died there; the date is unknown (Wadding), Jacobilli gives 2 January 1253.
- **Saint Agnes** = Saint Clare's younger sister, "Saint Agnes of Assisi" (Robinson note 126; Gilliat-Smith). Born about 1197 to 1198 (Robinson note 124). In the Process (witness 12) the sister who testifies is "Beatrice", a different sister; the Process also calls the sister who died shortly before 24 November 1253 "sor Inés, hermana de santa Clara" (W1.15, W6.15). Never merge Saint Agnes and Beatrice.
- **Saint Agnes of Prague** (canonized 12 November 1989). Older sources call her "Blessed". Never confuse her with Saint Agnes of Assisi.
- **Sister Agnes of Opportulo** (witness 10; the Spanish text writes "Inés"): a different woman, daughter of Opportulo di Bernardo of Assisi. Plain, no title.
- **Sister Benvenuta di Diambra** (witness 11): the sores healed by the sign of the cross; the vision of the white-robed virgins on Friday evening before the death. **Sister Benvenuta of Perugia** (witness 2): lost her voice for almost two years; entered religion in September of the same year as Saint Clare; says Saint Clare entered on Holy Monday. Two women. Always give the second name when either is named.
- **Amada** (witness 4, "Amata" in Robinson): Saint Clare's niece (W4.1). She is the sister who was alone with Saint Clare on the Friday before her death and was asked "Do you see the King of glory..." (W4.19). Plain, no title.
- Left plain, because no source read settles the title: Pacifica (Pacifica di Guelfuccio), Beatrice, Amada, Balbina, Felipa, Cecilia, Francisca, Cristiana (two women: witness 5, Cristiana di Cristiano of Paris, deaf in one ear; witness 13, Cristiana di Bernardo of Suppo, who lived in the house of the barred door), Angeluccia, Illuminata of Pisa, Bona di Guelfuccio, Ranieri di Bernardo, Pietro di Damiano, Juan Ventura (Giovanni), Brothers Philip, Bernard, Leo, Angelo, Juniper, Stephen. For Leo and Angelo, Wikipedia says "Blessed" but cites no decree. Juniper is "Servant of God" in Franciscan Media.
- Popes and others, no title: Pope Innocent III, Pope Gregory IX (Cardinal Hugolino, bishop of Ostia, before 1227), Pope Innocent IV, Pope Alexander IV (Cardinal Rainaldo, bishop of Ostia, protector of the Order, before 1254), Bishop Bartholomew of Spoleto (Accorombani), Emperor Frederick II, Vitalis of Aversa.
- Saints named with a title: Saint Francis, Saint Rufino (patron of Assisi), Saint Lawrence (feast 10 August), Saint Michael (shrine at Monte Gargano), Saint Agnes, Saint Agnes of Prague.
- Places: San Damiano, Assisi; the Porziuncola (Saint Mary of the Porziuncola); San Paolo (Benedictine nuns, near Bastia); Sant'Angelo di Panzo; San Giorgio; Santa Chiara; San Francesco (about two kilometers from San Damiano, Robinson note 131); Monticello (Robinson) = Monticelli (Chérancé), near Florence; Anagni; Spoleto; Prague.

## Dates and numbers

- Flight from home: the night after Palm Sunday, about 1211 (Gilliat-Smith); Robinson places it in 1212. Say "about 1211". Age "about eighteen" (W2.2, W13.1, W17.4; W19.2 says about seventeen when the marriage was pressed). The Process: Benvenuta of Perugia says "lunes santo" (Holy Monday).
- On Palm Sunday she went to the (cathedral) church; the bishop came down the steps and gave her a palm (Legend p. 14; Gilliat-Smith pp. 51, 53 write "cathedral"). The Latin says only "Ecclesiam".
- Saint Agnes joined Saint Clare sixteen days later (Legend p. 41), at Panzo; twelve men of the family came "the next day" (p. 41).
- She lived at San Damiano forty-two years (Legend p. 18; W1.3, W2.2, W12.5, W13.1, W17.3). Accepted the office of abbess after three years (W1.6; Legend p. 23). Witnesses also say she governed "almost forty-three years" (W4.17, W6.1).
- Illness: 28 years (Legend p. 62), 29 years (Pacifica, W1.17). Say "nearly thirty years of illness". Never "years in bed".
- Saint Agnes sent to Monticello about 1219 (Robinson note 154; Chérancé pp. 81-82). Her letter to Saint Clare is undated (Wadding: 1221). She came home early in 1253: "more than thirty years" (Chérancé p. 87). Never "thirty years" alone.
- Privilege of poverty: Innocent III wrote its first lines himself (Legend p. 26; Robinson doubts the printed text, note 114); Gregory IX's privilege *Sicut manifestum est*, 17 September 1228, "Datum Perusii" (Robinson notes 116, 185). Gregory IX's *Quo elongati*, 28 September 1230 (note 146). No source puts the name "privilege of poverty" in Saint Clare's own mouth: the Legend and the Process use it.
- Letters to Agnes of Prague: first 1234; second 1235 to 1238; third 1238; fourth 1253, on her deathbed (Mueller). Do not give a year for the founding of the convent in Prague (Robinson note 110 says 1236; Mueller says Agnes entered in 1234).
- Cardinal Rainaldo's visit: 8 September 1252; Rule drawn up 16 September 1252 (Robinson notes 151, 189). Christmas night 1252: she hears the friars' office at San Francesco and sees the manger.
- **Pope Innocent IV** came to Assisi in late April 1253 (27 April to 1 May, for the consecration of San Francesco; Robinson note 152) and visited her twice (note 153): first a few days after he arrived, and again about two days before her death. **The hand, the foot, the pardon, and Communion ("That same day she received Communion") are the second, August visit**: Process W3.24 says "pocos días antes" of her death, and the Legend (pp. 65-66) tells the pardon and the Communion on one day. Bull *Solet annuere*, 9 August 1253, two days before her death. She kissed the bull. Felipa: "the next day" she died (W3.32).
- Death: Monday 11 August 1253 (toward dawn, Robinson note 159), the day after the feast of Saint Lawrence, at San Damiano. The vision of Benvenuta di Diambra was the Friday evening before (W11.4); she spoke to her soul "between Friday and Saturday" (W11.3; W3.20). Funeral the next day (12 August), body to San Giorgio.
- Saint Agnes died 16 November 1253, ninety-seven days after her sister (Robinson note 164).
- Bull *Gloriosus Deus*, 18 October 1253. Hearings: 24 November at San Damiano (the sisters); 28 November (the community and Hugolino, Bona, Ranieri, Pietro at San Paolo); 29 November (Juan Ventura at San Paolo). Twenty witnesses.
- Canonization: Anagni, 1255, by Alexander IV, near the second anniversary of the death (Legend); copies of the bull *Clara claris praeclara* carry later dates in 1255 (Robinson note 180: 19 October).
- **Feast day: 11 August today (General Roman Calendar; memorial). First set for 12 August in 1255, because 11 August belonged to Saint Rufino (Robinson note 179).** `entry.json` holds 11 August.
- Spread of the Order: Saint Agnes to Monticello about 1219; Pacifica a year at Spello; Balbina a year and five months at Arezzo; Innocent IV in 1253: "almost every region of the world" (Process, bull of 18 October 1253, §3).
- Bull found at Santa Chiara in the spring of 1893, wrapped in an old habit of the Saint (Robinson Intro p. xviii; Gilliat-Smith pp. 116, 274: "believed to have worn"), now in a reliquary in the nuns' choir (Robinson App. I pp. 97 to 98). Procession on 22 June (Robinson note 123).
- Saint Francis died the evening of 4 October 1226 (Gilliat-Smith p. 74); de Chérancé says 3 October. His body lay at San Giorgio from 5 October 1226 (Robinson note 163). The funeral procession stopped at San Damiano (Celano First Life §§116 to 117; Mirror pp. 162 to 163).
- Counts: seven knights in her family's house (W19.1); twelve men at Panzo; fifty slices from half a loaf (W6.16); fifty pairs of corporals (W9.9; the Legend says "more than fifty"); Benvenuta di Diambra's sores twelve years (W11.1; W3.10), healed two years before (W11.1, "she believed"); a Friday in September, about the hour of Terce, for the Saracens (W9.2, Francisca's best memory; the sources give no year). Eleven months from Cardinal Rainaldo's visit (8 September 1252) to the bull reaching her (10 to 11 August 1253). Seventeen days without food (Legend p. 68).
- "How This Life Was Researched": eleven sources in the Sources list (the four letters count as four), three languages, twenty witnesses, four letters.

## Approved wording of quotations (plain modern English; our translation)

1. Gregory IX: "If you fear your vow, we release you from it." (n. 47). Saint Clare: "Holy Father, in no way do I ever want to be released from following Christ." (n. 48; Latin "nequaquam a Christi sequela in perpetuum absolvi desidero"; Legend, Acta Sanctorum §14; Robinson pp. 26 to 27). Never "not for all time".
2. The voice to Hortulana: "Do not be afraid, woman. You will give birth safely to a light that will shine very brightly on the world." (n. 4; Latin "clarius illustrabit", read as "very brightly", which agrees with the Process: "iluminará mucho", W3.28, W6.12). Never "more clearly".
3. Legend on Saint Francis (n. 8): "His words seemed to her like fire, and his deeds seemed more than human." (Latin "flammantia", not "velut ignis"; Robinson p. 11 "a flame".)
4. The voice from the cross at San Damiano (n. 23): "Francis, go and repair my house. As you see, it is completely destroyed."
5. Saint Francis on the wall of San Damiano, in French (n. 24; Testament §§40 to 41): "Come and help me build the monastery of San Damiano. Ladies will live here one day, and through their renowned and holy way of life our Father in heaven will be glorified in his whole Church."
6. Saint Francis to the sisters, Rule ch. VI (n. 33, n. 39): "I will and I promise, for myself and for my friars, always to take special care of you, as I do of them." Saint Clare: "He kept this promise faithfully as long as he lived." And: "I beg you all, my ladies, and I advise you, to live always in this highest life and poverty. … Watch yourselves well, so that you never leave it in any way because of anyone's teaching or advice."
7. Rule ch. VI, Saint Clare on herself and her first sisters (n. 15): they "feared no poverty, work, sorrow, shame or contempt from the world"; "We held them as great delights". Rule ch. VI (n. 13): the Father "to light up my heart by his grace".
8. Rule ch. VIII (n. 69): "The sisters shall not take anything as their own, neither a house nor a place nor anything." (The longer sentence about "that summit of the highest poverty" is no longer in the biography.)
9. "Who gave you these whole loaves?" (n. 50; W3.13). The friars (n. 51; Legend p. 60; Latin §37): "Let him take all the friars from us now, since he has taken away the ones who gave us the food of the soul."
10. Saracens: "If they come, put me in front of them." (n. 53; W3.18, W4.14). Prayer at the dining hall door (n. 55): "Does it please you, my Lord, to hand over to the pagans your defenseless servants, whom I have fed with your love? Lord, I beg you, protect these servants of yours. I cannot protect them now." The voice (n. 56): "I will always protect you." About the city: "My Lord, if it pleases you, protect this city as well, for it supports us out of love for you." Answer: "It will suffer troubles, but my protection will defend it." To the sisters (n. 56; W9.2): "Do not be afraid. I promise you that you will suffer no harm, now or in the future, as long as you obey God's commandments."
11. Vitalis (n. 58; W9.3): "We have received many good things from this city, and so we must pray to God to protect it."
12. Letters (our translation of the Latin): "most holy poverty and bodily want" (First Letter §6, n. 59); "O blessed poverty, who gives eternal riches to those who love and embrace her!" (§15); "Truly, it is a great and praiseworthy exchange: to leave the things of time for the things of eternity, to win heaven instead of earth, to receive a hundred for one, and to have a blessed life forever." (First Letter §30, n. 60; also "a great and praiseworthy exchange" n. 10); "One who is clothed cannot wrestle with one who is naked, because the one who has something to be grabbed by is thrown to the ground sooner." (First Letter §27, n. 19; used as a line to Saint Agnes of Prague, not as Saint Clare's own lesson at San Paolo); "What you hold, may you continue to hold. … What you do, may you go on doing and never stop." (Second Letter §11) and "Poor virgin, embrace the poor Christ." (§18, n. 61); "I can truly rejoice, and no one could ever take such joy from me, for I already hold what I longed for under heaven." and "Place your mind in the mirror of eternity" (Third Letter §§5 to 6, 12, n. 62); "But our flesh is not bronze, and our strength is not the strength of stone. No, we are frail and open to every bodily weakness. So I ask you, dearest, and I beg you in the Lord, to draw back wisely and with discretion from the indiscreet and impossible strictness in fasting that I know you have taken up." (Third Letter §§38 to 40, n. 63); "the other half of her soul" (Fourth Letter §1, n. 74); "Look closely, I say, at the beginning of this mirror: the poverty of him who was laid in a manger and wrapped in swaddling clothes. O wonderful humility! O astonishing poverty! The King of angels, the Lord of heaven and earth, is laid in a manger!" (Fourth Letter §§19 to 21, n. 75); "Remember your poor little mother." (§33), "the most prudent virgin, Agnes, our sister" (§38), "until we meet at the throne of glory of the great God" (§39) (n. 76).
13. The cat (n. 66; W9.8): "Rascal, you don't know how to carry it. Why are you dragging it on the ground?"
14. The vision at San Paolo (n. 67; Legend p. 63; Gilliat-Smith p. 47): "Do not weep, daughters, for her who will conquer. She cannot die until the Lord comes with his disciples." (Robinson "conquer"; Gilliat-Smith "conquer". The Latin is not in our saved text. Never "win".)
15. Christmas 1252 (n. 71, n. 72): "Lord God, here I am, left alone with you in this place." Next morning: "Blessed be the Lord Jesus Christ, who did not leave me when you left me. By the grace of Christ I really did hear all the services that were sung last night in the church of San Francesco."
16. Innocent IV, second visit (n. 77): "I wish to God I had no more need of forgiveness." Saint Clare after the visit (n. 78): "Praise the Lord, my little daughters. Today Christ has given me a gift so great that heaven and earth could not repay it. Today I have received the Most High himself, and I have seen his Vicar."
17. To Saint Agnes at the deathbed (n. 85; Legend p. 67): "It pleases God that I go, dearest sister. But stop crying. You will come to the Lord soon after me. Before I leave you, the Lord will give you a great comfort."
18. To Brother Rainaldo (n. 83; Legend p. 68): "Dearest brother, ever since I came to know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ through his servant Francis, no suffering has troubled me. No penance has been hard, and no illness has been difficult." The text says only "a friar" (the Legend says Brother Rainaldo).
19. To Amada (n. 86; W4.19): "Do you see the King of glory, whom I am seeing?"
20. To her soul (n. 89; Legend p. 70; W3.20, W11.3): "Go out without fear. You have a good guide for your journey. Go out, for he who made you has made you holy. He has always protected you, and he loves you as tenderly as a mother loves her son. Blessed are you, Lord, for you have made me." Answer to the sister: "I am speaking to my blessed soul." (n. 90). To the sisters (n. 90; W3.21): "You will remember what I am saying now, only as far as the One who makes me say it allows you."
21. Felipa (n. 96; W3.32): "truly Clara", "to the clarity of the eternal light". Agnes of Opportulo (n. 95; W10.10): "Precious in the eyes of the Lord is the death of his saints."
22. Alexander IV, *Clara claris praeclara* (n. 100): "Clare, bright with bright merits, shines brightly: in heaven with the brightness of great glory, and on earth with the splendor of lofty miracles."
23. **The Pope's note on the bull** (n. 91; Robinson App. I p. 97; Gilliat-Smith p. 274, "Ex causis manifestis michi et protectori monasterii fiat ad instar"): "For reasons clear to me and to the protector of the monastery, let it be made in the same form."
24. Note on the back of *Solet annuere* (n. 102): "Blessed Clare touched this and kissed it out of devotion many, many times."
25. Pacifica on the abbess's office (n. 28): Saint Francis "almost forced her"; "with much fear and humility" (W1.10). Teaching on trees and creatures (n. 77 of the old numbering; W14.9, not in the current biography): "always, for all things and in all things".
26. Saint Agnes of Assisi from Monticello (n. 35; Chérancé p. 83, our modernized wording of the English): "Dearest mother, what endless sorrow and sadness fill my body and soul! They weigh on me and torment me in more ways than I can say, and for one reason only: I am separated from you and from my holy sisters, when it seemed to me that we would live happily together until death. This grief does not leave me for a moment. It keeps on growing." She did not know whether she would ever be allowed to return; the Pope had granted that her sisters should have no possessions (Chérancé pp. 84 to 85). Saint Clare sent "a line in her own hand" and her veil (Chérancé p. 85, after Bartholomew of Pisa).
27. Saint Francis's message (n. 41; Mirror pp. 161 to 162): "Go and tell Sister Clare not to grieve or be sad because she cannot see me now. Let her know that she will surely see me before her death, and that she and her sisters will be greatly comforted when they do." Saint Clare feared she would die before him and never again see "her only father after God" (n. 40). At the window (n. 43; Celano §116): "to see the father who would not speak to them again, nor come back to them."
28. Testament (n. 44; §§43 to 44): "our pillar, our only comfort after God, and our support"; Saint Francis tended the sisters "in word and deed" as "his little plant". The Testament's authenticity is questioned; the biography says so in "A Note on the Sources".
29. Young Saint Clare (n. 7, n. 11, n. 13, n. 21, n. 22): she "decided in her heart" to do as Saint Francis did (W20.6); "so that the poor would not be cheated" (W13.11); "longed-for flight" (Legend); "with the help of Jesus Christ" (W13.1); "I thank God, sweetest sister, that he has heard my prayer for you." (Legend p. 41; Latin "exaudivit"); Saint Agnes: "Help me, dearest sister! Do not let them take me from Christ the Lord!" (Legend p. 42); the men: "She must have been eating lead all night". To the Perugia boy (n. 27; W4.11): "Take him to my mother, Sister Hortulana, and let her make the sign of the cross over him."

## Wordings fixed by the check of draft 5 (2026-10-06)

- Under her fine clothes she wore "a coarse white garment, a kind of hair shirt" (W20.4 "estameña [o cilicio] de color blanco"; Legend p. 9 "a little hair shirt"). Never "a cloth".
- "The mayor came with knights and armed men to keep watch all night over her body" (Latin "Accurrit Potestas cum caterva militum"; Legend p. 72). Never "sent".
- "On that second visit he came to her bed": see the Pope Innocent IV line above.
- "Saint Agnes had come home after more than thirty years."
- "Many years later she wrote to Saint Agnes of Prague" the line about the clothed and the naked. It is not tied to what Saint Clare "learned at that altar".
- The Perugia boy's healing, the cat, the seventeen days without food, and the two Benvenutas keep the wordings above.
- Saint Agnes's veil: Saint Clare sent "a line in her own hand" (de Chérancé: "a phrase signed by her hand").
- The bull reached her "near the end": a friar brought the bull to her bed with her daughters around her (W3.32; Legend p. 67).
- Her "fear" at the end (W3.23): she "was afraid she might have failed in some way to keep the faith promised at her baptism" (Spanish "en algo"; not "small").

## Wordings fixed by earlier checks (still in force)

- Juan Ventura (W20.9, n. 99 mentions the last witness only): he saw, "that year" after her death, a man from beyond the mountains carried to her tomb tied with ropes, raging or possessed, and healed there at once; he "thought it was in September" (said at the hearing of 29 November 1253); he did not know the man's name, "pues no era de estas partes"; everyone in the square "saw the man and ran with him to the tomb" ("lo vieron todos los de la plaza, y corrieron junto con él al sepulcro"); the healing happened at the tomb.
- "Her thread was made into fine cloth, and the sisters sewed the cloth into altar linens called corporals" (W1.11, W6.14, Legend p. 46: "linens"). No source says she wove the cloth.
- Saint Agnes's death: "ninety-seven days after Saint Clare" (Robinson note 164).
- Agnes of Prague's convent: no founding year.
- "Neither Pope Gregory nor Cardinal Rainaldo, the bishop of Ostia when they spoke" (W2.22, W3.14). Rainaldo was bishop of Ostia and protector of the Order in 1253 (Gilliat-Smith p. 48; Robinson Appendix I p. 96). Hugolino was bishop of Ostia before he became Pope.
- Francisca (W9.2): she "thought" it was a Friday in September, about Terce ("según creía"). Not "remembered". Saracens, miracle list (checked 2026-10-06): the list tells Sister Francisca's account (W9.2) with her own words: prayer "Lord, guard these servants of yours, for I cannot guard them" ("Señor, guarda Tú a estas siervas tuyas, pues yo no las puedo guardar"); voice "I will always defend you" ("¡Yo te defenderé siempre!"). She prostrated herself on the ground in prayer, and two sisters, Francisca and Illuminata of Pisa, were holding her ("sostenían a la madonna"). The Legend's fuller prayer and "I will always protect you" (item 10 above) stay the wording of the biography.
- Brother Philip is not called "Saint Francis's companion" in the text: the Process (W12.5, W17.3) says only "fray Felipe". Pacifica and Brother Bernard stay plain (no title).
- Opening: "The Pope offered her property, and she asked him instead for the right to own none." is no longer in the biography; the opening now says only that the daughter "wanted to be poor". The refusal of property is Gregory IX's offer (W1.13, W2.22; Legend p. 26) and his letter of 17 September 1228 ("as you have asked").
- The procurator's son (W3.15, W9.6): both witnesses name the father "messer Juan de maese Juan". W3.15 calls him the sisters' procurator. Felipa: the father "told them he had been healed suddenly" ("repentinamente"). Francisca: a five-year-old with fever and scrofula; she heard the father say in the speaking room ("locutorio") that the boy was healed "instantáneamente". The Process does not say whether it is one boy or two sons.
- Cecilia's cough (W4.9, W6.8): Sister Amada tells it; Sister Cecilia testifies that she was healed, "como dijo sor Amada".
