# Fact check: Saint Clare of Assisi (2026-10-06)

Files checked: `biography.html`, `summary.txt`, `entry.json` (saint fields, quotes, share kit). `miracles.html` was not touched. Backups: `biography.html.before-check`, `summary.txt.before-check`, `entry.json.before-check`. Check script: no MUST FIX line, no CHECK line (6,673 words, average sentence 15.0).

Method: all 116 notes were opened against the saved sources (S1 Robinson, S2 Gilliat-Smith, S3 Acta Sanctorum Latin, S4 the Process in Spanish, S5 the four letters). The saved files are complete; none was cut short. The Process file runs from the bull of 18 October 1253 to witness 20. Every block quote was compared with the Latin (S3, S5) or with the Spanish (S4) or, where only Robinson's English exists, with Robinson. The Latin of the death chapters is lost in the S3 text, so notes 91 to 100 rest on Robinson's English and on the Process.

## The writer's extra material (plan.md): all confirmed in the sources

- Saint Agnes joins Saint Clare sixteen days after she left home: S1 p. 41 ("Sixteen days after the conversion of Cläre").
- The twelve men at Panzo, "the next day", Monaldo's arm, the stream, Saint Francis cutting Saint Agnes's hair: S1 pp. 41-43.
- Rule chapter VI (Saint Francis's two writings to the sisters, last wish): S1 pp. 110-111. Chapter VIII ("summit of the highest poverty"): S1 p. 113.
- The bull found in 1893 at Santa Chiara, now in a reliquary in the nuns' choir; the two notes on its top edge; Abbot Cozza-Luzzi's judgment that both are in Innocent IV's hand; the note on the back: S1 pp. 97-98 and S2 p. 274 (Oliger says the same). The Latin note reads "Hanc (?) beata Clara tetigit et obsculata est pro devotione pluribus et pluribus vicibus".
- Robinson notes 116, 122-123, 137, 149, 151, 154, 162-164 and the Process articles listed in plan.md: all say what the paragraphs say.
- Letters: Letter 1 §§3, 5-6, 30; Letter 2 §§17-18 and Mueller's note; Letter 3 §§12-13, 38-40; Letter 4 §§4-6, 14-19, 33-34, 38: confirmed (see fixes 17 to 20 for two corrections).

## Fixes made

1. **Opening quotation, "not for all time"** (chapter 1, note 3; `entry.json` quotes, quote card, share moment). It could be read as "not forever". Latin: "nequaquam a Christi sequela in perpetuum absolvi desidero". Now: "in no way do I ever want to be released from following Christ."
2. **Last-illness quotation** (chapter "The Death of Saint Clare", note 92; `entry.json` quote). "no pain has troubled me. No penance has been hard, and no illness has been too hard" did not match Robinson p. 68: "no suffering hath troubled me, no penance hath been hard, no illness arduous." Now: "no suffering has troubled me. No penance has been hard, and no illness has been difficult."
3. **Titles.** "Saint Agnes of Prague" (canonized 12 November 1989: Franciscan Media, Saint of the Day, "Saint Agnes of Bohemia" ("Canonized in 1989"); Wikipedia article (12 November 1989, Pope John Paul II)). The sources of 1910 and 1914 call her "Blessed", which was right before 1989. Applied in the chapter "Letters to a Princess in Prague", the cite line of block quote 71, and "How This Life Was Researched". "Blessed Hortulana": Robinson note 137 (a martyrology of 1638 gives her the title "Blessed"; a biography is called "La Beata Ortolana") and the Wikipedia list of Poor Clares ("declared Blessed by popular acclaim"). Applied at all six places. "Saint Agnes" for Saint Clare's sister stays: Robinson note 126, Gilliat-Smith ("Saint Agnes of Assisi"), canonized 1753. Left plain, because no source read settles the title: Pacifica, Beatrice, Amada, Balbina, Brother Philip, Brother Bernard, Brother Leo, Brother Angelo, Brother Juniper. For Brothers Leo and Angelo the Wikipedia pages say "Blessed" but cite no decree. Brother Juniper is "Servant of God" in the Franciscan Media source. The sources do not say which Bernard or which Philip the Process means.
4. **Two women named Benvenuta.** The deathbed witness (witness 11) is now "Sister Benvenuta di Diambra" in the text (twice) and in notes 95, 96, 98. Sister Benvenuta of Perugia (witness 2) is the other one, named only in the Note on the Sources and in notes 1, 2, 51.
5. **Juan Ventura** (chapter "Saint Clare's Sisters Testify Under Oath"). The Process (W20.9) says "creía que fue en el mes de septiembre pasado": he believed it was September. "that September" and "something he had seen" claimed too much. Now: "something he had seen after her death, which he thought was in September." "Everyone in the square" is correct ("lo vieron todos los de la plaza"); kept.
6. **"Her cloth."** Process W1.11 and W6.14 say the cloth was made from her thread ("de este hilado mandó confeccionar una tela fina"); no source says she wove it. Now: "Her thread was made into fine cloth, and the sisters sewed the cloth into altar linens called corporals."
7. **Saint Agnes "followed" Saint Clare.** The Legend (p. 74) says "followed her sister" and Robinson note 164 gives 16 November 1253, ninety-seven days after the death. Both confirmed. "so it happened" replaced by "she would come to the Lord soon after her" (the words of the Legend, p. 67) and "ninety-seven days after Saint Clare". Her death before 24 November 1253 is shown by Process W1.15 and W6.15 ("hacía poco había pasado de esta vida"). "her sister's life" (which read as Saint Agnes's life) now says "Saint Clare's life".
8. **"Poor Ladies."** The phrase is no longer used; the 1230 sentence now says "the convents of Saint Clare's sisters". (The Legend, p. 17, does call her Order "the Order of the Poor Ladies".)
9. **"Contemplation"** now has its own short explanation: "Contemplation is quiet prayer that looks at God with love."
10. **Feast day.** `entry.json` held 12 August, the date set in 1255. The General Roman Calendar lists "11 August: Saint Clare, Virgin, memorial", and Franciscan Media's Saint of the Day for 11 August is Saint Clare. The field is now 2000-08-11 (the year 2000 is the site's placeholder year). The field review and the gaps list are updated. The text now says "her feast was first set for 12 August" (Robinson note 179: 11 August belonged to Saint Rufino).
11. **"nearly thirty years sick in bed."** The sources say the illness lasted 28 or 29 years (Legend p. 62; Pacifica, W1.17) and that she often could not get out of bed. They do not say she lay in bed for all of it. Changed in the opening chapter, in the letters chapter ("on her deathbed, after nearly thirty years of illness"), in the summary, and in the heading ("Saint Clare's Long Years of Illness"; the id is unchanged).
12. **"held to that refusal for more than forty years"** referred to the Pope's offer, which came in 1228. Now "held to that poverty".
13. **Summary: "led the sisters of San Damiano" for more than forty years.** She became abbess about three years after she entered, so about 39 years. Now "lived at San Damiano with her sisters" (forty-two years: Legend p. 18).
14. **Vitalis.** "The next night Vitalis left" followed Felipa alone; Francisca and the Legend say the next morning. Now "The next day".
15. **Saracens "came from families that had once ruled Sicily"** now follows Robinson note 121: "descendants of the Saracens who at one time dominated Sicily and Calabria."
16. **Friars' bread.** "lived on scraps of bread" now "bread and other scraps" (Legend p. 27: "fragments of bread and other odds and ends").
17. **Letter 4, Saint Agnes.** The letter says "virgo prudentissima Agnes, soror nostra" and that Clare's daughters commend themselves to Agnes of Prague. That Saint Agnes was Saint Clare's own sister, at her side, is Mueller's note. "a most wise virgin" is now "the most prudent virgin, Agnes, our sister"; note 73 now names Mueller's note. "sent greetings" kept (the Latin is "se ... recommendant").
18. **Agnes of Prague's convent "in 1236".** Robinson note 110 says 1236; Mueller says Agnes entered her monastery in June 1234, the date of Saint Clare's first letter. The year is dropped so the chapter does not contradict itself.
19. **"Fifteen years passed"** now "Nearly fifteen years" (Mueller: "nearly fifteen years after her third letter").
20. **Letter 3 and the princess.** "Agnes" and "she" near Saint Clare could not be told apart in four sentences; now "the princess" or "Saint Agnes of Prague".
21. **Sister Agnes of Opportulo.** "Sister Agnes" in the horsehair-shirt paragraph could be read as Saint Agnes; now "Sister Agnes of Opportulo" (witness 10).
22. **"under the guidance of Saint Francis"** replaces "in the hands of" (Legend p. 11: "committed herself wholly to the guidance of Francis").
23. **Legend date.** "written soon after her death" now "soon after she was declared a saint" (title page: 1255 to 1261; the Note on the Sources says the same).
24. **Felipa.** "Felipa had never heard a confession so beautiful" now "Felipa said she had never heard...".

## Checked and left as they are

- "A hair shirt", "the Passion", "censer": the three definitions the copy editor added are correct and add no fact about Saint Clare. Kept.
- "her beauty ... so her family began to look for a husband": Ranieri (W18.2) says "Como era bella de rostro, se trató de darle marido". Kept.
- "Christ" instead of "the Crucified" (note 7): the Latin says the Crucified, the Process says God; Christ is the same person. Kept.
- "the head of the friars in that province": Robinson says "Provincial Minister". Kept.
- "They had already given up on Saint Clare": Legend p. 41, "they had long since despaired of Cläre". Kept.
- "The tallest one covered Saint Clare": W11.4 ("la que parecía más alta la cubrió"). Kept.
- Amada (W4.1), "Francis ... under guidance", Cecilia hearing the voice story from Hortulana (W6.12), Beatrice selling part of her inheritance (W12.3): all confirmed.
- Where the sources disagree, the text follows the Process and says so in the Note on the Sources: Monday or Palm Sunday (W2.1 against the Legend); 1211 against 1212; the date of the canonization bull; Felipa's "the next day" after the bull of 9 August.
- Counts in "How This Life Was Researched": five sources, twenty witnesses, four letters, three languages. All true.
