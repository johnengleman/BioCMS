## SC-01 — Before her birth, about 1193 (Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: before the birth, about 1193 (S2 Part I ch.II: "in all probability" 1193; range 1190-1196). The place is "in ecclesia" (S3 §2), "in a certain church" (S1 p.7). No source names the church or the date more closely.
- Who was there: Saint Clare's mother, Hortulana (S3 §2; S4 W1.4) = "Ortolana" (S1 p.6). The voice is not identified further.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Hortulana was "about to bring forth" and her time was near: "parturi jam vicina" (near childbirth) [S3 §2]; "the time of her delivery was at hand" [S1 p.7].
  2. She prayed "ante crucem... Crucifixum attente oraret" (earnestly before the cross), asking to be brought safely through "partus periculo" (the danger of childbirth) [S3 §2; S1 p.7].
  3. She heard a voice [S3 §2 "vocem audivit"; S1 p.7]. Words below.
  4. "Taught by this oracle" she had the child named Clara at baptism, "sperans" (hoping) the promised brightness would come true [S3 §2 "Claram vocari jussit"; S1 p.7].
- Details: Before this, Hortulana had gone across the sea with pilgrims to the holy places, to Saint Michael's, and to the shrines of the Apostles [S1 p.6; S3 §1]. Pacifica says she travelled with her to ultramar, to Saint Michael at Monte Gargano, and to Rome [S4 W1.4: "viajó allende el mar por piedad"].
- Words spoken (the voice, Latin): "Ne paveas mulier : quia quoddam lumen salva parturies, quod ipsum mundum clarius illustrabit" (Fear not, woman: you will safely bring forth a light that will light the world more clearly) [S3 §2; the OCR reads "parturics" and "illuslrabit"; corrected here].
- Testimony (public-domain, Latin then English):
  - Thomas of Celano (ascribed), S3 §2: "Pragnans denique mulier, et parlui jam vicina, ctim anle crucem in ecclesia Crucifixum altente oraret, ut eam de partus periculo salubriler expediret, vocem audivit dicentem sibi : Ne paveas mulier : quia quoddam lumen salva parturics, quod ipsum mundum clarius illuslrabit." (copied with the OCR errors as in the file)
  - Robinson's English, S1 p.7: "When finally Ortolana was with child and the time of her delivery was at hand, as she prayed earnestly before the cross in a certain church, to the end that the Crucified might bring her safely through the perils of childbirth, she heard a voice saying to her: 'Fear not, woman, for thou shalt in safety bring forth a light which will illumine the world more clearly.'"
  - plain: While she was pregnant and near her time, she prayed hard before the cross in a church. She asked the Crucified to bring her safely through the birth. She heard a voice: "Do not be afraid, woman. You will give birth safely to a light that will shine on the world more clearly."
- Second witness (facts only, our words): Cecilia of Spello says she heard from Saint Clare's mother that, pregnant and praying before the cross, she heard a voice say she would bring forth "una gran luz que iluminaría grandemente al mundo" (a great light that would greatly light the world) [S4 W6.12]. Asked when it happened, Cecilia said "por el tiempo en que san Francisco pasó de esta vida" (around the time Saint Francis passed from this life): the time she heard it told, not the time of the voice, as the text reads. Felipa says Saint Clare herself told the sisters this [S4 W3.28: "había referido a las hermanas"]; her voice words: "Alumbrarás una luz que iluminará mucho al mundo".
- Inner life: nothing from Saint Clare herself beyond W3.28 (she told it).
- What changed: the child is named Clara ("clear, bright") because of the voice.
- Not in the sources: the name of the church; a dream or vision; the hour or season; a priest or midwife; any word from the Crucified's image; Favarone's reaction.
- Sources: S3 §§1-2; S1 pp.6-7; S4 W1.4, W3.28, W6.12; S2 ch.II.

## SC-02 — Before 1211, about 17-18 (Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: before she left home in spring 1211 [S2 ch.II p.13]. Her father's house in Assisi; Pietro di Damiano, her neighbour, says she was "aproximadamente diecisiete años" (about seventeen) [S4 W19.2]; four others say 18 (W2.2, W13.1, W17.4, W20.2). Say "about seventeen or eighteen".
- Who was there: Ranieri di Bernardo (his wife was a relative of Saint Clare, so he went to the house "con confianza") [S4 W18.2]. Bona di Guelfuccio, who went with her to Saint Francis [W17.3]. Saint Francis and Brother Philip [W17.3].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She was "bella de rostro" (fair of face), so a husband was sought [S4 W18.2]. Her father, mother, and relatives wanted to marry her "según su nobleza, magníficamente, con hombres grandes y poderosos" (according to her rank, splendidly, to great and powerful men) [W19.2]. Her house had "siete caballeros" (seven knights) [W19.1].
  2. Many relatives begged her to agree: "pero ella jamás accedió" (but she never agreed) [W18.2].
  3. Ranieri himself begged her many times. "ella no quería ni oírle; antes bien, ella le predicaba a él el desprecio del mundo" (she would not even hear him; she preached to him contempt of the world) [W18.2].
  4. Pietro: "no pudo ser convencida de ninguna manera, porque quería permanecer virgen y vivir en pobreza" (she could not be persuaded in any way, because she wanted to stay a virgin and live in poverty) [W19.2].
  5. Many times Bona went with her to talk to Saint Francis: "iba secretamente para no ser vista por los parientes" (she went in secret so as not to be seen by her relatives) [W17.3]. Francis "siempre la exhortaba a que se convirtiera a Jesucristo", and Brother Philip did the same; "ella les oía con gusto" (she heard them gladly) [W17.3].
  6. Legend: she went out "unica tantum familiari socia comitante" (with one trusted companion only) [S3 §5; S1 p.10]; "verba flammantia" (flaming words) [S3 §5], "to her his words seemed a flame" [S1 p.11].
- Details: she hid herself at home so passers-by could not see her [S4 W17.4 "se ocultaba"]. She sent her food to the poor [W17.1, W20.3] and wore a rough white cloth under her dress [W20.4]. Under the soft clothes, "a little hair shirt" [S1 p.9]. Legend: when the family wanted a noble match "she in nowise acquiesced, but, feigning to postpone earthly espousals, commended her virginity to God" [S1 p.9; "simulans... differre"; Latin not located].
- Words spoken: no direct words. Only "predicaba el desprecio del mundo" (indirect) [W18.2].
- Testimony (public-domain, S1 p.11, Legend, English; Latin S3 §5): "Francis visited Cläre and she more often visited him... For, accompanied by a single confidential companion, the girl, going forth from her paternal home in secret, frequently visited the man of God: to her his words seemed a flame and his deeds more than human. Father Francis exhorted her to contempt of the world."
  - Latin, S3 §5: "unica tantum familiari socia comitante, paternos lares puella egrediens, clandestinos ad virum Dei frequentabat accessus, cujus sibi verba flammantia, cujusque ultra liminem opera videbantur." (OCR "lmminem", "cujusquc" read as above.)
  - plain: With only one trusted companion, the girl left her father's house in secret. She often visited the man of God. His words seemed to her like fire, and his deeds seemed more than human.
- What changed: the family's plan for marriage fails against her refusal. She now goes in secret to the one who will lead her out.
- Not in the sources: any words she said to Ranieri; the name of the suitor; the day, the street, or how Ranieri was received. The Latin is "flammantia", not "velut ignis" (the notes' phrase was not found in the Latin text). Robinson's page 11 gives "flame". Bona's companion in S1 is unnamed ("confidential companion"); S1 note 96 only guesses it was her aunt Pacifica. Bartholomew of Pisa's story of her begging bread in sackcloth is late and doubted (S1 note 97): leave out.
- Sources: S4 W17.1-4, W18.1-2, W19.1-2, W20.3-4; S1 pp.9-11 and notes 96-97; S3 §§3-5; S2 ch.II.

## SC-03 — Palm Sunday night, 1211 (her house; the Porziuncola)
- Status: full
- When and where: Palm Sunday and the night after it. Year 1211: S2 Part I ch.II p.13 ("the night following Palm Sunday"; "spring of the year 1211") and later "Saint Clare took the veil on the Palm Sunday of the year 1211". Robinson note 101 says 1212 ("18 March"): the two books differ; write "about 1211" as the outline says. Place: the cathedral of Assisi for the palms (S1 p.14; S2 says "cathedral"); then her house; then Saint Mary of the Porziuncola.
- Who was there: Saint Clare; the bishop (Robinson note 98: "undoubtedly Guido II", 1204-1228); "trusty companions" (S1 p.14; nothing names them: note 99); Saint Francis's friars at the Porziuncola; Cristiana di Bernardo da Suppo, who lived in the house [S4 W13.1].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Before Palm Sunday she asked Saint Francis what to do. He ordered that on the feast day she go to the blessing of the palms "compta et ornata" (dressed and adorned) and that "nocte sequenti" (on the night following) she go out [S3 §7; S1 p.13].
  2. On Sunday, in the crowd of women, she stayed in her place "prae verecundia" (from modesty); the bishop came down the steps and put a palm in her hands [S3 §7: "palmam suis in manibus poneret"; S1 p.14].
  3. "Nocte sequenti" (the following night) she set out "with trusty companions" [S3 §7 "cum honesta societate"; S1 p.14].
  4. She would not use the usual door. She opened another one, blocked by "lignorum et lapidum pondera" (the weight of beams and stones), "miranda sibi fortitudine proprius manibus" (with a strength that amazed her, with her own hands) [S3 §7; S1 p.14].
  5. She went to Saint Mary of the Porziuncola. Friars keeping vigil "cum luminaribus" (with lights) received her [S3 §8; S1 p.14: "lighted torches"].
  6. "ibi manu fratrum crines deponens" (there, putting off her hair by the hands of the friars) [S3 §8; S1 p.14]. Beatrice, her sister, says Saint Francis cut it "ante el altar" [S4 W12.4 "la tonsuró ante el altar, en la iglesia de la Virgen María, llamada de la Porciúncula"; W17.5, from hearsay].
  7. Next day many marvelled at the open door that a young girl had opened [S4 W13.1: "a la mañana siguiente, muchos... se maravillaron"].
- Details: the door was "trancada con unos troncos pesados y con una columna de piedra" (barred with heavy logs and a stone column), things "que difícilmente hubieran podido ser removidos por muchos hombres" (that many men could hardly have moved); "ella sola, con el auxilio de Jesucristo, los apartó" (she alone, with the help of Jesus Christ, moved them) [W13.1]. She feared being stopped, "temiendo que se le impidiese la marcha" [W13.1]. Cristiana lived in the house then [W13.1].
- Words spoken: none in the sources.
- Testimony: Cristiana di Bernardo da Suppo (S4 W13.1), facts-only, in our words above. Public-domain text: S1 p.14: "The following night Cläre set about the accomplishment of the Saint's command, and, with some trusty companions, began her longed-for flight. But not wishing to leave by the usual door, she broke open, with a strength that astonished herself, another one which was walled up by a mass of beams and stones." plain: That night she began her flight, as Saint Francis had told her. She did not want to use the usual door, so she opened another one that was blocked up with beams and stones, with a strength that surprised even her.
- What changed: she leaves house, city, and family ("domo, civitate et consanguineis derelictis", S3 §8) and becomes a Sister of Penance at Saint Francis's hands.
- Not in the sources: the hour; weather; moonlight; who carried her; what she wore under her dress; any words to Francis; the names of the "trusty companions"; a monastery wall or gate on the road; whether her father was in the house; the distance walked. The Legend does not say the door led to the street. Robinson's note 100 (the "porta di mortuccio", walled up a year after a death) is his surmise, not the source.
- Benvenuta of Perugia: "ella había ingresado el lunes santo" (she had entered on Holy Monday) [S4 W2.1]. This differs from the Legend. Give it one sentence in the Note on the Sources; do not reconcile.
- Sources: S1 pp.13-15 and notes 98-101; S3 §§7-8; S4 W2.1, W12.4, W13.1, W17.5, W20.6; S2 ch.II p.13 and p.~50.

## SC-04 — After the night (San Paolo, Benedictine nuns)
- Status: full
- When and where: after the Porziuncola, at the church of Saint Paul, the house of Benedictine nuns near Bastia, about an hour's walk from the Porziuncola [S1 p.15; Robinson note 104]. The Legend says the pressure went on "per plures dies" (for many days) [S3 §9]; no source dates the first visit. "Days later" of the outline is not given: say "after she came to Saint Paul's".
- Who was there: her kinsfolk, "consanguinei" [S3 §9]; the sources say "sus parientes" [S4 W12.4, W18.3, W20.6] and name no one. Beatrice, her sister, told it [W12.4]. Brother Francis had led her there [S1 p.15].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Francis led her to Saint Paul's "to abide in that place until the Most High should provide another" [S3 §8; S1 p.15].
  2. News reached her kin, who "condemned the deed" and "ran to the spot" [S3 §9 "currunt ad locum"; S1 p.16].
  3. They used "Violentiae impetum, venena consiliorum, blanditias promissionum" (force, poisonous counsels, bland promises), urging her to leave a state "unworthy of her birth" [S3 §9; S1 p.16].
  4. "At illa pannos apprehendens altaris, caput denudat attonsum" (but she, seizing the altar cloths, bared her shorn head) [S3 §9]; "firmans se nullatenus a Christi servitio ulterius avellendam" (declaring that nothing would pull her away from Christ's service) [S3 §9; S1 p.16].
  5. The pressure went on "per plures dies"; "donec propinqui... quiescunt" (until the kin were quiet) [S3 §9].
  6. Three witnesses tell the stand in short form [S4 W12.4: "agarró los manteles del altar y se descubrió la cabeza, mostrándola rapada"; W18.3: "les mostró la cabeza rapada; y de esta manera la dejaron quedarse"; W20.6: "ella les mostró la cabeza rapada"]. Beatrice: "de ningún modo quiso acceder, ni se dejó sacar de allí, ni regresar con ellos" (she would in no way agree, nor be taken from there, nor return with them) [W12.4].
  7. After a few days she went to Sant'Angelo di Panzo and then to San Damiano [S3 §10; S1 p.17; S4 W12.5].
- Details: before this, Beatrice says Saint Clare sold her whole inheritance and part of Beatrice's and gave it to the poor [S4 W12.3]. Beatrice says she knew some of this herself and the rest "de la misma madonna Clara y de otros", and that it was "unos cuarenta y dos años" before [W12.5].
- Words spoken: only in indirect speech. Latin "se nullatenus a Christi servitio... avellendam" (that she would in no way be pulled from the service of Christ) [S3 §9]; English "declared that nothing would henceforth separate her from the service of Christ" [S1 p.16]. No direct quotation exists in the sources read.
- Testimony (public-domain, S1 p.16): "But Cläre, laying hold of the altar cloths and baring her tonsured head, declared that nothing would henceforth separate her from the service of Christ. Her courage increased as the war waged by her kindred waxed stronger; and love wounded by ill-treatment gave her strength." plain: She took hold of the altar cloths and bared her cropped head. She said nothing would now pull her away from serving Christ. Her courage grew as her family's pressure grew.
- What changed: the family gives up. She moves on to Panzo, then San Damiano, where "forty-two years" begin [S1 pp.17-18].
- Not in the sources: the names of the relatives or whether her father came; blows, shouting, or men dragging her (the Legend says "main force" in general only; Gilliat-Smith, S2 pp.~52-53, argues from the bull of canonization that the scene was calmer: "a sufficiently tame affair". That is his view; the bull was not read); a word or oath she spoke aloud; the nuns' reaction; the day or hour; where in the church the altar was.
- Sources: S3 §§8-10; S1 pp.15-17 and note 104; S4 W12.3-5, W18.3, W20.6; S2 Part I ch.II pp.~52-53.

## SC-05 — Lent, a Thursday (San Damiano)
- Status: full
- When and where: Lent, a Thursday, year not given [S4 W10.6]. San Damiano. Saint Clare became abbess about three years after she entered religion [S4 W1.6; S3 §11 "Triennio"; S1 p.23]. Robinson note 112 puts this "in 1215, or about that year" (computed from his 1212). With S2's 1211, write "about three years after she entered religion".
- Who was there: Saint Clare; a "servicial" (a serving sister) whose foot struck her; Agnes (Sor Inés), daughter of Opórtulo di Bernardo, who saw it [S4 W10.6]. Saint Francis, who urged her to rule [S4 W1.6; S3 §11; S1 p.23].
- What happened, in order:
  1. For three years she refused "nomen et officium Abbatissae" (the name and office of abbess), wishing "subesse potius quam praeesse" (to be under rather than over) [S3 §11; S1 p.23].
  2. "Cogente autem B. Francisco" (Blessed Francis pressing her) she took the government of the ladies [S3 §11]. Pacifica: "a ruegos e instancias de san Francisco, que casi la obligó" (at Saint Francis's requests and urging, who almost forced her) [S4 W1.6]. Pacifica adds she was there: "ella había estado presente".
  3. Fear, not pride, rose in her; the higher she was seen, the lowlier in her own eyes [S3 §11 "timor, non tumor"; S1 p.23].
  4. She poured water on the sisters' hands, served those who sat at table, washed the sick sisters' seats [S3 §11 "aquam infunderet... comedentibus ministraret"]. She gave orders "invita valde" (very unwillingly) and preferred to do a thing herself [S3 §11; S1 p.24]. In the cold she covered the sisters at night [S4 W2.3 "por la noche las cubría para protegerlas del frío"].
  5. She washed the feet of the serving sisters coming in from outside and kissed them [S3 §11 "ablutis oscula imprimit"].
  6. Once, as she hurried to kiss a servant's feet, the servant "tantam humilitatem non ferens" (not bearing so great humility) drew back her foot and "pedem... in ore percussit" (struck her in the mouth) [S3 §11; S1 p.24].
  7. She took the foot back gently and kissed the sole: "imprinted a fervent kiss upon the sole of it" [S1 p.24]; Latin "sub ipsa planta strictum..." (the OCR breaks off; line 143215 of the file continues in the next column).
- Details: Agnes (W10.6): "queriendo besárselos, como solía... le golpeó la boca con el pie. Y la madonna se regocijó con eso y le besó la planta de aquel pie." (wanting to kiss them, as she used to... struck her mouth with the foot. And the lady rejoiced at it and kissed the sole of that foot.) Asked how she knew: "porque lo presenció" (because she saw it). Asked when: "en la cuaresma"; asked the day: "un jueves". Bienvenida di Perusa says the sister struck her "incautamente" (unawares) [W2.3]; Felipa says Saint Clare "no desistió ni aun así, por humildad" (did not stop, even so, from humility) [W3.9]. Three witnesses and the Legend agree; only Agnes gives Lent and a Thursday and says Saint Clare "se regocijó".
- Words spoken: none in the sources.
- Testimony (public-domain, S1 pp.23-24): "She was once washing the feet of a certain servant and, being about to kiss them, the servant, not brooking so great humility, withdrew her foot, thus striking her lady in the face. But Cläre, gently grasping the servant's foot again, imprinted a fervent kiss upon the sole of it." Latin S3 §11: "Lavabat semel cujusdam servientis sororis pedes : quos dum deosculari properat, illa tantam humilitatem non ferens, pedem subtrahit, dominamque suo pede in ore percussit. At illa pedem famulae blande resumit, et sub ipsa planta strictum [osculum]..." (the file text is cut after "strictum"; the last word is not copied). plain: Once she was washing a servant sister's feet and hurried to kiss them. The sister could not bear such humility and pulled her foot away, and it struck Saint Clare in the mouth. Saint Clare gently took the foot again and kissed the sole.
- Inner life: nothing of her own words; Legend: "fear and not haughtiness arose in her heart" when she took the office [S1 p.23; S3 §11 "timor, non tumor"].
- What changed: Saint Clare rules as a servant; her humility is the first sign of how she will lead. Her authority "diminished her freedom" [S1 p.23].
- Not in the sources: the date or year; the name of the servant sister (W10 names no one); who else saw it besides Agnes; where in the house; whether the water was warm; what was said afterwards. Do not say the foot was "dirty" or that she laughed (Agnes says she "se regocijó", rejoiced, only).
- Sources: S1 pp.23-24 and notes 112-113; S3 §11; S4 W1.6, W2.3, W3.9, W10.6; S2 ch.II.
