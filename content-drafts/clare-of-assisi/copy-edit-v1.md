## Edits the checker should confirm

- Item 7, "she put herself wholly in the hands of Saint Francis": the draft said "in his hands", which could mean Christ. The Legend (S1, about line 2790 of the source file) says "Clare committed herself wholly to the guidance" of Saint Francis.
- Item 16, Amada: the draft's "deceive her" was unclear. Process W4.1 says that Saint Clare prayed for Amada: "que no permitiese que fuese engañada por el mundo".
- Item 13, "They had already given up on Saint Clare": the draft said "long before", but only sixteen days had passed. Confirm the wording against the Legend.
- Item 48, Juan Ventura: the draft said "something he believed he had seen". Process W20.9 says "vio" (he saw), so I removed "believed" (reverence point 6).
- Items 5, 39, 40: I added three short explanations of words (hair shirt, the Passion, censer). They are definitions, not facts about Saint Clare. Remove them if the checker prefers.

## Listed, not fixed (for the fact checker)

1. Title of Agnes of Prague. The Catholic Church canonized her in 1989, so she is "Saint Agnes of Prague". The text names her bare about ten times, in the heading "Letters to a Princess in Prague" and in chapters 9 and 10. I did not add the title, because no project source confirms it. If you add it, keep her apart from Saint Clare's sister Saint Agnes. One way: "Saint Agnes of Prague" every time, and "her sister Saint Agnes" for the sister.
2. Other possible titles, left plain: Hortulana (venerated as Blessed Ortolana?), Pacifica, Beatrice, Amada (Blessed Amata?), Brother Bernard (Blessed Bernard of Quintavalle?), Brother Juniper, Brother Leo, Brother Angelo, Thomas of Celano (see titles-pass.md). Please check each one.
3. Saint Agnes "followed" Saint Clare (chapter "Saint Clare's Sisters Testify Under Oath"): the outline says "No source says Agnes followed Clare". The Process (W1.15, W6.15) says that "sor Inés, hermana de santa Clara" had already died by 24 November 1253. Please check the date 16 November 1253 and the sentence "so it happened".
4. Quotation, opening scene: "I never want to be released from following Christ, not for all time." The phrase "not for all time" can read as "not forever" (the opposite meaning). Translate it again, for example "never, for all eternity".
5. Quotation, last illness: "No penance has been hard, and no illness has been too hard." The words "hard ... too hard" read badly. Translate it again from the Latin or the Robinson text.
6. "The sisters sewed her cloth into altar linens called corporals": Saint Clare spun thread. Does the source say who wove the cloth? "Her cloth" may add a fact.
7. Juan Ventura: "that September" and "everyone in the square saw it and ran with the man to the tomb". W20.9 says "aquel año, después de la muerte" and "público y notorio". Check where "September" and the square come from.
8. "Sister Benvenuta" at the deathbed is Benvenuta di Diambra (W11), and "Sister Benvenuta of Perugia" (W2) is a different woman (see scenes-3.md). The text says only "Sister Benvenuta" in chapter 13 and in "A Note on the Sources". A reader may think that they are one woman.
9. "She was also beautiful, and so her family began to look for a husband for her": check that the source gives her beauty as the reason.
10. "the convents of the Poor Ladies": the text does not say that this is a name for Saint Clare's sisters. Add a short explanation if the source supports it.
11. "Through contemplation Agnes was to transform her whole self": "contemplation" is a Church word with no explanation. Add a short plain explanation if wanted.
12. "Felipa had never heard a confession so beautiful": the source (W3) says "la testigo nunca había oído cosa igual". It is correct, but "Sister Felipa said she had never heard..." would show the attribution more clearly.

## Resolved by the checker (2026-10-06)

Edits to confirm:
- Item 7 ("in the hands of Saint Francis"): changed to "under the guidance of Saint Francis", the Legend's word (p. 11).
- Item 16 (Amada): confirmed, W4.1.
- Item 13 ("They had already given up on Saint Clare"): confirmed, Legend p. 41 ("long since despaired").
- Item 48 (Juan Ventura): "vio" (he saw) is right, but he only "creía" (believed) that it was September. Now "something he had seen after her death, which he thought was in September".
- Items 5, 39, 40 (three definitions): correct, kept.

Listed, not fixed:
1. Agnes of Prague: "Saint Agnes of Prague" (canonized 12 November 1989), applied everywhere. Saint Clare's sister stays "Saint Agnes", and the text keeps them apart ("the princess" where "she" would be unclear).
2. Other titles: Hortulana is "Blessed Hortulana" (Robinson note 137; Wikipedia list of Poor Clares), applied at six places. Pacifica, Beatrice, Amada, Brothers Bernard, Juniper, Leo, Angelo, Philip stay plain, because no source read settles the title (Juniper is "Servant of God").
3. Saint Agnes "followed": confirmed (Legend p. 74; Robinson note 164, 16 November 1253, ninety-seven days after Saint Clare). "so it happened" replaced; her death before 24 November is in W1.15 and W6.15.
4. "not for all time": now "in no way do I ever want to be released from following Christ" (Latin "nequaquam ... in perpetuum").
5. "no illness has been too hard": now "no suffering has troubled me. No penance has been hard, and no illness has been difficult" (Robinson p. 68).
6. "her cloth": the sources say the cloth was made from her thread; no one is said to have woven it. Sentence rewritten.
7. Juan Ventura: "that September" is his belief; "everyone in the square" is in W20.9. Both now follow the Process.
8. Benvenuta: the deathbed witness is now "Sister Benvenuta di Diambra".
9. Beauty and the husband: Ranieri says so (W18.2). Kept.
10. "Poor Ladies": no longer used; "Saint Clare's sisters".
11. "contemplation": one short sentence of explanation added.
12. Felipa: "Felipa said she had never heard a confession so beautiful."
