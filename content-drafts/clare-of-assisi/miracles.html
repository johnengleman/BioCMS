The Church remembers the wonders that God worked through the prayers of Saint Clare, both in her life and at her tomb. The accounts below come from the two oldest sources. Her sisters and the people of Assisi gave sworn testimony at the Church’s inquiry in November 1253, a little more than three months after her death. Soon after she was declared a saint in 1255, an author wrote the Legend of Saint Clare. Each account names its teller. Most of the healings in her life came when Saint Clare made the sign of the cross over the sick.

Healings During Saint Clare’s Life

Sister Benvenuta di Diambra is healed of sores

For twelve years Sister Benvenuta di Diambra had painful sores under her arm and on her chest. They had five openings, and each had to be plugged. One night she came to Saint Clare in tears and asked for help. Saint Clare got up from her bed, knelt, and prayed to the Lord. Then she made the sign of the cross over herself and over Benvenuta. She said the Our Father and touched the sores with her bare hand, and Benvenuta was healed. Benvenuta testified that she believed it had happened two years before, and that the sores never came back.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 11 (Sister Benvenuta di Diambra), art. 1; also witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 10, witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 8, and witness 14 (Sister Angeluccia), art. 5 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 54–55. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Amada is healed of dropsy and fever

Sister Amada was Saint Clare’s niece. For thirteen months she had been very ill with dropsy, fever, a cough, and pain in her side. Dropsy is an illness that makes the body swell with water. Her belly was so swollen that she could hardly bend her head. Saint Clare laid her hand on Amada and made the sign of the cross. She asked God to free Amada from the illness if that was better for her soul. Amada said that she was healed at once, and that the Lord healed her completely through the saint’s merits. Sister Felipa saw it and said that Amada’s body was normal again the next morning.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 4 (Sister Amada), arts. 1 and 7, and witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 11 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 55. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Cristiana hears again with her deaf ear

Sister Cristiana had been deaf in one ear for a long time. She had taken many medicines, and none of them helped. Saint Clare made the sign of the cross on her head and touched her ear. Then her ear opened, and she heard very well. Cristiana testified that it happened about a year before, in June or July.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 5 (Sister Cristiana), art. 1, and witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 10 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 55. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Benvenuta of Perugia gets her voice back

Sister Benvenuta of Perugia lost her voice for almost two years, and she could hardly even whisper. On the night of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary she saw in a vision that Saint Clare healed her with the sign of the cross. The Legend says that she waited for morning with longing. That same day Saint Clare made the sign of the cross over her, and she was healed.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 13, and witness 7 (Sister Balbina), art. 7 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 55. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Five sick sisters are healed at once

Once five sisters of San Damiano were sick at the same time. Saint Clare made the sign of the cross over them, and all five were healed at once. Sister Pacifica was one of them. For a long time she had suffered from an illness that made her feel very cold. She shook, and her teeth chattered. Pacifica said that the sign of the cross was the sisters’ medicine whenever they fell ill. No one could understand the words that Saint Clare said with it, because she said them so softly.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 1 (Sister Pacifica), arts. 16 and 18 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 55–56. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Cecilia is healed of a choking cough

Sister Cecilia had a bad cough that came on whenever she began to eat, so that she seemed about to choke. One Friday Saint Clare gave her a little bread to eat. Cecilia took it with great fear, but she ate it because Saint Clare told her to. After that the cough never troubled her again. Sister Amada told this story, and Sister Cecilia herself testified that Saint Clare had healed her.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 9, and witness 6 (Sister Cecilia), art. 8 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Balbina is healed twice

Sister Balbina was Saint Clare’s niece. One night she lay in great pain in her hip and began to moan. Saint Clare asked what was wrong and then lay down right on Balbina’s hip, where the pain was. After that she laid the cloth from her own head on the hip, and the pain left at once. Balbina said that this was more than twelve years before. About twenty years before, Saint Clare had also healed her of a constant fever and an abscess on her chest, when the sisters thought she was dying.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 7 (Sister Balbina), arts. 11–13 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Brother Stephen is freed from madness

A friar named Brother Stephen became mad. Saint Francis sent him to San Damiano so that Saint Clare would make the sign of the cross over him. She did, and the friar slept for a short time in the place where she used to pray. When he woke, he ate a little and went away healed. The Legend says that he went back to Saint Francis free of his madness.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 15, and witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 12 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 53. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A pebble falls from a small boy’s nose

A boy from Spoleto named Mattiolo was three or four years old. He pushed a small pebble up his nose, and no one could get it out. The child seemed to be in danger. He was brought to Saint Clare. As soon as she made the sign of the cross over him, the pebble fell out of his nose. Sister Benvenuta of Perugia saw the boy on the same day or the next.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 18 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 53–54. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A boy’s clouded eye is healed

A boy from Perugia had a cloud over one eye that covered it completely. He was brought to Saint Clare, and she touched his eye and made the sign of the cross. Then she said, “Take him to my mother, Sister Hortulana, and let her make the sign of the cross over him.” Blessed Hortulana did so, and the boy was healed. Saint Clare said that her mother had healed him, and Blessed Hortulana said that her daughter had healed him. So each gave the credit to the other.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 11 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 54. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A procurator’s son is healed of fever

The sisters had a procurator named Juan, a man who handled their business. Sister Felipa told of his son, who was very sick with fever and was brought to Saint Clare. She was there when Saint Clare touched him and made the sign of the cross, and the boy was well. Felipa did not see him again, but his father told the sisters that the boy had been healed at once. Sister Francisca told of a five-year-old son of Juan who had fever and scrofula, which causes swellings in the throat. Saint Clare made the sign of the cross over him and touched him, and he was healed. Francisca heard the father say in the speaking room that the boy had been healed at once, and she saw the boy come back healed soon afterward. The Process does not say whether the two sisters tell of the same boy.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 15, and witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 6 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Saint Clare knows that Sister Andrea is choking

Sister Andrea of Ferrara had scrofula in her throat, and she was strongly tempted to cure it herself. One night she squeezed her own throat so hard that she lost her voice. Saint Clare knew it by revelation. Saint Clare called Sister Felipa, who slept beside her: “Go down quickly to the dormitory, because Sister Andrea is very ill. Prepare a soft-boiled egg and give it to her to drink, and when she has her speech back, bring her to me.” Sister Andrea would not say what she had done, but Saint Clare told her everything in order. The Legend adds that Saint Clare told her to change her life, and that Andrea did. She was healed of the scrofula and died not long after of another illness.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 16, and witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 23 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 86–87. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A woman from Pisa is freed of five demons

About four years before the inquiry, a woman from Pisa came to the convent. At the place where visitors spoke with the sisters, she gave thanks to God and to Saint Clare. She said that the Lord had freed her from five demons through the saint’s merits, and that the demons had cried out, “The prayers of that saint burn us.” Sister Amada and Sister Balbina heard her tell it.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 20, and witness 7 (Sister Balbina), art. 14 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Sister Francisca is healed by a vow

Sister Francisca had a very serious illness in her head for more than six years. It made her teeth chatter and took away her memory. When Saint Clare was about to die, Francisca made a vow to her. She was healed at once, and she never suffered from that illness again.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 7 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

God Protects San Damiano and Assisi

Saracen soldiers leave San Damiano without harming anyone

During the war around Assisi, Saracen soldiers of the emperor’s army climbed the wall of San Damiano and came down into the cloister, the enclosed court inside the convent. Saint Clare was very ill, but she had herself brought to the door of the dining hall. The Blessed Sacrament was carried before her in a small box. It was the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ. Saint Clare lay on the ground and prayed with tears: “Lord, guard these servants of yours, for I cannot guard them.” Sister Francisca and Sister Illuminata of Pisa were holding her. Francisca heard a wonderfully sweet voice say, “I will always defend you.” Then the soldiers left without harming anyone. That evening Saint Clare told the two sisters who had heard the voice to tell no one while she lived. Sister Francisca gave this account under oath. The Legend gives the prayer and the answer in fuller words, and it says the voice sounded like a little child’s.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 2; also witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 20, and witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 18 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 36–37. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

The army of Vitalis of Aversa leaves Assisi

Vitalis of Aversa came with a great imperial army to besiege Assisi. He swore that he would not leave until he took the city. Saint Clare called her sisters at dawn and put ashes on her own shaved head and on the head of every sister. Then she sent them to pray that the Lord would free the city, and they fasted on bread and water that day. The next day Vitalis left with his whole army. The Legend says that the army broke up and that he never troubled that land again.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 19, and witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 3 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 38–39. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Wonders at San Damiano

Half a loaf feeds the sisters

One day the sisters had only half a loaf of bread, because the other half had been given to the friars. Saint Clare told Sister Cecilia to cut it into fifty slices and take them to the sisters in the dining hall. Cecilia answered that this would need the Lord’s miracle of the five loaves and two fish. “Go and do as I told you,” Saint Clare said. Cecilia testified that the Lord multiplied that bread into fifty good, large slices.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 6 (Sister Cecilia), art. 16 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 28. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

An empty jar is found full of oil

In about the second year at San Damiano the convent ran completely out of oil. It was summer. Brother Bentevenga was the friar who begged for the sisters. Saint Clare called him and asked him to go for oil. She washed a jar with her own hands and set it on a low wall near the door for him. When he came for it a little later, he found it full of oil. No one could find out who had filled it. The Legend says that God alone filled it, and that the friar grumbled that the sisters had called him for a joke.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 1 (Sister Pacifica), art. 15, witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 14, and witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 26 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 29. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A heavy door falls on Saint Clare

One July a very heavy door of the convent fell on Saint Clare. It was about seven years before the inquiry. Sister Angeluccia was there. She cried out, because she feared that the door had killed Saint Clare and she could not lift it alone. The sisters ran and found the door still on top of her. It was so heavy that three friars could hardly put it back in its place. Saint Clare said that it had not hurt her at all, and that she had felt it on her like a light cloak.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 5 (Sister Cristiana), art. 5; witness 6 (Sister Cecilia), art. 17; witness 14 (Sister Angeluccia), art. 6; witness 15 (Sister Balbina of Porzano), art. 2 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Saint Agnes cannot be carried away

Sixteen days after Saint Clare left home, her young sister Saint Agnes joined her at the church of Sant’Angelo di Panzo. The next day twelve men of the family came to take Saint Agnes home by force. They dragged her down the hillside while Saint Clare prayed in tears. The Legend says that the body of Saint Agnes then seemed fixed to the ground. The men and others who came to help could not carry her across a little stream, and the men went away.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 40–43. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

The water from Saint Clare’s feet tastes sweet

Sister Agnes of Opportulo asked again and again until Saint Clare’s feet were washed. Then she drank some of the water. She said that it was so sweet and pleasant that she could hardly describe it. No other sister tasted it, because Saint Clare threw it out at once.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 10 (Sister Agnes of Opportulo), art. 11 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Graces Saint Clare Told Her Sisters

She hears the Christmas services from her sickbed, 1252

On Christmas night in 1252 Saint Clare was too ill to get up, and the sisters left her alone while they went to the chapel. “Lord God, here I am, left alone with you in this place,” she sighed. Then she heard the organ, the responses, and the whole service of the friars at the church of San Francesco, as if she were there. She told Sister Amada that she had also seen the manger of the Lord. In the morning she said to her sisters, “Blessed be the Lord Jesus Christ, who did not leave me when you left me.”

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 30, witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 16, and witness 7 (Sister Balbina), art. 9 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 47–48. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

She follows Christ’s suffering through Good Friday

On the evening of Holy Thursday, Saint Clare shut herself in her room to follow the Lord in his agony. She sat on her bed all that night and all the next day, so absorbed that she seemed to feel nothing. On Friday night a sister lit a candle to remind her that Saint Francis had told her to eat every day. “What need is there of a candle?” Saint Clare asked. “Is it not day?” She told the sister not to tell anyone while she lived. Sister Felipa also testified that on one Good Friday Saint Clare seemed to feel nothing for the whole day and much of the night.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 51–52 (our modern English); Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 25 (our translation from the Spanish). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

Her vision of Saint Francis

Saint Clare told her sisters of a vision. She seemed to be carrying a jug of hot water and a towel up a high staircase to Saint Francis. She climbed as easily as if she were walking on level ground. Saint Francis offered her his breast and said, “Come, take, and suck.” What she drank was sweeter than she could ever say. What remained in her mouth seemed like gold so clear and bright that she could see herself in it as in a mirror. Sister Felipa told the vision under oath, and three other sisters said they had heard it from Saint Clare too.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 3 (Sister Felipa), art. 29; witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 16; witness 6 (Sister Cecilia), art. 13; witness 7 (Sister Balbina), art. 10 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

She answers the tempter at night

The Legend tells of a night when Saint Clare wept in prayer. The devil stood beside her in the form of a black boy. He told her not to weep so much, or she would go blind. “Whoever will see God will not be blind,” she answered at once, and he left. Later that same night he came back and warned her again. “He who serves the Lord suffers no harm,” she said, and he vanished.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 34 (our modern English). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

She foresees that a new sister will not stay

While Saint Francis was still alive, he sent five women to be received at San Damiano. Saint Clare received only four. She said that the fifth would not stay in the convent more than three years. The fifth woman insisted until Saint Clare accepted her. She stayed barely half a year. Sister Cecilia testified to this, and she said that Saint Agnes was present when Saint Clare spoke.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 6 (Sister Cecilia), art. 15 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

She foretells that a knight will have a son

A knight of Assisi named Hugolino had sent his wife Guiduzia back to her parents and had lived apart from her for more than twenty-two years. No one could persuade him to take her back. Then a message came to him from Saint Clare. She had learned in a vision that he would soon take his wife back and have a son, who would be a great joy to him. Hugolino was angry when he heard it. A few days later he felt such a strong desire that he took his wife back, and the son was born, as Saint Clare had seen. Hugolino testified to this himself, and his son was still alive.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 16 (Hugolino di Pietro Girardone), art. 4 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

Visions Her Sisters Saw

A great brightness where she prayed

Sister Benvenuta of Perugia saw a great brightness in the place where Saint Clare went to pray. It was so bright that she thought it was a real fire. She said that she alone saw it, and that it was before Saint Clare fell ill. Sister Amada said that when Saint Clare came back from prayer, her face seemed brighter and more beautiful than the sun.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 2 (Sister Benvenuta of Perugia), art. 17, and witness 4 (Sister Amada), art. 4 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A beautiful child beside her during a sermon

Brother Philip of Atri was preaching to the sisters in the week after Easter. It was about twenty-one years before the inquiry. Sister Agnes of Opportulo saw a very beautiful child beside Saint Clare. He seemed about three years old. She prayed that God would not let it be a deception. In her heart she heard the words, “I am in the midst of them.” She understood that the child was Jesus Christ. Then she saw a light like starlight around Saint Clare. After that a red light like sparks of fire covered Saint Clare’s head.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 10 (Sister Agnes of Opportulo), art. 8 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 59. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A child on her lap and two shining wings

Sister Francisca once saw a very beautiful child on Saint Clare’s lap at her breast. It was the first of May. She felt a sweetness she could not describe, and she believed without doubt that the child was the Son of God. Over Saint Clare’s head she also saw two wings shining like the sun. She said that she alone saw this, and that she had never told anyone until her testimony.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 4 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

The Body of the Lord appears as a child

About three years before the inquiry the sisters thought Saint Clare was about to die. It was around the feast of Saint Martin. A priest was to bring her Holy Communion. Sister Francisca saw a very great brightness over Saint Clare’s head, and the Body of the Lord seemed to her to be a small and very beautiful child. Saint Clare received Holy Communion and then said: “God has done me such a great kindness today that heaven and earth cannot compare with it.”

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 9 (Sister Francisca), art. 10 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A nun has a vision of Saint Clare’s deathbed

While Pope Innocent IV was still at Lyons, Saint Clare grew much worse. A Benedictine nun of San Paolo had a vision. She and her sisters seemed to stand weeping by Saint Clare’s bed at San Damiano. A beautiful woman at the head of the bed told them, “Do not weep, daughters, for the one who will conquer. She cannot die until the Lord comes with his disciples.” The author of the Legend saw the vision come true when Pope Innocent and his cardinals came to Assisi in 1253.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 63–64 (our modern English). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

Virgins in white come to her deathbed, 1253

On the Friday evening before Saint Clare died, Sister Benvenuta di Diambra was awake by her bed. She saw a great crowd of virgins dressed in white, with crowns on their heads, come in through the door. The tallest and most beautiful had a golden ball on her crown, like a censer for burning incense. Its light seemed to fill the house. This tallest virgin covered Saint Clare with a very fine cloth. Then she bent her face over Saint Clare, and after that they all vanished. Benvenuta told no one until she gave her testimony.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 11 (Sister Benvenuta di Diambra), art. 4 (our translation from the Spanish); Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 70–71. https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

After Saint Clare’s Death

A raging man is healed at her tomb

Juan Ventura of Assisi testified about a man from beyond the mountains. The man was raging, or else possessed, and he was carried to Saint Clare’s tomb tied with ropes. There he was healed at once. Juan Ventura thought this happened in September of that same year, after her death. He did not know the man’s name, because the man was not from those parts. He said that everyone in the square saw it and ran with the man to the tomb.

Source: Process of Canonization, witness 20 (Juan Ventura), art. 9 (our translation from the Spanish). https://franciscanosenmexico.com.mx/pdfvarios/sta%20clara/procesodecanonizacion.pdf

A young Frenchman is freed of frenzy

The Legend tells of a young Frenchman who served at the Pope’s court. A frenzy had taken away his speech and made his body wildly restless. No one could hold him, and he hurt himself in the hands of those who tried. His countrymen tied him to a stretcher with strong ropes and carried him, against his will, to the church of Saint Clare. They laid him before her tomb, and he was freed at once through the faith of those who had brought him.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 79. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A boy of Perugia is freed of a demon

A boy of Perugia named Giacobino seemed possessed by an evil demon. Twice a day a fury seized him. He threw himself into the fire, fell to the ground, and chewed stones until his teeth broke. Doctors could not help him. His father Guidolotto gave the child to Saint Clare in prayer and laid him on her tomb. The boy was freed at once and never suffered such an attack again.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 76–77. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A woman of Fratta is healed and freed

A woman named Alessandrina lived at Fratta near Perugia, and she was troubled by a demon. She had also lost the use of her left side, and her hand was withered. Remedies did not help her. She came to Saint Clare’s tomb with a sorrowful heart and called on her merits. Her hand was made whole, her side was healed, and she was freed from the demon. Another woman from the same place was freed at the tomb at the same time.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 78. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A man from Spello is healed

Valentino of Spello had epilepsy and fell to the ground six times a day. He also could not walk, because one of his legs was drawn up. He was brought on a donkey to Saint Clare’s tomb, and he lay there for two days and three nights. On the third day his leg gave a loud crack, though no one was touching him. He was healed of both illnesses.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 79. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A man blind for twelve years sees

Giacobello had been blind for twelve years. One night near the bridge at Narni a lady appeared to him in his sleep. “Giacobello, why do you not come to me at Assisi, so that you may be healed?” she said. He saw the same vision again at Spoleto. At Assisi the crowd at the tomb was so great that he could not get in, so he slept at the door with a stone under his head. A voice told him that the Lord would help him if he could get in. He begged the crowd to let him pass, took off his shoes and outer clothes, and came humbly to the tomb. There he fell asleep. Saint Clare said to him, “Get up, because you have been healed.” He got up able to see clearly, and he praised God.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 80–81 (our modern English). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A crushed hand is restored

Buon Giovanni of Perugia went out with his fellow citizens to fight the men of Foligno. A stone crushed his right hand. He spent much money on doctors, but the hand stayed useless. He often wished it cut off. Then he heard what the Lord was doing through Saint Clare. He made a vow and went to her tomb, where he offered a wax image of a hand and lay down on the tomb. Before he left the church, his hand was completely restored.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 82. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A bent boy from Bettona stands straight

Petriolo of Bettona had been ill for three years. His back was so bent that he walked bowed toward the ground, even with a stick. His father went to many doctors, and all of them said there was no cure. Then the father asked for the prayers of the new saint, Saint Clare. The boy was carried to her tomb, and before long he rose up straight and well. He walked and leaped, praising God, and he called on the crowds to praise Saint Clare.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 83. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A boy lame from birth walks

A ten-year-old boy of San Quirico near Assisi had been lame from birth. His shin bones were diseased, and his feet were turned crosswise. His mother had made vows for him to Saint Francis several times, but he did not get better. When she heard of Saint Clare’s miracles, she carried the boy to the saint’s tomb. After some days his shins were healed, and his body grew straight.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 84. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A boy from Gubbio walks after his parents’ vow

Giacomo di Franco of Gubbio had a five-year-old son who had never walked, because his feet were weak. The boy crawled in the ashes and could not even climb onto a bench. His parents entrusted him to Saint Clare. They promised that if she healed him, he would be called “the servant of Saint Clare.” As soon as they made the vow, the boy could use his legs. His parents brought him leaping to her tomb.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 84–85. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A woman of Bevagna walks home

Plenaria of Bevagna had suffered for a long time from a contraction in her side. She could only drag herself along with a stick. One Friday she had herself carried to Saint Clare’s tomb, and there she prayed with all her heart. On the next day she walked home on her own feet, completely well.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 85. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A girl’s swollen throat is healed

A girl of Perugia had long suffered great pain from scrofula, swellings of the throat. Twenty swellings were counted, and her throat seemed larger than her head. Her mother often brought her to Saint Clare’s tomb and prayed there with devotion. Once the girl lay all night before the tomb. Then she broke into a sweat, and the swellings began to soften. In time they disappeared without a trace.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 86. https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A boy taken by a wolf is found alive

Wolves were attacking people in the countryside. A woman named Bona lived at Monte Galliano near Assisi, and she had already lost one son to them. Then a wolf seized her other son by the neck and carried him off to the woods. She cried out to Saint Clare: “Holy and glorious virgin Clare, give me back my poor son…” Her neighbors ran after the wolf and found the child in the woods, with a dog beside him licking his wounds. Bona went with her neighbors to Saint Clare’s tomb, showed the boy’s wounds, and gave thanks to God and to Saint Clare.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, pp. 87–88 (our modern English). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A wolf lets go of a girl from Cannara

A girl from Cannara was sitting in a field in daylight, combing a woman’s hair. A wolf came near, and the girl thought it was a dog. Then it seized her head in its jaws and carried her off. The woman cried out, “Help, Saint Clare, help! I commend this girl to you.” The girl herself scolded the wolf: “Will you carry me any further, thief, when I have been commended to so great a virgin?” The wolf set her gently on the ground and ran away.

Source: Legend of Saint Clare, tr. Robinson, p. 89 (our modern English). https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfSaintClare

A note on these accounts: we read the sworn testimony of 1253 in a modern Spanish translation, and we made our English from it. The Legend was written by an author who read that testimony and spoke with Saint Clare’s companions, so many accounts appear in both.
