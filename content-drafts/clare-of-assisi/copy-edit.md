## Listed for the checker, not changed

1. **Which papal visit is the scene?** "He visited Saint Clare a few days after he arrived, and he came again in August, a few days before her death. He came to her bed and held out his hand…" The reader cannot tell if the hand, the foot, and the pardon belong to the April visit or the August one. "That same day she received Communion" depends on the answer. A fact is needed to fix it.
2. **Quotation that reads oddly:** "Do not weep, daughters, for one who will win." "Win" is flat for the Legend's "triumph" (or the Latin's word). Please check the translation; I did not touch it.
3. **"Saint Agnes had come home after thirty years"** (chapter 12). Chapter 11 says "More than thirty years had passed". She left about 1219 and came back early in 1253. Should this be "after more than thirty years"?
4. **"Under her fine clothes she wore a rough white cloth."** "wore a cloth" is odd English. The source word (a hair cloth? an undergarment?) is needed to fix it without adding a fact.
5. **"She called it the privilege of poverty."** Please confirm that the sources put this name in Saint Clare's mouth; otherwise "It is called…" is safer.
6. **Left on purpose:** "She would not accept it." comes three sentences before "She said no." in the same scene. This tells the outcome twice, but it is the writer's build-up to her answer to the Pope, so I left it.

## Resolved by the checker (draft 5, 2026-10-06)

1. **Which papal visit?** The hand, foot, pardon, and Communion belong to the second visit, in August. The Process (W3.24) puts the same words of Saint Clare ("I have received him today in the Sacrament and I have seen his Vicar") "a few days before" her death. The Legend (pp. 65-66) tells the visit and the Communion "on that day". Robinson's note 153 gives two visits: a few days after the Pope arrived, and about two days before her death. The text now says "On that second visit he came to her bed".
2. **"Do not weep, daughters, for one who will win."** The Latin of this chapter is missing from our saved Acta Sanctorum text (the OCR dropped it). Robinson (p. 63) gives "for her who will conquer", and Gilliat-Smith (p. 47) gives "for her who shall presently conquer". Both agree, so the text now reads "for her who will conquer".
3. **Thirty years.** Saint Agnes left about 1219 (Chérancé p. 81; Robinson note 154) and came home early in 1253; Chérancé p. 87 says "for more than thirty years". Chapter 12 now says "after more than thirty years".
4. **The rough white cloth.** The Process (W20.4) says "una estameña [o cilicio] de color blanco": a coarse white cloth garment, with the translator's gloss "or hair shirt". The Legend (p. 9) says she wore "a little hair shirt" under her fine clothes. The text now says "a coarse white garment, a kind of hair shirt", and note 5 cites Legend pp. 8-9.
5. **"The privilege of poverty" as her own name.** No source puts the name in her mouth. The Legend (p. 26) says she petitioned for "the privilege of Poverty"; the Process (W3.14, W12.6) uses the name in the witnesses' voice. "She called it" is now "She asked for the privilege of poverty".
6. **Repeated refusal lines.** "She would not accept it." is cut. The paragraph now builds from Saint Francis's warning to "She said no." once.
