## The copy editor's six items (resolved; details in `copy-edit.md`)

1. The hand, foot, pardon, and Communion are the second visit, in August (Process W3.24 "a few days before" her death). Text: "On that second visit he came to her bed".
2. "Do not weep, daughters, for one who will win" is now "for her who will conquer" (Robinson p. 63 and Gilliat-Smith p. 47 both say "conquer").
3. "after thirty years" is now "after more than thirty years" (Chérancé p. 87).
4. "a rough white cloth" is now "a coarse white garment, a kind of hair shirt" (W20.4 "estameña [o cilicio] de color blanco"; Legend p. 9).
5. "She called it the privilege of poverty" was not in any source. Now "She asked for the privilege of poverty".
6. "She would not accept it." is cut; the refusal is told once, "She said no."

## Errors of fact fixed

1. **"The mayor sent knights and armed men"** (ch. 13, n. 97). The Legend and the Latin say the Podestà came himself with them ("Accurrit Potestas cum caterva militum"). Now "The mayor came with".
2. **Letter line tied to the altar at San Paolo** (ch. 3, n. 19). "What she had learned at that altar" was an added cause. The line about the clothed and the naked is from the First Letter §27 to Saint Agnes of Prague, where it speaks of giving up wealth. Now "Many years later she wrote to Saint Agnes of Prague, and her words fit that day", and the quotation stands whole.
3. **"What you hold, may you always hold"** (ch. 9, n. 61). The Latin "quod tenes teneas" has no "always" (Mueller: "continue to hold"). Now "continue to hold".
4. **Testament quotation** (ch. 3, n. 24). The Latin has "famosa et sancta conversatione" ("renowned and holy way of life"); "renowned" had been dropped. Restored.
5. **Pope's margin note** (ch. 12, n. 91). "Known" was weak for "manifestis" and "ad instar" was dropped. Gilliat-Smith p. 274 gives "protectori monasterii" without a question mark. Now "For reasons clear to me and to the protector of the monastery, let it be made in the same form."
6. **"Her sisters sent their greetings"** (ch. 11, n. 76). The Fourth Letter §38 says the sisters ask for Saint Agnes of Prague's prayers. Now told so.
7. **"A few lines in her own hand"** (ch. 4, n. 36). Chérancé p. 85 says "a phrase signed by her hand". Now "a line".
8. **"The Pope had come and gone"** (ch. 11, n. 80). He stayed in Assisi until October (Robinson n. 166). Now "The Pope had visited her".
9. **"His cardinals had stood around him like disciples"** (ch. 11, n. 79). The Legend p. 64 says only that the cardinals are like disciples to the Pope, who stands in the Lord's place. Now told so, with no added gesture.
10. **"Failed in some small way"** (ch. 12, n. 95). The Process (W3.23) says "en algo", not "small". Now "in some way".
11. **"He brought it to the room where she lay"** (ch. 12, n. 92). The scene card lists the room as not in the sources. Now "to her bed, with her daughters around her".
12. **"Her family could see only loss"** (ch. 3). The sources give their opposition, not their thoughts. Softened to "saw only loss".
13. **"She lived it every day"** (ch. 7, n. 50). An absolute claim beyond W3.14. Now "she kept to it".
14. **Count in "How This Life Was Researched".** "Ten main sources" did not match the Sources list (eleven entries). Now "eleven sources, listed below".
15. **Notes.** Note 5: Legend pp. 8-9 (the hair shirt is on p. 9). Note 12: added Gilliat-Smith pp. 51, 53 (they write "cathedral"). Note 15: added Robinson n. 104 (Benedictine nuns, near Bastia). Note 42: added Celano §108 (Saint Francis asks to be taken to the Porziuncola to die). Note 96: Robinson n. 159 (the day of death), not n. 179 (the feast).
16. **Note on the Sources.** "Robinson doubts the printed text" is now "Robinson notes doubts about the printed text" (he reports Sbaralea's doubt in n. 114). "Gilliat-Smith says only that … believed" is now "writes that … was believed" (he says both "believed" p. 116 and "once belonged" p. 274).
