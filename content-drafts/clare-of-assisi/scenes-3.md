# Scene cards, group 3 — Saint Clare of Assisi

Keys as in `notes.md`. Files read are in `sources/`. S4 is facts-only (our own words; one short quotation). S1 and S3 are public-domain. S1 is an old English translation, so each quotation from it has a `plain:` line. S1 OCR joins and fixes are noted.

## SC-11 — The Friday before, to Monday 11 August 1253 (San Damiano)
- Status: full
- When and where: Friday evening ("el viernes, al anochecer"), then the Monday after ("el lunes siguiente") [S4 W11.4]. Saint Clare died 11 August 1253 [S1 note 179; S2 pp.244-245]. The Legend says "the day after that of Blessed Laurence" [S1 p.71]. Place: San Damiano, the room where she lay ("la habitación en que yacía") [S4 W11.4]. The weekday and the date are not matched in any source; do not state both as one proven fact.
- Who was there: Sister Amada, her niece, alone with her once [S4 W4.1, W4.19]. Sister Benvenuta di Diambra, who sat at the bed with other sisters [S4 W11.1, W11.3]. Sister Anastasia, who asked her a question [S4 W11.3, W3.22]. Sister Felipa [S4 W3]. Sister Inés (Agnes) of Opórtulo, daughter of messer Opórtulo (W10; not Clare's sister Agnes) near the end [S4 W10.1, W10.10]. In the Legend: Sister Agnes, Brother Rainaldo, Brother Juniper, Brothers Angelo and Leo [S1 pp.67-70].
- What happened, in order:
  1. The Friday before death, alone with Amada, Saint Clare said: "¿Ves tú al Rey de la gloria, al que yo estoy viendo?" (Do you see the King of glory, whom I am seeing?). "Y esto lo dijo más veces" (she said this more than once). "Pocos días después expiró" [S4 W4.19].
  2. Between Friday and Saturday, three days before her death, Benvenuta sat by the bed weeping. "Sin que nadie le hablase" (with no one speaking to her), Saint Clare began to "encomendar su alma" (commend her soul) [S4 W11.3]. Felipa gives the same words for "una noche, al comienzo del sábado" (a night, at the start of Saturday) [S4 W3.20].
  3. Anastasia asked to whom she spoke. Answer: "Hablo a mi alma bendita" (W11.3), or "Hablo a mi alma" (W3.22).
  4. That Friday evening Benvenuta, awake, saw a great crowd of virgins in white with crowns come in at the door. One taller one had a gold ball like a censer on her crown, and its light seemed to fill the house. She covered Saint Clare with a very fine cloth, bent her face to Saint Clare's face "o quizá sobre su pecho" (or perhaps her chest). All vanished [S4 W11.4].
  5. Felipa: all that night Saint Clare preached to the sisters, then made a confession Felipa had never heard the like of, because she feared she had failed the faith of baptism [S4 W3.23].
  6. Inés of Opórtulo: Saint Clare urged her to stay in prayer, asked her to say the prayer of the five wounds, and kept the Passion and the name of Jesus on her lips. "Casi la última palabra" to her was "Preciosa es a los ojos del Señor la muerte de sus santos" [S4 W10.10].
  7. Legend: Agnes begged her not to leave; Saint Clare said she would soon follow [S1 p.67; paraphrase]. She then said "no suffering hath troubled me" to Brother Rainaldo [S1 p.68].
- Details: seventeen days without food [S1 p.68]. Juniper's "flaming words" [S1 p.69]. Leo "kissed the bed", Angelo comforted the others [S1 p.69]. Next day: the Pope and Curia came and the Office of Virgins was asked for; burial at San Giorgio [S1 pp.72-73].
- Inner life: she called her soul "blessed" and thanked God who made her [S4 W3.20; S1 p.70]. She spoke of the Trinity so deeply that the sisters "no la podían entender bien" (could not follow it well) [S4 W3.20].
- Testimony (public-domain, Legend of Saint Clare, S1 p.70; author "ascribed" to Thomas of Celano, notes §9):
  > "But the most holy virgin adverting to herself, spoke to her own soul softly: "Go forth," she said, "without fear. For thou hast a good guide for thy journey. Go forth," she said, "for He who created thee hath sanctified thee and, protecting thee always, loveth thee with a love as tender as that of a mother for her son. Blessed be Thou, Lord," she said, "who hast created me." When one of the Sisters asked her to whom she was speaking, Cläre replied: "I am speaking to my blessed soul." Nor was that glorious guide far distant. For, turning to a certain daughter, she asked: "O daughter, dost thou see the King of Glory whom I behold?""
  (OCR fix: "her- seif" joined as "herself".)
  plain: The most holy virgin turned to herself and spoke softly to her own soul: "Go out without fear. You have a good guide for your journey. Go out, for He who made you has made you holy. He guards you always and loves you as tenderly as a mother loves her son. Blessed are You, Lord, who made me." A sister asked whom she was speaking to. Saint Clare said: "I am speaking to my blessed soul." Then she turned to one daughter and asked: "Daughter, do you see the King of glory, whom I see?"
  Sworn witness, S4 W11.3 (facts-only; one short quotation): "Vete en paz, pues tendrás buena escolta" (Go in peace, for you will have a good escort), Benvenuta di Diambra.
- What changed: she died, and the town and the Pope came to San Damiano the next day.
- Not in the sources: the room's name; who sat where; weather or the hour of death; what Amada answered; that Agnes (Legend) and Amada were the same person or in the same hour; a day count of "Saturday, Sunday". The Legend puts the "King of Glory" question to "a certain daughter"; the Process puts it to Amada alone. The Legend puts the virgin vision on a weeping sister; the Process names Benvenuta and says she told no one until 1253 ("no se lo había revelado a nadie nunca hasta ahora", W11.4). Witness 3 is Felipa de messer Leonardo de Gislerio [S4 W3.1], not Pacifica (Pacifica is W1). The intro list of S4 and the witness headings do not match in order (list: 7 Bienvenida de Opórtulo, 10 Cecilia, 11 Balbina; headings: W7 Balbina, W10 Inés de Opórtulo, W11 Benvenuta di Diambra). Use the headings. Notes and outline must be corrected.
- Sources: S4 intro (witness list), W3.20-23, W4.1, W4.19, W10.10, W11.1-4; S1 pp.66-74, notes 153, 154, 164, 179; S2 pp.244-245.

## SC-12 — 24-29 November 1253 (San Damiano; San Paolo, Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: Bull *Gloriosus Deus* "Dadas en San Juan de Letrán, quince días antes de las calendas de noviembre [18 de octubre], en el año onceno" [S4 bull §6; S1 note 178; S2 pp.244-245]. Hearings: 24 November in the cloister of San Damiano [S4 intro §7]; 28 November in the infirmary and in the cloister building [S4 W15]; 29 November "en la iglesia de San Pablo [de Asís]" (the bracket is the translator's) [S4 W20.1].
- Who was there: Bishop Bartholomew of Spoleto (Bartholomew Accorombani, S1 note 178). At the first sitting: Leonardo archdeacon of Spoleto, Jacobo "arcipreste de Trevi", Friars Leo, Angelo of Rieti and Marcos, and the notary Martín [S4 intro]. On 29 November the same Leonardo and "don Jacobo, párroco de Trevi" [S4 W20.1]. The witnesses are twenty; thirteen nuns are listed for the first day, and Lucia of Rome is added [S4 intro]. Sister Agnes died 16 November 1253, so she is not among them; Pacifica says she "hacía poco había pasado de esta vida" [S1 note 164; S4 W1.15].
- What happened, in order:
  1. 18 October 1253: Pope Innocent IV orders the bishop to inquire into her "vida, conversión, conducta religiosa" and miracles, by the interrogatory sent with the bull, and to send all of it "fielmente transcrito en escritura pública, y con tu sello" (faithfully copied in a public document, under your seal) [S4 bull §5]. The interrogatory is lost [S1 note 178].
  2. The bishop went in person to San Damiano and took the testimonies [S4 §7].
  3. 29 November, San Pablo: Juan Ventura de Asís swore. He lived in Saint Clare's father's house as "hombre de armas de la casa" (a man at arms of the household), when she was a girl of about eighteen [S4 W20.1-2].
  4. He told of "un ultramontano [francés] atacado de furia, o bien endemoniado" (a man from beyond the mountains, a Frenchman by the translator's bracket, seized by fury or possessed), carried "atado con cuerdas" (tied with ropes) to the tomb, where he was healed "repentinamente" (suddenly) [S4 W20.9].
  5. Asked, he did not know the man's name, "pues no era de estas partes" (he was not from these parts); he believed it was "septiembre pasado" and did not remember the day; "lo vieron todos los de la plaza" (everyone in the square saw it) and ran with him to the tomb [S4 W20.9].
- Details: the Legend tells of "a certain French youth attached to the Curia", "tied down on a bier with strong ropes", carried by his countrymen, "freed" in front of the tomb "through the faith of those who had brought him" [S1 p.79]. The Curia stayed in Assisi 12 August to 4 October 1253 [S1 note 166]. The tie between W20.9 and the Legend is our inference; no source joins them.
- Words spoken: none quoted in the source for the man or the crowd.
- Testimony (public-domain): S1 p.79 above, in the translator's English: "tied down on a bier with strong ropes and was carried by his countrymen against his will to the Church of St Cläre, and placed before her tomb; and forthwith he was freed in a most wonderful manner through the faith of those who had brought him." plain: He was tied to a stretcher with strong ropes. His countrymen carried him, against his will, to Saint Clare's church and put him in front of her tomb. At once he was freed, in a wonderful way, through the faith of those who brought him. Sworn witness (facts-only, short): "atado con cuerdas, al sepulcro ... a un ultramontano [francés] atacado de furia, o bien endemoniado, y allí quedó curado", Juan Ventura, S4 W20.9.
- What changed: the case after her death had a sworn record to send to Rome. Alexander IV canonized her in 1255 (S1 p.91). One source adds that Innocent IV died (7 December 1254) before the bishop had finished [S2 p.244].
- Not in the sources: that the hearings ended on 29 November (the last date given is 29); the weather; the church interior; how the bishop questioned each witness; whether the records were sealed and sent (the bull asks for it; no source says it was done). The man's name, his date in September, and who held the ropes are unknown. Do not call the man "possessed": the source says "furia, o bien endemoniado" (fury, or else possessed). The witness name is "Juan Ventura de Asís" in the Spanish; the Italian form is not in a source.
- Sources: S4 bull §§1-6, intro §7, W1.15, W15, W20.1-2, 20.9; S1 pp.79, 90-91, notes 164, 166, 178; S2 pp.244-245. S4 is facts-only; our own words except as marked.

## FN-1 — Bull *Clara claris praeclara*
Pope Alexander IV, in the first year of his pontificate, entered her among the saints "apud Anagniam in ecclesia majori" (at Anagni, in the principal church), "anno ... millesimo ducentesimo quinquagesimo quinto" [S3 Legend §71; S1 p.91]. No exact date is sure. Legend author: 11 August 1255 (the second anniversary of her death) [S1 note 180]. Sbaralea's copy, to the bishops of France: 19 October 1255 [S1 note 180; S2 p.245]. Eubel names a copy of 26 September 1255 [S2 p.245]. No original survives [S1 note 180]. The bull gives 12 August as her feast [S2 p.245; S1 note 179]. Praise line: "Clara claris praeclara meritis, magnae in coelo claritate gloriae ac in terra splendore miraculorum sublimium, clare claret" (Clare, bright by her bright merits, shines clearly: great in heaven by the brightness of glory, on earth by the splendor of lofty miracles) [S3 Commentarius §49, from Cherubini's Bullarium I, p.81]. The text is Latin, our translation; the play on "clara" is Clare's name.

## FN-2 — The Rule: poverty chapter and its bull
Chapter VIII, "Of Evangelical Mendicity and of Poverty in Particular" [S1 App. I p.113; Latin, S2 Part II Rule table, ch. VIII §36]. Latin: "Sorores nihil sibi appropri-ent, nee domum, nee locum, nee aliquem rem" (as printed in S2; OCR "nee" for "nec", "aliquem" for "aliquam"): the sisters keep nothing as their own, no house, no place, nothing. Also: "Haec est illa celsitudo altissimae paupertatis, quae vos charissimas sorores meas haeredes, et reginas regni coelorum instituit" (this is the height of the highest poverty that made you, my dearest sisters, heirs and queens of the kingdom of heaven). The bull is *Solet annuere sedes apostolica*, Innocent IV, 9 August 1253, at Cardinal Rainaldo's request [S1 App. I p.96, note 188]. Felipa (W3.32) says a friar brought the sealed letters ("Letras buladas") and she took the bull to her lips; she died "al día siguiente" [S4 W3.32]. S1 p.98 adds that it is "said" the bull was in her hands when she died; the reverse of the parchment has a note that Saint Clare touched and kissed it many times.

## FN-3 — The Order's spread, and Agnes of Prague
The Legend names no house on pp.21-22: it says "cities without number" were adorned with monasteries, and country places too [S1 pp.21-22; Latin not found in S3 in this session]. Note 110 names Princess Agnes of Bohemia, who founded a monastery of Clares at Prague in 1236 [S1 p.142, note 110]. Saint Clare's first letter (1234) is to "dominae Agneti, filiae excellentissimi ac illustrissimi regis Bohemiae" and says her fame reached "fere in toto ... orbe terrarum" (nearly the whole world) [S5-560 §§1, 3]. Houses named in the Process: Val de Gloria in Spello (Pacifica spent a year there to train the sisters, W1.14) and Arezzo (Sister Balbina de Coccorano, W7, spent a year and five months there by Saint Clare's order, W7.11) [S4]. S1 note 154: Agnes, her sister, was sent about 1219 to be abbess at Monticello near Florence.

Found but not assigned: none.
