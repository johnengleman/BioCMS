## Differences from facts.md (for the checker; not changed)

1. **Saracens, Saint Clare's prayer** ("saracens-leave"). The list: "Lord, guard these servants of yours, for I cannot guard them." facts.md item 9: "Does it please you, my Lord, to hand over to the pagans your defenseless servants, whom I have fed with your love? Lord, I beg you, protect these servants of yours. I cannot protect them now." The list's words are a fair translation of Francisca, W9.2 ("Señor, guarda Tú a estas siervas tuyas, pues yo no las puedo guardar"). facts.md gives the Legend's wording (n. 59). Decide which wording the list uses.
2. **Saracens, the voice.** The list: "I will always defend you." facts.md item 9: "I will always protect you." W9.2 has "¡Yo te defenderé siempre!", so the list follows Francisca. Decide.
3. **Saracens, order of actions.** The list says that Saint Clare lay on the ground and that Sister Francisca "was holding her up". The biography says that two sisters held her up and then she lay on the ground. W9.2 does not say that Francisca held her. Check the order, and whether to name Sister Illuminata of Pisa as the second sister.
4. **Hortulana inside a quotation** ("boy-of-perugia-eye"). Saint Clare says, "Take him to my mother, Sister Hortulana, and let her make the sign of the cross over him." facts.md says "Blessed Hortulana". I did not change the quotation. Outside it, I wrote "Blessed Hortulana" twice.
5. **Benvenuta di Diambra, time of the healing.** Before: "about two years before". facts.md: "healed two years before (W11.1, 'she believed')". I changed it to "she believed it had happened two years before" (a word change toward facts.md). W11.1 also says "September last". Confirm.
6. **Juan Ventura** ("man-in-ropes-1253"). facts.md: he "thought it was in September", with no year. The list says "the September after her death", and the heading says "1253". The list also gives a cause: "He did not know the man's name, because he was not from that region." facts.md gives no cause. Check both.
7. **Intro, "three months after her death".** facts.md: death 11 August, hearings 24 to 29 November 1253 (about three and a half months). Probably fine; confirm.

## Sentences listed but not fixed (for the checker)

1. **"Two children are healed of fever."** In the Spanish Process, both boys are sons of "messer Juan de maese Juan": the procurator's son (Felipa, W3.15) and the five-year-old with scrofula (Francisca, W9.6, "de Asís"). These may be one boy or two sons of the same man. Also, the sentence "His father later told the sisters that the boy had been healed at once" comes from Francisca's testimony (W9.6, "en el locutorio"), but the list puts it in the procurator's son's story. Check, and say whether the list should say these are the same father.
2. **Sister Andrea** ("sister-andrea"). "she was strongly tempted to cure it herself". W3.16 says "era muy tentada por querer sanar de ellas" (much tempted by wanting to be healed of them). The word "herself" may add a detail. Check.
3. **Sister Cecilia's cough.** The account ends "Sister Amada told this story", but the Source line also gives Cecilia's own testimony (witness 6, art. 8). Check whether to name both.
4. **Communion quotation** ("communion-as-a-child"). "God has done me such a great kindness today that heaven and earth cannot compare with it." This is W9.10 (Francisca). It is close to facts.md item 14 (after Pope Innocent IV's visit, n. 86), but it is a different occasion in the source. It is not in facts.md. Confirm that the two are kept apart.
5. **Nun of San Paolo.** "While the Pope was still at Lyons" and "the Pope and his cardinals". facts.md names Pope Innocent IV. Add the name if wanted.
6. **French youth.** "carried him ... to the church of Saint Clare". facts.md: the body went to San Giorgio. Check that the name of the church matches the Legend's wording.
7. **Sweet water and Benvenuta.** To use the facts.md names, I wrote "Sister Agnes of Opportulo was the daughter of Opportulo" and "Sister Benvenuta di Diambra was the daughter of Diambra". These sentences now explain the name. If the checker prefers, drop the second half.
8. **Dropsy, procurator, cloister, censer, Blessed Sacrament.** I added short explanations of these words: "Dropsy is an illness that makes the body swell with water"; "a procurator, a man who handled their business"; "the cloister, the enclosed court inside the convent" (the biography's words); "a censer for burning incense"; "It was the Body of our Lord Jesus Christ" (W9.2: "santísimo Sacramento del Cuerpo de nuestro Señor Jesucristo"). Confirm the dropsy and procurator explanations.
