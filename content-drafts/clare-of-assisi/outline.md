# Saint Clare of Assisi — outline v2 (tier B, standard)

Keys as in `notes.md` and the cards (`scenes-1.md` to `scenes-3.md`). S4 is facts-only: our words, short quotations marked "(our translation from the Spanish)". Use the quotation wordings in `facts.md`. The Pope's offer is S3 §14, not §13.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Clare wanted to be poor with "Christ the Poor One," whose poor mother laid him in a narrow manger [S1 pp.25-26]. First her noble family stood in the way [S4 W18.2, W19.2], then popes who loved her and urged property for safety [S3 §14; S4 W2.22], and at the end time itself: the Rule that wrote her poverty down still lacked the Pope's seal while she was dying [S1 pp.63-64, 96-98; S4 W3.32]. By prayer and her own refusals she kept that poverty for forty-two years, and the seal reached her at the very end.

Why it was beautiful to her: Christ himself was poor, from the manger on. Her own words, later: "a great and praiseworthy exchange" [S5-560 §30]; "O astonishing poverty! The King of angels, the Lord of heaven and earth, is laid in a manger!" [S5-572 §§19-21]. The reader first hears this in chapter 2, when she sells her inheritance and leaves home.

Famous for founding the Poor Ladies of San Damiano and keeping their poverty against the offers of popes [S1 pp.17-18, 26-27].

Set apart: she asked a Pope for the right to own nothing, which no one had asked before [S1 p.26]. A knight's daughter meant for "great and powerful men," she chose to live on begged scraps [S4 W19.1-2, W3.13].

## 2. Portrait

A strong will with a light heart. Always glad in the Lord [S4 W3.6]; she seemed to laugh at her penances [S1 p.32]; she called a cat a "rascal" [S4 W9.8]. She led by serving, giving orders "with much fear and humility" and mostly doing the task herself [S4 W1.10].

Her fault was severity to herself. The sisters grieved at her fasts and Pacifica wept [S4 W1.7], until Saint Francis and the bishop ordered her to eat [S4 W1.8]. In 1238 she warned Agnes of Prague against "indiscreet and impossible" fasting [S5-569 §40]. She was stubborn even before a Pope: she "resisted with a very strong spirit" [S3 §14].

Life with God: night prayer on the ground, a stone for a pillow [S4 W1.7]; praise "for all things and in all things" [S4 W14.9]. She spoke to God as a companion: "Lord God, here I am, left alone with you in this place" [S3 §29]. At the end, to her soul: "Blessed are you, Lord, for you have made me" [S1 p.70].

## 3. The opening

Hook: a broken picture. It shows the morning after the flight; chapter 2 tells the night from inside her and does not retell the morning.

> In the morning, many people in Assisi marvelled at a door. It belonged to a knight's house, and no one used it. Heavy logs and a stone column had barred it, and many men could hardly have moved them. Now it stood open, and Saint Clare, the daughter of the house, was gone. She was about eighteen, and her family had planned a splendid marriage for her. In the night she had moved the logs by herself, because she wanted the one thing their wealth could not give her: to be poor.

Facts: S4 W13.1, W19.1-2. Neither the opening nor the summary names the Pope's offer, the illness, the seal, or the canonization.

Thesis: Saint Clare led the sisters of San Damiano for forty-two years, and by 1253 her Order had spread to "almost every region of the world" [S1 p.18; S4 W1.3; S4 bull §1]. Two popes granted her the right to own nothing, Innocent III in his own hand and Gregory IX on 17 September 1228 [S1 p.26, note 116]. In her last days her Rule, built on that poverty, was approved [S1 pp.96-97, note 188]. Chapters 6, 9, 12, and 13 answer it.

Ending: chapter 13 keeps the inquiry, canonization, feast, and 1260 short, then closes on the parchment found at Santa Chiara in 1893 and its note in a hand of her time: "Blessed Clare touched this and kissed it out of devotion many, many times" [S1 pp.97-98; S2 p.274]. Then one or two sentences: to anyone pressed to trade what they love for safety, and to anyone ill and left alone, she offers her Christmas words that the Lord did not leave her when others did [S3 §29]. The death scene says only that she kissed the seal [S4 W3.32].

## 4. Chapters

1. **A Knight's Daughter Meets Saint Francis** — bridge: the voice to her mother, her name [S3 §2; S1 p.7]; SC-01. Pull: Holy Week was coming, and she asked him what she should do.
2. **Saint Clare Leaves Home at Night** — SC-02. Pull: her relatives soon learned where she had gone [S1 p.16].
3. **Her Family Comes to Take Her Back** — SC-03; bridge: Saint Agnes follows, the twelve men at Panzo, one paragraph [S1 pp.40-43]; San Damiano [S1 p.17]. Pull: she would live within those walls for the rest of her life, forty-two years.
4. **Abbess of San Damiano Against Her Will** — bridge: Saint Francis "almost forced her" [S4 W1.6]; the foot she kissed, one sentence [S4 W10.6]; Hortulana enters [S4 W1.5]; SC-04. Pull: Saint Francis had promised to watch over the sisters always.
5. **Saint Francis Dies** — SC-05. Pull: soon a Pope would ask her to let that poverty go.
6. **She Refuses the Pope's Offer** — SC-06. Pull: two years later the same Pope took the friars away.
7. **She Sends the Friars Away** — SC-07. Pull: in the years that followed, the emperor's soldiers came to the walls of San Damiano.
8. **Soldiers Inside the Convent** — bridge: Vitalis and the ashes [S4 W3.19, W9.3]; SC-08. Pull: far away, a king's daughter had chosen the same poverty.
9. **Letters to a Princess in Prague** — no scene; Letters 1-3 [S5-560, -563, -569]. Pull: nearly fifteen years passed before she wrote again, and by then she could not leave her bed.
10. **A Rule Written, Not Yet Sealed** — bridges: nearly thirty years ill, spinning, the cat [S4 W1.17, W9.8-9]; the San Paolo nun's vision, "She cannot die until the Lord comes with his disciples" [S1 p.63]; Cardinal Rainaldo's visit, 8 September 1252, and the Rule drawn up 16 September, waiting for the Pope [S1 pp.63-64, notes 151, 189]; SC-09. Pull: in late April 1253 the Pope came to Assisi, and the Rule still lacked his seal.
11. **The Pope Comes to Her Bed** — bridge: Letter 4, the manger, "your poor little mother"; Saint Agnes called home [S5-572 §§19-21, 33, 38; S1 note 154]; SC-10. Pull: he blessed her, but his seal had not come.
12. **The Seal Arrives, and Saint Clare Dies** — SC-11. Pull: within months a bishop came to San Damiano to question her sisters under oath.
13. **Saint Clare Is Declared a Saint** — short: funeral at San Giorgio, Saint Agnes's death, SC-12, canonization, feast, 1260, the parchment, today.

## 5. The scenes

Turning points told from inside her: SC-02, SC-03, SC-06, SC-07, SC-11. SC-08 and SC-09 are told in full through her own prayers.

Relationships and their endings:
- **Saint Francis:** SC-01 to SC-05; it lands at her deathbed, "through his servant Francis, no suffering has troubled me" [S1 p.68], with his companions Leo and Angelo there [S1 p.69], and she is buried where he was first laid [S1 pp.72-73].
- **Saint Agnes, her sister:** follows her [S1 pp.40-43]; called home in 1253 [S1 note 154]; the deathbed promise [S1 p.67]; dies ninety-seven days later [S1 note 164].
- **Cardinal Rainaldo:** writes her Rule [S1 pp.63-64], preaches at her funeral, and as Alexander IV declares her a saint [S1 pp.72-73, 91].
- **Agnes of Prague:** the letters end "remember your poor little mother" [S5-572 §33].

### SC-01 — Before 1211, about 17 (Assisi)
- What happens: relatives press a great marriage; she preaches contempt of the world to them. With Bona she goes in secret to Saint Francis; "his words seemed to her like fire."
- Why it is here: the desire stirs; Saint Francis enters her life.
- Have: S4 W17.3, W18.2, W19.1-2; S3 §5; S1 pp.9-11.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S4 W18.2; S1 p.11.
- Where to look: as Have.

### SC-02 — Palm Sunday and the night after, about 1211 (cathedral; her house; the Porziuncola)
- What happens: she sells her inheritance for the poor and will not sell it cheap to her relatives. The bishop puts a palm in her hands. At night, fearing to be stopped, she moves the barred door "with the help of Jesus Christ." At the Porziuncola, Saint Francis cuts her hair.
- Why it is here: the first great decision, and why poverty was beautiful to her.
- Have: S1 pp.13-14, 25-26; S3 §§7-8; S4 W12.3-4, W13.1, W13.11.
- Need: (1) S1 pp.25-26 and W12.3: was the sale before or after the flight? (2) S5-560: the Latin lines on poverty near §30, for her own voice. (3) S1 App. I p.110: does Rule ch. VI open with her own account of her conversion?
- Testimony: S4 W13.1 (Cristiana); S1 p.14.
- Where to look: as Have, plus S5-560 and S1 p.110.

### SC-03 — After the flight (San Paolo, near Bastia)
- What happens: relatives come with force and promises; she grips the altar cloths, bares her shorn head, and says nothing will pull her from Christ's service.
- Why it is here: her first public stand.
- Have: S3 §9; S1 p.16; S4 W12.4, W18.3, W20.6.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S1 p.16.
- Where to look: as Have.

### SC-04 — The hair shirt lent for three days (San Damiano)
- What happens: one scene for all her penances: Agnes of Opportulo borrows the horsehair shirt and returns it after three days. One sentence for the fasts and Pacifica's tears. Saint Francis and the bishop order her to eat; she obeys.
- Why it is here: her fault, and Saint Francis's care.
- Have: S4 W1.7-8, W2.8, W10.1; S1 pp.30-32; S3 §§17-18.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S1 pp.30-31.
- Where to look: as Have.

### SC-05 — Saint Francis's last wish and his death
- What happens: Saint Francis promises to care for the sisters as for his friars. Shortly before his death he writes: never leave this poverty "because of anyone's teaching or advice." Then he dies.
- Why it is here: it ends the friendship and leaves the poverty on her shoulders; her deathbed words in SC-11 depend on it.
- Have: Rule ch. VI [S1 App. I pp.110-111]; his body first laid at San Giorgio [S1 pp.72-73]; "around the time Saint Francis passed from this life" [S4 W6.12].
- Need: (1) date and place of his death from a saved source. (2) Was his body carried by San Damiano for the sisters to see? (3) Any act or word of hers at his death. If (2) and (3) are missing, tell the last wish and his death only, and merge the chapter into chapter 4. Invent no grief.
- Where to look: S2 Part I ch.IV-VI (not read); S1 notes 106-140; S3 Commentarius.

### SC-06 — The Pope's offer (San Damiano)
- What happens: Gregory IX comes in person and urges property for the dangerous times. She resists; he offers release from her vow; "Holy Father, in no way do I ever want to be released from following Christ." He gives way: the privilege of 17 September 1228.
- Why it is here: the central refusal. Saint Francis had warned against anyone's advice; now the advice came from a Pope who loved her.
- Have: S3 §14; S1 pp.26-27, note 116; S4 W1.13, W2.22, W3.14 ("fearing to lose it").
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S1 pp.26-27.
- Where to look: as Have. Tell it once only.

### SC-07 — 1230 (San Damiano)
- What happens: Gregory forbids friars to visit without his leave. She sighs, "Let him take all the friars from us…," and sends back even the friars who begged bread. The Pope relents.
- Why it is here: Saint Francis's promise to care for the sisters is threatened; she defends it with her own poverty.
- Have: S1 p.60, note 146; S3 §37.
- Need: (1) S3 §37 full Latin. (2) S1 p.60: how and to whom the Pope relented.
- Where to look: S1 p.60; S3 §37.

### SC-08 — A Friday in September, about Terce (San Damiano)
- What happens: Saracens in the cloister. Ill, she is led to the refectory door with the Sacrament and prays face down; a child-like voice answers, "I will always protect you." She orders silence while she lives.
- Why it is here: her life with God; courage for her daughters.
- Have: S3 §§21-22; S1 pp.36-37; S4 W3.18, W9.2.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S1 p.37; S4 W9.2.
- Where to look: as Have.

### SC-09 — Christmas night 1252 (her sickbed)
- What happens: left alone, she prays "Lord God, here I am, left alone with you in this place," hears the friars' Matins at San Francesco, and sees the manger.
- Why it is here: prayer in illness; loneliness answered; the manger of chapter 2 returns.
- Have: S3 §29; S1 pp.47-48, note 131; S4 W3.30, W4.16, W7.9.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: S1 pp.47-48.
- Where to look: as Have.

### SC-10 — Spring and summer 1253 (San Damiano)
- What happens: Innocent IV at her bed; she kisses his hand and foot and asks pardon; "I wish to God I had no more need of forgiveness." To her sisters: heaven and earth could not repay this gift. The nun's vision is fulfilled.
- Why it is here: the race: the Pope is at her bed, and the seal is still not given.
- Have: S1 pp.64-66, notes 152-153; S4 W3.24.
- Need: (1) S1 pp.64-65: did she ask him for the confirmation, and what did he answer?

### SC-11 — The last days to Monday 11 August 1253 (San Damiano)
- What happens: seventeen days without food; to a friar, the words about Saint Francis; to Saint Agnes, "You will come to the Lord soon after me." Friday: "Do you see the King of glory?"; her words to her soul; Benvenuta di Diambra's vision. A friar arrives with the sealed letters; she kisses the seal. Her last words, then death.
- Why it is here: the climax; seal and death answer the question together.
- Have: S1 pp.67-71, 96-98 (the Pope's own-hand note on the bull); S4 W3.20-23, W3.32, W4.19, W10.10, W11.3-4.

### SC-12 — 29 November 1253 (San Paolo, Assisi)
- What happens: Juan Ventura, once a man-at-arms in her father's house, swears to a stranger carried bound to her tomb and healed there.
- Why it is here: the case after death; one paragraph.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** four letters to Agnes of Prague, 1234-1253 [S5] (ch. 9, 11); the Rule, approved 9 August 1253 [S1 App. I pp.96-113] (ch. 10, 12).
- **What she changed:** the Order of the Poor Ladies, "cities without number" [S1 pp.21-22]; Spello and Arezzo [S4 W1.14, W7.11]; the Privilege of Poverty [S1 p.26, note 116]; a Rule built on poverty [S1 p.113] (ch. 6, 9, 12, 13).
- **Why famous:** the sworn testimony of 1253 [S4]; cures at her tomb [S1 pp.76-90]; Assisi's procession of thanks each 22 June [S1 note 123] (ch. 8, 13).
- **Path to sainthood:** inquiry ordered 18 October 1253, held 24-29 November [S4]; canonized at Anagni, 1255, by Alexander IV [S1 p.91]; feast first 12 August, today 11 August [S1 note 179; facts.md]; body moved to Santa Chiara, 1260 [S1 note 163] (ch. 13).

**Fact needs:**
1. Saint Francis's death and her response. Look: S2 Part I ch.IV-VI; S1 notes 106-140; S3 Commentarius.
2. Her Testament: her own account of her conversion and of Saint Francis. Not saved or read; the notes call it contested [notes §9]. Look: S2 Part II; S3 Commentarius; otherwise a public-domain edition, which is new research and needs approval. If used, attribute it.
3. The Order living today, from a saved source. Look: S1 introduction and notes; S2 Part II, last chapter.

## 8. What we leave out

- Oil jar, bread, Brother Stephen, Sister Andrea, the heavy door, the cures: miracles section.
- The Porziuncola meal and fire: legend (S2).
- The nursing vision of Saint Francis (W3.29): one line cannot explain it.
- The Holy Thursday trance and the boy at the sermon: they do not serve the question.
- Panzo as a full scene: it repeats San Paolo. One paragraph.
- The list of penances: one scene and one sentence.
- The Pope's offer in the opening or summary: told once, in ch. 6.
- Woman of Pisa, Hugolino's son, the foot water: minor or second-hand.
- Patron of television, the monstrance, 1240 and 1241: memory only.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth "about 1193" [S2]; flight "about 1211" (Robinson: 1212); Holy Monday (W2.1) goes to the Note on the Sources.
- Bull dated 9 August 1253; Felipa says she died the day after kissing it (W3.32); S2 says two days. Write "near the end."
- Witness 3 is Felipa. Saint Agnes and Beatrice (W12) are two sisters. Agnes of Opportulo and Agnes of Prague are other women. Two Benvenutas: di Diambra (W11), of Perugia (W2).
- Agnes of Opportulo borrowed the horsehair shirt, not the pigskin one.
- No year for the Saracens or Vitalis. Robinson's 1228 for Gregory's visit is his inference.
- "King of glory": to Amada alone (W4.19); to "a certain daughter" in the Legend.
- One kiss of the seal at death (W3.32); "many, many times" belongs to the parchment note only.
