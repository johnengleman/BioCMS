## SC-09 — The Grand Prince asks, and the letter reaches the army (1380)
- Status: full (with the Kulikovo question answered below).
- When and where: The Life gives no year, no day and no place for the visit. It says only that Dmitri "came to the elder" and then "left the monastery" [S5 ch. "О победе над Мамаем"; S1 ch.22]. The year 1380 comes from scholars (notes S3). The Life does not say "cell".
- Who was there: Grand Prince Dmitri Ivanovich; Sergius; the brothers (they pray with Sergius at the hour of battle); a messenger.
- What happened, in order:
  1. News comes that Mamai has gathered "all the horde of the godless Tatars" and is marching on Russia; the people are in great fear [S5].
  2. Dmitri, who trusts Sergius as a man of virtue and the gift of prophecy, asks whether Sergius commands him to go out against them [S5].
  3. Sergius blesses him, "arms him with prayer", and speaks the words below. Dmitri vows a monastery of the Mother of God if God helps him, takes the blessing, and leaves in haste [S5].
  4. Dmitri gathers all his men. They see the Tatar army, "very many", stop in doubt, and many are afraid [S5].
  5. At that hour a runner ("борзоходец") arrives with a letter from Sergius [S5].
  6. Dmitri and the whole army take courage. Dmitri prays, and the battle starts. The Tatars are beaten [S5].
  7. Sergius, praying with the brothers, sees the battle from many days' walk away. A little later he tells the brothers of the victory and names the fallen Russians, and offers prayers for them [S5].
  8. Dmitri comes back, thanks Sergius, gives a large gift, reminds him of the vow. Sergius finds a place on the river Dubenka and builds a church of the Dormition. He makes Savva hegumen [S5].
- Details: The letter is carried by a "борзоходец", a runner, on foot. The Life gives no name for him.
- Words spoken: see Testimony. Plain English, our own:
  - Sergius: "It is right, my lord, that you care for the Christian flock God has given you. Go against the godless. With God helping you, you will win, and you will come back to your own land safe, with great honor." Note: the Old Russian has "Богу помагающу ти" (God helping you), not a flat promise. Modern translators read it as "if God helps" (Bulanin) or "with God's help" (azbyka).
  - Dmitri: "If God helps me, Father, I will build a monastery in the name of the Most Pure Mother of God."
  - Sergius's letter: "Without any doubt, my lord, go boldly against their fierceness. Do not be afraid at all. God will surely help you."
  - Dmitri's prayer: "Great God, who made heaven and earth, be my helper against the enemies of your holy name." (Old Russian: «Боже великый, сътворивый небо и землю! Помощникь ми буди на противъникы святому ти имени».)
- Inner life: nothing from Sergius about this moment. His prayer is the Life's report only.
- Testimony (Old Russian, public-domain; S5, chapter "О победе еже на Мамаа и о монастыре, иже на Дубенке"; speaker: the biographer reporting Sergius, Dmitri and the letter):
  > Святый же, яко услыша сиа от великого князя, благословивъ его, молитвою въоружив, и рече: «Подобает ти, господине, пещися о врученном от Бога христоименитому стаду. Поиди противу безбожныхь, и Богу помагающу ти, побѣдиши и здравъ въ свое отечьство с великыми похвалами възъвратишися». Великый же князь рече: «Аще ми Богъ поможет, отче, поставлю монастырь въ имя пречистые Богоматере».
  > … И се внезаапу въ той час приспѣ борзоходець с посланием от святого, глаголюще: «Без всякого съмнѣния, господине, съ дръзновениемь поиди противу свирѣпьства их, никакоже ужасатися, всяко поможет ти Богъ».
  > … Зряше издалече, бяше растоаниа мѣстом и многы дни хождениемь, на молитвѣ съ братиею Богу предстоа о бывшей побѣдѣ на поганыхь.
- What changed: The army goes into battle. After the victory Dmitri pays his vow and Dubenka begins.
- The Kulikovo question:
  - The Life (S5, S1) names no battle, no field and no river. It says only "Mamai" and gives no year.
  - The oldest source I read that ties Sergius's blessing to the march that ended at Kulikovo is the Tale of the Rout of Mamai [S6]. It is the long story of Dmitri's victory over Mamai, and in it the prince goes "to the Life-giving Trinity" with his brother Vladimir Andreevich and the Russian princes to get Sergius's blessing. The Tale's editors (BLDR vol. 6) say it was "most likely written in the first quarter of the 15th century"; the surviving copies are of the 16th to 19th centuries, and the text is printed from a copy of the first half of the 16th century (RNB Q.IV.22). That is later than the Life's 1417-18 core, and I give it as a later tie, not as proof.
  - In the Tale: the visit is on a Sunday, the memory of Sts Flor and Lavr. Sergius asks the prince to hear the liturgy and eat at the monastery, and the prince is in a hurry. Sergius sprinkles him and his army with holy water and signs him with a cross on the forehead. He says "Go against the godless Polovtsy [the Tale's word for the Tatars], calling on God", then privately: "You will beat your enemies." The prince asks for two monks, Peresvet and Oslyabya. Sergius sends them with crosses sewn on their schemas.
  - The Tale also has its own letter: a messenger with "books" (letters) from Hegumen Sergius reaches the prince on the field: "Peace and blessing to the Grand Prince, to all Russian princes, and to the whole Orthodox army." The messenger also brings a small loaf of the Most Pure Mother of God. The prince eats it and cries out. This is not the Life's letter ("go boldly").
  - The Tale's editors call some of its details literary and say it has deliberate anachronisms, such as Metropolitan Cyprian giving a blessing in Moscow in 1380, when he was in Kiev.
  - The Lavra's own page [S7] says that Sergius prayed and remembered by name those killed "on the Kulikovo field". This is a modern Lavra statement, not an old source.
  - Kuchkin's view (the blessing story in the Life belongs to the Vozha battle of 1378, and was linked to Kulikovo later by the Tale) is in notes S3 (ru-wiki); I did not read Kuchkin himself.
  - I did not read the "Zadonshchina" or the chronicle tale of Kulikovo in this pass.
- Not in the sources: The Life gives no date, no place, no "cell", no weather, no crowd, no name for the runner. It names no Kulikovo, no Peresvet or Oslyabya, and no holy water or Sunday liturgy; those are the Tale's. It says nothing about Sergius's face, voice or words when the army was in doubt.
- Sources: S5; S1 ch.22 and notes 80-82; S6; S7; notes S3, S4 (letter in 1922 spelling matches S5: «безъ всякаго сомнѣнія, Господине, со дерзновеніемъ пойди противъ свирѣпства ихъ»).

## SC-10 — The Mother of God at the door of the cell (the monastery, date not given)
- Status: full.
- When and where: No year or day in the Life. The text implies night (Sergius spends "the rest of the night" awake afterward). The Lavra keeps 24 Aug (6 Sept) for the appearance (notes S3). Place: Sergius's cell and the "прусть", the porch or entry ("сени") [S5; S1 ch.26].
- Who was there: Sergius; his disciple Micah (Михей); the Mother of God with the apostles Peter and John; later Isaac and Simon.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Sergius says his usual cell prayers before an icon of the Mother of God, looks at the icon often, and prays for the monastery. He sings the akathist [S5].
  2. He finishes, sits down to rest a little, and tells Micah to be alert, because a wondrous and fearful visit is coming "in this hour" [S5].
  3. A voice says "Behold, the Most Pure One comes!" Sergius goes quickly out of the cell into the porch [S5].
  4. A great light, brighter than the sun, shines on him. He sees her with Peter and John, in light that cannot be told. He falls on his face, unable to bear it [S5].
  5. She touches him with her hands and speaks [S5].
  6. She is gone. Sergius is shaken, "out of his mind" for a while. He comes to himself and finds Micah on the floor, "as if dead from fear". He raises him [S5].
  7. Micah throws himself at his feet and asks what the vision was; his spirit "nearly parted from my body". Sergius's face "blooms with joy" but he cannot answer. He says: "Wait, child, my spirit trembles too" [S5].
  8. After a while he sends Micah for Isaac and Simon, and tells them "everything in order". They are filled with joy, sing a moleben to the Mother of God together, and give glory to God. Sergius stays awake the rest of the night [S5].
- Details: The light; Peter and John also shining; the icon (type not named); the akathist.
- Words spoken (our English from the Old Russian):
  - Sergius to Micah: "Child, be sober and keep watch, for a wondrous and fearful visit is about to come to us in this hour."
  - The voice: "Behold, the Most Pure One comes!"
  - The Mother of God: "Do not be afraid, my chosen one. I have come to visit you. Your prayer for your disciples and for your monastery has been heard. Do not grieve any more, because from now on it will have plenty of everything. Not only in your life, but after you go to the Lord, I will not leave your monastery. I will give what it needs without lack, and I will keep it and cover it."
  - Sergius to Micah: "Wait, child, for my spirit also trembles at the wondrous vision."
- Inner life: Sergius's prayer before the visit (Old Russian): «Пречистая Мати Христа моего, ходатайце и заступънице, крѣпкаа помощнице роду человечьскому!» The prayer asks her to be a mediator for "us unworthy" and for the holy place.
- Testimony (Old Russian, public-domain; S5, chapter "О посѣщении Богоматере къ святому"):
  > И се ему глаголющу, и абие глас слышашеся: «Се Пречистаа грядет!» Святый же слышавъ, скоро изыде ис келиа въ прустъ, еже есть сѣни. И се свѣт велий осѣни святого зѣло, паче солнца сиающа; и абие зритъ Пречистую съ двема апостолома, Петром же и Иоанном, в неизреченнѣй свѣтлости облистающася. И яко видѣ святый, паде ниць, не могый тръпѣти нестръпимую ону зарю.
  > Пречистаа же своима рукама прикоснуся святого, глаголя: «Не ужасайся, избранниче мой! Приидох бо посѣтити тебе. Се услышана бысть молитва твоя о ученицѣхъ своих, о нихже молишися, и о обители твоей, да не скорбиши прочее: ибо отнынѣ всѣмъ изообительствуеть, и не токмо донде же въ житие си, но и по твоем еже къ Господу отхождении неотступна буду от обители твоеа, потребнаа подавающи нескудно, и снабдящи, и покрывающи».
- What changed: Sergius has her promise for the monastery in his life and after. He tells only two disciples.
- Not in the sources: no year; no name for the icon; no description of her face or clothes; no words from Peter or John; no word of what Micah saw (only that he lay as if dead); no words from Sergius to Isaac and Simon (the text says only that he told them in order). This chapter has no order to keep it secret. The Life says "в неизреченнѣй свѣтлости" (in light beyond telling): do not describe the light further.
- Sources: S5 (Old Russian); S1 ch.26 (matches); notes S3.

## SC-11 — The death of Sergius (25 September 1392)
- Status: full.
- When and where: "в лѣто 6900-е месяца септевриа 25" (year 6900 from creation = 1392; the Life, S5). The Old Russian says he lived "лѣтъ 70 и 8" (78 years). The S1 editors doubt the 78 years because it does not fit events in the Life (S1 note 89). Place: the Trinity monastery. The modern Russian translation puts the brothers "in the saint's cell" [S1 ch.32]; the Old Russian says only "Братии же всѣм събраном" (all the brothers having gathered). Do not say "his cell" as fact for the deathbed. In notes, 25 Sept Julian = 8 Oct civil.
- Who was there: the "священное братство"; his disciples held him up; Nikon (named hegumen six months before). The Life does not name who held him.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Six months before, he knew his departure. He called the brothers and gave the care of the monastery to his beloved disciple Nikon, "young in body but gray-haired in mind". He told Nikon to feed the flock carefully, since he would answer for many. Then Sergius began to keep silence [S5].
  2. In September he fell ill. He called the new flock and gave a teaching: stay in Orthodoxy, keep one mind, keep purity of soul and body and unfeigned love, avoid evil desires, eat and drink without disorder, adorn yourselves most with humility, do not forget hospitality, avoid quarrels, count the honor and glory of this life as nothing, and wait for God's reward [S5].
  3. He said his last words (below).
  4. At the very end he took the Body and Blood of the Lord, "his disciples supporting his weak limbs with their hands" [S5].
  5. He raised his hands to heaven, prayed, and gave up his soul with the prayer [S5].
  6. A great fragrance poured from the body. The brothers wept. They laid the body on a bier and led it out with psalms and funeral prayers [S5].
  7. His face shone "like snow, not as is usual with the dead, but as of a living man or an angel of God". They buried him in the monastery he made [S5].
- Words spoken (our English from the Old Russian): "God is calling me, and I am going away from you. I give you to the Almighty Lord and to his Most Pure Mother. Let her be your refuge and a wall against the snares of the enemy and his barking." (лаяние = barking, railing.) The words of the final prayer after Communion are not given.
- Inner life: none in his own words beyond the above. The Life says he desired Jesus ("желанному Исусу").
- Testimony (Old Russian, public-domain; S5, chapter "О преставлении святого"):
  > «Азъ, Богу зовущу мя, отхожду от васъ. Предаю же вас всемогущему Господу и того пречистѣй Богоматери, да будет вамь прибѣжище и стѣна от сѣтей вражиих и лаяний их». И в самый убо исход, вън же хотяше телеснаго съуза отрѣшитися, владычняго тѣла и крови причястися, ученикъ руками того немощныя уды подкрѣпляемы. Въздвиже на небо руцѣ, молитву сътворивъ, чистую свою и священную душу съ молитвою Господеви предаст, в лѣто 6900-е месяца септевриа 25; живъ же преподобный лѣтъ 70 и 8.
  > Излия же ся тогда благоухание велие и неизреченно от телесе святого. … Лице же святого свѣтляашеся, яко снѣг, а не яко обычай есть мертвымъ, но яко живу или аггелу Божию…
- What changed: Nikon leads; the brothers bury him in the monastery; the Life says miracles at the shrine began at once.
- Not in the sources: the hour of day; the weather; who stood where; Nikon's actions at the bed; the words of the last prayer; the age of the brothers; the room. The Life does not call the place a "cell" in the Old Russian. The dying teaching is a list in the Life's words, not Sergius's quoted speech.
- Sources: S5; S1 ch.32 and note 89.

## SC-12 — The grave is opened (5 July, thirty years after the death)
- Status: full for facts (no Old Russian reached; facts only).
- When and where: "thirty years after" Sergius's death; the feast on "the 5th day of July" [S1 ch.33]. The year is not stated: thirty years counted from 1392 gives 1422 (notes S3: 5 July 1422). CONFLICT: the S1 editors' note 93 says "relics found 18 July 1423". Say the year is given as 1422 by most accounts and 1423 by this edition. Place: the Trinity monastery, at Sergius's grave.
- Who was there: a pious man who lived near the monastery and often prayed at the grave (name not given); Hegumen Nikon, "the saint's disciple"; "ruling princes" (not named); the "Holy Council" (clergy); Grand Prince "Georgy" Dmitrievich, "who was baptized by the saint" [S1 ch.33]. S1 note 94 says this is Yury Dmitrievich (1374-1434), second son of Dmitri Donskoy.
- What happened, in order:
  1. The man prays at the grave one night, falls into a light sleep, and Sergius appears to him [S1 ch.33].
  2. He wakes afraid and glad and tells Nikon at once. Nikon tells the whole brotherhood. The news spreads [S1 ch.33].
  3. Princes, the council and Yury Dmitrievich gather. The council opens the grave. The place fills with fragrance [S1 ch.33].
  4. They find the body whole and bright, and the clothes whole and untouched by decay. Water stands on both sides of the grave but does not touch the body or the robe. They glorify God [S1 ch.33].
  5. On 5 July they hold a feast, and with psalms and singing they put the relics in a shrine [S1 ch.33].
  6. Nikon, the council and the princes decide to build a stone Trinity church over the grave. Nikon builds it with the princes' help and has it painted by the old painters Daniil and Andrei, near the end of their lives [S1 ch.33; S1 note 95: wooden church of 1411; stone church built 1423-24 over the relics].
- Words spoken: Sergius, in the dream (original in S1, modern Russian; facts-only, one short quotation): «почему вы оставляете меня так долго во гробе, покрытого землей? Ведь вода теснит мое тело!» Our English: "Why do you leave me so long in the grave, covered with earth? The water presses on my body!" He tells the man to tell the hegumen.
- Testimony: none beyond the line above (S1 is a modern translation; facts only).
- What changed: The body is shown whole; the relics are put in a shrine; Nikon starts the stone church.
- Not in the sources: the dreamer's name; the other princes' names; how deep the water stood; who lifted the lid; what Nikon said; the hour; the weather. The Life says Yury was "baptized by the saint", not "godson"; "godson" is our reading of that. A source-check flag: S1 note 92 says the text from ch.33 on is the one written by Simon Azarin in 1646 (not Epiphanius, and not shown as Pachomius). The outline's "Pachomius" tag for this scene is not what this edition says. The earlier Old Russian text I read (S5) has no relics story.
- Sources: S1 ch.33 and notes 92-95; notes S3.

## FN-1 — Is the "Andrei" of Nikon's stone church Andrei Rublev, and the Trinity icon and the Lavra
The Life, ch.33, names only "Daniil and Andrei" as painters of Nikon's stone Trinity church [S1 ch.33]. The edition's note 74 says Andrei Rublev and Daniil Cherny painted the Andronikov Spassky cathedral, and that the Rublev passage is usually seen as Pachomius's addition. The Orthodox Encyclopedia says Pachomius's short Life of Nikon (about the mid-1440s) says Andrei and Daniil painted the stone Trinity cathedral (about 1425-27), their last work [S8]. The Lavra says an artel under Andrei Rublev and Daniil Cherny made the icons and frescoes in 1425-1427, and painted the "Trinity" as the main temple icon "in praise of Sergius"; the icon has been in the Tretyakov Gallery since 1929, with a copy in the iconostasis [S7]. I did not read a source on the dispute over the icon's date.

## FN-2 — One named case after death: the 1608-10 siege
The siege of the monastery by the forces of Sapieha and Lisovsky ran 23 Sept 1608 to 12 Jan 1610 (S1 note 134). In S1 ch.63 (headed "the appearance of Sergius to Andrei Boldyr"), a chief of the soldiers, Andrei, and his men fled the Lithuanian regiments and swore an oath before telling what they saw before an assault: a fast river between them and the monastery, and two gray-haired elders on the walls who shouted at them. The defenders did not know this. Then the assault failed with many dead in their ranks. No year for this assault is given. The chapter is a fragment of ch.46 of the "Skazanie" by Avraamy Palitsyn, cellarer of the monastery (d. 1627) (S1 notes 135, 138). I did not read Palitsyn's original.

## FN-3 — Why Sergius is patron of students, and whether it comes from SC-01
Yes, the link is the boy's school failure and the elder's blessing (the Life ch.3, in the notes). A priest in Foma (6 Oct 2025) gives this as the reason: Sergius "could not manage letters" and asked God for help, met an elder, received a piece of blessed bread, and then learned easily; so schoolchildren, students and teachers pray to him [S9]. The article says it became "a good tradition" and gives no date. I found no Lavra, Patriarchate or synod document that names him patron of students. Ru-wiki lists "students" as a patronage (notes S3). Treat this as a custom of prayer, with a modern church magazine as the source.

## Report
