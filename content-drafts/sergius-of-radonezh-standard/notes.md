# Sergius of Radonezh — research notes (tier B, one agent)

Rule: facts come from sources read in this session. Strength tags: A = early Life (Epiphanius 1417-18, as kept in the Pachomius text); B = later Life layers (Pachomius, 1440-59) or later 15th-c. text; C = modern scholar or encyclopedia; D = popular or unverified. "NEW" = likely missing on popular English pages.

## 1. Identity

- Birth name: Varfolomei (Bartholomew). Monastic name: Sergius (Sergii), given at tonsure on 7 Oct because that day honors Sts Sergius and Bacchus [S1 ch.6]. Titles: Hegumen of Radonezh, "Hegumen of the Russian land", wonderworker [S3].
- Parents: Kirill and Maria (boyar family of Rostov) [S1 ch.2]. Brothers: Stefan (eldest), Petr (youngest). Nephews: Klim and Ivan (Ivan became Theodore of Simonov) [S1 ch.5].
- Born in a village in the Rostov principality; the family moved to Radonezh [S1 ch.4-5]. Ru-wiki names the birthplace Varnitsy [S3].
- Birth date: DISPUTED. Pachomius line: 3 May 1314 (age 78 at death, S1 ch.32). Epiphanius line: 1322 (age about 70). Scholars: Kuchkin 3 May 1322; Kloss 29-31 May 1322; Borisov 3 May 1314. 19th-c. Life by Nikon gave 1319 [S3]. The Life gives no dates except the death date [S3].
- Death: 25 Sept 1392 (Julian) = 8 Oct civil (Gregorian). Age in the Life: 78 [S1 ch.32].
- Relics found: 5 July 1422 (Julian) = 18 July civil [S1 ch.33; S3].
- Glorification: the Church dates it 1452 in some encyclopedias; no single document exists. Golubinsky: 1449-50 letter of Met. Jona calls him "prepodobny". Veneration began before formal canonization rules [S3].
- Feasts (Orthodox, Julian with civil in brackets) [S3]: 25 Sept (8 Oct), repose; 5 (18) July, finding of relics. Also 6 (19) July, Synaxis of Radonezh saints; 24 Aug (6 Sept), Synaxis of Moscow saints; 23 May (5 June) Rostov-Yaroslavl saints; 23 June (6 July) Vladimir saints; 24 Aug (6 Sept) Appearance of the Mother of God to him, kept at the Lavra [S3].
- Catholic: ru-wiki says the name entered the Roman martyrology in 1969 under Paul VI, with feast 25 Sept (Gregorian); also kept by Anglicans on 25 Sept [S3]. Ru-wiki itself says evidence of Catholic veneration before 1969 is mixed. Needs a Catholic primary source (martyrology text) before use.
- Tradition type: Orthodox, with Eastern Catholic (feast kept) and Roman Catholic/Anglican commemoration per S3. Verify before stating the Roman Catholic claim.
- User note: the task gave feasts "5 July and 8 October". These are Julian 5 July (relics) and the civil date of 25 Sept (repose). Say which calendar each date uses.
- Patron: of Russia and of students [S3].
- Tier: B.

## 3. Timeline

Dates in the Life are rare. Order below is the Life's order. Dates are from scholars (S3) unless stated.

- Before birth: the child cries out three times in the womb during the Sunday liturgy: at the Gospel, at the Cherubic Hymn, and at "Holy things for the holy". Women ask "What will this child be?" [S1 ch.2] [S2]. Strength A. Hagiographic pattern: Epiphanius himself compares it with Ephrem and Alypius, who also had signs before birth [S1 ch.2 text]. Legend/miracle report.
- Birth: Bartholomew. He refuses milk on Wednesdays and Fridays as an infant [S1 ch.4 (his own words to his mother); S2]. A.
- Age 7: sent to school with his brother; he cannot learn letters; the teacher beats him in despair; he weeps in secret [S2; S1 ch.3 refers to it]. A for the failure.
- THE BOY AND THE ELDER (vision of the boy): father Kirill sends him to find lost foals. In a field under an oak stands a black-robed elder, a priest, praying with tears. The boy waits until he finishes. He asks for prayers to learn letters. The elder gives him a small piece of white blessed bread (prosphora): "Open your mouth, child, take this and eat". It tastes sweet as honey. The elder says "Your son will be a dwelling of the Holy Trinity" and will lead many. The boy begs him home; the elder asks the boy to read the psalter in the chapel; he reads clearly. The elder vanishes at the gate. Parents think he was an angel [S1 ch.3; S2]. A (early Life), counted as a vision. NEW detail: the Saul-and-donkeys parallel Epiphanius draws, and that the elder first prays alone at the oak.
- Age under 12: severe fasting: bread and water, nothing on Wed and Fri. His mother begs him to stop. He answers that nobody is clean before God, citing Job and Psalm 50; the mother says "you are not twelve years old and you speak of your sins" [S1 ch.4]. A. NEW.
- Family ruined: Kirill, a rich Rostov boyar, becomes poor through Horde tribute, Tatar raids and the Turalyk raid; Rostov passes to Moscow; the Muscovite governor Vasily Kocheva and Mina oppress the town; the Rostov elder boyar Averky is hanged head down [S1 ch.5]. The family moves to Radonezh (a town given by Grand Prince Ivan to his son Andrei) and settles near the Church of the Nativity of Christ [S1 ch.5]. A. NEW (English pages skip the Moscow violence).
- Parents ask him to wait for monasticism; he cares for them until they take the habit and die some years later; he buries them and keeps 40 days of memorials [S1 ch.5]. A.
- He gives his inheritance to Petr; his brother Stefan (a monk at Khotkovo, wife dead) goes with him to look for a desert place [S1 ch.5]. A.
- The hermitage in the forest: they find a spot "in the thicket where there was water" [S1 ch.5 text, as quoted in S3]. They cut logs, carry them on their shoulders, build a hut, a cell, a small church. Stefan says to dedicate it to the Holy Trinity, because of the child's three cries; the church is consecrated by Metropolitan Theognostus's priests, in the early reign of Grand Prince Simeon [S1 ch.5]. A. NOTE: the name "Makovets" for the hill does NOT appear in the S1 chapters I read or in S3 text I grepped. The Life calls it only "that desert place". Do not state "Makovets" without another source.
- Stefan leaves the desert after a short time as too hard, and goes to the Epiphany monastery in Moscow, where he knew the future Metropolitan Alexis [S1 ch.5]. A.
- Tonsure: on 7 Oct, feast of Sergius and Bacchus, by Hegumen Mitrofan. Age 23. After Communion the church fills with fragrance. Sergius stays seven days in the church eating only prosphora from the hegumen [S1 ch.6]. A (age and date); Pachomius age 23 is consistent with S3 (1337 or so is not given in the Life).
- Demons: while he sings matins, the church wall opens and demons in Lithuanian clothes and tall caps rush in and threaten him; he drives them off with Psalms and the cross. Days later, a second attack in his hut [S1 ch.7]. A, a miracle report. Vivid, NEW.
- Wild animals: wolves howl round his hut; bears pass. He fears a little, like any man, and prays [S1 ch.6]. A.
- THE BEAR: one bear comes to him not to harm but for food. Sergius sets a small piece of bread on a stump or log. If the bread is gone, the bear stands there for a long time "like a harsh creditor wanting his debt". If he has only one piece, he breaks it in two. He goes hungry rather than cheat the bear. It lasted "more than a year" [S1 ch.7]. A (strongest source). Marked as a miracle of peace. The Life says "arkuda, that is, a bear" [S1 ch.7 note 46].
- After two years or more (the Life: "I do not know, God knows"), monks begin to come, one, two, three. He warns them that there is hunger, thirst and poverty; they say they can bear it. Each builds his own cell [S1 ch.7]. A.
- Hegumen: Sergius is ordained priest and hegumen (the Life narrates his return "to his monastery" for the rite in ch.8). In the first years there are 12 brothers, he is the 13th, and the number stays at 12 for two or three years [S1 ch.8]. A. Symon, an archimandrite from Smolensk, joins [S1 ch.8].
- Nephew Ivan: Stefan brings his small son Ivan and gives him to Sergius to be tonsured, with the name Theodore [S1 ch.9]. A.
- His rule as hegumen: he works first, comes first to church, stands without leaning on the wall; recruits first wear a black long coat for a long time and do all work before tonsure [S1 ch.9]. At night he walks past the cells: where he hears prayer he blesses it; where he hears talk or laughter he knocks on the door or window and goes away; he speaks to them the next morning, "from afar", without anger [S1 ch.9]. A. NEW and vivid.
- THE FOUR DAYS OF HUNGER: the bread and salt run out. He forbids the brothers to go begging. He waits three days, then on the fourth morning takes an axe and offers to build a porch for the old monk Daniil, for pay in bread. He works all day; in the evening Daniil gives him a sieve of bread, old and moldy; he eats it with water. A monk grumbles: "our bread has gone moldy"; the brothers threaten to leave. While Sergius speaks, someone knocks at the gate with loaves, fresh, fragrant bread, no one learns who sent it. He tells the brothers to feed the carriers first, though hungry [S1 ch.10]. A. Unknown donor. NEW.
- His clothes: coarse undyed sermyaga cloth; he alone takes a bad piece of cloth that seven monks had refused, and wears the robe until it falls apart [S1 ch.11]. A.
- THE PEASANT AND THE PRINCE: a plowman comes from far away to see Sergius. He sees a man in a patched robe digging a garden through a fence gap; he refuses to believe it is the hegumen and says "I came to see a prophet and you show me an orphan". Sergius bows to the ground, kisses him, seats him at his right, feeds him. Then a prince arrives with his troop; guards push the peasant aside; the prince bows to Sergius; the peasant learns who he was and trembles [S1 ch.11]. A, strong; the Life says the brothers wanted to throw the peasant out and Sergius forbade them. NEW.
- THE SPRING: the growing community lacks water; brothers grumble "why did you settle where there is no water?" He prays in a ravine, near a puddle of rain water, and a spring rises, still used at the time of writing. People call it "Sergius's spring"; he is angry and says "not I gave you water but the Lord" [S1 ch.12]. A. Miracle report. NEW (the anger at the name).
- THE BOY WHO DID NOT DIE: a father carries a sick infant son to Sergius; the child dies in his arms; the father leaves to get a coffin. Sergius prays on his knees; the child lives. Sergius tells the father it was only cold: "the child warmed up in my cell"; "no one rises before the general resurrection"; he forbids him to tell. The father cannot stay silent [S1 ch.13]. A. Very good character note.
- The nobleman possessed by a demon (ch.14) and others: not read in full.
- Community life: Patriarch Philotheus of Constantinople sends a cross, paraman and schema with Greek envoys and a letter saying "one rule is missing: you have no common life". Metropolitan Alexis agrees. Sergius introduces the cenobitic rule: cellarer, cooks, bakers, infirmarian; no private property [S1 ch.16; S2]. A. Date unknown (not in Life). Note: Alexis agreed to the change with the Patriarch's letter in hand.
- Beyond the Trinity: Sergius founds or helps found Kirzhach (ch.17), Andronikov (ch.19 — by Alexis), Simonov (ch.20), Dubenka (ch.22), Golutvin (ch.23), Vysoky (ch.24) [S1 ch. titles; S4]. Not read in full; chapter list only.
- The angel at the liturgy: during the visit of Prince Vladimir, Isaac the Silent and Makarii see a fourth, shining priest serving behind Sergius. The prince's party has no priest. Sergius says "an angel of the Lord serves with me whenever I serve; tell no one while I live" [S1 ch.21; S2]. A.
- Death of Metropolitan Alexis approaching: Alexis gives Sergius a gold jeweled cross and offers him the metropolitan see. Sergius: "from youth I did not wear gold, and in old age I wish to stay poor". He refuses. Alexis dies in 1378 (6885) [S1 ch.25]. A. Sergius's stated prophecy about Archimandrite Michael (he dies on the way to Constantinople) is in the same chapter.
- THE BLESSING OF DMITRI DONSKOI (1380): Mamai gathers the Horde; Grand Prince Dmitri visits Sergius to ask whether to march. Sergius blesses him, "arms him with prayer", says go against the godless; Dmitri promises a monastery of the Mother of God if he wins. On the road the Russians see the huge Tatar host and stop in doubt. A messenger arrives with a letter from Sergius: go boldly, God will help [S1 ch.22; S2; S3]. A for the visit, the letter and the victory; the Life never says "Kulikovo", gives no date, and does not name Peresvet or Oslyabya. At the hour of the battle Sergius, standing in prayer with the brothers, sees it from many days away, names the fallen, and prays for them [S1 ch.22; S2]. B. After victory Dmitri visits, gives a gift, and Sergius builds the Dubenka monastery of the Dormition [S1 ch.22].
  - SOURCE CONFLICTS (S3): (a) Earliest text (as quoted in S3) says the prince will win "if God helps"; the Pachomius redaction reads "with God's help you will win and return unharmed", and Dimitry of Rostov strengthens it into a firm prophecy. (b) Kuchkin argues the blessing story belongs to the battle on the Vozha (1378), not Kulikovo. (c) Peresvet and Oslyabya come from the later "Tale of the Mamai Rout" (Skazanie o Mamaevom poboishche), not from the Life [S3]. S4 (1922) repeats the Peresvet-Oslyabya account as received tradition. (d) S3 repeats a claim that Sergius opposed a Genoese trade deal offered by Mamai; this is a modern scholar's reading cited in S3 [S3 ref 33]. Mark C; do not use as fact.
  - Rogovich/Klyuchevsky (S4) date the blessing as 1380 in prose; the Life itself gives no year.
- Trinity church and Mother of God: Dmitri's vow leads to Dubenka. Separately:
- THE VISION OF THE MOTHER OF God: Sergius prays the akathist before her icon, then tells his disciple Micah (Mikhei) "be vigilant, we will have a wondrous visit". A voice says "The Most Pure comes". A light brighter than the sun; she appears with Peter and John. Sergius falls on his face; Micah lies half dead. She touches Sergius: "Do not be afraid, my chosen one. Your prayer for your disciples and your monastery is heard; your monastery will have abundance, in your life and after your departure, I will not leave this place." Afterward Sergius is too shaken to speak and tells Isaac and Simon [S1 ch.26; S2]. A. Strong. The Lavra keeps 24 Aug (6 Sept) for the appearance [S3].
- Divine fire: his disciple Simon, as ecclesiarch, sees fire move around the altar, then fold like a veil and enter the chalice as Sergius communes. Sergius forbids him to tell until Sergius's death [S1 ch.31; S2]. A.
- Vision of the birds (S2 only here; not read in S1): a great flock of birds lights on the monastery; a voice says his disciples will be as many [S2]. B. Check Pachomius source.
- Death: six months before, he names Nikon as hegumen. He falls ill in September, gives a last teaching (keep Orthodoxy, unity, purity, hospitality, avoid quarrels, no honors), takes Communion with his disciples supporting him, raises his hands, prays, and dies on 25 Sept 1392 at 78. Fragrance fills the cell; his face is bright "not like a dead man's" [S1 ch.32]. A for events; the Life says "6900 (1392)".
- 1422, 5 July: thirty years later, a pious man sees Sergius in a dream: "why do you leave me so long in the grave covered with earth? Water presses my body". Hegumen Nikon opens the grave before princes, including Yury Dmitrievich (Sergius's godson), and a council. The body and robe are found whole and not touched by water standing at both sides; the relics are placed in a shrine. Nikon then builds a stone Trinity church painted by Daniil and Andrei [S1 ch.33]. B (Pachomius). The same date "5 July 1422" appears in S3.
- 1427: the Varnitsy monastery is founded at his birthplace [S3].
- 1448-52: glorification (see Identity). Maxim the Greek doubted his sanctity because Church leaders held land and wealth [S3]. Strength C.
- 11 April 1919: the Soviet commission opens the relics (after a decree of 1 Feb 1919 on opening relics). Bones and rotted robe found; a lock of fair-reddish hair wrapped in recent waxed paper is in the skull. A protocol is signed, including Archimandrite Kronid, the Lavra's vicar. Lev Kuleshov films it for a chronicle [S3]. C (cites the Soviet 1919 journal "Revolution and Church"). NEW.
- The head: Church tradition says Pavel Florensky and Count Yury Olsufiev secretly removed the saint's head before the opening and put in the skull of Prince Trubetskoy; the head stayed hidden in the altar of the Vladimirskaya church at Vinogradovo during the war and rejoined the relics in 1946 [S3]. Strength C/D (tradition; ru-wiki says "church tradition and historical research").
- The shrine becomes a museum exhibit at the Lavra museum; in summer 1941 it is sealed and evacuated to Solikamsk (25 July 1941 to 19 Nov 1944), then returned to Trinity cathedral [S3].
- 1946, Holy Saturday (20 April; Pascha was 21 April): the relics are returned to the Patriarchate and moved from Trinity cathedral (still museum-owned) to the Dormition cathedral. They now rest in Trinity cathedral [S3]. C.

## 4. Character

- Humble to the point of being mistaken for a beggar: patched sermyaga; the peasant takes him for an orphan [S1 ch.11].
- Hates praise: angry at "Sergius's spring"; forbids people to tell the dead-boy story; asks Isaac, Makarii and Simon to keep the angel and fire visions secret [S1 ch.12, 13, 21, 31]. A.
- Kind to animals and people alike: shares his last bread with the bear [S1 ch.7].
- Works with his hands: digs the garden, builds Daniil's porch for bread, carries water [S1 ch.10-11].
- Careful pastor: night rounds, knocks on doors, corrects the next day without anger [S1 ch.9]. A.
- Warns newcomers honestly about the hardship (hunger, thirst, no sleep), and does not drive anyone away [S1 ch.7, 9].
- Little humor: Epiphanius says he did not allow himself laughter, and when he wanted to smile "he did it with great modesty" [S1 ch.5]. A.
- Fears but prays: "he feared a little, like any man" when wolves came [S1 ch.6].
- Weakness or struggle: slow to learn letters as a child [S2; S1 ch.3]. The brothers grumble against him over hunger and water [S1 ch.10, 12]. A.
- Refuses office: a gold cross and the metropolitan see [S1 ch.25]. A.
- Critics: Maxim the Greek (16th c.) doubted his sanctity because of church wealth [S3]. C.

## 5. Quotations (exact Russian from sources; English is our own)

Rights note: S1 is a modern Russian translation: keep quotes short or paraphrase; paraphrases are marked. S4 is public domain.

1. Archimandrite/old text, Philotheus letter to Sergius, [S1 ch.16]. Russian: "Одного правила вам недостает – у вас не устроено общежитие." Our English: "One rule is missing: you have no common life." Facts-only source.
2. Metropolitan Alexis to Sergius [S1 ch.25]. "Прости меня, владыко, от юности я не был златоносцем..." (partial in source; exact text in S1 ch.25: the monk says he was not a "gold-bearer" from youth). Our English: "From youth I did not wear gold." Paraphrase.
3. Sergius to peasant's brothers [S1 ch.11]: paraphrase: do not throw him out; he came to me, not to you.
4. The elder to Kirill and Maria [S1 ch.3]. "Сын ваш будет обителью Святой Троицы и многих приведет вслед за собой к пониманию Божественных заповедей". Our English: "Your son will be a dwelling of the Holy Trinity and will lead many after him to understand the divine commandments."
5. The elder to the boy [S1 ch.3]. "Открой уста свои, чадо, возьми это и съешь". Our English: "Open your mouth, child, take this and eat."
6. The peasant [S1 ch.11]. "Я пришел посмотреть на пророка, а вы мне показываете сироту." Our English: "I came to see a prophet, and you show me an orphan."
7. The Mother of God [S1 ch.26]. "Не бойся, избранник Мой. Я пришла посетить тебя." Our English: "Do not be afraid, my chosen one. I have come to visit you."
8. Sergius on the spring [S1 ch.12]. Paraphrase: "Let me never hear you call this spring by my name, for I did not give you the water; the Lord gave it to us unworthy ones."
9. Sergius to the father of the sick boy [S1 ch.13]. Paraphrase: before the general resurrection no one rises; the child only grew warm in my cell.
10. The message to Dmitri (letter from Sergius), 1922 text, pre-1918 spelling [S4]: "безъ всякаго сомнѣнія, Господине, со дерзновеніемъ пойди противъ свирѣпства ихъ". Our English: "Without any doubt, lord, go boldly against their fierceness." Public domain. Matches S1 ch.22 and S3 quoting the Life.
11. Sergius at the tonsure to Mitrofan [S1 ch.6]. Paraphrase: "As the deer longs for the stream, so my soul longs for monastic and desert life."
12. Klyuchevsky via S4 on Sergius's blessing of Dmitri: "иди на безбожниковъ смѣло, безъ колебанія, и побѣдишь" (public domain, 1922; this is Klyuchevsky's wording, not the Life's). Our English: "Go boldly against the godless, without wavering, and you will win." Mark: Klyuchevsky/Rogovich wording; the "win" part is stronger than the early Life (see conflict in section 3).
13. Sergius's last words to the brothers [S1 ch.32]: paraphrase: "Called by God, I leave you. I entrust you to the Almighty Lord and his Most Pure Mother, who will be your refuge and wall against the snares of the enemy."
14. Sergius to the new monks [S1 ch.7]. Paraphrase: do not prepare your hearts for food, drink or rest, but for patience, for many afflictions.

## 7. Miracles (one line each)

Status key: recorded in the oldest Life = "Life"; added by Pachomius = "Pachomius"; later = "later".

- Before birth: three cries in the womb, Rostov region, mother Maria; Life (Epiphanius); not examined by the Church.
- Boyhood: reads suddenly after the blessed bread from the elder; Rostov region; Life.
- Demons break through the church wall in Lithuanian caps; hermitage; Life.
- Bear feeds from his hand for more than a year; hermitage; Life.
- Wild animals do not harm him; hermitage; Life.
- Spring from rainwater in the ravine; monastery; Life.
- Child "restored" (Sergius denies it); monastery; Life, with Sergius's own denial.
- Fresh bread arrives at the gate after the hunger; monastery; Life; donor never found.
- Angel serves with him at liturgy; seen by Isaac the Silent and Makarii, in Prince Vladimir's visit; Life.
- Sees the battle with Mamai from afar and names the fallen; monastery; Life.
- Mother of God with Peter and John appear; monastery; seen by Sergius, with Micah as witness; Life.
- Fire in the chalice; seen by Simon the ecclesiarch; Life.
- Prophecy about Archimandrite Michael dying en route to Constantinople; Life.
- 1422 incorrupt body; water at the sides of the coffin does not touch it; Pachomius; later also the relics miracles in ch.34-88 (not read; about 55 posthumous miracles listed in chapter titles, among them cures at the tomb, appearances to troops at Troitse-Sergiev during the siege of 1608-10, and to Cossacks at sea) [S1 chapter list].
- 1919 opening of the shrine: hair in skull and missing foot bones are reported by a Soviet protocol; popular and church tradition about the head hidden by Florensky [S3]. Status: recorded by the Soviet press and church tradition; not a miracle claim.

## 8. Other

- Works: Sergius left no writings. Ru-wiki says only "spiritual instructions" are known [S3].
- Disciples who became saints: Nikon (successor), Savva of Dubenka (first hegumen of the Dubenka Dormition monastery), Andronikus, Micah, Athanasius of Serpukhov and others (Sanidopoulos label list). Not read.
- Epiphanius says he collected material for 20 years, including his older brother Stefan's stories [S1 book note; S3]. Epiphanius also wrote a "Praise" of Sergius.
- Dimitry of Rostov's English Life (S2) includes the elder's name-less blessing and the post-Florence story of a presbyter in Latin lands whom Sergius leads back; not verified.
