The Church remembers Saint Sergius of Radonezh as a man through whom God worked many wonders, in his life and at his tomb. This list gathers those wonders as the Life of Saint Sergius tells them. Each account names who told it and when.

Most come from the Life of Saint Sergius, which his disciple Saint Epiphanius the Wise wrote in 1417 and 1418. That Life has come down to us in the form that the monk Pachomius the Serb gave it between 1440 and 1459. He added the finding of the relics and the first miracles at the tomb. In 1646 Simon Azarin added the miracles of later years. Each status line says which of these writers tells the account.

The Russian Church glorified Saint Sergius as a saint in the fifteenth century. To glorify a saint is the Orthodox Church’s act of naming a person a saint before the whole Church. The people honored him before the Church had written rules for this, so no single document gives the date. Saint Sergius himself asked the witnesses of several of these wonders to keep silent while he lived.

During Saint Sergius’s Life

Saint Sergius cries out three times before birth

Saint Sergius’s mother Saint Maria stood in church at the Sunday Liturgy, the Church’s main service. The child in her womb cried out at the Gospel reading and again at the start of the hymn that goes with the bringing of the bread and wine. He cried a third time at the priest’s call before Holy Communion, “Holy Things for the Holy.” The women nearby asked what this child would be. Years later his brother Stefan told Saint Sergius to name the forest church for the Holy Trinity because of the three cries.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, chs. 2 and 5, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

A baby who refuses milk on fast days

Saint Sergius was given the name Bartholomew at his baptism. As a baby he would not take his mother’s milk on Wednesdays and Fridays, which are the Church’s fast days. He also refused milk on days when his mother had eaten meat. He would not take milk from any other nurse.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 2, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

God gives Saint Sergius the gift of reading

Saint Sergius was sent to school at seven and struggled to learn his letters. He prayed to God in tears for help. One day his father sent him to look for some foals, and the boy found an old man in a black robe praying under an oak. The boy asked for his prayers, and the old man gave him a piece of holy bread that tasted sweet as honey. He told the boy that from that day the Lord would give him the knowledge of letters. At home the boy read the Psalms clearly and well, and his parents and brothers praised God. The old man said that the boy would be “a dwelling of the Holy Trinity” and then vanished at the gate. The parents believed that the old man was an angel, sent to give the boy the knowledge of letters.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, chs. 2–3, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Fragrance fills the church at Saint Sergius’s vows

The abbot Mitrofan made Saint Sergius a monk on 7 October. The Church keeps that day for Saints Sergius and Bacchus, so the new monk was given the name Sergius. After he received Holy Communion, the church filled with fragrance. The Life says he was twenty-three.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 6, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Demons break into the forest church

Saint Sergius went into his forest church at night to sing Matins, the early morning service. The wall opened, and demons in Lithuanian clothes and tall pointed caps rushed in and threatened to kill him if he did not leave. Saint Sergius used prayer, the Psalms, and the precious Cross as his weapons, and the demons vanished at once. He thanked God. A few days later many demons shouted at him during all-night prayer in his hut. God’s power scattered them. Saint Epiphanius thought that it took less than an hour.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 7, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Wolves and bears leave him unharmed

Wild animals often came to Saint Sergius in the forest, by night and by day. Wolves came in packs, howling and roaring, and sometimes bears came. Saint Sergius feared them a little, like any person. He prayed to God with all his heart, and the animals did him no harm.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 6, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A bear waits for bread at a stump

A bear came to Saint Sergius to find food, not to harm him. Saint Sergius laid a small piece of bread on a stump or log, and the bear took it and went away. When there was no bread, the bear stood and looked around for a long time, like a hard lender who wants his debt paid. When Saint Sergius had only one piece, he broke it in two. Sometimes he gave the bear all of it and went hungry. This went on for more than a year.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 7, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Fresh bread arrives at the gate after hunger

The monastery ran out of bread and salt, and Saint Sergius told the brothers not to beg but to ask God and wait. He waited three days, and on the fourth morning he took an axe and built a porch for an old monk named Daniil. Daniil paid him with a sieve of old, spoiled bread, and Saint Sergius blessed it and ate it with water. The hungry brothers threatened to leave. While Saint Sergius was still speaking to them, a cart of fresh warm loaves arrived at the gate. The gatekeeper ran to him: “Father, bless the men who brought the loaves! Through your prayers they have brought us much food.” The men said that a rich man who loved Christ had sent it from far away, and then they left. Before anyone ate, Saint Sergius led the brothers into church to sing a service of thanks to God. Then he blessed the loaves and shared them out, and the brothers praised God, who had fed them.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18). The Life tells it as God’s care for His servants, and it does not name the giver.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 10, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A spring rises at Saint Sergius’s prayer

The community grew and had no water nearby, and the brothers asked Saint Sergius why he had settled there. He told them to pray and not lose heart, because God had once brought water from a rock for Israel in the desert. Then he went down to a ravine with one monk and knelt beside a ditch that held a little rainwater. He prayed to God to show His glory and give them water in that place. He made the sign of the Cross over the water, and a full spring broke out of the ground. It was still in use when the Life was written, and the monks drew from it with thanks to God. Many sick people were healed by its water when they came to it with faith, and the Life says this went on in its own day. For ten or fifteen years people called it Saint Sergius’s spring. He was angry and told them that the Lord had given the water, not he.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 12, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A dead child lives again at the monastery

A man who lived near the monastery carried his sick baby son to Saint Sergius, and the child died while the father was asking for prayer. The father went out to get a coffin. Saint Sergius had pity on him. He knelt and prayed for the dead child, and the child came back to life. When the father returned, he found his son alive and fell at the saint’s feet to thank him. Saint Sergius told him that the boy had only been cold and had warmed up in the cell. He said that no one can rise before the resurrection of all the dead, and he told the father to tell no one. The father did not dare to spread the story, but he could not stay silent about it either. In his heart he praised God. The story became known through one of Saint Sergius’s disciples.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18). The Life’s chapter title calls it a raising from the dead, and Saint Sergius himself said that the boy was only cold.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 13, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A nobleman tormented by a demon is healed

A nobleman from far away on the Volga River was tormented day and night by a demon. He broke iron chains, and ten strong men could not hold him. His family heard what God was doing through Saint Sergius, and they took him to the monastery. On the way he screamed that he did not want to see the saint. When Saint Sergius heard that the man was near, he gathered the brothers in church and sang a service of prayer for him. Then he came out of the church with a cross in his hand, and the man cried out and threw himself into a pool of rainwater. He said later that he had seen great flames come from the cross. From that moment his mind was clear, by the grace of Christ and the prayers of the saint. He stayed a few days at the monastery and went home well, praising God, who had healed him.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 14, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

An angel serves with Saint Sergius at Liturgy

Saint Sergius served the Liturgy with his brother and his nephew while Prince Vladimir was a guest at the monastery. A monk named Isaac the Silent saw a fourth priest at the altar, shining brighter than the sun and following behind Saint Sergius. A monk named Makarii also saw him, and the monks learned that the prince’s party had brought no priest. Saint Sergius first said that only three priests had served. Then he told them that an angel of the Lord served with him whenever he served, and that they must tell no one while he lived.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 21, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Saint Sergius foretells Michael’s death at sea

Metropolitan Saint Alexis died in 1378, and the princes asked Saint Sergius to take his place as head of the Russian Church. Saint Sergius refused. A senior abbot named Michael took the seat instead and turned against him. Saint Sergius told his disciples that Michael would not get what he wanted and would not even see Constantinople, because pride had overcome him. Michael fell ill and died on the sea voyage to Constantinople. The Life says that after this everyone counted Saint Sergius among the prophets.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18). The edition’s note dates Michael’s death to 1379.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 25 and note 87, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Saint Sergius sees a battle from far away

Saint Sergius was praying with his brothers at the hour of Grand Prince Saint Dmitri’s battle with Mamai, the Tatar leader. From a place many days’ walk away he saw the fight. A little later he told the brothers of Saint Dmitri’s courage and said that the Russians had won. He named the men who had fallen and prayed for them. The Life does not name the battle, and later writers place it at Kulikovo in 1380.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59). See the note at the end.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 22, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com; “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

The Mother of God appears to Saint Sergius

Saint Sergius stood before an icon of the Most Holy Mother of God and prayed for his monastery. He sang the akathist, a hymn of praise to her. Then he told his disciple Saint Micah to stay alert. A voice said, “Look, the Most Pure One is coming!” Saint Sergius went out to the porch and saw her in a light brighter than the sun, with the apostles Saint Peter and Saint John. She touched him and said, “Do not be afraid, my chosen one. I have come to visit you. Do not grieve, for your prayer for your disciples and your monastery has been heard. … In your life and after you go to God, I will not leave this place.” Saint Micah lay on the floor as if dead from fear. Saint Sergius later told the whole story to two other disciples, Isaac and Simon. Together they sang a service of prayer to the Mother of God and gave glory to God.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18). The Lavra keeps 24 August (6 September on the modern calendar) in memory of the appearance.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 26, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com; “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

Fire enters the chalice at the Liturgy

Simon was the disciple who had heard of the Mother of God’s visit, and he was in charge of the church. During the Liturgy that Saint Sergius served, Simon saw fire move around the altar. The fire folded like a veil and entered the chalice, and Saint Sergius received Holy Communion from it. Saint Sergius called Simon over and asked why he was afraid, and Simon told him what he had seen. Saint Sergius told him to tell no one until after his own death.

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 31, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

A flock of birds promises many disciples

Saint Sergius was praying for his brothers late one evening when a voice called his name. A light brighter than day filled the night, and he saw a great flock of beautiful birds around the monastery. The voice said that the Lord had received his prayer for his spiritual children. His disciples would be as many as the birds, and they would not dwindle after him if they followed in his footsteps. Saint Sergius called his disciple Simon, who saw only a gleam of the light. Saint Sergius then told him all that he had seen and heard, and they rejoiced together.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 14, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

A bright face and fragrance at Saint Sergius’s death

Saint Sergius reposed in the Lord on 25 September 1392, which is 8 October on today’s civil calendar. He had received Holy Communion, and his disciples held him up while he raised his hands and prayed. A great fragrance came from his body. The Life says his face was bright, “not like a dead man’s.”

Status: told by Saint Epiphanius the Wise in his Life of Saint Sergius (1417–18).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 32, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

After Saint Sergius’s Death

Saint Sergius’s body is found whole after thirty years

The Life says that thirty years after Saint Sergius’s death, God chose to glorify His servant still more. A man who lived near the monastery often prayed at Saint Sergius’s grave. Saint Sergius appeared to him in a dream and asked, “Why do you leave me so long in the grave, covered with earth? The water presses on my body!” The man told the abbot Saint Nikon. Princes and a council of clergy gathered, and the council opened the grave. The place filled with fragrance. The body and the robe were whole. Water stood on both sides of the grave, but it had not touched them. Everyone gave glory to God. On 5 July they placed his holy relics in a shrine with psalms and singing. Relics are the saint’s body, which the Church keeps with honor. The Life says that the relics rest in the shrine to this day and give healing to the sick who come to them with faith.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapter on the finding of the relics. Pachomius the Serb first told the story, and Simon Azarin wrote the chapter in its present form in 1646. The Church keeps 5 July (18 July on the modern calendar) as the feast of the finding of the relics, in 1422. See the note at the end.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 33 and notes 92–95, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

Saint Sergius guides two travelers through a fortress

A priest named Symeon suffered in chains for opposing Metropolitan Isidore at a Church council in the West. Symeon and an envoy from Tver ran away toward Russia and lay down to rest on a mountain road near a fortress of robbers. In a dream an old man told Symeon that he would stay with them and lead them through the fortress without harm, and he said that he was Saint Sergius. At the fortress the travelers prayed, “Holy Sergius, cover us and help us!” Someone opened the iron gates for them and said nothing, and the gates closed again behind them. They reached Russia, and Symeon told the monastery’s brothers the whole story with tears. All of them glorified God and His saint.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59). Saint Dimitry of Rostov gave a shorter version of it, without the fortress.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 47, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

A mute young man speaks at the tomb

A young man from Yaroslavl came to Saint Sergius’s holy relics unable to speak. He kissed the relics and at once spoke clearly. Then he told how two years earlier he had woken blind on Great Friday. A year later his sight had returned, but his speech had gone. Then he heard of the miracles of Saint Sergius, and he came with faith.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 51, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A tormented boy is healed at the tomb

A boy from a noble family of Ryazan was tormented by an unclean spirit. He cut his clothes to pieces, and no one could calm him. At the tomb of Saint Sergius he was healed. He did not go home but became a monk at the monastery and helped the brothers with their work until he died.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Pachomius the Serb revised (1440–59).

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 52, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Attackers see a river and old men, 1608–10

Lithuanian regiments laid siege to the monastery from September 1608 to January 1610. The Life says that God gave the monastery victory through the prayers of Saint Sergius. A chief of soldiers named Andrei ran away from those regiments with his men, and they swore in God’s name before they told what they had seen. Before one assault they saw a fast river between them and the monastery, though no river ran there. Two gray-haired old men on the walls shouted at them. The men inside the monastery did not know about this, and the assault failed with many dead among the attackers. Andrei said that the Lord had spared him and his men so that they would repent.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Simon Azarin added in 1646. The chapter is taken from the story of the siege by Avraamy Palitsyn. He was the monastery’s cellarer, the monk in charge of its stores.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 63 and notes 134–138, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Cossacks see the monastery at sea, 1622

In 1622 a Don Cossack leader named Epifan Radilov came to the Trinity monastery with seventy men to keep a vow. They brought two silver-gilt cups as a gift to the Lord and to the saints. They told the abbot that the Turks had begun to win a sea battle against them. The Cossacks had prayed to the Holy Trinity and the Most Pure Mother of God, and they had called on Saint Sergius and Saint Nikon. They promised to visit the monastery if God gave them victory through the saints’ prayers. The Lord showed them His mercy at once. Many of them saw the monastery appear before them, and they took heart, attacked, and beat the Turks.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Simon Azarin added in 1646.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 74, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A soldier is healed at the tomb, 1632

In 1632 a sick soldier was brought to Saint Sergius’s tomb while painters were decorating the Trinity church. He screamed because his head and hands were cramping. After a service of prayer his health and mind returned, by the grace of Christ and the prayers of the saint. He left praising God and His saint.

Status: told in the Life of Saint Sergius, in the chapters that Simon Azarin added in 1646.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 75, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Other miracles in the Life, chapters 34–88

The later Life has 55 chapters after the finding of the relics, numbered 34 to 88. Most tell of miracles. Their titles name healings of the blind and the mute, victories in war, and appearances of Saint Sergius in Moscow and Kazan. Two chapters tell of other things: the death of Abbot Saint Nikon and a word of praise to Saint Sergius. Saint Dimitry of Rostov wrote that many healings were given through the holy relics and that healings still flowed from the shrine in his own day.

Status: told in the later chapters of the Life, by Pachomius the Serb and Simon Azarin. See the note at the end.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise, Pachomius Logothetes, and Simon Azarin, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, chs. 34–88 (chapter list), modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Saint Sergius’s shrine is opened in 1919

On 11 April 1919 a Soviet commission opened the shrine of Saint Sergius at the Trinity monastery, with Church representatives present. Kronid was the monastery’s deputy head, and he signed the record of what they found. The record lists bones and a rotted robe. In the skull lay a lock of fair reddish hair, wrapped in waxed paper of recent make. Church tradition says that Pavel Florensky and Count Yury Olsufiev had secretly taken the saint’s head before the opening and put another skull in its place. The tradition says the head was returned to the holy relics in 1946.

Status: a historical record rather than a miracle. This entry stands here because it tells what was found when the shrine was opened. The commission’s record and the Church’s tradition about the head differ, and both are given here as they are told.

Source: “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia, which cites the Soviet journal Revolution and Church (1919).

A Note on the Sources

The Life names no battle in the war with Mamai and gives no year. Two later works place the blessing of Saint Dmitri before the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380: the Tale of the Rout of Mamai and Saint Dimitry of Rostov’s Life. The historian V. A. Kuchkin has argued that it belongs before the battle on the Vozha River in 1378. Most accounts give the year of the finding of the relics as 1422, and the editors of the Russian edition give 1423. Of the chapters after the finding of the relics, only those told above were read in full for this list. The rest are known from their titles.
