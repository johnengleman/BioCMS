# Sergius of Radonezh — scene cards, group 1 (SC-01 to SC-04)

Source note for all four cards. S1 = Epiphanius the Wise (1417-18), Life of Sergius, as revised by Pachomius Logothetes; modern Russian translation on azbyka.ru: https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Epifanij_Premudryj/zhitie-i-chudesa-prepodobnogo-sergija-igumena-radonezhskogo/ (all chapters on one page; I read ch.2-7 in full this session). Rights: facts-only. I could not reach an Old Russian text in three tries (web search for the Old Russian wording; ru.wikisource page and search), so every "plain:" line below is translated from this modern Russian, not from the Old Russian. Each card keeps its Russian quotations under 40 words in total. The Russian is the modern translation's wording, not Epiphanius's own.

## SC-01 — The stranger under the oak
- Status: full
- When and where: Boyhood, after the failed schooling. The Life gives age 7 for the start of school (S1 ch.2) but no age for the oak scene (S1 ch.3). Place: a field where the boy was sent to look for animals; then the family home, in its chapel. No village name is given in ch.3.
- Who was there: Bartholomew (Varfolomei); his father Kirill sent him out; the stranger, an unnamed elder in a black robe, a priest by rank; later Kirill and Maria (called "boyars" in ch.3) and the brothers.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Bartholomew learns slowly. Stefan and Petr learn quickly. His parents scold him, the teacher punishes him, his schoolmates reproach him, and he prays alone with tears for help to learn letters (S1 ch.2 end).
  2. Kirill sends him to look for foals; the Life says "жеребят" (S1 ch.3). Epiphanius compares this with Saul sent to find lost donkeys.
  3. He finds a black-robed elder standing in the field under an oak, praying earnestly with tears. The boy bows, goes near, and waits until the prayer ends (S1 ch.3).
  4. The elder looks at him, calls him, blesses and kisses him, and asks what he seeks. The boy asks for prayer to learn letters.
  5. The elder lifts his hands, prays, says "Amen". He takes from his pocket, with three fingers, a small piece of white wheat bread, holy prosphora. He tells him to eat it.
  6. It tastes as sweet as honey. The boy quotes the psalm about words sweeter than honey (Ps. 118:103, as S1 cites it). The elder says: do not grieve over letters; from now on the Lord will give you better knowledge than your brothers and peers have.
  7. The boy falls on the ground at the elder's feet, weeping, and begs him to come to his parents' house. The elder goes.
  8. Kirill and Maria come out and bow. A meal is set. The elder first goes into the chapel with the boy, begins the Hours, and tells the boy to read a psalm. The boy says he cannot. The elder says read without doubt. He reads the psalter clearly and well. His parents and brothers are amazed and praise God.
  9. After the chapel the elder eats. The parents beg him to stay and ask about the three cries before birth. The elder tells them not to be afraid, and gives two signs: the boy will read well, and he will be great before God and people.
  10. At the end he says the line about the Holy Trinity, leaves, and the hosts follow him to the gate, where he becomes invisible. The parents think he was an angel (S1 ch.3).
- Details: the prosphora kept in a pocket and handed "like a treasure" with three fingers (S1 ch.3). The parents are said to have prepared a meal; the elder wished to pray first.
- Words spoken (modern Russian, S1 ch.3; plain English is ours):
  - Bartholomew to the elder: «Ты, святой отче, помолись за меня Богу, чтобы смог я научиться грамоте». plain: "Holy father, pray to God for me, so that I can learn to read."
  - The elder: «Открой уста свои, чадо, возьми это и съешь». plain: "Open your mouth, child. Take this and eat."
  - The elder: «Сын ваш будет обителью Святой Троицы и многих приведет вслед за собой к пониманию Божественных заповедей». plain: "Your son will be a dwelling of the Holy Trinity, and he will lead many after him to understand the commandments of God."
  - Bartholomew begging the elder to come home (paraphrase): his parents love people like you very much.
  - Kirill and Maria to the elder (paraphrase): they ask him to stay and ease their worry and sadness about the boy, and tell him about the three cries.
- Inner life: before the scene, prayer alone with tears: "Lord, let me learn letters; teach me and give me understanding" (paraphrase, S1 ch.2 end). At the taste of the bread he quotes Ps. 118:103 (S1 ch.3).
- Testimony: the Life narrates it; no witness speaks. S1 is a modern Russian translation, facts-only, so no long copy.
- What changed: the boy who could not read now reads any book; the family hears the first word that he will be a "dwelling of the Holy Trinity".
- Not in the sources: the boy's age at the oak; the village; the season or weather; the elder's name or face beyond "black-robed, venerable, like an angel"; any tears from the parents; the stranger's words to the parents beyond what is listed. The parents' own words are only a request to stay and a question about the three cries.
- Sources: S1 ch.2 (end), ch.3.

## SC-02 — Two brothers build a church in the forest
- Status: full
- When and where: after the parents' deaths and burial; a place "in the thicket of the forest where there was a spring of water" near Radonezh (S1 ch.5). The church was consecrated early in the reign of Grand Prince Simeon Ivanovich, "I think" (Epiphanius's own hedge, S1 ch.5). No year is given. "Makovets" does not occur in ch.5.
- Who was there: Bartholomew; his older brother Stefan, a monk of the Khotkovo Intercession monastery whose wife had died, leaving sons Klim and Ivan; later priests sent by Metropolitan Theognostus.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Parents ask him to wait for monastic life until they die. He serves them until they become monks, each in a monastery. They die after some years. He buries them, sings the funeral hymns, and keeps memorials for 40 days (S1 ch.5).
  2. He gives his father's inheritance and all in the house to Petr and takes nothing for himself (S1 ch.5).
  3. He goes to Stefan at Khotkovo and asks him to come and look for a desert place. Stefan agrees and leaves the monastery.
  4. They walk through many forests and come to a place in the thicket with a spring. They walk around it, like it, and pray.
  5. With their own hands they cut trees and carry the logs on their shoulders to the chosen spot. They build first a hut with a storeroom and roof, then a cell, then fence off and cut a small church.
  6. Bartholomew asks Stefan, as elder brother, which saint's feast the church should honor. Stefan tells him he knows already: their parents told him that God chose him before birth and gave a sign, the three cries, which priests and elders read as a sign that he would be a disciple of the Holy Trinity. So the church must be named for the Holy Trinity.
  7. Bartholomew sighs from the heart and agrees. He says he asked out of humility.
  8. The brothers take the bishop's blessing. Priests from Metropolitan Theognostus bring the antimension, relics of martyrs and what is needed. The church is consecrated to the Holy Trinity.
  9. Stefan stays a while, sees that desert life is hard (no food, drink, road, village, house, path, visitor), and leaves his brother for Moscow. There he lives in the Epiphany monastery where he knew Alexis, not yet metropolitan (S1 ch.5).
  10. Epiphanius then asks why the two brothers parted: one chose a city monastery, the other made the desert like a city.
- Details: the logs carried "on their shoulders"; hut, storeroom, cell, small church; consecration relics and antimension from the Metropolitan's priests (S1 ch.5). Bartholomew's body is not described in this scene.
- Words spoken (modern Russian, S1 ch.5):
  - Stefan: «Ты сам знаешь ответ на свой вопрос не хуже меня». plain: "You know the answer to your question as well as I do."
  - Stefan quotes the parents: «Не наш ты сын, но Божий дар». plain: "You are not our son, but a gift of God."
  - Bartholomew: «Правильно ты сказал, господин мой». plain: "You have spoken rightly, my lord."
  - Stefan's words on leaving: none; the narrator gives his reason.
  - Bartholomew's words when they chose the spot: none in the Life.
- Inner life: Bartholomew prayed with Stefan before cutting wood (S1 ch.5). The Life says he wanted "fasting and silence" more than anything (S1 ch.5 end). Epiphanius says Bartholomew's wish to build for the Trinity matched Stefan's answer: "that is what I wanted and planned" (paraphrase).
- Testimony: none; Epiphanius tells it. Rights: facts-only.
- What changed: a church of the Holy Trinity stands in the forest; Stefan goes; Bartholomew is alone.
- Not in the sources: any argument between the brothers; Stefan's words when he left; the hill's name; the year; the hours of work; who helped. The Life gives no sign of a grudge.
- Sources: S1 ch.2 (the three cries), ch.5.

## SC-03 — Fear in the wilderness, and the wall that opens
- Status: full
- When and where: after the tonsure of 7 October, inside the church of the Holy Trinity at matins (the demons come "through the wall"), and a few days later in his hut at all-night prayer. The first attack follows an undated "Однажды". The Life gives no year (S1 ch.6-7).
- Who was there: Sergius alone. The tonsuring hegumen Mitrofan had left (S1 ch.6).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Tonsure on 7 October, the feast of Sts Sergius and Bacchus, at age 23, by Hegumen Mitrofan, with the liturgy. After he takes Communion the church fills with a fragrance. Sergius stays in the church seven days, eating only the prosphora from the hegumen's hand (S1 ch.6).
  2. Mitrofan leaves. Sergius asks his blessing and prayer, and asks to be taught how to live alone and how to resist the enemy. Mitrofan answers with Paul's line that God does not allow temptation beyond strength (S1 ch.6).
  3. Epiphanius says wolves came in packs, howling, and bears sometimes came, by night and by day. "Sergius feared them a little, like any man, but he turned to God with earnest prayer" and was unharmed (S1 ch.6).
  4. Once at night he enters the church to sing matins. As he begins, the church wall parts and the devil enters, with many demon warriors, "not by the doors but like a thief". They wear Lithuanian clothes and tall pointed Lithuanian caps. They rush at him to level the church. They grind their teeth and threaten to kill him and tear him apart if he does not leave (S1 ch.7).
  5. Sergius arms himself with prayer and says Psalms 82 and 67 as S1 numbers them. He names the Holy Trinity, trusts the Mother of God, and the "Honorable Cross" in place of a weapon. The devil and his army vanish at once. He thanks God.
  6. A few days later, at all-night prayer in his hut, a noise and crashing begin, "not in a dream but awake". Many demons shout at him to leave: the place is far from people, he will die of hunger or be killed by robbers or wild beasts. Within about an hour (the narrator's "I think") God's power shelters him and scatters them. He says a thanksgiving prayer (S1 ch.7).
  7. Epiphanius adds that after this the devil tried many other visions and animals on him, all without harm (S1 ch.7).
- Details: church wall opens; Lithuanian clothes and pointed caps; the Honorable Cross as his weapon; Psalm verses; seven days with only prosphora; "the sound, crash, shaking" in the hut. Sergius's body or clothing: not described.
- Words spoken (modern Russian, S1 ch.6-7):
  - Narrator on the wolves: «Преподобный Сергий хотя немного боялся их, как всякий человек, но, однако, обращал усердную молитву к Богу». plain: "Sergius, although he feared them a little, like any person, still turned an earnest prayer to God."
  - Demons: «Беги, уходи отсюда и не оставайся больше здесь, на этом месте». plain: "Run, go away from here and do not stay in this place any longer."
  - Sergius (Ps. 67 as S1 numbers it): «Да воскреснет Бог, и исчезнут враги Его». plain: "Let God rise, and let his enemies vanish."
  - Sergius's thanks after the second attack (paraphrase): "Thank you, Lord, for not leaving me; you heard me quickly and had mercy."
  - The "Need" asked: the psalm verse he sang (answered: Ps. 82:2-3 and 67:1-4 as S1 numbers them; Slavonic numbering applies) and what the demons shouted (answered above and in ch.7, in two attacks).
- Inner life: his wish to be alone "without a single person", asked of God "for a long time" (S1 ch.6, paraphrase). In the hut: he thanks God for a "sign for good" so that those who hate him may see God's help (S1 ch.7, paraphrase). Before the tonsure: "as the deer longs for the stream" (S1 ch.6, paraphrase).
- Testimony: none from a witness; no one saw it. Epiphanius says the devil wanted to drive him out because he feared a monastery would rise (S1 ch.7, paraphrase). Rights: facts-only.
- What changed: he stays; Epiphanius says the demons feared the future monastery (S1 ch.7).
- Not in the sources: the hour of night; the weather; Sergius's body in the scene; how long after the tonsure; whether the second attack was the same night. The wolves are in general terms ("flocks, howling, roaring"), not as one night's event. The note's "he drives them off with Psalms and the cross" matches the Life. No one else saw it.
- Sources: S1 ch.6, ch.7.

## SC-04 — The bear at the stump
- Status: full
- When and where: the hermitage, in the years alone, before the first monks came; "more than a year" (S1 ch.7). The first monks came "when two years had passed, or maybe more or less" (S1 ch.7).
- Who was there: Sergius; one bear. The Life calls it "arkuda, that is, a bear" (S1 ch.7 and the editor's note 46).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Many wild animals pass or stop near him. Some come close, circle and sniff him (S1 ch.7).
  2. One bear used to come. Sergius saw it came not from malice but to take a little food (S1 ch.7).
  3. He brought a small piece of bread out of his hut and laid it on a stump or a log, so the bear would find ready food, take the bread in its mouth, and go.
  4. If there was no bread, the bear did not go for a long time. It stood and looked around, "like a harsh creditor who wants his debt."
  5. If he had one piece only, he divided it in two, one for himself and one for the bear.
  6. His food in the wilderness was bread and spring water only, a little. Often there was no bread for a day, and both he and the bear stayed hungry.
  7. Sometimes he gave the bear his one piece and went hungry, because he preferred to fast rather than to cheat the animal and send it away without food.
  8. The bear came many times a day, not once or twice, and it lasted more than a year (S1 ch.7).
- Details: the stump or log; bread and spring water; the bear standing and looking around. Nothing on the bear's size or color; nothing on a tame touch or a name. Other accounts (S2 and others) of the bear eating from the hand are not in S1 ch.7 as I read it; I did not check them.
- Words spoken: none.
- Words of the narrator, the exact Russian (modern translation, S1 ch.7): «Когда хлеба не хватало и пришедший, по обыкновению, зверь не находил приготовленного для него привычного куска, он долгое время не уходил и стоял, озираясь по сторонам, как жестокий заимодавец, желающий получить свой долг». plain: "When there was not enough bread and the bear, as usual, did not find its ready piece, it did not go away for a long time. It stood and looked around, like a hard creditor who wants his debt."
  - The Russian word is «заимодавец» ("lender"); the "creditor" in our notes is right.
- Inner life: none given in this passage. A general line follows: he bore all trials with joy and thanked God (S1 ch.7, paraphrase).
- Testimony: none. Epiphanius tells it. Rights: facts-only; the single quotation above is under 40 words.
- What changed: nothing in the plot; it shows his habit of giving away his last bread, and Epiphanius uses it to say that when God lives in a man all creation obeys him (S1 ch.6 end, paraphrase).
- Not in the sources: the bear's age, size, color, or name; a touch or a feeding from the hand; a first meeting; the season; what Sergius said to the bear; any witness.
- Sources: S1 ch.6 (end), ch.7.
