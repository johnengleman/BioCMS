This list gathers the wonders attributed to St. Sergius of Radonezh in the sources we read, with the source and status of each. Sergius was venerated as a saint before the Russian Church had formal rules for canonization, so none of these accounts was examined by a Church commission. Most come from the Life written by his disciple Epiphanius the Wise about twenty-five years after his death, which survives only in the revision of Pachomius the Serb; the status line says when an account belongs to a later layer. Sergius himself tried to hide several of them, and the Life says so. The list makes no claim of scientific proof, and it does not dismiss any account.

During Sergius’s Life

Three cries before his birth, Rostov country

While his mother Maria stood at the Sunday Liturgy, the child in her womb cried out three times: at the Gospel, at the Cherubic Hymn, and at the exclamation “Holy Things for the Holy.” The women nearby asked what this child would be. Epiphanius compares the sign with those told of Ephrem the Syrian and Alypius. His brother Stefan later named the forest church for the Holy Trinity because of the three cries.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 2, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

An infant who kept the fast days

As a baby, Bartholomew would not take his mother’s milk on Wednesdays and Fridays, the fast days of the Church.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 4, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

The elder under the oak and the gift of reading

At seven Bartholomew could not learn his letters, and his parents, his teacher, and his schoolmates found fault with him. Sent to find his father’s lost foals, he met a black-robed elder praying under an oak and asked for his prayers. The elder gave him a piece of blessed bread, which tasted sweet as honey; at home the boy then read the Psalter clearly in the chapel. The elder foretold that the boy would be “a dwelling of the Holy Trinity” and vanished at the gate, and the parents believed he had been an angel.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 3, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Fragrance at his tonsure

When Hegumen Mitrofan tonsured him on 7 October, the feast of Sts. Sergius and Bacchus, the young monk received Communion and the church filled with fragrance. The Life, in Pachomius’s revision, gives his age as twenty-three.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 6, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Demons in Lithuanian caps

While Sergius sang Matins alone in his forest church, the wall opened and demons in Lithuanian clothes and tall caps rushed in and threatened him. He drove them out with the Psalms and the sign of the cross. Some days later they attacked again in his hut, and he drove them off again.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 7, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Wolves and bears in the forest

Wolves howled around his hut and bears passed by. The Life says he feared a little, like any man, and prayed, and the animals did him no harm.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 6, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

The bear at the stump

A bear came to Sergius for food, and he left bread for it on a stump or log. When there was no bread, the bear waited “like a harsh creditor wanting his debt.” When Sergius had only one piece, he broke it in two and gave the bear half, going hungry rather than cheat it. This lasted more than a year.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 7, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Fresh loaves at the gate after four days of hunger

When the monastery ran out of bread and salt, Sergius forbade the monks to beg. On the fourth day he built a porch for the old monk Daniil and was paid with a sieve of moldy bread. While the brothers grumbled and threatened to leave, someone knocked at the gate with a cart of fresh, warm loaves. The drivers named no one but a wealthy benefactor far away, and left without staying to eat.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry. The Life presents it as God’s providence. The drivers said only that a wealthy man far away had sent the bread, and the brothers never learned his name.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 10, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

The spring in the ravine

When the growing community lacked water and the brothers complained, Sergius prayed in a ravine beside a puddle of rainwater, and a spring rose there. It was still in use when the Life was written. When people called it Sergius’s spring, he was angry and said the Lord, not he, had given the water.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 12, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

A father’s dead child who lived

A man brought his sick infant son to Sergius, and the child died in his arms. While the father went to get a coffin, Sergius prayed on his knees, and the child lived. Sergius told the father the boy had only been cold and had warmed up in the cell, said that no one rises before the general resurrection, and forbade him to tell. The father did not dare to spread the news, but he could not keep from praising God for it, and the story became known through one of Sergius’s disciples.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry. Sergius himself denied that it was a miracle.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 13, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

An angel serving at the Liturgy

During a visit of Prince Vladimir, the monks Isaac the Silent and Makarii saw a fourth, shining priest serving behind Sergius, though the prince had brought no priest. Sergius told them that an angel of the Lord served with him whenever he served, and that they must tell no one while he lived.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 21, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Prophecy about Archimandrite Michael

In the chapter on Metropolitan Alexis’s last days, the Life records a prophecy of Sergius about Archimandrite Michael, who later died on the way to Constantinople.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry. Recorded briefly; the notes did not record its details.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 25, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

Seeing the battle against Mamai from afar

At the hour of Grand Prince Dmitri’s battle with Mamai, Sergius stood in prayer with his brothers and saw the battle from many days’ walk away. When the enemy was fully beaten he told the brothers of Dmitri’s courage, named the fallen, and prayed for them. The Life does not name the battlefield; the later Tale of the Mamai Rout places the blessing before Kulikovo (1380), and the historian Kuchkin links it to the battle on the Vozha (1378).

Status: in the Life as it survives (Pachomius’s revision, 1440–59); the sources read do not say whether it was in Epiphanius’s own text.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 22, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com; “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

The Mother of God with Peter and John

After praying the akathist before her icon, Sergius told his disciple Micah to be vigilant, for they would have a wondrous visit. A voice announced the Most Pure, and the Mother of God appeared in a light brighter than the sun, with the apostles Peter and John. She touched Sergius, told him not to be afraid, and promised that his monastery would have abundance in his life and after, and that she would not leave the place. Micah lay half dead from the light; Sergius later told Isaac and Simon. The monastery keeps the memory on 24 August (6 September civil).

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 26, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com; “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

Fire entering the chalice

Simon, the ecclesiarch, saw fire move about the altar during the Liturgy, then fold like a veil and enter the chalice as Sergius received Communion. Sergius forbade him to tell anyone until after his death.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 31, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

A flock of birds and a promise of disciples

One night a voice called Sergius, and a light brighter than day filled the darkness. He saw a great flock of beautiful birds about the monastery, and a voice told him that his disciples would be as many and would not dwindle after him if they followed in his footsteps. His disciple Simon saw only a gleam of the light.

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 14, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also Dimitry of Rostov, “Life of Our Venerable Father Sergius of Radonezh,” English translation, johnsanidopoulos.com.

Fragrance and a bright face at his death, 1392

Sergius died on 25 September 1392 after receiving Communion, held up by his disciples. The cell filled with fragrance, and his face was bright, “not like a dead man’s.”

Status: recorded in the earliest Life (Epiphanius, 1417–18, as kept in Pachomius’s revision). Not examined by a Church inquiry.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 32, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.

After Sergius’s Death

A dream and an incorrupt body, 1422

Thirty years after his death, Sergius appeared in a dream to a devout man and asked why he was left so long in the grave, covered with earth: “Water presses my body.” Hegumen Nikon opened the grave before princes, among them Sergius’s godson Yury Dmitrievich, and a council. Water stood on both sides, but the body and robe were whole and untouched by it. The relics were placed in a shrine on 5 July, in the year 1422 according to modern scholarship.

Status: added in Pachomius’s revision (1440–59). The date is also given by modern scholarship. The Orthodox Church keeps 5 July (18 July civil) as the feast of the finding of the relics.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, ch. 33, modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru; also “Sergii Radonezhsky,” Russian Wikipedia.

Later miracles listed in the Life

The later Life has more than fifty chapters of posthumous miracles and tales in chapters 34–88, among them healings at his tomb, a warning to a monk of the Trinity monastery during its siege (1608–10 in the edition’s notes), and a vision of the monastery shown to Don Cossacks in a sea battle. Simon Azarin added miracles to the Life in the seventeenth century. These chapters were not read in detail for this list, so the accounts are not told one by one here. A few of the chapters in the range are not miracles at all, such as the death of Nikon and a eulogy of Sergius.

Status: recorded in later layers of the Life; the chapter titles were checked, and the siege and Cossack chapters were skimmed.

Source: Epiphanius the Wise and Pachomius Logothetes, Life and Miracles of Sergius of Radonezh, chs. 34–88 (chapter list), modern Russian translation, Azbyka.ru.
