## SC-05 — The fourth day of hunger
- Status: full
- When and where: the Trinity monastery in the forest, early years of Sergius as abbot, before the common life was set up. No date in any source. The place is the monastery and Daniil's cell.
- Who was there: Sergius; the old monk Daniil; the brothers; the unknown men who bring bread.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Bread and food run short. Sergius forbids the brothers to leave the monastery to beg from lay people; they must ask God and wait. He keeps the same rule himself [S1 ch.10; S5 p.525].
  2. He endures three days; at dawn on the fourth he takes an axe and goes to Daniil [S1 ch.10; S5].
  3. He offers to build Daniil's porch. Daniil agrees and fears the price. Sergius asks only for old, rotten bread [S1 ch.10; S5].
  4. He works all day. In S1 he tells Daniil to keep the bread until the ninth hour because he takes no pay before the work is done. In the evening Daniil brings out a sieve of bread. Sergius lays it before him, prays, blesses it, and eats it with water; there is no soup, salt, or other drink [S1 ch.10].
  5. Some brothers see dust coming from his mouth from the rotten bread and talk about his patience. One monk grumbles because the brothers have eaten nothing for two days. The brothers go to Sergius and threaten to leave next day [S1 ch.10; S5 p.526].
  6. While Sergius is still speaking, someone knocks at the gate. Fresh bread has come from someone unknown. In S5 the carriers refuse to stay and eat and hurry away. In S2 the monks first ask the carriers to eat with them [S5; S2].
  7. In S1 Sergius tells the brothers to feed the carriers first (from the notes, not re-read here).
- Details: an axe; a sieve (решето) of rotten bread and dry cakes; water only; "dust" from the spoiled bread rising from his mouth [S1 ch.10; S5].
- Words spoken (exact Russian from S5, public domain; my English):
  - Sergius to Daniil: «Я слышал, старче, что ты хочешь пристроить сени к своей келлии; я желаю, чтобы руки мои не оставались праздными, посему и пришел к тебе; позволь мне построить сени.» Plain: "Old man, I hear you want to add a porch to your cell. I do not want my hands to sit idle, so I have come to you. Let me build the porch."
  - Daniil: «Да, я уже давно желаю сделать сени, даже заготовил всё необходимое; жду только плотника из деревни; подрядить же тебя я не решаюсь, ибо ты возьмешь с меня большую плату.» Plain: "Yes, I have wanted a porch for a long time. I have everything ready. I only wait for a carpenter from the village. I do not dare hire you, because you will charge me a lot."
  - Sergius: «Не сделав работы, я не беру плату.» Plain: "I take no pay before I have done the work."
  - Grumbling monk: «Доколе ты будешь запрещать нам выходить из монастыря и просить у мирян хлеба? Еще одну ночь мы перетерпим, а утром уйдем отсюда и не воротимся, чтобы нам не умереть с голода.» Plain: "How long will you forbid us to leave and ask lay people for bread? We will bear one more night, and in the morning we will leave and not come back, so that we do not die of hunger."
  - Sergius to the brothers: «Если же Он питает птиц, — говорил святый, — то неужели не может подать пищу нам? Вот, теперь время терпения, мы же ропщем. Если мы перенесем кратковременное испытание с благодарностию, то сие самое искушение послужит нам на большую пользу; ведь и золото не очищается без огня.» Plain: "If He feeds the birds, can He not give food to us? Now is the time for patience, and we complain. If we bear this short trial with thanks, the trial itself will do us great good. Even gold is not made pure without fire."
  - The carriers at the gate: «Вот, это один христолюбец прислал авве Сергию и братии, живущей с ним.» Plain: "A man who loves Christ sent this to Abba Sergius and the brothers who live with him."
  - S1 (modern translation, short): monks cry «У нас хлеб заплесневел!» Plain: "Our bread has gone moldy!" (S1 ch.10). The same S1 speech also has the monks say they obeyed Sergius's teaching and now they starve (paraphrase).
- Testimony (S5 pp.525-526, Dimitry of Rostov, public-domain; the three lines of the bargain above are the passage; no longer text needed).
- Inner life: Sergius's own words about the trial are the Gospel appeal to the birds and the line about gold and fire (S5 p.526). The notes record that in S1 he spoke the words about the monks' bread while they were grumbling.
- What changed: the monks saw bread come from nowhere; Sergius's rule not to beg stood, and visitors begin to come (outline).
- Not in the sources: Daniil's name for the donor, any date, the weather, whether Sergius shouted or wept. The donor is never named. S5 has no talk of mold between the monks and Sergius; the "mold" cry is from S1 only.
- Sources: S1 ch.10; S5 pp.525-526; S2.

## SC-06 — The farmer at the garden fence
- Status: full
- When and where: the Trinity monastery, in the vegetable garden and then Sergius's cell or the monastery yard. No date.
- Who was there: Sergius; the farmer from a distant village; the brothers; a prince with many servants. The prince has no name in S1 or S5.
- What happened, in order:
  1. A farmer from a far village comes to see Sergius. He asks the brothers where he is [S5 p.531; S1 ch.11].
  2. Sergius is digging in the garden. The brothers send the farmer to the fence; he sees through a gap an old man in a poor, torn, patched robe digging [S5; S1].
  3. He thinks they mock him. The brothers say that is the abbot. When Sergius comes out of the garden the farmer turns his back and laughs scornfully (S1) or refuses to look (S5) [S1; S5].
  4. The brothers, angry, tell Sergius. Sergius calls the farmer, sets a meal before him, and treats him warmly [S5]. In S1 the brothers want to throw the farmer out and Sergius forbids it (notes).
  5. While they talk, word comes that a prince has arrived. Sergius goes out to meet him. The prince bows to the ground from far off and asks his blessing [S5].
  6. The prince and Sergius sit side by side and talk. The servants push the farmer far back. He asks a bystander who the old man is and is told it is Sergius [S5].
  7. He accuses himself, trembles at his lack of faith, and when the prince leaves he goes to Sergius, bows at his feet, and asks forgiveness [S5].
- Details: "patched" robe (S1 and S5); the cloth that seven monks refused and Sergius took, which he wore until it fell apart [S5 p.530; S1 ch.11]; the garden fence with a gap.
- Words spoken:
  - Farmer (S1 ch.11, one short quotation): «Я пришел посмотреть на пророка, а вы мне показываете сироту.» Plain: "I came to see a prophet, and you show me an orphan."
  - Farmer (S5, exact Russian, public domain): «Сколько труда понапрасну понес я! Пришел взглянуть на великого пророка и надеялся увидеть его в большой чести и славе, а вы смеетесь надо мной, показываете какого-то слугу.» Plain: "What work I have done for nothing! I came to see a great prophet. I hoped to see him in great honor and glory, and you laugh at me and show me some servant."
  - Brothers: «Старец, виденный тобою, и есть преподобный отец наш.» Plain: "The old man you saw is our abbot."
  - Sergius (S5): «Не печалься, друг: по милости Божией, отсюда никто не уходит печальным, и твое желание исполнится.» Plain: "Do not be sad, friend. By God's mercy, no one leaves here sad, and your wish will be granted."
  - Bystander (S5): «Разве ты пришлец здесь, что не знаешь сего старца? Это — преподобный Сергий.» Plain: "Are you a newcomer here that you do not know this old man? This is the holy Sergius."
- Inner life: S5 comment: the vain man is proud when praised; the humble man is glad when dishonored (S5 p.531, paraphrase). Epiphanius draws the lesson that the farmer looked only at the outside (S1 ch.11, paraphrase).
- What changed: the farmer asks forgiveness. S5 says he later came back and took the monk's habit (S5 p.532, paraphrase; not in the notes for S1).
- Not in the sources: Sergius's own words to the brothers (S5 gives no direct words; the notes record a paraphrase only); the prince's name; the farmer's name or village; the farmer's words of forgiveness. "Orphan" is in S1 only; S5 says "servant".
- Sources: S1 ch.11; S5 pp.530-532.

## SC-07 — Envoys bring the Patriarch's letter
- Status: full (no date)
- When and where: the Trinity monastery, then the Metropolitan's house in Moscow. The date is not in the Life or in S5. The notes already record that the Life gives no date.
- Who was there: Sergius; envoys of Patriarch Philotheus of Constantinople; Metropolitan Alexis; the monks.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Envoys from Patriarch Philotheus bring a blessing, a paraman and a schema (S5). In S1 the text names a gold cross with a paraman, a schema, and a rule for common life [S1 passage in the praise section; chapter not noted]. S1 says the monastery then had no gold or silver and lacked necessities.
  2. They bring a letter, quoted below [S5 p.533].
  3. Sergius goes to Metropolitan Alexis, shows the letter and asks what to do [S5].
  4. Alexis approves and advises strict common life [S5]. In S1 Alexis says Sergius has been given a great honor and adds "we also thank you and give a bit of advice"; the text says Sergius received counsel, not orders from the two heads of the Church (paraphrase).
  5. Sergius sets up the common life: no monk acquires or calls anything his own; food, clothing, and housing are shared. He chooses officers for the table, the household, the treasury, the care of the sick, and the church [S5 p.534].
  6. S5: surplus goes to alms, guests, and the sick; he promised the monastery would stand as long as they kept the works of mercy [S5 p.534].
- Details: a paraman (a cloth worn on the back with a cross), a schema (the great habit); the Patriarch's letter cites Psalm 133 on brothers living together [S5].
- Words spoken (exact Russian, S5):
  - Patriarch's letter: «Милостию Божиею архиепископ Константина града, Вселенский Патриарх, господин Филофей сыну и сослужебнику нашего смирения о Святом Духе Сергию благодать и мир и наше благословение! Мы слышали о твоей добродетельной жизни по заповедям Божиим, восхвалили Бога и прославили Имя Его. Но вам еще недостает одного и притом самого главного: нет у вас общежития.» Plain: "By God's mercy, the Archbishop of Constantinople and Universal Patriarch, lord Philotheus, to Sergius, son and fellow servant of our humility in the Holy Spirit: grace, peace, and our blessing. We heard of your virtuous life by God's commandments, and we praised God and glorified His name. But one thing, and the most important, is still missing for you: you have no common life."
  - Sergius to Alexis: «Владыко святый, как ты прикажешь?» Plain: "Holy master, what do you command?"
  - S1 form (short, per the notes): «Одного правила вам недостает – у вас не устроено общежитие.» Plain: "One rule is missing for you: you have no common life."
- Testimony: the Patriarch's letter above (S5 p.533, public-domain; about 70 Russian words).
- What changed: the monastery becomes a common-life house; monks lose private property. The monks' reaction is not recorded in S1 or S5.
- Not in the sources: the date of the letter; the names of the envoys; how the monks received the change; what Sergius felt. A scholar's date range from S3 was not looked up in this pass.
- Sources: S5 pp.533-534; S1 (praise section, found by grep "золотой крест"); S1 ch.16 (notes).

## SC-08 — The gold cross
- Status: full
- When and where: the dying Metropolitan Alexis calls Sergius to him. The text gives no place (the Metropolitan's seat was Moscow, but the sources read do not say so for this scene). Alexis died 12 February 1378 (S5 p.542; S1 ch.25 gives 1378, per the notes). The year of the meeting is not given.
- Who was there: Alexis and Sergius. No one else is named.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Alexis, seeing his end near and wanting Sergius as his successor, summons him [S5 p.541].
  2. He holds out his Metropolitan's paraman with a gold cross set with jewels [S5].
  3. Sergius bows and refuses the gold [S5].
  4. Alexis says he knows Sergius has always lived so, and asks him to obey; he puts the paraman on him himself [S5].
  5. Alexis says he wants to find a man to follow him and finds none but Sergius, whom everyone, from the prince down, wants. He asks him to take the rank of bishop now and the see after his death [S5].
  6. Sergius is troubled, says it is beyond his strength and that he is "the last of all men" [S5].
  7. Alexis presses him; Sergius asks him not to speak of it again, or let anyone else [S5 pp.541-542].
  8. Alexis stops, afraid Sergius would flee to distant deserts and Moscow would lose its light, and sends him back to his monastery in peace [S5 p.542].
  9. After Alexis died, many begged Sergius again [S5 p.542; text cut off in this pass; the notes say he refused].
- Details: gold cross with jewels on the Metropolitan's paraman. S1 praise section says: Alexis laid on him a gold cross with paraman wishing to make him metropolitan, and he did not obey.
- Words spoken (exact Russian, S5):
  - Sergius: «Прости мне, Владыко святый, от юности не был я златоносцем, а в старости тем более желаю пребывать в нищете.» Plain: "Forgive me, holy master. From my youth I have not worn gold, and in old age I want even more to stay in poverty."
  - Alexis: «Возлюбленный, я знаю, что таково было всегда твое житие; теперь же покажи послушание и приими подаваемое тебе от нас благословение.» Plain: "Beloved, I know you have always lived so. Now show obedience and accept the blessing we give you."
  - Alexis: «Мне хорошо известно, что, начиная с князя и до последнего человека, все желают тебя. Итак, восприими теперь сан епископский, а после моей кончины займешь мой престол.» Plain: "I know well that everyone wants you, from the prince to the last man. So accept the rank of bishop now, and after my death you will take my seat."
  - Sergius: «Прости меня, Владыко, — ответил он святителю, — сие выше моих сил. Никогда не найдешь во мне, чего ищешь: я — грешный и самый последний из всех людей.» Plain: "Forgive me, master. This is beyond my strength. You will never find in me what you seek. I am a sinner and the last of all people."
  - Sergius: «Владыко святый, если ты не желаешь изгнать меня из сих пределов, так что и не услышишь обо мне — то не говори более о сем и не позволяй никому другому обращаться ко мне с такими речами: никто во мне не найдет на то согласия.» Plain: "Holy master, if you do not want to drive me out of this land so that you hear nothing more of me, do not speak of this again, and let no one else speak to me so. No one will find me willing."
  - S1 (praise section, short): «Золота, – сказал он, – я никогда не носил». Plain: "I have never worn gold."
- Testimony: the first Sergius line and Alexis's reply above (S5 p.541, public-domain).
- Inner life: S5 says Sergius, who thought himself unworthy of the rank, was greatly troubled in spirit (paraphrase).
- What changed: Sergius refuses; Alexis lets him go; Alexis dies in 1378.
- Not in the sources: the room, the hour, the Metropolitan's illness, any witness, tears, Sergius's reaction after he left. S5 is a retelling; Epiphanius's own wording (S1 ch.25, read in the first pass) has the shorter "from youth I did not wear gold, and in old age I wish to stay poor".
- Sources: S5 pp.541-542; S1 ch.25; S1 praise section.

## Request from the editor: the blessing before the war with Mamai and the name "Kulikovo" (SC-09 material; not a card)

Answer: no early source read in this pass ties the blessing to the Kulikovo field by name. The ties come from later texts.

1. The Life (S1 ch.22, title "On the victory over Mamai and the Dubenka monastery"): names Mamai and the "godless Tatars"; no battle name and no year. Sergius blesses Dmitri; Dmitri vows a monastery of the Mother of God; the Russians stop in doubt; a runner brings Sergius's letter [S1 ch.22; read in full today]. In S1 the letter says: «смело вступай в бой со свирепым врагом, не сомневаясь и не страшась его, помощь Божия будет во всем с тобою». Plain: "Go boldly into battle against the fierce enemy, without doubt or fear. God's help will be with you in everything." (S1, modern translation, 24 words.)
2. A modern editor's note in the S1 edition on the Dubenka monastery (note 156) says Kulikovo took place a few days before the Dormition feast. This is the editor's link, not the Life's.
3. S5 (Dimitry of Rostov, 1690s, public-domain) names the field: «7 сентября 1380 года ополчение достигло Дона, переправилось через него и расположилось на знаменитом поле Куликовом» (S5 pp.543-544; page marker "[544]" removed from the middle of "знаменитом"). Plain: "On 7 September 1380 the army reached the Don, crossed it, and camped on the famous Kulikovo field." Dimitry also says Sergius's messengers came on the morning of 8 September, with the blessing of the Trinity, a loaf of the Mother of God, and a letter that promised victory. He adds Peresvet and Oslyabya, whom Sergius sends with schemas.
4. The "Tale of the Mamai Rout" (Skazanie o Mamaevom pobo(i)shche), Old Russian, edition of 1838 (Russky Istorichesky Sbornik, vol. 3; Wikisource; public-domain). Page 51: «Въ тоже время пріидоша книги отъ преподобнаго игумена Сергія, в нихъ же написано есть: „Великому Князю и всѣмъ Рускимъ Княземъ миръ и благословеніе всемоу православному воинству."» Plain: "At that time a letter came from the holy abbot Sergius, in which it was written: 'Peace and blessing to the Grand Prince, to all the Russian princes, and to all the Orthodox army.'" Page 17: «Преподобныи же Сергіи дастъ же имъ от тлѣнныхъ оружіе вмѣсто нетлѣнныхъ, и много твердъ доспѣхъ крестъ Хрістовъ нашитъ на схимѣ.» Plain: "The holy Sergius gave them, in place of perishable weapons, an unperishable one, a strong armor: the cross of Christ sewn on the schema." The same text sends the visit to the Metropolitan Cyprian and dates the army's departure 22 August; it names the battle in the title as the battle of Dmitri Ivanovich "of the Don" (title: "Повѣданіе и сказаніе о побоищѣ великаго Князя Димитрія Ивановича Донскаго").
5. Modern view (S6 below, facts only): the detailed account of the blessing is in only one source on Kulikovo, the Skazanie; "in the chronicles there is nothing of the kind". Most historians put the Skazanie 100 to 150 years after 1380. V. A. Kuchkin (1988) argued the blessing belongs to the 1378 battle on the Vozha, because Cyprian was in Moscow in 1378 and not in 1380. D. Volodikhin (Foma, 18 Aug 2026) defends the traditional view but admits the Skazanie is a late source and its answers are uncertain.

Advice for the writer: say "the war with Mamai" in the story. Put in the source note: "The Life does not name the battle. Later tradition, from the Tale of the Mamai Rout and Dimitry of Rostov, places the blessing before Kulikovo (1380). The historian Kuchkin places it before the 1378 battle on the Vozha." The chronicles themselves were not read in this pass; I found no source that quotes a chronicle linking Sergius to Kulikovo.

S6 = Dmitry Volodikhin, "Благословлял ли Сергий Радонежский Дмитрия Донского?", Foma magazine, 18.08.2026. https://foma.ru/blagoslovlyal-li-sergiy-radonezhskiy-dmitriya-donskogo.html. Rights: facts-only.
