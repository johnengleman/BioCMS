# Saint Sergius of Radonezh — scene outline (v2, tier A limits)

Keys: S1–S4 as in notes.md. D = Saint Dimitry of Rostov, Russian (scenes-2 "S5", pages). B = Old Russian Life, BLDR vol. 6 (scenes-3 "S5"). T = Tale of the Rout of Mamai. L, PE, FM = Lavra page, Orthodox Encyclopedia, Foma/Murzin (scenes-3 S7–S9). Bio = approved biography.html, by chapter. "Card nn" = the existing scene cards, with their old numbers.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Sergius wanted one thing: to live before God alone, in "fasting and silence," poor and unseen [S1 ch.5 end, ch.6]. The life God gave him stood in the way: the stranger's word that he would "lead many" [S1 ch.3], then monks who begged to stay, hunger and thirst, a patriarch's rule, a metropolitan's gold cross, and a prince at war [S1 ch.7–12, 16, 22, 25]. Grace and his choices resolved it: he accepted that he "had wanted to live there alone, but God had wanted a monastery in that place" [S1 ch.12], stayed its poorest worker, refused every honor, and his lonely prayer became grief for his many disciples, which the Mother of God answered: "Do not grieve any more… I will not leave your monastery" [B; S1 ch.26].

Why it was beautiful to him: "As the deer longs for the stream, so my soul longs for monastic and desert life" [S1 ch.6, paraphrase]. At death he went, the Life says, to "the desired Jesus" [B]. The reader first hears the desire in ch.2, when he gives all to Petr and seeks a desert place.

Famous as founder of the Trinity Lavra, the abbot who blessed Grand Prince Saint Dmitri before the war with Mamai, and "Hegumen of the Russian land" [S1 ch.22; S3]. In an age when Moscow's governors hanged Rostov's elder boyar and a metropolitan's cross was gold with jewels, he dug his garden in cloth seven monks had refused [S1 ch.5, 11; D p.541]. He gave his house the common life, where no monk owned anything, and his disciples carried it to new monasteries [S1 ch.16; ch. titles 17–24].

## 2. Portrait

Quiet, steady, hard to provoke. He did not let himself laugh; he smiled "with great modesty" [S1 ch.5]. Wolves frightened him "a little, like any man," and he prayed [S1 ch.6]. As a boy he was slow and wept alone over his letters [S1 ch.2 end]. He obeyed before he chose: he waited for his parents' deaths, and took the abbacy only when his brothers threatened to leave [S1 ch.5, 7–8].

His gift was patience with grumbling men. He corrected a noisy monk next morning "indirectly, quietly and gently, as if telling parables" [S1 ch.9]. He worked first and hardest, with axe and spade [S1 ch.10–11]. People were surprised at how small he made himself: a farmer took him for an orphan, and a spring named after him made him angry [S1 ch.11–12]. He would rather leave the land than take high office [D p.542].

His life with God was psalm, liturgy, and the Mother of God. He met terror with "Let God rise" [S1 ch.7] and prayed to the "Most Pure Mother of my Christ, mediator and defender" [B]. He spoke of God as the giver: "the Lord gave it" [S1 ch.12].

## 3. The opening

Hook: the paradox of the place. It tells no scene the body tells and does not touch the climax. Draft:

> For a long time Saint Sergius had asked God for one thing: to live without a single person near him. In a forest near Radonezh, God gave it to him. He had a hut and a small wooden church, no road led to them, and wolves came by night and by day. Today that place is the Trinity Lavra, the great shrine of the Russian Church. The Church calls the man who wanted to be alone there the Hegumen of the Russian land, the abbot of all Russia.

[S1 ch.5–6; S3]. Do not use the farmer at the fence here; the body tells it in ch.5.

Thesis: He founded the monastery that became the Trinity Lavra and gave it the common life at the Patriarch's request [S1 ch.5, 16; D pp.533–534]. His disciples carried it to Kirzhach, Simonov, Dubenka, Golutvin, and Vysoky [S1 ch. titles; S4]. He refused to become Metropolitan and blessed Saint Dmitri before the war [D pp.541–542; B]. Answered in ch.6, 7, 8, 12.

Ending: dates and glorification in ch.11; a short close in ch.12. Closing image: Holy Saturday, 20 April 1946, the eve of Pascha: the state gives his holy relics back to the Church; today they rest in the Trinity church Saint Nikon built over his grave [S3; S1 ch.33]. One quiet sentence after: this is the place where he once asked to live without a single person near him. What he offers a reader today, a love of God that made room for everyone who came, stays unstated.

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points, told slowly from inside him:** SC-01, SC-03, SC-05, SC-10, SC-12 (climax). Tell the rest shorter.

**Resistance in each period:** boyhood: letters (SC-01). Youth: his parents make him wait; Stefan leaves (SC-02). Hermit: fear and the demons' "Run" (SC-03). Early abbot: hunger and revolt (SC-05). Middle abbot years: scorn and his brothers' anger (SC-06), thirst and praise (SC-07), trouble in the house if confirmed (SC-09), the gold cross (SC-10). Last years: grief for his monks (SC-12). Compress all else to a few sentences per period.

**Relationships to their end:** Stefan: builds and leaves (SC-02), returns with Ivan (ch.4); his end is a fact need. Saint Alexis: counsel (SC-08), the cross, his death on 12 Feb 1378 (SC-10). Saint Dmitri: blessing and vow (SC-11); his son Yury, baptized by Saint Sergius, stands at the opened grave (SC-14).

**Losses at their dates:** Rostov and his parents (ch.2); Stefan (SC-02); solitude, one concrete day (SC-04); Saint Alexis (SC-10); the war dead, named one by one (SC-11); his repose (SC-13).

### SC-01 — The stranger under the oak (boyhood; a field on Rostov land, then the family chapel) [TP1]
- What happens: Sent for foals, the boy who cannot read finds an old monk praying in tears, eats holy bread sweet as honey, reads the psalm at home, and hears "a dwelling of the Holy Trinity."
- Why it is here: the call.
- Have: all, with his prayer in tears and Ps. 118:103 [card 01; S1 ch.2–3]. Need: nothing.

### SC-02 — Two brothers build a church; Stefan leaves (forest near Radonezh, under Grand Prince Simeon)
- What happens: He gives all to Petr and fetches Stefan. They carry logs and build hut, cell, and church; Stefan names it for the Holy Trinity. Stefan finds no food, road, or people, and goes to Moscow.
- Why it is here: the first decision, the desire named, the first parting. End with the Life's line: one brother chose a city monastery, the other turned the wilderness into a city.
- Have: all [card 02; S1 ch.5]. Need: his response to Stefan's leaving, if any (S1 ch.5–6).

### SC-03 — The tonsure and the night the wall opened (the forest church) [TP2]
- What happens: On 7 Oct Mitrofan makes him a monk ("as the deer longs for the stream") and leaves him with Saint Paul's word on temptation. Alone, he fears the wolves "a little." At night prayer, demons in Lithuanian caps break through the wall: "Run, go away from here." He answers: "Let God rise."
- Why it is here: the desire granted and first tested.
- Have: all [card 03; S1 ch.6–7]. Need: nothing.

### SC-04 — The bear at the stump (the hermitage, more than a year)
- What happens: One piece of bread, broken in two for the bear, or given whole; with none, the bear waits "like a hard creditor."
- Why it is here: the cost of solitude in one concrete day.
- Have: all [card 04; S1 ch.7]. Need: nothing.

### SC-05 — The fourth day of hunger (the young monastery) [TP3]
- What happens: He forbids begging, then builds Daniil's porch for moldy bread. The monks threaten to leave; "Even gold is not made pure without fire." Carriers bring bread from "a rich man who loved Christ"; he feeds them first and gives thanks before eating.
- Why it is here: his poverty against his monks' hunger; God answers.
- Have: all [card 05; S1 ch.10]. Testimony: D pp.525–526. Need: nothing.

### SC-06 — The farmer at the garden fence (the monastery, middle years)
- What happens: "You show me an orphan." Saint Sergius stops his angry brothers, bows, and feeds the farmer; a prince bows to him; the farmer asks pardon and later becomes a monk.
- Why it is here: scorn; he takes the low place.
- Have: all [card 06; S1 ch.11; D pp.530–532]. Need: nothing.

### SC-07 — The spring in the ravine (the growing monastery)
- What happens: "Why did you settle where there is no water?" He wanted to live there alone, he says, but God wanted a monastery. He prays by a rainwater ditch, and a spring rises. Its name angers him: the Lord gave it.
- Why it is here: the hinge of the driving question, in his words.
- Have: [S1 ch.12; Bio ch.5]. Need: the exact Russian of his answer, to quote it.

### SC-08 — The Patriarch's letter (the monastery, then Moscow; no date)
- What happens: "You have no common life." "Holy master, what do you command?" Saint Alexis counsels it; no monk owns anything; his promise on the works of mercy.
- Why it is here: his poverty becomes the rule of many houses.
- Have: all [card 07; D pp.533–534]. Testimony: the letter, D p.533. Need: nothing.

### SC-09 — Saint Sergius leaves for Kirzhach (CONDITIONAL)
- What happens: unknown; S1 ch.17 was not read.
- Why it is here: the middle years need one trouble inside the brotherhood. If S1 ch.17 has none, cut the card.
- Need: why he left and who was involved; his words at leaving and return; Stefan's part. Where: S1 ch.17–18; D after p.534.

### SC-10 — The gold cross (before 12 Feb 1378) [TP4]
- What happens: "From my youth I have not worn gold." Saint Alexis asks him to take the bishop's rank: "I am a sinner and the last of all people… do not speak of this again." Saint Alexis, afraid he will flee, sends him home. After Saint Alexis dies, he refuses again.
- Why it is here: the last great honor; inside, "greatly troubled in spirit" [D].
- Have: all [card 08; D pp.541–542]. Need: his response to Saint Alexis's death (S1 ch.25; D p.542).

### SC-11 — The prince's question and the runner's letter
- What happens: "With God helping you, you will win." The vow; the army in doubt; a runner with "go boldly." At prayer far away, he names the fallen and prays for each. Gift, Dubenka, Saint Savva.
- Why it is here: his word steadies the land; the Dmitri thread; the war dead.
- Have: all [card 09; B]. Testimony: B. Need: Saint Dmitri's later contact and death (S1 ch.22–24; S3).

### SC-12 — The Mother of God at the porch (his cell, at night) [TP5, CLIMAX]
- What happens: The akathist and his prayer; "Child, be sober and keep watch." Light brighter than the sun; she comes with Saint Peter and Saint John, touches him, and gives her promise. Micah lies as if dead; "my spirit also trembles." He tells Isaac and Simon and stays awake all night.
- Why it is here: the man who asked for no one grieves for his many, and heaven promises to keep them. Open straight into the prayer: no dates, feasts, or other wonders in this chapter.
- Have: all [card 10; B]. Need: nothing.

### SC-13 — The repose (the monastery, 25 Sept 1392)
- What happens: Saint Nikon named six months before; silence; illness; last teaching and last words; Communion held up by his disciples; hands raised; fragrance; a face like snow.
- Why it is here: the death; he goes to "the desired Jesus" [B].
- Have: all [card 11; B]. Need: nothing.

### SC-14 — The grave is opened (5 July, thirty years after)
- What happens: A dream: "The water presses on my body!" Saint Nikon, princes, and a council, with Yury among them, find body and robe whole between standing water.
- Why it is here: God glorifies him; the Dmitri thread closes.
- Have: all [card 12; S1 ch.33]. Need: nothing.

### SC-15 — Two old men on the walls (the siege, 1608–10)
- What happens: The soldier chief Andrei and his men desert to the monastery and swear they saw a river where none runs and two gray-haired old men shouting from the walls; the assault failed.
- Why it is here: a named case after death; the promise "after your departure."
- Have: all [card FN-2; S1 ch.63]. Need: nothing.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** none; "spiritual instructions" only [S3]. Last teaching, 1392 [B] → ch.10. Saint Epiphanius's Life, 1417–18 [S3] → ch.11.
- **What he changed:** the common life [S1 ch.16; D] → ch.6, 12; daughter houses [S1 ch. titles; S4] → ch.6; disciples who are saints: Saint Nikon, Saint Savva, Saint Theodore, Saint Micah [S1 ch.9, 32–33; B; S3] → ch.6, 8, 12; Saint Nikon's church and the Trinity icon, 1425–27 [PE; L] → ch.11.
- **Why famous:** the blessing [B; T] → ch.8; the finding of the relics and the healings at the shrine [S1 ch.33] → ch.11; the siege [S1 ch.63] → ch.12; patron of Russia, and of students by custom [S3; FM] → ch.12.
- **Path to sainthood:** relics found 5 July 1422; Saint Jona's letter, 1449–50; glorified then, 1452 in some encyclopedias [S1 ch.33; S3] → ch.11. Opened 1919, Solikamsk 1941–44, returned Holy Saturday 1946 [S3] → ch.12.

**Fact needs** (scene needs are in SC-02, 07, 09, 10, 11):
1. The Kirzhach departure: S1 ch.17–18; D after p.534. It decides SC-09.
2. Stefan's later life and death: S1 ch.17; Saint Epiphanius's preface; S3.
3. Saint Dmitri's death and any part Saint Sergius had in it: S1 ch.22–24; S3; PE.

## 8. What we leave out

- Fire in the chalice and the 24 Aug feast: they would crowd the climax.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- No birth year in the story; the age of 78 is the Life's.
- Repose 25 Sept 1392 (Julian; 8 Oct civil); relics 5 July, 1422 in most accounts. Use "reposed," "glorified," "holy relics."
- No battle name in the Life: "later accounts" give Kulikovo, 1380; Vozha 1378 in the Note. Keep "With God helping you."
- No "cell" for the blessing or deathbed; no "Makovets"; no "Donskoy." The bear eats from a stump, not his hand.
- Night rounds: "indirectly… as if telling parables." The bread giver: the carriers' report only. The spring was named by "people." Yury was "baptized by the saint."
- Simon of ch.26 and ch.31 is one man; Isaac the Silent is not shown to be the Isaac of ch.26.
- "Saint" before every saint's name, not before Stefan, Petr, Mitrofan, Daniil, Yury, or Andrei the soldier.
