# Sergius of Radonezh — scene outline (tier B)

## 1. The driving question

Sergius wanted one thing from boyhood: to live alone with God in the forest, poor and unseen [S1 ch.5, ch.6]. What stood in the way was the word of the stranger under the oak, that he would lead many [S1 ch.3]: monks came, hunger came, then a patriarch, a metropolitan and princes came [S1 ch.7, 10, 16, 22, 25]. Grace answered with the spring, the bread at the gate and the Mother of God's promise; his own choice was to stay the poorest worker among his monks, to refuse gold and high office, and to give God the credit [S1 ch.10-12, 25, 26].

He is famous as the founder of the Trinity monastery, the abbot who blessed the Grand Prince of Moscow before the war with Mamai, and the patron of Russia [S1 ch.22; S3]. In an age when Moscow's governors hanged a Rostov elder and Church leaders held land and wealth, he dug his own garden and wore cloth the other monks refused [S1 ch.5, ch.11; S3]. He brought the common life to his monastery, where no monk owned anything, and his disciples carried monasteries across the land [S1 ch.16; S1 ch. titles 17-24; S4].

## 2. Portrait

Sergius was quiet, steady and hard to provoke. Epiphanius says he did not allow himself to laugh, and when he smiled he did it "with great modesty" [S1 ch.5]. He was not fearless: when wolves howled round his hut, "he feared a little, like any man," and prayed [S1 ch.6]. He was slow as a child and could not learn letters while his brothers did [S2; S1 ch.3].

His gift was patience under grumbling. Monks complained about hunger and about water, and threatened to leave; he did not scold [S1 ch.10, 12]. On his night rounds he knocked on a noisy cell and walked on, and spoke to the monk the next morning, "from afar," without anger [S1 ch.9]. He worked first and hardest: he built a porch for bread, dug the garden, and wore a robe until it fell apart [S1 ch.10-11].

What surprised people was how small he made himself. A farmer took him for an orphan [S1 ch.11]. He was angry when monks called a spring by his name [S1 ch.12]. He split his last bread with a bear rather than cheat it [S1 ch.7].

His life with God was liturgy and psalm. He drove off terrors with Psalms and the cross [S1 ch.7]. He asked for monastic life "as the deer longs for the stream" [S1 ch.6, paraphrase]. At death he entrusted his monks "to the Almighty Lord and his Most Pure Mother" [S1 ch.32, paraphrase].

## 3. The opening

Hook: the paradox at the garden fence (SC-06), told short. Draft:

> A farmer walked a long way to see the famous abbot of the Trinity monastery. At the garden he found a man in a patched robe, digging. He would not believe this was the abbot. "I came to see a prophet," he said, "and you show me an orphan." The man with the spade bowed to the ground before him, kissed him, and sat him down to eat. Then a prince arrived with his soldiers, and the prince bowed to the same man.

[S1 ch.11]. The opening tells only this much; the full scene returns in chapter 5.

Thesis (after the opening): Sergius began a monastery in the forest near Radonezh that became the Trinity Lavra [S1 ch.5; S3]. He gave it the common life, where no monk owned anything, at the Patriarch of Constantinople's request [S1 ch.16]. He founded or helped found other monasteries [S1 ch. titles 17-24; S4], refused to head the Russian Church [S1 ch.25], and blessed the Grand Prince before the war with Mamai [S1 ch.22]. The Church calls him "Hegumen of the Russian land" [S3]. The biography answers this in chapters 6, 7, 8 and 12.

## 4. Chapters

1. **The Boy Who Could Not Read Meets a Stranger** — SC-01. Bridge: his fasting as a boy and his mother's worry [S1 ch.4]. Ends: the stranger's word about "the Holy Trinity", and the family's fortune about to fall.
2. **Two Brothers Build a Church in the Forest** — bridge: Rostov ruined, the move to Radonezh, the parents' death [S1 ch.5]; SC-02. Ends: Stefan walks away, and Bartholomew is alone.
3. **Sergius Lives Alone With Wolves and a Bear** — bridge: tonsure at 23 [S1 ch.6]; SC-03, SC-04. Ends: after two years or more, a stranger knocks and asks to stay [S1 ch.7].
4. **Monks Come and the Bread Runs Out** — bridge: his warnings to newcomers, made abbot, night rounds [S1 ch.7-9]; SC-05. Ends: the bread from nowhere, and visitors begin to come.
5. **A Farmer Mistakes Sergius for a Beggar** — SC-06; one sentence on the spring and his anger at its name [S1 ch.12]. Ends: news of the poor abbot reaches Constantinople.
6. **A Letter From Constantinople Changes the Monastery** — SC-07; bridge: monasteries founded by Sergius and his disciples [S1 ch. titles 17-24; S4]. Ends: the old Metropolitan Alexis sends for him.
7. **Sergius Refuses to Lead the Russian Church** — SC-08. Ends: Alexis dies in 1378, and Mamai gathers the Horde [S1 ch.22, 25].
8. **Sergius Blesses the Prince of Moscow Before Battle** — SC-09. Ends: the victory, the Dubenka monastery, and Sergius back at prayer [S1 ch.22].
9. **The Mother of God Visits Sergius** — SC-10; one or two sentences on the angel and the fire seen at his liturgy, which he told the witnesses to keep secret [S1 ch.21, 31]. Ends: he chooses Nikon to follow him [S1 ch.32].
10. **The Death of Sergius** — SC-11. Ends: thirty years of quiet at the grave.
11. **Monks Find His Body Whole Thirty Years Later** — SC-12; bridge: Nikon's stone church, the Life by Epiphanius, the first veneration [S1 ch.33; S3]. Ends: the monastery grows into a shrine for all Russia.
12. **How Sergius Changed the Russian Church** — the common life, the daughter monasteries, the disciples who became saints, the patronage of Russia and of students [S1 ch.16; S3; S4]. Ends: a revolution tries to end the shrine.
13. **His Relics Survive the Soviet Years** — 1919 opening, 1941-44 in Solikamsk, Holy Saturday 1946 [S3]. Closes the loop: the poor man the farmer took for an orphan still draws people to his grave, and the last line returns to his own words that the Lord, not he, gave the water.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — The stranger under the oak, boyhood (a field near the family village, Rostov land)
- What happens: Sent to find lost foals, the boy who cannot learn letters finds an old monk praying with tears under an oak. He asks for prayer; the monk gives him blessed bread, sweet as honey, and at home the boy reads the psalter aloud.
- Why it is here: Plants the question: a slow boy is told he will lead many.
- Have: the school failure, the teacher's beatings, weeping in secret [S2; S1 ch.3]; the foals, the oak, the boy waits for the prayer to end; "Open your mouth, child, take this and eat"; reads clearly; the stranger vanishes at the gate; "Your son will be a dwelling of the Holy Trinity and will lead many" [S1 ch.3; S2].
- Need: the boy's own request to the stranger, in Russian; his age if S1 ch.3 gives it; what the parents said to the stranger at the meal.
- Testimony: none.
- Where to look: S1 ch.3; S2.

### SC-02 — Two brothers build a church in the forest (near Radonezh)
- What happens: After the parents die, Bartholomew gives his share to Petr and goes with Stefan, a widowed monk, to a thicket by water. They carry logs on their shoulders and build a hut and a small church; Stefan names it for the Holy Trinity. Soon Stefan finds the place too hard and leaves for Moscow.
- Why it is here: His first free choice, and a hard part: his own brother cannot bear the life.
- Have: all events [S1 ch.5]; the three cries before birth as Stefan's reason [S1 ch.2, ch.5]; consecration by Metropolitan Theognostus's priests [S1 ch.5].
- Need: Stefan's words or the Life's words when he left; anything Bartholomew said when they chose the spot.
- Where to look: S1 ch.5.

### SC-03 — Night prayer and the wall that opens (the forest church, first years alone)
- What happens: Wolves howl round the hut and he is afraid "a little, like any man," and prays. At matins the church wall opens, and demons in Lithuanian clothes and tall caps rush at him. He drives them off with Psalms and the cross.
- Why it is here: Life with God: prayer under real fear, alone.
- Have: tonsure on 7 Oct by Mitrofan, seven days in the church [S1 ch.6]; wolves, bears, fear [S1 ch.6]; the attack [S1 ch.7].
- Need: the psalm verse he sang; what the demons shouted.
- Where to look: S1 ch.6-7.

### SC-04 — The bear at the stump (the hermitage)
- What happens: A bear comes for food. He sets bread on a stump; if there is none, the bear waits "like a harsh creditor wanting his debt." With one piece left, he breaks it in two. This lasts more than a year.
- Why it is here: Shows his poverty as kindness, not hardness.
- Have: all of it [S1 ch.7].
- Need: nothing beyond the exact Russian of the "creditor" line.
- Where to look: S1 ch.7.

### SC-05 — The fourth day of hunger (the monastery, early years as abbot)
- What happens: Bread and salt run out; he forbids begging. On the fourth morning he takes an axe and builds a porch for the old monk Daniil for a sieve of moldy bread. The monks grumble and threaten to leave. While he speaks, fresh loaves arrive at the gate from an unknown giver, and he feeds the carriers first.
- Why it is here: The hard part: his monks turn against his poverty, and he holds to it.
- Have: all events [S1 ch.10]; "our bread has gone moldy" [S1 ch.10].
- Need: Daniil's bargain in his own words; Sergius's words to the grumbling monks.
- Where to look: S1 ch.10.

### SC-06 — The farmer at the garden fence (the monastery)
- What happens: The farmer finds Sergius digging and refuses to believe he is the abbot. Sergius bows, kisses him, feeds him, and stops the monks from throwing him out. A prince arrives and bows to Sergius; the farmer trembles.
- Why it is here: The whole question in one scene; also the hook.
- Have: all events and the "orphan" line [S1 ch.11]; the patched sermyaga and the refused cloth [S1 ch.11].
- Need: Sergius's words to the monks; the prince's name if given; the farmer's words after he learns the truth.
- Where to look: S1 ch.11.

### SC-07 — Envoys bring the Patriarch's letter (the monastery, then Moscow)
- What happens: Greek envoys bring a cross, a paraman, a schema and a letter from Patriarch Philotheus: "One rule is missing: you have no common life." Sergius takes it to Metropolitan Alexis, who agrees. He sets up a cellarer, cooks, bakers and an infirmarian, and no monk owns anything.
- Why it is here: His own poverty becomes the rule of a whole community.
- Have: [S1 ch.16; S2]; quote 1.
- Need: how the monks received the change; the date range from scholars in S3.
- Where to look: S1 ch.16-17; S3.

### SC-08 — The gold cross (Metropolitan Alexis's presence, before 1378)
- What happens: The dying Alexis gives him a gold cross with jewels and asks him to become metropolitan. Sergius says he has not worn gold since youth and wishes to stay poor in old age, and refuses.
- Why it is here: The highest office is offered; his choice answers the question.
- Have: [S1 ch.25]; quote 2 (paraphrase).
- Need: the full exchange in Russian; where it took place.
- Where to look: S1 ch.25.

### SC-09 — The Grand Prince in the cell, and the letter at the camp (1380)
- What happens: Dmitri asks whether to march against Mamai. Sergius blesses him and tells him to go against the godless; Dmitri vows a monastery. On the road the army sees the Tatar host and stops in doubt, and a messenger brings Sergius's letter: go boldly.
- Why it is here: The poor abbot's word steadies a nation; he stays at his prayer.
- Have: [S1 ch.22; S2; S3]; letter [S4, quote 10]; at the hour of battle he names the fallen and prays [S1 ch.22; S2].
- Need: the exact blessing in S1 ch.22; the messenger's name if any.
- Testimony: the letter in S4 (public domain, 1922).
- Where to look: S1 ch.22; S4.

### SC-10 — The Mother of God at the cell (the monastery)
- What happens: After the akathist he tells Micah to keep watch. A light brighter than the sun; she comes with Peter and John and touches him: "Do not be afraid, my chosen one," and promises she will not leave the monastery. Micah lies as if dead.
- Why it is here: Life with God; the promise answers his fear for his monks.
- Have: [S1 ch.26; S2]; quote 7.
- Need: what he told Isaac and Simon afterward; the icon he prayed before.
- Where to look: S1 ch.26.

### SC-11 — The death of Sergius (his cell, 25 Sept 1392)
- What happens: He falls ill in September and gives his last teaching. He takes Communion held up by his disciples, raises his hands, prays and dies. Fragrance fills the cell, and his face is bright.
- Why it is here: The death.
- Have: Nikon named six months before; the teaching; age 78 in the Life [S1 ch.32]; quote 13.
- Need: the last prayer in Russian, if S1 gives it.
- Where to look: S1 ch.32.

### SC-12 — The grave is opened (the Trinity monastery, 5 July 1422)
- What happens: A man dreams that Sergius asks why he is left so long in a wet grave. Nikon opens it before princes, among them Yury Dmitrievich, Sergius's godson. Water stands at both sides, but the body and robe are whole.
- Why it is here: The real case after death; the start of the shrine.
- Have: [S1 ch.33; S3]; Nikon's stone church painted by Daniil and Andrei [S1 ch.33].
- Need: the dreamer's name; the other princes present.
- Where to look: S1 ch.33.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** He left none; only "spiritual instructions" are known [S3]. His last teaching, 1392 [S1 ch.32] → ch.10. Epiphanius wrote the Life in 1417-18 after 20 years of collecting, with Stefan's stories, and a "Praise" [S1 book note; S3] → ch.11.
- **The common life:** the cenobitic rule at the Patriarch's request, with Alexis's consent [S1 ch.16] → ch.6, ch.12.
- **Monasteries:** Kirzhach, Simonov, Dubenka, Golutvin, Vysoky; Andronikov by Alexis [S1 ch. titles; S4] → ch.6, ch.12.
- **Disciples:** Nikon, his successor [S1 ch.32-33]; his nephew Theodore of Simonov [S1 ch.9]; Savva of Dubenka and others [S2 label list, not read] → ch.12.
- **Refused the metropolitan see** before 1378 [S1 ch.25] → ch.7.
- **Why famous:** the Grand Prince's blessing (1380) [S1 ch.22]; the 1422 finding [S1 ch.33]; about 55 later miracles in S1 ch.34-88 [S1 chapter list]; Varnitsy monastery at his birthplace, 1427 [S3]; patron of Russia and of students [S3] → ch.8, 11, 12.
- **Path to sainthood:** relics found 5 July 1422 [S1 ch.33]; Metropolitan Jona's letter of 1449-50 calls him a saint [S3]; 1452 in some encyclopedias, with no single act [S3] → ch.11.
- **The Soviet years:** relics opened 11 April 1919; Solikamsk 1941-44; returned on Holy Saturday 1946 [S3] → ch.13.

**Fact needs:**
1. Whether "Andrei" in Nikon's church is Andrei Rublev, and the link of the Rublev Trinity icon to the Lavra (S1 ch.33 notes; S3 captions).
2. One posthumous case with a name and year, such as the 1608-10 siege appearances (S1 ch.34-88 chapter text).
3. Why he is patron of students, and whether it comes from SC-01 (S3).

## 7. Coverage check

1. Famous as a saint: ch.8, 11, 12.
2. Whole life: ch.1-10.
3. Hardships: ch.2 (family ruin, Stefan leaves), ch.3, ch.4, ch.5 (grumbling).
4. Famous deeds: ch.2, 6, 8.
5. Writings: ch.10, 11, 12 (none survive; his teaching and the Life).
6. Influence on the Church: ch.6, 12.

8. Inspiration: ch.4, 5, 7.
9. Distinctive: ch.5, 7, 12.

## 8. What we leave out

- Peresvet and Oslyabya: from the later "Tale of the Mamai Rout", not the Life [S3].
- The Genoese trade deal: one modern reading [S3].
- The dead child who "grew warm": a miracle; goes to the miracles section.
- The vision of the birds: S2 only, not checked in S1.
- The second demon attack, and the prophecy about Archimandrite Michael: repetition.
- The presbyter in Latin lands (S2): unverified.
- The later miracles (S1 ch.34-88): miracles section.
- The head hidden by Florensky: tradition, in conflict with the 1919 protocol; at most one sentence in "A Note on the Sources".
- The Catholic 1969 martyrology entry: unverified.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth: 1314 (Pachomius line) or 1322 (Epiphanius line) [S3]. Do not give one year as fact.
- Death: 25 Sept 1392 (Julian) = 8 Oct (civil). Relics: 5 July 1422 = 18 July civil. Say which calendar.
- "Makovets" is in none of the sources. Say "the forest near Radonezh".
- The Life never says "Kulikovo" and gives no year; Kuchkin links the blessing to the Vozha battle of 1378 [S3]. Write "the war with Mamai" in the story; put Kulikovo in the source note.
- The early text says he will win "if God helps"; later texts make it a firm prophecy [S3]. Use the letter's "go boldly" [S4].
- Quote S1 and S2 only briefly, in our own English; S4 is public domain.
- Names: Bartholomew (birth), Sergius (tonsure name), Stefan, Petr, Kirill and Maria, Theognostus, Alexis, Philotheus, Mitrofan, Daniil, Micah, Isaac the Silent, Makarii, Simon, Nikon, Yury Dmitrievich.
- Introduce Dmitri as the Grand Prince of Moscow; "Donskoy" is not in the notes.
- The Rostov violence: Moscow's governors hanged the elder boyar Averky [S1 ch.5]; state it plainly.
