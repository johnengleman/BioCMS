1. Biography, night rounds: "In the morning he called the monk over and spoke to him from a distance, without anger, until the man understood." The notes give S1's word as "from afar" (Russian "издалека"). Used of speech, that word usually means "indirectly" or "in a roundabout way". A reader will see physical distance. Check S1 ch. 9 and, if it fits, write "spoke to him indirectly".
2. Biography, spring: "People began to call it Sergius's spring, and that made him angry." The name has no "Saint", but it is the name people used (notes.md: "Sergius's spring"). The miracles list writes "Saint Sergius's spring". The owner should decide which form to use.
3. Who named the spring. Biography ch. 5 and miracles m09 say "people" named it. The last paragraph of the biography and the summary say "the monks named a spring after him". A checker should choose one, from S1 ch. 12.
4. Miracles, fortress: "The iron gates opened for them without a word and closed again behind them." Gates cannot speak. The source may mean "without a sound" or "with no one saying a word". Check S1 ch. 47.
5. Biography: "A disciple named Simon looked after the church." The text already named a disciple Simon (with Isaac, at the vision of the Mother of God). If he is the same man, write "His disciple Simon looked after the church". I do not know if he is.

(The source also says he did not rebuke him with anger or punish him.)
2. The spring's name. "Saint Sergius's spring" in the biography (the spring paragraph) and in the miracles list (m09). The closing paragraph and the summary name no spring title, so they need no change.
3. Who named the spring (S1 ch. 12). The source says "с того времени источник в течение десяти или пятнадцати лет называли Сергиевым", an unnamed "they". Then Saint Sergius says "вы называете" to those who used the name. It never says the monks did it. All three places now say "people": biography spring paragraph ("For ten or fifteen years people called it Saint Sergius's spring, and the name made him angry"), biography closing ("was angry when people named a spring after him"), summary ("when people named a spring after him"), and m09 ("For ten or fifteen years people called it Saint Sergius's spring").

Now the fortress account in the miracles list (ch. 47) reads: "Someone opened the iron gates for them and said nothing, and the gates closed again behind them."
