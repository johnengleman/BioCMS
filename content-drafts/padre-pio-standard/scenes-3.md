## SC-11 — A summer morning, 1950s (the old church)
- Status: thin (no dated confession by a named penitent; the rest is full)
- When and where: no dated morning. McCaffery describes the 1950s in general: the old church of Santa Maria delle Grazie, Mass at the side altar of St Francis. [N-1 p.56, p.77–78, p.64]
- Who was there: Padre Pio; the women pilgrims McCaffery calls "Holy Women"; Mrs Ghisleri (a first-time visitor, wife of Gino Ghisleri); McCaffery himself, who served his Mass seven times [EL-E16, W-S4].
- What happened, in order (all paraphrase of N-1, facts-only):
  1. In summer, some women queued for the best places at two or three in the morning. Others arrived late and pushed to the front. [N-1 p.77]
  2. Some women chained a chair or stool to the altar rails the night before to keep a seat. [N-1 p.77]
  3. Mrs Ghisleri took her place at the church door around 2:30 a.m. on her first visit and was knocked down and trampled. [N-1 p.77]
  4. The Mass was in Latin and lasted about an hour and a half. The church was crowded. [N-1 ch. on Mass, "hour and a half"]
  5. Afterwards the men followed him to the sacristy and stood silent while he took off his vestments and prayed with bowed head. [N-1, same passage]
  6. His day then turned on the confessional: fifty years of sitting there hour after hour. [N-1 ch. XX, p.56]
  7. To confess, pilgrims put their names on a numbered list and waited days. [N-1 p.51–52] Booking began by 1950 [EL-E13, it.wikipedia].
- Details: he rose long before 4 a.m. to prepare for Mass; ate only a spoonful of soup or a forkful of pasta, rice or vegetables once a day [N-1 p.56]. Women were confessed in the open confessional in the church; men in a screened cubicle in the sacristy [N-1 p.51]. Heat in summer and cold in winter [N-1 p.57].
- Words spoken: "It is the nights" (Padre Pio to McCaffery; N-1 p.64, paraphrase; book text: "It is the nights. If ever I allow myself to sleep, the pain of these ... is mutliplied beyond measure.") Only the first four words may be quoted. He said it in a "weary voice" as the hot days neared.
- Inner life: John Paul II, official: he recalls his own confession to Padre Pio (below).
- Testimony (official, short, copied exactly; N-2, English text on vatican.va): "I also had the privilege, during my young years, of benefitting from his availability for penitents. The ministry of the confessional, which is one of the distinctive traits of his apostolate, attracted great crowds of the faithful to the monastery of San Gio" (text cut there in my fetch; the sentence continues "San Giovanni Rotondo"). Speaker: John Paul II, homily, 16 June 2002. Rights: official.
  plain: As a young man I too was lucky to use his time for confession. Hearing confessions was a main part of his work, and it drew large crowds to the friary.
  Check: the outline quotes this as "I too, in my youth, benefited". That wording is not on the vatican.va English page I read. Use the exact text above.
- Named penitents in N-1 (undated, paraphrase only): "Giovanni", a Tuscan Communist who came by taxi, wept in the confessional and gave up his party card (N-1 ch. VII). Carlo Campanini, the actor, told a "winter's day in the old church" story about a Naples "Commendatore" sent away (N-1 p.58–59). These are two stories, with no year.
- What changed: the crowd's need for confession became the fixed shape of his day.
- Not in the sources: no date for the chained stool; no name of the women; no weather for a given morning; no word of what he said to any one woman.
- Sources: N-1; N-2; EL-E13; EL-E16.

## SC-12 — 1960 (the friary; Rome's visitor)
- Status: thin (the facts are secondary sources; his own reaction is not recorded)
- When and where: early summer 1960; San Giovanni Rotondo; Rome (the Pope's study).
- Who was there: Pope John XXIII; Mgr Pietro Parente (assessor of the Holy Office); Mgr Carlo Maccari (visitor); Padre Pio; the local superior Fr Rosario da Aliminusa [N-3 it.wikipedia].
- What happened, in order:
  1. John XXIII sent Mgr Carlo Maccari to investigate, to curb forms of popular faith seen as archaic. [N-3]
  2. In early summer 1960 Parente told the Pope about audio reels recorded at San Giovanni Rotondo. [N-3] The en.wikipedia page says the Vatican did not order the wiretap, according to Luzzatto, and says Ruffin reports the tapes held only humming and soft confessional voices. Both unproven. [N-4]
  3. 25 June 1960: the Pope wrote private notes (four sheets, unpublished until 2007, shown by Sergio Luzzatto). [N-3]
  4. Maccari held nine meetings; the en.wikipedia page calls them "odious and inappropriate" and gives his diary line (below). [N-4]
  5. Maccari's recommendations: move the friars gradually, bring an outside superior, limit a penitent to one confession a month, new statutes for the hospital. [N-4]
  6. The Pope later called Padre Pio a "straw idol" (idolo di stoppa). [N-4]
  7. Fr Rosario da Aliminusa wrote that Padre Pio never failed in obedience. [N-3] Freedom restored 30 July 1964 by Paul VI through Cardinal Ottaviani. [N-3]
- Words spoken (Italian exact from N-3, it.wikipedia, CC BY-SA, quoting Luzzatto; secondary):
  - John XXIII, 25 June 1960: "Mi dispiace di P.P. che ha pur un'anima da salvare, e per cui prego intensamente" = I am sorry for Padre Pio. He still has a soul to save, and I pray hard for him.
  - John XXIII, same notes: "Con la grazia del Signore io mi sento calmo e quasi indifferente" = By the Lord's grace I feel calm, almost unmoved.
  - Same notes, on the claim: "si vera sunt quae referentur" = if what is reported is true. (He wrote of "rapporti intimi e scorretti" with women of the circle. The Pope's own words are conditional.)
  - Maccari, diary (en.wikipedia, English only): "Reticence, narrowness of mind, lies — these are the weapons he uses to evade my questions ... Overall impression: pitiful."
- Inner life: Padre Pio's reaction is not in any source I read. N-5 (Messenger of St Anthony, Allegri 2018) gives a general line, "The Church is mother, even when she punishes", but with no date; use only with attribution to Allegri.
- Both sides: Maccari saw too much mixing of the sacred and the all-too-human and feared fanatics around him [N-4]. The Pope's own notes say he prays for him. Rebuttal story (Fr Carmelo Durante; Archbishop Cesarano and the sister's kisses on the hand) is told on it.wikipedia; unverified, from a Capuchin author. [N-3]
- What changed: the visitor's report was harsh; the friary stayed under Rome's watch until 1964.
- Not in the sources: any word of Padre Pio about the microphones; where in the cell any device sat; what he said at the nine meetings.
- Sources: N-3; N-4; N-5.

## SC-13 — November 1962 (his cell)
- Status: full
- When and where: Wojtyla's letter is dated 17 November 1962. Padre Pio's cell, San Giovanni Rotondo, a day or so later. [N-6, N-7]
- Who was there: Angelo Battisti, official of the Secretariat of State and administrator of Casa Sollievo; Padre Pio; far away, Bishop Karol Wojtyla (in Rome for the Council) and Wanda Poltawska, doctor and camp survivor in Krakow. [N-6, N-7, N-8]
- What happened, in order:
  1. Wojtyla wrote in Latin asking prayer for a mother of four girls in Krakow with cancer. [N-6]
  2. An Italian cardinal gave the letter to Battisti and told him to take it at once; Battisti drove to San Giovanni Rotondo. [N-6, Battisti's account]
  3. In the cell, Padre Pio told him, "Open and read it." He was praying. [N-6]
  4. Battisti read it. Padre Pio listened without a word. [N-6]
  5. He raised his head and spoke the line below, then bowed his head and prayed again. [N-6, N-7]
  6. Doctors found nothing to operate on, Poltawska says, on 22 Nov (N-8); Wojtyla's thanks letter says "21 November" (N-9, translation). Eleven days after the first letter he wrote the thanks. [N-9]
- Details: the Battisti account says the letter seemed to him an ordinary one among many [N-6]. Dates of the cure conflict: 21 Nov (Wojtyla's letter, per N-9) or 22 Nov (N-8, operation day).
- Words spoken. Latin letter, exact as printed by catholic.org (N-6; it has odd grammar, so it is a copy of that web text, not a checked edition): "Venerabilis Pater, rogo te orationem fundere pro quadam mater quattuor puellarum, Cracoviae in Polonia (durante ultimo bello per quinque annos in campo concentrationis in Germania, nunc in periculo gravissimo sanitatis et etiam vitae ratione canceris: ut Deus ei eiusque familiae misericordiam suam instante Beatissima Virgine ostendat. In Christo obligatissimus +Carolus Wojtyła."
  plain: Venerable Father, I ask you to pray for a mother of four girls in Krakow, Poland. In the last war she spent five years in a German concentration camp. Now her health and her life are in great danger from cancer. May God show his mercy to her and her family, through the Blessed Virgin.
  Padre Pio, as Battisti reported it (English only, catholic.org translation of Italian; N-6): "Little Angelo, to 'this one' one cannot say no." Other versions: "Angelo, to this one (this man), it is not possible to say no!" (N-7); "Angelino, he can't be refused!" (N-8). Italian original not found.
  plain: Angelo, we cannot refuse this man.
  Later, Battisti: "Little Angelo, save these letters, because one day they will become important." (N-6; N-9 gives similar.)
- Inner life: none from Padre Pio; he "remained in silent prayer" (N-7).
- Testimony: Battisti (N-6) at about 90 words is too long to copy under facts-only rights; use the short quote above.
- What changed: the letter was kept; sixteen years later Battisti (N-6) says he remembered it when Wojtyla was elected pope on 16 October 1978.
- Not in the sources: Padre Pio's tone or gestures beyond head raised; the room; the hour; any medical detail seen by Padre Pio.
- Sources: N-6 (catholic.org); N-7 (capuchins.org); N-8 (Our Sunday Visitor); N-9 (CARF).

## SC-14 — 22–23 September 1968 (church, cell, terrace)
- Status: full for the facts; the Italian original was not found (the Italian of Funicelli's 29 Sept 1968 account was not on any page I reached)
- When and where: Sunday 22 Sept, Mass (50th anniversary of the stigmata, moved to 22 Sept); night 22–23 Sept; Padre Pio's cell, its corridor and the verandah, Santa Maria delle Grazie. [N-5]
- Who was there: Fr Pellegrino Funicelli (slept in a room linked to his by intercom for three years); Friar Guglielmo; Dr Sala (his doctor); two doctors on night duty at Casa Sollievo (one was Dr Giovanni Scarale); the superior and other friars. [N-5]
- What happened, in order (paraphrase of Funicelli's interview, N-5, facts-only):
  1. Mass on the 22nd: he was confused at one point, fainted at the end, was carried to his room, then fainted again after coming down to bless the faithful. [N-5]
  2. About 9 p.m. he was in bed. Until midnight he called Funicelli five or six times to ask the time. He seemed quieter and prayerful, his eyes watery. [N-5]
  3. At midnight he asked, anxiously, that Funicelli stay. He held his hand tight; "trembling like a child with fever". [N-5]
  4. About one he asked whether Mass had been said; Funicelli said it was too early. He said, "You'll say it for me this morning." [N-5]
  5. He asked to confess, then asked Funicelli to renew his Act of Religious Profession. [N-5]
  6. He asked Funicelli to beg his brothers' pardon for the trouble he had caused, and to ask prayers for his soul. He blessed the brothers, his spiritual children, and the sick. [N-5]
  7. Soon after one he said he could not breathe in bed. He washed his face, combed his hair, and walked to the verandah straight and unaided. They looked at the starry sky about ten minutes. [N-5]
  8. He grew pale; Funicelli put him in the wheelchair, then the armchair. Lips bluish. He repeated "Jesus, Mary". [N-5]
  9. He told Funicelli, "No! Do not disturb anybody." Funicelli went for help anyway. [N-5]
  10. Oxygen and injections failed. He died peacefully, head bent toward his shoulder. [N-5]
- Details: portrait of his mother on the wall; he said he saw "two mothers" there (Funicelli: "Our Lady", his own reading). [N-5] The wounds were healed without trace or scar (EL-E13, W-S6; not re-read here).
- Words spoken (English only; Messenger of St Anthony translation of Allegri's interview, N-5). Short quotations: "Jesus, Mary, Jesus, Mary" and "No! Do not disturb anybody." The ACI Prensa Spanish version of the 29 Sept 1968 account (EL-E8, translation of a translation) gives the same sequence. The two accounts differ: ACI says Funicelli confessed him and has the "Have you said Mass?" talk near midnight; the interview has it about one. Time of death: 2:20 a.m. in N-5; 2:30 in EL-E8 and EL-E2.
  plain for the key line: Do not wake anyone.
- Inner life: his own request to renew his vows, and to be forgiven. Allegri's text (N-5) states his saying that the Church is a mother "even when she punishes".
- What changed: he died as a friar, forgiven and forgiving. The wounds had gone.
- Not in the sources: the rosary in his hand (not in N-5; the older notes carry it, check before use); whether anyone cried at the bedside; the exact words of the confession.
- Sources: N-5; EL-E8; EL-E2; W-S4 pp.125–126 (not re-read).

## SC-15 — 20 January 2000 (Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza)
- Status: thin (no official text of the boy's words; the best I found is a fan-site copy of the mother's account)
- When and where: 20 January 2000, San Giovanni Rotondo; the hospital Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza; Padre Pio's tomb. Left hospital 6 February 2000. [N-10 caccioppoli; N-11 CNA summary from search]
- Who was there: Matteo Pio Colella, age 7 (born 4 Dec 1992 per CNA summary); his mother Lucia; his father (the outline calls him a doctor; no source read in this pass); Fr Gerardo da Flumeri (photo caption, N-10).
- What happened, in order:
  1. 20 Jan 2000: bacterial meningitis; next day coma; septic shock, respirator; heart stopped and was restarted; doctors said he would die soon. [N-10; N-11]
  2. His mother prayed the Rosary at Padre Pio's tomb with friars. [N-10]
  3. Eight days after he fell ill he woke from the coma. [N-10]
  4. Asked what he remembered, he spoke (below). He picked out Padre Pio in a photograph. [N-10]
  5. A scan on 27 Jan showed no brain lesion; he left the hospital on 6 Feb 2000. [N-10]
- Words spoken: Matteo, via his mother, in English translation on a fan site (not official, so facts only): "Yes, I saw myself while I was sleeping from a distance, on the bed. There was an old man with a white beard. He had a long brown habit. He gave me his right hand and said: 'Matteo, don't worry, you will get well quickly.'"
  Italian original: not found. Official decree not read.
  plain: I saw myself asleep on the bed, from far off. An old man with a white beard and a long brown habit took my hand and said, "Matteo, do not worry. You will soon be well."
- Official dates (N-12, Dicastery for the Causes of Saints): the canonical process in Manfredonia-Vieste 11 June to 17 Oct 2000; medical board 22 Nov 2001; theologians 11 Dec; cardinals and bishops 18 Dec; decree on the miracle 20 Dec 2001; decree on canonization 26 Feb 2002.
- Inner life: his mother's own report (N-10): "With my face on the cold granite I saw with closed eye a friar with a beard ..." (facts-only; she described a vision at the tomb; not an official finding).
- What changed: this cure became the miracle for his canonization on 16 June 2002.
- Not in the sources: the ward room, the hour, the mother's tears, anything the doctors said besides "would die soon".
- Sources: N-10; N-11; N-12.

## FN-1 — Holy Office decree dates
Sources disagree, and I found no Acta Apostolicae Sedis text. Facts:
- 2 June 1922: Holy Office first restrictive measures (Vatican "Buone Notizie" page; Voce di Padre Pio).
- 31 May 1923: decree "non constat de supernaturalitate" (Voce di Padre Pio; Capuchin site padrepiodapietrelcina.com; it.wikipedia adds it was printed in L'Osservatore Romano on 5 July 1923).
- 1931: 23 May (Voce di Padre Pio; it.wikipedia "quinto decreto") against 9 June (Vatican page: "decretata il 9 giugno 1931"; EWTN). A caccioppoli page says 13 May. Avvenire, on Castelli's book, says the cardinals decided in March 1931.
Use "May 1923" and "spring 1931" unless the Castelli book settles it.

## FN-2 — Pius XI lines of 1933
No primary source found. "I have not been badly disposed toward Padre Pio, but I have been badly informed": EWTN "Highlights" (no source named, "early 1933"), en.wikipedia footnote 26, caccioppoli ("to an archbishop"). A search summary cites Ruffin's biography; I did not read it. The Cuccarollo line, "è la prima volta che il Santo Uffizio si rimangia i suoi decreti" and "più che reintegrato": it.wikipedia notes 39–41 (not read). The indult of 16 July 1933 came from Cardinal Sbarretti "for the extraordinary Holy Year" (caccioppoli, it.wikipedia). Use with "reportedly".

## FN-3 — Miracle and canonization decrees
Dicastery for the Causes of Saints (official): decree on the miracle (Matteo Pio Colella) 20 December 2001, in John Paul II's presence; decree on the canonization 26 February 2002. Heroic virtue: 18 December 1997. Beatification miracle (Consiglia De Martino) decree 21 December 1998. The AAS 94 text was not read. Source: causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/pio-da-pietrelcina.html.
