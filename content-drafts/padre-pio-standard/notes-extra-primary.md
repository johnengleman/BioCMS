# Padre Pio — notes, second pass: primary texts and verified words

Gap pass. About 40 tool calls. Does not repeat notes-own-words.md, notes-witnesses.md, notes-church-miracles.md.
Method: Voce di Padre Pio PDFs came back as raw PDF from fetch_text.py. I streamed them through `curl | pdftotext` (nothing saved in the project). The Italian below is copied from that text or from fetched web pages. No public-domain text of the Epistolario exists online. All Epistolario words are under copyright (Edizioni Padre Pio). Use short excerpts in Italian with our own English, as in the notes before.
Rights key: SHORT = short quote only. FACTS = facts only, no wording. OFFICIAL = Holy See text, short quote fine.

## 2. Timeline: new dated facts (from the letters)
- 8 Sept 1911: first letter to Fr Benedetto telling of the stigmata (Lotti dates it so) [X2]. Conflict with the 1910 date (see Other).
- 1915: Fr Agostino asks if he has the stigmata and the other Passion gifts; Padre Pio first evades. In the next letter he answers yes: marks were visible "specie in una mano"; he prayed and they were taken away; the sharp pain stayed (Epist. I p. 669). [X2] NEW for English pages.
- 18 May 1913: letter to Fr Agostino: he asked again for a grace that Our Lady had forbidden; he says she "montò sulle furie" and that from that day she and the other heavenly persons withdrew from him (Epist. I pp. 360-361). [X8] NEW. See Q12.
- 21 Dec 1915: to Raffaelina Cerase, on the priesthood (Epist. II pp. 537-540). He calls himself useless to souls and asks for her prayers. [X4]
- 4 Sept 1916: moves from Foggia to San Giovanni Rotondo "provvisoriamente"; stays to death. 11 Sept: writes to Fr Agostino: the community is edifying; his "night of the spirit" grows. The guardian Fr Paolino da Casacalenda adds a note in the envelope: "se si eccettuano soltanto i dolori interni... potrebbe dirsi veramente felice." [X7] The Voce pieces give 4 Sept; the earlier notes give 28 July (first visit) and 6 Sept in X2 (a different count: "due giorni dopo il suo arrivo, il 6 settembre"). Conflict: use "early Sept 1916".
- 30 Oct 1916: Fr Benedetto preaches the retreat, confirms Padre Pio at San Giovanni Rotondo with the care of the seminary boys and local souls. [X7]
- 28 Nov 1916 to 5 Jan 1917: army medical check; returns with six months of convalescent leave. 27 Jan to 5 Feb 1917: pneumonia and typhoid. Fr Paolino took his temperature twice: the normal thermometer burst after 42.5 C; the bath thermometer read 52 C. [X7] (Fr Fabiano reports this; it is a reported episode, not a clinical record.)
- 1917: seminary directors called to arms; only Frs Paolino and Pio remain; he hears the boys' confessions, gives two talks a week, eats and prays with them. [X7]
- 6 July 1917, to Fr Agostino: no time even to rest in the Father's heart; the boys and the souls he helps "a voce ed in iscritto" take all his time: "Non mi so negare a nessuno." [X7] NEW. See Q8.
- 18 Aug to early Nov 1917: Naples military hospital again; leave of four more months; back 12 Nov. 4 Mar 1918: fourth call-up; 15 March: definitively discharged; arrives home 17 March 1918 (so discharge is 15-17 Mar 1918, not 1917). [X7] Settles the conflict in the first notes.
- 16 Apr to 18 May 1918: stays at San Marco la Catola with Fr Benedetto to talk about his spirit and his ministry. [X7]
- 30 May 1918 (Corpus Domini): during Mass he offers himself for the end of the war, as Benedict XV asked; he suffers a lasting state he felt as "imprigionamento nell'inferno". [X7]
- Mar to Sept 1918: Fr Paolino witnesses two ecstasies, hears him speak with Jesus and Mary, and sees the gift of reading hearts (Fr Paolino's testimony as given by Fr Fabiano). [X7] Reported by witness.
- 5 Aug 1918: transverberation while hearing the seminary boys' confessions. His report to Fr Benedetto: Epist. I p. 1065 (see Q2). The pain lasted until the morning of 7 Aug. Fr Benedetto answers (Epist. I pp. 1067-1068) that 6 Aug is Transfiguration and the wound is a sign of God's love. [X3] NEW.
- 17 Oct 1918: Fr Benedetto asks him to tell all "per santa ubbidienza" (his letter of 19 Oct). Padre Pio had written on 17 Oct about "quel misterioso personaggio che mi impiagò tutto". [X10]
- 20 Sept 1918, a Friday, about 9-10 a.m.: friary nearly empty. Superior Fr Paolino is at San Marco in Lamis, the questing brother is out. [X10] Detail NEW.
- 20-21 Sept 1918: first to notice: Filomena Ventrella (20 Sept), Nina Campanile (21 Sept, diary), the seminary students (21 Sept, Fr Emilio da Matrice). [X10] See Scenes.
- About 30 Sept 1918: Fr Paolino learns from Nina Campanile 8-10 days later. He then writes to Fr Benedetto, who does not come and asks for silence. [X10]
- 22 Oct 1918: the long letter to Fr Benedetto (Q1). [X9]
- 1919, Ash Wednesday (5 March, per calendar; X4 gives only "giorno delle Ceneri del 1919"): Fr Benedetto Nardella writes to Fr Agostino after he visits (see Q14). [X4]
- 1921: Apostolic Visitor Mgr Raffaello Carlo Rossi inquires secretly for the Holy Office; 142 answers; his conclusions are "estremamente positive". The papers were published in a book in 2008 [X13]. Padre Pio's answer to "do you submit to the Church's authority?": see Q13. NEW.
- Holy Office decision date: X4 says 2 June 1922 (all but private morning Mass forbidden). Earlier notes give 31 May 1923. Conflict: check the decree before the writer picks a date. 16 July 1933 return to public Mass (X4); 25 Mar 1934 confessions of men resume, 12 May 1934 women (X14).
- Dec 1918: a second transverberation, "una lamina di ferro" from low in the heart to below the right shoulder. [X7]
- 16 May 1947 (X14): works start on the hospital. Earlier notes say 9 Jan 1940; the Vatican list says 16 May 1947. Conflict: check (possibly land purchase vs building).
- 1 July 1959: new church consecrated while he is in hospital. 6 Aug 1959: he recovers as the Fatima statue stops at San Giovanni Rotondo. [X14]
- 22 Sept 1968: last Mass at 5 a.m.; 23 Sept dies at 2.30; funeral 26 Sept, buried that evening in the crypt. [X14]

## 3. Cause steps (Positio summary, official facts) [X11]
- Nihil obstat 29 Nov 1982 (motu proprio Sanctitas Clarior). Archbishop of Manfredonia opens the diocesan (informative) process 1983-1990.
- 7 Dec 1990: the Congregation recognizes the process as valid.
- Positio on virtues completed; 13 June 1997 theological consultors: positive. 21 Oct 1997 Cardinals and Bishops, with the relator Bishop Andrea Maria Erba of Velletri-Segni: virtues heroic. 18 Dec 1997: decree, in the presence of John Paul II.
- Beatification miracle (Consiglia De Martino, Salerno): Salerno tribunal July 1996 to June 1997; valid 26 Sept 1997; medical board 30 Apr 1998; theologians 22 June 1998; cardinals 20 Oct 1998; decree 21 Dec 1998.
- The Congregation's own summary: three means of his mission: spiritual direction, reconciliation, the Mass; prayer groups called by him "vivai di fede e focolari di amore" (EWTN translation: "breeding grounds of faith and focuses of love") [X12]; Paul VI called them "un grande fiume di gente che prega" (EN). The text of the Positio is not online. I found no "Positio summary" other than this official biography.
- The summary lists virtues with these details: he passed "la giornata e gran parte della notte" in conversation with God; he accepted superiors' measures and calumnies in silence; he thought himself useless and unworthy. [X11] (paraphrase; official English wording is quotable if needed).

## 4. Character
- Wanted the stigmata hidden: to Nina Campanile on 21 Sept 1918, who tried to kiss his hand on the wound: "Se sapessi che umiliazione mi dai!" [X10 quoting her diary]. Told the Ventrella sisters: "Guai a voi se lo dite a qualcuno" [X10, Vittorina Ventrella memoir]. Hoped the wounds would soon vanish by prayer [X10].
- Gentle teasing to Nina: "Sei assente da tre giorni, ed io aspettavo una Maria, che venisse a portarmi una parola di conforto!" [X10]
- Alone in a nearly empty friary, still silent for a month; he told no one, not even Fr Agostino [X10].
- Overloaded: "Non mi so negare a nessuno" (6 July 1917) [X7].
- Fear of dying from blood loss; Thursday evening to Saturday the heart wound bleeds (Epist. I p. 1094) [X1].
- On the 50th anniversary, a friar said "Cinquant'anni, padre!" and he answered half absent: "Cinquantotto, sono cinquantotto." (his age: 58) [X2]. Lotti gives no date or source for the anecdote; mark as report. NEW.
- He closes letters by asking prayers: "Pregate assai per la mia conversione"; "pregate per me e per il mio cammino di santità" (Ruppi quotes the habit, no page) [X4].
- Humor/sharpness in a letter to his friend Don Peppino Orlando, 2 Aug 1922: "quando verrai ti voglio vedere più buono" [X4].

## 5. Quotations
All Italian is copied from the text named. English is ours. Rights SHORT unless marked. Page numbers are from the Voce articles, not from my own check of the Epistolario.
1. 22 Oct 1918, to Fr Benedetto (Epist. I; page not given by my sources). Italian: "Era la mattina del 20 dello scorso mese in coro, dopo la celebrazione della santa Messa, allorché venni sorpreso dal riposo, simile ad un dolce sonno." Ours: "It was the morning of the 20th of last month, in the choir, after Holy Mass, when a rest came over me, like a sweet sleep." Follow-on clause: "mi vidi dinanzi un misterioso personaggio, simile a quello visto la sera del 5 agosto... che aveva le mani ed i piedi ed il costato che grondava sangue." Ours: "I saw before me a mysterious figure, like the one of 5 August, with hands, feet and side dripping blood." And: "La sua vista mi atterrisce"; "Mi sentivo morire e sarei morto se il Signore non fosse intervenuto a sostenere il cuore." Ours: "His sight terrifies me... I felt I was dying, and would have died if the Lord had not held up my heart." Source X9 (and the second site, verified). The opening line of the passage: "Mio Dio, che confusione e che umiliazione io provo nel dover manifestare ciò che tu hai operato in questa tua meschina creatura!" Ours: "My God, what confusion and humiliation I feel in having to tell what you have done in this poor creature of yours." Use short clauses only (copyright).
2. 5 Aug 1918 (Epist. I p. 1065). "Me ne stavo confessando i nostri ragazzi la sera del cinque (agosto), quando tutto di un tratto fui riempito di un estremo terrore alla vista di un personaggio celeste..." and "Teneva in mano una specie di arnese, simile ad una lunghissima lamina di ferro, con una punta ben affilata, e che sembrava da essa punta che uscisse il fuoco." Ours: "I was hearing our boys' confessions on the evening of the fifth when I was filled with terror at the sight of a heavenly figure. He held a kind of tool, like a very long blade of iron with a sharp point, and fire seemed to come from the point." Also "A stento emisi un lamento, mi sentivo morire." [X3] Quote one sentence only.
3. Same letter: "Sento nel più intimo dell'anima una ferita che è sempre aperta, che mi fa spasimare assiduamente." Ours: "I feel in the depth of my soul a wound that is always open and makes me cry out in pain." (Epist. I p. 1065) [X1][X3]
4. Epist. I p. 1065: "La luce mi acceca prima di inebriarmi… Sono stanco e solo l'ubbidienza a voi evita di abbandonarmi completamente." Ours: "The light blinds me before it makes me drunk... I am tired, and only obedience to you keeps me from letting myself go." [X3]
5. Late 1918 to Fr Benedetto (Epist. I p. 1094): "La ferita del cuore gitta assiduamente del sangue, specie dal giovedì a sera sino al sabato. Padre mio, io muoio di dolore per lo strazio e per la confusione susseguente che io provo nell'intimo dell'anima. Temo di morire dissanguato…" Ours: "The wound of the heart throws out blood without stopping, above all from Thursday evening to Saturday. Father, I am dying of pain and of the shame that follows. I fear I will bleed to death." [X1]
6. Same letter, 22 Oct 1918: "Io sento nell'interno un continuo rumoreggiare, simile ad una cascata, che gitta sempre sangue." Ours: "I hear inside me a constant roar, like a waterfall, that always throws out blood." Then: "Mio Dio! È giusto il castigo e retto il tuo giudizio, ma usami al fine misericordia." Ours: "My God, the punishment is just and your judgment right, but in the end show me mercy." [X2]
7. Epist. I p. 1103, after the stigmata: "Quanto sei dolce ed amaro insieme! tu ferisci e risani, impiaghi e guarisci... Oh dolci tormenti!, perché siete tanto insoffribili e tanto cari insieme?" Ours: "How sweet and bitter you are together! You wound and you heal... O sweet torments, why are you so unbearable and so dear at once?" [X2]
8. 6 July 1917 to Fr Agostino: "Non mi so negare a nessuno." Ours: "I do not know how to say no to anyone." [X7]
9. Mass. Epist. III p. 59 as quoted by Don Ruppi: "Il mondo potrebbe stare anche senza sole, ma non può stare senza la Santa Messa." Ours: "The world could do without the sun, but it cannot do without the Holy Mass." The article itself says the popular form "Senza la Messa il mondo non potrebbe stare in piedi" is not the exact text, and that "il sole della giornata" is wrong. Use only the corrected form. [X6] NEW and a useful correction.
10. Mass. Other lines in X6 have no letter page: "Io nel sacrificio dell'altare metto tutti" (said to Cleonice Morcaldi); "La Messa non è un ricordo, ma il Calvario" (testimony of Fr Raffaele da Sant'Elia a Pianisi); "È Gesù che celebra, non io" (testimony of Fr Alessio Parente); "Non si può andare all'altare senza carità" (said to be Epist. III p. 45; check, the article's wording looks adapted). Use only as "remembered by witnesses" and not as his words in print. [X6]
11. Eucharist at age 24 (Epist. I p. 217, to Fr Benedetto): "Ho tale fame e sete prima di riceverlo, che poco manca che non muoia di affanno. Ed appunto perché non posso di non unirmi a lui, alle volte con la febbre addosso sono costretto di andarmi a cibare delle sue carni." Ours: "Before I receive him I have such hunger and thirst that I nearly die of longing. And because I cannot stay away from him, sometimes with fever on me I must go and feed on his flesh." (Ruppi gives the year as age 24, so about 1911.) [X5] NEW.
12. Prayer of quiet, Nov 1913 and after (Epist. I p. 420): "Non appena mi pongo a pregare, subito sento che l'anima incomincia a raccogliersi in una pace e tranquillità da non potersi esprimere colle parole. I sensi restano sospesi, ad eccezione dell'udito." Ours: "As soon as I begin to pray, I feel the soul gather into a peace and quiet no words can express. The senses are held still, except hearing." He adds that great noise around him does not disturb him. [X2] Good for prayer.
13. Obedience, 1921 inquiry. Asked if he would always submit to the Church's authority, he answers: "Sì, Eccellenza. Per la Santa Chiesa è lo stesso Dio che parla." Ours: "Yes, Excellency. In Holy Church it is God himself who speaks." Source: Card. Bertone, citing Mgr Rossi's published papers [X13]. OFFICIAL, quotable, but second-hand from the book.
14. Fr Benedetto Nardella (provincial) to Fr Agostino, Ash Wednesday 1919 (not Padre Pio, a witness): "In lui non sono macchie o impronte, ma vere piaghe perforanti le mani e i piedi... Il venerdì è sangue... quando dice messa il dono è esposto al pubblico, dovendo tenere le mani alzate e nude." Ours: "In him there are not marks or stains but true wounds piercing hands and feet... On Friday it is blood... when he says Mass the gift is shown to the public, because he must hold his hands raised and bare." [X4] NEW (primary witness, 1919).
15. On suffering, 18 May 1913 (Epist. I pp. 360-361): "Questa Madre santa montò sulle furie per l'ardire che ebbi nuovamente a chiedere detta grazia... Da quel giorno si ritirò da me assieme agli altri personaggi celesti." Ours: "This holy Mother flew into a rage at my daring to ask again for that grace... From that day she withdrew from me, together with the other heavenly persons." [X8] NEW. The grace is not named in the source. Do not guess it.
16. On priests, to Raffaelina Cerase (Epist. II pp. 537-540): "Ogni ministro del Signore dovrebbe mai sempre lavorare per la salute delle anime, non dovrebbe riconoscere mai stanchezza." Ours: "Every minister of the Lord should always work for the health of souls and never admit weariness." And of himself: "Io conosco troppo bene me stesso." ("I know myself too well.") [X4]
17. To Raffaelina Cerase, 1915 (Epist. II p. 110): "Pregate per la conversione di essi." Ours: "Pray for their conversion" (about bad priests). In the same letter he adds that she offered her Communion for all priests, "specie... per i cattivi". [X4]
18. 8 Sept 1911 / 1915 letter on stigmata (Epist. I p. 669): "furono visibili, specie in una mano... non per questo scomparve il dolore acutissimo che si fa sentire, specie in qualche circostanza ed in determinati giorni." Ours: "They were visible, especially in one hand... the very sharp pain did not go, and it is felt above all on some occasions and certain days." [X2]
19. On shame and the figure, 17 Oct 1918 (padrepio.it): "Sarà necessario che io pronunzi il fiat nel mirare quel misterioso personaggio che mi impiagò tutto..." Ours: "Must I say my fiat as I look at that mysterious figure who has wounded me all over...?" [X10] Rights SHORT.
20. Official summary of his sayings on prayer: "Nei libri cerchiamo Dio, nella preghiera Lo troviamo. La preghiera è la chiave che apre il Cuore di Dio" ("In books we seek God, in prayer we find him"). The Congregation's English: "In books we seek God, in prayer we find him. Prayer is the key which opens God's heart". No letter page given. [X11] OFFICIAL, no locator, same line already in the earlier notes.
21. Prayer groups: "vivai di fede e focolari di amore" (Italian form is back-translated from the EWTN English; the Italian decree text not read. Do not quote Italian until checked). [X12]
22. The Orlando account (tradition, no source): "Carponi mi trascinai dal coro alla cella" ("On all fours I dragged myself from the choir to my cell") [X10]. Not in the 22 Oct letter. Mark as oral account to a friend; do not mix with the letter.

Nothing found for confession in Padre Pio's own words with a locator. JPII 1994 (Penitenzieria address) names him with the Curé d'Ars and St John Nepomucene as a great confessor (OFFICIAL, https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/it/speeches/1994/march/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19940312_penitenzieria.html). Wikiquote-style confession sayings in the web are unsourced.

## 6. Scenes
1. 20 Sept 1918, a quiet Friday: nearly empty friary; one friar alone in choir; "riposo simile ad un dolce sonno"; the figure; he crawls (tradition) to his cell. [X9][X10]
2. 21 Sept 1918: Nina Campanile sees a "scottatura" on his right hand and says "Vi siete scottata la mano!" He hides his hands behind his back. [X10]
3. Fr Paolino laughs when told, then walks into the cell without knocking and sees the wound on the right palm and back, then the left back. [X10]
4. 5 Aug 1918: hearing the boys' confessions, then terror; the pain runs to the morning of 7 Aug. [X3]
5. Fr Paolino's thermometer bursting at 42.5 C in Jan 1917. [X7]
6. 22 Oct 1918: young friar writes the hardest letter of his life under holy obedience. [X9]
7. 1919: Fr Benedetto sees him barely standing at the altar and lets him say Mass because the gift will be "exposed" in his bare raised hands. [X4]

## 7. Miracles
No new miracle. Detail only: the Jan 1917 temperature stories (42.5 C and 52 C) come from Fr Paolino as told by Fr Fabiano [X7]. Reported, not investigated. Fr Paolino also reported ecstasy and heart-reading (Mar-Sept 1918) [X7].
