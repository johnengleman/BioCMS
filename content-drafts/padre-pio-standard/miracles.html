This list gathers the Church’s memory of God’s works through Saint Pio, whom the world knows as Padre Pio: the healings, the visions, and the other wonders told by those who knew him. The Church approved two healings as miracles, one for his beatification and one for his canonization. The other accounts come from his own letters, from the friars who lived with him, from doctors, and from the people he helped. Each account names who told it. A short note at the end tells where historians have asked questions.

Miracles approved by the Church

Beatification is the step before sainthood. In canonization the Pope declares a person a saint. The Church asks for one miracle before each step, usually a healing that doctors cannot explain.

A Salerno woman’s ruptured duct is healed, 1995

In November 1995 Consiglia (Lina) De Martino was in the hospital of Salerno, her home city, with a ruptured thoracic duct. That is a vessel that carries lymph. A devotional website tells her story. On 1 November she found a lump under her left collarbone, and two CT scans showed the rupture. Surgery was set for 3 November. Friar Modestino of Pietrelcina prayed for her healing at Saint Pio’s tomb from 1 November. She smelled Saint Pio’s perfume on 1 and 2 November. On 3 November the lump was gone, and on the morning of 4 November a voice told her she was healed. She left the hospital on 6 November without surgery.

Status: Approved by the Church as the miracle for his beatification. A church court in Salerno studied the case from July 1996 to June 1997. The Vatican’s medical board examined it on 30 April 1998, and the Voce di Padre Pio chronology says all five doctors on the board found the cure scientifically inexplicable. The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints records that the decree on the miracle was issued on 21 December 1998, in the presence of Saint John Paul II.

Source: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, “Pio da Pietrelcina”, https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/pio-da-pietrelcina.html; Congregation for the Causes of Saints, biography for the beatification, 1999, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/csaints/documents/rc_con_csaints_doc_19990502_padre-pio_en.html; Voce di Padre Pio, “Biography”, https://vocedipadrepio.com/en/biography/; caccioppoli.com, “The miracles of Lina De Martino and Matteo Pio Colella” (a devotional website), https://caccioppoli.com/28%20the%20miracles%20of%20Lina%20De%20Martino%20and%20Matteo%20Pio%20Colella.html

A boy of seven is healed of meningitis, 2000

Matteo Pio Colella was seven when he fell ill on 20 January 2000 in San Giovanni Rotondo. The Voce di Padre Pio chronology says he was taken that evening to the Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza, the hospital Saint Pio founded. Doctors there found hyper-acute meningitis. On 21 January he was put on a breathing machine, and the chronology calls his outlook desperate. A devotional website tells more. He went into septic shock and a coma. His mother went to Saint Pio’s tomb and prayed the Rosary there with the friars. She later said that she saw a bearded friar lift a child’s stiff body onto its feet. The chronology says that his mother prayed and asked for prayers, and that around 11 o’clock that day his condition began to improve, against all medical predictions. Eight days after he fell ill, Matteo woke. He spoke of an old man with a white beard in a long brown robe. The old man had taken his hand and said, “Matteo, don’t worry, you will get well quickly.” When his mother showed him a picture, Matteo named Saint Pio as the old man. He went home on 6 February.

Status: Approved by the Church as the miracle for his canonization. A church court of the archdiocese of Manfredonia-Vieste opened the process on 11 June 2000. The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints dates its close to 17 October 2000, and the Voce chronology to 17 October 2001. On 22 November 2001 the Vatican’s medical board examined the case, and the Voce chronology says it found the healing rapid, complete, lasting, and scientifically inexplicable. The decree on the miracle was issued on 20 December 2001, in the presence of Saint John Paul II. Matteo’s words come from his mother’s account on the devotional website.

Source: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, “Pio da Pietrelcina”, https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/pio-da-pietrelcina.html; Voce di Padre Pio, “Biography”, https://vocedipadrepio.com/en/biography/; caccioppoli.com, “The miracles of Lina De Martino and Matteo Pio Colella” (a devotional website), https://caccioppoli.com/28%20the%20miracles%20of%20Lina%20De%20Martino%20and%20Matteo%20Pio%20Colella.html

Visions and struggles Saint Pio reported

Saint Pio told of his visions and struggles in his letters and to his spiritual directors. The friars who lived with him wrote down some of what they saw.

Visions at the church in Pietrelcina, 1892

Saint Pio said later that from about the age of five he saw Our Lady and the Sacred Heart of Jesus at the altar rail of the church in Pietrelcina. He thought every child saw them. As an adult he said he had talked with Jesus, Mary, and his guardian angel in his younger years. He also told of a vision after Holy Communion on 1 January 1903 that showed him a lifelong struggle with Satan. On 5 January, his last night at home, he saw God and Mary encourage him.

Status: Saint Pio’s own later account, as Italian Wikipedia and the EWTN biography report it.

Source: “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; EWTN, “Padre Pio: His Biography”, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/padre-pio-his-biography-13815

Attacks of the devil at Venafro, 1911

Cardinal Bertone said in 2008 that Satan tormented Saint Pio with terrible visions and with blows. Bertone quoted a note of 10 June 1911 in which Saint Pio wrote that the devil wanted him for himself at any cost. In November 1911 Saint Pio stayed at the friary of Venafro. Father Agostino wrote in his diary that he and the superior saw many ecstasies and many attacks of the devil there. A devotional website draws on that diary for more. In some attacks the devil beat Saint Pio until he bled and tormented him with loud noises. Saint Pio called on the name of Jesus and was freed. He also tested a vision by telling it to praise Jesus aloud, and the devil vanished.

Status: Told by Cardinal Bertone in 2008 and by Father Agostino in his diary, which Father Lotti reports in 2011. The details of the blows and the test come from a devotional website.

Source: Cardinal Tarcisio Bertone, homily at San Giovanni Rotondo, 23 Sept. 2008, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/card-bertone/2008/documents/rc_seg-st_20080923_pietrelcina-40san-pio_it.html; L. Noises in his room at Foggia, 1916

Saint Pio stayed at the Sant’Anna friary in Foggia from 17 February to 4 September 1916. A devotional website says that terrifying noises came from his room at night. They ended with a boom that shook the walls and frightened the other friars. Saint Pio told Father Benedetto that the devil could not win and was exploding in rage.

Status: Told by Saint Pio to Father Benedetto, as one devotional website reports it.

Source: caccioppoli.com, “Padre Pio’s years in Pietrelcina 1883-1916” (a devotional website), https://www.caccioppoli.com/Padre%20Pio's%20years%20in%20Pietrelcina%201883-1916%20child,%20novice,%20priest,%20soldier,%20ill,%20convalescent.html

A fiery blade pierces his soul, August 1918

Saint Pio wrote to Father Benedetto that on 5 August 1918 he was hearing the seminary boys’ confessions when he saw a heavenly figure with a very long iron blade. Fire seemed to come from its point, and the blade pierced his soul. He sent the boy at confession away and wrote that he felt he was dying. The pain lasted until the morning of 7 August. Father Benedetto answered that 6 August was the feast of the Transfiguration and that the wound was a sign of God’s love.

Status: Saint Pio’s own account in letters to his spiritual director. The Voce di Padre Pio reports them in 2008.

Source: Voce di Padre Pio, July–Aug. 2008 (Epistolario I, pp. 1065–1068), https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2008_7-8_ita_2.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikiquote, https://it.wikiquote.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

A second iron blade pierces him, December 1918

In December 1918 Saint Pio described a second piercing. He felt an iron blade run from low in his heart to below his right shoulder, with bitter pain.

Status: Saint Pio’s own account, as Father Fabiano reports it in the Voce di Padre Pio in 2025.

Source: R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf

Friars see him in ecstasy, 1905 to 1918

In his first years of illness a friar saw him in ecstasy, lifted from the ground. The EWTN biography tells that Father Agostino found Saint Pio at Venafro on 28 November 1911, and he seemed to be dying. Father Agostino prayed in the chapel and came back to find him alert and joyful. His diary says that he and the superior saw many ecstasies at Venafro. In them Saint Pio spoke with Jesus, Mary, Saint Francis, and his guardian angel. Between March and September 1918 Father Paolino, the guardian, saw him in ecstasy twice and heard him speak with Jesus and Mary. His spiritual children also speak of levitation and of the gift of tongues.

Status: Told by the friars who saw these things, Father Agostino and Father Paolino. Italian and English Wikipedia give the early friar’s account and the reports of levitation and the gift of tongues.

The stigmata and those who saw them

Saint Pio carried the stigmata for about fifty years. Stigmata are the marks of the wounds of the crucified Christ in a person’s own body. Saint John Paul II spoke of them in his homily when he beatified him in 1999.

Red marks appear in his hands, 1910

Cardinal Bertone said in 2008 that the first stigmata appeared on 7 September 1910 under an elm at Piana Romana near Pietrelcina. In September 1911 Saint Pio wrote of a red mark the size of a coin in the middle of each palm, with sharp pain. He said he had kept silent because of “that cursed shame”. In 1915 he wrote that the marks had been visible, especially in one hand. He prayed and they were taken away, but the pain stayed.

Status: Saint Pio’s own account in letters. Cardinal Bertone repeated it in an official homily in 2008.

Source: L. Lotti, Voce di Padre Pio, Oct. 2018, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2018_10_ita_2.pdf; Cardinal Tarcisio Bertone, homily at San Giovanni Rotondo, 23 Sept. 2008, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/card-bertone/2008/documents/rc_seg-st_20080923_pietrelcina-40san-pio_it.html; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

Wounds appear after Mass, 20 September 1918

Saint Pio wrote that after Mass on 20 September 1918 he stayed in the choir of the church at San Giovanni Rotondo. A rest came over him “like a sweet sleep”, and he saw a mysterious figure with hands, feet, and side dripping blood. He felt he was dying, and then he saw that his own hands, feet, and side were pierced and bleeding. At first he told no one. On 17 October he wrote to Father Benedetto of a mysterious figure who had wounded him. On 19 October Father Benedetto ordered him to tell everything plainly, and Saint Pio did so in a letter dated 22 October.

Status: Saint Pio’s own account. He wrote it under obedience to his director, Father Benedetto.

Source: P. M. Mainolfi, Realtà Sannita, https://www.realtasannita.it/articoli/chiesa-cattolica/ecco-la-vera-storia-della-stimmatizzazione-di-padre-pio.html; F. Bosco, padrepio.it, 2022, https://www.padrepio.it/padre-pio-racconta-che-ha-ricevuto-le-stimmate-il-20-settembre-1918/; “Death of Padre Pio,” L’Osservatore Romano, 1968, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/death-of-padre-pio-5764; John Paul II, beatification homily, 1999, https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/homilies/1999/documents/hf_jp-ii_hom_02051999_padre-pio.html

Others see the wounds within days, 1918

Filomena Ventrella saw red marks on Saint Pio’s hands on 20 September 1918, and the seminary boys saw a wound in each of his palms the next day. Nina Campanile wrote in her diary that on 21 September she saw a mark on the back of his right hand. She cried out that he had burned his hand, and he was stunned and hid his hands behind his back. When she tried to kiss the wound he said, “If you knew what humiliation you give me!” About ten days later she told the guardian, Father Paolino. The next day Father Paolino walked into Saint Pio’s cell without knocking and saw the wounds on his hands.

Status: Told by the witnesses themselves in memoirs, a diary, and a report, as the shrine’s website padrepio.it prints them. Nina Campanile was a woman of the town. The guardian is the superior of a friary.

Source: F. Bosco, padrepio.it, 2022, https://www.padrepio.it/padre-pio-racconta-che-ha-ricevuto-le-stimmate-il-20-settembre-1918/

Doctors examine the wounds, 1919 to 1925

In 1919 Father Benedetto wrote that the marks were “true wounds piercing the hands and the feet”. Dr Luigi Romanelli was the chief physician of the civil hospital of Barletta. He examined them on 15 and 16 May 1919 and wrote that he was sure they were not shallow. When he pressed on both sides of the palm, he felt the hollow between them. The Roman doctor Giorgio Festa came in the autumn of 1919 and again in 1920. In 1925 he sent the Holy Office a favorable report. He wrote that the stigmata were not the product of an outside injury or of strong chemicals.

Status: Told by Father Benedetto and Dr Romanelli in 1919 and by Dr Festa in 1925. Ruppi and the two Wikipedia articles report them.

Source: C. F. Ruppi, Voce di Padre Pio, Dec. 2005, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2005_12_ita_3.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

A bishop visits for the Holy Office, 1921

In June 1921 the Holy Office secretly sent Bishop Raffaello Carlo Rossi of Volterra to San Giovanni Rotondo. He began on 14 June and stayed eight days. Cardinal Bertone said in 2008 that the report holds 142 detailed answers from Saint Pio. Under oath Saint Pio told the bishop that after Mass on 20 September 1918 he saw Our Lord on the cross. He said a voice told him he was joined to the Lord’s Passion. In his report of 4 October 1921 Bishop Rossi called the stigmata a real fact and found neither fraud nor the devil’s work. He wrote that Saint Pio was a good friar and practiced the virtues. He found in him a deep humility and a rare simplicity.

Status: Told in Bishop Rossi’s report. It appears in the magazine Oggi (10 September 2008), the Voce di Padre Pio, and English Wikipedia.

Healings, bilocations, and other wonders

These accounts come from people who knew Saint Pio, from friars and doctors, and from later books and websites.

Twenty days on Holy Communion alone, Venafro, 1911

In 1911 Saint Pio stayed at the friary of Venafro. The Voce di Padre Pio chronology says he was almost always confined to bed there because of his illness. One Capuchin friar said he lived there for at least twenty days on the Holy Eucharist alone.

Status: Told by one friar, as English Wikipedia reports it.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; Voce di Padre Pio, “Biography”, https://vocedipadrepio.com/en/biography/

Fevers too high for thermometers, 1917 and 1920

From 27 January to 5 February 1917 Saint Pio lay in bed with pneumonia and typhoid, and Father Paolino took his temperature. Father Fabiano writes that the ordinary thermometer burst when it passed 42.5 degrees Celsius. A bath thermometer then read 52 degrees. In 1920 Dr Giorgio Festa measured his temperature twice a day. Italian Wikipedia says his report recorded peaks of 48 to 48.5 degrees on some days.

Status: Told by Father Paolino and by Dr Festa. Father Fabiano reports the first in 2025, and Italian Wikipedia reports the second.

Source: R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

Reading of hearts, 1918

Father Fabiano writes in the Voce di Padre Pio that between March and September 1918 Father Paolino saw for himself that Saint Pio had the gift of reading hearts. Italian Wikipedia adds that many people testified that in confession he named their sins before they spoke.

Status: Told by Father Paolino, whom Father Fabiano quotes in 2025. Italian Wikipedia reports the penitents’ words.

Source: R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

Saint Pio tells a bishop of bilocation, 1921

Bishop Rossi asked Saint Pio about bilocation, which means being in two places at once. Saint Pio answered that he did not know how it happened and did not give it much thought. He said he had been present with a certain person or in a certain place. He did not know whether his mind was carried there, whether he saw a picture of the place, or whether his body was there. Then he told of two cases. He said it was the first time he had spoken of them openly. In one he stood by the bed of a sick woman of San Giovanni Rotondo at night while he was in the friary, and she was healed. In the other he met a man at Torremaggiore and urged him to give up his vices. The man later came to San Giovanni Rotondo.

Status: Saint Pio’s own sworn answer to Bishop Rossi on 15 June 1921, as the magazine Oggi prints it from the report that Francesco Castelli published in 2008. English Wikipedia gives the same answer in English.

Source: Oggi, 10 Sept. 2008, pp. 32–34, on F. Castelli, Padre Pio sotto inchiesta (Ares, 2008), reprinted at fattisentire.org, https://www.fattisentire.org/Stimmate-bilocazioni-ipertermie-i-documenti-inediti-su-Padre-Pio/; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

The sweet scent around Saint Pio

Many people told of a sweet scent around Saint Pio, and some said his blood smelled of violets, roses, or lilies. In 1921 Bishop Rossi wrote of a very intense and pleasant scent like violets, and he could not find its source. He wrote, “I have smelled it, just as I have seen the stigmata.” John McCaffery wrote that he smelled it many times, both at San Giovanni Rotondo and far from it. Asked about the perfume, Saint Pio called it “just sweets for the children”.

Status: Told by many witnesses. Bishop Rossi wrote of it in 1921, as Father Elder (2011) and English Wikipedia report. McCaffery wrote of it in 1978.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; J. M. Bilocation to Udine, 1905

A devotional website tells this account. In February 1905 Saint Pio wrote down that he had found himself in Udine, where a father was dying and a child was being born. The child was Giovanna Rizzani Boschi, born on 18 January 1905. She met Saint Pio in 1923, and he told her that he had been in Udine at her birth and at her father’s death. She later gave testimony in the process for his beatification.

Status: Told on one devotional website, which names Giovanna Rizzani Boschi as one of the witnesses at the beatification process.

Source: caccioppoli.com, page on Padre Pio’s bilocations (a devotional website), https://caccioppoli.com/Close%20encounters%20of%20a%20special%20kind%20with%20Padre%20Pio%20around%20the%20world,%20without%20leaving%20the%20convent..html

Saint Pio and General Cadorna, 1917

The same website says that General Luigi Cadorna, the army’s supreme commander, was about to take his own life after the defeat at Caporetto. It gives the date as 12 November 1917. A friar appeared before him and told him not to do such a foolish thing. After the war Cadorna visited Saint Pio without warning. Saint Pio called out from a distance that they had had a narrow escape that night. Another summary gives 9 November for the date.

Status: Told on one devotional website.

Source: caccioppoli.com, page on Padre Pio’s bilocations (a devotional website), https://caccioppoli.com/Close%20encounters%20of%20a%20special%20kind%20with%20Padre%20Pio%20around%20the%20world,%20without%20leaving%20the%20convent..html

Saint Luigi Orione sees him in Rome

Saint Luigi Orione was a priest. He said he saw Saint Pio come smiling toward him through the crowd in Saint Peter’s in Rome, and then Saint Pio disappeared. Saint Pio was at his friary at the time. A devotional website dates the report to 29 April 1923, the day Saint Thérèse of Lisieux was beatified. Italian Wikipedia dates it to 1925, the year of her canonization.

Status: Told by Saint Luigi Orione, as a devotional website and Italian Wikipedia report it.

Source: “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; caccioppoli.com, page on Padre Pio’s bilocations (a devotional website), https://caccioppoli.com/Close%20encounters%20of%20a%20special%20kind%20with%20Padre%20Pio%20around%20the%20world,%20without%20leaving%20the%20convent..html

A farmer from near Padua is cured

A farmer from near Padua told John McCaffery on a train that a farm accident just after the war had left him dying, with a blood clot in each lung. He prayed for someone closer to God to plead for him, and a bearded monk appeared by his bed. The monk laid a hand on his chest and smiled, and then he vanished. The man felt cured at once, and his doctors confirmed it. Months later he saw the monk’s photograph in a house in Padua. That evening he took the train to San Giovanni Rotondo. After the man’s confession Saint Pio asked him how his lungs were.

Status: Told by the man himself to McCaffery, who published it in 1978 and wrote the book from memory.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. I, pp. 1–3, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Father Dominic is cured of cancer

Father Dominic was an American Capuchin at San Giovanni Rotondo. He told McCaffery that Saint Pio had cured him of cancer. He said he had just had his yearly check-up and expected no sign of it.

Status: Told by Father Dominic himself, as McCaffery tells it in 1978.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. II, pp. 5–6, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Saint Pio comes to Monsignor Damiani of Uruguay

Monsignor Damiani was the vicar-general of the diocese of Salto in Uruguay. He visited Saint Pio and stayed in the friary. He had a weak heart, and one day he had an attack while Saint Pio was hearing confessions in the church. Friars sent word at once, but Saint Pio went on hearing confessions. When he came to the bedside, the attack was over. He told the Monsignor that it had not been fatal. The Monsignor said he wished he could have Saint Pio with him at his death. Saint Pio answered that he would help him wherever he was. Back in Uruguay the Monsignor told this to his bishop and his friends. One morning he was found dying, and on his bedside table lay a scrawled message of three words: “Padre Pio came.”

Status: Told to John McCaffery by Father Dominic, and published by McCaffery in 1978.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. XII, p. 33, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Bilocation to a deathbed in Milwaukee

Father Dominic told his father in Milwaukee the story of Monsignor Damiani. His father asked him to beg Saint Pio for help at his own death. When Father Dominic asked, Saint Pio gave him an understanding look and slowly nodded. Two years later the father died at the home of a married daughter. Father Dominic’s sister told him that their father could not move alone, yet they found him in his bed and not on the veranda. She said they saw a Host on his tongue.

Status: Told to McCaffery by Father Dominic, and published by McCaffery in 1978.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. XII, pp. 33–34, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

A Hawaiian woman smells perfume in her room

McCaffery met an American woman from Hawaii on the train to Foggia. She had come to see Saint Pio and was afraid to go on. At Foggia they met Father Dominic as he got off the same train. The next day she told McCaffery that she had smelled a strong perfume in her room. It was not one she had brought. McCaffery told Father Dominic the time. Father Dominic said Saint Pio had acted quickly, because he himself had just spoken to Saint Pio about her. McCaffery says she wrote to him that Christmas that she could go to Mass and Holy Communion for the first time in eleven years.

Status: Told by McCaffery, who was there, and by the woman herself, in his 1978 book.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. II, pp. 4–7, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

A girl born without pupils sees, 1947

A 1999 book by Malachy Carroll tells of Gemma di Giorgi, a girl from Sicily who was born without pupils. Her grandmother brought her to Saint Pio in 1947. On that trip the girl began to see objects, including a steamboat and the sea. The grandmother begged Saint Pio to ask God for the child’s sight. He told her that neither she nor the child should weep, because the child could see.

Status: Told in Malachy Carroll’s 1999 book, as English Wikipedia cites it.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

A prophecy for Saint John Paul II, 1947

In 1947 the young Polish priest Karol Wojtyła visited Saint Pio. He later became Saint John Paul II. Saint Pio heard his confession. Cardinal Alfons Stickler reported that Saint John Paul II told him that Saint Pio foretold he would reach the highest post in the Church. Father John Aurilia was Saint Pio’s secretary for some years. He tells it differently. In his account Saint Pio called the young priest “your holiness” and asked for his blessing.

Status: Told by Cardinal Stickler and Father Aurilia, as English Wikipedia reports it.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

He is healed when the Fatima statue visits, 1959

A chronology in the Vatican newspaper says that on 1 July 1959 the new church of the friary was consecrated while Saint Pio was ill in the hospital. It says that on 6 August 1959 the statue of Our Lady of Fatima stopped at San Giovanni Rotondo. At that time he was healed of the illness that had struck him at the end of April.

Status: Told in a dated chronology in the Vatican newspaper, L’Osservatore Romano, on 2 May 1999.

Source: L’Osservatore Romano, “Buone Notizie,” 2 May 1999, https://www.vatican.va/news_services/or/or_quo/bnw/bn25a.html

Saint John Paul II writes to Saint Pio

On 17 November 1962 Bishop Karol Wojtyła of Kraków wrote to Saint Pio in Latin. He later became Saint John Paul II. He asked for prayer for a mother of four daughters in Kraków who was gravely ill with cancer. His letter says she had spent five years in a German concentration camp. Angelo Battisti was the administrator of Saint Pio’s hospital, and he took the letter to San Giovanni Rotondo. Battisti said later that Saint Pio had him read it aloud and then said, “Angelo, to this one it is not possible to say no!”

Status: Told by Battisti, as Father Elder (2014) and the Fundación CARF (2025) report it.

Wanda Półtawska is cured before surgery, 1962

The woman in the letter was Wanda Półtawska, a doctor from Kraków. Her surgery was due on 21 or 22 November, but the doctors found nothing to operate on. On 28 November Saint John Paul II wrote again to thank Saint Pio. He said she had been suddenly cured on 21 November before the operation. Her husband told him that the doctors faced a mystery.

Status: Told in the two letters, as Father Elder (2014) and the Fundación CARF (2025) report them. The accounts differ on the date of the planned operation.

A stage lamp stays dark over the altar

Some time before Saint Pio died, the BBC filmed his Mass for a documentary by Mischa Scorer. Scorer told McCaffery that the crew set up the lamps the night before, and one shone straight onto the altar. The crew left it that way. The next morning every lamp worked except that one. McCaffery took it as a sign that Saint Pio would not have his Mass disturbed.

Status: Told to McCaffery by the producer. The reading of it is McCaffery’s own, in his 1978 book.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. XLVIII, pp. 127–128, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

After Saint Pio’s death

The wounds fade, 1968

McCaffery wrote that the wounds began to fade some time before Saint Pio died. Italian Wikipedia says the friars who prepared his body saw no mark. It also says the doctor at his bedside found the wounds completely healed, without trace or scar.

Status: Told by McCaffery in 1978 and by witnesses, as Italian Wikipedia reports them.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), pp. 125–126, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

The tomb is opened, 3 March 2008

Archbishop D’Ambrosio reported what was found when the tomb was opened after midnight on 3 March 2008. The top of the skull was partly skeletal, but the chin and the rest of the body were well preserved. The hands looked as if they had been manicured, and no stigmata could be seen. A silicone mask made from a 1968 photograph was used when his body was shown for veneration.

Status: Told in the archbishop’s report of the exhumation, as English Wikipedia and Catholic News Agency give it.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; Catholic News Agency, 3 March 2008, https://www.catholicnewsagency.com/news/padre_pio_exhumed_body_is_well_preserved

A note on questions historians ask

Some historians and writers have asked questions about parts of this list. We record them here with respect. The Church’s own judgment stands above them. In 1933 Pope Pius XI lifted the restrictions that the Holy Office had set, and in 2002 the Church declared Saint Pio a saint.

In 1920 the priest and doctor Agostino Gemelli came to the friary and asked to see the wounds. Saint Pio said he needed written permission from the Holy Office, and Gemelli had none. Gemelli left without examining them and later wrote that the friar was hysterical. Writers differ on whether he came on his own or was sent by Rome. In July 1919 Professor Amico Bignami thought the wounds began as a skin disease and were completed by suggestion or by a chemical such as iodine. In May 1923 the Holy Office issued a decree that nothing supernatural was established, and the Vatican newspaper printed it on 5 July 1923. Bishop Rossi wrote in 1921 that he could not confirm many of the healings people told him about. He added that some of Saint Pio’s letters gave credible accounts, but without a doctor’s word he left the question open.

The historian Sergio Luzzatto used the testimony of a Foggia pharmacist, who said that Saint Pio had obtained carbolic acid and veratrine. Saint Pio said he used them to sterilize syringes and for a joke on the friars with snuff. The journalists Saverio Gaeta and Andrea Tornielli answer the charge. Stanisław Dziwisz was Saint John Paul II’s secretary, and he denied the prophecy. George Weigel’s biography leaves it out. For the accounts of Udine, General Cadorna, and Saint Luigi Orione we found no source from the time.

The sources also differ on small points. Saint Pio’s letter of 22 October 1918 says he was alone in the choir. The Vatican newspaper’s obituary of 1968 tells that a friar found him unconscious behind the high altar. The archbishop’s report of 2008 speaks of a well-preserved body and does not use the word incorrupt. EWTN’s timeline gives other dates for the canonization miracle, and Italian Wikipedia gives later dates for Matteo’s waking and discharge. We follow the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints and the Voce di Padre Pio.
