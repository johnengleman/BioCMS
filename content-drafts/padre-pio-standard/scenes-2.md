## SC-06 — Friday 20 Sept 1918, about 9-10 a.m., the choir
- Status: full
- When and where: Friday 20 Sept 1918, between 9 and 10 a.m. The choir of the small church of Santa Maria delle Grazie, San Giovanni Rotondo [SX1; SX2 says "about ten"]. The choir is a loft over the church door, three rows of wooden stalls, with a small window to the square and a crucifix on the rail. [SX2, Mainolfi's description; no source named, treat as the author's]
- Who was there: no one. The superior, Fr Paolino, was at San Marco in Lamis. The questing brother, Fra Nicola da Roccabascerana, was out collecting. [SX1]
1. After Mass he stays in the choir to give thanks. [SX1, SX2]
2. He is "surprised by a rest, like a sweet sleep"; the senses and faculties are in a stillness he cannot describe. [letter, SX2]
3. He sees a mysterious figure, like the one of 5 August, with hands, feet and side dripping blood. [letter]
4. He feels he is dying; the figure withdraws; he sees his own hands, feet and side pierced and bleeding. [letter]
5. He tells no one, not even his superior, director, or Fr Agostino. He writes it only on 22 Oct, after the provincial orders him on 19 Oct "per santa ubbidienza". [SX1]
- Words (all [LETTER], short clauses; Epistolario I, page not found in any source I read):
  - "Mio Dio, che confusione e che umiliazione io provo nel dover manifestare ciò che tu hai operato" — My God, what confusion and shame I feel in having to tell what you have done. (22 Oct 1918, to Fr Benedetto) [SX2, SX1]
  - "riposo, simile ad un dolce sonno" — a rest, like a sweet sleep. [SX2]
  - "grondava sangue" — dripping blood. [SX2]
  - "Mi sentivo morire" — I felt I was dying. [SX2]
  - 17 Oct 1918: "quel misterioso personaggio che mi impiagò tutto" — that mysterious figure who wounded me all over. [SX1]
- Inner life: shame first ("confusione", "umiliazione"). In the oral account to Don Peppino Orlando (no source for date or witness; tradition) he says a great light struck his eyes and Christ appeared, said nothing, vanished; he came to on the floor; "Carponi mi trascinai dal coro alla cella" (on all fours I dragged myself from the choir to my cell); he lay on his bed, prayed, looked at the wounds and wept. [SX1, mark as oral account]
- Testimony: no passage may be copied at length (Epistolario under copyright; no public-domain text exists). Use the clauses above.
- What changed: the wounds stay for fifty years; the man who wanted to hide now carries a sign everyone can see.
- Not in the sources: the Italian page of the 22 Oct letter; a doctor, a witness, or a friar finding him (the 1968 Vatican obituary says a friar did; the letter says he was alone); the weather; the time he left the choir; the distance he crawled (only SX1 says the corridor was long and the Orlando account says "carponi"). SX2 adds "blood drops on the floor" without a source.
- Sources: SX1, SX2, EP-X3, EP-X9, EP-X10.

## SC-07 — Saturday 21 Sept 1918, and about 8-10 days later (the friary)
- Status: full. Correction to outline: the guardian scene is NOT on 21 Sept. It comes 8-10 days later.
- When and where: Saturday 21 Sept 1918, afternoon, the friary [Nina Campanile's diary, via SX1]. Fr Paolino's scene: about 30 Sept, in Padre Pio's cell the day after Nina speaks to him [SX1].
- Who was there: Nina Campanile, a woman of the town. Seminary students, who noticed that morning. Fr Paolino da Casacalenda, guardian. Filomena Ventrella saw the red marks on the 20th [SX1].
1. 18 Sept: Nina kisses his hand; no mark. [SX1]
2. 21 Sept: he teases her gently about her absence. She hands over a Mass offering and sees "una stimmata" in the middle of the back of his right hand, which "seemed a burn". [SX1]
3. She pretends not to understand and cries out the burn line. He turns pale and puts his hands behind his back. [SX1]
4. At parting she tries to kiss the wound. He says the humiliation line. He tells her he will pray hard so the Lord will make everything go away. [SX1]
5. That morning the students saw a wound in his palms; he walked with difficulty and his face was redder than usual (Fr Emilio da Matrice). [SX1]
6. Filomena Ventrella had seen red marks on the 20th. Her sister Vittorina remembers him telling them: "Guai a voi se lo dite a qualcuno". [SX1]
7. Eight or ten days on, Nina tells Fr Paolino, smiling. He laughs aloud. Next day he enters the cell without knocking; Padre Pio is writing; he sees the right palm and back, then the left back. [SX1]
8. Paolino writes to Fr Benedetto the same day and asks him to come. Benedetto does not come and asks for the greatest silence. [SX1]
- Words (Nina's diary, as printed by SX1; copy exactly, short):
  - "Oh, Padre! Vi siete scottata la mano!" — Oh, Father! You have burned your hand! (the diary has "scottata", feminine; copy as printed)
  - "Se sapessi che umiliazione mi dai!" — If you knew what a humiliation you give me!
  - "Sei assente da tre giorni, ed io aspettavo una Maria, che venisse a portarmi una parola di conforto!" — You have been away three days, and I was waiting for a Mary to bring me a word of comfort!
  - "Pregherò tanto il Signore, che farà scomparire ogni cosa." — I will pray the Lord so hard that he makes all of it disappear.
  - Paolino: "È mai possibile che Padre Pio abbia ricevuto le stigmate senza che io, che sto sempre con lui, non me ne sia accorto?" — Is it possible that Padre Pio received the stigmata and I, who am always with him, did not notice?
- Details: right hand: the first mark was on the back, in the centre. Paolino was "sempre con lui" yet noticed nothing for a week.
- Inner life: he believed the wounds would vanish soon through prayer. [SX1]
- Testimony: Nina's diary and Paolino's memoir are modern prints with no rights statement; keep to the short lines above.
- What changed: the secret is out in town on day one; the provincial orders silence.
- Not in the sources: Nina's tone, who else was in the room, what the guardian felt on seeing the wound, how long he stood. Dates of the Ventrella visit beyond "the 20th".
- Sources: SX1, EP-X10, EP-X4 (Fr Benedetto, 1919, "vere piaghe", not re-read).

## SC-08 — June 1921, the friary (Bishop Rossi)
- Status: full, with one weak line (the soap)
- When and where: the visit began 14 June 1921 and lasted eight days (fan site, citing Castelli 2011) [SX6 caccioppoli page, W-S5]. San Giovanni Rotondo friary. Rossi finished his written report on 4 Oct 1921; it was printed in 141 pages by the Vatican press [SX4]. Bertone says it holds 142 detailed answers [EP-X13].
- Who was there: Mgr Raffaello Carlo Rossi, bishop of Volterra, sent secretly by the Holy Office; Padre Pio, then 34.
1. Rossi questions him; Padre Pio answers on oath (the fan site gives an English text: "On September 20, 1918 I saw the Lord..."; check before use). [W-S5]
2. Asked if he means always to submit to the authority of the Church, he answers (Bertone's homily quoting Rossi's papers) — see words. [EP-X13]
3. Rossi smells a violet scent himself and says so in his report. [SX5]
4. He ends cool, not converted, but sure of facts. [SX5]
- Words:
  - Padre Pio: «Sì, Eccellenza. Per la Santa Chiesa è lo stesso Dio che parla» — Yes, Excellency. In Holy Church it is God himself who speaks. [EP-X13, Bertone 23 Sept 2008; OFFICIAL, second-hand from the book]
  - Rossi, Italian from his 1921 report: «P. Pio è un buon religioso, esemplare, esercitato nella pratica delle virtù» — Padre Pio is a good religious, a model, practised in the virtues; he adds that Pio shines in "una sentita umiltà e una singolare semplicità". [SX4]
  - Rossi, English from Castelli's translation: "This very intense and pleasant fragrance, similar to the scent of the violet ... is attested by everyone ... I have smelled it, just as I have seen the 'stigmata.'" [SX5]
  - Rossi: "I feel complete indifference and I would say almost coldness, so much did I want to maintain a serene objectivity" [SX5, partly elided by the source]. Plain: he wanted to stay cool and fair.
  - Rossi, Jan 1922: "The future will reveal what today cannot be read in the life of Padre Pio of Pietrelcina." [Olson blog on Castelli, goodreads page, English]
  - Soap and "serene, jovial, even humorous": English only, on the caccioppoli page (SX6/W-S5): "I have examined the monk's cell and could find nothing that would cause such a scent. There was only plain soap." No Italian and no named primary found in three tries. [CHECK] Do not present as exact.
- Testimony: none copied at length (translation under copyright; Rossi's report text is not free online).
- What changed: the secret visitor's report is positive; the Holy Office later still decides against him (1922-23).
- Not in the sources: the room, the order of questions, Padre Pio's tone, a scene of the soap search, the dates of each day, whether the submission answer fell on a particular day (Bertone gives none).
- Sources: SX4, SX5, SX6, EP-X13, W-S5.

## SC-09 — July-Sept 1923 (San Giovanni Rotondo)
- Status: full. Correction to outline: the order was NOT dropped on 17 Aug. The window march has no date.
- When and where: order for a move to a friary in the Marche given 30 July 1923 by the Capuchin minister general, Fr Giuseppe Antonio da San Giovanni in Persiceto, to Fr Luigi Festa d'Avellino [SX3]. Read to Padre Pio on the night of 8 Aug (midnight) in the friary [SX3].
- Who was there: Padre Pio; Fr Luigi Festa; guardian Fr Ignazio da Ielsi; mayor Francesco Morcaldi; the town.
1. 8 Aug, midnight: Fr Luigi reads him the order. Padre Pio bows his head with arms folded and answers (words below). He asks to be sent not out of the province but to Montefusco. Fr Luigi says it will be carried out only when Rome gives further word. [SX3]
2. 10 Aug: a mason, Donato Centra, threatens him with a pistol in the church: "O vivo o morto devi rimanere con noi". [SX3]
3. 12 Aug: he writes the mayor, upset that he might unwillingly cause deaths; asks him to help the superiors' will; and asks that his bones lie "in un tranquillo cantuccio di questa terra". [SX3, paraphrase except the clause]
4. 15 Aug evening: eight Fascists with clubs climb to the friary to ask him to stay. [SX3] The Prefect says the order can be done "even over corpses" (12 Aug) [SX3]; De Bono says on 31 Aug it cannot be done without bloodshed [SX3].
5. 21 Aug: the minister general tells the mayor the move is put off for now. 1 Sept: he asks the Interior Ministry to suspend it. 6 Sept: the mayor reports it "frozen". [SX3, Sept 2023]
- Words:
  - Padre Pio, 8 Aug 1923: «Eccomi a sua disposizione, partiamo subito, quando sono con il superiore sono con Dio» — Here I am at your service, let us leave at once; when I am with my superior I am with God. [SX3]
  - Observer, London, Rome correspondent, summer 1923, exact: "Padre Pio himself lays no claim to miraculous gifts; he is the most modest and simple of men." [EL-E7, Costello 2018, quoting the paper; original not seen]
- Details: the Voce text names the mayor's arrangement with the head of the Fascist militia to watch the friary day and night, and his plan to put field guards inside the house with a siren [SX3]. Costello writes that "the population rose up and went in procession to the monastery... refused to leave until he had shown himself to them at a window" and that "40 local Fascists undertook to guard the building by turns" [EL-E7]. These two lines are Costello's own prose, not in quotation marks; whether they are the Observer's words is not shown. The Voce account has no window march.
- Inner life: he was "immensely worried" at being a cause of bloodshed [SX3]. Voce 27 Aug: he writes that he will obey any order but that leaving without special help from God would mean deaths of priests and damage to churches and a danger to his own life. [SX3, paraphrase]
- Testimony: none at length. Costello's text is modern copyright.
- What changed: the move is frozen; the town shows how far it will go; he answers with obedience.
- Not in the sources: the date of the window march; what he said at the window; numbers in the crowd; the weather. Earlier, on 26 June 1923, the people's protest led to Mass in the church again [W-S3].
- Sources: SX3, EL-E7, W-S3, W-S5.

## SC-10 — 9 Jan 1940 (a cottage by the friary) and 5 May 1956
- Status: full for 1940; the 1956 line is thin (not re-read)
- When and where: 9 Jan 1940, meeting called for 6:30 p.m. In a cottage close to the convent where the Sanvico and Sanguinetti families lived [SX6, fan site citing Mor73]. Correction to outline: the committee did not meet in the friary. The doctors afterwards went to Padre Pio for approval [SX6].
- Who was there: Dr Mario Sanvico (veterinarian from Perugia), Dr Guglielmo Sanguinetti, Dr Carlo Kisvarday (pharmacist), their wives, and Ida Seitz; all three men were his spiritual children [SX6].
1. Late 1939 he tells Sanvico he wants a hospital near the convent. [SX6, CNA EL-E6 agree]
2. 9 Jan 1940: they form a committee; the paper says all will be done "first subject to the advice of Padre Pio". [SX6]
3. They go to him; he approves. [SX6]
4. He gives a coin: CNA (EL-E6): "I also want to give my offering", a 10-cent franc from a Swiss man that morning. SX6: "I want to make the first donation", a 10-franc gold coin; Ida Seitz gives 30 francs, the others 967 lire. The two sources disagree; do not state the coin without saying "a small coin". [EL-E6, SX6]
5. 11 Jan he points out the site; 14 Jan he gives the name "Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza". [SX6]
6. War (10 June 1940) stops all; the society forms in 1946. [SX7]
- Words:
  - "I also want to give my offering" (CNA, English; Italian not seen).
  - "Tonight starts my great earthly work" (SX6, English only; EL-E15 en.wikipedia gives "This evening my earthly work has begun"; Italian not seen).
  - "The Work is not mine... but Providence's." (CNA, English) [EL-E6]
  - At the opening 5 May 1956 (CNA): "a seed has been sown on the Earth that [God] will warm with the rays of his love... a place of prayer and science." [EL-E6]
- Details: 1956 beds: CNA says 250; the Vatican 2006 text says 300 [SX7]. The same text says Card. Lercaro opened it for Pius XII.
- Testimony: none at length.
- What changed: a friar with no money starts a hospital with a coin; the crowds turn toward the sick.
- Not in the sources: the Italian words, the room inside the cottage, the time of day Padre Pio gave the coin, any scene of Paul Dudley White ("congratulations on your wounds", W-S4 pp.45-46: the book text did not search in this session; not re-checked, keep the line facts-only and paraphrased).
- Sources: SX6, SX7, EL-E6, EL-E15, W-S4.
