Key prefixes follow the outline (W-, OW-, CM-, EL-, EP-). New sources read in this session carry the key N- and are listed in full at the end of each card.

## SC-01 — 1897 or 1901 (Pietrelcina, the Forgione farm at Piana Romana)
- Status: thin (the boy's words exist only in English; the year and the father's trip are uncertain)
- When and where: the year is disputed. EWTN says 1897 [N-1]. The Opera Padre Pio prayer-groups site says 1901 [N-2]. Place: Pietrelcina. One fan site (N-3) says the friar stopped at the Forgione farm in Piana Romana.
- Who was there: the boy Francesco Forgione; his parents Grazio and Maria Giuseppa; a Capuchin alms-collector, fra' Camillo da Sant'Elia a Pianisi, of the friary at Morcone [N-1, N-3, EL-E13].
- What happened, in order:
  1. A young Capuchin walked the countryside asking for gifts [N-1].
  2. The boy was drawn to him and told his parents he wanted to be a friar with a beard [N-1, N-3].
  3. His parents went to Morcone (13 miles north) to ask if the friars would take him. The friars were interested, but he needed more schooling than his three years of village school [N-1].
  4. To pay a private tutor, his father went to America to find work [N-1, N-2]. After this the boy was confirmed (27 Sept 1899), was tutored, and finished the entry requirements [N-1].
- Details: parents could not read; family prayed at the dawn bell and said the Rosary nightly [EL-E1]. The 1901 school years: a letter of 5 Oct 1901 "to his father in America" is cited by N-3 (see below); so the father was abroad in 1901.
- Words spoken: boy to parents, "I want to be a friar... with a beard" [N-1, English only]. Italian original: not found (3 tries).
- Inner life: later he said that Fra Camillo's beard "was so impressed in my mind that nobody could distract me from my desire to become a bearded friar" [N-3, quoting Pre98 p. 23; English only; not checked against the book].
- Testimony: none from a public-domain source.
- What changed: the family chose to pay for his schooling, and the father went abroad.
- Not in the sources: the year the father LEFT. N-3 says "probably" Brazil or Argentina in 1897, probably the Erie Railroad in 1899, and notes that no written account of his trips exists. Return 1903 [N-3, EL-E17]. Do not state a departure year as fact. EL-E13 puts the entry request in spring 1902, a different order [EL-E13]. No weather, no season, no dialogue beyond the one line.
- Sources: N-1 EWTN, "Padre Pio: His Biography", https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/padre-pio-his-biography-13815 (English, facts only). N-2 Opera Padre Pio, Prayer Groups, "The Founder", https://gruppidipreghiera.operapadrepio.it/?p=19299 (English, official site, facts). N-3 caccioppoli.com, "1 Francesco Forgione in Pietrelcina", https://caccioppoli.com/1%20Francesco%20Forgione%20in%20Pietrelcina.html (English fan site, secondary; cites Gia12 p.21 and Pre98 p.23; facts only). EL-E13, EL-E1.

## SC-02 — 1907–1908 (Serracapriola friary)
- Status: thin (the outline's picture does not match the source)
- When and where: academic year 1907–08, friary of Serracapriola. Good Friday was 17 April 1908 [EL-E13, it.wikipedia, N-4].
- Who was there: Fra Pio; his lector Padre Agostino da San Marco in Lamis; the friars of the house; Grazio Forgione; the parish priest of Pietrelcina, Salvatore Pannullo [N-4].
- What happened, in order (N-4):
  1. Fra Pio meditated on the Passion and wept so much that his eyes grew sick. The weeping stopped at Serracapriola.
  2. On Good Friday 1908 he already had chest trouble; a migraine struck him and lasted for his whole stay, and kept him from some lessons.
  3. The friars were alarmed by his "endless" pains and his chronic loss of appetite. They sent word by telegraph to Pannullo, who summoned Grazio.
  4. Grazio arrived, was lodged in the friary, and took his son home for a health holiday on the advice of the doctor who had examined him.
- Details: he wrote his parents that summer: "qui si sta un po' male" because of the heat, and "Non v'impensierite" [N-4, a letter; short]. Our English: "It is a little hard to be here... do not worry."
- Words spoken: Padre Agostino about him: "Era buono, obbediente, studioso, sebbene malaticcio" [N-4, from Agostino's Diario]. Plain: "He was good, obedient, studious, though sickly."
- Not in the sources: the weeping did NOT happen on Good Friday; the telegram went to the parish priest, not straight to the father; no date for the father's trip or for the leave; no word that he "could not eat" because of the weeping. The writer must not join these into one Good Friday scene.
- Sources: N-4 it.wikipedia "Pio da Pietrelcina", https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina (CC BY-SA, Italian, secondary; cites Allegri p. 23 and Agostino's Diario). EL-E13.

## SC-03 — 7 Sept 1910 and Sept 1911 (Piana Romana; the parish priest)
- Status: full on the facts; two dating conflicts remain
- When and where: 7 Sept 1910, afternoon, under the elm at Piana Romana near Pietrelcina, a month after his ordination (10 Aug 1910) [N-5 official; N-4].
- Who was there: Padre Pio; Don Salvatore Pannullo ("Zi' Tore"), parish priest of Pietrelcina 1901–28 [N-6].
- What happened, in order:
  1. After Mass he went daily to Piana Romana, where his brother Michele had built him a hut, to pray in the open air, which helped his lungs [N-4].
  2. There the first marks came, on 7 Sept 1910 [N-5, N-4].
  3. He wrote on 8 Sept 1911 that the marks had come "for almost a year" and that he kept silent because of "quella maledetta vergogna" [N-4, citing Epistolario I p. 234].
  4. Pannullo was the first to know [N-4, N-6].
  5. 1915: asked by Padre Agostino, he said the marks were once visible, "specie in una mano"; he prayed and they went; the sharp pain stayed (Epist. I p. 669) [EP-X2, N-4].
- Details: hut built by Michele; elm [N-4, N-5]. "A little red in the middle of the palm, the size of a cent coin, with strong, sharp pain, more in the left hand; some pain under the feet" [N-4, from the Sept 1911 letter; no page given].
- Words spoken (Zi' Tore dialogue). It now has a named source: it.wikipedia gives it with a footnote to Raffaele Iaria, "Zi' Tore. Il 'parroco' di Padre Pio. Don Salvatore Pannullo" (Tau, 2022). I did not read Iaria. Wikipedia sets it the morning after the 8 Sept 1911 letter [N-4]. The Italian: Padre Pio: «Zi' Tore, fatemi la carità, chiediamo a Gesù che mi tolga questa confusione. Voglio soffrire, morire di sofferenza, ma tutto nel nascondimento». Plain: "Zi' Tore, do me the kindness: let us ask Jesus to take this shame from me. I want to suffer, even die of suffering, but all in hiding." Pannullo: «Figlio mio, io ti aiuto a pregare... però, se è volontà di Dio, devi piegarti a fare in tutto e dappertutto la sua volontà». Plain: "My son, I will help you pray... but if it is God's will, you must bend to do his will in all things." EWTN gives the first line in English, undated, tied to 1910: "I do want to suffer, even to die of suffering, but all in secret" [N-1].
- Inner life: "quella maledetta vergogna" (that cursed shame) [N-4]; "un po' di rosso" in the palms [N-4].
- What changed: he asked God to hide the marks; they went, but the pain stayed.
- Not in the sources: the Zi' Tore dialogue is told as the scene the day after the letter, while N-6 (a Sept 2022 notice of Iaria's book) says Pannullo learned of the marks "a month after the ordination", i.e. 1910. The two dates conflict; mark the dialogue "as later told" and give no hour or room. EL-E13's "the next morning in church" is Wikipedia's wording only. No weather. The 1910 date comes from Bertone (official) and the convent; the first written report is 1911.
- Sources: N-4; N-5 Card. T. Bertone, homily 23 Sept 2008, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/secretariat_state/card-bertone/2008/documents/rc_seg-st_20080923_pietrelcina-40san-pio_it.html (Italian, official; says first stigmata "sotto l'olmo di Piana Romana, a Pietrelcina il 7 settembre del 1910, un mese dopo la sua ordinazione"); N-6 AgenSIR, 25 May 2022, https://www.agensir.it/quotidiano/2022/5/25/libri-pubblicato-il-volume-zi-tore-il-parroco-di-padre-pio-don-salvatore-pannullo/ (Italian, news item, facts); EP-X2 (Lotti, Voce Oct 2018, which I re-read: gives the 1915 letter text, Epist. I p. 669); EL-E13. Letters: [LETTER] rights, short lines only.

## SC-04 — 28 Nov 1911 (Venafro friary)
- Status: full on the date and the diary; the "chapel" story rests on one source
- When and where: 28 Nov 1911, Venafro, friary; first diary entry timed 9:45 to 11 a.m. He stayed about forty days, from the end of October to 7 Dec 1911 [N-7, N-8, EL-E13].
- Who was there: Padre Pio; Padre Agostino da San Marco in Lamis; the superior, Padre Evangelista da San Marco in Lamis [N-7].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Padre Agostino's fourth notebook says: at Venafro in November 1911 he and Evangelista noticed the first supernatural phenomena; he saw many ecstasies and many diabolical attacks, and wrote down what he heard from Padre Pio's mouth [N-7].
  2. The first notebook opens with a transcript headed "Venafro 28-11-1911 (dalle 9,3/4 alle 11 antim.)" [N-7].
  3. EWTN: told that Padre Pio was ill, Agostino ran in, thought him dying, went to pray in the chapel, and on returning found him alert and full of joy [N-1]. No other source I read confirms the chapel.
  4. Padre Agostino later brought Communion into ecstasy and said "for holy obedience receive Jesus"; Padre Pio did not remember it afterward (Diary p. 210) [N-9]; undated.
- Details: the ecstasies are described as familiar talk with Jesus, Mary, St Francis, and his guardian angel [N-7]. In the transcript he says to Jesus: «è tanto dolce stare con te!... oh come si sta bene!» [N-7]. Plain: "It is so sweet to be with you! Oh, how good it is here!" He asked Jesus to let him feed on him each morning [N-7, paraphrase].
- Inner life (letters; cited from the notes, not re-read): hunger for Communion "con la febbre addosso" (EP-X5, Epist. I p. 217); peace in prayer "da non potersi esprimere colle parole" (EP-X12, Epist. I p. 420); 18 May 1913, Our Lady "montò sulle furie" (EP-X8, Epist. I pp. 360-361). [LETTER] short only.
- What changed: the superiors now saw the first ecstasies; he went home on 7 Dec 1911.
- Not in the sources: Agostino's diary words in Italian are only the Lotti extracts above; no diary text about the chapel; no room, no hour of the return; no tears or gestures.
- Sources: N-7 L. Lotti, "La permanenza a Venafro", Voce di Padre Pio, April 2011, pp. 38-40, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2011_4_ita_4.pdf (Italian, copyright; facts and short quotes). N-8 Lotti, Voce Jan 2011, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2011_1_ita_3.pdf (arrival end Oct 1911; "circa quaranta giorni"). N-9 Padre Pio TV, 3 Dec 2019, https://www.padrepio.it/?p=31505 (English, facts). N-1; EL-E1, EL-E17 (fan site; gives the Agostino diary line in English).

## SC-05 — 1916–1917 (Santa Maria delle Grazie, San Giovanni Rotondo)
- Status: full
- When and where: from 4 Sept 1916; seminary year 1917; the friary of San Giovanni Rotondo [N-10].
- Who was there: Padre Pio; the guardian, Padre Paolino da Casacalenda; the seminary boys (fratini); Padre Agostino, to whom he wrote [N-10].
- What happened, in order:
  1. 4 Sept 1916: he moved "provisionally" from Foggia to San Giovanni Rotondo and stayed until death [N-10]. 11 Sept he told Agostino he found a most edifying community, that his "night of the spirit" grew; Paolino added a note in the envelope [N-10].
  2. 30 Oct 1916: the provincial Benedetto confirmed him there to care for the boys and for many local souls [N-10].
  3. 28 Nov 1916 to 5 Jan 1917 he was away for an army medical check; he returned with six months' leave [N-10].
  4. February 1917: the seminary director, Padre Angelico Colagrossi, was called up. In December Padre Luigi Consalvo followed [N-10; the year of the December call is not stated; read it in context]. Only two priests stayed: Paolino and Padre Pio [N-10].
  5. Padre Pio became spiritual guide of the boys: confessed them, gave two talks a week, watched over study and the weekly walk, joined their recreation, led at lunch and supper, and prayed with them [N-10].
  6. 6 July 1917 he wrote to Agostino [N-10]. 18 Aug to 12 Nov 1917 Naples military hospital again; discharge 15 March 1918, home 17 March [N-10].
- Words spoken: Paolino, 11 Sept 1916: «se si eccettuano soltanto i dolori interni con cui il Signore vuol provarlo, potrebbe dirsi veramente felice». Plain: "apart from the inner pains with which the Lord wishes to test him, he could be called truly happy." Padre Pio, 6 July 1917 [LETTER, short]: «Non mi so negare a nessuno». Plain: "I do not know how to say no to anyone." Context: «la cura di questi ragazzi... ed il soccorrere e consolare anime a voce ed in iscritto... mi rubano tutto il tempo» (the care of these boys and the helping of souls, by voice and in writing, takes all my time).
- Details: 7 priests/friars in the house at first, 3 laymen, 25 student friars [N-10, the "three priests, three lay, 25 fratini" count; EWTN says seven friars before the war [N-1]]. Fever episodes (42.5 and 52 degrees) are Paolino's report as told by Fabiano; not a record. 29 July 1918: alone for a week, he spent nearly all morning hearing confessions [N-10].
- What changed: the boys, and soon many local people, became his daily work.
- Not in the sources: no scene of a single boy's confession; no names of boys; no month for the 1917 spring call-ups beyond Feb and Dec; no building description.
- Sources: N-10 Fr R. Fabiano, "Padre Paolino da Casacalenda, guardiano del convento di San Giovanni Rotondo", Voce di Padre Pio, Sept 2025, pp. 22-25, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf (Italian; facts and short letter lines; text read through pdftotext streamed, not saved). N-1; EP-X7.
