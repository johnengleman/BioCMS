This list gathers the healings, visions, and other wonders attributed to Padre Pio, with their sources and their status. Two of them, the healings examined for his beatification and his canonization, were approved by the Church after medical and theological review. The others are the reports of witnesses, of his friends, of devotional writers, and of Padre Pio himself, and each entry says who reported it. The list makes no claim of scientific proof, and it does not dismiss any account. Where the sources disagree, or where a story is contested, the entry says so.

Miracles approved by the Church

A ruptured thoracic duct healed, Salerno, 1995

Between 1 and 6 November 1995, Consiglia (Lina) De Martino of Salerno was healed of a ruptured thoracic duct without treatment. A church tribunal in Salerno examined the case from July 1996 to June 1997, and its work was declared valid on 26 September 1997. The Vatican’s medical board found the cure scientifically inexplicable on 30 April 1998, the theologians agreed on 22 June, and the cardinals and bishops on 20 October.

Status: Approved by the Church as the miracle for his beatification (decree of 21 December 1998).

Source: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, “Pio da Pietrelcina”, https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/pio-da-pietrelcina.html; Congregation for the Causes of Saints, biography for the beatification, 1999, https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/csaints/documents/rc_con_csaints_doc_19990502_padre-pio_en.html; EWTN, “Highlights of the Cause for Padre Pio”, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/highlights-of-the-cause-for-padre-pio-13861

A seven-year-old with meningitis, San Giovanni Rotondo, 2000

On 20 January 2000 Matteo Pio Colella, aged seven, fell ill in San Giovanni Rotondo with fulminant bacterial meningitis in its septic form, and he recovered.

Status: Approved by the Church as the miracle for his canonization (decree of 20 December 2001, per the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints; one English chronology gives other dates).

Source: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, “Pio da Pietrelcina”, https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/pio-da-pietrelcina.html; Voce di Padre Pio, “Biography”, https://vocedipadrepio.com/en/biography/

During Padre Pio’s life

These accounts come from Padre Pio’s own letters, from the friars and doctors who knew or examined him, from the bishop sent by the Holy Office in 1921, and from friends and pilgrims. None of them was approved by a Church decree.

Visions in the church of Pietrelcina, from 1892

Padre Pio later said that from about the age of five he saw Our Lady and the Sacred Heart come to the altar rail of Santa Maria degli Angeli in Pietrelcina, and that he thought all children saw the same. He also said that on 1 January 1903 a vision after Communion showed him a lifelong struggle with Satan, and that on 5 January, his last night at home, he saw God and Mary encouraging him.

Status: His own later account; not investigated.

Source: “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; EWTN, “Padre Pio: His Biography”, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/padre-pio-his-biography-13815

The stigmata, 1910 to 1968

Padre Pio later said that marks first appeared in his hands on the afternoon of 7 September 1910 at Piana Romana, near Pietrelcina. His letter of September 1911 describes a red mark the size of a coin in each palm, with sharp pain, and in 1915 he wrote that the marks had been visible, especially in one hand, and had gone at his prayer while the pain stayed. On 20 September 1918, after Mass in the choir of Our Lady of Grace at San Giovanni Rotondo, he saw a mysterious figure with bleeding hands, feet, and side, and found his own hands, feet, and side pierced and bleeding; he described it under obedience in a letter of 22 October 1918. Filomena Ventrella, Nina Campanile, the seminary boys, and the guardian, Father Paolino, saw the wounds within about ten days. In 1919 his provincial, Father Benedetto, wrote that they were “true wounds piercing the hands and the feet.” Dr. Romanelli (1919) was certain they were not superficial, Professor Bignami (1919) thought them begun by disease and completed by suggestion or a chemical such as iodine, Dr. Festa reported favorably to the Holy Office in 1925, and Father Gemelli (1920) called the friar hysterical without examining him. Bishop Rossi’s report of 1921 called the stigmata a real fact, neither fraud nor the devil’s work, while the Holy Office in 1923 found nothing supernatural established. The historian Luzzatto later revived testimony from a Foggia pharmacist’s family that Padre Pio had obtained carbolic acid and veratrine; Padre Pio had told Rossi it was for sterilizing syringes and for a joke on the friars, and the journalists Gaeta and Tornielli call the testimony unreliable. The wounds faded before his death, and the doctor at his bedside is said to have found no scar.

Status: Studied by doctors and by the Holy Office; no Church decree approving them as miraculous was found; contested. John Paul II spoke of the stigmata in his beatification homily. The Vatican’s 1968 obituary says a brother found him unconscious behind the high altar, while the accounts that go back to Padre Pio place him alone in the choir with the friary nearly empty.

The transverberation, August and December 1918

On the evening of 5 August 1918, while hearing the confessions of the seminary boys at San Giovanni Rotondo, Padre Pio saw a heavenly figure holding a very long iron blade with fire at its point, which pierced his soul; he sent the boy away, and the pain lasted until the morning of 7 August. “I barely let out a moan; I felt I was dying,” he wrote to Father Benedetto, who answered that 6 August was the feast of the Transfiguration and that the wound was a sign of God’s love. In December 1918 he described a second piercing, by a strip of iron, from low in his heart to below the right shoulder.

Status: Padre Pio’s own account in letters to his spiritual director.

Source: Voce di Padre Pio, July–Aug. 2008 (Epistolario I, pp. 1065–1068), https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2008_7-8_ita_2.pdf; R. Ecstasies, 1905 to 1918

During the years of his illness as a young friar, one of his brothers claimed to have seen him in ecstasy and raised above the ground. At Venafro on 28 November 1911 Father Agostino found him apparently dying, prayed in the chapel, and returned to find him alert and joyful, the first ecstasy recorded. Between March and September 1918 the guardian at San Giovanni Rotondo, Father Paolino, saw him in ecstasy twice and heard him speak with Jesus and Mary. His followers later spoke of levitation and the gift of tongues among his gifts.

Status: Reported by individual friars; popular tradition for levitation and tongues.

Source: EWTN, “Padre Pio: His Biography”, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/padre-pio-his-biography-13815; R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

Twenty days on Communion alone, Venafro, 1911

In 1911, when Padre Pio was confined to bed at the friary of Venafro, one friar said that he lived for more than twenty days on the Eucharist alone.

Status: Reported by a single witness.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

Fevers beyond the thermometer, 1917 and 1920

In January 1917, when Padre Pio had pneumonia and typhoid, the guardian Father Paolino took his temperature; by his account the ordinary thermometer burst after 42.5 °C, and a bath thermometer then read 52 °C. In 1920 Dr. Giorgio Festa measured his temperature twice a day; later accounts of his report give peaks of about 48 °C.

Status: Reported by a friar and a doctor; not a clinical record; disputed.

Source: R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

Reading hearts

Between March and September 1918 Father Paolino came to believe that Padre Pio could read the hearts of those who came to him. In 1921 Padre Pio told Bishop Rossi, the Holy Office’s visitor, that he sometimes perceived other people’s sins or virtues clearly. Asked about bilocation, he said he did not know how it happened: whether his mind, some representation of him, or his body was present elsewhere. In his report of 4 October 1921 Rossi described the many healings people claimed as unconfirmed.

Status: Padre Pio’s own statements to the Holy Office’s visitor and a friar’s testimony; the healings were not confirmed.

Source: R. Fabiano, Voce di Padre Pio, Sept. 2025, https://www.vocedipadrepio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025_9_ita_2.pdf; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

The perfume

Many people reported a perfume associated with Padre Pio, sometimes far from San Giovanni Rotondo. Bishop Rossi, the Holy Office’s visitor in June 1921, noted a very intense and pleasant fragrance like violets, whose source he could not find. His blood was said to smell of violets, roses, or lilies. Asked about the perfume, Padre Pio called it “just sweets for the children.”

Status: Reported by many witnesses, including the Holy Office’s visitor; not investigated as a miracle.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. III, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Luigi Orione in St. Peter’s Square

Luigi Orione, a priest later canonized, reported seeing Padre Pio in St. Peter’s Square in Rome while Padre Pio was at his friary. The Italian Wikipedia dates the report to 1925.

Status: Reported bilocation; not confirmed by the Church.

Source: “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina

A farmer from near Padua, after the Second World War

McCaffery met a farmer from near Padua on a train who told him that, after a farm accident, he had developed clots in both lungs and was dying. He prayed for someone closer to God to intercede, saw a bearded monk at his bedside who put a hand on his chest and vanished, and was cured, as his doctors confirmed. Months later he recognized the monk in a photograph in a house in Padua, went to San Giovanni Rotondo the same night, and after his confession Padre Pio asked him how his lungs were.

Status: Reported by the man himself to John McCaffery; not investigated by the Church.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. I, pp. 1–3, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Father Dominic’s cancer

Father Dominic, an American Capuchin at San Giovanni Rotondo, told McCaffery that Padre Pio had cured him of cancer.

Status: Reported by the man himself; shrine tradition.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. II, pp. 5–6, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

Father Dominic’s father, Milwaukee

Father Dominic’s father in Milwaukee asked his son to beg Padre Pio to help him at his death, and Padre Pio nodded when asked. Father Dominic’s sister later told him that their father, who could not walk without help, was found in his bed rather than on the veranda where they had left him, with a Host on his tongue.

Status: Reported bilocation, told to McCaffery by Father Dominic.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. XII, pp. 33–34, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

A woman from Hawaii, and the perfume

McCaffery met an American woman from Hawaii on the train to Foggia who had come to see Padre Pio and was afraid to go on. At Foggia they met Father Dominic off the same train. The next day she told McCaffery she had smelled a strong perfume in her room that she had not brought, and Father Dominic said it was the time he had spoken to Padre Pio about her: “Quick off the mark.”

Status: Reported by a witness; not investigated.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. II, pp. 4–6, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

A girl born without pupils, Sicily, 1947

Gemma di Giorgi, a Sicilian girl born without pupils, was brought to Padre Pio by her grandmother in 1947. She is reported to have seen afterward, including a steamboat on the sea, and Padre Pio is reported to have told the grandmother that the child could see.

Status: Popular account, from a 1999 book by Malachy Carroll cited in an encyclopedia; no medical file found.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

A prophecy to Karol Wojtyla, 1947 or 1948

A story says that when the young Polish priest Karol Wojtyla visited Padre Pio, Padre Pio foretold that he would reach the highest post in the Church; one version says he called him “your holiness.” Cardinal Stickler reported that Wojtyla had confided it to him, Dziwisz denied it, Weigel’s biography leaves it out, and John Paul II himself dismissed it as mistaken.

Status: Contested.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; J. M. Elder, “The Miracle,” capuchins.org, 2014, https://www.capuchins.org/posts/the-miracle

His own recovery, August 1959

In the summer of 1959 Padre Pio was ill, and on 1 July the new church of the friary was consecrated while he was in the hospital. On 6 August 1959, when a statue of Our Lady of Fatima stopped at San Giovanni Rotondo, he recovered.

Status: Recorded in a dated chronology published by L’Osservatore Romano; not investigated.

Source: L’Osservatore Romano, “Buone Notizie,” 2 May 1999, https://www.vatican.va/news_services/or/or_quo/bnw/bn25a.html

Wanda Półtawska, Krakow, 1962

On 17 November 1962 Karol Wojtyla wrote to Padre Pio in Latin asking prayers for a forty-year-old mother of four daughters in Krakow, a survivor of five years in a German concentration camp, gravely ill with cancer. Angelo Battisti, administrator of the Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza, read the letter aloud to Padre Pio, who said, by Battisti’s account, that this one could not be refused. The woman, Dr. Wanda Półtawska, was due for surgery on 21 or 22 November; the doctors found nothing to operate on. On 28 November Wojtyla wrote again to thank Padre Pio, saying she had been suddenly cured before the operation, and her husband told Wojtyla the doctors faced a mystery.

Status: Reported in later accounts of the two letters; not investigated as a miracle. The accounts differ on the date of the planned operation.

Source: Fundación CARF, “Friendship between Saints,” 2025, https://fundacioncarf.org/en/amistad-entre-santos-juan-pablo-ii-y-padre-pio/; J. M. Elder, “The Miracle,” capuchins.org, 2014, https://www.capuchins.org/posts/the-miracle; Our Sunday Visitor, obituary of Wanda Półtawska, https://www.oursundayvisitor.com/wanda-poltawska-st-john-paul-iis-soulmate-and-adviser-dies-at-101/

A lamp that would not light, 1968

When a BBC crew under Mischa Scorer filmed Padre Pio’s Mass some time before his death, they set one lamp to shine straight onto the altar. The next morning, Scorer told McCaffery, every lamp worked except that one. McCaffery took it as a sign that Padre Pio would not have his Mass disturbed.

Status: Told to McCaffery by the producer; McCaffery’s own interpretation.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), ch. XLVIII, pp. 127–128, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf

After Padre Pio’s death

The wounds gone, 23 September 1968

McCaffery wrote that the stigmata had faded gradually before Padre Pio died. After his death, by the Wikipedia accounts, the doctor at the bedside found the wounds completely healed, without trace or scar, and the friars who prepared the body saw no mark. The sources differ on whether the wounds faded over months or in the last days.

Status: Reported by witnesses; not presented by the Church as a miracle.

Source: John McCaffery, The Friar of San Giovanni (1978), pp. 125–126, https://ia601501.us.archive.org/11/items/friar-of-san-giovanni/Friar_of_San_Giovanni_text.pdf; “Pio da Pietrelcina,” Italian Wikipedia, https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pio_da_Pietrelcina; “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio

The opening of the tomb, 2008

When the tomb was opened after midnight on 3 March 2008, Archbishop D’Ambrosio reported the body in fair condition: the skull partly skeletal, the chin and body well preserved, the hands looking manicured, and no visible stigmata. A silicone mask made from a 1968 photograph was used for the display.

Status: The archbishop’s report of the exhumation; no miracle was claimed, and the report did not call the body incorrupt.

Source: “Padre Pio,” English Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio; Catholic News Agency, 3 March 2008, https://www.catholicnewsagency.com/news/padre_pio_exhumed_body_is_well_preserved
