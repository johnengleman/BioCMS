# Padre Pio of Pietrelcina — scene outline (standard 1b)

Tier A. Catholic. 1887–1968. Canonized 16 June 2002.

**Key prefixes.** Three notes files number their sources S1, S2… separately, so each key carries a file prefix:

- **OW-** `notes-own-words.md` (OW-S3 = it.wikipedia; OW-S4 = it.wikiquote)
- **CM-** `notes-church-miracles.md` (CM-S4b = caccioppoli miracles page)
- **EL-** `notes-extra-life.md` (E keys; "C-only" = EL-E17 fan site)
- **EP-** `notes-extra-primary.md` (X keys)

**Rights marks.** [OFFICIAL] Holy See text: short lines quotable. [PD] public domain (1920, 1923 newspapers). [LETTER] his Epistolario: under copyright, so short Italian clauses in our own English only. [SHORT] modern copyrighted witness or book: paraphrase, one short quote at most. ## 1. The driving question

He wanted to be "only a poor friar who prays" [OW-S1 OFFICIAL] and to suffer "all in hiding" [EL-E13 CHECK]. Instead, wounds in his hands made him the most watched priest in Italy, and the Church he obeyed suspected, silenced, and investigated him for forty years [W-S3, EL-E13]. He answered with obedience ("In Holy Church it is God himself who speaks" [EP-X13 OFFICIAL]) and turned the crowds toward the confessional, the altar, and a hospital; in his last days the wounds faded without a scar [EL-E13, W-S6].

**Why famous:** the Capuchin with the stigmata who heard confessions for fifty years in one friary, built a great hospital, and was canonized by a Pope who had confessed to him [W-S1, CM-S3].

**Set apart:** In the age of newspapers and television he never left one small friary for 52 years, and the world came to him [W-S1, EL-E7]. He joined a peasant's mystical prayer to modern medicine, in a hospital meant for "prayer and science" [EL-E6].

## 2. Portrait (not published)

A peasant's son, direct and quick. Sick all his youth, with fevers and weak lungs [EL-E13, EP-X7]. Funny with visitors: he offered to take fifty per cent of a talkative wife [W-S4 p.78]; he laughed at the idea of keeping for himself what belonged to God [W-S4 pp.62–63]. Bishop Rossi found him "serene, jovial, even humorous" [W-S5]. Severe in the confessional, yet penitents came back [CM-S3]. Ashamed of the wounds: he hid his hands and told the women of the town not to tell [EP-X10]. He fought vainglory, "a martyrdom" [OW-S4], and asked friends to pray "for my conversion" [EP-X4]. He could not refuse anyone [EP-X7].

**Life with God.** Mass was the center of the day: "The world could do without the sun, but it cannot do without the Holy Mass" [EP-X6 LETTER]. He knew peace in prayer "no words can express" [EP-X12 LETTER] and darkness: "I seek God, but where can I find him?" [OW-S4 LETTER]. "Under the Cross one learns to love" [OW-S2 OFFICIAL].

## 3. The opening

**Hook: a scene at the edge** (SC-09), then back to the hidden wish.

> In the summer of 1923, word went round San Giovanni Rotondo that the Church was taking its friar away. The townspeople marched to the friary and would not leave until he showed himself at a window. A London newspaper's man in Rome went to see this friar, and he did not find a wonder-worker. "Padre Pio himself lays no claim to miraculous gifts," he wrote. "He is the most modest and simple of men." Twelve years earlier, the man at the window had begged his village priest for one thing: to suffer, even to die of suffering, but all in hiding.

Facts: EL-E7 [PD]; W-S3 (1923 transfer order); EL-E13 (1911 words, CHECK; fallback: "My God, what confusion and humiliation I feel", EP-X9 LETTER).

**Thesis:** He carried wounds like Christ's for fifty years [EP-X4, W-S3]. The altar and the confessional were "the two poles of his life" [EL-E12 OFFICIAL]. He left a hospital and a worldwide movement of prayer groups [W-S1, EL-E15]. Paul VI said he was famous because he said Mass humbly and heard confessions from morning to night: "a man of prayer and suffering" [W-S1 OFFICIAL]. Answered in chapters 12–14.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — 1897 (Pietrelcina, the Forgione home)
- What happens: a bearded Capuchin quester visits; the boy says he wants to be "a friar... with a beard"; his father goes to America to pay tutors.
- Why: the first wish to belong to God, and its cost to the family.
- Have: dawn prayer, nightly Rosary, parents unable to read [EL-E1]; quester, America [EL-E1, EL-E13, W-S3]; the five-year-old who thought all children saw Our Lady [EL-E13].
- Need: the boy's words in Italian; the year his father left.
- Where to look: EL-E1, EL-E13.

### SC-02 — Good Friday 1908 (Serracapriola friary)
- What happens: meditating on the Passion, he weeps until his eyes grow sick; he cannot eat; the friars telegraph his father, who takes him home.
- Why: the hard part; a body that cannot keep the Rule.
- Have: [EL-E13]; teacher: "good, obedient, studious, though sickly" [EL-E13 SHORT].
- Need: the exact telegram chain.
- Where to look: EL-E13.

### SC-03 — 7 Sept 1910 and Sept 1911 (Piana Romana hut; the parish priest)
- What happens: after Mass he prays under the elm by his brother's hut; red marks appear in his palms; a year later he asks the parish priest to pray them away.
- Why: plants the wish for hiddenness.
- Have: hut, elm [EL-E13, EL-E18]; 1910 date [EP-X13 OFFICIAL]; "a little red, the size of a cent coin" [OW-S3 LETTER]; "that cursed shame" [EL-E13]; Zi' Tore dialogue [EL-E13 CHECK]; marks taken away by prayer, pain stayed [EP-X2, Epist. I p.669 LETTER].
- Need: a named source for the Zi' Tore dialogue; the Italian of the 8 Sept 1911 letter.
- Where to look: Epistolario I; W-S5.

### SC-04 — 28 Nov 1911 (Venafro friary)
- What happens: Padre Agostino finds him seemingly dying, prays in the chapel, returns, and finds him bright and joyful: the first recorded ecstasy.
- Why: life with God.
- Have: [EL-E1]; hunger for Communion "with fever on me" [EP-X5 LETTER]; peace in prayer [EP-X12 LETTER]; Our Lady "flew into a rage" when he asked again, 1913, his own fault in his words [EP-X8 LETTER].
- Need: Agostino's own diary words.
- Where to look: EL-E1; EL-E17 (C-only).

### SC-05 — 1917 (Santa Maria delle Grazie, San Giovanni Rotondo)
- What happens: the teachers are at war; he and the guardian run the seminary alone; he hears the boys' confessions and writes that he cannot say no to anyone.
- Why: covers the army years and the arrival; the crowd of souls begins.
- Have: arrival 1916, call-ups, discharge March 1918 [EP-X7, W-S3]; "Non mi so negare a nessuno" [EP-X7 LETTER]; guardian: "apart from interior pains… truly happy" [EP-X7].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: EP-X7.

### SC-06 — Friday 20 Sept 1918, about 9–10 a.m. (the choir)
- What happens: the friary is nearly empty; after Mass he prays alone; a figure with bleeding hands, feet, and side appears; his own are pierced. A month later he writes it under obedience.
- Why: the center of the life; the hidden wish fails.
- Have: 5 Aug lead-in [EP-X3 LETTER]; "a rest came over me, like a sweet sleep", "I felt I was dying" [EP-X9 LETTER]; guardian away [EP-X10].
- Need: Epistolario page of the 22 Oct letter.
- Testimony: 22 Oct 1918 letter [EP-X9]: copyrighted, so three or four short clauses only.
- Where to look: EP-X9, EP-X10.

### SC-07 — 21 Sept 1918 (the friary)
- What happens: a townswoman cries, "You've burned your hand!"; he hides it behind his back. The guardian laughs at the news, then walks in without knocking and sees.
- Why: hiding, in small human acts.
- Have: all [EP-X10]; "If you knew what humiliation you give me!" [EP-X10 SHORT]; Fr Benedetto 1919: "true wounds" [EP-X4 LETTER].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: EP-X10.

### SC-08 — June 1921 (the friary; Rome's secret visitor)
- What happens: Bishop Rossi questions him for eight days, smells violets, and finds only plain soap; asked if he will always submit to the Church, he says yes.
- Why: obedience under suspicion, in his own words.
- Have: [W-S5, CM-S5]; "Yes, Excellency. In Holy Church it is God himself who speaks" [EP-X13 OFFICIAL]; bridge: doctors 1919 [EL-E13]; a famous priest-doctor refused without written orders, 1920 [W-S5].
- Need: Rossi's own words on the soap.
- Where to look: W-S5 (Castelli 2011).

### SC-09 — Summer 1923 (San Giovanni Rotondo)
- What happens: Rome orders him moved; the town marches to the friary until he appears at a window; men guard it in turns; the move is dropped.
- Why: the hook; fame he did not seek.
- Have: order 30 July, stopped 17 Aug [W-S3]; march, window, guards, Observer [EL-E7 PD]; Southern Cross 1920 [EL-E7 PD].
- Need: what he said about the order.
- Where to look: W-S3; EL-E13.

### SC-10 — 9 Jan 1940 (the friary; first hospital committee)
- What happens: friends give small sums for a hospital; he hands over a ten-cent Swiss coin: "I also want to give my offering." Close on 5 May 1956: an American heart doctor congratulates him "on your wounds".
- Why: the crowds turned toward the sick.
- Have: [EL-E6 SHORT]; "This evening my earthly work has begun" [EL-E15]; 250 beds [EL-E6]; Paul Dudley White [W-S4 pp.45–46 SHORT]; "The Work is not mine… but Providence's" [EL-E6].

### SC-11 — A summer morning, 1950s (the old church)
- What happens: women queue from 2:30 a.m. and once chain a stool to the altar rail; his Mass lasts ninety minutes; then the confessional, hour after hour.
- Why: life with God in the daily round.
- Have: [W-S4 pp.56–57, EL-E16 SHORT]; booking from 1950 [EL-E13]; "It is the nights" [W-S4 p.64]; severe, yet penitents returned [CM-S3 OFFICIAL].

- Where to look: W-S4.

### SC-12 — 1960 (the friary; Rome's visitor)
- What happens: microphones hidden in his cell; Rome's visitor holds nine meetings and judges him harshly; the Pope writes privately that he prays for him.
- Why: the hardest trial, told fairly from both sides.
- Have: Maccari diary [EL-E15 SHORT]; John XXIII, 25 June 1960 [EL-E13]; rebuttal story [EL-E13, unverified]; freedom restored 30 July 1964 [EL-E13]; guardian: never failed in obedience [EL-E13].
- Need: his own reaction, if a witness records it.
- Where to look: EL-E13.

### SC-13 — Nov 1962 (his cell)
- What happens: an official reads aloud a Latin letter from Kraków asking prayer for a mother of four with cancer; he says, "Angelo, to this one it is not possible to say no!" Eleven days later: she was cured before surgery.
- Why: ties the 1947 visit to the canonization.
- Have: [EL-E9, EL-E10, EL-E11 SHORT].
- Need: the Latin wording of either letter.
- Where to look: EL-E9, EL-E11.

### SC-14 — 22–23 Sept 1968 (church, cell, terrace)
- What happens: at the anniversary Mass he nearly falls; at night he asks Fr Pellegrino to stay, confesses, asks his brothers' forgiveness for "all the trouble", renews his vows, walks fast to the terrace, then sinks repeating "Jesus, Mary", and dies at 2:30.
- Why: the death; a fault owned; the wounds gone.
- Have: [EL-E8 SHORT, paraphrase only]; [W-S4 pp.125–126]; no scar [EL-E13, W-S6]; funeral [EL-E2].
- Need: Funicelli's Italian original.
- Where to look: EL-E8.

### SC-15 — 20 Jan 2000 (Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza)
- What happens: Matteo, 7, a doctor's son, falls into septic shock; his mother prays at the tomb; he wakes and says a bearded old man in a brown habit told him he would get well. Close on 16 June 2002.
- Why: the real case after death, in his own hospital.
- Have: [CM-S4b SHORT]; JPII 2002, "I too, in my youth, benefited" [CM-S3 OFFICIAL]; "this humble Capuchin friar astonished the world" [CM-S1 OFFICIAL].
- Need: the boy's words from an official source.
- Where to look: Dicastery decree (not read).

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** letters to his directors, 1910–22, written under obedience; Epistolario I–IV from 1971 [OW-S3]; letters to Raffaelina Cerase 1914–15 [EP-X4]. Chapters 3, 5.
- **What he changed:** Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza, 5 May 1956 [W-S1], left to the Holy See [EL-E14]. Prayer Groups from 1942 [OW-S3]: about 700 in 1968, about 3,300 in 60 countries in 2013 [EL-E15]; statutes 1986 [CM-S5]. JPII named him with the Curé of Ars as a great confessor, 1994 [EP section 5, Penitenzieria address, OFFICIAL]. Chapters 8, 13.
- **Why famous:** Italian press from May 1919, foreign by 1920 [EL-E7]; Allied soldiers after 1943 [EL-E7]; about 2 million pilgrims a year (estimate) [CM-S5]; relics in St Peter's, Feb 2016 [CM-S6]. People bring sins and sickness. Chapters 7, 9, 13.
- **Path:** nihil obstat 29 Nov 1982; diocesan process 1983–90; heroic virtue 18 Dec 1997 [EP-X11]; beatified 2 May 1999; canonized 16 June 2002 [W-S1]; feast 23 Sept [OW-S1]. Chapter 14.
