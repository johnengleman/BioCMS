## 1. The driving question

Saint Pio wanted to love Jesus by suffering with him, as "only a poor friar who prays" [OW-S1 OFFICIAL], and he wanted it hidden: "I want to suffer, even to die of suffering, but all in hiding" [card 03]. God gave him the suffering but not the hiding: in 1918 the wounds became visible, the world climbed his mountain, and the Church he obeyed doubted, silenced, and watched him. He answered with obedience and the confessional, and at the very end the hiddenness was given back.

**Why it was beautiful to him:** "In love he delights me; in sorrows I delight him" [OW-S4 Q12 LETTER]; "Under the Cross one learns to love" [OW-S2 OFFICIAL]. Hiding kept the gift between him and Jesus; vainglory was "a martyrdom" to him [OW-S4 Q8]. First heard: chapter 1 (the boy who wants the friar's robe); named in full in chapter 3, his first decision as a priest: to ask that the marks be taken away.

**The later life's question.** The great turn comes at 31. After it: now that everyone can see, what does he love, and what does it cost? He loves the souls who climb the hill ("I do not know how to say no to anyone" [EP-X7]) and the Mass. It costs his rest ("It is the nights" [W-S4 p.64]), his freedom (1922–33, 1960–64), and his good name. Tell chapters 6–11 through this question.

**Why famous:** the Capuchin with the wounds of Christ who heard confessions for fifty years in one friary, built a hospital, and was canonized by a Pope who had confessed to him [W-S1, CM-S3].

**Set apart:** in an age of newspapers he stayed 52 years in one small friary, and the world came to him [W-S1, EL-E7]. He joined a peasant's prayer to modern medicine: "prayer and science" [EL-E6].

## 2. Portrait (not published)

A peasant's son, direct and quick, sick all his youth [EL-E13]. Funny: fifty per cent of a talkative wife [W-S4 p.78]; mad or a thief to keep what was God's [W-S4 pp.62–63]; he teased Nina that he had waited three days for "a Mary" [card 07]. Bishop Rossi saw "a singular simplicity" [card 08]. Severe in the confessional, yet penitents came back [CM-S3]. Ashamed of the wounds; hid his hands behind his back [card 07]. Asked friends to pray "for my conversion" [EP-X4]. Could not refuse anyone [EP-X7]. Faults in his own words: he dared to ask Our Lady again for a refused grace [EP-X8]; at the end he asked pardon for "all the trouble" [card 14].

**Life with God.** He went to the altar "with fever on me" from hunger for Communion [EP-X5]. Peace in prayer "no words can express" [EP-X12], and darkness: "I seek God, but where can I find him?" [OW-S4 Q9]. "The world could do without the sun, but it cannot do without the Holy Mass" [EP-X6].

## 3. The opening

**Hook: a scene at the edge.** The 1923 window march is told only here; chapter 7 tells the midnight order instead.

> In the summer of 1923 the people of San Giovanni Rotondo heard that their friar was to be sent away. They walked up the mountain to the friary and would not go home until he came to a window. That summer a London newspaper's man in Rome went to see him and found no wonder-worker: "Padre Pio himself lays no claim to miraculous gifts; he is the most modest and simple of men." The friar at the window was Saint Pio of Pietrelcina, whom the world still knows as Padre Pio. For five years he had carried open wounds in his hands, his feet, and his side, and much of Italy knew it. He had begged God that no one would ever see them.

Facts: EL-E7 [PD]; card 09; EP-X2 and card 07 (his prayers that the marks would go). Keep out of the opening: the "all in hiding" line (chapter 3), the death and the healed wounds (chapter 12), Paul VI's words (chapter 13).

**Thesis** (narrator's words): For fifty years Saint Pio spent his days at the altar and in the confessional of one friary [W-S4 p.56, EL-E12]. He left a hospital that belongs to the Holy See and a worldwide movement of prayer groups [EL-E14, EL-E15]. The Pope who canonized him had confessed to him as a young priest [CM-S3]. Answered in chapters 9, 10, 13.

**Ending.** Climax: the last night, ending on the healed hands (chapter 12). Then one short scene (SC-15) and a short close: hospital, prayer groups, one sentence for beatification and canonization, with Matteo Colella's healing in one clause. **Closing image:** the prayer groups, "a great river of people who pray" (Pope Saint Paul VI [EP-X11]; Fact need 4), then one quiet sentence: his wish to be "only a poor friar who prays."

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points (told from inside him):** TP1 SC-03, TP2 SC-06, TP3 SC-07, TP4 SC-09, TP5 SC-14 (climax).

**Resistance in each period; compress the rest to a sentence or two:** childhood, no money (SC-01); formation, his body (SC-02); Pietrelcina, shame and darkness (SC-03, SC-04); 1915–18, overwork (SC-05); 1918–23, suspicion and the order (SC-08, SC-09; doctors and Gemelli in one paragraph); 1924–33, loss and silence (SC-10); 1934–56, war and money (SC-11); 1950s, pain and crowds (SC-12; the jokes in one paragraph); 1960–68, the second inquiry (SC-13).

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** letters to his directors 1910–22, under obedience; Epistolario I–IV from 1971 [OW-S3]. Ch. 3–5.
- **What he changed:** Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza, 1956, left to the Holy See [W-S1, EL-E14]; prayer groups from 1942, about 3,300 in 60 countries by 2013 [EL-E15]; named a great confessor with the Curé of Ars, 1994 [EP §5]. Ch. 9, 13.
- **Why famous:** press from May 1919 [EL-E7]; about two million pilgrims a year (estimate) [CM-S5]; people bring sins and sickness. Ch. 6, 10, 13.
- **Path:** beatified 2 May 1999, canonized 16 June 2002 [W-S1]: one sentence, ch. 13.
