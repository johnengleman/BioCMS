# Mary of Egypt — research notes (tier B) — COMPLETE (session 2)

## 1. Identity
- Names: Mary of Egypt; Maria Aegyptiaca (Lat); Μαρία ἡ Αἰγυπτία (Gk); Mary the Egyptian; Venerable Mother Mary of Egypt [S4]
- Dates: trad. b. c.344, d. c.421 [S3 CathEnc]. Life itself gives no year; Bollandists chose 421; other scholars put it a century later [S3, S2 p.16]. Day of death in Life: 1 April (Fermoutin), night of Passion (Holy Thursday/Good Friday) [S1]. Latin version gives 2 April [S2 p.16].
- Tradition: shared (Orthodox, Catholic, Oriental Orthodox; also Anglican) [S4]. Coptic feast: Parmouti 6 [S4 - check].
- Feasts: Eastern Orthodox 1 April + Fifth Sunday of Great Lent [S4]; Roman Catholic 1 April (Ordinary Form) / 2 April (Extraordinary Form/Martyrology) [S4]; older: Latin 9 April (Butler/old Martyrology), Roman Calendar 3 April [S3]; Zosimus 4 April Greeks [S2].

## 3. Timeline (the Life's story; strength: LIFE-ONLY, late, tradition via monks)
- Frame: Zosimas, priest-monk in a Palestinian monastery since childhood; at 53 thinks himself perfect; an angel tells him to go to a monastery by the Jordan [S1]. NEW detail: "taken from his mother's breast" into the monastery [S1].
- Lenten rule of the Jordan monastery: after liturgy on Forgiveness Sunday monks kiss and ask pardon, gates open, sing Ps 26 ("The Lord is my light") and scatter across the Jordan; no one speaks or asks how another fasted; return Palm Sunday [S1]. (Rule explains the meeting.)
- Zosimas walks 20 days into the desert; at the sixth hour, singing the Hour, sees a naked, sun-blackened figure with hair white as fleece, who flees [S1].
- She calls him "Abba Zosima" by name, asks for his cloak to cover herself; he throws it, turning away [S1]. Both lie prostrate, each begging "Bless me!" [S1].
- She prays raised about a forearm's length off the ground; Zosimas doubts "is it a spirit?"; she answers that she is "earth and ashes, and flesh alone" and makes the sign of the cross [S1].
- Her story: born Egypt; left parents at 12 for Alexandria; 17 years of sexual life; refused money often; lived by begging and spinning flax [S1]. NEW/vivid: she says it was not for gain but appetite ("doing free of charge what gave me pleasure") [S1]. Latin/other versions may differ in wording: check not done.
- Summer: sees crowd of Libyans and Egyptians running to the sea for Jerusalem, Exaltation of the Cross; no money, so pays with her body; drops her spinning wheel and runs to the shore [S1].
- Ship: about ten or more young men; she seduced many on the voyage [S1].
- Jerusalem: Exaltation day; at the church door an unseen force stops her three or four times; she stands in the porch weeping [S1]. (Life says "church"/"temple", the church where the Cross was shown; tradition: Holy Sepulchre. Check Greek wording.)
- Icon of the Mother of God above the porch: prays and vows to renounce the world; then enters freely, venerates the Cross [S1].
- Voice: "If you cross the Jordan you will find glorious rest." [S1 paraphrase]
- A stranger gives her three coins; she buys three loaves; asks the way to the Jordan; reaches the Church of St John the Baptist at sunset; communicates; sleeps on the ground; next morning crosses by a small boat [S1].
- 47 years in the desert: two and a half loaves lasted years; 17 years fighting memory of meat, fish, wine and "profligate songs"; she prays to the Mother of God and light calms her; then herbs; clothes rotted away [S1].
- She says she never read: "the word of God ... by itself teaches a man knowledge" (paraphrase) yet quotes Scripture [S1].
- She asks silence till her death; tells him not to cross the Jordan next Lent; to bring the Eucharist at sunset on Holy Thursday ("the Last Supper") to the Jordan bank; to tell Abbot John to correct the monastery [S1].
- Year later: Zosimas ill with fever on the first Sunday of Lent, cannot leave; Holy Thursday brings chalice, figs, dates, soaked lentils [S1].
- Jordan crossing: moonlit night; she makes the sign of the cross over the water and walks across; forbids him to kneel ("you are a priest and carrying the divine Gifts") [S1].
- Communion; she says Nunc dimittis; takes three lentil grains; crosses the river again [S1].
- Next year he finds her dead on the far side, hands crossed, face to the east; writing in the sand: bury Mary; she died "Fermoutin ... called April ... on the first day, on the very night of our Lord's Passion" [S1]. Translator's note in S1: Zosimas deduces she was carried in one hour a 20-day distance.
- No spade: he picks up a piece of wood; the lion appears, licks her feet; at his request digs the grave with its front paws; both leave; lion "like a lamb" [S1]. Zosimas covers her with his own old cloak [S1].
- Zosimas tells the brothers; Abbot John corrects abuses; Zosimas dies almost 100 [S1].
- Closing: monks kept the story orally; Sophronius "wrote it down" [S1].

## Authorship, date, historicity (S5 Baring-Gould pp.15-17; S3)
- Author claim: Nicephorus Callistus (d. 1341) attributes it to Sophronius, patriarch of Jerusalem (d. 639); Papebroeck disputed it and gave it 150 years more age [S5]. BG argues for Sophronius: Cross interest, friend John Moschus [S5].
- Author's own statement: the monks passed the story by word of mouth; writer "committed it to writing... wishing to add nothing to the truth" (BG's translation, p.16) [S5].
- Date problem: Life gives no year; Life puts death on Good Friday 1 April (Greek) vs 2 April (early Latin); BG lists possible years 421, 423, 427, 511, 516, 522 (1 April) or 443, 454, 527, 538, 549 (2 April) [S5]. 421 chosen by the Bollandists [S5].
- Earlier trace: Cyril of Scythopolis, Life of Cyriacus (d. 556): two disciples find a naked woman, Mary, in the desert beyond the Jordan, who says she was a public singer and player; later found dead in her cave and buried with spades from the laura of Souka. BG calls it "perfectly authentic" and calls the Life "a romance founded on fact" [S5]. (BG's own view; modern scholars may differ; not read.) Note: this Mary is a musician, different from the Life's heroine.
- Gregory the Great letter to Candidus about 596 mentions lions burying "Paul the first hermit and S. Mary of Egypt", probably a later insertion [S5].
- Cited at Nicaea II, 787, on images [S5].
- Latin version attributed to Paul the Deacon (d. 797); Hildebert of Le Mans verse paraphrase; Anglo-Saxon fragments (Gloucester Fragments, Earle 1863) [S5].
- Relics (S5 p.43 PDF): body taken from Jerusalem by Abbot Luke of Carbone (Calabria), head to convent of S. Mary of Egypt at Naples, body translated to Urbino cathedral [S5; check].

## 1 addendum. Identity facts
- Coptic feast: Baramouda (Parmouti) 6, "Departure of St. Mary of Egypt", year 137 A.M. (421 A.D.), born Alexandria c.345, "seventy-six years" of life, from Christian parents [S9]. NB Coptic Synaxarium says she went to "the door of the church of the Resurrection" [S9] and that a lion stood beside her body and writing said "Bury Mary, the poor woman, in the dust of which she was created" [S9; paraphrase; differs from S1 detail where the lion appears while Zosimas digs].
- Feast conflicts: S6 says Orthodox 1 April; Catholic 2, 3, 9 or 10 April by place [S6]. CathEnc: Latin 9 April, Roman Martyrology 2 April, Roman Calendar 3 April; Greek date likely correct; the Western dates may mark relic arrivals [S3].
- Fifth Sunday of Lent: moved from 1 April because weekday saints' feasts are forbidden in Lent (Laodicea canon 51) and the Life is read in Lent [S7]. First attested on that Sunday: a Jerusalem manuscript of 1201; common by end of the 14th c.; before that the Sunday's focus was Rich Man and Lazarus; Palamas homily 13 does not mention her [S7]. So the Sunday is late; the Thursday reading is older (below).
- Patronage (modern, popular, no old source): chastity, deliverance from carnal passions, demons, fever, skin diseases, temptations of the flesh [S10, S12]; "patron saint of penitents" [S10]. Mark as popular, not decreed.

## 3 addendum. Date and authorship, weighed
- Dates inside the Life: she died 1 April on Holy Thursday night (Greek: "the very night of our Lord's Passion", S1). S6 says Holy Thursday and lists Julian-calendar years where 1 April = Thursday and Easter = 4 April: 443, 454, 527, 538, 549; S6 argues only the last two or three fit the ages given. BG (S5) reads the same day as Good Friday and lists other years. Sources conflict on the weekday: tell the reader the night "of the Passion" is ambiguous.
- Ages by arithmetic from the Life [S1; S6 as author of the sums]: left home at 12, 17 years in Alexandria (about 29 at conversion), 47 years in the desert (about 76 when Zosimas met her), died about 78 [S6 items 3, 9]. Compute: 12+17=29; 29+47=76; she died about a year later. These are sums from the Life, not independent dates.
- Authorship conflict: Catholic Encyclopedia says the Life was "written not very long after her death", names Sophronius (patriarch 635) as the usual attribution, but says the Bollandists believe it was written before 500 so it is "from some other hand" [S3]. BG: Papebroeck gave it 150 years more age; BG himself favors Sophronius [S5]. S6 and S7 treat Sophronius as author and say the 9th-century is the earliest manuscript [S6 item 13]. Wikipedia lead: Sophronius credited; later scholarship suggested earlier "from some other hand" [S12]. Web search summaries (not read in full text): modern scholars mostly doubt Sophronius, and place the Greek Life late 6th or 7th c. [search result, no page read; mark UNVERIFIED].
- Relation to Cyriacus story: S5, S10, S6 item 21 all state the Cyril of Scythopolis Life of Cyriacus tells of a naked woman named Mary in the desert beyond the Jordan who had been a singer/actress, found dead and buried by monks. S5 calls the Life "a romance founded on fact." S6 rejects borrowing, relying on the Life's own claim (S6 item 21). Honest line for the entry: the oldest firm trace of a penitent Mary in the Judean desert is the Cyriacus episode (6th c.), which differs from the Life; the Egyptian prostitute story appears only in the Life. S10 says Cyril "contributed" the story; Wikipedia says Cyril contributed details on Mary of Egypt (c.344-421) [S12, weak].
- The Life says her story was passed by word of mouth by the monks of Zosimas' monastery to the writer [S1, S5]. Greek manuscripts: about 27 in the National Library of Athens and 37 in Paris [S6 item 15, check].
- Cited at Nicaea II (787, fourth session) for the veneration of images [S5, S12]. Icon location: S6 item 6 says the Theotokos icon stood on a raised place in the courtyard before Constantine's church; Piacenza Pilgrim (c.570) and Epiphanios the Monk (8th c.) mention it (S6 only; not verified in the primary texts). A chapel at the Holy Sepulchre commemorates her conversion [S6 item 17, not verified].
- Does the Life say "Holy Sepulchre"? S1 (modern translation) says "church" with the Cross shown from it. S9 says "church of the Resurrection." Wikipedia/S6 say Holy Sepulchre. Safe wording: "the church in Jerusalem where the Cross was shown (tradition: the Holy Sepulchre complex)".
- Church of St John the Baptist on the Jordan: built under Anastasius I (491-518) [S6 item 7, not verified]. If true, the Life must postdate 491, which sits badly with a 421 death only if one trusts the Life's setting; the Life itself makes no date claim, only S6's reading. Keep the point as "S6 claim".
- Cross relic problem: S6 item 5: Persians took Cross fragments in 614, returned 629; S6 uses this to date the setting to before 614 [reasoning of S6, not in S1].

## Liturgy and the Great Canon
- Great Canon of Andrew of Crete (7th c.): four parts, nine odes; some odes carry extra troparia to Andrew or to Mary of Egypt [S12/OrthodoxWiki Great Canon]. On the Wednesday evening of week 5 (Matins of Thursday) the whole Canon is read with the Life of Mary: first half of the Life after the kathisma, second half after the third ode [S11]. Special canon to Mary is chanted along with it [S11]. Next day a Presanctified Liturgy is held, fast eased (wine and oil) [Wikipedia Great Canon, S12].
- The 7th-century origin of the Thursday reading: a search summary says it dates "from the time of the Sixth Ecumenical Council"; no page read confirming it; UNVERIFIED. OrthodoxWiki says Andrew himself was the author of the Life and implemented the practice: this contradicts S1/S6 (Sophronius); treat as unreliable [OrthodoxWiki Great Canon, seen in search excerpt].
- Tie between Mary and Andrew in the Canon's text: not read (only the existence of troparia to her). Do not quote.

## Relics and cult
- Relics tradition (S5 PDF p.43, Baring-Gould, public domain): Abbot Luke carried the body from Jerusalem in 1059 to Carbone; head to the convent of S. Mary of Egypt at Naples; body later to Urbino cathedral; a finger given to the Naples convent in 1335 by the Queen of Aragon; larger part claimed at Cremona; jaw at S. Erasmus, Cremona; portions at Antwerp (from Portugal, 1610) [S5]. BG notes the story that Hormisdas gave relics to Eleutherius of Tournai is "questionable" [S5]. CathEnc: relics at Rome, Naples, Cremona, Antwerp [S3]. NB: the Life says Zosimas buried her in the desert; the relic claims are late and unproven.
- Moscow: In 1707 the diplomat Yemelyan Ukraintsev gave the Sretensky Monastery the relic (right foot) in a silver reliquary, a gift from Patriarch Dositheos of Jerusalem; it was lost after the monastery closed (1925); in 2004 a bone of the right foot from the Greek monastery of St Nicholas on Andros was brought to the monastery; in 2009 Patriarch Kirill consecrated her chapel [S8]. NEW for English readers: the silver reliquary survives in the State Historical Museum, Moscow [S8].
- Rome: S6 item 18 says the Temple of Portunus was rededicated to her in 872 (not verified; the building stood as S. Maria Egiziaca).
- Art (S12): East shows her gaunt and old; West often confuses her with Magdalene. Icon types: 17th-c. Ukrainian icon with her Life in scenes, Athonite icons (S12; Wikimedia Commons candidates, not checked).
- Literary afterlife (S6 items 20): Goethe's Faust, Mahler 8th. Latin translation by Paul the Deacon in the 8th c. (S6; S5 says d. 797); Paul "of Naples" per search summary, not read. Hildebert of Le Mans verse (S5). Anglo-Saxon translation (S5). Nuremberg Chronicle includes her (S12).

## 4. Character (from the Life; lived voice in the Life is the author's composition, per S5)
- Honesty: she insists her sin was appetite not profit [S1].
- Humor/ordinary detail: tells Zosimas that when she was a girl on the quay she added "a few more words" that made the young men laugh [S1].
- Faults after conversion: cravings for meat, fish, wine, and old songs for 17 years; she lies on the ground for a day and a night until a calm light comes [S1].
- Humility: refuses his bow on the water because he carries the Gifts [S1].
- Shy modesty: asks for his cloak before she will face him [S1].
- Zosimas' flaw: pride at age 53; he learns from a woman [S1].
- Reaction of critics: S5 doubts the account; S6 defends [S5, S6].

## 7. Miracles (all LIFE-ONLY, late, no independent witness)
- Barred at the church door, then admitted after prayer before the icon: Life [S1], repeated CathEnc [S3], Coptic [S9].
- Levitation at prayer; naming Zosimas and knowing the monastery's rule (insight) [S1].
- Survival 47 years in the desert; clothes rotted, fed on herbs and God's word [S1].
- Walking on the Jordan, twice (once each way) [S1]; CathEnc retells [S3].
- Moved from the Jordan to the place of death in an hour (Zosimas' inference, 20 days' walk) [S1].
- Writing in sand though she was illiterate (S6 inference) [S6].
- Lion digs grave [S1, S3 "legend", S9]; BG: "if the legend is to be believed" [S5].
- No posthumous miracles found in the sources read; cult is by the Life, relics, and liturgy.
- Status all: popular tradition, accepted by liturgical use; no church "approval" document read.
