# Facts — Saint Nicholas of Myra (checked 2026-10-07)

The miracle list and the teachings essay must use these exactly. Notes refer to biography.html.

## Names and spellings
- Saint Nicholas, bishop of Myra, born at Patara, in Lycia (southwest Turkey today). Harbor of Myra: Andriake. (notes 1, 23)
- The three officers: Nepotianus, Ursus, Eupoleonis. The prefect: Ablabius. The governor: Eustathius (he blames Eudoxius and Simonides). The judge named in the Praxis: Datianus. The emperor: Constantine (plain, no "Saint"). Plan.md and titles-pass.md: Justinian also plain. (notes 31, 37, 38, 41)
- Place names in the Byrra story: the square Leonti, the shrine of Crescentius and Dioscorus, the place Byrra. (note 34)
- Theognes: "a bishop of the Marcianists" (Andrew of Crete). (note 30)
- Relics: forty-seven men from Bari; Matthew; the priest Grimoald; the sailor Disigius; Archbishop Ursus; Abbot Elias of Saint Benedict's; the monk Mark (Greek account). (notes 66-76)
- Source names: Praxis de stratelatis; Michael the Archimandrite; Saint Symeon Metaphrastes; Jacobus de Voragine, Golden Legend (Caxton, 1483); the sermon under the name of Saint Proclus; the sermon credited to Saint Andrew of Crete; Nicephorus of Bari; the Greek account of the Translation; Hospinian in Brand 1813.

## Dates and numbers
- Died 6 December, about the middle of the fourth century. Years given: 342, 343, 345, 352. (note 59; Note on the Sources)
- Council of Nicaea, 325 (the Church remembers him there; doubted by scholars; see the Note). (note 64)
- The Praxis: written down by the end of the sixth century (Anrich 460-580; Cioffari: before the middle of the fifth century). (note 3)
- Michael's Life: between 814 and 842. Andrew's sermon: early 700s. (notes 3, 63)
- Justinian's church in Constantinople: the sixth century (Blachernae; Justinian ruled 527-565). Sinai icon: before the year 800 (seventh to eighth century). (note 3)
- Gold promised to Ablabius: 1,700 pounds (seventeen hundred pounds of gold). Silver the governor was to receive: more than 200 pounds. The silver was offered to the governor; it was not paid. (notes 38, 41)
- Famine: a hundred measures from each ship; grain for two years. (notes 24, 26)
- The officers left for Myra on 4 November and arrived on 13 April (Proclus §5). (note 57)
- Relics: Bari men reached Myra in 1087; forty-seven armed men; four monks; Myrans fled to a mountain twelve stades (about a mile and a third) away; the ships were nearly three miles from the shrine; the mourning was heard for nearly two miles. Arrival in Bari 9 May 1087. Two (Nicephorus) or three (Greek account) killed. Forty-seven healed the first night and next day. Crypt: October 1089, Pope Urban II. (notes 65-77)
- The tomb stayed at Myra "more than seven hundred years" (the Myrans say 775). (note 65)

## One approved wording for each quotation
- Psalm 143:10, from Michael §9: "Lord, teach me to do what you want, because you are my God." (note 1)
- The father's prayer, Michael §15: "Show me, merciful Master, your angel among men." (note 10)
- The father at the third gift, Michael §17: "If Christ had not stirred up your goodness, we would long ago have destroyed our lives in a shameful trade. The Lord has saved us through you." (note 14)
- The Golden Legend, the gold: "He called: 'Sir, do not run away. Let me see you and know you.' He ran harder and saw that it was Nicholas. At once he knelt and tried to kiss his feet, but the saint would not allow it. He only asked the man not to tell anyone about this as long as he lived." (note 13)
- At the church door, Michael §23: "Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of your Holiness, master." (note 18)
- To the captains, Michael §37: "Take a hundred measures out of each cargo, and I will answer for your loss to the treasury official in Constantinople." (note 24)
- To the sailors, Michael §34 and §36: "You called me, and I am here to help you." "Know yourselves, children, I beg you, and steer your hearts toward pleasing the good and compassionate Master." (notes 27, 29)
- To Theognes, Andrew (Ephesians 4:26): "Come to reconciliation, brother, before the sun sets on our anger." (note 30)
- To the governor, Praxis: "You sacrilegious shedder of blood! How dare you face me, when you have been caught in so many evil deeds?" and "Eudoxius and Simonides did not do this. Silver and gold did." (notes 37, 39)
- The officers: "Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us." (note 52) Nepotianus: "Saint Nicholas, servant of Christ, though you are far from us, bring your prayers near to us." (note 44)
- To the emperor in the dream: "Constantine, rise and free the three officers you have put in prison. They were condemned on hearsay." Then: "I am Bishop Nicholas, a sinner. I live in the chief city of Lycia." (notes 45, 46) To Ablabius: "I am Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of God. I am the chief bishop in Myra." (note 48)
- Constantine, Praxis: "I did not give you your lives. Saint Nicholas gave them, the one you called on." (note 54)
- Constantine, Proclus §5: "Go quickly. When you reach him, kiss his feet and ask him to remember me before God, too. Take him these small gifts. Tell him not to repay anger with anger, but to ask forgiveness for what I have done against God." (note 55)
- Death, Golden Legend: "Lord, into your hands I commend my spirit." (note 60)
- Andrew's phrases: "miraculous and ardent help"; "turning a hurricane into a breeze and a storm into a calm." (note 63)
- Metaphrastes: "The admirable Nicholas helped to bring this about as a member of the sacred synod." (note 64)
- Greek account, the monk Mark: "By God's will I am leaving the Roman world. Where I go, I go only as a visitor. Here I will stay forever." (note 76)
- Hospinian, our translation of fol. 153 (Brand 1813, vol. 1, p. 327, note d): the children believed that Saint Nicholas went through the towns and villages with his servants and "came in through the closed windows" and handed out the gifts. (note 79)

## Disagreements between sources
- Death year (342, 343, 345, 352). Turks (Nicephorus) or Persians (Greek account). Departure from Myra 11 April (Nicephorus) or eleven days before 1 May (Greek account). Two or three killed at Bari. Forty-seven cured (Nicephorus, Greek account) or thirty (Butler). Justinian's church: "about 430" (Butler) or the sixth century (Justinian's reign). Golden Legend: grain accounted for at Alexandria, "doubled" third bag, name without "sinner"; Michael differs on each. All shown in the Note on the Sources.
