Long before he became the patron of sailors and children, Saint Nicholas was a fourth-century bishop in the southwest of what is now Turkey. The oldest story about him is fierce. Three innocent men kneel on the ground with linen over their faces, an executioner holds his sword raised above them, and the bishop of Myra is running through his city to reach them. Yet the same man worked harder to hide his good deeds than wrongdoers work to hide their crimes. This is the life of the bishop of Myra, as the Church has remembered it for more than sixteen hundred years.
