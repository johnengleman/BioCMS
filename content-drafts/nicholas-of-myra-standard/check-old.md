# Check — Nicholas of Myra (standard mode, tier B)

Files checked: biography.html and summary.txt. Originals are saved as biography.html.before-check and summary.txt.before-check. Raw source text re-read for: Praxis (Jones), Proclus (Sewell and Eastbourne), Andrew of Crete (Olkinuora), Nicephorus (Jones), the Greek account (McGinley and Musurillo), Michael (secs 9-41), Golden Legend (Caxton), Roman Martyrology (older English), Sanidopoulos history, Cioffari, St Nicholas Center "Saint in Bari", Butler vol. 12 pp. 91-94, Wikipedia on the slap legend. The lint script shows no MUST FIX and no CHECK lines.

## Fixes

### Cards overruled the outline or the text
- Opening thesis ("a dark window in Patara"): cut "Patara". Card SC-01 says the city is not named in the dowry story. Also cut the invented "dark".
- Ch. 4 (officers): the text said Nicholas "had visited them in a dream". The dreams went to Constantine and Ablabius, not the officers. Now "the bishop who had saved them".
- Ch. 5 (famine): cut "every measure of it was counted". That is the Golden Legend's detail, not Michael's. Card SC-06 says not to combine versions.
- Ch. 3: cut "pushed through the crowd", "on the far side", "walked straight", "a guard ran", and "with all their heart". None is on the cards. Now "earnestly" (Praxis).
- Ch. 2: "young man" in the election scene cut in three places. Card SC-02 lists his age as not in the sources. "Steps" to the throne cut (card: "could hardly climb to the throne").
- Ch. 1: the father "heard the gold hit the floor" and "in the dead hours". Michael sec. 17 says he perceived the arrival because the gold fell into the house, and he overtook Nicholas at a run. Rewritten to match.
- Ch. 9: cut "They counted the days" (invented). The lark scene now follows Nicephorus.
- Ch. 10: "the pledge the sailors had shouted from the deck" is now "had made". Nothing says they shouted it.
- Ch. 3: Nepotianus told the emperor "everything from the harbor to the sword at Byrra" is now "what Nicholas had done" (Praxis).

### Quotations and modern translations
- Golden Legend dowry block (Caxton): "He only asked" is now "He only required" (Caxton: "required him not to tell"). The rest matches.
- Golden Legend wheat and death blocks: match Caxton. The death block keeps one ellipsis for "in the year 343".
- Proclus, sec. 2 (to Ablabius): "as the price for taking these men's lives" changed meaning ("through your willingness to deprive these people of life and light"). Now "because you were willing to take life and light from these men". The cite says "in a vision", because Proclus says "visions" and the Praxis says dreams.
- Proclus, secs. 4-5 (emperor): a gap of about 250 words had no ellipsis. Added "…". Restored "this short life" ("temporary life" in the source), "too" ("me, too"), "pitiful", and "fortunate" for "happy reign". "Give him these gifts" is now "Take him these small gifts".
- Proclus, sec. 4 (sword): matches the source, with "boldness" as "courage".
- Andrew of Crete: "harbor of inner peace" is now "harbor of detachment from worldly troubles". The translator's note on the Greek word says "detachment from worldly emotions and troubles", and "inner peace" added a meaning. "Wonderful and warm help" is now "miraculous and warm help" (source: "miraculous and ardent").
- Roman Martyrology: the modern text said Constantine "was far away". The source says Nicholas was at a great distance. Also "persons" was "men". Fixed. Now introduced and cited as the older English text, because the page is a hobby site and the current Latin text was not checked.
- Praxis block quotes ("Silver and gold did"; "Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us"; Michael's short phrases): checked, same meaning.
- Cites: "Praxis de stratelatis, 5th century" is now "date uncertain, about 400 to 600". "Proclus, 5th century" is now "about 440". The source page says 440, but the work is listed as spurious.

### The two glosses
- "twelve stades away, a little over a mile": the 12 stades are in Nicephorus and the Greek account. A stade is about 600 feet, so 12 stades is about 1.4 miles. Kept, as "about a mile and a third".
- "The Greeks still called their empire Roman": not on any card, and the source gives no explanation of "the Roman world". The follow-on claim ("so the words meant that he had left the East for good") was an unsupported reading. Now one plain sentence: the writer meant the Greek empire of his day, which still called itself Roman. If the owner wants no gloss at all, cut that sentence.

### Absolute and unsupported claims
- "He left no writings, and no one who knew him wrote his life": now "nothing written in his own lifetime survives".
- "The man himself had spent his life trying not to be seen": cut. Now "Other old stories show him giving in secret".
- "Beginning with strangers calling his name": the Praxis begins at Andriake. Now "in it strangers call on him by name".
- "No words at all" for Nicholas lifting the father: cut (source discussion in the story, and the Golden Legend does give words).
- "The father kept his oath as long as he lived" (twice): not in any source. Now "was to keep the secret", then "After that, the story was told".
- "All of Europe read the Golden Legend": now "many people across Europe".
- "No one who stood at his bed wrote down what he said": cut. The Golden Legend gives last words.
- Death chapter: the great age and short illness are Metaphrastes (tenth century). Now said so.
- "The first thing pilgrims came for": now "Pilgrims came for it". "A ship three miles from shore": the men were at their ships three miles from the shrine. Fixed.
- "Then the pilgrims stopped coming": invented. Now "invaders came to Lycia".
- "The ships to Palestine stopped on the coast of Lycia": the source only says pilgrims stopped at Myra. Fixed.
- "The sailors came first ... Children came last": cut as unsupported order. Butler gives a different reason for children, so the window is no longer said to be why.
- "The three bags made him the giver of gifts": softened to "lie behind his place as the giver of gifts". "At least twelve hundred years" is now "about".
- "Andrew told the famine story ... it had grown": Andrew is earlier than Michael. Now "tells it differently". "A feast that was already old" cut.
- "The older and shorter lists": the sources do not say which lists are older. Now "The shorter lists".
- "Michael knew neither story": now "tells neither story".
- Arius slap: the first version says "a certain Arian"; Arius came later. The date (1300s) comes from Wikipedia, so it is now "Scholars find the tale first in the 1300s".
- "Michael says only that the tomb ...": Michael also gives the burial and the angels. Reworded.
- Section ending in ch. 3 ("they would need both") now points to Nepotianus remembering it in the cell.
- Closing: "Neither has anyone since" replaced. "The Catholic Church still prints it" (current text unchecked) is now "the old Roman Martyrology names it". The Martyrology entry is the officers' story, so "the story of the three officers" now says that.
- Optional memorial "that each parish may choose": now "may be kept or left out".

### Thin or single-source claims
- Andrew's sermon (card SC-09 is thin; Anrich doubts it is Andrew's; its miracle part mixes in Nicholas of Sion): now "a sermon was preached at Myra ... credited to Andrew". The Note on the Sources says so.
- Young man healed at Bari (card SC-12 is thin): now "Nicephorus says" for the five years. The Note says only Nicephorus gives the five years and blindness (the Greek account gives a deaf and dumb spirit only).
- Sailors' storm story (Cioffari: fits Nicholas of Sion): Note on the Sources now says it.
- Manna: the "every 9 May, clergy" claim was not in the sources. St Nicholas Center says the rector extracts it at the May feast. The "shrine says" for 1953-57 was really an Orthodox history (Sanidopoulos). Now "Orthodox writers say". The Manuel law is now credited to Butler.
- The 47 cures: Nicephorus says "that night and on Monday", the Greek account "that night and the following morning". 9 May 1087 was a Sunday. Now "that night and the next day", in the text and the Note.
- Justinian's church: Butler's "about 430" conflicts. Added one line to the Note.
- Golden Legend sailors answer in Alexandria, and Michael's go to Constantinople. Added one line to the Note.
- Persians (Greek account) against Turks (Nicephorus): added to the Note.

### Counts in "How This Life Was Researched"
- "All read in English translations": Butler, the Catholic Encyclopedia, and Cioffari are not translations. Now "the old texts".
- "Two accounts written in the same century": only Nicephorus is of 1087, and the Greek account survives in a 13th-century manuscript. Fixed.
- "Rarely found in English" is now "Popular retellings often leave out" (notes: popular pages skip it).
- Checked and left: twenty sources (S1-S20), seven more pages (FN-1 two, FN-2 three, FN-3 two). Bribes: 200+ pounds of silver to the governor, 1,700 pounds of gold to Ablabius, both in the Praxis text.

### Checked and left as written
- Absolute words: "only son" (Michael), "first" and "all" in the narrative, "Orthodox ... only the Mother of God and John the Baptist stand ahead" (Sanidopoulos says "after the Mother of God and John the Baptist ... the most venerated"). "The tallest building in Myra" (Michael sec. 29, "excelled all other buildings in height", and Cioffari's inscriptions).
- Late legends: the Nicaea tale, the Diocletian prison, the Sion pilgrimage and storm are all marked as late or as the other Nicholas's.
- Facts-only quotations (Praxis, Nicephorus, Greek account) stay short, as the notes require.
- "Then came the day that made his name" (end of ch. 2) is a narrator line, not a source claim. Kept.

### Mechanics
- Lint: no MUST FIX. Three CHECK lines (three "The" starts twice, one stacked-phrase sentence) fixed. Now clean. Average sentence 15.1 words.
- Every changed sentence follows the straight-sentence rules (actor first, one short opening phrase, at most two commas, none over 30 words).
- summary.txt: "Every time a stranger asked his name" is now "When strangers asked his name in the old stories".
