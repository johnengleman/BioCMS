# Nicholas of Myra — scene cards, group 1 (SC-01 to SC-04)

Read in this session: S2 Michael (English, secs 1-35 read in part, secs 9-24 and 31-34 in full), S3 Praxis de stratelatis (English, Jones), S4 Golden Legend (Caxton), S7 Metaphrastes (English selections), S12 Proclus (English). I did not reach any Greek or Latin original. Plain English lines are checked against the English only.

## SC-01 — The third night (dowry gold)
- Status: full
- When and where: undated; night, at a window of a neighbor's house. Michael says the man's home was "next to Nicholas'" (S2 sec.10). The city is not named in the dowry story. The notes' "Patara" is an assumption from sec.3 (birth city); do not state it as fact. After Nicholas's parents had died (S2 sec.9; S4).
- Who was there: Nicholas, young and rich, parents dead. The neighbor, once well-born and now poor, with three daughters (S2 sec.10; S4 calls him "a nobleman").
- What happened, in order:
  1. The neighbor is so poor that he plans to put his three daughters in a brothel. No one will marry them lawfully. [S2 sec.10; S7; S4]
  2. Nicholas, who has decided to give away his wealth, learns of it. He does not speak to the man, to spare him shame and to keep his own giving quiet. [S2 sec.12; S7]
  3. At night he throws a bag of gold through the window and hurries home. [S2 sec.12]
  4. In the morning the father finds it, weeps, thanks God, and marries off the eldest. [S2 sec.13]
  5. After that wedding Nicholas throws a second, equal bag the same way. The father cannot speak, prays silently to be shown who the giver is, and marries off the second daughter. [S2 sec.14-15]
  6. The father stays awake on the following nights. Late at night Nicholas throws the third bag. [S2 sec.16]
  7. The father hears the gold fall, runs after him, overtakes him, and knows him. He falls face down at his feet and calls him savior of himself and his three daughters, "after God". [S2 sec.17]
  8. Nicholas lifts him up, binds him by oath to tell no one while he lives, and lets him go. [S2 sec.17]
- Details: a "bag" of gold (S2); "a mass of gold wrapped in a cloth" (S4); the bag is "tied" (S7). S7 says the father tested the gold with his fingertips for fear it was false, and that Nicholas could not stay hidden because of his family's standing. S7 puts the third visit "at the third hour in the dead of night" (paraphrase; S7 is facts-only).
- Words spoken: Nicholas speaks no words in S2 or S7 when he lifts the father. S4 gives the father's cry as he chases: "Sir, flee not away so but that I may see and know thee." Plain: "Sir, do not run from me. Let me see you and know who you are." [S4, Caxton]. S2 sec.17 has the father say that if Christ had not stirred Nicholas's goodness, "we would have long ago destroyed our own lives" by a shameful trade (paraphrase; S2 translation rights-limited).
- Inner life: S2 sec.9 gives Nicholas's prayer after his parents die: "Teach me, Lord, to do Your will, because You are my God" (Psalm 143.10, as quoted by Michael). Plain: "Lord, teach me to do what you want, because you are my God." S7 says he worked harder to hide his good acts than evil men work to hide theirs (paraphrase).
- Testimony (Golden Legend, 1275, Caxton English 1483; public-domain; speaker: the compiler; S4 "The Gift of Gold for the Three Daughters"):
  > "And a little while after this holy servant of God threw in another mass of gold, which the man found, and thanked God, and purposed to wake, for to know him that so had aided him in his poverty. And after a few days Nicholas doubled the mass of gold, and cast it into the house of this man. He awoke by the sound of the gold, and followed Nicholas, which fled from him, and he said to him: Sir, flee not away so but that I may see and know thee. Then he ran after him more hastily, and knew that it was Nicholas; and anon he kneeled down, and would have kissed his feet, but the holy man would not, but required him not to tell nor discover this thing as long as he lived."
  - plain: "A little later Nicholas threw in a second bag of gold. The man found it, thanked God, and decided to stay awake to learn who had helped him in his poverty. After a few days Nicholas made the gift double and threw it into the man's house. The man woke at the sound of the gold and followed Nicholas, who ran from him. He called: 'Sir, do not run away. Let me see you and know you.' He ran harder and saw that it was Nicholas. At once he knelt and tried to kiss his feet, but the saint would not allow it. He only asked the man not to tell anyone about this as long as he lived."
- What changed: two daughters are married, the third is saved, and the father holds a secret that will not stay kept.
- Not in the sources: what Nicholas says when he lifts the father; the sisters' names or ages; the weight of the gold; the weather; the house layout beyond "the window"; the city. Sources differ on the third gift (S4 says it was doubled; S2 says "similar").
- Sources: S2, S4, S7 (keys as in notes.md).

## SC-02 — Dawn at the church door (election)
- Status: full
- When and where: the night before the see of Myra is filled, then "the early hours of dawn" (S2 sec.23), at the entrance of the church of Myra. S7: "about the hour of Matins", in the vestibule.
- Who was there: bishops of the nearby cities and leading clergy of Myra's church (S2 sec.21); the one bishop who received the revelation (not named in any source); Nicholas; afterward the crowds of Myra (S2 sec.23-24). The earlier bishop of Myra has just died.
- What happened, in order:
  1. The bishop of Myra dies. The nearby bishops and leading clergy seek a worthy successor. [S2 sec.21]
  2. One advises the group to pray. The Lord shows one of them what to do. [S2 sec.22]
  3. The man tells the others, who stay praying inside. He goes to the entrance. [S2 sec.23]
  4. At dawn Nicholas comes, alone, to enter the church. The bishop takes hold of him and asks his name. [S2 sec.23]
  5. Nicholas answers in an "unaffected and mild voice". The bishop is moved by his humility. [S2 sec.23]
  6. The bishop leads him to the other bishops. They praise God, bring him into the middle of the church, and show him to the crowds. [S2 sec.23]
  7. The people accept it and set him on the bishop's throne. [S2 sec.24]
- Details: S4 says the bishop "kept the doors" and the bishops were warned to fast and pray. S7 says the news spread and crowds came; the bishops announced him "not by human but by divine determination" (paraphrase).
- Words spoken: Voice to the bishop, exact (S2 sec.22): "Go to the house of God at night and stand at the entrance, and whoever comes to enter the church quietly before anyone else, take this man and appoint him to the office of bishop. His name is Nicholas." Plain: "Go to the church at night and stand at the door. Take the first person who comes in quietly, and make him bishop. His name is Nicholas."
  - Bishop: "How are you called, child?" Nicholas: "Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of your Holiness, O master." Plain: "I am Nicholas, a sinner, and your servant, master." [S2 sec.23]
  - Bishop: "Come with me, child, because I have something to show you." Plain: "Come with me, my child. I have something to show you." [S2 sec.23]
  - S4 differs: Nicholas says only "I have to name Nicholas" ("My name is Nicholas"), and the bishop answers "Nicholas, servant and friend of God, for your holiness ye shall be bishop of this place." The word "sinner" is in S2 and S7 only.
- Inner life: his reaction to the news is not in S2. S7 says he was "slow and hesitant" and could hardly climb to the throne (paraphrase). S4 says he refused strongly.
- Testimony (Golden Legend; public-domain; S4 "St. Nicholas is Chosen Bishop of Myra"):
  > "And when he had warned all for to be in fastings and in prayers, this bishop heard that night a voice which said to him that, at the hour of matins, he should take heed to the doors of the church, and him that should come first to the church, and have the name of Nicholas they should sacre him bishop. ... And the bishop took him when he was come and demanded of him his name. And he, which was simple as a dove, inclined his head, and said: I have to name Nicholas."
  - plain: "He told everyone to fast and pray. That night the bishop heard a voice. It told him to watch the church doors at the hour of matins, and to make bishop the first man to come in, whose name would be Nicholas. ... When Nicholas came, the bishop took hold of him and asked his name. Nicholas, humble as a dove, bowed his head and said, 'My name is Nicholas.'"
- What changed: a young layman becomes bishop of Myra.
- Not in the sources: that the bishops prayed "all night" as one act (S2 says only "at night" and that they kept praying); Nicholas's age; weather; a lamp or torch; his reply to the crowd.
- Sources: S2, S4, S7.

## SC-03 — The sword at Byrra
- Status: full
- When and where: in the time of Constantine, undated. The port Andriake, three miles from Myra; then a plaza called Leonti; the martyrium of Crescentius and Dioscorus; the place of beheading called Byrra (S3).
- Who was there: Nicholas; the three condemned men (not named in S3, S12, or S2); the executioner; a great crowd; the officers Nepotianus, Ursus, and Eupoleonis. They were sent by Constantine to put down unrest in "Frigia" (S3 note 2: not identified). A "Judge Datianus" is named by the townspeople as the one who sentenced the men (S3). The same source later calls the governor Eustathius (see SC-04).
- What happened, in order:
  1. The officers' ship waits at Andriake with no wind. Some soldiers go to find food. Looters pose as soldiers and are arrested, and a crowd riots in the plaza Placomatus. [S3]
  2. Nicholas hears the noise, walks down, and asks what is wrong. The officers greet him. He invites them to Myra for his blessing and a meal. [S3]
  3. Townspeople tell him that three innocent men have been sentenced to beheading and the city is in turmoil because he is absent. He is "downcast". [S3]
  4. He takes the officers and crosses the city. He asks people in the Leonti plaza whether the men are alive, and is sent toward Byrra. [S3]
  5. Running, he finds a great crowd and the executioner with his sword raised. The three have linen over their faces, hands tied behind them, knees bent, heads bowed. [S3]
  6. He takes the sword and throws it to the ground, loosens their chains, and takes them with him into the city. [S3]
- Details: linen cloths over faces; hands tied behind; the narrator quotes Proverbs 28:1, "The righteous are bold as a lion" (S3). S2 sec.31 says the sentence was given "because of a gift of gold" and Nicholas "suddenly appeared", shaking the sword to the ground.
- Words spoken: Nicholas to the officers at Andriake, S3 (paraphrase): he asks where they come from, on whose business, and why. They answer that they are peaceful and ask his prayers. S3 gives no words from the scene at Byrra itself.
- Testimony (Proclus, Encomium, sec.4; public-domain; speakers: the officers telling the emperor, as Proclus writes it; S12):
  > "This saint, always in possession of the fear of God and boldness, with beautiful and swift-running feet gave life, with God's help, to those who were going to die at the fatal hour, and, taking control of the two-edged sword that was held in the hand of the man who was about to cut off [their heads], he cast it away, and unbinding the captives he released them and restored them to their own homes."
  - plain: "This saint was always full of the fear of God and of courage. He ran to the men who were about to die, and with God's help he saved them. He took the double-edged sword from the man who was about to behead them and threw it away. He untied the prisoners and sent them home."
- What changed: three men live; Nicholas now carries the anger that drives SC-04; the officers have seen it all.
- Not in the sources: the crowd's reaction; the names of the three men; the officers' words at Byrra; the time of day or weather. S3 says the executioner was "anticipating" Nicholas's coming; that detail is unexplained.
- Sources: S3, S2, S12.

## SC-04 — In the governor's hall
- Status: full (testimony limited: no public-domain text tells the hall scene)
- When and where: the same day, in the Pretorium of Myra, before Eustathius, called the Praeses (governor) (S3).
- Who was there: Nicholas; Eustathius; a guard; the three freed men; the officers of the army (S3).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Nicholas walks to the Pretorium, thrusts open the door, and enters. [S3]
  2. A guard tells the governor what has happened. Eustathius walks up to honor him. Nicholas turns away. [S3]
  3. Nicholas calls him a bloodshedder and a sacrilegious man, says he will not spare him, and says he will tell Constantine how many and how grave his sins are and how he runs his province (paraphrase). [S3]
  4. The governor falls to his knees and begs. He says that he is not the guilty one, but the heads of state Eudoxius and Simonides. [S3]
  5. Nicholas answers with the line below. He had learned that the governor was to receive more than two hundred pounds of silver for the executions. [S3]
  6. The officers speak earnestly for the governor, and Nicholas pardons him once the charges against the three men are cleared. [S3]
  7. At a meal with Nicholas the officers ask his prayers and sail away. [S3]
- Details: a door pushed open, the governor's kneeling. S2 sec.31 says Nicholas gave "fitting remarks of outrage" to the "juror blinded by gold" and so freed them.
- Words spoken: Nicholas, exact (S3, Jones, one short quotation): "It is not Eudoxius and Simonides who did this, but silver and gold." Plain: "Eudoxius and Simonides did not do this. Money did." Eustathius's excuse and plea are in S3 as given at step 4; its exact words are not copied here (facts-only).
- Testimony: none copyable. S3 is facts-only; the passage is S3 "Citizens of Myra", paragraphs from "Walking down to the Pretorium" to "granted him pardon".
- What changed: the governor is stopped and forgiven; the officers leave knowing Nicholas's name.
- Not in the sources: what the officers said to win the pardon (S3 says only that they "earnestly spoke"); who the guard was; the size of the room. The outline's "1,700 pounds of gold" is wrong for this scene. In S3 it is the sum promised to the prefect Ablabius in Constantinople, a later scene (SC-05). The governor's sum in S3 is "more than two hundred pounds of silver". S2 sec.31 gives no sum.
- Sources: S3, S2.
