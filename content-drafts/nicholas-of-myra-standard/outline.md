# Saint Nicholas of Myra — scene outline (tier A, v2, 2026-10-06)

Old outline: `outline-v1.md`. Facts: notes.md, notes-teachings.md (T-S keys), scenes-1/2/3.md, the approved biography.html, and the check logs. No `sources/` folder exists.

## 1. The driving question

When his parents died, the young heir took God for his father and asked to give up his life and all he owned to God [S2 sec.9]. He wanted to do it unseen, a servant who called himself "a sinner" [S2 sec.12, 23; S7]. In the way stood his family's name, a city that made him its bishop, a governor and a prefect who sold lives for silver and gold, and his own anger [S7; S3]. Grace finished what he began: he laid down his life for three condemned men, as his Master did [S2 sec.31], and God made the hidden servant's name the one strangers call on. "It is not I who give you your life, but the one you called on" [S3; S12 sec.5].

Why it was beautiful to him: God was now his only father, and the money became treasure in heaven [S2 sec.9]. His prayer was a psalm: "Lord, teach me to do what you want, because you are my God" [S2 sec.9, Ps 143:10]. The reader hears it at the first decision: the opening and ch. 1.

Famous because: the Orthodox Church honors him after only the Mother of God and Saint John the Baptist [S5]; patron of sailors, travelers, and children, and root of the December gift-giver [S6; S11; T-S7].

Set apart: a bishop who ran to the place of execution and stood between bought justice and the sword [S3]. Where rich men gave in public, he gave at night and bound the man he helped to silence [S2 sec.12, 17; S7].

## 2. Portrait

He moves fast. Told that three men will die, he is "downcast", and then he runs [S3]. He takes the sword from the executioner's hand and throws it down. In the governor's hall he turns from the honor offered him and calls the governor a shedder of blood [S3]. The anger is real, and so is the mercy: when the officers plead, he pardons the same man [S3]. He scolds the sailors he has just saved for their greed and impure lives [S2 sec.36].

The other side is hiddenness. He works harder to hide his good deeds than evil men work to hide their crimes [S7]. He comes to church plainly dressed, "like one of the many clergy" [S2 sec.35]. Chosen bishop, he hangs back [S7]. The notes show no humor.

Life with God: he prays in psalms, from his first decision to his last breath ("Into your hands") [S2 sec.9; S4]. Whenever a stranger asks his name, he answers: a sinner [S2 sec.23; S3].

## 3. The opening

Hook: the saint's own words at the first decision. It raises the question (can a rich man give everything and stay hidden?) and spends no scene the body tells. Ch. 1 begins after the prayer, with the neighbor.

> On the coast of Lycia, in the town of Patara, a rich couple died and left everything to their only son. The young man took God for his father then. He asked God to let him give up his life and all he owned, and he prayed in the words of a psalm: "Lord, teach me to do what you want, because you are my God." He meant to give it away where no one would see, and he wanted no name but "a sinner." The people he helped would not let him stay hidden.

[S2 sec.3-4, 9, 12, 23; S7]

Thesis: Saint Nicholas was bishop of Myra in the fourth century [S6]. His oldest story was written down within a century or two of his death [S11]. Emperor Justinian built him a church in Constantinople in the sixth century, and his face was painted at Sinai and in Rome before 800 [S5; S10]. Two things set him apart: a bishop's courage on behalf of the condemned, and a giver's wish to stay hidden [S3; S2 sec.12]. Ch. 5, 6, and 10 answer it.

The thesis and summary.txt must not mention the prison, the dreams, the emperor, or "three times a sinner". They belong to the climax.

Ending: feasts, Martyrology, and manna first and short. Closing image: on the eve of his feast, parents in many lands give their children presents in secret and tell them the gifts come from Saint Nicholas [T-S7, Hospinian via Seal; S11]. One quiet sentence after it may return to the father at the window. No lesson paragraph.

## 5. The scenes

Turning points, told slowly from inside him: TP1 SC-01, TP2 SC-02, TP3 SC-05, TP4 SC-08 (climax), TP5 SC-10.

Resistance by period (compress the rest of each period): youth, a father who keeps watch (SC-01); election, the honor (SC-02); early years, captains who refuse and sailors who sin (SC-03, SC-04); Constantine's reign, a bought governor and his own anger, then a prefect bought far away (SC-05 to SC-08); old age, illness (SC-10); after death, invaders, theft, an armed quarrel (SC-11 to SC-13). The sources give no long trial, so no "one concrete day".

Relationships and their ends: the father, the oath (SC-01) and the story told after his life (closing). The bishops who chose him; bishops and clergy carry his body by torchlight (SC-10; do not say the same men). The people of Myra: they acclaim him (SC-02), eat his grain (SC-03), run to him for the condemned (SC-05), and lament on the shore in 1087 (SC-11). Nepotianus and the officers: harbor, prison, return (SC-05, SC-07, SC-09). Eustathius: anger, then pardon (SC-06). Constantine: dream, gifts, a plea for prayer (SC-08, SC-09).

Losses at their dates: his parents, in youth (opening, one beat: God as his father) [S2 sec.9]; his own illness at a great age (SC-10) [S7]; Myra's loss of his body, April 1087 (SC-11).

### SC-01 — The third night (a neighbor's house, after his parents' death) — TP1
- What happens: a ruined neighbor plans to put his three daughters into shame; Nicholas throws gold through the window on three nights; the father keeps watch, runs after him, falls at his feet; Nicholas lifts him and binds him to silence.
- Why: the first decision acted out; the root of the December gift.
- Have: card SC-01 [S2 sec.9-17; S7; S4]. Inner: no visit, to spare the man shame and keep the gift hidden [S2 sec.12; S7]; his family's standing would not let him hide [S7].
- Need: Michael's words on God as his father and on giving up his life and goods, own English [S2 sec.9].
- Testimony: Golden Legend chase [S4], plain English on the card.

### SC-02 — Dawn at the church door (Myra) — TP2
- What happens: the bishops pray; one hears a voice; at dawn the first man to come in alone answers, "Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of your Holiness, master"; the people set him on the throne.
- Why: life with God; the hidden man meets an honor.
- Have: card SC-02 [S2 sec.21-24; S7; S4]; he hangs back [S7]; acclamation [S10].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-03 — Grain ships at Andriake (Myra's harbor)
- What happens: famine; he asks each Alexandrian ship for a hundred measures; the captains refuse, since the grain is the state's; he answers for the loss himself; the cargo arrives full; Myra eats for two years.
- Why: he puts himself in the debt so his people live.
- Have: card SC-06 [S2 sec.37-39]. Michael's version only; the Golden Legend dialogue may stand as a block quote.
- Need: nothing.

### SC-04 — Sailors in the church (at sea, then Myra)
- What happens: sailors in a storm call his name; "You called me, and I am here to help you"; ashore they find him plainly dressed among the clergy; he tells them to amend their lives.
- Why: life with God; his hiddenness; a hard word to grateful men.
- Have: card SC-07 [S2 sec.34-36].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-05 — The sword at Byrra (Myra) — TP3
- What happens: a riot at Andriake brings him to the officers' ship; townspeople tell him three innocent men will die; he runs to Byrra, takes the raised sword, throws it down, frees them.
- Why: the desire becomes courage; he lays down his life for three men, imitating his Master [S2 sec.31].
- Have: card SC-03 [S3; S12 sec.4]; "downcast" [S3]; Proverbs 28:1 [S3].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Proclus sec.4 [S12].

### SC-06 — In the governor's hall (Myra)
- What happens: he thrusts the door open, turns from the governor's honor, threatens to tell the emperor; the governor blames two officials; "Eudoxius and Simonides did not do this. Silver and gold did"; the officers plead; he pardons.
- Why: the fault and the mercy in one scene.
- Have: card SC-04 [S3]; the governor "was to receive" more than 200 pounds of silver [S3].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-07 — The prison (Constantinople)
- What happens: an envious commander promises Ablabius 1,700 pounds of gold; the officers are condemned to die that night; the warden weeps; Nepotianus remembers Myra and prays with tears.
- Why: the build to the climax.
- Have: card SC-05 [S3].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-08 — Two dreams and the emperor's court — TP4, CLIMAX
- What happens: that night he stands before Constantine and before Ablabius and names himself a sinner and servant of God; at dawn the dreams match; the officers stand silent at court; "Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us"; the emperor frees them: "It is not I who give you your life."
- Why: the answer to the driving question; the hidden servant known by name, still a sinner in his own word.
- Have: card SC-05 [S3; S12 sec.2-5]; God's grace to help those far away who call on him [S2 sec.32-33]. Follow the Praxis order.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Proclus sec.4-5 [S12]. The fullest scene of the life.

### SC-09 — The officers come back to Myra (short)
- What happens: they bring the emperor's gifts, cut their hair before him, and give alms with tears for many years.
- Why: closes the officers and Constantine.
- Have: [S3]; Constantine asks to be remembered before God [S12 sec.5].
- Need: Fact need 1.

### SC-10 — His death (Myra, 6 December, mid-4th century) — TP5
- What happens: a great age, a short illness; he asks God for his angels and sees them; Psalm 30 to "Into your hands"; he dies amid the thanksgivings; bishops and clergy carry him by torchlight; perfume rises from the tomb.
- Why: life with God; the psalm-prayer of the opening ends here.
- Have: card SC-08 [S4; S7; S2 sec.41]. No last words to his people exist.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Golden Legend death passage [S4].

### SC-11 — The tomb is broken (Myra, April 1087)
- What happens: forty-seven armed men from Bari; four monks; a glass vial falls and does not break; Matthew draws his sword; a gentler monk tells of the saint's warning; the tomb is full of holy oil; the scent reaches the ships; the monks cry, "Our lord and dear father, why do you leave us?"
- Why: the end of Myra's bond with its father.
- Have: card SC-10 [S9; S8].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-12 — Perdikca (on the voyage, short)
- What happens: winds drive them back to Patara; five sailors confess on the Gospels to taking pieces and return them; Disigius dreams: "In twenty days we will all arrive together at the city of Bari."
- Why: a hard part, theft among the rescuers; his birthplace seen once more.
- Have: card SC-11 [S9].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-13 — Bari, 9 May 1087
- What happens: the city runs to the harbor; the sailors' pledge; an armed quarrel with Archbishop Ursus (two or three dead); the body at Saint Benedict's altar, then Saint Eustratius's; forty-seven healed that night and the next day; the monk Mark: "Where I go, I go only as a visitor. Here I will stay forever."
- Why: the real case after death, with place, year, and names.
- Have: card SC-12 [S9; S8 sec.14-21]; Urban II, October 1089 [FN-2].
- Need: nothing.

- **What he changed:** 6 December in both Churches, Catholic optional memorial [S5; FN-1]; 9 May, Kiev within a few years of 1087 [FN-3], Saint Nikodemos's service [S18]; 29 July, restored 2004 [S17] — ch. 10. ## 9. Accuracy notes

- Death: 6 December, "about the middle of the fourth century". Birth "around 270" is tradition [S5].
- Dowry: the city is not named; not Patara. No age at the election. The father's silence is his oath, not a recorded fact.
- Governor Eustathius "was to receive" more than 200 pounds of silver: offered, not paid. The 1,700 pounds of gold went to Ablabius [S3].
- Justinian's church: sixth century, Blachernae [S5]; never "about 430". The Blachernae church of about 450 is the Mother of God's.
- Famine: Michael measures at Constantinople, the Golden Legend at Alexandria; never merge with Andrew's "three gold coins".
- Nicaea and the slap: later tradition, told with trust in ch. 8; doubts go in the Note [S7; S16; S10].
- 1087: "April 1087"; the vial falls before Matthew's sword; Turks [S9]; forty-seven cured "that night and the next day".
- Bari: Saint Benedict's altar, then Saint Eustratius's in the old governor's court; not "the governor's hall".
- Rights: Michael, Praxis, Nicephorus, and the Greek account allow short quotes only, in our own English. Long passages: S4, S12, S13, S1.
- Name a teller inside the story only where versions differ (chase, famine, death).
