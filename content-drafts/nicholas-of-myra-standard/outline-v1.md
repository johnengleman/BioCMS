# Nicholas of Myra — scene outline (tier B)

## 1. The driving question

Nicholas wanted to do good where no one could see it, and each time he was named he called himself "a sinner" [S2 sec.23; S3]. The people he saved would not keep quiet, and the powerful men he faced wanted his work stopped. He kept to secret giving and to the plain name of sinner, and God made his name the one the helpless call on: "It is not I who have granted you your life, but he whom you called on, Saint Nicholas" [S3, own English].

Famous because: after the Mother of God and John the Baptist he is one of the most venerated saints in the Orthodox Church [S5], the patron of sailors, travelers, and children [S6], and the distant source of the December gift-giver [S5, S11].

Set apart: a fourth-century bishop who used his office against the bribes of governors and won the lives of condemned men [S3; S10]. In an age that honored public generosity, the oldest stories show him giving at night and asking for silence [S2 sec.17].

## 2. Portrait

The stories show a man who moves fast. He is "now running" to the execution field [S3], and he pulls the sword from the executioner's hand. He pushes into the governor's hall, refuses the honor offered him, and names the real culprits: "silver and gold" [S3]. He has a temper. He threatens the governor with the emperor, and a modern scholar calls the quick-tempered side "well in tone" with his character [S10]. Then he forgives the same governor when the officers ask him to [S3].

The other side is secrecy. He throws gold through a window at night and makes the father swear silence [S2 sec.17]. In his own church he dresses "like one of the many clergy," so that sailors he saved do not know him at first [S2 sec.35]. He scolds those he rescues: their greed and lust invite God's correction [S2 sec.36]. An old account remembers him as "venerable and angelic in appearance" [S2 sec.40]. The notes show no humor.

His life with God shows in a name and a prayer. Three times he names himself "a sinner, servant of God" [S2 sec.23; S3]. The officers' prayer, "Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us" [S3], shows how others saw his bond with God. At death he chanted thanksgivings [S7] and said Psalm 30 to "into your hands" [S4].

## 3. The opening

Hook: scene at the edge. The oldest story about Nicholas begins with strangers calling his name.

Draft:

> Three army officers sat in a prison in Constantinople, falsely accused and facing death. Once, in the city of Myra, they had watched a bishop run to an execution field and take the sword out of the executioner's hand. Now they prayed to that bishop's God: "Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us." This is the oldest story anyone tells about Nicholas. It begins with strangers saying his name, and the man himself had tried hard never to be seen.

Thesis (after the opening): The Catholic Encyclopedia says that almost nothing is certain about him except that he was bishop of Myra in the fourth century [S6]. Yet within a century or two of his death his story was being written down [S11], an emperor built him a church in Constantinople [S5], and pictures of him reached Sinai and Rome [S10]. What set him apart was a bishop's courage for the condemned and a giver's wish to stay hidden [S3; S2]. Chapters 11 and 12 answer it.

Writer's rule for a life of legend: anchor each chapter in its strongest story. Tell late stories as stories, with one plain attribution ("the oldest Life, written about five hundred years later, tells…"). Put the sources questions in "A Note on the Sources".

## 4. Chapters

1. **Nicholas Secretly Pays Three Sisters' Dowries** — SC-01. Ends: the father swears silence, a promise that will not hold.
2. **Nicholas Becomes Bishop of Myra** — SC-02. Bridge: the temple of Artemis [S2 sec.28-29; S10]. Ends: a bribed governor is about to execute three innocent men.
3. **Nicholas Stops an Execution at Myra** — SC-03, SC-04. Ends: three army officers who saw it leave for Constantinople, where they will need him.
4. **Three Officers Pray to Nicholas from Prison** — SC-05. Ends: the freed officers owe their lives to a name, and the name begins to travel.
5. **Nicholas Feeds Myra in a Famine** — SC-06. Ends: the sailors of the grain ships carry his name across the sea.
6. **Sailors Call on Nicholas in a Storm** — SC-07. Ends: still in plain clothes among his clergy, he comes to his last illness.
7. **The Death of Nicholas at Myra** — SC-08. Ends: a sweet smell rises from his tomb, and pilgrims come.
8. **Pilgrims Come to His Tomb at Myra** — SC-09. Bridge: Justinian's church, icons, the first Life, the stories that grew (Nicaea, Nicholas of Sion). Ends: invaders take Lycia and the monks flee to the mountains [S5; S8].
9. **Sailors from Italy Take His Bones** — SC-10, SC-11. Ends: the ship sails home on a sailor's dream of twenty days.
10. **The Bones of Nicholas Reach Italy** — SC-12. Ends: the sick of the city come that first night.
11. **How Nicholas Changed the Church** — no scene; feasts, services, patronages.
12. **Why the Whole World Knows Nicholas** — close the loop to the window and the prison.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — The third night (Patara, after his parents' death)
- What happens: A rich young man throws gold through a window on three nights; the father waits up, catches him, falls at his feet; Nicholas lifts him and binds him to silence.
- Why it is here: the secret giver, the root of the driving question and of the December gift.
- Have: Michael sec.10-18 [S2]; father calls him savior "after God" [S2 sec.17]; old recensions differ in the number of children [S10].
- Need: Michael's setting (city, house) and the father's need; Nicholas's exact words when he lifts the father, own English; any Golden Legend detail [S4].
- Testimony: Golden Legend dowry passage, public domain [S4].
- Where to look: S2 sec.10-18; S4 "Three Gold Balls"; S7 dowry story.

### SC-02 — Dawn at the church door (Myra)
- What happens: The bishops pray all night; one dreams that the first man to enter is chosen; Nicholas walks in and says, "Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of your Holiness."
- Why it is here: life with God; the first of three times he calls himself a sinner.
- Have: S2 sec.21-24; S7; acclamation of a layman likely true [S10].
- Need: the dream's exact wording; Nicholas's reaction when told he is chosen.
- Where to look: S2 sec.21-24; S7.

### SC-03 — The sword at Byrra (outside Myra)
- What happens: Three men kneel with linen over their faces; the executioner's sword is raised; Nicholas runs through the crowd, takes the sword, throws it down, frees them.
- Why it is here: the best-attested deed [S11]; his courage for the condemned.
- Have: S3; Roman Martyrology cites this story alone [S3 note 1].
- Need: why the three officers were at Myra; the names of the three condemned, if given; the crowd's reaction.
- Testimony: Proclus encomium, public domain [S12].
- Where to look: S3; S12.

### SC-04 — In the governor's hall (Myra)
- What happens: Nicholas pushes open the door, refuses the governor's honor, threatens him with the emperor, names the bribe; then forgives him at the officers' request.
- Why it is here: the hard part, his temper, and his mercy.
- Have: "It is not Eudoxius and Simonides who did this, but silver and gold" [S3]; bribe of 200 pounds of silver and 1,700 pounds of gold [S3]; temper [S10].
- Need: Eustathius's excuse and plea, own English; what the officers said to win his pardon.
- Where to look: S3; S12.

### SC-05 — The prison and the dreams (Constantinople)
- What happens: The officers, framed, pray; Nicholas appears in dreams to Constantine and to the prefect Ablabius; the emperor asks the officers what magic they use, then frees them.
- Why it is here: the first people to call on his name; the opening's scene told in full.
- Have: prayer and dream words [S3]; "How is it that you have sent a certain Nicholas to punish me?" [S12 sec.4]; "It is not I who have granted you your life" [S3]; Nepotianus and Ablabius are known men [S10].
- Need: the charge and the sentence; the emperor's gifts and message to Myra; Ablabius's dream words.
- Testimony: Proclus sec.3-5, public domain [S12].
- Where to look: S3; S12.

### SC-06 — Grain ships at Andriake (Myra's harbor)
- What happens: In famine, Nicholas asks each Alexandrian grain ship for 100 measures and promises them no loss; the grain arrives in Constantinople in full; Myra eats for two years.
- Why it is here: care for his city; trust in God's provision.
- Have: S2 sec.37-39; Andrew of Crete, "three gold coins" [S13].
- Need: the captains' objection and his words, own English.
- Where to look: S2 sec.37-39; S13.

### SC-07 — Sailors in the church (at sea, then Myra)
- What happens: Sailors in a storm cry to him; "Behold, you have called me, and I am here to help you"; later they find him among the clergy and know him; he rebukes their sins.
- Why it is here: life with God; the root of the sea patronage; his scolding.
- Have: S2 sec.34-36; better fits Nicholas of Sion [S10].
- Need: his rebuke, own English.
- Where to look: S2 sec.34-36.

### SC-08 — His death (Myra, 6 December, mid-4th century)
- What happens: A short illness; he prays for angels, sees them, chants thanksgivings, says Psalm 30 to "into your hands"; bishops carry him by torchlight; perfume and oil at the tomb.
- Why it is here: the required death scene; life with God.
- Have: S7; S4 death passage; S2 sec.41.
- Need: Metaphrastes' exact wording of the last moments; any last words to his people.
- Testimony: Golden Legend death passage, public domain [S4].
- Where to look: S7; S4.

### SC-09 — A sermon at the tomb (Myra, early 8th century)
- What happens: Andrew of Crete preaches at the tomb; praises the man "turning a hurricane into a breeze."
- Why it is here: the living cult before Bari.
- Have: S13; delivered at the tomb [S5].
- Need: what Andrew says of pilgrims or the oil at the tomb.
- Where to look: S13; S5.

### SC-10 — Breaking the tomb (Myra, April 1087)
- What happens: 47 armed men from Bari reach the shrine; four monks guard it; Matthew draws his sword; a vial of oil falls and does not break; Matthew smashes the lid; the coffin is full of liquid; the smell reaches the ships three miles off.
- Why it is here: the best-documented event of the whole story.
- Have: S9; S8; S4 "forty-seven knights".
- Need: the monks' words and the vision they report; the monks' lament.
- Where to look: S9 "Confrontation and a Second Vision"; S8.

### SC-11 — Becalmed at Perdikca (on the voyage)
- What happens: The ship will not move; five sailors confess on the Gospels that they took fragments, and return them; a sailor, Disigius, dreams of arrival in twenty days.
- Why it is here: a hard part, theft among the rescuers, told by an eyewitness.
- Have: S9.
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: S9.

### SC-12 — Bari, 9 May 1087 (the harbor and the city)
- What happens: The crowd meets the ship; the sailors quarrel with Archbishop Ursus; the relics go to the governor's hall; 47 are cured that first night; a young man ill for five years is healed.
- Why it is here: the real case after death, in a named place and year.
- Have: S8 sec.16-20; S9; Butler gives 30 cured [S1 p.94].
- Need: the young man's healing, in detail, own English; the sailors' pledge.
- Where to look: S9; S8 sec.16-20.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings, sermons, and works:** none survive. His works are deeds; the Trinity speech is a formula [notes §4]. Chapter 11 says so in one sentence.
- **What he changed in the Church:**
  - Feasts: 6 December and 9 May in both Churches [S5, S17] — ch. 11.
  - Russian Church: 29 July, revived 2004 under Patriarch Alexis II [S17] — ch. 11.
  - St Nikodemos of Mount Athos wrote a service for the Translation [S18] — ch. 11.
  - The Bari basilica, relics under the altar by Pope Urban II [S5] — ch. 10.
  - The manna collected at Bari each 9 May [S5] — ch. 12.
- **Why he is famous:**
  - Praxis, 5th-6th c., quoted by Eustratios after 583 [S11] — ch. 4, 8.
  - Justinian's church at Blachernae [S5] — ch. 8.
  - Sinai icon, 7th-8th c.; Santa Maria Antiqua fresco, 8th c. [S10] — ch. 8.
  - Michael's Life, 814-842 [S2] — ch. 8.
  - Myra as a stop for Holy Land pilgrims; sailors' and travelers' patron [S5] — ch. 6, 12.
  - Patronages: Greece, Russia, Naples, Lorraine, Moscow; sailors, merchants, bakers, children [S6] — ch. 12.
  - December gift-giver from the dowry story, "at some remove" [S11; S5] — ch. 12.
- **Path to sainthood:** veneration from burial [S2 sec.41]; no formal process; feast in both Churches [S5]; Roman Martyrology entry [S3 note 1] — ch. 8, 11.

**Fact needs:**
1. Roman Martyrology wording and Roman calendar rank for 6 December — S3 note 1; S6.
2. Date Pope Urban II placed the relics in the basilica — S5.
3. When the Russian Church took up the 9 May feast — S5.

## 7. Coverage check

1. Famous as a saint: opening, ch. 11, 12.
2. Whole life: ch. 1-7; youth has only the dowry story (notes lack more).
3. Hardships: ch. 3, 4, 5. The notes give no personal suffering beyond late legend; cost shows in risk and famine.
4. Famous deeds: ch. 1, 3, 4, 5, 6.
5. Writings: none exist; ch. 11 says so.
6. Influence on the Church: ch. 8, 10, 11.
7. Connection: ch. 1 (secret help), ch. 5 (famine), ch. 12 (gifts, sailors, children).
8. Inspiration: ch. 1, 3-4 (courage and forgiveness), ch. 7.
9. Distinctive: ch. 3, 4 (a bishop against bribes), ch. 1 (hidden giving).

## 8. What we leave out

- Infancy fasting on Wednesdays and Fridays: a monastic commonplace [S10].
- Slap of Arius at Nicaea and the jail vision: first seen in the 14th century [S15; S16]; one sentence in ch. 8 as late legend.
- Prison under Diocletian: first seen c.900; Butler doubts it [S1 p.92; S11].
- Pilgrimage, storm calming, Ammonios: Nicholas of Sion [S11; S14].
- Praxis de tributo: likely Nicholas of Sion [S10].
- Demon's oil flask: belongs in the miracles section.
- Parents' names Theophanes and Nonna: 17th c. [S17].
- Venetian voyage of 1100: tertiary source only [S15].
- Poveromo carved bone: family tradition.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Death year: 342, 343, 345, or 352 [S1, S4, S6, S5]. Write "about the middle of the fourth century".
- Birth "about 270" is tradition [S5].
- Nicaea: disputed. Not in the oldest short lists [S6]; on Theodore the Lector's list (c.515), which Anrich calls an interpolation and Cioffari authentic [S10; S11]. Michael does not mention Nicaea [S2].
- Justinian's church: Butler "about 430" conflicts with Justinian's reign 527-565 [S1 p.91; S5]. Use "Emperor Justinian".
- Cured at Bari: 47 [S8; S9] vs 30 [S1 p.94]. Use 47, attributed.
- 1087 invaders: Turks [S9], not Persians [S8].
- Translation year 1087, not 1081 [S18 error].
- Election, sailors, famine, death: strength C. Attribute to "the oldest Life".
- Governor: Eustathius [S3]; S10 says "Eustratios"; use Eustathius.
- Michael and Praxis translations are rights-limited: own English only; long passages only from S4, S12, S1.
