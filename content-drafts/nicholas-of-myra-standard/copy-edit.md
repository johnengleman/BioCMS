## Listed, not changed (for the fact checker)

1. "That was not enough for the men who had paid." The story names only one man who paid: the army's commander, who promised Ablabius the gold. The Praxis says "those who were plotting against the officers put great pressure upon the prefect". "The men who had paid" may be stronger than the source. A fix needs the checker's wording.
2. A Note on the Sources: "It then calls the governor Eustathius, and this account follows the governor." The meaning is not clear. The Praxis names "Judge Datianus" as the man who ordered the beheading, and later "Eustathius the Praeses", whom Saint Nicholas confronts. The story says "A judge had ordered three men beheaded" and then confronts the governor. Does "follows the governor" mean that this account treats one man or two? The checker should give the plain sentence.
3. "As they talked, Saint Nicholas said that the condemned men must be coming out by now." "Coming out" of where is not said. The Praxis is no clearer ("the victims ought by now to be coming out"). Left as it is.
4. "Some of the men wanted to take the old wonder-working icon of the saint as well, but they were stopped." Nobody is named as the actor. The source is passive too ("they were definitely forbidden to do so"). Left as it is.
5. "In hard times he became the grain merchant of his whole country." An odd picture for a man who begged grain on credit, but it is Michael's own figure ("a readily available corn-dealer to all of his fatherland"). Left as it is.
6. "He was gentle with everyone, but fierce with sinners." Sinners are part of "everyone", but this is Andrew's own contrast ("mild towards everyone, but … terrible towards sinners"). Left as it is.
7. Titles: the emperor Constantine and the emperor Justinian are left without "Saint". Both are saints in the Orthodox Church, not in the Catholic calendar (see `titles-pass.md`). This page is for both Churches.
8. "Saint" inside quotations ("Lord God of Saint Nicholas, hear us"; "Saint Nicholas, servant of Christ"; "Saint Nicholas gave them") is in the Praxis translation itself. Not changed.
