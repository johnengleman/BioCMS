# Check of miracles.html (Augustine of Hippo)

Original kept as miracles.html.before-check. Checked against the notes, scene cards, City of God XXII.8 (Dods English and the Latin on la.wikisource.org), Possidius chapters 12, 15, 28, 29 (Weiskotten), Butler vol. 8, Confessions III and VIII (Pusey), Sermons 321-324 (Latin, augustinus.it), Sciberras, and Boodts.

## Added (five accounts)
- Firmus the merchant (Possidius ch. 15): new h3 "A merchant is converted by an unfinished sermon, Hippo", in "During Augustine's life" after the wrong-turn account. Marked as God's plan, not a healing. No year, none in the source.
- Paralysed young countryman at Zubedi: new h3 after the Zubedi farm account. Augustine was at the burial of the earth; he does not say he saw the cure. Status says so.
- Young man at Victoriana, eye healed after seven days: it is the same man as the old demoniac entry, so the old entry was expanded and not duplicated. The heading now names the eye.
- Bishop who prayed for a young man he never saw: new h3 after the oil-and-tears account. Augustine names no one and gives no place or date.
- Petronia: rewritten from a thin entry to the full account (cured at Uzalis, hair girdle and ring, Bagrada estate, ring found at her feet). Latin "ibi ... sanata est" supports "cured there". "A Jew" is left out of the text (not needed).

## Fixes
- Intro of the Stephen group: "credited to the martyrs and not to Augustine" was too strong (Hesperius, Florentius, Innocentius). Now says Augustine credits God, who works through martyrs, sacraments, and prayer. Also "healings at the shrines" widened to "wonders".
- Milan blind man: "before the court" was not in the source. Source: the emperor was in the city and a great crowd saw it. Added that Ambrose found the bodies after a dream.
- Innocentia: added the order of events (doctor asked, she told, he answered with contempt, she replied). "Sign of the cross" changed to "sign of Christ" (Latin "signo Christi"; Dods). Quotation checked against the Latin.
- Gouty doctor: "Augustine had the story from others" is not in the source. Source: Augustine says he and a few brothers nearby know of it. Added the devils' ban on baptism and that he never had gout again.
- Curubis comedian: "had the man brought to Carthage to check the cure" was wrong. The source says Augustine already had the facts from reliable people. "Old" dropped (the Latin has no "senex"; Dods does).
- Oil and tears: she anointed herself; "a priest anointed her" was wrong. "Young woman" and "from his own knowledge" added.
- Martial: "at dawn asked for the bishop" corrected. He called for the bishop before dawn, the bishop was away at Hippo, priests baptized him. Added the son-in-law's prayer. No place given (the source says only "there").
- Caspalium: the woman had died ("defuncta"), not "believed dead". Heading and text fixed.
- Bassus, Audurus, Irenaeus, Eleusinus: small additions from the source. "Tribune" changed to "a man of tribune rank" (Latin "vir tribunicius").
- Monica's dream: Augustine "said the dream meant" changed to "tried to explain" (Pusey: "would fain bend it to mean").
- Garden scene: "light of certainty" changed to "light of calm" (Pusey: "serenity").
- Siege: added that Possidius records Augustine's prayer (ch. 29). Possidius chapters 28 and 29 now in the Source line. "Fourteen months" is Butler and Possidius ("almost fourteen"). July 431 confirmed in Weiskotten's note.
- Pavia: Sciberras's words are "a number of miracles, attributed to the relics" (commas added to the quotation). "Neither ruling was about the miracles" is not in the source and was cut. Now says both rulings concerned whether the relics were genuine. Benedict XIII confirmed it in writing (22 Sept 1728).
- Seashore legend: Caxton saw the scene shown on an altar at the Blackfriars in Antwerp (Boodts: "saw it depicted"). The old line "earliest art the source names" was wrong. Boodts calls the Gozzoli fresco "one of the earliest".
- Uzalis boy: the old entry said "about 426" and "known only from a database summary". I read Sermon 324 in Latin. It is an infant son, a catechumen, who died in his mother's lap; she took him to Stephen's shrine; he revived, was baptized, and later died again. The sermon follows the Palladia day (it finishes the sermon that her cure interrupted), so it belongs to Easter week, and no year is given for the event. Heading, text, status, and Source line were replaced.
- Paulus and Palladia: status now says Sermons 321-324 (Easter Monday on) belong to these days. The year 425 still rests only on the Oxford database summary, and the status says so.
- Deathbed quotation: added "the" to match Possidius ("Go to the bishop Augustine"). Meaning unchanged.
- Quotations confirmed against the Latin or Pusey: "See how the Twenty Martyrs have clothed you"; Innocentia's reply; "Christ, receive my spirit"; "Take up and read"; Monica's "Where you are, there he is".

## Checked and left alone
Demoniacs and deathbed healing (Possidius ch. 29); wrong road (ch. 12); toothache (Butler cites Confessions IX.4); Innocentius; Zubedi farm; Florentius; Tibilis; Lucillus; Eucharius; two gout cases; Paulus and Palladia details; Pavia dates; Milan date 386 and Carthage date about 388 rest on the notes and Butler, not on chapter 8.
