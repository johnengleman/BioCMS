## 1. The driving question

Augustine wanted happiness and wisdom with his whole heart, but he looked for them outside himself: in pleasure, in fame as a speaker, and in a sect. His own will stood in the way, and he prayed for chastity "but not yet" [OW-S1 VIII.7.17]. His mother's tears, a bishop's sermons, and one verse read in a garden broke the "not yet"; he then gave his life away until he died owning nothing in a besieged city, and so lived out his own first line: "our heart is restless until it rests in you" [OW-S1 I.1.1].

He is famous for the Confessions, his own sins told as a prayer to God, and as one of the four Latin Doctors of the Church [CM-S4].

He told his faults without disguise, in a book addressed to God [W-S2; W char.]. As a priest he preached before his bishop against African custom, and as a bishop he lived poor in a monastery inside his church and refused to be treated as a wonder-worker [W-S1 ch.5; CM-S1 ch.29].

## 2. Portrait

A brilliant, restless talker who could not stop asking why. He mocked his younger self ("Give me chastity... but not yet") and admitted he feared God would answer too soon [OW-S1 VIII.7.17; OW-S2]. He was honest about low motives: he stole pears for the company, and "alone I had never done it" [OW-S2]. He loved hard: a dead friend was "half of my soul" [OW-S1 IV.6.11], and losing the mother of his son left his heart "torn and wounded and bleeding" [W-S2 Bk VI]. He lied to his mother to escape her [W-S2 Bk V]. He wept easily: at hymns, at his ordination, over sacked cities, over the psalms at the end [OW-S1 IX.6.14; W-S1 ch.4, 28, 31]. As bishop he was plain and strict: earthenware dishes by choice, no swearing, a carved warning against gossip on his table, and he got "so angry" when bishops broke it [W-S1 ch.22, 25]. He kept no keys to the treasury [W-S1 ch.24]. He said his own consecration had broken a rule [W-S1 ch.8].

His life with God: he spoke to God directly; the whole Confessions says "you." He found God within after searching outside: "You were within, and I was outside" [OW-S1 X.27.38]; "I entered into my inmost self, with you as my guide" [OW-S1 VII.10.16].

## 3. The opening

Hook: a small sin, told decades later to God.

> There was a pear tree near the family vineyard, heavy with fruit that was neither pretty nor tasty. One night a sixteen-year-old boy and his friends stripped it and carried off the pears. He did not want them. He had better pears at home, and he threw the stolen ones away. Years later that boy was a bishop in Africa, and he told the story to God in a book. He still wanted to understand it, and he admitted the plainest part: alone, he would never have done it.

[OW-S1 II.4.9; OW-S2; W-S2 Bk II]. Add no other details.

Thesis: The book was the Confessions, a whole life told as a prayer [W-S2]. The thief became bishop of Hippo for more than thirty years, lived poor among his clergy, and argued with Manichees, Donatists, and Pelagians [W-S1 ch.5, 6, 12, 18]. After Rome fell he wrote the City of God: "two loves made two cities" [W-S3 p.478; OW-S4b XIV.28]. In 1298 the Church named him one of its four Latin Doctors [CM-S4]. Chapters 12 and 15 answer it. Chapter 13 closes the pear loop: he dies with nothing to leave [W-S1 ch.31].

## 4. Chapters

1. **Augustine Steals Pears He Does Not Want** — SC-01. Birth 354, parents, baptism put off. Ends: his father dies; Carthage.
2. **Augustine Joins a Sect in Carthage** — SC-02. Cicero's Hortensius, the companion and Adeodatus, the Manichees. Ends: "the son of these tears."
3. **Augustine Loses His Closest Friend** — SC-03. Ends: he leaves home.
4. **Augustine Lies to His Mother and Sails Away** — SC-04. Rome, illness, students who do not pay. Ends: a post in Milan.
5. **Augustine Hears the Bishop of Milan Preach** — SC-05. Faustus's empty answers; the companion sent back to Africa. Ends: "not yet" still holds.
6. **Augustine Turns to God in a Milan Garden** — SC-06. Ends: they run to tell Monica.
7. **The Bishop of Milan Baptizes Augustine** — SC-07. Ends: the family starts home.
8. **Augustine's Mother Dies on the Journey Home** — SC-08, SC-09. Ends: three quiet years in Africa.
9. **The People of Hippo Make Augustine a Priest** — SC-10. Letter 21, the monastery. Ends: Valerius fears another church will take him.
10. **Augustine Becomes Bishop Against His Will** — SC-11. Fortunatus debate 392. Ends: enemies on the roads.
11. **Augustine Defends the Church in Africa** — SC-12. Donatists, the wrong road, Crispinus, Pelagians. Ends: Rome has fallen.
12. **Rome Falls and Augustine Writes the City of God** — no scene. Stephen's relics 424, Maximinus 428, the Retractationes. Ends: ships from Spain bring the Vandals.
13. **Augustine Dies While the Vandals Besiege Hippo** — SC-13, SC-14. Ends: the city burns; the library survives.
14. **Augustine's Bones Are Found in an Italian Church** — SC-15. Ends: what no army could carry off.
15. **How Augustine Changed the Church** — no scene. Closes the restless-heart loop.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — The pear tree, about 370 (near the family vineyard)
- What happens: he and friends strip a tree at night and throw the fruit away.
- Why: the divided will in its smallest form; the hook.
- Have: [OW-S1 II.4.9, II.6.12; OW-S2; W-S2 Bk II].
- Need: the full night narrative in Conf. II.4.9; Latin and section for "alone I had never done it."
- Testimony / look: OW-S1 II.4.9-II.8.16.

### SC-02 — Monica's dream and the bishop, about 373-383 (Africa)
- What happens: Monica dreams on a wooden rule and corrects her son at once; a bishop refuses to argue with him, then says "the son of these tears" cannot perish.
- Why: grace works through his mother while he resists.
- Have: [W-S2 Bk III].
- Need: Latin of both passages; the town if named.
- Look: OW-S1 III.11-12.

### SC-03 — The friend dies, Thagaste
- What happens: a close friend dies; his home town becomes a horror to him.
- Why: love without God cannot rest; a hard part.
- Have: "half of my soul," "nolebam dimidius vivere" [OW-S1 IV.6.11].
- Need: the friend's illness and baptism; the year.
- Look: OW-S1 IV.4.7-IV.7.12.

### SC-04 — The shore at Carthage, about 383
- What happens: he lies that he waits for a friend's ship; Monica prays all night at St. Cyprian's oratory; he sails.
- Why: his worst act against the person who loved him most.
- Have: "The wind blew and swelled our sails"; "Thou wert hurrying me" [W-S2 Bk V; W-S5 intro].
- Need: Latin of the passage; Monica the next morning.
- Look: OW-S1 V.8.15.

### SC-05 — Ambrose reading in silence, Milan, 384-386
- What happens: crowds keep him from Ambrose; he sits in the open room and watches the bishop read without a sound.
- Why: the man he cannot reach yet.
- Have: [OW-S1 VI.3.3; W-S2 Bk VI]; "hanging and fixed" on the sermons [W-S1 ch.1].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-06 — The garden, Milan, 386
- What happens: Ponticianus tells of Antony; Augustine weeps under a fig tree, hears "Take up and read," reads Romans 13:13-14; Alypius reads the next verse; Monica "leaps for joy."
- Why: the turn of the driving question.
- Have: [OW-S1 VIII.12.29; OW-S3; W-S2 Bk VIII].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Conf. VIII.12.28-30, Latin, our English (OW-S1).

### SC-07 — Easter eve 387, Milan
- What happens: Ambrose baptizes Augustine, Alypius, and Adeodatus, about fifteen; afterward he weeps at the hymns.
- Why: life with God; the past let go.
- Have: [W-S3 p.445-446; W-S1 ch.1; W-S2 Bk IX; OW-S1 IX.6.14].
- Need: his words about Adeodatus in Conf. IX.6.14.

### SC-08 — The window at Ostia, 387
- What happens: mother and son lean on a window over a garden and talk of the life of the saints.
- Why: the clearest scene of his life with God.
- Have: [W-S2 Bk IX; OW-S3 IX.10.23]; Monica's "no further delight in any thing in this life."
- Need: Latin of IX.10.24-25.
- Testimony: Conf. IX.10.23-26 (OW-S1), our English.

### SC-09 — Monica's death, Ostia, 387
- What happens: nine days of fever; "Lay this body anywhere"; she dies; Adeodatus cries out; Evodius starts a psalm; Augustine holds back his tears.
- Why: grief held under faith; a hard part.
- Have: [OW-S1 IX.11.27; W-S2 Bk IX; OW-S3 IX.12.29].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-10 — Seized in church, Hippo, 391
- What happens: he stands unaware in the crowd; the people seize him for ordination; he weeps; some misread the tears.
- Why: the man who fled his mother cannot flee this call.
- Have: [W-S1 ch.3-4]; Letter 21, "praying, reading, and weeping," "till Easter" [OW-S5 §1, 4].
- Need: Latin of Possidius ch.4.
- Testimony: W-S1 ch.4.

### SC-11 — The bishop's table, Hippo
- What happens: carved lines against gossip; a bishop breaks them; Augustine threatens to leave the meal.
- Why: a real person; the rule of a common life.
- Have: couplet, "so angry," Possidius present [W-S1 ch.22, 25].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-12 — Firmus at his feet, Hippo
- What happens: a sermon wanders off its topic; a Manichee merchant falls weeping at his feet, and later becomes a priest.
- Why: one real person changed; his old sect answered.
- Have: [W-S1 ch.15].
- Need: the year.

### SC-13 — The prayer at table, Hippo, summer 430
- What happens: under siege he tells the bishops his threefold prayer; they join it; fever comes in the third month.
- Why: the last choice: God's will, whatever it is.
- Have: [W-S1 ch.28-29; CM-S1 ch.29].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-14 — Death, Hippo, 28 August 430
- What happens: a visitor asks him to heal a relative; "if I had any power..."; he lays on his hand. Psalms hang on the wall; ten days alone; he dies as the bishops pray.
- Why: the death; rest found.
- Have: [W-S1 ch.29, 31; CM-S1 ch.29, 31; W-S5 for the date].
- Need: any last words in Possidius ch.31; Latin of the deathbed passage.
- Testimony: Possidius ch.31, 100-200 words (W-S1).

### SC-15 — Pavia, 1 October 1695
- What happens: bricklayers at a crypt altar find a marble box with charcoal letters; inside, another marble box and a silver box.
- Why: the real case after death.
- Have: [CM-S6]; 1728 confirmation by Bishop Pertusati and Benedict XIII [CM-S6; CM-S3 p.505]. Facts only.
- Need: the words of the letters; a public-domain account.
- Look: CM-S6; CM-S3 p.505.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** Confessions, about 401 [OW-S2] (ch.1-8, 15); Letter 21, 391 [OW-S5] (ch.9); City of God, 413-426 [W-S3 p.478; OW-S4] (ch.12); Homilies on 1 John [OW-S7] and Sermo 256 [OW-S8] (ch.12); On Christian Doctrine [OW-S6] (ch.15); Retractationes [W-S1 ch.28] (ch.12); debates with Fortunatus 392 [W-S1 ch.6] and Maximinus 428 [W-S3 p.478].
- **What he changed:** monastery in the church, goods in common, 391 [W-S1 ch.5] (ch.9); council rule on consecration [W-S1 ch.8] (ch.10); Pelagianism condemned by African councils, Innocent, Zosimus, and Honorius [W-S1 ch.18] (ch.11); library kept [W-S1 ch.31] (ch.13); Doctor, Boniface VIII, 1298 [CM-S4] (ch.15); relics to the Hermits of St Augustine, 1327 [CM-S6] (ch.14).
- **Why famous:** feast in the Martyrology of Jerome and at Carthage by the 6th century; Caesarius kept it [CM-S3 p.505] (ch.15). Theodosius invited him to Ephesus in 431 [CM-S3 p.505] (ch.14). Liutprand brought the relics to Pavia about 720-725; shrine 1362; arm to Annaba 1842; Popes at the tomb 1960, 2004, 2007 [CM-S6] (ch.14). Patron of brewers, printers, theologians; sore eyes [CM-S8] (ch.15, one line).
- **Path to sainthood:** no formal canonization; acclaim of the people [CM-S8]; early feast 28 August [CM-S3 p.505]; Doctor 1298 [CM-S4]; Orthodox feast 15 June [CM-S8; OW-S9] (ch.14-15).

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Patricius: Possidius says both parents were Christian; Augustine and Butler say Patricius was baptized just before death, 371 [W-S1 ch.1; W-S5]. Follow Augustine.
- Consecration: "about 395-396"; the "391 coadjutor" note is an error [W-S5 ch.8 n.].
- 386 for the garden comes from the task brief; baptism Easter 387 [W-S5; CM-S5].
- Rome before Ostia in 387: Butler only [W-S3 p.446].
- Siege: "almost fourteen months," fever in the third month; no start date [W-S1 ch.28-29].
- "Late have I loved you" is modern wording; "ad te" means "toward you" [OW-S1 I.1.1].
- "Canta et ambula" = "sing and walk," not "march"; 418 date unverified [OW-S8].
- "Love, and do what you want" is about correction from love [OW-S7].
- "Half of my soul": a poet; do not name Horace [OW-S1 IV.6.11].
- Names: Alypius, Monica, Adeodatus, Evodius, Tagaste/Thagaste. Pavia letters: charcoal [CM-S6], not engraved [CM-S3].
