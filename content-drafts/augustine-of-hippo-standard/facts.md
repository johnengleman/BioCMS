# Facts: Augustine of Hippo (checked 2026-10-07)

The miracle list and the teachings essay must use these exactly. Notes are the numbers in `biography.html`.

## Names and spellings
- Saint Augustine of Hippo (Aurelius Augustinus). Father Patricius; mother Saint Monica; brother Navigius; one sister (unnamed in the sources); son Adeodatus; the woman he lived with is never named. Friends: Saint Alypius, Evodius, Ponticianus. Bishops: Saint Ambrose of Milan, Valerius of Hippo, Megalius of Calama. Biographer: Saint Possidius, bishop of Calama. Donatist bishop Crispinus of Calama. Merchant Firmus. Manichee priest Fortunatus; Manichee teacher Faustus.
- Places: Thagaste (Tagaste), about fifty miles southeast of Hippo and about a hundred and fifty miles southwest of Carthage (notes 3, 7); Hippo Regius (modern Annaba); Madaura; Carthage; Rome; Milan; Ostia.
- Books: Confessions, City of God, The Teacher, The Mirror, Retractations, Rule of Saint Augustine.

## Dates and numbers
- Born 13 November 354 at Thagaste (note 3). Died 28 August 430 in his seventy-sixth year; Possidius says he lived 76 years (note 101). The day comes from Prosper via Weiskotten.
- Father died 371 and was baptized just before the end (note 7). Hortensius at 19, his father two years dead (note 9). Manichee hearer nine years, from 19 to 28 (note 13). Carthage teaching from 374 for nine years; to Rome about 383 (note 27).
- Garden at Milan, late summer 386 (Butler: 386, August or September; Catholic Encyclopedia: September 386). Baptized by Saint Ambrose at Easter 387 with Saint Alypius and Adeodatus, who was about fifteen (note 52). Saint Monica died at Ostia on the ninth day of her illness, in her fifty-sixth year and his thirty-third (note 62).
- Back at Carthage about September 388; almost three years at Thagaste (note 66). Adeodatus died at about eighteen, place and year unknown (note 67).
- Ordained priest 391; Letter 21 to Valerius written 391; Fortunatus debate 28 August 392 at the Baths of Sossius; Saint Alypius bishop of Thagaste 394; Augustine made bishop about 395 or 396; Valerius died 396 (notes 71–75).
- Confessions written 397–400 (Benedict XVI, 20 February 2008, note 2). City of God begun 413 and finished 426 (note 87). Saint Ambrose died 397 (note 78).
- Vandals and Alans with some Goths landed in Africa (Weiskotten: 428); siege of Hippo almost fourteen months; Saint Augustine fell sick in the third month; about ten days before the end he asked to be left alone; the Vandals left in July 431 (notes 92, 95, 100, 103).
- Relics: Bede and Sciberras place the move to Pavia in the 720s; marble shrine ordered 1362; Pope Gregory XIII forbade searching 1580; found Tuesday 1 October 1695; bishop of Pavia ruled them genuine 1728; Pope Benedict XIII confirmed on 22 September 1728; piece of the arm to Annaba 1842; shown on 24 April and 28 August (notes 104–105).
- Boniface VIII, 1298: feasts of the four Latin Doctors ordered kept in the whole Church (note 107). Council of Orange 529. Hermits of Saint Augustine united 1256 (notes 106–107). Feast on 28 August in calendars from the sixth century; Orthodox feast 15 June (note 108).

## Quotations (one wording each)
- Conf. I.1.1 (note 11, 109): "You made us toward yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you."
- Conf. III.4.7 (note 10): "…with an unbelievable fire in my heart I longed for the deathlessness of wisdom."
- Conf. VIII.7.17 (note 14): "Give me chastity and self-control, but not yet."
- Conf. IV.6.11 (note 25): "…one soul in two bodies… I did not want to live as half a man."
- Conf. V.8.15 (note 28): "I lied to my mother, and to such a mother." Also (note 29): "Through my own desires you were carrying me off, to put an end to those very desires."
- Conf. VIII.11.27 (note 46) and VIII.12.28 (note 47): "How long, how long, 'tomorrow and tomorrow'? Why not now? Why not in this hour an end to my shame?"
- Conf. VIII.12.29 (note 48): "Take up and read, take up and read." The verse: Romans 13:13–14.
- Conf. IX.10.24 (note 55): "…we touched it a little, with the whole beat of the heart."
- Conf. IX.10.26 (note 57): "What am I doing here?"
- Conf. IX.11.27 (note 59): "Here you will bury your mother." (note 60): "Lay this body anywhere… remember me at the Lord's altar." (note 61): "Nothing is far from God."
- Conf. X.27.38 (note 77): "Late have I loved you, beauty so ancient and so new, late have I loved you!"
- Letter 21 (note 71): "The Lord laughed at me, and chose to show me by experience what I am."
- Possidius 4 (note 70): the ordination, "while he wept freely". Possidius 29 (note 94): "Either that he free this city… or at least that he take me from this world to himself." Possidius 27 (note 96): "If never, well and good. If some day, why not now?" Possidius 31 (note 102): "he slept with his fathers, as the Scripture says, well nourished in a good old age."
- City of God XIV.28 (note 87): "two loves made two cities: the earthly city by love of self, even to contempt of God; the heavenly city by love of God, even to contempt of self."
- Letter 211.5 (note 106): "with your hearts and minds one in God."

## Disputes and choices
- Patricius baptized only at the end (Saint Augustine); Possidius says both parents were Christian (note 3).
- Marble box at Pavia: Butler says engraved; Sciberras says charcoal (note 105).
- Adeodatus's death: Butler "about the eighteenth year of his age"; no place or year (note 67).
- Possidius 31 "paucissimi de poenitentia": told as "a few of David's psalms of repentance".
- Possidius is the bishop beaten on the road (Weiskotten), not named by himself (note 83).
