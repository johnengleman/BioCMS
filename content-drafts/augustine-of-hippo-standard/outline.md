## 1. The driving question

Saint Augustine wanted a wisdom that would not die and a love that could not be lost. At nineteen, he wrote, "every empty hope suddenly lost its worth to me, and I longed for the deathlessness of wisdom" [OW-S1 III.4.7]. His own divided will stood in the way: he loved the company, the woman, and the fame, and he prayed "Give me chastity and self-control, but not yet" [OW-S1 VIII.7.17]. Grace came through his mother's tears, a bishop he could not reach, and a child's voice in a garden; then his own choices followed, until he gave even his life to God's will in a besieged city, and his restless heart came to rest.

Why it was beautiful to him: "You made us toward yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in you" [OW-S1 I.1.1]; God was "beauty so ancient and so new" [OW-S1 X.27.38]. The reader first hears the desire in chapter 2, at the Hortensius, the first great decision. The restless-heart line is planted there as his later words, and it comes back in the last sentence.

Famous for the Confessions and the City of God, and as one of the four Latin Doctors of the Church [CM-S4]. He set himself apart by telling his own sins to God in a book [W-S2]. As a bishop he lived poor in common, never held the treasury key, refused to be treated as a wonder-worker, and said his own consecration broke a rule [W-S1 ch.5, 8, 24; CM-S1 ch.29].

## 2. Portrait

A brilliant, restless talker who could not stop asking why. He mocked his younger self ("but not yet") and admitted "I was afraid you would hear me too soon" [OW-S1 VIII.7.17]. He was honest about low motives: "alone I would not have done it at all" [OW-S1 II.8.16]. He loved hard: a dead friend was "half of his soul" [OW-S1 IV.6.11]. He lied to his mother to get away [OW-S1 V.8.15]. He wept easily: at hymns, at his ordination, over burned cities, over the psalms at the end [OW-S1 IX.6.14; W-S1 ch.4, 28, 31]. As a bishop he was plain and strict. He ate from earthenware by choice, forbade swearing, and lost his temper when close friends gossiped at his table [W-S1 ch.22, 25]. When a donor asked for his gift back, he groaned, returned the deed at once, and refused the coins [W-S1 ch.24; copy-edit.md].

Life with God: the Confessions speaks to God as "you." He found God inside after searching outside: "You were within, and I was outside" [OW-S1 X.27.38]; "I entered into my inmost self, with you as my guide" [OW-S1 VII.10.16]. At the end he prayed alone, with the psalms on the wall [W-S1 ch.31].

## 3. The opening

Hook: a small sin, told to God decades later. Draft:

> There was a pear tree near the family vineyard, loaded with fruit that was neither pretty nor tasty. One night, in his sixteenth year, a boy and a gang of friends shook it bare and carried the pears away, and he threw his share to waste. He had better pears at home. Decades later, as a bishop in Africa, he told that night to God in a book, and he still asked what he had loved in it. Not the pears, he decided: alone, he would not have done it at all.

[OW-S1 II.4.9, II.6.12, II.8.16; card scenes-1 SC-01]. Add no other details.

The opening and summary.txt must not name the garden, the verse, Ostia, or the death in the siege. Cut the old bio's "God broke the 'not yet' through... one verse read in a garden."

Thesis (2-4 sentences): The book was the Confessions, written between 397 and 400, a life told as a prayer [Bio; CM-S7]. The boy became bishop of the port of Hippo for more than thirty years, wrote the City of God after Rome fell, and in 1298 was named one of the four Latin Doctors [W-S1 ch.8, 31; W-S3 p.478; CM-S4]. Chapters 11, 12, and 15 answer it.

Ending plan: Chapter 14 is the climax and ends on his death. Chapter 15 is short. It covers the poor man's empty will and the kept library, Pavia in one scene, and then the Rule, the Doctor title, and the feasts. Closing image: Saint Possidius wrote that in his books "the faithful may always find him living" [CM-S1 ch.31]. A reader who opens the Confessions today finds the answer on its first page: "our heart is restless until it rests in you" [OW-S1 I.1.1]. End there.

11. **Saint Augustine Becomes Bishop Against His Will** — consecration about 395-396; Valerius dies 396; the Confessions written; SC-11; the returned deed; Saint Ambrose's dying words. Ends on its own image: the table.
12. **Saint Augustine Defends the Church in Africa** — SC-12; Firmus and Crispinus at most a paragraph each; Pelagians; Rome 410; City of God ("two loves"); the Retractationes. Pull: ships from Spain bring the Vandals.

15. **How Saint Augustine Lives On in the Church** — SC-15 short; part 6 items; closing image.

## 5. The scenes

Turning points told slowly, from inside him: SC-02, SC-07, SC-08, SC-10, SC-13. Climax: SC-14.

**Resistance in each period** (compress the rest of each period): boyhood, the gang (SC-01). Carthage, his mother's refusal (SC-03) and the friend's rebuke (SC-04). Milan, the bishop out of reach and his own "not yet" (SC-06). Conversion, old habits whisper "Are you letting us go?" (SC-07). Thagaste, his son's death (beat, ch.10). Priest, the crowd against his will and "I did not yet know how to handle an oar" (SC-10; Letter 21). Bishop, middle years, his friends' gossip and his own temper (SC-11), then armed men on the road (SC-12). Bishop, last years, whether bishops should flee (ch.13), then the siege (SC-13).

**Losses at their dates:** father, 371, one line [W-S5]; the friend, early twenties [OW-S1 IV.4.7-7.12]; the companion, Milan, about 385, "torn and wounded and bleeding" [W-S2 Bk VI]; Saint Monica, Ostia, 387; Adeodatus, after 388 (Fact need 1), with "you took his life from the earth quickly" and his memory of the boy "fearing nothing" [card scenes-2 SC-07, IX.6.14]; Valerius, 396 [W-S5 ch.8 n.]. Long trial, one concrete day: the table under siege (SC-13).

**Relationships and their ends:** Saint Monica ends at Ostia. Adeodatus: born at Carthage, baptized at fifteen, cries out at Saint Monica's death, dies young. The companion ends at the parting and her vow. Saint Ambrose: "like a father," the silent reading, the baptism. His thread closes in ch.11 with the dying words Saint Augustine praised: "I have not lived so that I would be ashamed to live among you, and I do not fear to die, because we have a good Lord" [W-S1 ch.27]. Saint Alypius: the next verse, barefoot in the frost, then bishop of Thagaste, 394 [W-S5]. Valerius dies in 396. Saint Possidius: forty years, then "we stood by" at the deathbed.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** Confessions 397-400 [Bio; CM-S7] (ch.11, 15); Letter 21, 391 [OW-S5] (ch.10); City of God 413-426 [W-S3 p.478] (ch.12); Retractationes [W-S1 ch.28] (ch.12); Letter 211, 423 [FN-1] (ch.15); Letter to Honoratus [W-S1 ch.30] (ch.13).
- **What he changed:** monastery with goods in common, 391 [W-S1 ch.5] (ch.10); council rule on consecration [W-S1 ch.8] (ch.11); the Rule of Saint Augustine, kept by the Hermits (united 1256) and the Canons Regular, and copied by Saint Caesarius [FN-1; Bio] (ch.15); Second Council of Orange 529 [FN-2; Bio] (ch.15); the library he ordered kept [W-S1 ch.31] (ch.15); Doctor, Boniface VIII, 1298 [CM-S4] (ch.15).
- **Why famous:** feast in the Martyrology of Jerome and at Carthage by the 6th century; kept by Saint Caesarius [CM-S3 p.505]; Liutprand brings the relics to Pavia about 720; shrine 1362; arm to Annaba 1842; popes at the tomb 1960, 2004, 2007; showings on 24 April and 28 August [CM-S6] (ch.15). Patrons: one line [CM-S8].
- **Path to sainthood:** no formal canonization recorded; venerated by the people from early times [CM-S8]; feast 28 August; Orthodox feast 15 June, hymn "expounder of the City of God" [FN-3] (ch.15).

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Patricius: follow Saint Augustine; he was baptized just before his death in 371 [W-S5].
- Consecration: "about 395 or 396." Garden: "late summer 386." Do not give his age at the garden (32 or 33 in the sources).
- Saint Monica took to her bed within five days of the window talk, or a little more. She died on the ninth day of her illness, not after "nine days of fever." She "rejoiced and triumphed"; do not write "leaps for joy" (check.md 1; card SC-06).
- The shore: do not say she found out his plan. Say "the memorial of Saint Cyprian," not "oratory," and give no hour (check.md 2-4).
- Death day 28 August is from Saint Prosper. He spoke no recorded last words. The psalms were on "sheets." The siege lifted in July 431, after his death.
- Confessions "between 397 and 400" (copy-edit.md, resolved item 3), not 401.
- Hermits united 1256 (Bio, CE); the Butler card says 1254. Follow the Bio.
- Garden: "a light of assurance." "Late have I loved you" is a modern wording, which is acceptable. Check the end of Q21 before quoting it.
- Titles: Saint Monica, Saint Alypius, Saint Ambrose, Saint Possidius; plain "Evodius" (the owner has not decided). Do not identify the Hippo officer as Evodius.
- "Half of his soul": do not name the poet. Pavia: say "letters," with no form. 24 April is "his conversion" per CM-S6.
