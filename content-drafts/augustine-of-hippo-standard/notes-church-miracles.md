## Identity points for this focus
- Cult: no formal canonization. "Canonized by popular acclaim" [S8]. Feast on 28 Aug mentioned in the Martyrology of Jerome and in the Carthage martyrology, "as old as the sixth century" [S3 p.505]. Feast kept with great solemnity in the life of St Caesarius (6th c.) [S3 p.505].
- Doctor of the Church: Boniface VIII, 1298, decree ordering feasts of the four Latin doctors kept as doubles; Decretals Book VI, cap. "Gloriosus" [S4]. Augustine is one of the four (with Gregory, Ambrose, Jerome) [S4].
- Feast: 28 Aug (West); 15 June (East); 4 Nov (Assyrian) [S8]. Death date 28 Aug 430 [S3 p.503; S7].
- Patronage: brewers, printers, theologians, many cities and dioceses; invoked against sore eyes [S8]. No old source found for the reasons (to check).
- Tradition type: Western saint; also venerated in Orthodoxy as Blessed (S8, not read in depth).

## Timeline (death and relics)
- 429-430 Vandals reach Hippo, about end of May 430; siege 14 months; Count Boniface and Possidius with other bishops took refuge in Hippo [S3 p.502]. In the third month Augustine fell ill with fever [S1 ch.29; S3].
- Siege prayer. At table he told his guests he asked God to free the city, or give his servants strength, "or at least that He may take me from this world unto Himself." Close paraphrase of S1 (p.119 area, ch.29). Possidius: God did not deprive him of the reward of his prayer. NEW for English pages? Not checked.
- Last ten days. About ten days before death he asked that no one enter except at doctors' hours and mealtimes [S1 ch.31 |143; S3 p.503]. He had the shortest penitential psalms copied and hung on the wall by his bed, and read them "weeping freely and constantly" [S1 ch.31]. (S1 wording: "he wept freely and constantly.")
- 28 Aug 430 Death. Mind clear, sight and hearing intact, "he slept with his fathers" [S1 ch.31]; sacrifice offered, buried, no will because "as a poor man of God he had nothing from which to make it" [S1]. Ordered the library kept [S1; S3].
- Burial place: church of Peace, called St Stephen's since Augustine had deposited there a portion of Stephen's relics in 424 [S3 p.504]. Body respected by the Arian Vandals; library spared [S3 p.504]. (Butler's claim that the Vandals spared the body: source Butler only; Possidius does not say.)
- 431: Theodosius sent a rescript to invite Augustine to Ephesus; he was already dead [S3 p.505].
- About 498 (or 6th-7th c.): bishops exiled by Huneric/Thrasamund to Sardinia carry the relics to Cagliari, church later called Sant'Agostino Vecchio [S3 p.504 "about fifty years after"; S6]. [C] S6: "no reliable documents" mention this; some scholars date it to the 6th-7th century. Source for the story: Bede (De temporum ratione; also True Martyrology per Butler) [S3; S6].
- About 720-725: Liutprand, king of the Lombards, ransoms the relics and brings them to Pavia, San Pietro in Ciel d'Oro (then "in Cella Aurea") [S6; S3]. Butler: Liutprand obtained them from the Saracens for a great sum [S3 p.504]. S6: "buys at a high price". S8: carried by Peter, bishop of Pavia, uncle of Liutprand (check; not primary). 12th-c. story: Liutprand went barefoot to Genoa to meet them [S6; legend, [L]].
- 9th c. Oldrad, archbishop of Milan, wrote a history of the translation for Charlemagne, "extracted from authentic archives then kept at Pavia" [S3 p.504]. Not read directly. Butler says Oldrad had the relics hidden: lead coffin inside silver inside marble, name Augustinus engraved [S3 p.505]. Compare S6: found 1695 as marble box with charcoal letters, then second marble box, then silver box with Lombard-style crosses [S6]. CONFLICT: engraved (Butler) vs charcoal (S6).
- 1022: Archbishop of Canterbury obtained a piece of the left arm [S6]. Tradition that the heart is in Antwerp [S6; [C]].
- 1327 (Jan): John XXII bull Veneranda Sanctorum Patrum gives the Hermits of St Augustine custody [S8; S6 says order of John XXII in 1327]. Arca (marble shrine) commissioned 1362 [S6; S8 Giovanni di Balduccio 1362-1365].
- 1580: Gregory XIII forbids any search or moving of the remains [S6].
- 1 Oct 1695 (Tuesday morning): bricklayers working at the altar of the crypt find a Carrara marble box about 120 x 30 x 30 cm; sacristan friar called as prior away; charcoal letters read as Augustine [S6]. Inquiry Nov 1695 to Feb 1696 and 1698; no decision; "a number of miracles, attributed to the relics" accelerated events [S6]. NEW: details of the box and the sacristan.
- 1728: Benedict XIII asks for conclusions; bones reopened May, work ended 20 June; procession from Duomo; plenary indulgence 40 days; bishop Francesco Pertusati confirmed authenticity in July (proclamation 16 July; Te Deum, fireworks; threat of excommunication on dissenters); Benedict XIII confirmed in writing 22 Sept 1728 [S6]. Butler: "inconstestably proved authentic by the bishop of Pavia in 1728, whose sentence was confirmed by pope Benedict XIII" [S3 p.505]. Status [A] (papal confirmation), but [C] among scholars of the time, per S6.
- 1734 relics briefly moved to the Duomo; 1759 Hermits take them to a former Jesuit convent; 1799 Augustinians expelled by Napoleon; relics in Duomo [S6]. (S8 says expelled 1785.)
- 1842: a portion of the right arm (cubitus) given to bishop Dupuch for Algeria; now in the basilica at Annaba [S6; S8]. S8: placed in a glass tube in the arm of a marble statue.
- 1884: inventory of 225 bone pieces and glass containers [S6]. 1896: church reopened, relics reinstalled [S6; S8].
- Exposition twice a year: 24 April (conversion) and 28 Aug [S6]. Paul VI visited May 1960; John Paul II venerated them November 2004; Benedict XVI visited 22 April 2007 [S6].

## Miracles
Setting. Possidius' Life is almost without miracles; three small items below [S1]. Augustine's own big catalogue is for Stephen's relics and other martyrs in City of God XXII.8 [S2]. Church status: no official approval of any of these ancient miracles was found. Say "reported".

### Miracles of Augustine himself
1. Demoniacs. While priest and bishop, when asked to pray for certain demoniacs, he prayed "with many tears and the demons departed from the men." Reporter: Possidius, personal knowledge [P]. [S1 ch.29 |119]
2. Healing in his last illness. A man brought a sick relative and asked him to lay hands on him. Augustine answered that if he had any power in such things he would have used it on himself first. The man said he had a dream: "Go to the bishop Augustine that he may lay his hand upon him, and he shall be whole." Augustine did so, and "immediately God caused the sick man to depart from him healed." [P] [S1 ch.29 |119] (Butler repeats it [S3 p.504].)
3. Visits to the sick: he went to pray over the sick and lay on hands when asked [P, custom, not a miracle] [S1 ch.27].
4. Hippo not taken during the siege: Butler says it was "ascribed to his prayer" [S3 p.504]. [L/weak]. Conflict: S3 itself says Hippo was abandoned and partly burned after the siege lifted. Contested.
5. Butler notes more miracles in Woodhead's life of St Austin [S3 p.504]; not read.

### Miracles at relics of Stephen (reported by Augustine in the City of God XXII.8) [Aug]
Augustine himself says (S2) that the Stephen relics reached Hippo less than two years before writing, and that "those which have been published amount to almost seventy at the hour at which I write." At Calama there are "incomparably more." He says he cannot record all.
6. Milan, 386: blind man restored at the relics of Protasius and Gervasius, found by Ambrose; Augustine says it happened when he was there, before the court [Aug] [S2].
7. Carthage, about 386-388: Innocentius, ex-advocate, cured of fistulae after prayer; before the second operation the surgeons found "a perfectly firm cicatrix." Augustine says it happened "in my presence, and under my own eyes" [Aug] [S2]. Scene: Innocentius threw himself on the ground, praying with sobs; Augustine "could not pray at all"; bishop Aurelius and Saturninus of Uzalis among visitors [S2].
8. Carthage: Innocentia, a noblewoman, cancer of the breast; physician advised no remedy; told in a dream to ask the first woman out of the baptistery after baptism to sign her sore with the cross; cured; her answer to the doctor: "What great thing was it for Christ to heal a cancer, who raised one who had been four days dead?" [Aug] [S2].
9. Carthage: gouty doctor baptized, freed from gout; devils appeared as black boys in a dream [Aug, reported to him, hearsay] [S2].
10. Curubis: old comedian cured of paralysis and hernia at baptism; Augustine had him brought to Carthage by order of Aurelius to check [Aug] [S2].
11. Zubedi: Hesperius' farm freed of evil spirits after a priest offered the Eucharist; holy earth from Jerusalem hung in his bedroom [Aug] [S2].
12. Victoriana, near Hippo: demoniac young man freed at the monument of Protasius and Gervasius [Aug] [S2].
13. Hippo: a young woman freed from a devil by oil mixed with the tears of a praying priest [Aug] [S2].
14. Hippo: Florentius, poor tailor, prays to the Twenty Martyrs for a coat; finds a fish with a gold ring; cook Catosus: "See how the Twenty Martyrs have clothed you." [Aug] [S2].
15. Tibilis: bishop Projectus carrying Stephen's relics; blind woman gets the flowers, touches her eyes, sees [Aug] [S2]. Sore eyes link to Stephen and Milan blind man. Patronage for eyes not tied to a source here.
16. Sinita: bishop Lucillus, long fistula, cured when carrying the relics in procession [Aug] [S2].
17. Calama: Spanish priest Eucharius cured of stone by relics Possidius brought; later raised from death when the priest's cloak was laid on him [Aug] [S2]. Possidius appears as a person in this report.
18. Martial (old nobleman, Calama region?) converted: son-in-law left flowers from Stephen's oratory at his head; at dawn he asked for the bishop, was baptized; last words "Christ, receive my spirit" [Aug] [S2]. (Place not stated in the text I read; check.)
19. Two men healed of gout (a citizen; a stranger given a cure) [Aug] [S2].
20. Audurus: boy crushed by a wagon wheel, laid at the shrine, revived uninjured [Aug] [S2].
21. Caspalium: woman believed dead, dress taken to shrine; wrapped in it, her breath returned [Aug] [S2].
22. Hippo: Bassus the Syrian, daughter dead; her dress laid on the body; restored [Aug] [S2].
23. Hippo: son of tax-gatherer Irenaeus died; anointed with the martyr's oil; revived [Aug] [S2].
24. Eleusinus, tribune: laid his dead infant son on the shrine, took him up alive [Aug] [S2].
25. Uzalis: many miracles; relics placed there earlier by Evodius; Petronia, noblewoman, cured; Augustine had her write a libellus [Aug] [S2].
26. Hippo, Easter 425 (c.): Paulus and Palladia of Caesarea in Cappadocia, cursed by their mother, tremor; Paulus healed on Easter Sunday morning holding the bars of the shrine; Palladia healed on the Tuesday as the libellus was read, while Augustine was speaking [Aug, eyewitness; sermons 321-324 per S9] [S2]. Quote: she "rose up cured" and "such a shout of wonder rose". Best scene for the writer.
27. Uzalis: boy brought back to life; Sermon 324 c.426 [Aug; via S9 only].

Status for all 6-27: reported by Augustine, personal or collected testimony. Church approval not found.
