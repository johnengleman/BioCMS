# Augustine of Hippo: teachings and influence outline

Keys: T- = notes-teachings.md; OW-, W-, CM- = biography notes. Facts-only sources (Catechism, LG, Benedict XVI, Leo XIV, CNA, Sanidopoulos, Orange translation): one short sentence of quotation at most, otherwise paraphrase. Latin quotations: use our own English from the notes.

## 1. Kind of saint

**A teacher of the whole Church.** Popes from 431 on named his books as the Church's teaching on grace, a council built its canons from them, and he is one of the four Latin Doctors (1298) [T-S4, T-S6, T-S20, CM-S4]. **Essay: about 5,000 words.**

## 2. Central claim

Augustine taught the Western Church how to speak of grace: God's help comes first, even before the first wish to believe. Within a century of his death, popes and the Council of Orange made that teaching the Church's own, and the line runs on to Trent and the Catechism. In the Confessions he also gave the Church a new way to look for God within the self.

## 3. Section plan

### 1. Opening (no heading, about 200 words)
- Hook: on 18 May 2025 Leo XIV, the first pope from the Order of St Augustine, began his first homily with "our heart is restless until it rests in you" [T-S11, T-S27]. Augustine had been dead 1,595 years. Then one year after his death: Celestine I, 431, says Rome always counted him "among the best teachers" (our English from the Latin) [T-S4].
- The central claim in two sentences.
- Kind: across the whole Latin Church; in the East, honored later and with reserve.

### 2. "What Augustine Left Us" (about 450 words)
- Possidius's list counts at least 1,030 numbered writings; more than 300 letters and almost 600 sermons survive [T-S10, 20 Feb 2008]. Shorthand writers took the sermons down, and he often revised them [T-S2]. Give the totals as "the papal count"; the Benedictine figures (270 letters, 363 sermons) count differently [T-S2].
- How sure: the main works are certain. The Retractationes (426–428) are his own review of his books, in order [T-S2].
- Start with: **Confessions** (about 400) [T-S10, T-S2]; **City of God** (413–426, after the sack of Rome) [T-S2, T-S10]; **Enchiridion** (421, for Laurentius) [T-S2]; **Letter 130 to Proba** on prayer [T-S19]. On the Trinity, named but marked as hard: he stopped dictating it because "few can understand" it (Letter 169) [T-S10].
- Free English: Schaff's Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers on New Advent [T-S2 note].

### 3. "What Augustine Taught About God" (about 1,300 words; six h3 ideas)
1. **God's grace comes first.** Words: "da quod iubes et iube quod vis" (Conf. X.29.40) [T-S17]; the will itself is prepared by the Lord (On the Gift of Perseverance) [T-S18]. Occasion: Pelagius; Carthage 412, Innocent I 417, Zosimus 418 [T-S1, T-S8 CCC 406]. Then the monks of Hadrumetum and Marseilles; Prosper wrote to him; he answered that even the first wish for faith is a gift [T-S1]. He admitted he had not seen this in his early books (Retractations I.23.3, as T-S1 cites). New? Only as Portalié 1907 says: "the first to put them in a strong light" [T-S3].
2. **All share Adam's wound.** CCC 406: the doctrine was "articulated more precisely" because of his work against Pelagius [T-S8]. Note one sentence of difference: Portalié says he was not its inventor [T-S3]; Florovsky says he formulated it [T-S23].
3. **The Church holds sinners, and Christ acts in the sacraments.** Occasion: Donatists; Carthage, June 411, 286 Catholic and 279 Donatist bishops [T-S1]. Words, short: a proud minister cannot profane Christ's gift (In Jo. ev. 5.15, CCC 1584; paraphrase) [T-S8]. Built on Cyprian and Optatus (Portalié) [T-S1].
4. **Believe in order to understand.** "I believe, in order to understand" (Sermon 43.7.9, CCC 158) [T-S8]; "If you understood him, it would not be God" (CCC 230) [T-S8]. The search began with evil: "I sought whence evil comes" (CCC 385) [T-S8]; evil comes from the misuse of freedom (City of God, as T-S1 cites). Letter 120 to Consentius [T-S2].
5. **One God in three Persons.** Pius XI gives his rule: the Persons differ in relation, but "not three gods" (De Trin.) [T-S4]. The Spirit proceeds from the Father and, by the Father's gift, from both (De Trin. 15.26.47, CCC 264) [T-S8]. He called his own analogies from the soul obscure [T-S2]. One line: the East disputes this point (see section 7).
6. **Two loves made two cities.** OW-Q11; occasion 410 [T-S10]; peace as "the tranquillity of order" (OW-Q16). The bio quotes both: here explain them, and use Benedict's question of what we may expect of God and of politics [T-S10].

### 4. "What Augustine Taught About Everyday Life" (about 900 words; five h3)
1. **How to pray.** "Learned ignorance" (Letter 130.15.28) [T-S19]; every prayer of Scripture is in the Lord's Prayer (Letter 130.12.22, CCC 2762) [T-S8]; Christ prays for us, in us, and is prayed to by us (CCC 2616) [T-S8]. Scene: a bishop writes to the widow Proba.
2. **Let love be the root.** OW-Q13 with its context (the father who corrects) [OW]. Scene: the table rule against gossip, and his anger when bishops broke it [W-S1 ch.22]. Bio has both: one sentence each, then the meaning.
3. **Sing and walk.** OW-Q12, Sermo 256.3. Scene: he wept at the hymns of Milan (OW-Q19). Short.
4. **Money held lightly.** Possidius reports what he told his people: he would rather live on their gifts than manage estates [W-S1 ch.23]. Scene: the deed sent back unasked [W-S1 ch.24]. Mark the words as reported, not his own text.
5. **A bishop who is also a Christian.** "For you I am a bishop; but with you I am a Christian" (Sermon 340.1, LG 32) [T-S9]. Scene: he wept at his ordination; Letter 21 asks for time "by praying, reading, and weeping" [OW-Q17]. Bio has the scene: one sentence.
- Also usable, one line: the justification of the sinner is greater than creation (CCC 1994) [T-S8].

### 5. "Augustine's Special Devotion" (about 400 words)
The God within. "intravi in intima mea duce te" (OW-Q22); "intus eras et ego foris" (OW-Q3). "Confessio" means both confession of sin and praise of God [T-S10]. Benedict XVI calls the Confessions "without precedent" [T-S10, 9 Jan 2008]. What was new: the claim rests on Benedict; say so. Close with the Eucharist as the bond of the one body: "O sign of unity! O bond of charity!" (CCC 1398) [T-S8].

### 6. "How Augustine Changed the Church in His Lifetime" (about 600 words)
- Councils: Hippo, 8 Oct 393 (his talk became On Faith and the Creed); Portalié says he shaped Carthage 398, 401, 407, 419 and Mileve 416, 418 [T-S1].
- A school of bishops: Possidius names ten disciples made bishops [T-S1]; "as one fire kindles another" (Possidius via Pius XI, our English) [T-S4]. Names: Alypius (Thagaste, 394), Evodius (Uzalis), Possidius (Calama) [W].
- Opponents won: Felix the Manichee, 404, signed the acts with him [T-S1]; Firmus (bio; one line). Donatism faded [T-S1].
- Pelagians: Innocent I called the African letter "full of faith" and called Augustine a dear friend [T-S4].
- Force: Portalié says he moved from debate to approving "rigid laws" (Letter 93, 408) but asked that no one be killed (Letter 100) [T-S1]. Two sentences, attributed; say that readers still argue about it.
- 426: he named Heraclius his successor to spare the city an election [T-S1].

### 7. "How Augustine's Influence Grew After Death" (about 1,100 words; six links, all DOCUMENTED)
1. **431–534: Rome defends him.** Celestine I, 431 [T-S4, T-S3]; Gelasius calls him a light of the Church's teachers [T-S4]; Hormisdas, 520: learn Rome's teaching on grace from his books, above all those to Hilary and Prosper [T-S4]; John II, 534 [T-S4].
2. **529–531: Orange.** Capitula sent by Felix IV, "drawn almost in their entirety" from Augustine and Prosper [T-S20]; conclusion: no one can love or believe God unless grace comes first [T-S5]; it rejects predestination to evil [T-S5]. Boniface II, 25 Jan 531, names "Bishop Augustine of blessed memory" [T-S6]. Say the canons do not name him.
3. **Gregory and Charlemagne.** Gregory I: read the works of your countryman, his fine wheat beside our bran (Reg. X.37, our English) [T-S4]. Einhard: Charlemagne liked his books read at meals, above all City of God [T-S16].
4. **1298 and the Rule.** Boniface VIII names the four Latin Doctors [CM-S4]. His writings were called "The Rule of St. Augustine" in eighth- and ninth-century codices [T-S14]; canons took it up after 1059 [T-S14, T-S15]; Premonstratensians and Gilbertines [T-S15]; Hermits of St Augustine united 1256 (bull of 4 May) [T-S14]. Pius XI: many congregations follow it today [T-S4 para 47].
5. **Trent and the long quarrels.** CCC 406 links Orange (529) and Trent (1546) on original sin [T-S8]. Luther was an Augustinian friar from 1505 (membership only) [T-S13, T-S15]. Jansenius's "Augustinus" (1640) was condemned in 1641 and 1653 [T-S12]; in 1690 Alexander VIII condemned holding a teaching only because it is "clearly based on Augustine" [T-S3]. Lesson: the Church keeps him and reads him through its own judgment. Benedict XIV, 1748, leaves the schools free [T-S3].
6. **The East, slowly and with reserve.** Little of him was in Greek: On the Trinity by Planoudes before 1281 [T-S22]; Confessions and City of God only in the 20th century [T-S24]. Nikodemos's Synaxarion praises him as a great teacher [T-S25]. Nektarios loved a Greek book under his name that was not his [T-S24]. Gennadius criticized his teaching on the Spirit [T-S21, T-S25]. Romanides judged him harshly (attributed, one sentence) [T-S21]. Tell it without taking a side.
- Unifying fact: the Ten Commandments numbering in Catholic and Lutheran teaching is his; the Orthodox follow the Greek Fathers' division (CCC 2065–2066) [T-S8]. Place it in link 5 or in section 8.
- CLAIMED, attributed only, never a link: Portalié on Anselm, Lombard, Aquinas, Bernard [T-S3]; Isidore, Bede, Alcuin [T-S3].

### 8. "Augustine in the Church Today" (about 500 words)
- Doctor of Grace: Roman Missal Preface I of Saints (En. in Ps. 102.7, via CCC) [T-S8]; Pius XI, "Ad salutem humani", 20 Apr 1930 [T-S4].
- Readings from him in the liturgy of Christ the King, added by Pius XI [T-S4 para 26].
- Catechism: quoted often; examples CCC 158, 1398, 2066, 2616 [T-S8]. LG 32, 1964 [T-S9].
- Benedict XVI, 2008: "the greatest Father of the Latin Church" [T-S10]. Leo XIV, 2025: the restless heart and Sermon 359.9 [T-S11].
- Feasts: 28 August; 15 June in the East, where the OCA lists "Blessed Augustine" [T-S26]. Two sentences on the mixed Orthodox view, pointing to section 7.
- Living communities: the Order of St Augustine and the congregations under the Rule [T-S27, T-S4].

## 5. What we leave out

- Relics, Pavia, the child by the sea: the biography covers them.
- Healings at Stephen's relics: these belong in the miracle list.
- Patron of brewers and printers: no source.
- "Pray as though everything depended on God", "The world is a book", "In essentials unity", "Solvitur ambulando": not his, or not found.
- Charlemagne's idea of rule shaped by City of God: not found.
- Dominicans and Servites under the Rule; Teresa, Pascal, Newman: only leads.
- Bradwardine, Wyclif, Bessarion, Gottschalk, Descartes: CE only, and minor.
- Seed-reasons and the "fixity of species": the CE reading; too easy to misread as science.
- Barlaam and the 14th-century councils: Romanides's opinion, too detailed for the essay.

## 6. Accuracy notes

- Romanides says Orange condemned Augustine's teaching. This conflicts with T-S5, T-S6, and T-S20. Never state it as fact; it may appear only as his opinion.
- Do not repeat these CE slips: De Moribus "368", "Galatians (324)", "eighteen months' siege" (Possidius: almost fourteen). Credit CE judgments ("philosophy of history", "first to synthesize") to Portalié, 1907.
- Conflict with the biography: it says Alexander IV united the Hermits in 1254. T-S14 gives 1256 (meeting 1 March, bull 4 May). Use 1256 and flag it to the editor.
- Dates of works: Confessions "about 400" (T-S10 397–400; the bio "about 401"). On the Trinity: "finished about 420" [T-S10].
- Trent and the Orange canons do not name him. Credit "his very words" to Pius XI.
- Teachings set aside: punishment of unbaptized children (Auctorem fidei, 1794) and wording that made concupiscence the same as sin (Trent V) [T-S3, T-S7]. Say this plainly.
- Rule: say "a rule that goes back to his house and Letter 211"; authorship is debated [T-S14, T-S15].
- The Meditations and the Kekragarion are not his [T-S2, T-S24]. The Athanasian Creed is not his [T-S3].
- Luther: give only his order membership; the doctrinal link is disputed. Jansenists claimed him, and Rome rejected their reading.
- Names: Augustine of Hippo; "Doctor of Grace"; Pius XI, "Ad salutem humani"; OCA "Blessed Augustine"; Leo XIV is the first pope from the Order of St Augustine.
