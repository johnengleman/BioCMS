# Nicholas of Myra — plan

## The driving question

Nicholas left no writings, so we cannot follow his inner drama the way we follow Thérèse's. The drama is instead in the memory of him. Almost nothing about the man is historically certain, yet he became the saint whom East and West call on in danger. How did that happen, and who was he? The answer: the stories come in layers over a thousand years, and every layer, from the oldest to the latest, shows the same man. He runs toward people in danger. He gives in secret. He calls himself "a sinner". And when people call him, he comes. The hook raises the question with the oldest story (a bishop running to take the sword from an executioner). The ending answers it with the sailors' line, "you have called me, and I am here to help you", and with the people who still call on him at Bari and across the Orthodox world.

## Why he is famous

He is one of the most venerated saints in both Churches, patron of sailors, children, and many lands, and the distant source of Santa Claus.

## What set him apart

His oldest story is about justice: a bishop who stops an execution and tells a governor that the real criminal is bribe money. Every later layer adds a rescue, the gift, the grain, and the sea, so he became the saint of anyone in trouble.

## The hook

The scene at Byrra from the Praxis de stratelatis (strength A-B): the crowd, the three men with linen over their faces, the raised sword, and the bishop running. Then the paradox: the oldest story has no gold and no Santa Claus, and the Catholic Encyclopedia says "scarcely anything historically certain" is known of him. The story returns to Byrra in chapter 6 and answers the question in the last chapter.

Eight block quotes.

## Open loops

1. "Who was he?" (opening) — answered in the last chapter: the same man in every layer.
2. "A sinner": the name he gives himself at the church door returns in the emperor's dream.
3. The secret oath at the window — answered at the end: the one wish the world has refused him.
4. The three officers at Myra in chapter 7 — they return as prisoners in chapter 8.

## Length

The notes honestly support about 4,300 words, below the tier B range of 5,000–8,000. The Bari translation and the Praxis carry the most detail. The early life is thin and stays short. Nicholas left no writings, so there is no inner life to draw on.
