# Nicholas of Myra — research notes (tier B, one agent)

Date of research: 2026-10-01. Read in this session: items marked in Sources. Not read: original Greek or Latin (Anrich, Mombritius, Surius, Migne PG 116), Nicephorus/John the Deacon in Latin (only English translations), Theodore the Lector, Gelzer's Nicaea lists, Jones 1978 book, Cioffari 1987. Every Greek/Latin claim below is through an English translation or a scholar's summary.

## 1. Identity

- Names: Nicholas of Myra; Nicholas of Patara (birthplace); Nicholas the Wonderworker (Orthodox "Thaumaturge"); St Nicholas of Bari (West, from the relics); Nikolaos, Nikolai Chudotvorets; Italian San Nicola. [S1, S2, S10]
- Dates: born Patara, Lycia, "about 270" by tradition [S5]; died 6 Dec, year given as 342 (Butler, from the Bari translation history), 343 (Golden Legend), "345 or 352" (Catholic Encyclopedia, Sanidopoulos). No firm date. [S1 p.92, S4, S6, S5]
- Place: Patara (birth); Myra (bishop; Turkish Demre today per S5). Relics at Bari since 9 May 1087.
- Tradition type: SHARED (Catholic and Orthodox). Orthodox: one of the most venerated saints after the Mother of God and John the Baptist [S5]. Catholic: Roman Martyrology entry; named in Roman calendar (no first-hand text read for the calendar; see Other).
- Feast days (as stated in sources read): 6 December (repose) and 9 May (translation of relics to Bari) in both traditions [S5, S17]. Russian Church also keeps 29 July, "Nativity of St Nicholas", revived 2004 under Patriarch Alexis II [S17]. St Nikodemos of Mount Athos wrote a service for the Translation, to be chanted 10 May; Greek lands celebrate the translation on varied days (9, 10, 20 May) [S18]. Old-calendar equivalents (19 Dec, 22 May): NOT sourced here, see Other.
- Research tier: B. Early sources are thin and late. The honest core is: bishop of Myra, 4th century, a cult by the 5th-6th century.

## 3. Timeline (date, event, detail, strength, source)

STRENGTH KEY: A = early and independent; B = early but one witness or mixed with Nicholas of Sion; C = 9th-10th c. biography; D = late legend.

- c.270, Patara: birth. Only son of rich Christian parents. Source: Michael (c.820-840, strength C). Names Theophanes and Nonna come from Dimitri of Rostov, 17th c., via S17: strength D. Michael gives no parent names. [S2 sec.3-4, S17]
- Infancy: refuses the breast on Wednesdays and Fridays, or takes it once. Michael's own monastic motif. Cioffari calls it a "monastic commonplace" [S2 sec.5; S10 "monastic commonplaces"; S1 p.92]. Strength D, legend.
- Dowry of the three daughters: Michael sec.10-18 (c.820-840) is the first full telling. Nicholas, rich after his parents die, throws three bags of gold through a window at night; the father stays awake for the third and catches him; Nicholas makes him swear silence. Cioffari: three recensions (Sinaitic, Byzantine, Ethiopic) are "very ancient and very different", so the core is old. The oldest (Sinaitic) has two girls and a boy Nicholas who steals from living parents; Ethiopic has four girls; Byzantine/Western has three. [S10; S2; S7 "Story of the Dowry Gold" cited PG 116 328A in S5]. No separate "Praxis de tribus filiabus" text read by me; treat the name as unverified. Strength B-C. Core plausible but not provable.
- Election as bishop of Myra: previous bishop dies; one bishop dreams: stand at church door at night, the first man to enter is Nicholas. He answers "Nicholas, a sinner, a servant of your Holiness" [S2 sec.21-24, S7]. Cioffari reads a layman elected by acclamation as likely true, because Gratian's Decretum states it [S10]. Strength B-C. (Butler: abbot of Holy Sion, made archbishop; this is the Nicholas of Sion mix, not in Michael's election story, S1 p.92.)
- Pilgrimage to Holy Land and the storm at sea: found in Vita Compilata (a merge of Michael with Nicholas of Sion): a ship from Ascalon, captain Mena, "a favorable wind blowing", Jerusalem "in a few days" [S14 sec.21]. This episode belongs to Nicholas of Sion (d. 564 or later, see Other). Metaphrastes added "the calming of the storm and the resurrection from death of the sailor Ammonios" per S11. Strength D for Myra's Nicholas. Michael has NO storm calming in the pilgrimage.
- Sailors who call on his name (Michael sec.34-36, c.820-840): sailors in a storm cry out; "Behold, you have called me, and I am here to help you"; later they find him in church and recognize him; he rebukes their sins. Cioffari: more fitting to Nicholas of Sion, but the origin of the sea patronage [S2, S10]. Strength C. The story is a posthumous-style vision while he is alive (he is in his church); it is not a "stilling" by his own command.
- The three generals and the three innocents (Praxis de stratelatis): the "earliest known" Life fragment [S3 intro, "about AD 400" per Sanidopoulos from Jones]. Dating disputed: Anrich 460-580; Cioffari between Nicholas's death and mid-5th c. [S11]. Eustratios of Constantinople (after 583) quotes it [S11]. Proclus Encomium (maybe 440, maybe later; Anrich says not by Proclus) retells it [S11, S12]. Roman Martyrology cites this story alone [S3 note 1]. Strength A-B, the best attested story. Two parts: (a) Nicholas runs to the place of execution (Byrra), seizes the sword from the executioner, frees three condemned men, confronts governor Eustathius: "It is not Eudoxius and Simonides who did this, but silver and gold" [S3; paraphrase of S3, text read via Jones translation]; (b) three officers (Nepotianus, Ursus, Eupoleonis) later framed in Constantinople; Nicholas appears in dreams to Constantine and to prefect Ablabius: "I am Nicholas, a sinner, servant of God. I am the Metropolitan in Myra." Emperor frees them [S3]. NEW: popular pages skip the detail of 200 pounds of silver and 1,700 pounds of gold bribe [S3]. Historical hooks: Nepotianus and Ablavius are known to Zosimos and Eunapios; Cioffari accepts the frame, calls the dream-visits hagiographic devices [S10].
- Burning of temple of Artemis at Myra (Michael sec.28-29): Cioffari notes the Myra Artemis temple being the tallest is supported by Opramoas inscriptions [S10]. Michael adds that demons flee. Strength C, with an archaeological check on the setting.
- Famine and grain ships (Michael sec.37-39, also Andrew of Crete early 8th c.): Alexandrian grain ships at Andriake; Nicholas asks each to unload 100 measures, promising indemnity; grain arrives in Constantinople unchanged; feeds Myra two years [S2]. Andrew of Crete's version: Nicholas stands by the sailors "in the manner of a sailor" and leaves "three gold coins" on the ship [S13]. Cioffari: "too generic", maybe indirect, per Jones in circulation in the 6th c. [S10]. Strength B-C.
- Council of Nicaea, 325: see section 8 (disputed).
- Praxis de tributo (tax relief; Nicholas goes to Constantinople, prays all night at Blachernae): manuscripts 11th c.; Anrich and Falconius date circulation to end of 7th or 8th c. Language ("Osios") points to the monk Nicholas of Sion; Blachernae built c.450 [S10]. Strength D for Myra.
- Death, 6 December: Michael says only that he "left his mortal life", body buried in church, "gushed forth a sweet-smelling perfume" (sec.41) [S2]. Metaphrastes: short illness, died chanting thanksgivings, borne by bishops and clergy with torches, laid in the temenos at Myra [S7]. Golden Legend: he prayed for angels, saw them, recited Psalm 30 ("In te Domine speravi") to "in manus tuas", died 343; oil from head to feet [S4]. Strength C-D.
- Post-death cult: Justinian built a church in his honor in Blachernae (so Butler p.91: "about the year 430"; Sanidopoulos says reign of Justinian 527-565; Conflict: Butler's 430 cannot be Justinian) [S1 p.91; S5]. Sinai icon 7th-8th c. [S10 caption]. Oldest fresco: Santa Maria Antiqua, Rome, early to mid 8th c. [meth page caption, S read via Methodius intro]. Andrew of Crete homily early 8th c. delivered at the tomb per S5 (Migne PG 97). Proclus encomium: see above.
- 1036: Lycia occupied by Saracens (per S5; date not checked elsewhere). Greek Anon.: monks fled "twelve stadia into the mountains" for fear of the Persians [S8; the source says Persians, others say Turks: see conflicts].
- 20 April to 9 May 1087, translation to Bari: Greek Anon. narrative: citizens of Bari, headed for Antioch with grain and merchandise, planned to take the relics; Venetians planned the same, with "iron instruments" [S9]. Forty-seven armed men went from the ships to the shrine; four custodian monks; a Bari man, Matthew, drew his sword on a monk, demanding the tomb [S9, S8]. The monk told them of a vision a year earlier [S9 "Confrontation and a Second Vision"]. Priest Lupus dropped a glass vial of the saint's oil on the stones and it did not break: taken as consent [S8]. Matthew smashed the lid with a hammer; coffin "full of holy liquid up to the umbilicus" [S9]. The perfume reached the other company 3 miles away at the ships [S9]. Relics delayed at Perdikca until five sailors returned stolen fragments (sworn on the Gospels) [S9]. A sailor, Disigius, dreamed: "At the end of twenty days we will all arrive together at the city of Bari" [S9, paraphrase]. Arrived Bari 9 May 1087 [S8 sec.19]; first cures that night: 47 persons (Greek Anon. and Nicephorus); Butler says 30 cured on the first day (conflict) [S8 sec.20, S9, S1 p.94]. Strength A for the event: Nicephorus within about a week (per S9 intro), John the Archdeacon of Bari independently (S1 p.94, via Butler). Strength D for the sensory details, since the 13th-c. Greek Anon. is later.
- 1100: Venetian fleet under Bishop Enrico Contarini took remaining bones from Myra per S15 (Wikipedia, tertiary); S8 notes also say the Venetians accomplished a translation in 1100 [S8 note 3, partial text]. Butler p.94: Venetians took only what the Bari men left, two other bishops (Theodorus and another Nicholas) and some oil [S1 p.94].
- 1953-1957: relics of Bari re-examined and placed in a new tomb in the crypt; manna "continues" to flow [S5]. Not verified from a second source.

## 4. Character

- Gives in secret; makes the father swear silence about the gold [S2 sec.17].
- Runs. In the generals story he is "now running" to the execution field [S3 paraphrase].
- Fierce toward corrupt officials, then forgives: after threatening Eustathius with the emperor, he pardons the repentant governor at the officers' request [S3].
- Humble when named: "a sinner" in three separate stories (election, Constantine dream, Ablabius dream) [S2, S3].
- Shows up unadorned in church "like one of the many clergy" so sailors do not at first know him [S2 sec.35].
- Teacher who scolds: tells rescued sailors their greed and lust invite divine correction [S2 sec.36].
- Cioffari: the quick-tempered side "was well in tone with Nicholas's character" with Eustratios (the governor) and Bishop Theognis [S10]. Cioffari reasons Nicholas hated prefect Ablavius while Athanasius admired him, so their silence about each other is explicable [S10]. Treat that as his argument, not as fact.
- Orthodoxy and doctrine are a Michael motif; the speech on the Trinity is formulaic.

## 6. Scenes (all marked by strength)

1. The father at the window (Michael sec.16-17): the third night, the father stays awake and catches Nicholas by the gold's noise, falls at his feet. Nicholas lifts him and swears him to silence. Strength B-C.
2. Dawn at the church door (sec.22-23): the bishops pray all night, the first man in is a young man who calls himself a sinner. Strength C.
3. Running to Byrra (Praxis): the crowd, the executioner with sword raised, three men with linen over their faces; Nicholas takes the sword and throws it down. Strength A-B.
4. The pretorium (Praxis): Nicholas pushes the door open, refuses the governor's honor, names "silver and gold" as the real culprits. Strength A-B.
5. Dreams of Constantine and Ablabius (Praxis): "how did you get into my palace?" and the vanishing; emperor asks officers what magic they use. Strength B.
6. Grain ships at Andriake (Michael; Andrew of Crete): hundred measures; "three gold coins" left behind per Andrew. Strength B-C.
7. Sailors in the church (Michael sec.35): the men recognize him by "his manifestation and help" despite plain dress. Strength C.
8. Breaking the tomb at Myra (Nicephorus; Greek Anon.): vial of oil does not break, mallet, fragrance, the monks' lament. Strength A for the event, D for details.
9. Bari, 9 May 1087: crowd, quarrel with archbishop Ursus, relics carried through the harbor gate to the praetorium; altar of St Eustratius torn down for his. [S8 sec.16-18; S9 "The Sailors' Pledge"]. Strength A-B.
10. Perdikca (Nicephorus): ship stuck in calm; five sailors admit taking relic fragments. Strength A (Nicephorus); eyewitness.

## 7. Miracles (one per line: date, place, person, report, reporter, status)

- Dowry gold: undated, Patara or Myra, three daughters and their father; Michael c.820-840 (earlier recensions per Cioffari); popular/hagiographic; not Church-approved as an event, but in liturgical texts per S5.
- Three innocents saved at Myra: 4th c.; Myra; Eustathius; Praxis (5th-6th c., Cioffari: earlier); the only one named in the Roman Martyrology [S3 note 1]; Church-approved only in the sense of liturgical use.
- Three generals saved through dreams: Constantinople, under Constantine; Nepotianus, Ursus, Eupoleonis; Praxis, Eustratios (after 583) [S11], Proclus; same status.
- Sailors rescued by his appearance: Michael; popular.
- Grain ships multiply: Andriake; Michael; Andrew of Crete: popular.
- Temple of Artemis destroyed: Myra; Michael; popular.
- Demon's oil flask thrown into sea: pilgrims to Myra; Michael sec.44-48 (posthumous); popular.
- Fragrant oil from the tomb: Myra from burial; Michael, Metaphrastes; physical phenomenon reported for centuries; Emperor Manuel's novella mentions "a fragrant unctuous matter" at Bari (per Butler p.94, citing Balsamon) [S1]. Status: recorded by the shrine at Bari.
- Oil in the tomb in 1087: coffin full of liquid [S9]. Recorded by eyewitness Nicephorus.
- Vial of oil not breaking, Lupus: Myra, April 1087; Greek Anon., Nicephorus; popular.
- 47 cured at Bari, first night: Bari, 9-10 May 1087; Greek Anon. and Nicephorus (Butler: 30, conflict) [S8, S9, S1].
- Young man possessed five years, deaf, dumb, blind, healed on "Thursday": Bari, May 1087; Nicephorus [S9].
- Manna of Bari: 9 May yearly, clergy collect the liquid, and "continues" after 1953-57 restoration per S5; shrine-recorded; not independently examined in sources I read.

## 8. Other

- NICAEA (conflict among sources, mark clearly):
  - Michael's Life (c.820-840) mentions Arius and Sabellius as doctrines Nicholas opposed but does NOT mention Nicaea; I grepped the full translation [S2 sec.25-26].
  - Earliest mention of Nicaea in his biography: Nicetas of Paphlagonia eulogy, before 890; Synaxarion life c.900 adds prison and Nicaea [S11]. Metaphrastes (975-1000): "The admirable Nicholas helped to bring this about as a member of the sacred synod" [S7].
  - Lists: Catholic Encyclopedia (1911) says his name is in no old list [S6]. Cioffari: "lack of his name in the most ancient lists" but Theodore the Lector (c.515, Historia Tripartita) names him [S10]. Wikipedia: he appears on three early lists; Theodore's is "generally considered the most accurate"; short lists (c.200 names) omit him, long lists (c.300) include him; Wikipedia says 151st in Theodore [S15, tertiary]. Pearse: Anrich says Theodore's entry is a later interpolation, Cioffari says authentic [S11]. Cioffari guesses Theodore took it from the ancient Life quoted by Eustratios in 583 [S10].
  - Not named by Athanasius or Eusebius [S15].
  - SO: the instruction "not in the lists" is true for the oldest, shorter lists; false as a flat statement. He is on Theodore's list, whose authenticity is disputed. Present as "disputed; first attested c.515 at the earliest."
- SLAP OF ARIUS: Cioffari treats it as one of two "popular developments" (with the brick-and-flame Trinity speech) [S10]. Nicholas Center says legend "over 500 years old" [S16]; Wikipedia says first attested in the 14th century [S15, tertiary]. Neither Michael nor Metaphrastes (as excerpted) nor the Golden Legend text I read mention a slap. LATE LEGEND, D. The jail, vision of Jesus with the Gospels and Mary with an omophorion are told in S16 (popular; undated source).
- NICHOLAS OF SION CONFUSION: Life of Nicholas of Sion (c.564) was mixed into Nicholas of Myra's biography by the Vita Compilata and Metaphrastes [S5, S11, S10, S1 p.91 n.]. Butler: Falconius (1751) claimed they were the same; Assemani and Putignani refuted this, Nicholas of Pinara died 699 [S1 p.93 note]. Sion elements: pilgrimage to Jerusalem, abbot of Holy Sion, storm calming, healings.
- CONFLICTS: death year 342/343/345/352; Persians vs Turks as the threat in 1087 [S8, S9: Turks]; Butler 30 cured vs 47; Sanidopoulos service page says "1081" for the Translation but 1087 is correct in all other sources [S18 vs S1, S5, S8, S9]; Butler dates Justinian's church to about 430, Sanidopoulos to 527-565.
- DIOCLETIAN IMPRISONMENT: Greek histories "agree" he was imprisoned and confessed in Diocletian's persecution, "The silence of other authors make many justly suspect" [S1 p.92]. First seen in Synaxarion c.900 per S11. Late.
- PATRONAGE (CE 1911): Greece, Russia, Kingdom of Naples, Sicily, Lorraine, Liège, Campen, Corfu, Freiburg, Moscow; mariners, merchants, bakers, travellers, children [S6]. Sanidopoulos: travelers and sailors, from Myra as stopover for pilgrims bound for the Holy Land [S5]. Santa Claus: derived "at some remove" per Pearse; the dowry gold story explains gift-giving [S5, S11, S6]. Three gold balls: Golden Legend heading "Three Gold Balls" exists (line not read in full).
- RELIC FACTS: Archbishop of Bari then was Ursus (Greek Anon. and Butler's account [S8]); abbot Elias of St Benedict held the relics temporarily; later Pope Urban II deposited them under the main altar of the new basilica (S5, who says a "magnificent basilica"; not seen in S8 text I read). Sailors' privilege: burial along basilica wall; a bone with the saint's face carved, in the Poveromo family (family tradition; S9 notes).
- RUSSIA: Bari translation witnessed by Theodore, envoy of Metropolitan John II of Kiev; his "Skazanie" is a Russian source (per S5). Not read.
- IMAGE CANDIDATES (not checked for rights): Repin, "St Nicholas saving three innocents" (1888, Russian Museum, S20 caption); fresco at Sucevita Monastery, Romania (S20); Sinai icon 7th-8th c. and Santa Maria Antiqua fresco early-mid 8th c. (S10 caption, Methodius page caption); Boguslawski illuminations (St Nicholas Center collection).
- TO CHECK (no source read): Old-calendar dates (19 Dec; 22 May); Roman calendar rank of 6 Dec; Latin of John the Deacon's account; whether the Greek manuscript Praxis de tribus filiabus exists as a named text (Cioffari mentions the Sinaitic MS and Ethiopic recension); Venetian 1100 voyage in a primary source; Andrew of Crete's mention of Bishop Theognis (Cioffari's summary [S10]; I did not find it in the translation I read); Gelzer's lists; Eustratios 583 text.
