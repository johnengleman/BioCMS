# Thomas Aquinas — witnesses notes (final, session 2)

Focus: people who knew Thomas, the 1319 Naples process, the oldest Lives. Latin quotations are copied from OCR of Prümmer and Laurent (only OCR long-s and spacing fixed). English lines are our own paraphrase or translation. Page numbers are printed pages of the Fontes edition.
Marks: LEG = pious story, no eyewitness. EARLY = written within about 45 years. SWORN = deposed in 1319. HEARSAY = sworn but second or third hand.

## 2. Identity (witness level)
- Born near Roccasecca to Landulf, Count of Aquino, and Theodora [S1 Tocco p.66-67]. Tocco: died in "anno vitae suae XLIX" [S1 Tocco p.138], so born c.1225. Ptolemy: "Obiit quinquagesimo vitae suae anno; alii dicunt XLIV" [S1 Calo p.45 n.5]. Gui: bachelor at Paris aged 27 in 1252, master aged "fere XXXI" in 1254 (Prümmer: error) [S1 Gui p.257]. Eyewitness James of Caiazzo: Thomas "poterat esse quatraginta sex annorum vel circa" when first seen [S2 n.42 p.319].
- Died "septimo die Martii, hora matutinali" 1274 at the Cistercian abbey of Fossanova [S1 Tocco p.138].
- Family: brother Rainald killed by Frederick II; brother Landulf; niece Francesca (daughter of brother Philip) wife of Annibaldo of Ceccano; sister Countess of San Severino (Theodora in Gui [S1 Gui p.194]; Mary in Laurent's note [S2 p.350 n.b]); sister abbess of Santa Maria at Capua; sister killed by lightning in infancy.
- Catholic. Canonized 18 Jul 1323 [S1 Tocco p.58]. Feast 7 March (older calendar).

## 3. Timeline
- c.1225 Birth. Lightning strikes the tower at Roccasecca; his sister, asleep in the tower, and the horses in the stable die; Theodora runs trembling to the bed where nurse and baby sleep and finds him safe. [S1 Tocco p.67] LEG. Theodora herself was Tocco's source for the early years: "ex fideli narratione matris eius est habitum" [S1 Tocco p.69].
- Before birth: hermit Brother Bonus of the mountain tells Theodora her son will seem a monk-abbot to the parents but will be a Dominican without equal. [S1 Tocco p.66-67; S1 Calo p.18] LEG. HEARSAY: Lady Catherine of Morra, his great-niece, swore in Feb 1318 before Tocco, a judge and a notary that she heard it from Theodora [S2 n.62 p.350]. NEW (the sworn chain).
- Infancy: Ave Maria paper in his fist; he cries until given a scrap of writing. Tocco sets it at a public bath in Naples; Gui at home [S1 Tocco p.67-68; Prümmer's comparison S1 Tocco p.59-60]. LEG. NEW (two versions).
- c.1230 Age 5, to Monte Cassino with a master; withdraws from other noble boys with a lesson sheet in hand; quiet, "non verbis garrulus". [S1 Tocco p.69] Bartholomew of Capua: his father "monachavit" him intending the abbacy [S2 n.76 p.371]. Prümmer rejects the oblate reading [S1 Tocco p.71 n.1].
- Naples schooling under Martin (grammar, logic) and Peter of Ireland (natural science); a Dominican sees three times a ray of light from the boy's face. [S1 Tocco p.70] LEG/EARLY.
- c.1240 Enters the Dominicans at Naples. Fr John of San Giuliano "monuit" him (Tocco); Fr Thomas Agni of Lentini, prior, gave the habit (Gui); Bartholomew: John "dicebatur notorie" to have received him [S1 Tocco p.71; S1 Gui p.171; S2 p.372]. Gui: c.1240, aged 14 [S1 Gui p.257].
- Kidnap: Theodora comes to Naples; the friars send him via Terracina and Anagni to Rome (Santa Sabina) to hide him and plan Paris; she sends a messenger to his brothers with Frederick II at Acquapendente "sub maternae benedictionis obtentu"; the brothers get the emperor's leave, post guards on the roads, find him "juxta cujusdam fontis irrigua quiescentem cum quatuor Fratribus", attack "non ut fratres, sed ut hostes", fail to tear off the habit he "fortiter tenebat" [S1 Tocco p.72-73; S1 Gui p.172]. Calo: by a well [S1 Calo p.21]. Ptolemy: Rainald with Peter della Vigna took him from Master John the Teuton, who was leading him to Rome [S3 XXII.20]. NEW (Peter della Vigna).
- Confinement at Monte San Giovanni and Roccasecca. Tocco and Gui: the mother holds him until her sons return. Bartholomew (sworn, thirdhand): the FATHER held him "plusquam per annum" in the paternal castle and relented at his wife's prayers [S2 p.372]. CONFLICT. Length: Tocco "fere per duos annos"; Cantimpré "annis duobus vel tribus"; Gerard "quasi per annum"; Ptolemy "longo tempore" [S1 Calo p.24 n.4].
- Innocent IV, then in Tuscany, orders the emperor to punish; Frederick seizes the brothers; the friars drop the case for fear of scandal [S1 Tocco p.73]. Cantimpré: Master John feared "judicium sanguinis" and softened the emperor [S1 Calo p.22 n.1]. EARLY.
- Prison: Bible, Sentences, Aristotle's fallacies "ut dicitur" [S1 Tocco p.74]; he asked at once for Bible and breviary [S2 p.372]; sisters taught, one converted and later abbess [S1 Tocco p.74]. Brothers tear his habit; he wraps himself in the shreds [S1 Tocco p.74]. Fr John of San Giuliano visits and swaps tunics for him in secret [S1 Tocco p.76].
- Temptation: a lovely girl in courtesan dress is shut in his room; he seizes a burning brand, drives her out, burns a cross on the wall with it, prays, sleeps; two angels gird him; he cries out in pain and tells no one until near death, then Reginald [S1 Tocco p.75-76; S1 Gui p.173; S1 Calo p.23-24]. SWORN HEARSAY: Tocco in 1319, from Fr Robert of Sezze via his uncle Stephen [S2 n.61 p.349]. Cantimpré (c.1260) says only that "mulieres" were shut in and Thomas stood "fortius quam prius" [S1 Calo p.22 n.1]. EARLY.
- Release: mother "dissimulata prudenter" lets him be lowered by rope to waiting friars [S1 Tocco p.77]. Ptolemy: Thomas himself arranged it, with friars at the wall at night [S3 XXII.21]. CONFLICT. Tocco: he left prison "quasi diu mansisset in studio scholasticae disciplinae" [S1 Tocco p.77]. Gui dates the release c.1242, aged 16 [S1 Gui p.257].
- Cantimpré's contemporary version is flawed: Thomas entered at Bologna, was cited to the pope, told to drop the habit and take the abbacy of Monte Cassino, fled the curia and went to Cologne [S4 lib.1 cap.20]. EARLY.
- Then Rome, Master John the Teuton, Paris, Cologne [S1 Tocco p.77]. Tocco in 1319 mentions only Cologne [S2 p.351]. Ptolemy: aged 25 when he first came to Paris [S3 XXII.21].
- Cologne under Albert: silent, studious, devout; fellows call him "bovem mutum"; a student offers to repeat Albert's lecture on Dionysius's Divine Names and falters; Thomas fills it in and begs secrecy; the student tells Albert; a notebook page is found; next day Albert sets him a hard question, then four arguments; Albert's remark [S1 Tocco p.77-79]. LATE: first written c.1317-1323, about 70 years later; Tocco hedges "fertur"; not in the 1319 testimonies.
- c.1252 Bachelor at Paris after Albert wrote to the Master of the Order; the Master hesitated until Cardinal Hugh urged it [S1 Tocco p.80-81]. c.1256 Mastership: Thomas pleads youth; the Chancellor orders it; prays with Ps 11 "Salvum me fac"; sleeps; an old Dominican gives him the text "Rigans montes" [S1 Tocco p.84-85]. Gerard de Frachet (before 1260) tells it as a dream of someone giving him a book with the verse [S1 Tocco p.85 n.1]. EARLY. Thomas told it to the prior of Fossanova at Maenza in 1274 [S2 n.49 p.331].
- Paris: John Peckham baits him; the Paris joke; Louis's table; dead sister and Romanus visions [S2 p.374, 376; S1 Tocco p.116-119].
- 1260 Chapter of Naples: he is made "predicator generalis" ("qui ex Parisio redierat magister in theologia"). 14 Sep 1261 Chapter of Orvieto: "Assignamus fr. Thomam de Aquino pro lectore in conventu Urbevetano". 1265 Chapter of Anagni: "teneat studium Rome" [S2 p.582-583 Documenta].
- Orvieto/Rome years: Ptolemy travelled with him from Rome and stopped at Molara, castle of Cardinal Richard; Thomas had tertian fever, his companion continuous fever; Thomas sent his companion the relics of St Agnes he wore from Rome; the sick man recovered [S1 Calo p.45 n.5 quoting S3 XXIII.10]. EYEWITNESS. Tocco in 1319 told it at second hand [S2 n.60 p.348]. Rome: Holy Week sermons at St Peter's, woman with flux touches his cloak [S1 Tocco p.126-127]. Clement IV offers the archbishopric of Naples and the abbey of San Pietro ad Aram; Thomas refuses and begs not to be promoted [S1 Tocco p.116; S3 XXII.39].
- 1272 Chapter of Florence "committimus plenarie fr. Thome de Aquino" the general study at Naples. 2 Oct 1272: royal letter names him executor of the will of Roger of Aquila (his nephew, Count of Fondi). 15 Oct 1272: Charles I of Anjou orders one ounce of gold a month for "frater Thomas de Aquino ... aput Neapolim in theologia legere" [S2 p.578-580, 583]. 3 Apr 1273: passport for niece Francesca of Ceccano to go to the baths of Pozzuoli [S2 p.581]. 29 Sep 1273 Chapter of Rome: he is a definitor [S2 p.583].
- Naples 1272-74: Mass at dawn in St Nicholas chapel; another Mass; lecture; writing and dictating; dinner; cell; writing again [S2 n.77 p.374]. Bartholomew "nunquam recordatur quod viderit eum extra claustrum, nisi semel, tantum circa vesperas" [S2 p.374]. Lent 1273: daily sermons on the Our Father; "quasi totus populus civitatis Neapolis" comes [S2 n.87 p.391]. John Blasi heard a whole Lent on the Hail Mary, Thomas preaching "oculis clausis" [S2 n.70 p.362].
- December 1273 (about the feast of St Nicholas, 6 Dec, my inference from Reginald's words): Mass in St Nicholas chapel; "mira mutatione commotus"; writing stops in Part III, on Penance [S2 n.79 p.376-377]. Tocco, Calo and Gui put his "end of writing" speech at San Severino, Reginald tugging his cape [S1 Tocco p.120; S1 Gui p.194, "per triduum fere"].
- Talking crucifix: Tocco, Calo, Gui report the sacristan saw Thomas raised two cubits in prayer in St Nicholas chapel and heard the image speak [S1 Tocco p.108; S1 Calo p.38; S1 Gui p.189]. LATE/PIOUS: no 1319 witness tells it (searched "crucifix" in S2). Calo names the sacristan James of Caserta; Tocco and Gui name Dominic of Caserta. Tocco: after it Thomas "parum scripsit".
- Early 1274 Lyon journey: summoned by Gregory X, carrying the treatise against the Greeks [S1 Tocco p.129]. Near Teano, descending "per viam Burgi Novi", he strikes his head on a fallen tree and is half stunned [S2 n.78 p.375]. After the Naples Mass Bartholomew places a visit to his sister the Countess of San Severino ("vix locutus est") [S2 p.377]. He falls ill at Maenza (Magenza), castle of niece Francesca and Annibaldo of Ceccano [S2 n.8 p.276; n.49 p.330]. Prior James of Fossanova and monks Peter, John of Pedemonte and Fidelis ride to Maenza and stay 4-5 days [S2 p.330-331]. Herring story there (Miracles).
- To Fossanova: "melius est quod reperiar in domo religiosorum quam in domibus secularium"; he rides or is carried about six miles and lies there "quasi per mensem" [S2 n.8 p.277]. Tocco says he was going "versus Romam" [S1 Tocco p.130].
- At the entrance (parlour or cloister): "Hec requies mea in seculum seculi" [S1 Tocco p.130; S2 p.277, 332]. Monks carry logs "propriis humeris" [S2 p.277]. He briefly expounds the Song of Songs at the monks' request [S1 Tocco p.131]. He tells Reginald that God has granted that no dignity change him [S1 Tocco p.136-137].
- Last Communion in Lent, before monks, Dominicans and Minorites; Tocco: Thomas on the floor, the long profession of faith [S1 Tocco p.131-132]; sworn Peter: shorter submission to the Church [S2 n.49 p.332]; bull repeats it [S2 p.523]. Peter: he lived three days after Communion and died on the third day [S2 p.332]. Tocco: extreme unction the next day, "post paullulum suum spiritum Domino reddidit" [S1 Tocco p.132].
- 7 March 1274 dawn: death. Tocco: a monk dreams a star falling and two others joining it as the bell tolls; a comet-like star over the monastery for three days [S1 Tocco p.133]. Fr Paul of Aquila dreams in Naples that St Paul leads Thomas out of his classroom [S1 Tocco p.133-134]. Subprior John of Ferentino/Florentino, blind about 20 days, puts his eyes to the dead man's eyes and sees [S2 n.51 p.335-336].
- Funeral: Bishop Francis of Terracina OFM, 4-5 Franciscans, many Dominicans, nobles of Campania, about 100 monks and lay brothers [S2 p.335-336; S1 Tocco p.135]. Francesca asks that the bier be carried to the monastery gate and mourns with two women; the mule Thomas rode breaks its rope, comes to the bier and falls dead [S1 Tocco p.135]. LEG (Tocco only). A Dominican long his confessor preaches: Reginald (Tocco) or Peter of Sezze (Tocco's own sworn account) [S1 Tocco p.136; S2 p.345 n.c, 332 n.].
- 2 May 1274: the Paris Faculty of Arts writes to the Dominican General Chapter at Lyon, asks for his bones and for his unfinished philosophical works [S2 p.583-586]. NEW for English pages.
- Burial before the high altar. The monks, fearing the Dominicans or the pope would take the body, move it secretly to St Stephen's chapel. After about seven months Thomas appears to the prior "Reduc me ad locum pristinum"; the tomb is opened, fragrance fills the chapel and cloister, the body is whole in its habit, with a small crack at the tip of the nose; the monks sing the Mass of a confessor, "Os iusti meditabitur" [S2 n.8 p.278; n.52 p.336-337; S1 Tocco p.138-140; bull S2 p.524-525].
- 1318-19 Province of Sicily sends William of Tocco to Avignon; Naples inquiry 23 Jul-18 Sep 1319; Fossanova inquiry 10-26 Nov 1321; bull 18 Jul 1323 [S1 Tocco p.57-58].

## 4. Character
- Silent, absent-minded: "ponebatur coram eo scutella et removebatur pluries ipso non sentiente"; leaves garden recreation alone [S2 p.374].
- Mild in debate: "nullis verbis gloriosis et ampullosis utens"; Peckham's taunts met "cum dulcedine et humanitate" [S2 p.374].
- Cheerful, even face: "alacrem vultum, mitem et suavem" [S2 p.374].
- Tall, heavy, bald at the front: "magne stature et calvus in fronte" [S2 n.42 p.319]; "magnus in corpore, procere et recte stature" [S1 Calo p.40]; the exhumed body was "grossum, pingue et magnum" [S1 Tocco p.139].
- Plain eater: "nunquam curabat de delicantia et singularitate ciborum nec de cultu vestimentorum" [S2 p.346]; one meal a day in the refectory [S2 p.362].
- Counselled even by kings: St Louis sought his advice in hard cases [S1 Tocco p.108-109].
- Prayed before every study; tears; Reginald: "profusus orabat lacrymis" [S1 Tocco p.105].
- Very sensitive to pain: "Erat enim miro modo passibilis" [S1 Tocco p.121]. NEW.
- Chaste, avoided women's company, scolded friars who sat long with women [S1 Tocco p.75].
- Refused honours: mastership he feared; cardinal's hat he turned down [S1 Tocco p.84-85; S2 p.375-376].
- Preached in his own Neapolitan, plainly [S1 Tocco p.122].
- Humble before servants: monks carrying logs embarrass him [S2 p.277].
- A fighter: runs at the demon with a raised fist (Blasi) [S2 p.362].
- Critics: Peckham's sharp words [S2 p.374]; the Cologne friars' joke [S1 Tocco p.78]. No hostile witness survives in the process.

## 5. Quotations (Latin original, our English, locator; all public-domain)
1. "Letare vere o domina; filium enim paries, qui vocabitur thomas, in quo profecto erit abyssus sapientie plenioris." "Rejoice, lady; you will bear a son called Thomas, and in him will be an abyss of fuller wisdom." [S1 Calo p.18] LEG.
2. "Gaude, Domina, quia tu es pregnans et paries filium quem vocabis Thomam." Sworn hearsay version. [S2 n.62 p.350]
3. "Quare venisti huc ad tentandum me." "Why have you come here to tempt me?" (to the demon, fist raised) [S2 n.70 p.362, John Blasi, sworn]
4. "lumen ei in tenebris affuit, et in vinculis plus libertatis invenit." "Light came to him in the dark, and in chains he found more freedom." [S1 Tocco p.74]
5. "Ecce ex parte Dei cingimus te cingulo castitatis quod nulla possit temptatione dissolvi." Angels: "Behold, from God we gird you with the belt of chastity that no temptation can undo." [S2 n.61 p.349]
6. "Nos vocamus istum bovem mutum; sed ipse adhuc talem dabit in doctrina mugitum, quod in toto mundo sonabit." Albert, "fertur": "We call him the dumb ox; but he will give such a bellow in teaching that it will sound through the whole world." [S1 Tocco p.79] LATE.
7. "Rigans montes de superioribus suis: de fructu operum tuorum satiabitur terra." (Ps 103:13) [S1 Tocco p.85; S2 n.49 p.331]
8. "In veritate plus vellem Crisostomum super Matheum." "In truth I would rather have Chrysostom on Matthew." [S2 n.78 p.376]
9. "Modo conclusum est contra haeresim Manichaeorum." "Now it is settled against the Manichaean heresy." Prior: "Advertatis Magister, quia nunc estis in mensa Regis Franciae." [S1 Tocco p.116-117]
10. "De quadam quaestione, super quam diu cogitaveram, nunc pulchram rationem inveni." "I had thought long on a question and now I have found a fine answer." [S1 Tocco p.117]
11. "Quidquid videas in me, cave ut non me voces." "Whatever you see in me, take care not to call me." (before the candle burned to his fingers) [S1 Tocco p.121]
12. "Fili Raynalde surge, et accende candelam, et accipe quaternum, in quo super Isaiam scripseras." [S1 Tocco p.105] "Ex parte Dei tibi praecipio, ut in vita mea non audeas revelare." [S1 Tocco p.106]
13. "Thoma bene scripsisti de me. Quam ergo recipies a me mercedem de tuo labore?" "Domine, non nisi te." "Thomas, you have written well of me; what reward will you take?" "Lord, only yourself." [S1 Calo p.38; S1 Tocco p.108] LATE/PIOUS.
14. "Raynalde, in nullo statu possum esse ita utilis ordini nostro, sicut in isto." [S2 n.78 p.375]
15. "Raynalde, sis securus quod ego nunquam in perpetuum mutabo statum." [S2 p.376]
16. "Raynalde, non possum." and "Raynalde, non possum quia omnia que scripsi videntur mihi palee." [S2 n.79 p.377]
17. "Omnia que scripsi videntur michi palee respectu eorum que vidi et revelata sunt michi." "All I have written seems like straw beside what I have seen and what was shown to me." [S2 p.377]
18. "Venit finis scripturae meae, quia talia sunt mihi revelata, quod ea quae scripsi et docui, modica mihi videntur." (San Severino version) [S1 Tocco p.120; S1 Gui p.194]
19. "Si Dominus voluerit me visitare, melius est quod reperiar in domo religiosorum quam in domibus secularium." [S2 n.8 p.277, Abbot Nicholas, sworn]
20. "Hec requies mea in seculum seculi, hic habitabo quoniam elegi eam." (Ps 131:14) [S1 Tocco p.130; S2 p.277, 332]
21. "Unde hoc michi ut sancti viri deferant michi ligna?" "Why is this granted me, that holy men carry wood for me?" [S2 n.8 p.277; S1 Tocco p.131]
22. "Si maior scientia, quam fides de hoc Sacramento in hac vita haberi potest, in illa respondeo, quod vere credo..." [S1 Tocco p.132]
23. "Sumo te pretium redemptionis animae meae, sumo te viaticum peregrinationis meae, pro cuius amore studui, vigilavi, et laboravi, te praedicavi et docui." [S1 Tocco p.132]
24. "Ego de isto santissimo Corpore et aliis sacramentis multa docui et scripsi, in fide Christi et sancte romane Ecclesie cuius correctioni omnia subicio et expono." (sworn eyewitness) [S2 n.49 p.332]
25. "ita inveni eum purum sicut puerum V annorum quia nunquam sue carnis sentiit corruptelam." (funeral sermon) [S2 n.49 p.332; S1 Tocco p.136]
Extra: Paris Arts Faculty, 2 May 1274: "stellam matutinam preeminentem in mundo" and "ipsius iam defuncti a vobis ossa humiliter pro maximo munere postulamus" [S2 pp.584-585]. Ptolemy: "Cum autem jam esset XVI annorum, habitum Fratrum Praedicatorum assumsit" [S1 Calo p.20 n.2 quoting S3].

## 6. Scenes
1. Roccasecca storm: lightning, the sister dead in the tower, mother running to the baby's bed. [S1 Tocco p.67]

10. Fossanova: monks carrying logs, the Host received on the floor, death at dawn; the monks' bell. [S2 p.277; S1 Tocco p.131-133]

## 8. Other
- Dumb ox: earliest text found is Calo/Tocco/Gui, c.1317-1323, none from the 1319 depositions. Chesterton's use was not checked.
- Talking crucifix: likewise not in sworn evidence.
