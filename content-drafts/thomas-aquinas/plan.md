# Thomas Aquinas: plan

Tier A. Target about 8,500-9,000 words of prose. The three notes files hold about 12,600 words, rich in scenes from Tocco and the 1319 Naples process, so the low-to-middle tier A range is honest. No section is padded.

## The driving question

As a child Thomas (by a late legend) kept asking "What is God?"; as a man he wrote that "about God we cannot know what he is, only what he is not", and that the least knowledge of the highest things is worth more than the surest knowledge of small things. He wanted God himself, not the abbacy his family planned, not the archbishopric the pope offered, not a cardinal's hat. What stood in the way: his family's ambition (a year or two of captivity), the weakness of the human mind before God (the owl dazzled by the sun), and the honors his own brilliance kept drawing to him. The resolution: in December 1273, after Mass in the chapel of St Nicholas, something he saw made all his writing seem "like straw", and he stopped; three months later he received the Eucharist at Fossanova saying that for love of Christ he had studied, kept vigil, and worked. The legend of the crucifix ("What reward will you take?" "Lord, only yourself") states the same answer and is told as legend.

The hook raises the question: why would the man who wrote the Summa stop writing and call it straw? The chapter "Like Straw Beside What I Have Seen" answers the first half (he saw something more); "This Is My Rest Forever" answers the whole (all of it had been love, and he hands it back to the Church).

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: the Summa Theologiae and the Summa contra Gentiles; Doctor of the Church (1567), patron of Catholic schools (1880).

Set apart: he chose the new begging friars against a noble family that fought him with prison, and refused every rank afterward. He put the whole of reason, Aristotle included, at the service of faith, sure that grace "does not destroy nature but perfects it", and argued each question by stating the case against himself first.

## The hook

Scene at the edge: Naples, December 1273. After Mass in the chapel of St Nicholas he stops writing; Reginald presses him; "Reginald, I cannot"; the oath of secrecy; "straw" (Bartholomew of Capua's sworn hearsay from Reginald, attributed in a few words). Then: How did the boy from Roccasecca come to that chapel, and what did he see? Answered in chapters 10 and 12.

## Chapters

1. **Reginald, I Cannot** (hook, thesis). Quote: the straw line (Bartholomew, 1319). Pull: it began in a castle at Roccasecca.
2. **A Boy Meant for Monte Cassino.** Lightning legend, Theodora as Tocco's source, Monte Cassino at five, "What is God?" (legend), Naples schools, the Dominican habit. Pull: his mother sets out for Naples.
3. **Not as Brothers but as Enemies.** The flight toward Rome, the spring, the habit held fast; Peter della Vigna (Ptolemy). Pull: the castle door shuts.
4. **A Burning Brand in the Tower.** Books in prison, the sisters, torn habit, the girl and the brand, the girdle (told to Reginald near death); jailer and escape conflicts attributed; the rope. Quote: Tocco's "light in the dark". Pull: to Albert.
5. **The Dumb Ox of Cologne.** Albert, the lecture on the Divine Names, the bellow (labeled late, "it is said"). Pull: Paris.
6. **Water on the Mountains.** Bachelor and master; "Rigans montes" dream; Peckham's taunts met gently; Paris hill and Chrysostom. Quote: SCG I.2 (Rickaby). Pull: the Italian years and the offer of Naples.
7. **The Friar Who Refused Naples.** Preacher general, Orvieto, Corpus Christi office (Ptolemy), archbishopric refused, the Agnes relics at Molara, the woman in Rome. Quote: Pange lingua / Lauda Sion lines. Pull: back to Paris.
8. **At the Table of the King.** Louis's dinner; absent-mindedness; prayer with tears; Isaiah at night; the method of the Summa: grace perfects nature; friendship with God, wine and horses; the bow. Quotes: ST I q.1 a.5 ad 1; II-II q.23 a.1. Pull: Naples.
9. **A Lent of Sermons in Naples.** Bartholomew's portrait, the day's order, sermons in his own dialect, eyes closed, the demon and the fist, "illuminate rather than shine", the crucifix legend. Quote: II-II q.188 a.6. Pull: December.
10. **Like Straw Beside What I Have Seen.** The full scene, the oath, San Severino and its version without "straw". Pull: the pope's summons.
11. **A Fallen Tree on the Road.** Borgo Nuovo, cardinals, "never change my state", Maenza, the herrings (three accounts). Pull: "better a house of religious".
12. **This Is My Rest Forever.** Fossanova, the logs, Song of Songs, last Communion, death. Quote: the viaticum prayer (Tocco). Pull: the funeral.
13. **The Bones the Masters Asked For.** Funeral, preacher conflict, mule (legend), the subprior, the Paris letter, the body moved and found, the hand. Pull: the inquiries.
14. **A Galley off Monte Argentario.** Tocco's storm, John XXII, the processes, the bull, Toulouse, Doctor, Leo XIII; end on the Adoro te thirst line (attributed).
