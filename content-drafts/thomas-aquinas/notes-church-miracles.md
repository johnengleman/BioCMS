# Thomas Aquinas — notes: church-miracles (tier A)

Status key: APPROVED = named in the bull Redemptionem misit (18 July 1323) after the Naples (1319) and Fossanova (1321) inquiries. WITNESS = reported by a named witness in Tocco or Gui, not in the bull. LEGEND = vision/late story. CONTESTED = sources disagree.

## 2. Timeline (church-miracles focus)
- c.1225 (or 1227, Calo) birth at Roccasecca; death age 49 by Tocco "anno vitae suae XLIX" [S1 p.138; S5]. 
- Sister killed by lightning when Thomas a small child [S1 p.22 note, citing Tocco c.2] — Tocco's own detail; not read in text. Check.
- Lifetime marvels in Tocco: the angels' girdle of chastity after he drove a woman from his room with a burning brand and marked a cross on the wall with it [S1 p.~85, Tocco c.XXVI?]. WITNESS (Tocco: from his confessor Reginald). NEW to many English pages? No: common. Mark LEGEND/WITNESS.
- Salerno: Thomas seen raised two cubits from the floor in prayer after Matins by Brother James and his companion [S1 Tocco c.XXXIII p.107]. Naples: sacristan Dominic of Caserta sees him raised two cubits in St Nicholas chapel and hears the crucifix say "Thoma bene scripsisti de me..." [S1 Tocco c.XXXIV p.108]. Tocco says he was then writing Part III of the Summa. WITNESS.
- St Severino ecstasy: long rapture at his sister's castle; Reginald shakes him by the cape; Thomas says his writing is at an end, "modica mihi videntur" [S1 Tocco c.XLVI p.120]. WITNESS. (Date 1273 not given in text I read.)
- Jan 1274: Gregory X calls him to Council of Lyons; he carries the treatise against the Greeks; falls ill at Maenza (niece Francesca's castle) [S1 p.129; S2 p.55-56; S5].
- Maenza: loses appetite; asks for herrings he ate in France; doctor John of Piperno finds a basket of fresh herring among sardines on a mule-load from Terracina; Thomas refuses to eat, as David refused water [S1 Tocco c.LVI p.129-130; S2 p.56]. WITNESS (doctor told many "adhuc vita supersunt").
- Fossanova entry: "haec requies mea in saeculum saeculi" in the cloister [S1 p.130-131; S2 p.56; S5]. Lodged in abbot's room; monks carry wood on their shoulders; he dictates on the Song of Songs [S1 p.131; S2 p.57].
- 7 March 1274, morning ("hora matutinali"), Fossanova: death after viaticum and extreme unction [S1 Tocco c.LXV p.138]. Last confession to Reginald: "like a child of five" [S1 p.136]. Eucharist profession of faith and submission to the Roman Church [S1 p.131-132; S2 p.57-58].
- Death-hour signs: a monk of Fossanova sees a star fall on the monastery, two stars join it, and it rise [S1 p.133]; a comet-like star over the monastery three days before, gone at his death [S1 p.133]. Brother Paul of Aquila at Naples sees St Paul lead Thomas out of the school ("Succurrite, fratres...") and the hour is matched [S1 p.133-134]. Rainerius of Pisa at Anagni, third day after: Thomas says Mass, right eye larger than left [S1 p.137-138]. WITNESS/LEGEND (visions).
- Blind subprior John of Ferentino cured at the body (face on face) [S1 Tocco c.LXI p.134-135; S3 ch.IX]. WITNESS (Tocco; not in the bull's ten).
- Funeral: bishop of Terracina (Franciscan), monks, Dominicans; niece Francesca not allowed in; the funeral is carried to the door; her mourning; Thomas's mule breaks its rope, walks to the bier, and drops dead [S1 Tocco c.LXII p.135]. LEGEND/WITNESS: oddity, in Tocco only.
- Burial in the church; abbot (Gui: Prior James of Florence; Tocco: "Abbas") fears the Dominicans will take the body (it was "commended as a deposit"), moves it secretly at night to St Stephen's chapel [S1 Tocco c.LXVI p.138-139; Gui p.~209]. Thomas appears in dream, reproves the abbot. Seven months later (Tocco note: "septem mensibus", testimony of Peter of Monte S. Giovanni) the tomb is opened with iron tools: great fragrance fills chapel and cloister; body whole, in habit, "although the body had been large and fat and the ground wet and deep" [S1 p.139; Gui p.210; S4 §9: "about seven months"]. APPROVED (bull miracle I). 
- Monks carry him to the great altar in a marble tomb; cantor begins by chance "Os iusti meditabitur sapientiam" [S1 p.140]. 
- Tocco: mason Jacobus lifts earth fallen by the chest; it smells; a noble takes some earth as a relic [S1 p.140]. WITNESS.
- Fourteenth year (c.1288 by Butler; 1284 by Conway): Abbot Peter of Monte S. Giovanni, asked by Thomas's sister Theodora (countess of San Severino), opens the tomb: odor again; body whole "except the tip of the nose, slightly eaten away"; right hand cut off, which Tocco says is still undecayed and kept at Salerno [S1 Tocco c.LXVIII p.141-142; S2 p.59; S3 ch.X]. APPROVED (bull: "some after seven years, others after almost fourteen years" smelled the odor, S4 §9).
- Hand relic, 42 years after death (c.1316): a canon-cleric from Salerno scorns the hand, is seized with trembling, is cured on kissing it, and the smell clings to his hood [S1 Tocco c.LXIX p.142-143]. APPROVED (bull miracle IV, S4 §12, told without names).
- Later: Count Thomas of San Severino gives the hand to the Dominicans of Salerno; one friar smells it only on the second approach [S1 p.144].
- c.1303: Bartholomew of Capua petitions Benedict XI [S8]. Conway: 1304 the Cistercians cut off the head fearing a Dominican claim [S3 ch.X] — conflict with S8 (head moved later to Priverno/Piperno). CONTESTED.
- 1317-1318: Robert of San Valentino (vicar of the Order) and the provincial chapter at Gaeta have Tocco and Robert of Benevento gather the miracles [S1 p.147]. Pope John XXII, on the petition: "Nos credimus quod Fr. Thomas est gloriosus sanctus in caelo..." [S1 p.148]. Second consistory: he would count it a great glory if the Church could inscribe Thomas, provided miracles are found [S1 p.148-149]. 
- Sea storm: Tocco and Robert's galley driven to Monte Argentario, a new wind turns it; they had asked Thomas to save the miracle records [S1 Tocco p.147-148; Gui p.255-256; S3 ch.IX]. WITNESS (Tocco himself).
- 13 Sept 1318: Archbishop Humbert of Naples, Bishop Angelo of Viterbo, notary Pandulf of Sabello named commissioners [S8; S1 p.58, Prümmer on Humbert/Angelo]. Naples inquiry 21 July-18 Sept 1319 (S1 p.57: "a die 23 Julii ad 18 Sept." 1319; S8 differs by two days). Tocco, 4 months at Fossanova collecting miracles while waiting for the aged archbishop [S1 p.149]. 
- 1 June 1321: second inquiry ordered, held at Fossanova 10-26 Nov 1321 (S1 p.57; S8 says 27 Nov); commissioners: bishops Peter of Anagni and Andrew of Terracina, Pandulf [S1 p.57; S8]. Over 100 witnesses [S8].
- During the process: Archbishop Humbert's incurable tibial ulcer vanishes overnight; Viterbo's bishop cured of fever; Pope's niece Mary d'Arnaud cured of dropsy [S3 ch.IX; Gui no.87 on Naples archbishop p.~249]. WITNESS (Conway unsourced; Gui lists the archbishop).
- 14 July 1323 first ceremony at Avignon; 18 July 1323: Veni Creator, bull Redemptionem misit read; feast fixed at 7 March; indulgence of one year and forty days at the tomb [S4 §20-21; S8]. The Franciscan who would "rather die" than see it dies a day later: tradition [S8] LEGEND.
- 18 July 1323 sermon (Conway quoting the Pope): "He has wrought as many miracles as he has written Articles" [S3 ch.IX]. Tocco/other primary source for this line not found in my read of S1: tradition/CONTESTED attribution.
- 1324-28: Dominican chapters raise him to the second place after Dominic; liturgy done by 1328 [S8].
- 1348: Count Honorat Caetani of Fondi gets the body from the Cistercians [S8]. Conway: the body was in a chest at Fondi; Thomas walks out of the chest before the count's mother and the bishop of Fondi, lies down again [S3 ch.X]. LEGEND.
- 1368-69: Urban V hands the body to the Order at Montefiascone (Elias of Toulouse, Master General; "he is our brother and our flesh"); relics reach Prouille; 28 Jan 1369 arrive at the Dominican church in Toulouse, with Louis of Anjou and 150,000 people [S3 ch.X; S2 p.59; S5: 28 Jan 1369]. 
- 1585 (claim): another skull said to be Thomas's found at Fossanova [S8]. CONTESTED.
- 1567: Pius V orders his feast and office kept equal to the four Latin Doctors [S2 p.60]; Doctor of the Church, 1567 [S3 Epilogue; S5]. "Mirabilis Deus", 11 April 1567: from a web search only, not read — to check.
- 1628 grand shrine at Toulouse; Revolution: remains moved to St Sernin; 24 July 1878 gold-silver sarcophagus blessed by Cardinal Desprez [S3 ch.X; S5]. 1974 return to the Church of the Jacobins [S8, citing Torrell]. 
- 4 Aug 1879: Aeterni Patris [S6]. 4 Aug 1880: Cum hoc sit, Thomas patron of universities, academies, colleges, Catholic schools; Latin Pontifex: "San Tommaso ... patrono delle Università degli Studi, delle Accademie, dei Licei, delle scuole cattoliche" [S7]. 
- 1969: feast moved from 7 March (often in Lent) to 28 January, the day of the 1369 arrival at Toulouse [S8]. The date of the reform decree: to check.
- Arms: right arm to Paris (Dominican chapel), now Rome, the Minerva; left arm bone to Naples cathedral 1603 [S3 ch.X; S5].

## 3. The miracles in the bull (APPROVED), ten events [S4 §9-18]
1. Fragrance and integrity of the body after about seven months; smelled again after seven and almost fourteen years [S4 §9].
2. Gout of ten years cured at the tomb: a surgeon (Tocco names him Raynaldus, doctor of San Lorenzo, who then rides a horse) [S4 §10; S1 p.145].
3. Man paralyzed after a nightmare vision, lying like a corpse, taken to tomb and cured [S4 §11].
4. Scorner of the hand relic, trembling and cyst on his head, cured on kissing the hand; scent clings to him [S4 §12].
5. Cleric with angina, speechless, carried to tomb; wife not allowed into church (women barred) [S4 §13].
6. Seven weeks of daily fever cured in one night on a vow [S4 §14].
7. Deaf mother of a two-month-old cured overnight [S4 §15].
8. Girl with throat swelling cured by relics wrapped in cloth, eats bread [S4 §16].
9. Lay brother of Fossanova, arm in sling for three months, cured asleep on the tomb [S4 §17].
10. Four-year-old with a tumor on back/legs, placed on tomb, cured without surgery [S4 §18].
Count: the bull prints ten. Conway says 96 were attested and submitted for canonization [S3 ch.IX]; Tocco's list in S1 reaches no.57 plus a further numbered catalogue in Gui [S1 p.~145-160, Gui nos.1-102]. Gui's last words: "Multa quidem et alia miracula operatus est deus per sancti thome de aquino merita in diversis mundi partibus, que non sunt scripta in presenti tractatu." [S1 Gui p.256]. A manuscript note adds "55 miracles at Pavia" [S1 p.256 note] — WITNESS, uncorroborated.
The bull is clear that the Pope examined "the sanctity of the life and the authenticity of the miracles ... not only once, but again and again" [S4 §19, translation].

## 4. Other miracle claims (WITNESS unless noted), selected from Tocco and Gui, with S1 page
- Tocco's catalogue nos.1-57 (pp.145-160): leper Thomas of Piperno cured after Thomas appears at night: "Surge et esto sanus" [S1 no.7 p.146]; demonic ape paralysis cured, victim becomes lay brother [no.3 p.145]; lame from birth stands [no.9 p.147]; lightning/hail stopped by invoking him [no.20]; sinking man saved [no.36]; knight and horse falling from rock saved [nos.56-57]; storms stilled at Fossanova by procession to tomb [no.8].
- Gui nos.1-102 (pp.~240-256): includes the Naples archbishop (no.87), a drowning/pregnancy case (no.89), a doctor rebuked in apparition for crediting nature (no.90) [Gui p.251], and no.100: a dead mother at Tagulano in Piedmont revived when her Dominican son Rufinus and family pray after the 1323 canonization [Gui p.254]; the note says it is missing in Cod. Vat. 1218. The only claimed raising of the dead in these texts. CONTESTED/LEGEND (single manuscript tradition). Also a stillborn child revived long enough to be baptized, no.89 [Gui p.251].
- Bernardus's translation account: seven months, but "some wrote seven years" [Gui p.210]. 

## 5. Character (for the miracle section)
- Humility at death: asked God not to be raised to high office; tells Reginald "Noli fili de hac causa turbari" [S1 p.136].
- Refused the herrings: he took the miracle as a gift he would not use for appetite [S1 p.129-130].
- Reginald: "like a child of five" in his general confession [S1 p.136].
- Weight: big, fat, heavy man; the body at the tomb "grossum, pingue et magnum" [S1 p.139; also Calo in S5: tall, heavy, wheat-colored].
- The Cistercians' hospitality; the monks carried wood for him [S1 p.131].

## 6. Quotations (originals, exact from S1 OCR; English is mine)
1. "Fili Raynalde, haec requies mea in saeculum saeculi; hic habitabo, quoniam elegi eam." — "Son Reginald, this is my rest for ever; here I will dwell, for I have chosen it." Tocco c.LIV, S1 p.130. public-domain.
2. "Si maior scientia, quam fides de hoc Sacramento in hac vita haberi potest, in illa respondeo, quod vere credo, et pro certo scio, hunc Deum verum esse et hominem" — "If any knowledge greater than faith can be had of this sacrament in this life, I answer in that knowledge that I truly believe and know for certain that this is true God and true man." S1 p.132.
3. "nihil contra te dixi unquam, sed si quid dixi, ignorans dixi nec sum pertinax in sensu meo" — "I never said anything against you; if I said anything, I said it in ignorance, and I am not stubborn in my own opinion." S1 p.132. (OCR "perlinax" fixed to "pertinax".)
4. "Unde hoc mihi, ut servi Dei serviant homini" — "Why is this given to me, that servants of God should serve a man?" S1 p.131.
5. "Thoma bene scripsisti de me. Quam ergo recipies a me mercedem de tuo labore? ... Domine, non nisi te." — "Thomas, you have written well of me. What reward will you take?" "Lord, nothing but yourself." S1 p.108, Gui p.~212.
6. "Venit finis scripturae meae, quia talia sunt mihi revelata, quod ea quae scripsi et docui, modica mihi videntur" — "The end of my writing has come, for such things have been revealed to me that what I wrote and taught seems little." S1 p.120.
7. "Nos credimus quod Fr. Thomas est gloriosus sanctus in caelo, quia vita sua fuit sancta, et doctrina eius non potuit esse sine miraculo." — John XXII, S1 p.148 (Tocco's report).
8. "quia ipse plus illuminavit Ecclesiam, quam omnes alii Doctores, in cujus libris plus proficit homo uno anno, quam in aliorum doctrina toto tempore vitae suae" — John XXII to the cardinals, S1 p.149.
9. "Noli fili de hac causa turbari, quia hoc a Deo inter alia mea desideria petii" — "Do not be troubled, son; I asked this of God among my desires" (to be spared high office). S1 p.136.
10. "mulus ... mortuus sine alia infirmitate defecit" — the mule died "with no other illness" at the bier. S1 p.135.
11. "Iste Sanctus digne in memoriam vertitur hominum, qui ad gaudium transiit Angelorum" — antiphon the cantor began. S1 p.139-140.
12. "Quam receptam devote cum lacrymis fecit ipsam diligentius conservari" — Theodora and the hand. S1 p.142.
13. English from the bull (translation, S4 §8): "his life has witnessed his miracles, and his miracles have witnessed his life". Facts-only translation; quote short.
14. Leo XIII, Aeterni Patris (S6, Vatican English): "Thomas reigned supreme; and that the minds of all, of teachers as well as of taught, rested in wonderful harmony under the shield and authority of the Angelic Doctor."
15. Urban V and Elias, English (S3 ch.X; Conway; PD): "Holy Father," answered Elias, "he is our brother and our flesh."
16. Albert the Great in Paris, 1276 (S3 ch.IX, Conway, source not given): "What a glory it is," he exclaimed, "for the living to be praised by the dead!" CONTESTED (no primary source found).
17. Gui end: "Multa quidem et alia miracula operatus est deus per sancti thome de aquino merita in diversis mundi partibus, que non sunt scripta in presenti tractatu." S1 p.256.

## 8. Conflicts, cautions, other
- Date of first move: Conway says October 1274 and the second move to the tomb in 1279; Tocco/Gui say seven months after death and Gui says some wrote seven years [S1 p.139, Gui p.210; S3]. Use Tocco/Gui/bull.
- Hand: 14th year (c.1288, S2 p.59) vs 1284 (S3). Tocco says "quarto decimo anno obitus". 
- Who was the abbot: Tocco says "Abbas"; Gui names Prior James of Florence; in 1288 Abbot Peter of Monte San Giovanni [S1 p.139-141; Gui p.209].
- Head: Cistercians cut it off 1304 (S3); Priverno/Piperno tradition and 1585 skull (S8). The Toulouse 1369 gift included "the head" per Urban V (S3). Unresolved: CONTESTED.
- Body boiled for relic distribution (S8) — not in S1/S2/S3; secondary only; do not state as fact.
- "Incorrupt" claim: the Tocco and Gui accounts say the body was whole and fragrant at seven months and at ~14 years (nose tip eaten away, right hand removed). No claim that the body is incorrupt today; the bones are in Toulouse (S5, S8: "fifty bones" per Wikipedia).
- Miracle count: the bull prints ten; Conway says 96 were attested; Gui goes to 102. Do not say the bull lists 96.
- Patronage per sources: all Catholic universities, academies, colleges and schools (1880); principal patron of Naples (Pius V 1567, Clement VIII 1603) [S2 p.60]; patron of Priverno/Piperno [S8].
- Images to look for: the Toulouse sarcophagus, Fossanova abbey, Carafa Chapel, Zurbarán (not researched).
