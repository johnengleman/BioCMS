# Plan: St. Seraphim of Sarov

## The driving question

What did Seraphim have that the robbers could not find? In September 1804 three peasants beat a hermit nearly to death for his money and found an icon and some potatoes. Seraphim was a merchant's son from Kursk who wanted only God, the forest, and silence. Illness, demons, a beating that bent him for life, an order back to the monastery, and the death of his spiritual father stood in the way; he answered each by going further in: the stone, the silence, the oak coffin, the locked door. Then, in 1825, the Mother of God sent him out of the enclosure, and the man who had fallen on his face to avoid a passing monk spent his last eight years greeting every stranger with "My joy, Christ is risen!" The answer comes in the snow in November 1831: the aim of the Christian life, he told Motovilov in the language of a Kursk trader, is to acquire the Holy Spirit, a capital of grace that is never lost. What he had gathered alone, he gave away at his door.

The hook raises the question (the robbers looking for money). Chapter 12 answers it (the capital of grace, the light in the snow). The ending resolves it: the bones lost and found, the pilgrims, and the saying remembered in his name: acquire a peaceful spirit, and thousands around you will be saved.

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: the best-loved saint of Russia, the hermit of Sarov whose canonization in 1903 drew the tsar and a crowd of hundreds of thousands, and whose teaching on acquiring the Holy Spirit is read across the Orthodox world.
Set apart: he lived the hardest solitary disciplines of the ancient desert in eighteenth-century Russia, and then turned them inside out into joy, greeting every visitor with the Easter greeting in any season. He took care of a small community of women at Diveyevo against the will of some of his own brothers.

## The hook

Type: scene at the edge. 12 September 1804, the forest garden: three peasants demand money; Seraphim holds an axe, strong enough to fight, puts it down and crosses his arms: "Do what you need." They find an icon and some potatoes. Next day he crawls into the monastery during the Liturgy, and later begs the abbot not to punish them. Thesis: twenty-seven years later, bent forever by that beating, he would tell a young man in the snow what his treasure was. Answered in chapter 12; closed in chapter 16.

## Chapters

1. **An Icon and Some Potatoes** (1804, hook). Block quote: none. Pull: it began in a merchant's house in Kursk.
2. **A Merchant's Son in Kursk** (1754–1776). Father's church; the fall; the icon in the rain; the shop. Flash-forward quote: the Kursk merchants. Pull: friends who planned to leave with him.
3. **A Copper Cross for the Road** (1776–1780). Farewell; Kiev; Sarov; obediences; dropsy. Block quote: the True Physician. Pull: "a divine visit".
4. **The Night of the Divine Visit** (1780–1794). Healing; Ksenia's hand; tonsure; deacon; Holy Thursday vision; swollen legs; asks for the desert. Pull: the forest.
5. **Lions and Leopards in the Forest** (1794–1804). Cell, snyt, Alatyr refusal, stones. Block quote: the pillar and tenderness. Pull: 12 September 1804.
6. **Bent for the Rest of His Life** (1804–1810). Five months; vision; plea for the robbers; their fire and pardon; Isaiah dies; three years of silence; the tray of bread or cabbage; return to the monastery. Pull: the door shuts.
7. **An Oak Coffin in the Entry** (1810–1825). Enclosure; the governor; "my joy"; portrait; critics; vanity; Manturov. Block quote: Manturov healing. Pull: a gift of women not yet given.
8. **Sent Out by the Mother of God** (1825–1831). Vision; stones; flies; horse; resolve; the rule for lay people. Block quote: the flies. Pull: the family at Diveyevo.
9. **The Orphans of Diveyevo** (1825–1831). Mill; ditch; humor; healings; the spring; opposition; cholera; Annunciation 1831; Anthony Medvedev. Block quote: never left the monastery. Pull: a paralyzed landowner.
10. **A Landowner Under a Pine** (Sept 1831). Pull: Seraphim already knew his question.
11. **Brighter Than the Noon Sun** (Nov 1831). Block quotes: the capital; the light; the perfume. Answers the robbers' treasure. Pull: a year left.
12. **Crowns Are Being Prepared** (1832–1833). Block quote: last words at the Liturgy. Death; the copper cross returns. Pull: the funeral.
13. **Eight Days in the Cathedral** (1833–1861). Funeral; the Lives and censorship; the bear. Pull: the canonization.
14. **The Tsar Who Carried the Coffin** (1883–1903). Bones; politics; healings; the crowds; the Conversation printed. Pull: the third time.
15. **Mittens in a Museum Storeroom** (1920–1991). Soviet opening; loss; the spring; Leningrad 1990; Diveyevo 1991.
16. **My Joy, Christ Is Risen** (today). Feasts; the spring; Sarov the nuclear city; 2003; the treasure resolved; ends on "My joy! Christ is risen!"

Departures from the first plan: the silence chapter was merged into chapter 6, and the afterlife was split into three chapters plus an ending, to give the relic story room.

Block quotes: 9.

## Open loops

1. The robbers' treasure: answered in chapter 12 (capital of grace) and chapter 16.
2. The copper cross from his mother: returns at his death, on his chest.
3. The forbidden desert and the locked door: why the hermit ended among crowds, answered in chapter 9.
4. The prophecy of the fire at his death (chapter 13): fulfilled the same morning.
