## SC-06 — Bread or cabbage, 1807–1810
- Status: full
- When and where: after Isaiah died (1807) until the move into the monastery, the forest hermitage cell. EL5 says the silence lasted "about three years" in these forms, then Seraphim stopped coming to the monastery on Sundays and feasts. The weekly food came on Sunday. Move into the monastery: 8 May 1810 (EL5) or 9 May 1809 (notes, SG; accuracy note in outline).
- Who was there: Seraphim; an unnamed brother who carried the food (EL5).
- What happened, in order:
  1. After Isaiah's death he took on the work of silence (EL5, opening). He took as models Arsenius the Great and John the Silent (EL5).
  2. If visitors came to his hermitage, he did not appear to them. If he met someone in the forest, he fell on his face and did not lift his eyes until the person passed (EL5).
  3. Before the three years ended he stopped coming to the monastery on Sundays and feasts. One brother carried food to the hermitage, "especially in winter" when Seraphim had no vegetables of his own. It came once a week, on Sunday (EL5).
  4. In winter there was no road. The brother waded through snow up to the knees in a blizzard with a week's supply (EL5).
  5. In the entry the brother said a prayer. Seraphim said "Amen" to himself and opened the door from cell to entry. He stood with arms crossed on his chest and eyes down. He did not bless the brother or look at him (EL5).
  6. The brother prayed, bowed to Seraphim's feet, and put the food on a small tray on the table in the entry. Seraphim put on the tray either a small piece of bread or a little cabbage. This told the brother what to bring next Sunday: bread or cabbage (EL5).
  7. The brother asked for his prayers and went back to the monastery without hearing one word (EL5).
- Details: the tray (лоточек) on a table in the entry; the blizzard and knee-deep snow; the Amen said to himself.
- Words spoken: none to the brother. On silence generally, Elagin gives Seraphim's saying (original Russian): «Когда мы в молчании пребываем – говорил о. Серафим, – тогда враг, диавол, ничего не успеет относительно к потаенному сердца человеку» (EL5). Our English: "When we remain in silence, the enemy, the devil, can do nothing against the hidden man of the heart."
- Inner life: EL5 says the tray and the face-down bow were only the outward signs. The real work was "silence of the mind", giving up every worldly thought to give himself wholly to the Lord. In his own words (as Elagin reports them) silence gives "tenderness and meekness" and, in the end, "peace of soul"; it brings a person near to God and makes him "as it were an angel on earth" (EL5, same page).
- Reason given for the silence: two reasons in EL5. (a) He took Arsenius and John the Silent as models. (b) Asked by brothers who regretted his withdrawal why he lived alone when he could teach by word and example, he answered with the Fathers: «возлюби праздность безмолвия предпочтительно насыщению алчущих в мире» (Isaac the Syrian), and Gregory the Theologian: it is fine to theologize for God, but better to purify oneself for God (EL5). Our English of the first: "Love the leisure of silence before the feeding of the hungry in the world."
- Testimony (Elagin 1863, ch.5, public-domain): «Сложив руки на груди крестообразно, он становился у дверей потупив лицо долу на землю: сам ни благословит брата, ни даже взглянет на него. А пришедший брат, помолившись, по обычаю, и поклонившись старцу в ноги, полагал пищу на лоточек, лежавший на столе в сенях. С своей стороны о. Серафим клал на лоточек же или малую частицу хлеба или немного капусты. Пришедший брат внимательно замечал это. Сими знаками старец безмолвно давал знать, чего принести в будущее воскресенье: хлеба или капусты.»
- What changed: the brothers asked why; the abbot's council later forced a choice, and he moved into a monastery cell (see SC-07).
- Not in the sources: the brother's name (EL5 gives none for the hermitage; Pavel is named only for the later monastery cell, see SC-07). Any word, nod, or look from Seraphim. His mood or weather beyond "blizzard", "winter". The notes' claim that the silence was "total" for three years is Elagin's phrasing "about three years" (около трех лет); Sergius' dating of 1807 comes from the notes (W-S1), not re-read here.
- Sources: EL5 (opening paragraphs and the paragraph "Многие из братии..."), notes [W-S2 ch.5].

## SC-07 — The coffin in the entry, 1810–1825
- Status: full
- When and where: the monastery cell at Sarov (a cell with an entry, "сени"), from the move in 1809/1810 to the opening of the door. Sergius (SG) says five years of enclosure with almost no visitors; 17 years in all in the cell (SG).
- Who was there: Seraphim; the brother who brought food (named in EL5 as Father Pavel, living close by); later visitors: Bishop Jonah of Tambov (came to the cell and was refused), and the governor of Tambov A. M. Bezobrazov and his wife (autumn 1815).
- What happened, in order:
  1. He took nothing into the cell, not even necessities, "to cut off self-will". The cell held an icon with a lamp and a stump for a chair. He used no fire (EL5).
  2. He wore an iron cross of five vershoks on ropes under his shirt on his shoulders. Elagin says this is probably what earlier Lives call chains, and that he did not wear real chains or hair-shirt and did not advise others to (EL5; SG says "on his body he wore chains").
  3. His food was only толокно (oatmeal) and chopped white cabbage; his drink water. The brother (Father Pavel) set it at the door and prayed. Seraphim covered himself with a large linen so that nobody saw him, took the dish on his knees "as if receiving food from the hands of God", carried it in, then put the dish back and hid his face again. Sometimes he did not appear, and the brother carried everything away: he fasted (EL5).
  4. SG gives the same facts shortly: food was brought "through one brother living with him" into the entry and set down, until his death. No fire; his hair so matted that scissors barely cut it off "like a cap".
  5. His weekly rule: Matthew on Monday, Mark Tuesday, Luke Wednesday, John Thursday, Acts and Epistles on other days. Through the door in the entry one could sometimes hear him reading and explaining aloud (EL5).
  6. He asked to have a coffin made and set in the entry, "to never forget the hour of death". It was hollowed with its lid from one oak, unpainted, and stood always in the entry. He often prayed there (EL5; SG).
  7. After about five years he loosened the enclosure: the cell door stood open, anyone could come, but he still did not answer questions (EL5).
  8. Bishop Jonah of Tambov came to the cell and Seraphim kept his vow of silence. Abbot Nifont suggested taking the door off its hooks; Jonah refused: "как бы не погрешить нам". EL5 compares Arsenius the Great: "if I open for you, I will open for all".
  9. A week later the governor of Tambov, Alexander Mikhailovich Bezobrazov, came with his wife to ask Seraphim's blessing. Seraphim himself opened the door first and blessed them. "It was in the autumn of 1815" (EL5). SG: "the former governor of Tambov", September 1815, was the first to receive his blessing.
- Details: icon with lamp; stump for a chair; no stove; iron cross on ropes; sacks of sand and stones (CH adds: sacks of sand and stones served as his bed); oak coffin, hollowed with lid, unpainted, in the entry; oatmeal and chopped white cabbage; the large linen cloth over the face; the Gospel schedule.
- Words spoken: «когда я умру, умоляю вас, братия, положите меня в моем гробе» (to Sarov brothers, EL5). Our English: "When I die, I beg you, brothers, put me in my coffin." Sergius' description: «сидел в келлии своей как во гробе, подобно живому мертвецу» (SG): "he sat in his cell as in a tomb, like a living dead man."
- Inner life: the coffin is for remembering the end; SG ties it to Sirach 7:39 («Помни последняя твоя»). Prayer: he read the Jesus Prayer and the Mother of God prayer by turns; sometimes he stood before the icon reading nothing and bowing not at all, "only contemplating the Lord with the mind in the heart" (EL5).
- Testimony (Elagin 1863, ch.5, public-domain): «Чтобы никогда не забывать о часе смертном, чтобы яснее представить и ближе видеть его пред собою, о. Серафим упросил сделать для себя гроб и поставить его в сенях затворнической келлии. По его желанию, гроб был приготовлен; его выдолбили, а также и крышку к нему от цельного дуба, и он, некрашеный, всегда стоял в сенях. Здесь старец часто молился, готовясь к исходу от настоящей жизни.»
- What changed: autumn 1815, the first outsider receives his blessing. After that brothers come, and later laypeople, but he stays in the cell ten more years (CH: 15 years of visits in the monastery cell without leaving it).
- Not in the sources: how food reached him is answered: via the brother Pavel (EL5) or "one living with him" (SG). Do not add a visible "window" or a bell. Do not say the coffin was used as a bed: CH says sacks of sand and stones were his bed; Chichagov and Diveyevo sisters (CH, Varvara Ilyinichna) later call the coffin "ark" with rusks in it (a later time). Do not say Seraphim spoke to the governor; the sources only say he opened the door and blessed them. Conflict: CH puts a Virgin appearance in 1815 telling him to open the door (CH, early paragraph after the coffin passage), but EL5 and SG say he opened it first for the governor in autumn 1815 and no apparition is named. Use the plain version.
- Sources: EL5, SG, CH (paragraphs on the cell; "В 1815 году Господь, по новому явлению ...").

## SC-08 — The door opens, 25 Nov 1825
- Status: full (one gap: no named visitor of the day itself; see Not in the sources)
- When and where: 25 Nov 1825, feast of Clement of Rome and Peter of Alexandria (SG; CH). From the cell at Sarov to the forest; the Virgin's appearance by the Sarovka river, below the old Theologian's well (CH quoting Motovilov's notes).
- Who was there: Seraphim; later, daily visitors. Nikolai Motovilov wrote the Sarovka account in his notes, "as Seraphim himself told me and many others" (CH quoting Motovilov).
- What happened, in order:
  1. After 17 years in the cell Seraphim was worn out, with strong headaches. He asked the doctors. They said: open a vein or go out into the air. He had never been treated, so he chose the air (SG).
  2. He had a rule to do nothing without a sign from above. So in prayer he laid before God his intention and asked for divine confirmation, which he received (SG). CH: "in 1825 he began to ask the Lord for a blessing to end the enclosure."
  3. SG: on 25 Nov 1825, the memory of Clement and Peter, "for the first time he went out from the enclosure into the forest". He built a second cell at the Sarovka, about two versts from the monastery.
  4. CH (own words): the Mother of God, with these saints, appeared in a dream-vision and allowed him to leave the cell and visit the hermitage.
  5. CH quoting Motovilov's notes: walking through the forest by the Sarovka to his far hermitage, he saw the Mother of God below the old well, and behind her two apostles, Peter and John the Theologian. She struck the ground with a staff, a spring burst out, and she said: «Зачем ты хочешь оставить заповедь рабы Моей Агафии – монахини Александры?» (Why do you want to abandon the commandment of My servant Agafia, the nun Alexandra?) She told him to take eight sisters, build the mill community at Diveyevo, and begin with a two-stone windmill cut from Sarov forest. See SC-09.
  5a. Mark: items 4 and 5 differ. Chichagov gives both. Present item 5 as "Motovilov's notes say" and item 4 as Chichagov's summary.
  6. SG: from then on, except feasts and Sundays, he went into the forest nearly every day, cut wood for the cell, tended potato beds, and returned to the monastery by evening. On the way he carried stones in a sack. Asked why, he said: «я, по Ефрему Сирину, томлю томящаго мя» (I, after Ephraim the Syrian, torment him who torments me).
  7. SG: after the exit he went to the early liturgy in the hospital church, and then back to his cell "with a crowd of visitors". Many came every day, especially in summer. Some waited in the monastery to see him; others went to his forest cell.
  8. SG: whoever came, "poor man in rags or rich man in bright clothes ... he kissed all with love, bowed to the ground to all, and while blessing even kissed the hands of many unordained". CH adds that thousands came daily in the last ten years, about 2000 people a day in his cell (CH).
  9. A Diveyevo sister's dated visit shows the greeting (CH, Praskovya Ivanovna, Candlemas 1828/29): he opened the door, "met her cheerfully, calling her: радость моя!" and later "Гряди, гряди, радость моя!"
- Details: stones and sand in the sack, with the Gospel (CH); white linen smock, worn camilavka, axe or hoe in hand (CH); the antiphon he sang in the forest: «Пустынным непрестанное Божественное желание бывает, мира сущим суетнаго кроме» (SG).
- Words spoken: «я, по Ефрему Сирину, томлю томящаго мя» (SG). The greeting «Радость моя!» (CH, several places).
- Inner life: SG says the antiphon was "not a simple beating of the air but the expression of his heart's feeling; he did it in rapture of spirit".
- Testimony (Sergius 1841, public-domain): «Но так как сие он положил себе за правило, чтоб ничего не предпринимать, не получив какого-нибудь извещения свыше; то и в сем случае открыл он в молитвенном возвышении к Господу Богу намерение свое и просил на сие Божественного извещения, которое и получил. Почему 1825 года ноября 25 го дня, в память Святителей Климента Папы Римского и Петра Александрийского, в первый раз вышел он из затвора в лес.»
- What changed: the enclosure ends; the Diveyevo mill community is given to him.
- Not in the sources: his exact words asking for confirmation (SG only summarises). A named, dated visitor on 25 Nov itself. The notes say "Christ is risen" greets every visitor (EL5): I did not find that greeting in the Elagin ch.5 text I read, and not in SG or CH. The writer must source it elsewhere or leave it out. Do not add the weather or the hour. Do not make the sack the first thing he carried out the door: SG ties the sack to the walks between cell and forest.
- Sources: SG, CH (paragraphs from "Принимая посетителей к себе ..." through Motovilov's notes), EL5.

## SC-09 — Mantirov gives everything, 1823–1827
- Status: thin on dates, full on the story
- When and where: Sarov, in the entry of the enclosed cell, beside the coffin (CH). The Zhitie places the healing in the years of the cell and the money in 1827 ("В этом же 1827 году", CH). CH does not give the year of the healing. I did not find it in the Zhitie, in Elagin ch.7, or in Chichagov's Letopis chapters 1 to 5 (no "Мантур" found there). The outline's "1823" is unsourced.
- Who was there: Seraphim; Mikhail Vasilievich Mantirov (Мантуров), a landowner of Nucha, Ardatov district, 40 versts from Sarov; his serfs who led him in (CH).
- What happened, in order:
  1. Mantirov had served in Livonia, married Anna Mikhailovna Erntz, a Lutheran, and fell so ill that he left service and came to his estate. The best doctors could not name the disease. Pieces of bone fell out of his legs (CH).
  2. His serfs led him with great difficulty to the entry of the cell. Seraphim came out and asked: «Что пожаловал, посмотреть на убогого Серафима?» Mantirov fell at his feet and begged for healing.
  3. Seraphim asked three times: «Веруешь ли ты Богу?» Three times he said yes. Then Seraphim: «Радость моя! Если ты так веруешь, то верь же и в то, что верующему всё возможно от Бога, а потому веруй, что и тебя исцелит Господь, а я, убогий Серафим, помолюсь.»
  4. He sat Mantirov near the coffin in the entry, went into the cell, and came out with holy oil. He said «По данной мне от Господа благодати я первого тебя врачую!» (the first he healed), anointed Mantirov's legs, put linen stockings on them, then filled the skirts of his coat with rusks and told him to walk to the monastery guesthouse with the load.
  5. Mantirov walked, found himself well. He fell at Seraphim's feet; Seraphim raised him and said sharply: «Разве Серафимово дело мертвить и живить, низводить во ад и возводить? ... Господу Всемогущему, да Пречистой Его Матери даждь благодарение!»
  6. Some time later Mantirov returned. Seraphim greeted him: «Радость моя! А ведь мы обещались поблагодарить Господа, что Он возвратил нам жизнь-то!» Mantirov: «Я не знаю, батюшка, чем и как; что же вы прикажете?!»
  7. Seraphim, "looking at him in a special way, said cheerfully": «Вот, радость моя, всё, что ни имеешь, отдай Господу и возьми на себя самопроизвольную нищету!» Mantirov thought of the rich young man, of his young wife. Seraphim read the thoughts: «Оставь всё и не пекись о том, о чём ты думаешь; Господь тебя не оставит ни в сей жизни, ни в будущей; богат не будешь, хлеб же насущный всё будешь иметь.» Mantirov answered at once: «Согласен, батюшка! Что же благословите мне сделать?»
  8. Seraphim tested him: "let us pray and I will show you how God instructs me" (CH).
  9. By blessing Mantirov sold the estate, freed his serfs, kept the money for a time, and bought only 15 desyatins at Diveyevo on the place Seraphim showed, with the order never to sell it but leave it to Seraphim's community. He lived there with his wife in want, was mocked by friends, and reproached by Anna Mikhailovna (CH).
  10. From then on Seraphim called him "Мишенька" and left all Diveyevo business to him alone (CH).
  11. In 1827 Seraphim told Mantirov that the "poor little community" had no church of its own and should have one built beside the Kazan church porch, to the Nativity of the Son; "выстрой ты храм ... сиротам моим!" Mantirov's kept money paid for the Nativity church (CH).
  12. The mill: EL7 notes the mill ground on 7 July 1827; Diveyevo sisters carried timber with Sarov horses (notes [W-S2 ch.7], not re-read here); CH gives a sisters' story about fetching logs from Seraphim in the forest (Praskovya Semyonovna).
- Details: 40 versts to Nucha; holy oil; linen stockings (чулки из посконного холста); rusks (сухари) in the coat skirts; the coffin in the entry as the seat; Mantirov's pieces of bone.
- Words spoken: as above, all exact from CH (original Russian).
- Inner life: not given; CH only says Seraphim looked "in a special way" and was cheerful. EL5/EL7 not read for a prayer here.
- Testimony (Chichagov 1903, public-domain, 105 words): «Тогда о. Серафим, взглянув на него особенным образом, весело сказал: «Вот, радость моя, всё, что ни имеешь, отдай Господу и возьми на себя самопроизвольную нищету!» Смутился Мантуров; тысяча мыслей пробежали у него в голове в один миг, ибо он никак не ожидал такого предложения со стороны великого старца. Ему вспомнился евангельский юноша, которому Христос предложил также добровольную нищету для совершенного пути в Царство Небесное...»
- What changed: a rich man becomes poor, and the money builds Diveyevo's first church. He stays Seraphim's closest friend.
- Not in the sources: the year of the healing; the amount of money; the date of the second visit. The notes' phrase "the sisters carry timber" belongs to EL7 and CH (Praskovya Semyonovna story), not to the Mantirov scene. Do not say Mantirov built the mill with the money: CH ties his money to the church, not the mill.
- Sources: CH (Mantirov paragraphs, and "В этом же 1827 году ..."). Spellings: Мантуров, Мантурова; the notes' Mantirov/Manturov/Mantorov.

## SC-10 — "Some are scandalized", 1825–1832
- Status: full
- When and where: Sarov monastery and the forest cell, after 1825. The sayings are undated in SG. The Evdokia testimony speaks of Abbot Nifont's time (CH).
- Who was there: Seraphim; an unnamed brother (SG); Abbot Nifont, two soldiers on guard, Sister Evdokia Efremovna (monk Evpraksia) (CH); Fr Georgy (Nikolo-Barkovskaya) and the former guest-master Gury (CH).
- What happened, in order:
  1. SG: "Для некоторых тяжек к видению" (for some he was hard to look at); but he kept peace with those who hated him and treated them gently (SG).
  2. SG: a brother told him: "You are bothered by people of both sexes, and you let everyone in without distinction." Seraphim answered with Hilarion the Great, who did not close his door for strangers: if I close mine, and people thirsty for a word of comfort conjure me by God and go away sad, what answer will I give at the Last Judgment? (paraphrase).
  3. SG: another brother: "Why do you teach everyone?" He cited Tobit 12:6 and the Church's hymn: "do not hide the words of God."
  4. SG: someone said to him: "Некто сказал Отцу Серафиму: Тобою некоторые соблазняются." His answer: «Я не соблазняюсь тем, что мною одни пользуются, а другие соблазняются.» The speaker and date are not given.
  5. Concrete opposition (CH, Sister Evdokia Efremovna): Sarov monks "гнали и преследовали его за нас постоянно". Seraphim had given her a heavy sack and sent her through the Holy Gates ("никого не бойся"). Two soldiers on guard seized her and took her to Abbot Nifont. The abbot, "strict", made her untie the sack: old bast shoes, broken crusts, wood chips and stones. Nifont cried: «Ах, Серафим, Серафим! ... сам-то мучается, да и Дивеевских-то мучает!» and let her go. A second time the sack held sand and stones, and again she was taken to the abbot.
  6. Evdokia, after: "Саровские игумен и казначей с братиею больно скорбели на батюшку, что всё даёт-де нам, посылает" — the abbot and treasurer were hurt that he sent supplies (honey, linen, oil, candles, incense, wine) to the sisters (CH).
  7. CH: Seraphim prayed that a century-old tree would bow toward the sisters as a sign. Next morning it lay uprooted in calm weather. Two witnesses (Anna Alekseevna; Georgy and Gury) tell it. Gury's account: Seraphim told them: "вы и многие меня за это зазирали, что для чего я ими занимаюсь" (you and many reproached me for this).
  8. Humor: Evdokia says "он ... всё переносил благодушно, даже смеялся, и часто, сам зная это, шутил над нами" (CH). Another sister, Varvara Ilyinichna, saw him laugh and strike his hands when she cried over a Diveyevo punishment (CH).
- Details: the sack of old shoes, crusts, wood scraps, stones, sand; soldiers at the gate; the oak or "pine" uprooted: Gury's account says he was cutting a fallen pine, the Zhitie calls it a "century tree" (вековое дерево); Seraphim's way of sending Evdokia through the front gate instead of the back gate by the horse yard.
- Words spoken: «Я не соблазняюсь тем, что мною одни пользуются, а другие соблазняются.» (Seraphim, SG). English: "I am not scandalized that some profit from me and others are scandalized." Also the Nifont exclamation above (CH).
- Inner life: SG: he never took credit for good deeds; he answered praise with «Не нам, Господи, не нам, но имени Твоему даждь славу» (Ps 113:9). Why he cared for Diveyevo: the commandment of the old abbots Pachomius and Isaiah that Sarov never abandon the community (Seraphim to Georgy and Gury, CH).
- Testimony (Sister Evdokia Efremovna, in Chichagov 1903, public-domain, about 65 words): «То всем уж известно, как не любили Саровцы за нас батюшку о. Серафима; даже гнали и преследовали его за нас постоянно, много-много делая ему терпения и скорби! А он, родной наш, всё переносил благодушно, даже смеялся, и часто, сам зная это, шутил над нами.»
