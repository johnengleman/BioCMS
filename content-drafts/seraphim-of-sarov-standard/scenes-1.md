## SC-01 — The icon in the rain, c. 1764 (Kursk, the Moshnin yard)
- Status: full
- When and where: Prokhor was "about ten" (SERG; ELAG1 says ten). Kursk, the street where the parents' house stood; the procession turns into the Moshnin yard.
- Who was there: Prokhor, his mother Agafia, clergy of the Kursk Znamenie (Sign) monastery carrying the icon (SERG).
- What happened, in order:
  1. A long illness; in the worst time Prokhor sees the Mother of God in a dream. She promises to visit and heal him; he tells his mother on waking (ELAG1 only).
  2. Clergy carry the Kursk wonderworking icon along his street. A heavy rain makes mud and bog (SERG: "грязь и топь"); they take the icon through Prokhor's yard (SERG). ELAG1 adds that this was "probably" to shorten the way and avoid the mud: the reason is Elagin's guess.
  3. His mother meets the icon, carrying the sick boy; she lets him kiss it. SERG: the icon is then carried over him, and he is healed. ELAG1: she brings him to the icon and holds him "under its shadow".
  4. ELAG1: he began to recover from that time and soon was well. SERG: mother and son give thanks.
- Details: Name in ELAG1: icon "Знамения Божией Матери". SERG: "Курская Чудотворная Икона". The boy had begun church-reading lessons; the illness stopped them (ELAG1). The fall from the building at age seven is the preceding event in both Lives (not our scene).
- Words spoken: none quoted. The Mother of God's promise in the dream is given only as indirect speech (ELAG1).
- Testimony (SERG, Sergius 1841; public-domain; original Russian):
  "Когда было ему около десяти лет, случилась с ним болезнь, которою он одержим был долгое время. Священнослужителям тамошнего Знаменского монастыря случилось идти с Курскою Чудотворною Иконою Божией Матери по городу тою улицею, на которой находился дом родителей Прохора, и как в то время был сильный дождь, от которого произшедшая грязь и топь препятствовали далее нести Икону улицею; то для удобнейшего шествия понесли Ее чрез двор Прохора. Мать Прохорова, увидевши сие, и обрадовавшись сему внезапному случаю, вышла в сретение Чудотворной Иконы Божией Матери, неся на руках и больного отрока Прохора – она поднесла его к Иконе и дала ему приложиться к оной. По сем Чудотворная Икона перенесена была чрез него, и в тоже время отрок получил чудесное и совершенное исцеление."
  English: When he was about ten he fell ill, and the illness held him a long time. The clergy of the Znamensky monastery happened to be carrying the wonderworking Kursk icon of the Mother of God along the street where Prokhor's parents lived. A heavy rain made mud and bog that blocked the way, so they carried the icon through Prokhor's yard. His mother saw this, rejoiced at the sudden chance, and came out to meet the icon carrying the sick boy. She brought him to the icon and let him kiss it. Then the icon was carried over him, and at that same time the boy received a wonderful and complete healing.
- What changed: the Mother of God enters his life; ELAG1 says his studies went on and he came to love the Bible and spiritual books.
- Not in the sources: the child's words, his mother's words, the day or month, the size of the crowd, who carried the icon by name, the sound or colour of the rain beyond "heavy". SERG does not mention the dream. SERG says "complete healing at that same time"; ELAG1 says gradual recovery. The two Lives differ on this.
- Sources: SERG (p. 1841, early chapter); ELAG1 ch.1; W-S1; W-S2 ch.1.

## SC-02 — The copper cross, 1777 or 1778 (Kursk)
- Status: full
- When and where: age 17 (ELAG1: "семнадцатилетний", "на семнадцатом году"); SERG says "на восемнадцатом году" (18th year). Kursk, the family house; the brother Alexei lives with the mother.
- Who was there: Prokhor, his widowed mother Agafia. Elagin names the brother Alexei as then living with her.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Prokhor works in his elder brother's shop; his heart is not in trade (ELAG1). He reads spiritual books to friends on holidays and befriends a Kursk holy fool (ELAG1).
  2. He asks his mother carefully whether he might go to a monastery; she does not oppose it (ELAG1).
  3. Merchant friends (Druzhinin, Bezkhodarny, Melenin and two more) say they may join him (ELAG1).
  4. He obtains release from the Kursk town society (ELAG1; SERG).
  5. Farewell: they sit a little, by Russian custom; Prokhor rises, prays, bows to his mother's feet and asks her blessing. She lets him kiss the icons of the Saviour and the Mother of God, then blesses him with a copper cross (ELAG1).
  6. He keeps it to the end, always worn openly on his chest (ELAG1).
  7. SERG: before Sarov he walked to Kiev to venerate the relics, then reached Sarov 20 Nov 1778 (eve of the Entry of the Mother of God).
- Details: shop goods: straps, tar, cord, bow-yokes, harness, bast shoes, iron (ELAG1). The cross is copper (ELAG1). He says later: "Родом я – из Курских купцов, и когда не был в монастыре, мы, бывало торговали таким товаром, который больше барыша дает" (ELAG1, quoting him to N. A. M. = Motovilov at Sarov): "I come from Kursk merchants, and when I was not in the monastery we traded in goods that give more profit."
- Words spoken: Elagin gives no words of the mother and none of Prokhor at the farewell.
- Testimony (ELAG1, Elagin 1863; public-domain; Elagin narrates, he is not an eyewitness):
  "Трогательно было его прощание с матерью! По русскому обычаю, собравшись совсем, они посидели немного, потом Прохор, встав, помолился Богу и, поклонившись матери в ноги, просил ее родительского благословения. Агафья дала ему приложиться к иконам Спасителя и Божией Матери, потом благословила его медным крестом. Взяв с собою этот крест, как знамение материнского благословения, как символ, в котором соединяются все обязанности инока, о. Серафим до конца жизни сохранил его, нося всегда открыто на груди своей."
  English: His farewell to his mother was touching. By Russian custom, when all had gathered, they sat a little. Then Prokhor stood, prayed to God, bowed to his mother's feet and asked her parental blessing. Agafia let him kiss the icons of the Saviour and the Mother of God, then blessed him with a copper cross. He took this cross as the sign of his mother's blessing, a symbol that holds all a monk's duties, and kept it to the end of his life, always worn openly on his chest.
- Inner life: ELAG1 (narrator, not Seraphim) says she wept bitterly, he "probably wept no less", and he crossed himself toward the church and left "like Abraham". This is Elagin's reading, not a witness: mark as such if used.
- What changed: he leaves the trade and the town for Sarov; the cross stays on his chest at death (ELAG1 ch.10 per notes).
- Not in the sources: any spoken goodbye, the room, the hour, the season, the brother's presence at the farewell, any words of Agafia. The year is not fixed: SERG says 18th year, ELAG1 17th.
- Sources: ELAG1; SERG; W-S1; W-S2 ch.1.

## SC-03 — The True Physician, 1780 (Sarov)
- Status: full
- When and where: 1780, Sarov hermitage; Prokhor is a novice; he lies sick in his cell (CHICH: the later site of the hospital). Illness lasted three years, at least half of it in bed (CHICH).
- Who was there: Prokhor; the builder (abbot) Pachomius; elder Isaiah; treasurer Hieromonk Joseph; later the church-woman Ksenia, who heard the story from him.
- What happened, in order:
  1. His whole body swells. No doctor can name the disease; they suppose dropsy (CHICH). Pachomius and Isaiah take turns at his bed (CHICH).
  2. They fear for his life. Pachomius urges a doctor or bloodletting (CHICH; SERG).
  3. Prokhor answers (see words). Pachomius does not mention medicine again (SERG).
  4. Joseph offers to serve an all-night vigil and a liturgy for Prokhor's health; Pachomius agrees. Prokhor is confessed and communed; SERG: the Communion was brought to his cell after the liturgy and he was left so.
  5. SERG: "in the following night he was found worthy of a divine visit of some kind", then he soon recovered fully. CHICH: he recovered quickly, which astonished everyone.
  6. CHICH: later Seraphim told some people what he saw: after Communion the Mother of God appeared in unspeakable light with Apostles John and Peter; she turned to John and pointed to Prokhor.
  7. CHICH: Seraphim told Ksenia the Mother of God laid her right hand on his head and touched him with the staff in her left; a hollow formed on his right thigh and the water ran out. He had her put her hand in it.
- Details: bed, cell, vigil, Communion; the illness and the doctor's refusal. A hollow big enough that "a whole fist goes in" (Ksenia, CHICH). The 1804 healing vision in ELAG4 (see SC-05 note) has the same Mother of God / Peter / John scene but with doctors present: do not merge the two.
- Words spoken:
  - Prokhor to Pachomius (SERG, original): "я предал себя Истинному Врачу душ и телес, Господу нашему Иисусу Христу, и Пречистой Его Матери, Преблагословенной Деве Марии." English: "I have given myself to the True Physician of souls and bodies, our Lord Jesus Christ, and His Most Pure Mother, the Most Blessed Virgin Mary." CHICH adds: "если же любовь ваша рассудит, снабдите меня, убогого, Господа ради, небесным врачевством – причастием Св. Тайн" (if your love thinks fit, give me, poor man, for the Lord's sake, the heavenly medicine, the Communion of the Holy Mysteries).
  - The Mother of God to John (CHICH): "Этот Нашего рода!" English: "This one is of Our kin."
- Testimony (CHICH, 1903; public-domain; Seraphim speaking to Ksenia, as she told it):
  "«Правую-то ручку, радость моя,– говорил о. Серафим церковнице Ксении,– положила мне на голову, а в левой-то ручке держала жезл; и этим-то жезлом, радость моя, и коснулась убогого Серафима; у меня на том месте, на правом бедре-то, и сделалось углубление, матушка; вода-то вся в него и вытекла, и спасла Царица Небесная убогого Серафима; а рана пребольшая была, и до сих пор яма-то цела, матушка, погляди-ка, дай ручку!» – «И батюшка, бывало, сам возьмёт, да и вложит мою руку в яму,– прибавляла матушка Ксения,– и велика же она была у него, так вот весь кулак и взойдёт!»"
  English: "Her right hand, my joy," Father Seraphim said to the church-woman Ksenia, "she laid on my head, and in her left hand she held a staff. With this staff, my joy, she touched poor Seraphim. In that place, on the right thigh, a hollow formed, mother. All the water ran out into it, and the Queen of Heaven saved poor Seraphim. The wound was very big, and the pit is still there, mother. Look, give me your hand!" "And Father would take my hand himself and put it into the hollow," Mother Ksenia added, "and it was so big that my whole fist went in."
- Inner life: SERG: he later "told the secret" only to some. CHICH: the illness strengthened his faith, love and hope in God.
- What changed: he first stakes his life on God; he recovers; Joseph and Pachomius see his faith; he later collects money for the hospital church (CHICH).
- Not in the sources: the season, Communion hour, any words between Joseph and Prokhor, who else was in the room, what Prokhor wore. The hollow: CHICH says thigh; the notes say Ioasaf puts it in the side (W-S3, called unreliable). SERG gives no words of the Mother of God and no Peter or John.
- Sources: SERG; CHICH; OW-S2; OW-S3; CM-S1; W-S3.

## SC-04 — A thousand nights on a stone, c. 1794–1807 (forest)
- Status: full
- When and where: during the hermit years in the forest cell, near Sarov. Dates are not given in the passages; the notes place the hermit years 1794–1804.
- Who was there: Seraphim alone at the stone; "one of the brothers" later; abbot Nifont's draft reply to the bishop of Tambov.
- What happened, in order:
  1. He wants to stand on a stone in imitation of Symeon the Stylite (SERG).
  2. Two stones: one in his cell, one in the forest half-way between the monastery and the cell (SERG). By day, in the cell, he stood from morning to evening, leaving only to eat the food a brother brought. By night, on the forest stone, from evening to morning, hands raised, crying the publican's cry (SERG).
  3. For 1,000 days and 1,000 nights. The body changed: the hair on his head became like felt; a leg disease came that lasted to his death (SERG).
  4. Done so secretly that nobody knew while it lasted (SERG).
  5. ELAG4: the bishop of Tambov put a private question about him to abbot Nifont; Nifont's draft reply, kept in the monastery papers, said they knew of his deeds and life, but of any secret acts, "also of the standing of 1000 days and nights on a stone, nobody knew".
  6. SERG: later he told some of the brothers. One, struck with wonder, said it exceeded human strength. Seraphim answered with Symeon's forty-seven years. See the exchange below.
  7. ELAG4: he gave up the stone prayer when he saw it would wear out spirit as well as body. ELAG4 says he said this: any harsh treatment of the body that exhausts the spirit is "unreasonable".
- Details: ELAG4: he prayed on his feet or on his knees, with raised hands, "like St Pachomius" (Elagin's comparison); the cell-stone kept day and night prayers equal; he left it only for rest and food. He ate only potatoes and vegetables from his garden, then for three years "снить" (SERG). Notes (CM-S4) give 17 hours a day: not read here.
- Words spoken (SERG, original; Elagin has a nearly equal version in ELAG4):
  - Brother: that it is beyond human strength.
  - Seraphim: "сей подвижник благочестия сорок семь лет стоял на столпе, а мои труды похожи ли на его подвиг?" English: "That ascetic of piety stood forty-seven years on a pillar, and is my labour anything like his?"
  - Brother: "Конечно, в этом подвиге для тебя ощутительна была помощь укрепляющей благодати?" English: "Surely you felt the help of strengthening grace in this struggle?"
  - Seraphim: "Иначе не достало бы сил человеческих ... внутренне подкреплялся и утешался я тем небесным даром, который снисходит свыше от Отца Светов", then after a pause: "когда умиление есть, то с нами Бог." English: "Otherwise human strength would not have sufficed. I was strengthened and comforted inside by that heavenly gift that comes from above from the Father of Lights." Then, after a pause: "When there is tenderness of heart, God is with us."
  - Prayer: "Боже, милостив буди мне грешному" ("God, be merciful to me a sinner").
- Testimony (SERG, Sergius 1841; public-domain; Sergius says he had this "from him himself" in the prologue):
  "Образ стояния его был таковой: на одном камне, что был у него в келлии, стоял он с утра до вечера, сходя с него только для принятия пищи, какую приносил ему усердствовавший к нему брат из монастыря; на другом же стоял с вечера до утра с воздетыми горе руками, взывая к Господу мытаревым гласом: Боже, милостив буди мне грешному. В таковом подвиге он находился тысячу дней и тысячу ночей; от чего все тело его изменилось, волосы сделались на голове подобны войлоку, а в ногах получил болезнь, которая продолжалась до самой его кончины."
  English: This was his manner of standing. On one stone, in his cell, he stood from morning to evening, coming down only to take food, which a brother from the monastery who was devoted to him brought. On the other he stood from evening to morning with hands raised, calling to the Lord in the publican's voice: God, be merciful to me a sinner. He kept this labour a thousand days and a thousand nights. His whole body changed, the hair on his head became like felt, and in his legs he got a disease that lasted to his death.
- Inner life: "when there is tenderness of heart, God is with us" is the only statement; he does not describe the prayer itself.
- What changed: the bent legs for life; the secret later told; ELAG4 says he left the stone prayer. The notes' outline ending (three peasants hear he has money) is in ELAG4 ch.4 (see SC-05).
- Not in the sources: the weather, the animals, the time of year, the length of one day of standing beyond "morning to evening", the brother's name, where the brother stood, the date of the talk. SERG puts the telling "later"; ELAG4 puts it "at the end of his days". The two Lives differ on when he told.
- Sources: SERG; ELAG4 ch.4; W-S1; W-S2 ch.4; OW-S2; CM-S4.

## SC-05 — The axe laid down, 12 Sept 1804 (forest)
- Status: full
- When and where: 12 Sept 1804, in the forest. ELAG4: while chopping wood in the forest. SERG: he was in his vegetable garden. The two sources differ.
- Who was there: Seraphim, three peasants unknown to him, dressed as peasants (ELAG4). Later: abbot Isaiah, the monastery confessor, brothers. Peasants named by ELAG4: serfs of the landowner Tatishchev, of the village Kremenki, Ardatov district.
- What happened, in order:
  1. The three demand money: "to you worldly people come and bring money" (SERG and ELAG4). He says: "I take nothing from anyone." They do not believe him (both).
  2. ELAG4: one rushes him from behind to throw him down but falls himself; the three become a little frightened but go on.
  3. ELAG4: he had great strength and an axe, and the thought of defence flashed across his mind. He recalled "all who take the sword shall perish by the sword" (Mt 26:52). He lowered the axe to the ground and, arms crossed on his chest, said the words below.
  4. One picks up the axe and strikes him with the butt on the head; blood flows from mouth and ears (both). He falls unconscious.
  5. They drag and beat him toward the porch of the cell; they talk of throwing him in the river. They tie his hands and feet and leave him in the entry (ELAG4; SERG: tied and dragged to the entry).
  6. They search the cell: oven broken, floor taken up. They find only an icon and some potatoes (ELAG4); SERG: only scanty food. They flee in terror (ELAG4: conscience speaks).
  7. He comes to, loosens himself, thanks God, prays that God forgive the killers, spends the night in pain in the cell (ELAG4).
  8. Next day he comes to the monastery "with great difficulty" during the liturgy (SERG: "с большим трудом пришел"; ELAG4 "сам пришел"). Blood in beard and hair, teeth knocked out, ears and mouth caked with blood; brothers are horrified. He says nothing; asks for abbot Isaiah and the confessor and tells them (both).
  9. ELAG4: after the 7th day Isaiah sends to Arzamas for doctors; they find his head broken, ribs smashed, chest crushed; the Mother of God appears to him in a vision (see SC-03 note). After this he can walk. SERG: "some time later, through the Mother of God's intercession, as he told one brother, he was healed".
  10. The peasants are found. He forgives them and asks the abbot not to punish; he writes the landowner (ELAG4). Plea quoted below. Later a fire burns their homes, and they come weeping for his pardon and prayers (ELAG4).
- Details: ELAG4: an earlier tree-fall had already bent him; the beating made it worse; thereafter he walked with a small axe, a hoe or a stick. SERG: after healing he asks leave to return to the forest; the abbot urges him to stay; he says he is "not afraid of such attacks".
- Words spoken:
  - To the robbers (ELAG4, original): "делайте, что вам надобно" — "Do what you need."
  - To them first (SERG; ELAG4): "я ни от кого ничего не беру" — "I take nothing from anyone."
  - Plea to the abbot (ELAG4, original): "В противном случае – говорил о. Серафим – я оставляю Саровскую обитель и удаляюсь ни для кого неведомо в другие святые места." English: "Otherwise," said Father Seraphim, "I leave Sarov and go away, unknown to anyone, to other holy places."
  - SERG on the plea: "в противном случае лучше он соглашался, чтоб его из обители выслали, нежели им какое-либо оскорбление нанесли" (he would rather be sent away from the monastery than see them offended).
- Testimony (ELAG4, Elagin 1863; public-domain; Elagin narrates, not an eyewitness):
  "О. Серафим имел большую физическую силу и, вооруженный топором, мог бы не без надежды обороняться. Эта мысль и мелькнула было мгновенно в его уме. Но он вспомнил при сем слова Спасителя: вси приемшии нож, ножем погибнут ( Мф. 26:52 ), не захотел сопротивляться, спокойно опустил на землю топор и сказал кротко, сложивши крестообразно руки на груди: «делайте, что вам надобно». Он решился претерпеть все безвинно Господа ради."
  English: Father Seraphim had great physical strength, and armed with the axe could have defended himself with some hope. That thought flashed into his mind. But he remembered the Saviour's words: all who take the sword shall perish by the sword. He did not want to resist, calmly set the axe down on the ground and said meekly, his arms crossed on his chest: "Do what you need." He resolved to suffer everything, innocent, for the Lord's sake.
- Inner life: SERG: he is resolved "to endure all insults, imitating the martyrs", and tells the abbot he does not fear such attacks. ELAG4: he thanks the Lord that he was found worthy to take wounds for Him.
- What changed: the permanently bent back; he returns to the forest (SERG; five months in the monastery per notes, not re-read); the villains are forgiven. SC-06 follows when Isaiah dies in 1807 (SERG).
- Not in the sources: "crawled": both Lives say he "came" with great difficulty; no source says he crawled. No source gives the peasants' words to each other beyond the demand, nor the hour, weather or the abbot's words at the door. The fire's cause is Elagin's inference ("Divine warning"); no source says Seraphim wished it.
- Sources: ELAG4 ch.4; SERG; W-S2 ch.4 Q4; W-S1; OW-S2; CM-S1.
