# Saint Seraphim of Sarov: scene outline (v2)

Keys: OW = notes-own-words.md, W = notes-witnesses.md, CM = notes-church-miracles.md. W-S1 = Sergius 1841; W-S2 ch.N = Elagin 1863; OW-S3 = Chichagov 1903; OW-S1 = the Conversation. SC-nn = scene cards (exact Russian there). Dates Julian. Facts in biography.html are approved; check.md lists removals. Old outline: outline-v1.md.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Seraphim wanted to give himself wholly to God, to be His alone. He sought this by going away: forest, stone, silence, a shut cell with a coffin. His body, a beating, the death of his spiritual father, and the brothers stood in the way, and so did the danger that the desert would close him to his neighbor. Grace answered in 1825: the Mother of God sent him out and gave him a community of poor girls, and what he had gathered in silence began to flow to everyone who came.

Why it was beautiful to him, first heard in ch. 2 (1780), at the first great decision: "I have given myself to the True Physician of souls and bodies, our Lord Jesus Christ, and His Most Pure Mother" [W-S1 Q1; SC-03]. The Mother of God answers: "This one is of Our kin" [OW-S3; SC-03]. He belongs to Their family; in 1831, pointing to the icons: "Here are my family" [W-S4 Q18]. And: "When there is tenderness of heart, God is with us" [W-S1 Q3].

Famous as Russia's best-loved saint, glorified in 1903 before at least 100,000 pilgrims and the emperor [CM-S2; CM-S4]. Set apart: he lived the ancient desert life when "they forbid us the desert" [W-S1 Q10]. Then he bowed to the ground before rich and poor alike [W-S1].

## 2. Portrait

A Kursk merchant's son who kept the merchant's talk: virtues are goods "that give more profit", grace is "capital" [OW-S1 Q3-Q4]. Strong enough to fight three men with an axe; he set it down [W-S2 ch.4]. Hard on himself (stone, iron cross on ropes, sacks of stones for a bed, a coffin) [W-S1; SC-07], gentle in rebuke [OW-S2]. He spoke so fast that Bogdanov wrote his questions down beforehand [SC-12]. He teased: the perfume question "on purpose" [OW-S1 Q12]; "seven maidens than one widow" [W-S2 ch.7]; Evdokia says he bore persecution "even laughing" [OW-S3]. He laughed and clapped when a sister cried [SC-10]. He refused credit and portraits: "Who am I, poor Seraphim?" [W-S1 Q7]. Some brothers found him "hard to look at" [OW-S2]. A fault he saw himself: he overdid the stone, and later called harshness that exhausts the spirit "unreasonable" [SC-04].

With God: standing prayer, the tax collector's prayer, the Jesus Prayer "walking and sitting" [OW-S3 Q19], "contemplating the Lord with the mind in the heart" [SC-07]. "The Lord seeks a heart full of love for God and neighbor; that is the throne where He likes to sit" [OW-S1 Q14].

## 3. The opening

Hook: a broken picture.

> Every Sunday a monk of Sarov carried a week's food out to a hermit's hut in the forest, and in winter he waded through snow to his knees to get there. At the entry he said a prayer. Inside, the hermit said "Amen" to himself, opened the inner door, and stood with his arms crossed and his eyes on the ground. He did not bless the brother or even look at him. He only laid a scrap of bread or a little cabbage on a tray to show what to bring next Sunday, and the brother walked home without having heard one word. For three years Saint Seraphim of Sarov lived like this, and no one at Sarov knew what he was keeping in the silence.

Facts: [SC-06; W-S2 ch.5]. The tray is told here only. The opening and summary.txt do not show the open door, "my joy", the clearing, or 1903 (the current summary spends the open door).

Thesis (2–3 sentences): Russia's best-loved saint [CM-S2; CM-S4]; he lived the old desert life in an age that forbade it [W-S1 Q10] and later received thousands, rich and poor alike [W-S1]; the poor girls he cared for became the Diveyevo convent, where his holy relics rest [CM-S4]. Do not name the light or "not for you alone". Answered in ch. 7, 10, 13–14.

Ending: strongest image = Mother Margarita, 92, lights his candle and receives his relics at Diveyevo, 1 Aug 1991 [CM-S8; SC-15]. Before it, briefly: feasts, the spring, the Conversation still read. At most one quiet sentence after. What he offers today, felt in the story: prayer is open to "rich and poor… righteous and sinner" [OW-S1 Q5], and a stranger is "my joy".

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points, told slowly from inside him:** TP1 the True Physician (1780); TP2 the axe (1804); TP3 the door (1825); TP4 the light (Nov 1831, climax); TP5 the last day and the kneeling death (1833).

**Climax rule:** ch. 10 opens straight in the clearing. Nothing before it previews the light. No source talk before the block quote (cut "he wrote it down… ready to swear"); the authenticity debate goes only in "A Note on the Sources". The merchant quote is told here, once.

**Relationships, to their end:**
- Agafia: farewell and cross (ch. 1) → 1831, "Pray for my parents Isidor and Agafia" [W-S4, Jan 1831] and "Here are my family" (ch. 11) → the copper cross at his death (ch. 12). Her death: fact need.
- Pachomius: receives, nurses, tonsures him, gives the forest; his charge never to abandon Diveyevo [SC-10]. His death: fact need.
- Isaiah: sponsor 1786 → abbot in 1804 → dies 1807; response: silence (ch. 5).
- Pavel: feeds him in the cell → "he never judges anyone" [W-S2 ch.10 Q23] → hears the Easter hymns, finds him (ch. 12).
- Nifont: "nobody knew" (3) → the door hooks (6) → "Ah, Seraphim, Seraphim!" (9) → asked at Christmas 1832 to bury him in the coffin → buries him (12).
- Mantirov: healed, gives all, "Mishenka", the church (8). His end: fact need.
- Motovilov: healed (9) → the clearing (10) → Kursk 1834, apple trees for Diveyevo [W-S3] → his papers printed 1903 (13).
- The sisters ("my orphans"): 1825 → Evdokia's sack → bread money and memorial service, 1833 → closed 1927 → Margarita, 1991.

**Losses at their dates:** father c. 1757/1762; his mother 1777/78; the illness from 1780 (one day: Pachomius and Isaiah at his bed, Joseph brings Communion); five months broken after 1804; Isaiah 1807; the enclosure (one day: Pavel's dish, the linen over his face); the brothers' hostility (gladness and burden together); Ladyzhensky, spring 1832: "we will not see each other again", three times.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** none by his hand. The Conversation, recorded by Motovilov, printed 1903 [OW-S1; OW-S5] (ch. 10, 13). His short rule for lay people [OW-S3 Q18] (ch. 7, one line). "Nastavleniya" is mostly the Fathers [OW-S4]: leave out.
- **What he changed:** Diveyevo, begun 1825–27, a convent from 1861, where his relics rest [W-S2 ch.7; CM-S4] (ch. 7–8, 14). Anthony Medvedev, told "do not forsake my orphans", became abbot of the Trinity Lavra and helped the Life reach print [W-S3] (ch. 13). His spring [CM-S6] (ch. 14, one line).
- **Why famous:** pilgrims at the grave; the Lives from 1841 [W-S3]; the 1903 crowds; the Conversation; the 1991 return (ch. 13–14).
- **Path:** chapel 1861; proposal 1883 (Pobedonostsev opposed); 94 cases examined 1892–94; examination 11 Jan 1903; Synod Act 29 Jan 1903; uncovering of the holy relics 19 July 1903; feasts 2 Jan and 19 July (15 Jan, 1 Aug New Style) [CM-S2; CM-S4] (ch. 13).

**Fact needs:**
1. Pachomius's death and his charge about Diveyevo. Look: Chichagov, Zhitie (OW-S3); Letopis ch.1–5 (azbyka).
2. Mantirov's later life and death. Look: Chichagov, Letopis.
3. Agafia's death, and whether he learned of it. Look: Elagin ch.1–2 (W-S2); Chichagov, Zhitie.

- Levitation, the abbesses, the light at Glinsk: thin or legend [OW-S3; CM-S1].

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth 1754 (Church) vs 1759 (early Lives, grave): use 1754; note.
- Enclosure 1810–1825 = fifteen years; Sergius's "seventeen" counts from 1809. Use fifteen.
- A falling tree and the beating both bent him [W-S2 ch.4].
- He "came with great difficulty", never "crawled" [SC-05].
- No "Christ is risen" greeting (not found). Use "my joy" and "Little father! Little mother! My joy!" [CM-S2].
- Hollow: right thigh, in Ksenia's account [SC-03].
- Cross: Elagin ch.1 says he wore his mother's copper cross to the end; ch.10 says a copper crucifix on his neck. Link with care.
- Mantirov's money built the church, not the mill; no year for his healing [SC-09].
- "True joy" is the emperor's note of 26 Jan 1903 [SC-14]. Crowds 100,000–300,000.
- Relics opened 17 Dec 1920, found 1990; never "vanished" [CM-S4]. Orthodox words: reposed, glorified, holy relics. "Saint" before every saint's name.
