This list gathers the Church’s memory of the works God did through Saint Seraphim of Sarov: healings, visions, and words of foreknowledge. The accounts come from the oldest witnesses, the early Lives, the Diveyevo sisters, and the journal kept at Sarov in 1903. Each account names who told it and gives its source. A Church commission examined ninety-four cases in 1892–1894, and the Holy Synod accepted its findings when it glorified him in 1903. Our sources give the commission’s numbers, not its single cases. Where the Lives differ on a detail, the account says so. Questions about a few accounts are gathered in a short note at the end.

Cases examined by the Church

The Holy Synod governed the Russian Church. Before it glorified him in 1903, a Church commission examined the healings God had worked through his prayers after his death. Our sources give its numbers and its verdict, not the single cases.

A Church commission examines 94 cases, 1892–1894

A Church commission worked from 3 February 1892 to August 1894 in 29 dioceses. It investigated 94 cases of help received through Saint Seraphim’s prayers, and witnesses confirmed most of them with sworn statements. Hundreds of other letters lay unrecorded in the Sarov archive. The report went to the Holy Synod through the bishop of Tambov in 1895, and two more collections followed in 1897. In its act of 29 January 1903 the Synod wrote that no one could doubt the validity of the investigated cases.

Status: investigated by a Church commission. The Holy Synod accepted its findings when it glorified him in 1903.

Source: Holy Synod, Act of 29 January 1903 (English translation, ROCOR), https://synod.com/english/pages/heritage/canonofsersar.html

During Saint Seraphim’s life

Saint Seraphim was born Prokhor Moshnin in Kursk. These accounts come from early Lives by monks who knew him, from the memories of the Diveyevo sisters, and from Motovilov’s papers. They include healings, visions, and words of foreknowledge.

A Kursk fool for Christ foretells Saint Seraphim’s future

A modern chronicle of Saint Seraphim’s life tells a story from Diveyevo sources. About 1757 a local fool for Christ met Saint Seraphim’s mother Agafia with her three-year-old son. A fool for Christ is a person who seems foolish to the world for the sake of faith. The man told Agafia that the boy would be a strong intercessor before the Holy Trinity and would pray fervently for the whole world. An intercessor is one who pleads for others.

Status: tradition, recorded in the chronicle.

Source: Prp. Serafim Sarovskii: khronika zhizni, https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/prp-serafim-sarovskij-hronika-zhizni/

Saint Seraphim survives a fall, Kursk, about 1761

Saint Seraphim was about seven when he fell from a church that was being built in Kursk. His mother ran down expecting to find him dead, but he was standing up unhurt. The Life of 1841 says only that he fell “from the height of the building.” Elagin’s Life of 1863 names a bell tower.

Status: told in the Lives of 1841 and 1863. See the note at the end.

The Mother of God heals Saint Seraphim, about 1764

Saint Seraphim was about ten when he fell ill for a long time. In Elagin’s Life of 1863 he dreamed that the Mother of God promised to visit and heal him. The Life of 1841 says that heavy rain made mud that blocked the street. So the clergy changed their road and carried the wonderworking Kursk icon of the Mother of God through the Moshnin yard, and his mother brought him out to kiss it. Then they carried the icon over him, and the Life of 1841 says he received a complete healing at that moment. Mother and son gave thanks to the Mother of God, their Intercessor and Healer.

Status: told in the Life of 1841. The dream is in Elagin’s Life only, and Elagin says the boy began to recover from that day and was soon well. Elagin names the icon as the Mother of God of the Sign. The Brookline text says Saint Seraphim was nine.

Saint Seraphim recovers after a divine visit, Sarov, 1780

In 1780 Saint Seraphim was still a novice when his body swelled with dropsy, and the monk Avel says it lasted about three years. He refused a doctor and bleeding and said, “I have given myself to the True Physician of souls and bodies, our Lord Jesus Christ and His Most Pure Mother.” The treasurer Joseph served an all-night prayer service and the main church service for him. Holy Communion was brought to his cell after the service. The Life of 1841 says that the next night he was found worthy of “a divine visit,” and he soon recovered. Saint Seraphim Chichagov says the Mother of God appeared with the apostles Saint Peter and Saint John, laid her right hand on his head, and touched him with her staff.

Status: told in the Lives, and the details differ. Georgy’s Life of 1844 has the Mother of God lay her hands on his head. Saint Seraphim Chichagov says a hollow formed on his right thigh, and that Saint Seraphim later had a woman named Ksenia put her hand into it. Ioasaf’s Life of 1849 puts a hollow in his side instead and tells of hair that fell off like felt.

Saint Seraphim sees Christ on Holy Thursday, Sarov

Saint Seraphim served as a deacon at Sarov. On Holy Thursday the Lord Jesus Christ appeared to him during the liturgy, surrounded by the heavenly hosts. Georgy’s Life gives the account in Saint Seraphim’s own words, and the Brookline synaxarion repeats it. A synaxarion is a book of short Lives of saints. The Life of 1841 records visions during the services in general terms.

Status: told in Georgy’s Life (1844) in Saint Seraphim’s own words.

Source: “Rannie zhitiinye povestvovaniia o prepodobnom Serafime Sarovskom”, https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/rannie-zhitijnye-povestvovanija-o-prepodobnom-serafime-sarovskom/; J. Saint Seraphim recovers after the robbers’ beating, Sarov, 1804

On 12 September 1804 three peasants beat Saint Seraphim in the forest with the blunt side of an axe. Elagin says the abbot sent to Arzamas for doctors. They found his head broken, his ribs smashed, and his chest crushed. Elagin says the Mother of God then appeared to him in a vision, and after that he could walk. The Brookline synaxarion says she came with the apostles Saint Peter and Saint John and said of him, “This is one of my kind.” Saint Seraphim told one brother that God had healed him through the prayers of the Mother of God. He stayed bent for the rest of his life.

Status: recorded in the Lives. Sergius says Saint Seraphim was in his vegetable garden, and Elagin says he was cutting wood in the forest. Elagin says he spent about five months in the monastery.

Saint Seraphim opens his door after a vision, 1815

Saint Seraphim Chichagov says that in 1815 the Mother of God appeared to Saint Seraphim again. By this appearance the Lord told him not to hide his light and to open the door of his cell. Elagin says that Saint Seraphim opened the door himself in the autumn of 1815 to bless the governor of Tambov. Sergius says only that the former governor of Tambov was the first to receive his blessing in September 1815. Neither Elagin nor Sergius names a vision.

Status: told in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life (1903).

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/; N. V. Saint Seraphim heals Mikhail Mantirov’s legs, Sarov, about 1823

The landowner Mikhail Mantirov could hardly walk, and his serfs led him to Saint Seraphim’s cell at Sarov. Saint Seraphim asked him three times whether he believed in God, and Mantirov said yes each time. Saint Seraphim told him, “My joy, if you believe so, then believe also that everything is possible from God for one who believes. Believe that the Lord will heal you too. I, poor Seraphim, will pray.” He anointed Mantirov’s legs with oil, and Mantirov walked to the monastery guesthouse and found himself well. He came back and fell at Saint Seraphim’s feet. Saint Seraphim lifted him up and said, “Give thanks to the Lord Almighty and to His Most Pure Mother!” Later Saint Seraphim asked him to give everything to the Lord, and Mantirov’s money paid for a church at Diveyevo.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life, which gives no year. Russian Wikipedia dates it 1823 and calls it the first recorded healing.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/; “Serafim Sarovskii,” Russian Wikipedia, https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%BC_%D0%A1%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9

Saint Seraphim leaves his cell after a vision, 1825

Sergius’s Life of 1841 says that Saint Seraphim asked God to confirm his plan to leave his cell, and that he received the confirmation. On 25 November 1825 he went out into the forest for the first time. Saint Seraphim Chichagov says the Mother of God appeared in a dream-vision with Saint Clement and Saint Peter of Alexandria and allowed him to leave. Saint Seraphim Chichagov also quotes Motovilov’s notes. They say that she appeared to him by the Sarovka river with the apostles Saint Peter and Saint John. She told him to take eight sisters and build the mill community at Diveyevo.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life and in Motovilov’s notes; the two accounts differ on the setting. Russian Wikipedia gives the Diveyevo instructions as a tradition: eight sisters named one by one, the place for the community, and a ditch around it.

A candle burns in the ground, Diveyevo, 1825–1827

Elagin tells this in his chapter on Saint Seraphim and Diveyevo. Sister Maria Ivanovna was in charge of meals for the sisters. One spring morning before the feast of the Holy Trinity she went to the cellar early. She saw a candle burning in the ground behind the sisters’ cell. When she woke the other sisters, the candle was gone. At daybreak the earth at that spot was found dug up. Saint Seraphim then gave the sisters his blessing to dig a ditch around the place of the vision.

Status: told by Elagin from Sister Maria Ivanovna (1863).

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/

A century-old tree is uprooted at Saint Seraphim’s prayer

Saint Seraphim Chichagov says that Saint Seraphim wanted to show everyone that God approved of his care for the Diveyevo community. He chose a century-old tree and prayed that it would bow as a sign. The next morning the tree lay torn up by its huge roots, though the weather was calm. Saint Seraphim Chichagov gives two witness accounts. One comes from the sister Anna Alekseevna, and the other from Georgy and Gury. They found Saint Seraphim cutting up a fallen pine, and he told them he had asked the Lord for this sign.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life from the witnesses.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

The Mother of God opens a spring near Sarov

In a vision Saint Seraphim saw the Mother of God strike the ground with her staff, and water flowed. Saint Seraphim Chichagov quotes Motovilov’s notes. They place the vision by the Sarovka river below the old Theologian’s well. Saint Seraphim dug a well at the spot. He told the monk Anastasius to ask the Mother of God to give the water power to heal. In Helen Kontsevich’s Life he said that the Mother of God had promised the water a greater blessing than the waters of Bethesda in Jerusalem.

Status: told in Motovilov’s notes and in Helen Kontsevich’s Life.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “The Healing Spring of Saint Seraphim of Sarov in Diveyevo” (excerpts from Helen Kontsevich), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2015/01/the-healing-spring-of-saint-seraphim-of.html; Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Water from Saint Seraphim’s sleeve stops a sister’s cough

Helen Kontsevich’s Life tells that Sister Paraskeva Semyonovna of Diveyevo was sick and coughing. Saint Seraphim said to her, “Why are you coughing? … Stop, enough!” She answered, “I can’t, Father!” Then he scooped up some water with his sleeve and poured it over her. The illness left her at once, and Kontsevich says it never came back.

Status: told in Helen Kontsevich’s Life.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “The Healing Spring of Saint Seraphim of Sarov in Diveyevo” (excerpts from Helen Kontsevich), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2015/01/the-healing-spring-of-saint-seraphim-of.html

A paralyzed sister rakes hay and is healed

Saint Seraphim Chichagov records the story of Sister Evdokia Nazarova, who told it herself. As a young girl she had suffered for two years from paralysis of her arms and legs. People led her to Saint Seraphim with great difficulty, and he put a rake in her hands and told her to rake hay. Then she felt something fall away from her, and she raked like a healthy person. She worked until evening prayers and walked home healed.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life, from her own telling.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

A sister’s toothache is cured by Father Pavel

Saint Seraphim Chichagov tells how Sister Ksenia Kuzminichna had a toothache that kept her awake at night. Saint Seraphim told her, “Go to Father Pavel, and he will heal you.” She found Pavel and told him that Saint Seraphim had sent her. He held her face tightly in both hands and stroked her cheeks several times, and her toothache stopped.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life, from Ksenia’s own telling.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Saint Seraphim tells a peasant where his horse is

Elagin and Saint Seraphim Chichagov tell of a peasant who ran into the monastery and fell at Saint Seraphim’s feet. Thieves had stolen his horse, and he had no other way to feed his family. Saint Seraphim held the man’s head against his own and told him to guard himself with silence. He named a village and told the man to pass behind four houses there. The man would find a small gate, and he was to lead his horse out through it without a word. The brothers at Sarov later heard that the man found his horse at that place.

Status: told by Elagin and Saint Seraphim Chichagov (1863, 1903).

Source: N. V. Saint Seraphim is seen praying in the air

Saint Seraphim Chichagov says that Princess E.S.Sh. told this story. She had a sick nephew from Petersburg, and he was carried to Sarov on a bed. Saint Seraphim told him to lie still and not to turn over while he prayed. The young man looked anyway and saw Saint Seraphim standing in the air in prayer, and he cried out. Saint Seraphim told him to tell no one until after Saint Seraphim’s death, or his illness would return. The young man got up and left the cell on his own feet, though he leaned on others. He told the story only after Saint Seraphim had died.

Status: told by Princess E.S.Sh. in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life; her nephew kept it secret until Saint Seraphim’s death.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Two abbesses pass the toll-houses at Saint Seraphim’s prayer

Saint Seraphim Chichagov records that Saint Seraphim himself told this story. Two nuns who had both been abbesses died. The Lord showed him how their souls were led through the “airy toll-houses.” Saint Seraphim prayed for three days and begged the Mother of God for them. By her prayers the Lord forgave them, and they passed through all the toll-houses. In Orthodox tradition the toll-houses are the tests that a soul meets after death.

Status: his own report, recorded by Saint Seraphim Chichagov.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Saint Seraphim feeds a bear near his far hermitage

Elagin’s Life of 1863 tells this from Matrona Pleshcheeva, a Diveyevo sister. She saw Saint Seraphim feeding a bear near his far hermitage, and he let her feed it too. He told her to tell no one for eleven years after his death, and she told it when the eleven years were over. Ioasaf’s Life of 1849 tells it as well.

Status: told by Matrona Pleshcheeva in Ioasaf’s Life (1849) and Elagin’s Life (1863). See the note at the end.

Twelve appearances of the Mother of God

The Brookline synaxarion says the Mother of God appeared to Saint Seraphim twelve times in his life. The last appearance was on the feast of the Annunciation in 1831. With her came Saint John the Baptist, Saint John the Theologian, and twelve virgin martyrs and monastic saints.

Status: told in the Brookline synaxarion. Saint Seraphim Chichagov says Sister Evdokia Efremovna recorded the visit of 25 March 1831.

The Mother of God visits Saint Seraphim, 1830

Saint Seraphim Chichagov records an account from Father Vasily Sadovsky, dated 1830. It says Saint Seraphim told of a visit from the Mother of God. She said to him, “My beloved, ask of me whatever you want!” Saint Seraphim said that he asked her for the salvation of his orphans.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Life.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Saint Seraphim foretells Anthony Medvedev’s new post, 1831

Anthony Medvedev was the abbot of the Vysokogorsk monastery. In January 1831 he came to Saint Seraphim troubled by thoughts of his own death. Saint Seraphim told him, “Divine Providence is entrusting a vast monastery to you.” He also said, “Do not abandon my orphans when your time comes,” and asked, “Pray for my parents Isidor and Agafia.” A monk who traveled with Anthony wept on the way home. Saint Seraphim had told the monk, “Now you will soon be parted from your abbot.” In March 1831 Anthony was made head of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra. A lavra is a great monastery.

Status: recorded in Saint Seraphim Chichagov’s Chronicle; fulfilled.

A peasant with signs of cholera is cured, 1831

The chronicle gives the story of a Likhachev peasant known only by his initials E.V. In 1831 he felt the signs of cholera and set out for Saint Seraphim’s cell, and he could barely crawl there. Then Saint Seraphim told him to walk around the monastery and pray in the cathedral. He said, “There God’s mercy will heal you.” The man did so and returned to the guesthouse completely well.

Saint Seraphim heals Motovilov under a pine, Sarov, 1831

Nikolai Motovilov recorded that he had been nearly paralyzed for more than three years. Saint Seraphim’s face outshines the sun, Sarov, 1831

In November 1831 Saint Seraphim talked with Motovilov in a snowy clearing in the forest. Motovilov wrote that Saint Seraphim said the Lord had shown him what Motovilov had wanted to know since boyhood: the aim of the Christian life. The light spread several fathoms around and lit the snow. A Russian fathom is about two meters. Motovilov felt peace, sweetness, joy, and warmth in the winter forest.

Saint Seraphim foretells his death, 1832–1833

Elagin records that a few months before Saint Seraphim died he had a brother blow out a candle. He told Brother Pavel, “While I live there will be no fire. When I die, a fire will reveal my death.” On 2 January 1833 Pavel smelled smoke at his door, and linen and books were smoldering inside. The brothers found that Saint Seraphim had reposed in the Lord, on his knees before the icon of the Mother of God.

Thunder over Leningrad when the relics are found

One account says that on the night the relics were found in Leningrad there was thunder and lightning over the city.

Status: told by Sanidopoulos. He dates the find to 11 January 1991. Russian Wikipedia says the remains were found in autumn 1990 and handed over on 11 January 1991.

Saint Seraphim’s candle is lit at Diveyevo, 1991

Sanidopoulos tells that before Saint Seraphim died he gave the Diveyevo nuns a candle. He said that with it they would one day receive him again. The candle passed from one generation of nuns to the next until Mother Margarita had it. She was 92 in 1991 and the last nun of Diveyevo from before its closing in 1927. When the relics came to Diveyevo that year, she lit the candle to receive them. Russian Wikipedia says the relics were welcomed there on 1 August 1991.

Status: told by Sanidopoulos (2010) and in Russian Wikipedia.

Source: J. Pilgrims report cures at the spring today

At the spring of Saint Seraphim pilgrims immerse themselves three times, even when the air is below freezing. Pilgrims tell of many healings there.

Status: told in Helen Kontsevich’s Life, as Sanidopoulos gives it.

A Note on the Sources

A few accounts above carry questions, and we keep them here. Elagin names a bell tower as the place Saint Seraphim fell from in about 1761. Russian Wikipedia notes that the tower did not yet exist then. The bear story rests on Matrona Pleshcheeva’s telling. A file in a Moscow archive holds Metropolitan Saint Philaret’s papers. It says that before Matrona died she said the monk Ioasaf had taught her to tell it. On 7 December 1849 Abbot Isaiah of Sarov wrote that Ioasaf’s accounts of miracles did not match what was known at Sarov. The conversation with Motovilov about the Holy Spirit is recorded by Motovilov alone, and its authenticity has been debated since Nilus published it in 1903. The scholar Strizhev has studied the note with the prophecies about Russia. He holds that only the passage Motovilov marked as Saint Seraphim’s words can be traced to him, and that the rest is Motovilov’s own. The saying “I will glorify the tsar who glorifies me” is handed down about Saint Seraphim, but the sources we read name no early witness for it.
