This list gathers the accounts of healings, visions, and foreknowledge attributed to Seraphim of Sarov, with their sources and their status. Each entry says who reported the event and how the report was treated. It makes no claim of scientific proof, and it does not dismiss any account. Only the cases examined by the Church commission of 1892–1894 were investigated and accepted by the Holy Synod, and our sources do not name them one by one. Every other entry is a report, and its status line says who made it. Where the sources disagree, the entry says so.

Cases examined by the Church

The Church’s inquiry before the canonization of 1903 examined healings attributed to Seraphim’s prayers after his death. Our sources give its numbers and its verdict, not the individual cases.

Ninety-four cases before the Synod, 1892–1894

From 3 February 1892 to August 1894 a Church commission gathered reports of healings and help through Seraphim’s prayers from twenty-nine dioceses. It investigated ninety-four cases, and most of them were confirmed by the sworn statements of witnesses. Its report went to the Holy Synod through the bishop of Tambov in 1895, and two more collections followed in 1897. Hundreds of other letters lay unrecorded in the Sarov archive. In its act of 29 January 1903 the Synod wrote that the cases left no doubt about their validity.

Status: investigated by a Church commission and accepted by the Holy Synod for the canonization of 1903.

Source: Holy Synod, Act of 29 January 1903 (English translation, ROCOR), https://synod.com/english/pages/heritage/canonofsersar.html

During Seraphim’s life

These accounts come from the Lives written by monks who knew him, from the memories of the Diveyevo sisters, and from Motovilov’s papers. They include healings, visions, and words of foreknowledge.

A fall from the unfinished church, Kursk

When Prokhor was about seven, he fell from high on the church his family was building in Kursk. His mother ran down, sure that she would find him dead, and found him standing, unhurt. The Life of 1841 says only that he fell “from the height of the building.” The bell tower named in Elagin’s Life of 1863 did not yet exist at the time, according to the Russian Wikipedia article, which treats that detail as legend.

Status: recorded in the Lives; the bell-tower detail is a later addition.

Healed under the icon of the Sign, Kursk

At about ten (the Brookline synaxarion says nine), Prokhor fell gravely ill for a long time. Elagin’s Life says that in a dream the Mother of God promised to visit and heal him. Rain and mud turned a procession with the Kursk icon of the Mother of God of the Sign through the Moshnin yard. His mother carried him out, he was held up under the icon (the Russian Wikipedia article says he kissed it), and he recovered.

Status: recorded in the Lives; the dream is in Elagin’s Life only.

Dropsy cured after “a divine visit,” Sarov, 1780

As a novice he swelled with dropsy, an illness that the monk Avel says lasted about three years. He refused a doctor and bleeding, saying he had given himself to “the True Physician of souls and bodies.” The treasurer Joseph served a vigil and the Liturgy for him and brought him Communion, and the next night came what the Life of 1841 calls “a divine visit.” Georgy’s Life (1844) says the Mother of God laid her hands on his head; Chichagov says she came with the apostles Peter and John, laid her right hand on his head, and touched his thigh with the staff in her left, and that Seraphim later had a woman named Ksenia put her hand into the hollow it left. Ioasaf’s Life (1849) alone adds a hollow in his side and hair that fell off like felt.

Status: recorded in the Lives; the details differ between them.

Source: Sergius (Vasiliev), Skazanie o zhizni i podvigakh … ottsa Serafima (1841), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/skazanie-o-zhizni-i-podvigah-blazhennnoj-pamjati-ottsa-serafima-sarovskoj-pustyni-ieromonaha-i-zatvornika/; Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/; “Rannie zhitiinye povestvovaniia o prepodobnom Serafime Sarovskom”, https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/rannie-zhitijnye-povestvovanija-o-prepodobnom-serafime-sarovskom/; Prp. Christ in the Liturgy on Holy Thursday

As a deacon at Sarov, serving at the Liturgy on Holy Thursday, Seraphim saw Christ surrounded by the heavenly hosts. Georgy’s Life gives the account in Seraphim’s own words, and the Brookline synaxarion repeats it. The Life of 1841 records visions during the services in general terms.

Status: recorded; his own report.

Source: “Rannie zhitiinye povestvovaniia o prepodobnom Serafime Sarovskom”, https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/rannie-zhitijnye-povestvovanija-o-prepodobnom-serafime-sarovskom/; J. Recovery after the robbers’ beating, 1804

On 12 September 1804 three peasants beat him with the butt of an axe, breaking his head and chest, and he spent about five months in the monastery. The Brookline synaxarion says the Mother of God appeared with the apostles Peter and John and said of him, “This is one of my kind.” Seraphim himself told one brother only that he was healed by the intercession of the Mother of God. He walked bent for the rest of his life.

Status: recorded in the Lives.

Source: Sergius (Vasiliev), Skazanie o zhizni i podvigakh … ottsa Serafima (1841), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/skazanie-o-zhizni-i-podvigah-blazhennnoj-pamjati-ottsa-serafima-sarovskoj-pustyni-ieromonaha-i-zatvornika/; J. Mikhail Manturov’s legs, 1823

Mikhail Manturov, later a benefactor of Diveyevo, had lost the use of his legs. Seraphim told him, “If you believe so, then believe this too: that to one who believes, everything is possible from God … and I, poor Seraphim, will pray.” He anointed Manturov’s legs with oil, and Manturov was healed. Seraphim then asked him to give everything to the Lord and take on voluntary poverty, and Manturov’s money later built the Diveyevo mill and church. The Russian Wikipedia article calls this the first recorded healing and dates it 1823.

Status: recorded in Chichagov’s Life.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/; “Serafim Sarovskii,” Russian Wikipedia, https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%BC_%D0%A1%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9

The vision that ended his enclosure, 25 November 1825

Before leaving his cell, Seraphim asked God to confirm it. On 25 November 1825, the feast of St. Clement, the Mother of God appeared to him with St. Clement and St. Peter of Alexandria and released him from his enclosure, according to Chichagov’s Life of 1903, which calls it a dream-vision. In the account Motovilov wrote down from Seraphim’s own telling, he saw her by the Sarovka as he walked through the forest, and she gave the community at Diveyevo into his care. In that account she also showed him the plan of the community, the ditch to be dug around it, and eight of its sisters by name.

Status: recorded in Chichagov’s Life and in Motovilov’s notes; the two accounts differ on the setting, and the details of the plan rest on Motovilov’s notes.

Source: Sergius (Vasiliev), Skazanie o zhizni i podvigakh … ottsa Serafima (1841), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/skazanie-o-zhizni-i-podvigah-blazhennnoj-pamjati-ottsa-serafima-sarovskoj-pustyni-ieromonaha-i-zatvornika/; Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/; “Serafim Sarovskii,” Russian Wikipedia, https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%BC_%D0%A1%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9

A candle in the ground, Diveyevo, about 1827

Sister Maria Ivanovna reported that she saw a candle burning in the ground behind Seraphim’s cell, and then it was gone. The next morning the earth there had been dug. Elagin tells it in his chapter on the mill community and the ditch of the Mother of God.

Status: recorded in Elagin’s Life; one witness.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/

The Theologian’s Spring near Sarov

In a vision, Seraphim saw the Mother of God strike the ground with her staff, and water flowed. He dug a well at the spot and told a man named Anastasius to ask for healing from it. The Mother of God, he said, had promised to give the water a greater blessing than the waters of Bethesda in Jerusalem.

Status: Diveyevo tradition, from Helen Kontsevich’s Life.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “The Healing Spring of Saint Seraphim of Sarov in Diveyevo” (excerpts from Helen Kontsevich), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2015/01/the-healing-spring-of-saint-seraphim-of.html

Paraskeva Semyonovna’s cough, Diveyevo

Sister Paraskeva Semyonovna was coughing when Seraphim threw water from the spring over her from his sleeve. “Why are you coughing? … Stop, enough!” he said. “I can’t, Batiushka!” she answered, and the cough stopped.

Status: Diveyevo tradition, from Helen Kontsevich’s Life.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “The Healing Spring of Saint Seraphim of Sarov in Diveyevo” (excerpts from Helen Kontsevich), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2015/01/the-healing-spring-of-saint-seraphim-of.html

Evdokia Nazarova rakes hay, Diveyevo

Sister Evdokia Nazarova had been paralyzed for two years. Seraphim told her to go out and rake hay, and she was healed.

Status: recorded by Chichagov from the memories of the Diveyevo sisters.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Ksenia’s toothache, Diveyevo

A woman named Ksenia, who served in the church, had a toothache. Chichagov records that it was cured by Father Pavel, at Seraphim’s sending.

Status: recorded by Chichagov from the memories of the Diveyevo sisters.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

The peasant’s stolen horse

A peasant whose horse had been stolen ran to Seraphim. Seraphim pressed the man’s head to his own and told him which village to go to and which gate to look behind, and warned him to guard himself with silence. The monks of Sarov passed the story on as something they had heard.

Status: hearsay at Sarov, recorded by Elagin and Chichagov.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/; Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Lifted from the ground in prayer

A noblewoman whom Chichagov identifies only as “Princess E. S. Sh.” told of seeing Seraphim raised from the ground as he prayed. The researchers did not read her full account.

Status: one witness, recorded by Chichagov.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Two abbesses and the toll-houses

Seraphim himself said that he had prayed for three days for two departed abbesses, and that by this prayer they were saved from the “airy toll-houses.”

Status: his own report, recorded by Chichagov.

Source: Seraphim (Chichagov), Zhitie prepodobnogo Serafima Sarovskogo (2nd ed., 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Chichagov/zhitie-prepodobnogo-serafima-sarovskogo-chudotvortsa/

Feeding a bear

Matrona Pleshcheeva said she once saw Seraphim feeding a bear, and Elagin’s Life says that Seraphim told her to tell no one for eleven years after his death. A file in the Moscow archives (Metropolitan Philaret’s papers) records that on her deathbed she said the monk Ioasaf had taught her to say she had seen it. On 7 December 1849 Abbot Isaiah of Sarov wrote that Ioasaf’s “miracles” did not match the truth known at Sarov.

Status: contested; the witness is reported to have withdrawn it.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/; “Rannie zhitiinye povestvovaniia o prepodobnom Serafime Sarovskom”, https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/rannie-zhitijnye-povestvovanija-o-prepodobnom-serafime-sarovskom/; “Serafim Sarovskii,” Russian Wikipedia, https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%BC_%D0%A1%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9

Twelve appearances of the Mother of God

The Brookline synaxarion says the Mother of God appeared to Seraphim twelve times in his life. The last, on the feast of the Annunciation in 1831, was with St. John the Baptist, St. John the Theologian, and twelve virgin saints.

Status: synaxarion tradition.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “Saint Seraphim the Wonderworker of Sarov” (Brookline synaxarion text), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2013/01/saint-seraphim-wonderworker-of-sarov.html

A lavra foretold to Anthony Medvedev, January 1831

Anthony Medvedev, abbot of the Vysokogorsk monastery, came to Seraphim terrified of death. Seraphim told him that Divine Providence was entrusting him with a vast lavra, and added, “Do not forsake my orphans,” and “Pray for my parents Isidor and Agafia.” A monk wept when Seraphim told him, “You will soon part from your hegumen.” Anthony became abbot of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra.

Status: recorded in Chichagov’s Chronicle; fulfilled.

Source: Prp. Serafim Sarovskii: khronika zhizni (quoting Chichagov’s Letopis and Motovilov), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/prp-serafim-sarovskij-hronika-zhizni/

A cholera patient, 1831

In the cholera of 1831 Seraphim treated a sick peasant, whom the chronicle connects with Likhachev, with the icon of the Mother of God called Tenderness, blessed water, and prosphora, and the man was cured.

Status: recorded in Chichagov’s Chronicle.

Source: Prp. Serafim Sarovskii: khronika zhizni (quoting Chichagov’s Letopis and Motovilov), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/prp-serafim-sarovskij-hronika-zhizni/

Nikolai Motovilov under the pine, 9 September 1831

Motovilov had been nearly paralyzed for more than three years when he was brought to Sarov between 5 and 9 September 1831. On the 9th he was set down under a pine by the Sarovka. “But I’m not a doctor, you know,” Seraphim said; he asked whether Motovilov believed, and then said, “Ah, if you believe, then you are already well!” Motovilov recorded that he was healed. Sanidopoulos describes his paralysis of the legs as recurrent.

Status: Motovilov’s own report.

Source: Prp. Serafim Sarovskii: khronika zhizni (quoting Chichagov’s Letopis and Motovilov), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/prp-serafim-sarovskij-hronika-zhizni/; J. Sanidopoulos, “The Authenticity of the Conversation Between St. Seraphim of Sarov and Nicholas Motovilov” (2020), https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2020/01/the-authenticity-of-conversation.html

Light in the snow, November 1831

In Motovilov’s account of their conversation on acquiring the Holy Spirit, Seraphim took him by the shoulders, and Motovilov could not look at him: his face had become brighter than the sun. The light lit the snow for several fathoms around, and Motovilov felt peace, sweetness, joy, warmth in the winter forest, and a fragrance finer than any perfume. The account was published only in 1903, by Sergei Nilus, from Motovilov’s papers.

Status: one witness; the authenticity of the published text is debated.

Source: N. A. Motovilov, Beseda prepodobnogo Serafima s N. A. Motovilovym (publ. 1903), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/beseda-prepodobnogo-serafima-s-n-a-motovilovym/; J. Ladyzhensky’s wrist, 1832

In the spring of 1832 the officer O. V. Ladyzhensky, on his way to China, visited Seraphim with his sisters. Seraphim gripped his sore wrist, and the pain went. He told him three times, “We will not see each other again.” His sister Ekaterina Ladyzhenskaya reported it.

Status: recorded in Elagin’s Life.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/

A serf boy from Vladimir, 1832

Seraphim told a boy from Vladimir that he would enter a monastery and be freed from serfdom. Both came true, and the boy became the monk Vasily.

Status: recorded in Elagin’s Life; fulfilled.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/

Foreknowledge of his death, 1832–1833

Near the end of 1832 Seraphim had a brother blow out a candle and said, “Just so, they will not see me either.” He measured his grave beside the altar of the Dormition cathedral. He told Brother Pavel, “While I live there will be no fire; but when I die, my death will be revealed by a fire,” and on 2 January 1833 it was smoke from his cell that led the brothers to find him dead. He had told Father Theoktist, “So you will serve in Diveyevo”; when Theoktist’s sleigh broke on the road, he stopped at Diveyevo, and the sisters asked him to serve the memorial service.

Status: recorded in the Lives.

Source: N. V. Elagin, Zhitie startsa Serafima (1863), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Nikolaj_Elagin/zhitie-startsa-serafima-sarovskoj-obiteli-ieromonaha-pustynnozhitelja-i-zatvornika/; Sergius (Vasiliev), Skazanie o zhizni i podvigakh … ottsa Serafima (1841), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/skazanie-o-zhizni-i-podvigah-blazhennnoj-pamjati-ottsa-serafima-sarovskoj-pustyni-ieromonaha-i-zatvornika/

Sealed letters for the tsars

The Brookline account says Seraphim left a sealed letter “for the fourth sovereign who will come to Sarov,” which was handed to Nicholas II in 1903; its contents are unknown, and the account says he was visibly shaken. The Russian Wikipedia article says that Seraphim gave Motovilov a sealed packet with the message that Motovilov’s wife would live to see the whole imperial family come to Diveyevo. A saying, “I will glorify the tsar who glorifies me,” is attributed to him without an early witness.

Status: tradition.

Source: J. Sanidopoulos, “Translation of the Holy Relics of Saint Seraphim of Sarov”, https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2013/07/translation-of-holy-relics-of-saint.html; “Serafim Sarovskii,” Russian Wikipedia, https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%BC_%D0%A1%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9; Prp. Serafim Sarovskii: khronika zhizni (quoting Chichagov’s Letopis and Motovilov), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/prp-serafim-sarovskij-hronika-zhizni/

Prophecies about Russia from Motovilov’s papers

Sergei Nilus published, from Motovilov’s manuscripts, prophecies about Russia, the Antichrist, and a “great Diveyevo secret.” The scholar A. Strizhev notes one passage that Motovilov marked as Seraphim’s exact words, foretelling a revolution, the plunder of the Church, and then Russia’s glory. Strizhev holds that the rest is Motovilov’s own opinion and calls the whole extremely doubtful.

Status: contested.

Source: A. Strizhev, “Chego ne izrekal prepodobnyi Serafim” (2004), https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Serafim_Sarovskij/chego-ne-izrekal-prepodobnyj-serafim-k-voprosu-o-psevdotserkovnom-mifotvorchestve/

A return like the Seven Sleepers

A tradition reported in 1991 says that Seraphim foretold that, like the Seven Sleepers, he would return to life at Diveyevo.

Status: tradition; reported in a 1991 magazine article.

Source: “St. Seraphim Begins the Journey Home,” Orthodox America 105 (1991), https://roca.org/oa/volume-xi/issue-105/st-seraphim-begins-the-journey-home/

After Seraphim’s death

These accounts begin on the night he died. Most come from the summer of 1903, when a journal of healings was kept at Sarov during the canonization celebrations, and from the Diveyevo spring.

A light over Glinsk, 2 January 1833

At the Glinsk hermitage, Father Filaret saw a light rising in the sky on the night Seraphim died and said, “That is the soul of Father Seraphim ascending!” The Brookline synaxarion repeats the story.
