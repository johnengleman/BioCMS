A merchant's son from Kursk, a strong man with an axe in his hands, once set the axe down in front of three robbers and let them beat him nearly to death. Saint Seraphim of Sarov wanted only to belong to God, and he looked for Him in the Russian forest: a thousand nights on a granite stone, years of silence, a cell with an oak coffin at the door. But at the bedside of a dying abbot he had promised to watch over a small community of poor women in a nearby village. What could a man who hid even his face give to anyone? And what would God make of his promise?
