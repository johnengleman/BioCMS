# Seraphim of Sarov: notes, focus church-miracles
Researcher: church-miracles agent. Not covered here: own words, witnesses (other agents). Identity is brief.

## 1. Identity
- Born Prokhor Isidorovich Moshnin (also spelled Mashnin), Kursk [S4][S5]. Monastic name Seraphim.
- Birth year CONFLICT: 1754 (current Church position, birth 19 July OS = 30 July NS) vs 1759 (pre-revolution Lives, gravestone said age 73) [S4]. The Church chose 1754 before the 250th anniversary in 2004, from Kursk Ilyinsky church records and the Spiritual Regulation (monk must be 30; 1759 would make him 26 at tonsure 1786) [S4]. Sanidopoulos and the Brookline synaxarion still say 1759 [S1]. Wikipedia EN: "1754 or 1759" [S5].
- Died 2 Jan 1833 OS (14 Jan NS), Sarov, kneeling before the "Tenderness" (Umileniye) icon [S4][S2].
- Tradition: Orthodox only (Anglican Communion also commemorates, 2 Jan [S5]; flagged "failed verification" on Wikipedia). John Paul II called him a saint in "Crossing the Threshold of Hope" [S4][S5].
- Feast days: 2 Jan OS (15 Jan NS), repose [S2][S4]. 19 July OS (1 Aug NS), uncovering of relics and his birthday [S2][S4][S6]. The Synod ordered both days kept [S2]. Task note "2 Jan and 1 Aug" matches OS/NS: 1 Aug is the NS date of 19 July. Check which calendar the entry uses.
- Rank: venerable (prepodobny), wonderworker of Sarov [S4].
- Tier A.

## 3. Timeline (church-miracles scope)
- 1754 (or 1759) 19 July OS: birth in Kursk, merchant family, father Isidor died when he was three [S4]. Contested year, see section 1.
- c.1761 or c.1764: fall from the bell tower of the church being built in Kursk, unhurt. Detail: his mother Agathia took him up the building at about age seven [S4]. LEGEND-status: the tower did not exist in 1761; first appears in Elagin's 1863 Life; the 1841 Life by Hieromonk Sergius says only that he fell "from the height of the building" [S4]. NEW (source dispute).
- c.age 10: serious illness, dream of Mother of God promising healing; the Sign (Znamenie) icon carried past the house in procession; mother carried him out to kiss it; he recovered [S4]. Brookline text says "Kursk icon", age nine [S1]. Icon name conflict.
- 1776 (or 1775): pilgrimage to Kiev Caves; Elder Dositheus blessed him and sent him to Sarov [S4] (see also S1 "Dositheos the Recluse" label, not read).
- 1778: novice under Elder Joseph at Sarov [S4]. Brookline says entered at 19 [S1].
- 1780-1783: swelling illness, three years; healed after vision of Theotokos with Peter and John [S1][S4].
- 19 Mar 1785: ryasophore. 13 Aug 1786 (Synod Act says 18 Aug): tonsured Seraphim. Two months later hierodeacon (Act). 2 Sept 1793: hieromonk [S2][S4]. Date conflict on tonsure: 13 vs 18 Aug.
- 1794: hermit in forest cell about 5 km from Sarov [S4]. Fifteen years [S1][S2].
- Holy Thursday: vision of Christ in the liturgy, "encompassed by the heavenly hosts" [S1].
- 1804 (dated by Life): robbers beat him with the blunt side of an axe, broke his head and chest; Theotokos appears with Peter and John; five months in the monastery; stooped for life; he pleads for the robbers, no punishment [S1][S4]. Words attributed to Theotokos: "This is one of my kind" [S1].
- 1807-1810: pillar-like prayer on a rock, "about 1000 days" [S4]; "a thousand nights on a rock" [S1][S2]. Abbot Isaiah II letter of 1848: he stood immobile 17 hours a day, over three years [S4].
- 8 May 1810: returns to monastery; next day begins reclusion [S4]. 15 years [S2].
- 1815 (or 1823): opens his door; greets all "Radost moya" and "Christ is risen" in every season [S4]. Synod Act adds: "Little father! Little mother! My joy!" [S2].
- 1823: first recorded healing: Mikhail Mantorov, benefactor of Diveyevo [S4].
- 25 Nov 1825: vision ends reclusion (Theotokos with Sts Clement and Peter of Alexandria); tradition: she also gives him the Diveyevo plan, the ditch (kanavka) and the eight sisters by name [S4]. Brookline: she appeared 12 times in his life, last at Annunciation 1831 with twelve virgin saints, John the Baptist and John the Theologian [S1].
- 1828: Serebryakov portrait (only lifetime portrait) [S4].
- Sept 1831: healing of landowner N. A. Motovilov [S4]. The "Conversation" is dated Nov 1831 [S10].
- 2 Jan 1833: death, kneeling before Tenderness icon, head bowed, hands on breast [S2]. Brookline: chanting Paschal hymns; Philaret of Glinsk saw his soul rise in light [S1]. LEGEND-status for the vision (hagiographic).
- 1833: sealed packet given to Motovilov: his wife would live to see the whole Imperial family come to Diveyevo [S4]. Brookline: a letter "for the fourth sovereign who will come to Sarov", given to Nicholas II in 1903, contents unknown, he was "visibly shaken" [S3]. NEW. Prophecy tradition; first printed under USSR in 1992 per S10.
- 1861: chapel over his grave beside the Dormition cathedral [S4].
- 1839-1863: first Lives (Sergius 1837/1841; Joasaph 1849; Elagin 1863) [S4].
- 27 Jan 1883: first document proposing canonization, G. K. Vinogradov to Pobedonostsev; coronation of Alexander III; Pobedonostsev unenthusiastic [S4].
- 3 Feb 1892 to Aug 1894: Church commission on miracles in 29 dioceses; 94 cases investigated, most confirmed by sworn witness statements; report to Synod via Bishop of Tambov in 1895; hundreds of unrecorded letters in Sarov archive; 1897 two more collections [S2]. NEW (details of the process).
- 19 July 1902: Nicholas II expresses wish to finish the process [S2]. Witte: tsar demanded canonization from Pobedonostsev personally, probably at the empress's urging, spring 1902 [S4]. Pobedonostsev blamed Archimandrite Seraphim Chichagov for putting the idea to the tsar; General Kireev recorded Pobedonostsev calling Chichagov "a great schemer and rogue" [S4].
- 11 Jan 1903: examination of the coffin and remains by Metropolitan Vladimir of Moscow, Bishops Dmitri of Tambov and Nazary of Nizhny, Archimandrite Seraphim of Suzdal (Chichagov), Prince Shirinsky-Shikhmatov and four clergy; signed affidavit [S2][S4]. Result in S4: the body had decayed, a skeleton remained; Metropolitan Anthony had to publish a statement that incorruption is not required [S4]. Not stated in S2.
- 26 Jan 1903: tsar writes on the report in his own hand: "I have read [this] with a sense of true joy and profound compunction." [S2]
- 29 Jan 1903: Synod Act glorifies him [S2].
- 25 June to 4 July 1903 and after: journal of healings kept by Hieroschemamonk Simeon; examples in miracles section [S7].
- 3 July 1903: relics moved from grave to church of Sts Sabbatius and Zosimas [S5][S7].
- 17-20 July 1903: "Sarov celebrations"; crowd of 100,000+ (S4), 200,000-300,000 (S3), "about 300,000" (S10). Number conflict.
- 18 July 1903: last memorial service (panikhida) by Metropolitan Anthony Vadkovsky; from then prayers go to him as a saint [S5]. Detail: a woman gave birth at the emperor's feet and he offered to be godfather [S10]. Source is weak; mark popular story.
- 19 July 1903: liturgy, relics placed in the cypress coffin given by the tsar and tsarina and carried in procession; tsar was one of the bearers [S3][S5]. Two churches to him consecrated: one at his forest cell, one on 22 July at Diveyevo [S5].
- Summer 1904: Tsarevich Alexei born, about 12 months after the empress bathed in the spring [S6]. Mosolov and Witte (in S4) tie the heir to the shrine; S10 says Dr Philippe recommended the trip in 1902. Dispute on who prompted her.
- Nov 1920 to 1922: Temnikov district Soviet votes (Nov 1920) to open the shrine; opening 17 Dec 1920 with a report: a wadding figure in brocade and silk; slippers with amethysts; old dark brown bones, a vertebra, a rib and parts of the lower jaw missing; red hair of head and beard tied to the skull with ribbons; lime deposit and mould [S4]. 1922: relics taken to Moscow, Museum of Religious Art, Donskoi Monastery [S4]. The Dormition cathedral at Sarov was blown up in the 1950s [S11].
- 1927: Diveyevo convent closed [S8]. Dormition/Seraphim church at Donskoi: one of the first USSR crematoria built there in 1927 [S4]. Sarov monastery closed 1927 per S11 ("quarter-century" before the 1950s demolition) and 1926 per S3. Date conflict.
- 1946: Sarov removed from maps, nuclear weapons city [S11].
- Autumn 1990: unknown unnumbered remains found in the storage rooms of the Museum of History of Religion (Kazan Cathedral, Leningrad) [S4]. 5 Dec 1990: commission of Bishops Eugene of Tambov and Arseny identify them; main evidence: mittens embroidered "Prepodobne otche Serafime" and "Moli Boga o nas" [S4]. NEW (English pages say only "found").
- 11 Jan 1991: remains handed over; Liturgy by Patriarch Alexy II at Kazan cathedral [S4][S3][S8].
- 6-7 Feb 1991: carried to Moscow by train, procession to the Epiphany Cathedral; thousands; some Diveyevo nuns present [S4][S9].
- 28 July 1991: procession leaves Moscow; 1 Aug 1991: welcomed at Diveyevo [S4]. Mother Margarita lit the candle that Seraphim gave the nuns [S8].
- 1991: Patriarch Alexy II places Seraphim's cell icon of the Theotokos in the Vladimir church of the Patriarch's residence in Chisty Lane [S4].
- 2003 July: 100th anniversary at Sarov; 29 July procession with relics from Diveyevo; 31 July Putin attends; the Dormition cross placed on cathedral site [S4].
- 2006: Synod decides to open Assumption Sarov Hermitage [S4].
- Sept 2007: first prayer service to Seraphim as patron of nuclear scientists [S4]. NEW.
- 15 Sept 2016: Patriarch Kirill sends a relic to Pope Francis; kept beside his bed per S4/S5. 19 Oct 2016: relics aboard Soyuz MS-02 [S5].

## 4. Character (from this focus)
- Joy: "they never saw him sad or despondent"; he tried to give that joy to others [S2].
- Meekness and no acquisitiveness; extreme humility [S2].
- Bent over after the 1804 beating; received people "stooped over" [S2][S4].
- Forgave his attackers and asked that they not be punished [S4].
- Lived on vegetables (potato, beet, onion, goutweed/snyt) in the forest [S4].
- Gave blessed bread (dried crusts, sukhariki), holy water, lamp oil [S2][S4].
- Critics: Pobedonostsev called the initiative improper; Old Believers doubt the glorification [S4][S5]; Wikipedia EN discusses Old Believer sympathies [S5]. Present fairly.
