# Benedict of Nursia: plan

## The driving question

Benedict wanted to be alone with God. He left Rome, his books and his father's house, slipped away even from his old nurse, and hid for three years in a cave that only one monk knew. Gregory's phrase for what he sought is "dwelt with himself". But people kept finding him: a priest at Easter, shepherds, monks who wanted an abbot and then tried to poison him, Roman nobles with their sons, a Goth with a lost billhook, an envious priest, a Gothic king, his own sister. How did the man who fled to a cave become the father of monks in the West, the author of a Rule for living *together*? The answer is in the life: each time people came, he received them, and when a community failed him he left without bitterness and tried again. The Rule is that life written down (Gregory: he could not teach otherwise than he lived). The ending resolves it: the hermit dies standing, held up by the hands of his monks, and his Rule ends with Christ "who will lead us all together to eternal life".

## Why famous

He wrote the Rule that made him, in Paul VI's words, the founder of monastic life in the West, and he is patron of Europe.

## What set him apart

He was a hermit who became a lawgiver of community, and his Rule asks for "nothing harsh, nothing heavy", treats every guest as Christ and the monastery's pots as altar vessels. He met hatred (two poisonings, a slanderous priest) with mildness, and wept for the enemy who died.

## The hook

Scene at the edge: the monks who begged him to be their abbot hand him a poisoned glass of wine; he makes the sign of the cross and it shatters; his mild reply ("Did not I tell you before hand, that our manner of living could never agree together?"); he walks back to the wilderness. Then the paradox: this failed abbot wrote the Rule of the West, whose last chapter asks monks to bear each other's weaknesses with great patience. Answered in "A Little Rule for Beginners" and the death scene.

## Chapters (prose target about 8,500 words)

1. **The Abbot They Tried to Poison** (hook, thesis, question). Quote: the reply to the poisoners. Ends: it began in Rome with a student who drew back his foot.
2. **Learned Ignorance, Unlearned Wisdom.** Nursia, Rome, flight with the nurse, the sieve at Effide. Ends: he slips away even from her.
3. **Bread on a Rope.** Subiaco, Romanus, the bell, the Easter priest (quote), shepherds, the nettles. Ends: monks come to ask him to be abbot.
4. **Dwelling With Himself.** The refusal, the poison (recalled briefly), Gregory's gloss, twelve monasteries, Maurus and Placidus, the water. Ends: a boy falls into the lake.
5. **Running Across the Lake.** The Goth's billhook; Maurus on the water; the contest of humility. Ends: not everyone rejoiced (Florentius).
6. **The Crow and the Loaf.** Florentius's loaf and the crow (quote), the dancers, the departure, the death and the tears, Maurus's penance. Ends: south to Cassino.
7. **Fire on Apollo's Mountain.** The temple, the two oratories (plant: St John's oratory will be his grave), the devil's taunt, the wall and the boy. Ends: the abbot sees what he is not shown.
8. **Nothing Hidden From the Abbot.** The meal at a woman's house, the flagon and the snake, "Sign your heart", the famine meal. Gregory: the spirit of prophecy is not always present. Ends: the fame reaches a king.
9. **A King in Borrowed Shoes.** Totila, Riggo, the prophecy (quote), Rome will decay of itself, the weeping over Cassino's sack. The escape of the monks; they carry his story to Rome; Gregory's four witnesses (quote). Ends: Gregory says the Rule shows his life best.
10. **A Little Rule for Beginners.** Prologue (quote), guests as Christ (quote), cellarer, wine, abbot loved more than feared, ladder of humility, "Ora et labora" note, chapter 72 (quote). Ends: rules have limits; his sister.
11. **The Storm His Sister Asked For.** The dead child ("my weakness cannot bear", quote), then Scholastica (quote), "she did more which loved more", the dove, one grave.
12. **The Whole World in a Ray.** Servandus, the tower window, Germanus (paraphrase only for the world in a ray).
13. **Standing, With Hands Raised.** Foretold death, open grave, communion, held up (quote), the lit road. Buried where Apollo's altar had been.
14. **Cross, Book, and Plough.** The sacks of Cassino, 1944, Pius XII on the crypt, 1964 Pacis nuntius, the medal (medieval), Fleury doubt. End: the Rule calls itself a little rule for beginners; Christ "will lead us all together".
15. A Note on the Sources; How This Life Was Researched; Sources.

## Open loops

1. The question of the hook (hermit to father of community): answered in ch. 10 and 13.
2. The oratory of St John built on Apollo's altar (ch. 7): it becomes his and Scholastica's grave (ch. 11, 13).
3. Benedict weeps over the future sack of his abbey and prays only that the monks live (ch. 9): they live, and their memory becomes the book we read (ch. 9 end, ch. 14).
4. The poisoned cup (ch. 1) and the crow's loaf (ch. 6) return on the medal (ch. 14), as two attacks the medal joins.

## Rules for this saint

Gregory's chapter numbers are the Latin 38-chapter division. Rule cited by chapter (verses only for Prol. 45, 4:21, 72:11). No "Ora et labora" as his. Crow, not raven. Billhook Goth and twelve-shilling debtor kept apart. Wall (ch. 11) and tower vision (ch. 35) kept apart. No death year in the story; the conflict goes in the note. Parents' names, Holy Saturday, Maurus in Gaul only as late tradition. World-in-a-ray only as paraphrase.
