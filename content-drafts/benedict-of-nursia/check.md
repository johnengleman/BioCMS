## Fixes in biography.html

1. Totila scene (A King in Borrowed Shoes). "Three times he was told to rise" and "face down" were not in Gregory. Gregory: Totila "durst not come near, but fell down to the ground"; Benedict, "speaking to him twice or thrice," asked him to rise, then lifted him. Rewritten to match.
2. Totila setup. "In the 540s he was campaigning in the south of Italy" (not in Gregory; Gardner puts the visit probably in 542) became "Italy was at war". "Royal escort" became "a train of courtiers". "Sent him up the mountain" became "sent him to the abbey".
3. Crow scene. "Tame crow" and "put the loaf in front of it" are not in Gregory. Gregory: a crow came daily from the nearby wood; Benedict threw it the loaf, and told it "again and again." The crow hopped and cried as if it would obey and could not. Fixed. A crow that "would not touch" the loaf was an overstatement.
4. "Wept" and "in tears" for Florentius. Gregory says Benedict was "passing sorrowful, and lamented much." Changed to "mourned" and "sorrowing". The same change is made in entry.json (teachings, miracles, share moment).
5. Death vision. "Spread with rich hangings" became "hung with woven cloth" (Gregory: "hung and adorned with tapestry"; "tapestry" itself trips the lint glow list). "One in his cell at the monastery" became "one in his cell".
6. Wall versus tower. Kept apart correctly. Tower scene corrected: Benedict slept at the top and Servandus at the foot (one stair for both), not "the two of them" together. Benedict sent word to Theoprobus, who sent the messenger to Capua.
7. Billhook. "Gregory and Peter both thought of Elisha" fixed: only Peter does. "The Goth was miserable" fixed: he ran in fear to Maurus. The odd "monastery that held a senator's son" was cut. Billhook Goth and twelve-shilling debtor stay separate. "Benedict had no money at all" became "did not have so much money" (Gregory).
8. Poison scene. "The life they had promised / had vowed" is not in Gregory (he says only that regular life was kept). Cut in both places. "Some other Father" corrected to the original "father suitable to your own conditions".
9. Nurse. "Old nurse" and "the only one of his household who followed" are not in Gregory. Rewritten from his "his nurse alone, which did tenderly love him, would not by any means give him over". "Near the church of St. Peter" became "at".
10. Easter. The priest speaks only after prayer and talk; full line restored: "Rise up, brother, and let us dine, because to-day is the feast of Easter."
11. Small invented or stretched details removed: "years later" (Benedict's report of the briers), "very nearly too late" (Placidus), "saw through walls" (Scholastica), "a few monks" with Benedict at the meeting house, "the devil, Gregory says, sat on the stone" (the monks concluded it), "monastery door" (Gregory: cell door), "dressed as a doctor" (Gregory: "like a physician"), "cleric of Gregory's own church" (Gregory: "our sub-deacon"), "priest of Aquinum" (Gregory: clergyman), "Theoprobus, whom he had brought to the faith" (Gregory: converted by his counsel), "Benedict gave as freely as he received" (replaced by Gregory's own account of the wealth given away).
12. Dead child scene. Gregory: the father laid the body at the gate and ran to find Benedict, and met him coming home from the fields. The draft had them meet at the gate. Fixed.
13. Terracina quotation was cut mid-sentence and ended with a new question mark; restored to Gregory's full words.
14. Claims stronger than the evidence. "Nearly all anyone knows of his family" became "all Gregory says". "No one on earth knew" became "no one else knew". "No one knows at what point he wrote the Rule" became "the sources do not say". "One of the most quoted lines" and "one of the most famous sentences" were softened (Gardner says the vision "deeply impressed the mediaeval mind"). "Begged for only one thing" became "begged to have the lives of his monks spared". "Never sounds like" became "does not sound like". "Those spared lives are the reason we know anything" was cut; Valentinian at the Lateran is the one stated link. "Ends by asking" became "near its end" (chapter 72 is not the last chapter). "In the middle stand four Latin words" became "Among them" (4:21).
15. Pacis nuntius. "John XXIII had wished it before him" was wrong. The Latin says Pius XII hoped God would favor those working to unite the nations of Europe, and John XXIII "ex animo concupivit" the same; the patronage was requested by cardinals, bishops, order superiors, and lay leaders. Rewritten.
16. Orthodox line. "Gregory, the monk who raised the dead" could be read as Gregory. The synaxarion says Benedict "raised the dead, foretold the future". Fixed.
17. Ora et labora. Kept as not in the Rule (Kardong, now named as quoted by Subiaco Abbey). Added: Paul VI's letter quotes "ora et labora" (confirmed in the Latin), so a reader who meets it there is not misled.
18. Archaeology line. "Where Apollo's temple and altar had been" went beyond the source (de Vogüé via Wikipedia: traces of the two oratories "with their pre-Christian cellars"). Fixed.
19. Note on the Sources. The Catholic Encyclopedia gives 543 only, not 21 March; Gardner gives 21 March 543 "according to the tradition". Fixed. Added Gardner's 542 for Totila's visit. The Clark sentence lost "forger of the 680s" and "most scholars accept Gregory" (not confirmed in any text read), and "Adalbert de Vogüé" as a defender (unconfirmed). Now: Clark doubted; Meyvaert answered; debate known from abstracts only. Removed "in full" after Meyvaert and de Vogüé in the unread-sources list (neither was read at all).
20. Zalla. Gregory's 1608 text says "Galla"; Gardner's note says "more usually Zalla". Both now shown.
