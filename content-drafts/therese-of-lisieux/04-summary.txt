In the summer of 1897, a dying nun in Normandy heard a sister in the kitchen say that she had never done anything worth speaking about. She was twenty-four. As a child she had flown into furies and been tied into bed at night with cords. At fourteen she broke an order and asked a pope for leave to enter a convent. Less than twenty-eight years after that summer, she was a saint. What did her sisters miss, and how did a hidden life become a book that reached readers far beyond her convent? This is the story of Thérèse of Lisieux.
