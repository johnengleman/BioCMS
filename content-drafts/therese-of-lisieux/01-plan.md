Family names that will appear in the sources:

- Father: Louis-Joseph-Aloys-Stanislas Martin (b. Bordeaux 22 Aug 1823, d. 29 Jul 1894). Watchmaker and jeweller.
- Mother: Marie-Azélie ("Zélie") Guérin (b. 1831, d. Alençon 28 Aug 1877). Alençon lacemaker.
- Sisters who lived: Marie (Sœur Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Carmel of Lisieux); Pauline (Mère Agnès de Jésus, prioress); Léonie (Sœur Françoise-Thérèse, Visitation of Caen); Céline (Sœur Geneviève de la Sainte-Face, Carmel of Lisieux).
- Four children died young: Hélène, Joseph-Louis, Joseph-Jean-Baptiste, Mélanie-Thérèse.
- Uncle and aunt in Lisieux: Isidore Guérin (pharmacist) and Céline Fournet (Mme Guérin). Cousins: Jeanne (Mme La Néele) and Marie Guérin (Sœur Marie de l'Eucharistie, Carmel of Lisieux).

No source disagrees about her identity.

Small date conflicts to settle in stage 2:

- The English Wikipedia summary gave "baptized 2 April 1874". This is wrong. The French Wikipedia and the baptismal record give 4 January 1873. April 1874 is when she came home from Semallé.
- The local *Novissima Verba* file (1952 edition) says Léonie died "in June, 1944". Other sources give 16 June 1941.

## 2. Evidence era and research strategy

Thérèse is **mixed**: she died in 1897, but her cult, the processes (1910–1917), the canonization (1925) and the critical editions came later. For the life itself, the best evidence is 19th-century and French: her autograph manuscripts, her 266 letters, the family letters of Zélie and Louis, and the sworn depositions of her sisters and fellow nuns. Stage 2 should read the pre-1930 printed texts as public domain, and should use the modern critical editions and the Archives du Carmel website for facts only. The Histoire d'une âme of 1898 was edited by Pauline, so every quotation must be checked against the original manuscripts.

## 5. English baseline

Pages read: English Wikipedia ("Thérèse of Lisieux"); Vatican biography for the doctorate (vatican.va, `ns_lit_doc_19101997_stherese_en`); *Divini amoris scientia* (1997); EWTN library (text from Crawley's *Lives of Saints*); Franciscan Media "Saint of the Day". The old *Catholic Encyclopedia* has no article on her (New Advent 14515b is Teresa of Ávila).

Facts and stories that these pages share:

- Born 2 Jan 1873 at Alençon; youngest of nine children, five daughters lived; parents Louis (watchmaker) and Zélie (lacemaker), canonized 2015.
- Mother died when she was four; the family moved to Lisieux.
- Pauline and then Marie entered the Carmel; Thérèse was hurt by Pauline's departure.
- Illness in 1883; cured by the smile of a statue of the Virgin (13 May 1883).
- Christmas 1886 "conversion": she overcame her oversensitivity.
- Prayer for the murderer Pranzini, who kissed the crucifix before his execution.
- Pilgrimage to Rome in Nov 1887; she asked Leo XIII to let her enter at 15.
- Entered the Carmel 9 Apr 1888 at 15; habit 10 Jan 1889; profession 8 Sep 1890.
- Mother Marie de Gonzague as prioress; Pauline elected prioress (Mother Agnès) in 1893; Thérèse helped with the novices.
- Plays about Joan of Arc (1894–1895).
- Act of Oblation to Merciful Love, 9–11 June 1895.
- Spiritual sisterhood with two missionaries, Maurice Bellière and Adolphe Roulland.
- The "Little Way": small acts with great love; the "elevator" image; confidence and self-surrender.
- Three manuscripts (A, B, C) written under obedience; edited by Pauline as *Histoire d'une âme* (1898); a bestseller.
- Tuberculosis from Good Friday 1896; trial of faith; infirmary from July 1897.
- Died 30 Sep 1897, age 24. Last words "My God, I love you!" "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth."
- Beatified 1923, canonized 1925; patron of missions 1927; patron of France 1944; Doctor of the Church 1997 (third woman).
- Patronages: missions, florists, pilots, the sick; the "Little Flower"; the shower of roses.
- Wikipedia adds: wet nurse Rose Taillé; bullying at the abbey school; Céline's photographs; exhumation 1910; Jesuit Almire Pichon; Franciscan Alexis Prou's retreat of 1891.

Not in the baseline (good targets for stage 2):

- Her father's ancestry at Athis-de-l'Orne, and his youth as a would-be monk at the Great St. Bernard.
- The daily life of the Martin home as told in Zélie's own letters (about 200 survive).
- Louis Martin's illness and the Bon-Sauveur asylum in Caen (1889–1892) and how the family and town spoke of it.
- The exact testimony of named witnesses at the processes: sisters, novices, the Benedictine teachers, the doctors (Dr. de Cornière), and the chaplain.
- The texts as she wrote them: the differences between the manuscripts and Pauline's 1898 edition.
- The Carmel's own history (founded from Poitiers 1838; Mother Geneviève) and its internal tensions.
- The Diana Vaughan / Léo Taxil hoax of 1896–1897, which used her photo as Joan of Arc.
- The early spread of the cult: the "Pluie de roses" volumes, the soldiers of 1914–1918, and the pilgrim flood to the grave before 1923.

### 8.1 Miracles

**(a) Approved miracles (official).** The Archives du Carmel says the Congregation of Rites accepted 4 miracles in all: 2 for beatification and 2 for canonization.

| For | Person | Illness and cure | Local source (PDF page) | Official document |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Beatification (1923) | Sœur Louise de Saint-Germain, Daughters of the Cross, Ustaritz (Basses-Pyrénées) | Stomach ulcer with haemorrhages from 1912; rose petals round her bed; cured about 22 Sep 1916 | Laveille p. 425–426 (her own account) | Same decree: AAS 15 (D8) |
| Canonization (1925) | Maria (Marie) Pellemans, Belgian | Tuberculosis; cured through her intercession (at her tomb, 1923, to verify) | Laveille p. 438, 444 | Decree approving miracles, 1925: AAS 17 (D9) |

Also official, for context: the decree on heroic virtues, 14 Aug 1921 (AAS 13, D10). The processes themselves: de Teil and Basevi 1913 (D3); the Summarium of 1919 (not found online).

**(b) Reported favours collected by the Carmel (popular reports, not approved).**

- *Pluie de roses*: 10 volumes, 1907–1926, with more than 3,200 published accounts. The Carmel recorded about 8,000 accounts between 1898 and 1925. Publication stopped in 1914 during the process and started again in 1920. Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/naissance-dune-sainte/les-miracles/pluie-de-roses/ (verified). The volumes are public-domain but no scan was found online. Gallica is the next place to look.
- English selections ("Shower of Roses") are in Taylor 1924 (D6) and the 1926/1930 revised Taylor editions (D7). Laveille cites "Shower of Roses for the year 1912, p. 107" and "extract from vols. i and ii, p. 110" (PDF p. 412–416).
- Antoinette Guise, *Les miracles de Sr Thérèse de l'Enfant-Jésus entre 1898 et 1926* (master's thesis), a PDF linked from https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/naissance-dune-sainte/les-miracles/les-miracles-de-therese/ (page verified; PDF not opened). facts-only.

**(c) Well-attested later accounts (popular reports unless stated).**

- Gallipoli (Apulia, Italy), 1910: she appeared to the Carmelite prioress and left banknotes to pay a debt. It had a canonical inquiry but was not an approved miracle. Laveille p. 413–414; local Taylor p. 186; Archives page above.
- First World War: French soldiers' devotion to her, ex-votos from the trenches, and the fighter pilot Bourjade. Laveille p. 421–424 and p. 432. The Archives site has a page "Thérèse et la Première Guerre mondiale" (URL not yet verified). The *Pluie de roses* volumes of 1920–1926 hold many war accounts.
- Later famous accounts (for example the story of Édith Piaf's childhood eyesight) are popular reports only. Use them only with a named source.
