# Miracles and extraordinary events — life, family and history part (M-LF-)

Scope of this file. It lists the miracles and extraordinary events that my sources report, in the life-and-family story of Thérèse and after her death (as Laveille and the Carmel report them). The other dossier parts hold the process testimony and the official decrees. Each entry is a report ("Céline testified that…"), not a proof, and not a dismissal.

Status words used: **approved for beatification or canonization** (chosen for the papal decrees), **investigated by the Church** (named in a process), **recorded by the shrine or monastery** (printed by the Carmel, in the *Pluie de roses* volumes or by Laveille, no Church finding seen), **popular tradition** (later family or pious tradition). "PD" means public-domain; "facts-only" means my own words.

Ordering: section A holds family events and events in her life (M-LF-001–011, 016, 018–021, 044–045); section B holds events after her death (M-LF-012–015, 017, 022–041); section C holds the cause of Louis and Zélie (M-LF-042–043). Numbers 001, 002, 020 and 021 were fixed in the cards of facts.md before this file was written, and numbers are not in strict order across sections.

## A. Events in the family story and during her life

### M-LF-001 — Recovery of the baby Thérèse in the wet nurse's lap, Alençon, March 1873
- Period: during life (infancy), modern
- What was reported: Zélie wrote that the baby, near death, was fetched a wet nurse (Rose Taillé of Semallé), who reached the house at 10.30 a.m. Zélie prayed on her knees at the feet of St Joseph in her room. When she came down she saw the baby "nursing with all her heart". At about 1 p.m. the baby brought up some mouthfuls and "fell as if dead" on the nurse. Five people stood round; a workwoman wept; no breath could be seen. After a quarter of an hour "ma petite Thérèse ouvre les yeux et se met à sourire. From that moment she was completely cured."
- The person helped: Thérèse Martin, about nine weeks old, with a weak intestinal illness that Zélie feared was the illness of her dead children
- Witnesses and reporter: Zélie (writer, March 1873, to Mme Guérin); "five around her" (the nurse, a workwoman and others, unnamed); the doctor M. Belloc gave the advice
- Status: recorded by the family (a letter). Zélie does not call it a miracle. It is not one of the cases in the Cause.
- Source: CF 89 (raw-notes/letters-text/de-mme-martin-a-mme-guerin-cf-89-mars-1873.txt; https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/de-mme-martin-a-mme-guerin-cf-89-mars-1873/); LAV p.48–49; F-LF-041, F-LF-043; rights: public-domain letter, our English
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: CF 87 (1 Mar 1873) says the child had improved before anyone prayed to St Francis de Sales (M-LF-002). Laveille smooths the letters.
- Medical or natural facts: Dr Belloc examined the child on the Monday evening and prescribed rice-water and lime-water in milk twice a day, and said that only the breast could save her. She was fed by the wet nurse; the baby brought up some mouthfuls after feeding. No other treatment is named.

### M-LF-002 — The aunt's vow to St Francis de Sales, January–February 1873
- Period: during life (infancy), modern
- What was reported: Zélie's sister Sr Marie-Dosithée, at the Visitation of Le Mans, prayed to St Francis de Sales and vowed that if the child lived she would bear his name, Françoise. The Superior told the nuns to write at once. The aunt wrote that, if Zélie refused, the saint was free to take the child back, and she need only "have a coffin made". Zélie refused, saying the child had improved before the prayers.
- The person helped: baby Thérèse
- Witnesses and reporter: Zélie, writing to Isidore on 1 Mar 1873; the aunt's letter is not itself preserved in my sources
- Status: popular tradition / family letter
- Source: CF 87; F-LF-042; rights: public-domain letter, our English
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The child was in fact baptised "Marie-Françoise-Thérèse" (baptism record, LAV p.461); Zélie's refusal concerned the daily name. Zélie thought the letter would make people believe she had lost her head.
- Medical or natural facts: none stated beyond the illness in M-LF-001

### M-LF-003 — The cure of Thérèse by the smile of the statue of Mary, Pentecost, 13 May 1883
- Period: during life, modern
- What was reported: After weeks of illness (trembling, terror, hallucinations, delirium-like states in which she did not know her sisters, falling from the bed), Thérèse, in Marie's room with Marie, Léonie and Céline kneeling at the statue, looked at the statue and saw it "become animated" and the Virgin smile at her; "all my pain vanished; two big tears gushed from my eyes". She then knew Marie without effort. Marie, seeing her face "radiant like one in ecstasy", saw the gaze fixed on the statue for four or five minutes; Thérèse told her she had seen Our Lady. From then "no trace" of illness was seen, and next day she resumed her normal life.
- The person helped: Thérèse Martin, aged 10, ill since March 1883
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie (Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur), Léonie and Céline in the room; Thérèse wrote it in Ms A (27r–31v); Marie deposed at the Process (Summarium 1919 p.416). Louis wrote to Nogrix (1883) that she was "tout à fait guérie".
- Status: investigated by the Church (in the process testimony); not one of the miracles chosen for the decrees
- Source: LAV p.129–132; Carmel page https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/la-vie-de-sainte-therese-de-lisieux/la-maladie-de-therese-a-10-ans/ (date); letter to Nogrix (raw-notes/letters-text/de-louis-martin-a-m-francois-julien-nogrix-1883.txt); F-LF-138 to F-LF-143, F-LF-147; rights: public-domain and unknown
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The nuns' questions afterwards troubled Thérèse (F-LF-143). She never used the word "miracle". Laveille ascribes the illness to the demon; no diagnosis is given; the Carmel has a psychiatrist's page (not read).
- Medical or natural facts: Dr Notta treated her and said "Science is powerless in face of these phenomena; nothing can be done." Symptoms in F-LF-139. No treatment is named.

### M-LF-006 — The Hodierne prophecy

### M-LF-008 — The novice who was "instantaneously and extraordinarily changed"
- Period: during life, modern (1893–1897)
- What was reported: A religious, before her profession, worn out and in interior trial, asked Thérèse for comfort before evening prayer. Thérèse said, "There is the bell for Prayer; I have no time now to console you… God wills you to suffer alone for the moment." In chapel the novice doubted her vocation for the first time, then felt "an instantaneous and extraordinary change" and came out "absolutely transformed". Next day Thérèse said she had felt such pity for her that she had prayed for her at the start of Prayer.
- The person helped: an unnamed novice
- Witnesses and reporter: the novice, quoted in *Conseils et souvenirs* (LAV p.293–294, citing p.291)
- Status: recorded by the monastery
- Source: LAV p.293–294; rights: public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: Name and date not given.
- Medical or natural facts: none

### M-LF-019 — Mother Geneviève and the butter, the apricots, the thaw
- Period: earlier Carmel history (not Thérèse), modern
- What was reported: (1) The cook said the butter was finished; Mother Geneviève said to use what was left. After two months Sister Madeleine said the small piece never grew less; a few days later an alms came and the pot was empty. (2) A hard frost withered the apricot blossom; the foundress prayed and heard "Be not disturbed, there will be apricots"; the tree bore so much fruit that leaves were hardly visible. (3) On a bitter washing day, while she prayed before the tabernacle, a thaw set in "like spring".
- The person helped: the community
- Witnesses and reporter: Sister Madeleine and others, in FND
- Status: recorded by the monastery
- Source: FND p.42–43
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: FND also says hers was "not a life of continual marvels".
- Medical or natural facts: none

### M-LF-013 — Perfumes of lilies and violets, and of roses
- Period: after death, modern
- What was reported: Several nuns noticed a perfume of lilies and violets near Thérèse's body where no flowers grew (Oct 1897). At the exhumation of 6 Sep 1910 the bones "exhaled a sweet perfume" noted by several witnesses, and the earth that touched the coffin kept its fragrance for months. At the translation on 26 Mar 1923 workmen and bystanders noticed "a distinct perfume of roses" when the slabs were loosened. Laveille also says that Thérèse had favoured Sr Louise de Saint-Germain with heavenly perfumes before her cure (M-LF-017).
- The person helped: none
- Witnesses and reporter: nuns; several witnesses; workmen
- Status: recorded by the monastery
- Source: LAV p.399, p.420–421, p.428
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The exhumation of 1910 found only bones; a natural cause is not discussed by Laveille.
- Medical or natural facts: only bones remained after thirteen years (as she had foretold).

### M-LF-015 — Abbé Charles Anne, Lisieux, 1906 (beatification miracle)
- Period: after death, modern
- What was reported: Abbé Anne, a native of Lisieux and seminarian at Bayeux who defended Thérèse in recreation debates, began to fail in June 1904. On 23 Aug 1906 he collapsed after a haemorrhage. Doctors found "large cavities already in the lungs, burning fever… utter aversion to food"; death was expected within days. He made a novena to Thérèse with a relic round his neck. One evening, when the nursing Sister urged him to offer his life, he pressed the relic to his heart and prayed, "Little Thérèse, you are in heaven… I am on earth where there is good to be done. You must cure me." At once he sat upright, with suffocation, pain and fever gone. Doctors called in haste declared him in perfect health. Laveille says, "instead of the lungs which were consumed and destroyed, new lungs had suddenly been formed". He was in 1928 chaplain of the Lisieux hospital, serving eight hundred patients.
- The person helped: Abbé Charles Anne, seminarian
- Witnesses and reporter: the nursing Sister; the doctors; Laveille, 1925
- Status: approved for beatification (one of the two miracles chosen for the decree of 11 Feb 1923, per Laveille)
- Source: LAV p.409–411, p.426
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: Other dossier parts hold the process documents. Laveille's "new lungs" is his own gloss.
- Medical or natural facts: advanced pulmonary consumption, haemorrhage, cavities, fever, no food; no drugs named.

### M-LF-017 — Sr Louise de Saint-Germain, Ustarritz, September 1916 (beatification miracle)
- Period: after death, modern
- What was reported: A Daughter of the Cross at the provincial convent of Ustarritz (Basses-Pyrénées), with a dangerous stomach ulcer and frequent haemorrhage since 1912, twice stopped from teaching in Spain, given the last sacraments in early 1915, then stopped asking for a cure. She wrote that in Sept 1916 she was persuaded to renew her prayer. In the night of 10 Sep Thérèse came and said, "Be generous; you will soon be cured; I promise it." In the morning three sisters sleeping in the infirmary found rose-petals of different colours around her bed. After more days of "real martyrdom" she fell asleep on the evening of 21 Sep after a violent attack and "awoke on the following morning completely cured". She asked to go to Mass, was sent back to bed with a plentiful breakfast, then resumed her duties. Laveille says "formal certificates of renowned doctors" attest it. Thérèse had earlier favoured her with heavenly perfumes.
- The person helped: Sr Louise de Saint-Germain
- Witnesses and reporter: Sr Louise (Journal des Pèlerins, 5–18 Oct 1924); three sisters; doctors (unnamed)
- Status: approved for beatification (the second miracle chosen for the decree of 11 Feb 1923, per Laveille)
- Source: LAV p.425–426
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: Laveille cites a 1924 article for an event before the 1923 decree; the printed process volumes were not seen.
- Medical or natural facts: stomach ulcer with haemorrhages; last sacraments; no treatment named.

### M-LF-022 — Mgr Augouard and a missionary with sleeping sickness, 1910–1912
- Period: after death, modern
- What was reported: Mgr Prosper Augouard, Vicar Apostolic of Ubanghi, wrote from Brazzaville on 10 Nov 1912: for two years he had been confined by painful rheumatism for three months. He read her Life, asked her for his cure or his death, then withdrew the petition in the night: "I ask nothing now for myself, but save Fr … from death." The missionary had sleeping sickness; sent to France, he was examined at the Pasteur Institute and "pronounced entirely free from any microbe", and returned to the Congo healthy. The bishop himself "was cured the day after I had withdrawn my petition". He made a thanksgiving pilgrimage to Lisieux.
- The person helped: Mgr Augouard; an unnamed missionary
- Witnesses and reporter: Mgr Augouard (letter printed in *Pluie de roses* 1912 p.107)
- Status: recorded by the shrine or monastery
- Source: LAV p.411–412
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The missionary's name is withheld in the letter.
- Medical or natural facts: rheumatism; sleeping sickness diagnosed; Pasteur Institute examination.

## C. The cause of Louis and Zélie Martin

### M-LF-042 — Pietro Schilirò, Monza, 2002 (beatification miracle for Louis and Zélie)
- Period: after death (of the parents), modern
- What was reported: Pietro, born 25 May 2002 in Monza with a severe lung malformation and unable to breathe unaided, was the subject of a novena to Louis and Zélie suggested by a Carmelite. His condition turned on 29 Jun 2002. He left the clinic by 27 Jul 2002.
- The person helped: Pietro Schilirò, newborn; parents Valter (Walter) and Adele
- Witnesses and reporter: the parents; the clinic; the diocesan inquiry closed at Milan 10 Jun 2003 (Card. Tettamanzi)
- Status: approved for beatification (miracle approved 3 Jul 2008; beatification 19 Oct 2008)
- Source: https://louisandzeliemartin.org/history-of-the-cause ; https://www.louisandzeliemartin.org/the-beatification-miracle ; https://www.ilgiornale.it/news/tettamanzi-celebra-guarigione-miracolosa-monza.html (via helper); facts-only
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The clinic-discharge date has medium confidence; Fr Sangalli's first name was not verified.
- Medical or natural facts: severe lung malformation; respiratory support (as reported)

### M-LF-043 — Carmen Pérez Pons, Valencia, 2008 (canonization miracle)
- Period: after death (of the parents), modern
- What was reported: Carmen was born on 15 Oct 2008 in Valencia at about six months' gestation, with a grade 4 intraventricular brain haemorrhage plus infection and lung and heart problems. The Carmelites of Serra gave the family prayer cards of Louis and Zélie. Prayer began around 19 Oct 2008. She improved within days, left intensive care on the third day, and went home on 2 Jan 2009.
- The person helped: Carmen Pérez Pons, newborn; parents Carmen and Santos
- Witnesses and reporter: parents; eight doctors testified at the diocesan inquiry (7 Jan – 21 May 2013)
- Status: approved for canonization (miracle approved 18 Mar 2015; canonization 18 Oct 2015)
- Source: https://therecord.com.au/news/international/extraordinary-story-of-carmen-leads-to-canonisation-for-parents-of-st-therese/ ; https://www.louisandzeliemartin.org/canonization-miracle ; https://www.aciprensa.com/noticias/54764/... (via helper); facts-only
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: ACI Prensa wrongly says she was born four days after the beatification (it was four days before). Inquiry close: 21 May vs 27 May 2013.
- Medical or natural facts: grade 4 intraventricular haemorrhage, infection, lung and heart problems (as reported)

## D. Items considered and not listed as miracles

- Zélie's tumour and death: F-LF-090 to F-LF-104 (no cure reported).
- The vision of the veiled man (F-LF-128): reported by Thérèse and her sisters as a vision or foreseeing, not as a healing; it is a card, not a miracle entry.
- The "Snow" (M-LF-005), "Hodierne" (M-LF-006) and similar are listed under "extraordinary events" because the Carmel and Laveille report them in that way.
- The miracle attributed to Mgr de Teil's request in the parlour (LAV p.413 fn.) is a remark, not a miracle.
- The first-communion "notebook", the 1883 novena and Louis's letter to Nogrix: see M-LF-003.
