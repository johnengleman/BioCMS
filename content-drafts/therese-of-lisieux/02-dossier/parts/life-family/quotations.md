# Quotations — life, family and historical context (life-family part)

Two sections. (A) Thérèse's own words about her family and life, as printed in Laveille's English of 1928 (public-domain). Most come from the *Histoire d'une âme* (HA) of 1898, which is the **edited** text (Mère Agnès, Fr Madelaine and others cut and changed it: see F-LF-241). Another part of the dossier compares the manuscripts; a writer who wants her exact words should use that comparison. (B) Words of the family and others about her, with the French original (from the Carmel transcriptions in `raw-notes/letters-text/`, rights "unknown", so our own English), the date, and the CF number where there is one.

Locator "LAV p.N" is the PDF page of Laveille (book page + 20). "F-LF" numbers link to cards in `facts.md`.

## A. Thérèse: her own words (English of Laveille 1928; public-domain)

1. "My earliest memories are of smiles and tender caresses." — HA ch. i p.8, in LAV p.66. Context: how she recalled her Alençon childhood (no separate card; see F-LF-065 and F-LF-076 for Zélie's parallel letters).
2. "No one can imagine how I loved papa and mama… I was very demonstrative." — HA ch. i p.8, LAV p.69. Context: the child's love for her parents.
3. "My happy disposition changed completely… timid and shy and sensitive to excess; a look sufficed to make the tears flow." — HA ch. ii p.22, LAV p.98. Context: after her mother's death. F-LF-107.
4. "See, papa, my name is written in heaven!" — HA ch. ii p.30, LAV p.104. Context: Orion's belt as a "T", on a walk with Louis. F-LF-121.
5. "You know well, Victoire, that this offends the good God." — LAV p.103. Context: to the maid Victoire, who told small lies in fun. F-LF-111.
6. "Have I been good today? Is the good God pleased with me?" — LAV p.105. Context: in bed, if not, she cried. F-LF-116.
7. "I want to see what a blessed rosary is like." — LAV p.110. Context: under a street lamp after her first confession. F-LF-122.
8. "I am so small that no one would notice me." — LAV p.110. Context: to Marie on Christmas Eve, wishing to join the older children. F-LF-123.
9. "Papa! Papa!" — LAV p.116. Context: the veiled man in the garden. F-LF-128.
10. "Lead me; I am going to close my eyes." — LAV p.126. Context: to cousin Marie Guérin on the way home from school. F-LF-136.
11. "There was one pupil in particular, aged fourteen… I knew not how to defend myself, and simply wept in silence." — HA ch. iii p.37, LAV p.123. Context: the bully at the Abbey. F-LF-133.
12. "Oh, the big black beast!" — LAV p.128. Context: at the sight of Louis's hat in 1883. F-LF-139.
13. "All at once the statue became animated… Then all my pain vanished; two big tears gushed from my eyes and fell silently." — HA ch. iii p.48, LAV p.130–131. Context: the cure, 13 May 1883. F-LF-141.
14. "Ah, how sweet it was, this first kiss of Jesus to my soul!… Thérèse had disappeared like the drop of water which is lost in the ocean's depths; Jesus alone remained." — HA ch. iv p.58, LAV p.136. Context: First Communion, 8 May 1884. F-LF-148.
15. "I was the ass who, on seeing the lap-dog petted, placed his unwieldy hoof on the table to be kissed in turn." — HA ch. iv p.67, LAV p.142. Context: the headache scene at Trouville. F-LF-153.
16. "For a big girl like Thérèse, this is too childish; I hope this will be the last time." — LAV p.147 (Louis's words, as she heard them). Followed by: "Driving back my tears, I went down quickly to the dining-room… looking as happy as a queen." — HA ch. v p.75. F-LF-159.
17. "In an instant Jesus, pleased with my good will, accomplished the work that I had failed to do in many years." — HA ch. v p.75, LAV p.148. Context: Christmas 1886.
18. "My God, I feel assured that You will pardon the unfortunate Pranzini… But this is my first sinner; for that reason I ask but a sign of repentance for my own consolation." — HA ch. v p.76, LAV p.154–155. F-LF-164.
19. "Ah, I see diamonds" (Révérony's words) and "I did more than show my diamonds to Monseigneur, I gave them—shedding tears." — HA ch. v pp.89–91, LAV p.166–167. F-LF-171.
20. "Most Holy Father, in honour of your Jubilee, permit me to enter Carmel at fifteen." — LAV p.184 (audience of 20 Nov 1887). "O Holy Father, if you only said yes everyone else would agree." F-LF-179.
21. "Come, follow me… we shall be able to get down." — LAV p.179. Context: the Coliseum barrier. F-LF-176.
22. "I put my little finger through an opening in the reliquary, and was thus able to touch one of the precious nails." — HA ch. vi p.110, LAV p.181. F-LF-178.
23. "I acted towards Him… like a child who believes that everything is allowed her, and who regards as her own the treasures of her Father." — HA ch. vi p.110, LAV p.181.
24. "I heard nothing but the sound of weeping. As for me, I did not shed tears… my heart beat so violently that I asked myself whether I was not about to die." — HA ch. vii p.116, LAV p.195. Context: 9 Apr 1888. F-LF-187.
25. "From the first, my way was strewn with more thorns than roses." — HA ch. vii p.117, LAV p.200. F-LF-192.
26. "Papa was waiting for me at the enclosure door… 'Ah, here is my little Queen'… This day was his triumph, his last feast on earth." — HA ch. vii p.124, LAV p.214. F-LF-270.
27. "The moment I entered the cloister… I saw it all covered with snow. What tenderness on the part of Jesus!" — HA ch. vii p.124, LAV p.215. M-LF-005.
28. "The three years of my father's martyrdom seem to me the dearest and most fruitful of our life; I would not exchange them for the most sublime ecstasies." — HA ch. vii p.126, LAV p.217. F-LF-202.
29. "Jesus wants me to be an orphan… Tomorrow's feast will be for us a feast of tears." — letter to Céline, 23 Sep 1890, LAV p.228. F-LF-204.
30. "It shall not be said that a woman in the world will do more for her husband, an ordinary mortal, than I for my well-beloved Jesus." — LAV p.233. F-LF-205.
31. "I have come to Carmel to save souls, and more especially to pray for priests." — LAV p.229. Context: canonical examination before profession. F-LF-203.
32. "I am strong as iron; but God can break iron as easily as potter's clay." — letter to Céline, 1894, LAV p.251. F-LF-217.
33. "Our dear father is making us feel his presence… he has not yet been a month in heaven, and already… all your projects succeed." — letter of 19 Aug 1894, LAV p.251–252. F-LF-215.
34. "Suppose everyone were to do the same…" — LAV p.286; and "Our kindness should never degenerate into weakness." — LAV p.286–287 (on training novices). F-LF-212.
35. "At times I long to hear some other thing than praise… a wholesome little salad shall be served to me, highly spiced and with plenty of vinegar." — HA ch. x p.190, LAV p.288–289. Context: the novices' frankness.
36. "I am like a lark except when the fever attacks me; happily, it only comes at night, when larks sleep with heads hidden beneath their wings." — letter to M. and Mme Guérin, 16 Jul 1897, LAV p.459. F-LF-234.
37. "It is not to remain in the golden ciborium that Jesus descends each day from heaven, but to find another heaven, the heaven of our soul." — HA ch. v p.80, LAV p.267–268.
38. "The opinion of creatures! Ah, happily the good God has always given me the grace of being absolutely indifferent to it." — LAV p.373. Context: overheard talk of what the Prioress would say after her death. F-LF-230.
39. "Do you see that little glass?… One would believe that it contained a delicious liqueur; in reality, I take nothing more bitter. Well, that is an image of my life." — LAV p.373 (HA ch. xii p.235). F-LF-230.
40. "Do you see that dark place down there beside the chestnut trees?… As regards both soul and body, I am in a place like that. What darkness! Yet I am in peace there." — 28 Aug 1897, LAV p.391 (Summarium 1919 p.925). F-LF-236.
41. "To hear on my bed of death that I am not a good religious, what joy!" — LAV p.344. F-LF-230.
42. "I will spend my heaven in doing good upon earth." — 17 Jul 1897, to Mère Agnès, LAV p.382–383 (Summarium 1919 p.893 §2585). F-LF-233.
43. "No… I will come down." (to sisters who said she would look down from heaven; 25 Jul 1897) — LAV p.381.
44. "Oh, no… you will see; there will be as it were a shower of roses." — 7 Jun 1897, to Marie, LAV p.378. F-LF-232.
45. "Oh, my Mother, present me to the Blessed Virgin without delay. Prepare me to die well." — 30 Sep 1897, LAV p.397. F-LF-237.
46. "Oh, I would not wish to suffer less." — LAV p.398, followed by "Oh… I LOVE Him! My God, I… LOVE… THEE!" F-LF-237.

## B. The family and others on Thérèse, on the family and on the times (French original; our English)

### B1. Zélie (letters; CF numbers)

1. CF 85 (16 Jan 1873, to her sister-in-law, Mme Guérin): « … comme ma petite dernière; tous me disent qu'elle est belle, elle sourit déjà… elle m'a fait un sourire délicieux. » — "…like my last little one; everyone tells me she is beautiful, she already smiles… she gave me a delightful smile." Context: the baby's first smile, at two weeks. F-LF-039.
2. CF 86 (17 Jan 1873, to Isidore): « Faudra‑t‑il encore perdre celle‑là ? » — "Will I have to lose this one too?" F-LF-041.
3. CF 87 (1 Mar 1873, to Isidore): « Si quelqu'un voyait cette lettre, il croirait que j'ai perdu la tête ! » — "If anyone saw this letter, they would think I had lost my head!" Context: the aunt's vow and the coffin. F-LF-042.
4. CF 89 (March 1873, to Mme Guérin): « Enfin, un quart d'heure se passe, ma petite Thérèse ouvre les yeux et se met à sourire. À partir de ce moment, elle fut complètement guérie. » — "At last a quarter of an hour goes by, my little Thérèse opens her eyes and begins to smile. From that moment she was completely cured." M-LF-001.
5. CF 106 (20 Jul 1873): « C'est un gros bébé; elle est brunie par le soleil; sa nourrice la brouette dans les champs, montée sur des faix d'herbe; elle ne crie presque jamais. » — "She is a big baby; she is tanned by the sun; her nurse wheels her in a barrow across the fields, sitting on bundles of grass; she hardly ever cries." F-LF-044.
6. CF 112 (30 Nov 1873): « Des femmes de la campagne, habillées comme sa nourrice, voilà le monde qu'il lui faut ! … elle a peur des gens habillés à la mode ! » — "Country women, dressed like her nurse, that is the company she needs! … she is afraid of people dressed in fashion!" F-LF-049.
7. CF 117 (1 Jun 1874): « Je n'ai jamais eu d'enfant si forte, excepté la première; elle paraît très intelligente; je suis bien heureuse de l'avoir, je crois que ce sera la dernière. » — "I never had such a strong child, except the first; she seems very intelligent; I am very happy to have her; I think she will be the last." F-LF-052.
8. CF 118 (24 Jun 1874): « … je continue ma dentelle jusqu'à dix heures le soir et me lève à cinq heures… Enfin, plus j'ai de mal et mieux je me porte ! » — "…I go on with my lace until ten in the evening and get up at five… Anyway, the more trouble I have, the better I am!" F-LF-053.
9. CF 147 (5 Dec 1875, to Pauline): « Le bébé est un lutin sans pareil… Je suis obligée de corriger ce pauvre bébé, qui se met dans des furies épouvantables; quand les choses ne vont pas à son idée, elle se roule par terre comme une désespérée croyant que tout est perdu, il y a des moments où c'est plus fort qu'elle, elle en est suffoquée. C'est une enfant bien nerveuse… » — "The baby is an imp without equal… I have to correct this poor baby, who flies into dreadful rages; when things do not go her way she rolls on the floor like someone in despair who thinks all is lost; there are times when it is stronger than she is, and she is choked by it. She is a very nervous child…" F-LF-063. (Laveille omits this.)
10. CF 147 (same letter): « Oh ! que je voudrais bien que tu mourrais, ma pauvre petite Mère ! … C'est pourtant pour que tu ailles au Ciel » — "Oh, how I wish you would die, my poor little Mother! … It is so that you go to Heaven." F-LF-062.
11. CF 157 (26 Mar 1876, to Pauline): « Thérèse est un petit lutin qui est la joie de toute la famille, elle est extrêmement intelligente. » — "Thérèse is a little imp who is the joy of the whole family; she is extremely intelligent." And « Papa, je suis "toquée" » — "Papa, I'm bumped." F-LF-070.
12. CF 158 (7 May 1876, to Mme Guérin): « Je crois que je l'aime plus que toutes les autres, c'est sans doute parce qu'elle est la plus jeune. » — "I think I love her more than all the others; no doubt it is because she is the youngest." F-LF-065.
13. CF 159 (14 May 1876, to Pauline): « … d'une intelligence supérieure à Céline, mais bien moins douce et surtout d'un entêtement presque invincible; quand elle dit « non », rien ne peut la faire céder, on la mettrait une journée dans la cave qu'elle y coucherait plutôt que de dire « oui ». » — "…more intelligent than Céline but much less gentle, and above all with an almost invincible stubbornness; when she says 'no', nothing can make her give way; you could put her in the cellar for a day and she would sleep there rather than say 'yes'." F-LF-064. (Laveille p.80 omits this.)
14. CF 164 (16 Jul 1876, to Pauline): « La pauvre petite avait peur du photographe. Elle qui sourit toujours, faisait la « lippe » comme lorsque les larmes sont près de venir; il a fallu la rassurer. » — "The poor little one was afraid of the photographer. She who always smiles pouted, as when tears are near; she had to be reassured." F-LF-074.
15. CF 169 (22 Oct 1876, to Pauline): « Faut pas nous mettre dans le toupet que Papa nous emmènera tous les jours. » — "Don't put it in our heads that Papa will take us out every day." F-LF-075.
16. CF 170 (29 Oct 1876): « … je m'envolerais avec toi qui serais au Ciel, comment que le bon Dieu ferait pour me prendre ?… Tu me tiendrais bien fort dans tes bras. » — "…I would fly up with you who would be in Heaven; how would the good God take me?… You would hold me tight in your arms." F-LF-076.
17. CF 172 (3 Nov 1876): « … elle est fine comme l'ombre… très vive, mais son cœur est sensible. » — "…she is fine as a shadow… very lively, but her heart is tender." And « on est comme les deux petites poules, on ne peut pas se séparer ! » — "We are like the two little hens; we cannot be parted!" F-LF-077.
18. CF 177 (17 Dec 1876, to Mme Guérin): the doctor: « Savez‑vous que ce que vous avez là est d'une nature très grave ? C'est une tumeur fibreuse. » — "Do you know that what you have there is very serious? It is a fibrous tumour." F-LF-091.
19. CF 195 (22 Mar 1877, to Pauline): « … elle ne mentirait pas pour tout l'or du monde, elle a de l'esprit comme je n'en ai jamais vu à aucune de vous. » — "…she would not lie for all the gold in the world; she has wit such as I never saw in any of you." F-LF-086 (quoted by Thérèse, Ms A 11r).
20. CF 205 (7 Jun 1877): Zélie: « … tout tourne à la gloire de Dieu, mais qu'il ne pense pas absolument qu'à lui; il ferait bien un miracle pour moi, quand même personne au monde ne le saurait. » (CF 205, 7 Jun 1877). — "He does not think only of himself; he could well work a miracle for me even if nobody in the world knew of it." F-LF-096, M-LF-044.

### B2. Louis (letters)

21. To Nogrix, 1883: « Thérèse, ma petite Reine, c'est ainsi que je l'appelle, car c'est un beau brin de fille, je te l'assure, – est tout à fait guérie ; les nombreuses prières ont emporté enfin le Ciel d'assaut et Dieu, si bon, a bien voulu capituler. » — "Thérèse, my little Queen — that is what I call her, for she is a fine girl, I assure you — is completely cured; the many prayers have taken Heaven by storm at last and God, so good, has been willing to yield." F-LF-147.
22. To Marie, Rome, 27 Sep 1885: « Dis à ma « Perle fine » que je suis trop heureux, et que « je tends le dos », car cela ne peut pas durer. » — "Tell my 'Fine Pearl' that I am too happy, and that 'I offer my back', for this cannot last." F-LF-155.
23. To Marie, 11 Sep 1885: « … ma « Reine de France et de Navarre » » — "my 'Queen of France and Navarre'". F-LF-155.
24. To Marie, Rome, 27 Sep 1885: « Mais quelle tristesse que le Saint-Père (Léon XIII) soit ainsi en captivité ; cela fait tache… » — "What sadness that the Holy Father (Leo XIII) is thus a captive; it leaves a stain…" F-LF-259.
25. To Marie, Milan, 6 Oct 1885: « Tout ce que je vois est splendide, mais c'est toujours une beauté terrestre et notre cœur n'est rassasié de rien, tant qu'il ne voit pas la beauté infinie qui est Dieu. » — "Everything I see is splendid, but it is always an earthly beauty, and our heart is satisfied with nothing until it sees the infinite beauty which is God." F-LF-155.
26. To the Nogrix family, 10 Apr 1888: « Thérèse, ma petite reine, est entrée hier au Carmel !… Dieu seul peut exiger un tel sacrifice… Ne me plaignez pas, car mon cœur surabonde de joie. » — "Thérèse, my little queen, entered Carmel yesterday!… Only God can ask such a sacrifice… Do not pity me, for my heart overflows with joy." F-LF-196.
27. Louis's words to Thérèse (as she reports them), 10 Jan 1889: « Ah, here is my little Queen » (LAV p.214, English; HA ch. vii p.124). F-LF-270.
28. Louis, Christmas 1886 (as she heard it): "For a big girl like Thérèse, this is too childish." F-LF-159 (English of Laveille; the French is in the Manuscript).

### B3. Céline, Mme Guérin, Isidore and others

29. Céline to her aunt Pauline Romet, 18 Feb 1889: « … dans la ville on l'appelait : « le saint Patriarche ». Et le voilà parti !… » — "…in the town they called him 'the holy Patriarch'. And now he is gone!…" F-LF-198.
30. Céline, same letter: « Il est d'une bonté incroyable; avec son revolver il était bien loin de vouloir nous faire du mal, il voulait au contraire nous défendre, dans son imagination il voyait des choses épouvantables, des carnages, des batailles; il entendait le canon et le tambour, je le détrompais en vain… » — "He is incredibly kind; with his revolver he was far from wanting to harm us; he wanted on the contrary to defend us; in his imagination he saw terrible things, carnage, battles; he heard the cannon and the drum; I tried in vain to undeceive him…" F-LF-198.
31. Céline, same letter: « … peut-être aurait-on dû attendre et essayer, avant d'agir, tous les moyens de le lui enlever, car il était si bon, si doux, il nous embrassait avec tant de tendresse ! » — "…perhaps we should have waited and tried every means to take it from him before acting, for he was so good, so gentle, he kissed us with such tenderness!" F-LF-198.
32. Céline, same letter: « … je ne désire qu'une chose c'est d'être comme dame en chambre dans quelque communauté pour être auprès de lui et accourir au moindre signe, je ne puis plus vivre ici. Lisieux me semble désert. » — "…I desire only one thing, to be a lodger in some community to be near him and run at the slightest sign; I can no longer live here. Lisieux seems deserted to me." F-LF-200.
33. Mme Guérin to Jeanne La Néele, 10 May 1892: « … ses pauvres jambes ne peuvent plus le porter. Il fallait le porter dans la voiture. Il a pleuré tout le temps et paraît si heureux d'être au milieu de ses enfants. » — "…his poor legs can no longer carry him. He had to be carried into the carriage. He wept the whole time and seems so happy to be in the midst of his children." F-LF-210.
34. Céline to Mère Agnès, Marie and Thérèse, 29 Jul 1894: « Papa est au Ciel ! .. J'ai reçu son dernier soupir, je lui ai fermé les yeux… Il s'est éteint tout doucement à 8h1/4. » — "Papa is in Heaven! I received his last breath, I closed his eyes… He went out very gently at 8.15." And « … j'ai vu au firmament comme un globe lumineux… » — "I saw in the sky something like a luminous globe…" F-LF-215; M-LF-011.
35. Delatroëtte at the enclosure door, 9 Apr 1888 (English of Laveille, p.196; Mère Agnès, Apostolic Process p.162): "Well, my Reverend Mothers, you can now chant a Te Deum…" F-LF-188.
36. Delatroëtte, autumn 1887 (LAV p.164): "Again this young girl… Let her remain with her father until she has reached her majority." F-LF-170.
37. Isidore Guérin, June 1887 (LAV p.162–163): "This would be… a unique case in the whole of France… a miracle would be necessary." Then: "A miracle is no longer necessary… You are a little privileged flower that the Saviour wills to cull for Himself." F-LF-169.
38. Bishop Hugonin, 31 Oct 1887 (LAV p.167): "All is not lost, little one… You shall certainly receive my reply in Italy." F-LF-171.
39. Leo XIII, 20 Nov 1887 (LAV p.184): "Well, my child, you shall enter if it be God's will." F-LF-179.
40. Brother Siméon, Nov 1887 (LAV p.185–186): "That never happens in Italy." F-LF-180.
41. Marie de Gonzague to Thérèse, postulant (LAV p.200): "It can be easily seen that our cloisters have been swept by a child of fifteen. Too bad!" F-LF-192. And (LAV p.200): "After all, this child does absolutely nothing." And (LAV p.317): "This child is continually complaining."
42. Marie de Gonzague, shortly before her death (LAV p.277): "I have my little Thérèse to intercede for me. I am sure that I shall owe my salvation to her." F-LF-243.
43. Mère Geneviève to Thérèse (LAV p.237): "Serve God in peace and joy; remember, my child, that our God is the God of Peace." F-LF-207.
44. Fr Pichon to Thérèse, 1888 (HA ch. vii p.119): "In the presence of God… I declare that you have never committed a single mortal sin." F-LF-195.
45. Isidore Guérin to Thérèse, 25 Jul 1897 (English of Laveille p.384–385): "You were your good mother's precious pearl, last in coming, your venerated father's 'little queen'… It is said that the swan, though silent during its whole life, breaks into a sublime song as death approaches. Your letter… is, without doubt, your last song for us." F-LF-234.
46. Marie at the Process (Summarium 1919 p.416, LAV p.132): "Her countenance became radiant like one in ecstasy." F-LF-142.
47. Céline on Thérèse's early years (Apostolic Process p.126, LAV p.118): "a period of darkness… a veil thrown over those qualities"; "she cried for having cried". F-LF-126.
48. Léonie on Thérèse at home (LAV p.151): "in truth the joy of the family". F-LF-163.
49. Sr Joséphine de la Croix, the Guérin servant (LAV p.121–122): "When the little one found herself alone with me… she became affectionate and confiding." F-LF-131.
50. A Carmel sister (LAV p.344): "she does nothing remarkable; one cannot even say that she is exactly a good religious." F-LF-230. (About Thérèse in the infirmary.)
51. Maurice de Waleffe in *Le Journal* (LAV p.419–420): "Her short and uneventful life was that of a lily which opens at morn and silently fades at eve… Here we have the real miracle." F-LF-246. (English of Laveille; the French of the article was not seen.)
52. Pius XI, 11 Feb 1923 (LAV p.427): "a miracle of virtues and a prodigy of miracles." F-LF-248. (English of Laveille.)
