# Scenes — life, family and historical context (life-family part)

Twenty-eight scenes that my sources support in enough detail to tell as small stories. Each line gives the card numbers (F-LF in facts.md, M-LF in miracles.md), then one sentence on why the scene matters. Where a scene rests on one witness, the card says so.

1. **The dawn walk to Semallé, March 1873** (F-LF-041, F-LF-043, F-LF-044, M-LF-001). Zélie walks alone to fetch a wet nurse, meets two men on a lonely road and does not care, and a baby "fallen as if dead" opens her eyes and smiles after a quarter of an hour: it shows the mother's fear and how near the fifth Martin daughter came to dying.
2. **The aunt's coffin letter, February 1873** (F-LF-042, M-LF-002). A Visitation nun writes that the saint may take the child back and Zélie need only "have a coffin made"; Zélie refuses to change the name: it shows Zélie's temper and her doubt of pious pressure.
3. **The child in the wheelbarrow, and afraid of fashionable ladies** (F-LF-044, F-LF-047, F-LF-048, F-LF-049). At Rose Taillé's farm the baby rides on bundles of grass and screams at a fashionably dressed visitor: it gives her first year in the country, away from the lace-maker's house.
4. **Rolling on the floor, and the cellar** (F-LF-063, F-LF-064; CF 147, CF 159). Zélie describes rages "stronger than she is" and a stubbornness that would sleep in the cellar rather than say yes; Laveille left these out, and they show the child the family knew.
5. **Louis's fishing lines in the attic** (F-LF-091, F-LF-092; CF 177). The day the doctor says "a fibrous tumour", Louis puts away his fishing lines in the attic and stays away from the club: it shows the household in tears before Zélie's death.
6. **Lourdes and the return "as gay as if I had been cured"** (F-LF-096, F-LF-097, M-LF-044). Zélie in the icy pool, Léonie sleepwalking on the train, five francs left; Louis waiting an hour on the platform: it shows a mother's hope and her refusal to be crushed.
7. **The last kiss and "Pauline will be my mama"** (F-LF-103 to F-LF-106). After Zélie's death the four-year-old chooses her second mother: it explains her attachment to Pauline and the wound of 1882.
8. **The sea at Trouville, August 1878** (F-LF-124). The first sight of the sea, the "golden path" of the sun, the rock beside Pauline: it gives a memory from which she drew images later.
9. **The veiled man in the garden** (F-LF-128, F-LF-129). A little girl sees a bent figure with a veil walk toward the fir trees, and her sisters search behind the trees: it is the family's own "sign" of Louis's illness, told fourteen years later.
10. **The illness of 1883 and the smile of the statue** (F-LF-138 to F-LF-143, M-LF-003). The "big black beast" that was her father's hat, the fall between bed and wall, three sisters kneeling and the cure on Pentecost Sunday, 13 May 1883: it is the central childhood event, told by Thérèse, by Marie and by Louis.
11. **First Communion, 8 May 1884** (F-LF-146 to F-LF-149). The notebook of 818 sacrifices, the first mistress with her small lantern and the tears "because she had not her mother near": it shows the intensity of a ten-year-old and Pauline's profession on the same day.
12. **Christmas 1886: the shoes on the hearth** (F-LF-159). She overhears her father, Céline says "wait", and she goes down and laughs: the "conversion" scene that ended her tears, seen with Louis and Céline in the room.
13. **Pentecost 1887: the white flower** (F-LF-168, F-LF-169). She tells her father at the back door of the garden; he gives her a small white flower; three days later her uncle says "a miracle would be necessary" and then says it is no longer needed: it shows the opposition of the family and its end.
14. **The armchair at Bayeux, 31 October 1887** (F-LF-171). A fourteen-year-old with her hair put up sits "buried in a monumental seat" and gives the Bishop her "diamonds" (tears): it is the humorous and touching scene of her first meeting with the Church's authority.
15. **Over the barrier at the Coliseum, and the nail at Santa Croce** (F-LF-176, F-LF-178). She climbs down for the martyrs' dust while her father calls, and puts her little finger through a reliquary: it shows her boldness and her father's pride.
16. **The audience with Leo XIII, 20 November 1887** (F-LF-179, F-LF-180, F-LF-182). Révérony forbids speaking, Céline says "Speak!", Thérèse kneels, the guards lift her, Louis meets her in tears; on the way home Révérony offers his carriage at Assisi: it is the best known scene of her youth, with one eyewitness (Laveille) in the same hall.
17. **Entry and the "Te Deum" speech, 9 April 1888** (F-LF-186 to F-LF-189). Louis kneels for her blessing; the superior tells the nuns to sing a Te Deum and warns them that the responsibility is theirs: it shows how she came in.
18. **The flight to Le Havre, 23–27 June 1888** (F-LF-197). Louis disappears, asks for money from a post office, and is found with his beard shaved off; a fire burns next door: it is the first public sign of the family's ordeal.
19. **Clothing in Alençon lace and snow, 10 January 1889** (F-LF-270, M-LF-005). She wears white velvet and her mother's lace; "Ah, here is my little Queen"; snow in the cloister: it is Louis's "last feast" before the asylum.
20. **12 February 1889: the revolver and the Bon-Sauveur** (F-LF-198, F-LF-199, F-LF-200). Céline's letter to her aunt of six days later, with the cannon and the drum, "the holy Patriarch" and her doubt about the committal; then the signing of the interdiction: the darkest scene of the family.
21. **The veiling without her father, 24 September 1890** (F-LF-204). Torrents of tears and "Jesus wants me to be an orphan": it shows the price of the ordeal in Carmel.
22. **The night of the influenza, winter 1891–92** (F-LF-209). The sacristan of nineteen lays out a dead sister by candlelight and crowns her with roses; the superior weeps and changes his mind: it shows her strength and the end of the opposition.
23. **10 May 1892: Louis carried into the carriage** (F-LF-210). Louis, who "wept the whole time", is brought to the Guérin house and two days later carried to the Carmel, where he says "In heaven": it is the return of a broken father.
24. **Louis's death at La Musse, 29 July 1894** (F-LF-215, M-LF-011). Céline closes his eyes at 8.15 and writes of a luminous globe: it ends the family's five years.
25. **The Taxil hoax: a photograph of Joan of Arc** (F-LF-220, F-LF-225, F-LF-226). The Carmel play with devils behind a screen; the photo sent to "Diana Vaughan"; the press conference of 19 April 1897 showing it: it shows the small convent touched by the world's anticlerical plot.
26. **The first blood, night of 2–3 April 1896** (F-LF-222, F-LF-223). A burning stream rises in the night; she is glad; she says nothing until the morning: it starts the last eighteen months.
27. **Death, 30 September 1897** (F-LF-233, F-LF-236, F-LF-237). The Office of St Michael, the arms in a cross, Céline cooling her lips with ice, "Oh, I would not wish to suffer less", the last words and the gaze above the statue: it is the scene the Carmel later told most often.
28. **The funeral of 4 October 1897 and the translation of 26 March 1923** (F-LF-239, F-LF-249). Few mourners and a wooden cross, then fifty thousand pilgrims, a carriage in white and silver, and a perfume of roses: it sets the two ends of her story side by side.
