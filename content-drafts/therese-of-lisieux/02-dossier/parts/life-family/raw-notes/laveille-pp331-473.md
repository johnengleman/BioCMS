## Ch. XII (end): "The Little Way of Spiritual Childhood" (pp. 331-338)
p331 [Laveille comment] Little Way = imitate child's warm affection for parents; to "win Jesus by caresses" (Thérèse phrase); lose no little sacrifice; smile always. Cites St Augustine: to him that loves nothing costs. Footnote: G. Martin, La "petite voie" d'enfance spirituelle, p. 53.
p332 "Divine lift" image: Thérèse recalls infancy, as a little child trying to climb unaided the stairs to her mother's room; mother came at her call, took her in her arms, carried her up. Mother's arms = her lift to first floor; arms of Jesus = lift to pure love.
p332 Quote Père G. Martin: "Herein, we believe ... lies the chief originality of the 'little way' of childhood, making it truly a 'new way, very short and straight' to arrive at perfection."
p333 Père Martin warns against "indolent quietism"; cites Cant. v 2 "I sleep, but my heart watcheth". Footnote 2: G. Martin, op. cit., cf. p. 69; Laveille calls it "the best undoubtedly that has been written up to the present on the ascetical doctrine" of Thérèse; his theory of the little way taken in part from it.
p334 Virtues of the little way for all Christians: humility, spirit of poverty, confidence, filial love. [Laveille comment] she presented holiness "within the reach of every soul of goodwill, even to the lowliest and poorest."
p334 Footnote: an unnamed "learned theologian": "St Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus has cleared the way to heaven." An unnamed "eminent prince of the Church": "What I admire in this little saint is her charming simplicity. She has excluded mathematics from our relations with God."
p334-338 Long quote: Discourse of Pope Benedict XV at promulgation of Decree on heroicity of her virtues, August 14, 1921 (footnote p338). Key lines: life "wholly characterized by the merits of Spiritual Childhood"; "the 'secret of holiness,' not only for the French, but for the faithful throughout the entire world"; calls her "the new French heroine"; image of a small child running to mother's arms when frightened; spiritual childhood excludes pride, presumption, self-sufficiency; supposes lively faith, confident recourse to God; cites Matt. xviii 3 and Mark x 15; says it is obligatory "even for those who have lost their first innocence" (Nisi conversi fueritis...).
p338 Footnote: Pius XI homily at canonization, May 17, 1925: "We to-day conceive the hope of seeing spring up in the souls of Christ's faithful a holy eagerness to acquire this evangelical childhood"; links it to "reform of human society" set in early days of his pontificate and to "this solemn Jubilee" (1925); quotes her closing prayer from the Autobiography: "O Jesus, we beseech Thee to cast Thy divine eyes upon a great number of little souls, and to choose out of this world a legion of little victims worthy of Thy love."

## Ch. XIII: Virtues of the Little Way in practice: humility, simplicity, spiritual poverty, confidence; consecration to Merciful Love (pp. 339-)
p339 Chapter plan. Thérèse saw herself "unable ... to climb the rugged steps of perfection" (Histoire d'une Ame, ch. ix, p. 154). Consecration to "the Merciful Love of the Good God."
p340 She saw her father's humiliation (his illness) as "the most precious sign of the Saviour's mercy" [paraphrase by Laveille].
p340 Her fellow-worker in an assigned duty was an invalid Sister with eccentric character who gave her bitter reproaches. Scene told by a novice: Thérèse came "looking quite radiant"; said: "I have been with Sister X., and she told me everything that displeased her in me. She thinks, perhaps, that she has caused me pain; but no; on the contrary, she has given me pleasure. How I wish to meet her again, so as to greet her with a smile!" Just then the same Sister knocked; Thérèse received her "with sweet affability, just as if they had been exchanging compliments".
p341 An old nun said that Thérèse, "though only twenty", was charged with formation of the novices and had more need to direct herself. Thérèse: "Ah, Sister, you are indeed right; I am even far more imperfect than you imagine."
p341 Mère Agnès de Jésus: "I have seen her ... ask pardon with touching humility of the Sisters to whom she believed she had caused pain." In last illness to one Sister: "Oh, I sincerely ask your pardon. Pray for me." "And large teardrops rolled down her pale cheeks." Source: Summarium of 1919, p. 309, § 758.
p341 Few days before death, to Mère Agnès: "I experience very great joy not only in knowing that I am considered imperfect, but above all in realizing how great is my misery, and in feeling that I have so much need of God's mercy at the moment of death."
p341 To a novice: "To be humble ... we must joyfully consent to the commands of all. ... you should consider yourself as a little slave whom everyone has the right to command." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 413, § 1043.
p342 To novices' flattery: "These ... mean nothing to me. I am in reality what the good God thinks I am.... You envy me. But do you not know that I am very poor? It is the good God who gives me all according as I need it." Histoire d'une Ame ch. ix p. 152: "a poor, insignificant little creature, nothing more."
p342 Thérèse: "I think that I am the fruit, perhaps, of the desires of an unknown soul to whom I shall owe all the graces given me by the good God." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 412, § 1042.
p342 Letter to Abbé Bellière (unpublished letter of April 25, 1897): "Think not that humility prevents me from recognizing the gifts of God. I know that He has done great things in me, and I sing of them every day with joy."
p342 During illness, a Sister asked her to do some painting; Thérèse already very weak; Mère Agnès intervened (continued p343).
p343 Painting scene (cont.): Mère Agnès, present, told the Sister that the invalid "was consumed by fever" and work requiring application would exhaust her; Sister insisted; Thérèse's face "became slightly flushed from the inward strife"; she felt her sister had noticed. That evening note to Mère Agnès: "Just now your child has shed sweet tears, tears of repentance, but still more of thankfulness and love. To-day, I have shown you my virtue, my treasures of patience, I who preach so well to others! I am glad you have seen my imperfection. ... You did not chide me. ... you are for me the symbol of Divine Mercy. ... I confess that I am far more happy through having been imperfect than if, sustained by grace, I had been a model of patience." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 309, § 759.
p343 Scene, last illness: one morning Communion brought to her sickbed; at the Confiteor an extraordinary sentiment of humility. To Mère Agnès after thanksgiving: "I saw Our Saviour about to give Himself to me, and this humble confession seemed so necessary! ... I felt myself, like the publican, a great sinner. ... When I felt the sacred Host on my lips, I shed tears. I believe those were tears of perfect contrition." Added: "Ah, how impossible it is to arouse such sentiments in oneself! The Holy Spirit alone can produce them in the soul." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 310, § 760.
p344 Scene, last illness: a lay Sister, acting as "temporary infirmarian", offered her food that "would infallibly have brought on vomiting"; Thérèse gently refused, citing the danger. The Sister said afterwards: "I do not know why they talk so much of Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus; she does nothing remarkable; one cannot even say that she is exactly a good religious." Told of it, Thérèse smiled; to a visiting Sister: "To hear on my bed of death that I am not a good religious, what joy!" That Sister later: "It is the most edifying souvenir that I have retained of the Servant of God." Source: footnote 2, Summarium 1919 (OCR garbled "Tbtd. 5 7.3, SeLOLO" — page/§ unreadable in text).
p344 [Laveille comment] her life "unknown by many of her companions in the cloister"; she wished to remain one of the novices while directing them.
p345 "How simple it is to love Thee, my Saviour!" (her writing). Childhood playthings named: her doll, her bird-cage, her bowl of goldfish, her butterfly-net. In Carmel: needlework, sweeping the cloister, washing rough clothing.
p345 Her chief penance = the Rule kept silently "during the long months of nine years" (her years in Carmel). Favourite book: the Imitation; before all, the Gospels. [Laveille comment] learned men could not place "prayer of quiet" or "spiritual nuptials" in her life. Verse quoted from Verlaine.
p346 Shortly before death to Mère Agnès: "I have found great consolation in the thought that never in my spiritual life have I been able to pay back a single one of my debts to the good God ... I recalled the words of St John of the Cross, and repeated ... 'Oh, my God, I beseech Thee to pay all my debts for me.'" Source: Summarium 1919, p. 265, § 630.
p346 "Nothing remains in my hands ... Everything I get is for the Church and souls." Source: Conversation with Céline, July 12, 1897.
p346 To a novice: "You are quite little; remember that. And one who is quite little has no need of 'beautiful thoughts.'"
p346-347 Dialogue with Céline, who regretted lacking original pious thoughts and tender feeling. Thérèse: "The good God ... well knows the beautiful thoughts and ingenious ideas which we would like to have." Céline: "Yes, but you feel very tenderly towards the good God, whereas I do not..." Thérèse: "Certainly it will, especially if you accept the humiliation of this deficiency ... Say: 'My God, I thank Thee that I have not a single tender feeling, and I rejoice to see them in others.'" Source: Summarium 1919, p. 610, §§ 1773, 1774.
p347 Parable of labourers of the eleventh hour charmed her: "if we abandon ourselves ... we shall be rewarded and paid as much as the greatest saints."
p347 "My consolation is to have no consolation on earth." (Histoire d'une Ame ch. xi, p. 208). Quote from Louis Théolier, "Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus: Meditation on the Grandeur of Spiritual Childhood," Religious Studies (May 5, 1923): she walks in "the bleak night"; her own words: "To suffering ... I have become accustomed to respond with a smile."
p348 During father's illness she wrote to Céline: "Life is but a dream. Soon we shall awaken, and what joy that will be! The greater our sufferings are, the brighter will be our glory with God."
p348 "I know well I shall never be worthy of that for which I hope, but I stretch out my hands to Thee like a little beggar, and I am confident that Thou wilt grant all I ask, because Thou art so good." When prayers unanswered she thanked Our Saviour and saints for exercising her patience. Source: Summarium 1919, pp. 594-595, §§ 1708-1713.
p349 Céline (Soeur Geneviève de la Sainte-Face): "What I envy you are your works of zeal. I wish I could compose beautiful poetry like yours which would make God loved...." Thérèse: "we should not grieve over our powerlessness, but apply ourselves solely to love.... let us offer to the good God the works of others." Quotes Tauler on the Communion of saints ("If I love the good that is in my neighbour as well as he loves it himself, this good belongs to me as well as to him"). Source: Summarium 1919, p. 594, § 1706.
p349 On her health: "The good God sees all ... I abandon myself to Him. He knows well how to inspire our Mother to see that I am looked after should it be necessary." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 493, § 1333.
p349-350 A novice met "strong opposition in the community" at the time of admission to profession. Thérèse: "Are you confident of succeeding in spite of all?" Novice: "Yes ... nothing can shake my confidence." Thérèse: "Hold fast your confidence. It is impossible for the good God not to respond to it, for He ever measures His gifts by our hope in Him. Nevertheless, I admit that if I had seen you waver, I would have felt doubtful myself". The novice was admitted and became a fervent religious. (Name not given.)
p350 Trial of faith: temptation against faith and hope lasting "not less than eighteen months"; heaven seemed closed.
p350 R. P. Godefroy Madelaine (text: "Godefrey"): "I saw her at this period of her life ... Judging from the exterior, no one could guess the pain that was within." Thérèse: "I try ... not to let others suffer on account of my pain. It is known only to God, to our Mother Prioress, and to the confessor of the convent." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 528, § 1424.
p350 Illness: community doctor said of her: "Out of a hundred persons attacked as she is, perhaps two at most might have a chance of recovery." Thérèse gaily to a sister: "What a misfortune if I were going to be one of those two!" Asked "Then you are not afraid of death?": "On the contrary, it frightens me greatly when I see it represented in pictures as a spectre. But death is not a spectre. ... 'Death is the separation of soul and body.' Well, I do not fear a separation which will reunite me for ever to the good God." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 596, § 1716. (Doctor not named here.)
p351 "It is Jesus alone ... who has instructed me. No book, no theologian taught me; yet I feel in the depths of my heart that I am in the right." "strewing before Jesus the flowers of little sacrifices".
p352 [Laveille comment] lines of Histoire d'une Ame "said to have been written in her blood" (the vocations passage; Laveille compares to St Paul's Ego libentissime impendam).
p352-353 Quoted: "To be Thy spouse, O Jesus, to be a Carmelite, to be, by my union with Thee, the mother of souls ... I find the vocation of soldier, of priest, of apostle, of doctor, and of martyr... I feel within me the courage of a Crusader"; priesthood desired but she admires St Francis of Assisi's refusal of it; wishes to be missionary "from the very creation of the world until the very end of time"; "Martyrdom! That was the dream of my youth. This dream has grown in my little Carmelite cell."; to be flayed like St Bartholomew, boiling oil like St John, ground by beasts like St Ignatius of Antioch, sword like St Agnes and St Cecilia, and "like Joan of Arc, murmur the name of Jesus as I burned at the stake"; wishes torments of days of Antichrist reserved for her.
p353-354 She turned to St Paul (1 Cor. xii 31, xiii): "Be zealous for the better gifts. And I shew unto you yet a more excellent way." Love is the heart of the mystical body: "if love were extinct, apostles would no longer preach the Gospel, nor martyrs shed their blood." "O Jesus, my Love, I have at length discovered my vocation. My vocation is love. ... In the heart of the Church, my mother, I shall be love.... Thus I shall be all." Source: Histoire d'une Ame ch. xi, p. 213 ff.
p354 Jesus as her "Divine Eagle" who carries her on His wings; "lift" image again.

## Act of Oblation to Merciful Love (pp. 355-363)
p355 Act pronounced on the feast of the Holy Trinity, June 9, 1895. Her title: "Act of oblation as victim of holocaust to the Merciful Love of the Good God." It came to her during holy Mass. Her thought: "O my Divine Master ... shall Thy Justice alone receive victims of holocaust? Has not Thy merciful Love itself need of them? Everywhere it is ignored, rejected." ... "O Jesus, may I be that happy victim! Consume Thy little victim by the fire of Divine Love."
p355 Scene: at end of thanksgiving after Mass she came to Mère Agnès de Jésus, then prioress, "her face aglow with heavenly fervour", to ask consent; Mère Agnès gave authorization "without however appearing to attach special importance to the matter."
p356 She then wrote it as a formula and wanted it revised by a theologian. The Act was found after her death in a copy of the Holy Gospels "which she carried night and day near her heart."
p356-358 Full text of the Act quoted. Key lines: "O my God, Most Blessed Trinity, I desire to love Thee and to make Thee loved"; "I long to be a saint, but I know that I am powerless, and I implore Thee, O my God, to be Thyself my sanctity"; "Remain in me as in the Tabernacle; never leave Thy little victim"; "for having made me pass through the crucible of suffering"; hopes to see "the sacred stigmata of Thy passion shine in my glorified body"; "At the close of life's day, I shall appear before Thee with empty hands, for I ask not, Lord, that Thou wouldst count my works"; "I offer myself as a Victim of Holocaust to Thy Merciful Love, imploring Thee to consume me unceasingly"; "that so I may become a very martyr of Thy love"; "at every heart-beat, to renew this oblation an infinite number of times, till the shadows fade away". Signed "MARIE-FRANÇOISE-THÉRÈSE DE L'ENFANT JÉSUS ET DE LA SAINTE FACE, Rel. Carm. Ind." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 552, § 1526.
p358 Examined by R. P. Lemonnier of the Missionaries of La Délivrande, who came to preach a retreat at the Carmel. He found it orthodox; changed only one adjective: she had written "Infinite are the desires that I feel within my heart"; on his advice she put "Immense" (theologically more exact) (footnote 3). He thought two requests daring (stigmata in heaven; Christ's presence kept between Communions) but did not cut them.
p358 She kept the consecration secret except from two novices; recited it very often; renewed it until her last hour.
p359-360 R. P. (G.) Martin on its effects: soul lives "in an act of perfect love"; oblation "does not give impeccability" (La "petite voie" d'enfance spirituelle, p. 84). Laveille: the Act does not bring exceptional sufferings as a necessary consequence [Laveille comment].
p360 Her earlier words: "I fear but one thing; it is to keep my own will. Take that, for I choose everything that Thou willest." Habit "to look into the eyes of the good God".
p360 "If my Jesus appears to forget me, well, He is free to do so, since I am no longer mine but His. He will tire more quickly of making me wait than I of waiting for Him." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 260, [§] 630.
p361 In agony, told "It is frightful what you are suffering," she replied: "No ... it is not frightful; could a little victim of love consider frightful what her Spouse sends her?" (Histoire d'une Ame ch. xii, p. 247).
p361 Zeal for sanctification of priests = great preoccupation of her religious life; grew after the oblation.
p361 Pius XI: "spiritual childhood is a way which, without giving to everyone assurance of reaching the heights to which God has led Thérése, is not only possible, but easy for all." Source: Discourse for the Approbation of Miracles, February 11, 1923.
p362 Wound of love (compare transverberation of St Teresa of Avila). Her words: "Some days after my offering to Merciful Love ... I was commencing in the choir the Way of the Cross, when I suddenly felt myself wounded with a dart of fire so ardent that I thought I should die. ... It seemed to me that an invisible power plunged me wholly in fire. Oh, what fire! what sweetness!"
p362 Footnote: the same day she told Mère Agnès (then Prioress), who paid no special attention; Thérèse did not mention it again nor in her manuscript. Only on July 7, 1897 did Mère Agnès question her again and get the account. She added: "As for me, I have had the experience but once, and that for an instant only; I then fell back immediately into my habitual aridity."
p362-363 Earlier transports: "once in particular during the novitiate when I remained for a whole week far indeed from this earth ... I acted, as it seemed, with a body not my own; for me there seemed to be a veil thrown over every earthly thing." But on the Way of the Cross day: "a minute, a second more, and my soul must have parted from the body.... Alas! I found myself still on earth, and aridity returned immediately to my soul." Source: Histoire d'une Ame ch. xii, p. 228.

## Ch. XIV: Beginning of her illness; Histoire d'une Ame; Novissima Verba; end of exile; funeral (pp. 364-)
p364 She believed God sends messages by dreams (footnote cites St Joseph's dream).
p364-365 Dream of May 10, 1896 (text: "roth of May"; Histoire d'une Ame ch. xii, p. 211): at first light of dawn, walking in a gallery "with our Mother alone"; three Carmelites in white mantles and large veils, from heaven; tallest raised her veil and covered Thérèse with it; she recognized "the Venerable Anne of Jesus, Foundress of Carmel in France." Face "beautiful with a beauty not of this earth ... illumined by a marvellously delicate light". Thérèse: "O Mother ... tell me if God will leave me long on earth. Will He come soon for me?" Answer: "Yes, soon ... soon.... I promise you." Thérèse: "tell me if the good God requires nothing more of me than my poor little acts and my desires. Is He satisfied with me?" Answer: "God desires nothing more from you; He is satisfied, well satisfied. ..." She laid hands on her head and caressed her; Thérèse thought of her sisters and wished to ask graces for them; then woke.
p364 Footnote: Anne of Jesus = companion of St Teresa of Avila; brought to France by Père de Bérulle; established the Teresian reform in France; died in odour of sanctity 1621; Leo XIII signed decree introducing her Cause. Thérèse admits that before the dream she rarely thought of her.
p365 Footnote: Thérèse's words recorded by Mère Agnès in last illness: "From my childhood, I had a strong presentiment that I should die young. The good God has always made me desire what He willed to give me." Source: Summarium 1919, p. 889, § 2574.
p365-366 FIRST HAEMOPTYSIS: night of April 3, 1896, between Holy Thursday and Good Friday. Her poetic phrase: "as it were, a far-off murmur announcing to her the coming of the Spouse." Her account (related in 1897): "At the time of Lent, last year, I felt stronger than ever before ... notwithstanding the fast which I observed in all its strictness, continued until Eastertide. ... Not having obtained permission on the Thursday evening to remain at the sepulchre all night, I went to our cell at midnight. No sooner did my head rest on the pillow, than I felt a burning stream rise to my lips; I believed that I was going to die, and my heart was thrilled with joy. However, as I had just extinguished our little lamp, I mortified my curiosity until morning and slept peacefully. At five, when the signal for rising was given ... going to the window, my suspicions were verified by finding our handkerchief all blood-stained."
p366 Then: "With great fervour I assisted at Prime, and afterwards at Chapter. I then hastened to kneel beside you, Mother, and confide to you my joy. I did not feel the slightest fatigue or suffering, and so I easily obtained permission to finish Lent as I had begun. Thus, on Good Friday, I shared in all the austerities of Carmel without any mitigation." "I was in transports of joy at the thought of going to heaven." At close of day she again went to her cell (continued p367: second haemorrhage).
p367 Second night (Good Friday night, April 1896): "when my good Jesus gave me, as on the preceding night, the same sign" (second haemorrhage). "I enjoyed such unclouded living faith, that the thought of heaven was my greatest happiness." Source: Histoire d'une Ame ch. ix, pp. 156 ff.

## Writing of Histoire d'une Ame (pp. 367-370)
p367 SCENE: one winter evening in December 1894, at recreation, Mère Agnès de Jésus (then Prioress), Soeur Marie du Sacré-Coeur and Thérèse gathered around the fire in the community-room ("the only place where the nuns had permission to warm themselves"). Thérèse told incidents of her early years.
p367 Soeur Marie du Sacré-Coeur took the Prioress aside: "It is really a pity, that although Soeur Thérése writes verses for one or other of the Sisters, she writes nothing that will preserve to us the memories of her childhood. Our little sister is an angel who will not long remain on earth, and we shall be sorry later on to have lost these details which mean so much for us."
p367 Mère Agnès hesitated some weeks, then ordered Thérèse to write, for her (Mère Agnès's) feast day, an account of the principal events of her childhood. Thérèse began at the opening of 1895; wrote only in rare leisure moments; sacristy duties took her free time.
p367-368 January 20, 1896: going to choir for evening Prayer, she knelt and gave the Prioress the manuscript, "written without erasure on poor paper that a servant would have disdained to use." Mère Agnès acknowledged it by an inclination of the head, laid it on her stall, and did not read it until months later, when relieved of office of Prioress. Thérèse showed no anxiety; when told it had not been read she was not disturbed.
p368 This first manuscript = first eight chapters of Histoire d'une Ame. Mère Agnès, no longer Superior, could not order her to continue. Seeing her ill, she persuaded Mère Marie de Gonzague, then Prioress, to have Thérèse write of her life in the cloister -> Chapters IX and X. Pages written during her retreat of 1896 on her "little doctrine", addressed to Soeur Marie du Sacré-Coeur -> Chapter XI.
p368-369 August 1, 1897: Mère Agnès told Thérèse she meant to have the manuscript read in the community and later published; feared some Sisters would oppose. Thérèse: "My Mother, after my death, my manuscript should not be spoken of to anyone until it is published. If you do otherwise, or if you delay the publication, the demon will set many snares for you in order to hinder God's good work ... a work that is very important...."
p369 Imprimatur of Mgr Hugonin (Bishop of Bayeux) dated March 8, 1898; Histoire d'une Ame appeared October 1898. "A few weeks later, by reason of newly-arisen circumstances, its publication would have been impossible" (circumstances not specified).
p369 SCENE: Mère Agnès asked her to revise an incomplete passage; entering the infirmary soon after, found her eyes wet with tears. "You are crying?" Thérèse: "It is indeed the manifestation of my soul.... Yes, these pages will do a great deal of good. Through them God's gentleness and sweetness will become better known. ..." Then: "Ah, I know it, everyone will love me. ..." [Laveille comment: gift of prophecy.]
p369 Pius XI (then pope) on "this marvellous book, the freshness and grace of which are so natural" — source: Letter of Cardinal Vico, May 14, 1923.
p370 Brief read at canonization by the Secretary of Briefs: "The book on her own life written by Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus in the limpid beauty of her mother-tongue ... is not only in the hands of all, but its sweetness penetrates the hearts of men most estranged from Christian perfection. Numbers of them have been converted by reading it".
p370 Abbé Henri Bremond wanted her narratives and letter extracts for his planned "Légende d'Argent"; letters "are of an incomparable freshness, grace, and charity." Source: quoted by M. A. des Rotours, La Bienheureuse Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus, p. 150; Bremond's study in L'Inquiétude religieuse, 2nd series, 1909.
p370 Copies: "the 400,000 copies of the Histoire d'une Ame now in circulation" (as of Laveille's writing).

## Illness 1896-1897 (pp. 370-)
p370-371 On Good Friday 1896 the Prioress was the recently re-elected Mère Marie de Gonzague. Deceived by Thérèse's energy, she did not think the haemorrhage serious; allowed all austerities; Thérèse even spent the interval between the long Good Friday Offices cleaning the convent windows. A novice found her "pale as death and almost fainting", after fasting on bread and water, offered to replace her; refused.
p371 She hid her state from her three sisters; they noticed pallor but suspected nothing. Some weeks later they heard "the dry persistent cough that nothing could alleviate". The Prioress grew uneasy and relieved her of sacristy duties.
p371 DOCTORS: Dr. de Cornière, who usually attended the community, and Dr. La Néele, "a relative by marriage of the invalid" (Francis La Néele, husband of cousin Jeanne Guérin), had noticed nothing alarming. To prevent recurrence of haemorrhage they gave a course of painful treatment: "massage, blistering, cupping, cauterizing, etc." She endured them smiling, with words of gratitude. A strengthening regimen banished the cough for a few months.
p371 To a novice: "Sickness is truly too slow a conductor; I rely on love alone."
p371-372 Improvement revived hope of going to the Carmel of Hanoi, who again asked for her. She could go only if completely cured; in November 1896 began a novena to Venerable Théophane Vénard. Right after the novena a grave relapse.
p372 SYMPTOMS: "intense and continuous fever, joined to persistent difficulties of digestion". Mère Marie de Gonzague still let her follow all community exercises. Before going to the infirmary, when going to her cell unaided she stopped at each step to take breath; took an hour to undress; "There on her hard and narrow pallet, wrapped in two poor blankets, she would await, nearly always in feverish unrest, the return of day".
p372 All winter strength declined. Dr. de Cornière: "I shall not be able to cure her ... besides, this soul is not made for earth."
p372 End of Lent 1897: very alarming symptoms. Doctor continued severe remedies, "especially more and more frequent applications of the cauterizing needle." After each course she had to stay very quiet for some hours.
p372-373 SCENE: lying in her cell during recreation after cauterization, she heard a Sister in the kitchen: "Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus will soon die, and in truth, I ask myself what can our Mother say of her after her death? She will be embarrassed, for this little Sister, amiable though she is, has surely done nothing worth recounting." Footnote: in France the Prioress sends to all monasteries of the Order a biographical notice (circular) on each deceased nun. The infirmarian: "If you had set any value on the opinion of creatures, you would be indeed disappointed today!" Thérèse: "The opinion of creatures! Ah, happily the good God has always given me the grace of being absolutely indifferent to it."
p373 Infirmarian: "It is said ... that you have never suffered very much." Thérèse pointed to a glass with bright red liquid (a medicine): "Do you see that little glass? ... One would believe that it contained a delicious liqueur; in reality, I take nothing more bitter. Well, that is an image of my life. ... I have known how to turn every bitterness into joy and sweetness." "You are suffering greatly at this moment, are you not?" "Yes. But then, I have so much desired it." Source: Histoire d'une Ame ch. xii, p. 235 etc.
p373 Constant temptations against faith prevented her enjoying thought of death; "double suffering, moral and physical".

Oh, my brother, how happy I am to die. ... suffering united to love is the only thing which appears to me desirable in this valley of tears." I think of all the good that I want to do after death, to have little children baptized, to help priests, missionaries, the whole Church...."

## Death, September 30, 1897 (pp. 396-399)
p396 September 29 from early morning: "distressing rattle in the throat". About midday to the Prioress: "Mother, is this the death-agony? ... How am I to prepare for death?..." Office of St Michael the Archangel read to her in French, and the prayers for the dying. Doctor came on usual visit. After he left: "Is it today, Mother?" Mère Marie de Gonzague: "Yes." Sisters: "The good God ... is joyous today because He is going to receive you into heaven." Thérèse: "And I too! ... Oh, if I should die immediately, what a happiness!" Later: "I am utterly exhausted—can do no more ... Ah, pray for me. If you but knew!" After Matins, hands joined: "Yes, my God! yes, yes! I accept all willingly."
p396 Thursday, September 30, 1897: morning, looking at the miraculous statue facing her bed: "Oh, I have prayed to her so fervently; but it is pure agony without any measure of consolation." All day burning fever. "Ah ... if this is the agony, what then is death?"
p397 To Prioress: "Oh, my Mother, I assure you that the chalice is full to the brim." "Yes, my God, do whatever Thou willest, but have pity on me." To sisters: "My little sisters, pray for me." "My God, Thou who art so good; oh yes, Thou art good, I know it...."
p397 About 3 o'clock: she put her arms in the form of a cross; Mère Marie de Gonzague placed on her knees an image of Our Lady of Mount Carmel. "Oh, my Mother, present me to the Blessed Virgin without delay. Prepare me to die well." Prioress reminded her she had understood humility. Thérèse: "Yes, I have never sought anything but the truth. Yes, I have understood humility of heart...." "All that I have written of my desire for suffering, all is most true." "I do not repent ... of having surrendered myself to love." "I would never have believed that it was possible to suffer so much, never, never! I cannot explain it except by the ardent desire I have had of saving souls."
p397 About 5 o'clock: Mère Agnès, alone with her, saw a sudden change in her face; bell rung hurriedly to call the community to the infirmary. She smiled at each Sister, then contemplated her Crucifix.
p398 For two hours "the terrible death-rattle tore her chest. She trembled in every limb, and the sweat of death was so profuse that soon the bed-covering and mattress were saturated." Soeur Geneviève cooled her lips with a small particle of ice; Thérèse gave her a look and smile ("little companion of her childhood").
p398 About 7 o'clock, no change, the Prioress dismissed the community. Thérèse: "Has the agony not yet come, Mother? Am I not going to die?" "Yes, my child, this is the agony; but the good God wishes perhaps to prolong it for a few hours...." "Well then, let it be so. Oh, I would not wish to suffer less." Gazing at the Crucifix: "Oh ... I LOVE Him! My God, I ... LOVE ... THEE!" — her last words.
p398-399 She sank back, head leaning to the right; then suddenly raised herself "as if called by some mysterious voice", opened her eyes "which beamed with heavenly peace and indescribable joy", gazed fixedly at a point a little above the statue of the Blessed Virgin facing her bed; face regained "its lily whiteness". Duration: companions say "For a space sufficient to recite a Credo." Then her head fell back and she expired "in this seraphic ecstasy".
p399 DEATH: September 30, 1897, about 7.20 p.m.; age twenty-four years and nine months.
p399 After death: clothed in her religious habit, crown of white roses on her head, palm branch in her hand; laid first on the paillasse from her own cell, brought down to the infirmary. Footnote: the palm was found intact in the coffin at the first exhumation (1910), thirteen years later.
p399 Carmel custom: deceased nun laid out with face uncovered near the grille of the choir before burial. The public saw her through the cloister bars; crowds came to pray and passed rosaries, medals, crucifixes through to touch the body.
p399 MIRACLE (first): the lay-sister who had harshly treated her over her arrangement of wreaths around Mère Geneviève's coffin suffered from "cerebral anaemia"; repentant, she kissed the saint's feet, leant her forehead on them some moments, "and felt herself suddenly cured" (lay-sister not named).
p399 Other nuns noticed perfume of lilies and violets where no flowers grew. [Laveille comment: beginning of the "shower".]
p400 FUNERAL: Monday, October 4, 1897. "A goodly number of priests" in the chapel. Procession to the cemetery "small and unassuming". Buried in "the little enclosure recently acquired by the Carmelites in the cemetery of Lisieux" (p399); "A deep grave had been dug in a corner of the enclosed space reserved to the Carmelites." Only liturgical prayers; body quickly covered with clay; "The few mourners withdrew".

## Ch. XV: First phases of glorification; "rain of roses"; raised to the altar (pp. 401-)
p401 [Laveille] Carmel's fervour "had been at times affected by the unequable temperament of Mère Marie de Gonzague", explaining trials inflicted on Thérèse "by two or three Sisters who were unconscious of her high virtue." After the death the most prejudiced nuns reproached themselves.

Footnote: he asked through Thérèse to celebrate Mass to his last day; died just as he was going to the altar, November 15, 1919, in his seventy-seventh year; had made her Act of oblation (Legion of Victims of Merciful Love).

p409 MIRACLE (Beatification miracle no. 1): M. l'abbé (Charles) Anne, native of Lisieux, seminarian at the Grand Seminary of Bayeux (seminarians debated Thérèse at recreation; he defended her). Health failed from about June 1904. August 23, 1906: collapsed after a haemorrhage; advanced consumption.
p410 Anne: doctors found "large cavities already in the lungs, burning fever ... utter aversion to food"; death within days expected. Novena to Thérèse; relic hung round his neck. One evening the nursing Sister, sure he would die that night, urged him to sacrifice his life. He seized the relic, pressed it to his heart, prayed silently: "Little Thérése, you are in heaven; of that I feel sure. I am on earth where there is good to be done. You must cure me." At once he sat upright; suffocation, pain and fever gone; wanted to get up. Doctors, hastily called, declared him in perfect health: "Instead of the lungs which were consumed and destroyed, new lungs had suddenly been formed". Worn look vanished after a few days of normal food. [Laveille: "a veritable resurrection."]
p410-411 Abbé Anne now (1920s) chaplain of the public hospital of Lisieux, serving eight hundred patients; "in the prime of life" and perfect health.
p411-412 MIRACLE: Mgr Prosper Augouard, Vicar Apostolic of Ubanghi (Bishop of Upper French Congo). In 1910 attacked by severe rheumatism threatening permanent helplessness. His letter, BRAZZAVILLE, November 10, 1912: "Two years ago, I was confined to my room for three months by very painful rheumatism ... I had an opportunity of reading the Life of little Soeur Thérése ... I besought the dear Carmelite to ask God for my cure or my death ... Having reflected during the night, I withdrew this petition ... 'Let me suffer, since such is the will of God. I ask nothing now for myself, but save Fr ... (naming one of my missionaries) from death.'" The missionary had sleeping sickness; sent to France, examined at the Pasteur Institute and "pronounced entirely free from any microbe"; returned to the Congo healthy (had had symptoms for two years). "To crown all, I myself ... was cured the day after I had withdrawn my petition." He made a thanksgiving pilgrimage to her tomb at Lisieux; asks the Pope to introduce the Cause. Source: Shower of Roses for 1912, p. 107.

Early 1909 appointed by Mgr Lemonnier and the Carmel to forward the Cause; worked 13 years; died at Paris May 20, 1922, holding her picture.

p416 CONVERSION: a former United Free Church minister of Lochranza in Arran wrote from Edinburgh April 23, 1911 to the Prioress of Lisieux: read the English translation of the Autobiography more than a year before, opened at random; "the work of a genius as well as a theologian ... a poet too of the first order"; "'Thérése is here in this room'"; heard her: "See how Catholic saints love Jesus Christ. She has written a book ... coming to Lisieux as a sceptic, I read this book, and I in my turn was thrilled with tender admiration. Here we have the real miracle." Bones "exhaled a sweet perfume (noticed by several witnesses)"; earth that touched the coffin kept fragrance for months. (Palm intact: see p399.)
p421-422 WAR 1914-18: soldiers ("poilus") invoked her; with St Michael and St Joan of Arc "one of the invisible captains". Artilleryman: "Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus, protect me, take the place of mother who is there no longer." Source: Interventions de Soeur Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus pendant la guerre, Preface.

General Duplessis vowed a pilgrimage to her tomb if spared; afterwards proclaimed her help (Interventions, p. 188). Bullets stopped/flattened by relics, medals, pictures, booklets: many such "bucklers" sent to the Carmel.
p422-423 Air-pilot Bourjade (Père Jean Bourjade), Missionary of the Sacred Heart of Issoudun, later missionary in New Guinea until his death; compared with Guynemer. Kept letters of thanks to her, published in La Croix, February 19 and 21, 1925. As foot-soldier on scout duty: "How often ... when obliged to advance under the hail of shells ... have I under thy protection, Soeur Thérése, come through all without a scratch." His plane carried her picture as emblem. "on more than ten occasions, I have had hair-breadth escapes of fatal accidents.... do not forget that it is your fault if I am decorated".

p425-426 MIRACLE (Beatification miracle no. 2): Soeur Louise de Saint-Germain, Congregation of the Daughters of the Cross, provincial convent of Ustarritz (Basses-Pyrénées); from 1912 a dangerous stomach ulcer with frequent haemorrhage; twice tried to resume teaching in a Spanish convent, stopped by violent haemorrhages; Thérèse favoured her with heavenly perfumes; early 1915 given last sacraments; stopped asking for cure. Her account: "In the beginning of September, 1916 ... a Sister ... induced me to renew my petitions ... on the night of September 10, Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus came herself to me and said: 'Be generous; you will soon be cured; I promise it.' She then disappeared. In the morning, the three Sisters who were sleeping in the infirmary were greatly surprised to find rose-petals of different colours scattered around my bed." More days of "real martyrdom"; "only on the evening of September 21 that, after a most violent attack, I suddenly and very unexpectedly fell asleep to awake on the following morning completely cured." Felt well and very hungry; asked to go to Mass; Mother Superior sent her back to bed with a plentiful breakfast; resumed duties; perfect health since. Source: Journal des Pèlerins, October 5 to 18, 1924. Attested by "formal certificates of renowned doctors".
p426 The Anne and Louise de Saint-Germain cures were chosen for the papal Decree on miracles. First Decree, signed August 14 (1921), declared heroic virtue (Benedict XV's discourse, "the secret of holiness").

Same day he called her an "exquisitely fine miniature of perfect holiness", "his dear star", a "miracle of virtues and a prodigy of miracles." Source: Discourse on Approbation of her miracles, Feb. 11, 1923.
p427 Decree de tuto, March 19, 1923: Pius XI called her "the true flower of love come from heaven to earth to astonish heaven and earth." (Discourse on promulgation of Decree de tuto.)

Pontifical Brief read (virginity, her life, little way, intercession); privileges: public exposition of relics, Mass and Office for diocese of Bayeux and Carmelite churches and convents.

Bishop of Bayeux celebrated the new Mass. He chose her as "his patron and counsellor", the "Star" of his pontificate. Pius XI: "She has in truth thrown light on questions that I was unable to solve." And: "These days I feel quite near me the presence of Blessed Thérése." Source: La Bienheureuse Thérèse ... sa béatification, p. 110 (garbled "Dy lio").

Source: ...sa béatification, p. 140.

## APPENDIX (a): Unpublished letters of St Thérèse to the Guérin family — fragments (pp. 448-)
p448 Letters reached the author too late for the body of the book; published "with the consent of Mme La Néele, née Jeanne Guérin, who has handed them over to the Carmel of Lisieux."
p448-449 LETTER To her cousin Jeanne Guérin, July 1887 (footnote: she had just come back from a holiday in Trouville with the Guérin family). Teases Jeanne: "I suspect that you are not sorry to hear no longer my sermons on death, and to escape from my eyes 'which fascinated you.'" Silkworms: "I have to announce to you the death of eight of my little silkworms; I have now only four. Céline has given them so much care in my absence, that she has succeeded ... in making them almost all die of vexation or apoplexy." Fears the other four "will follow them to the grave". "It seems quite strange to find myself at Les Buissonnets once more. I was really surprised this morning to see Céline at my side."
p449 LETTER To Mme Guérin, Rome, November 14, 1887 (aunt's feast-day greeting). "she desires at least that a word from her heart should travel over the mountains in her stead." On the coming papal audience: "I do not know how I shall speak to the Pope; indeed, if the good God did not guide me in everything, I know not what I should do. ... We do not yet know on what day the audience will be. It appears that, when giving a public audience, the Holy Father passes along in front of the faithful; I do not think he stops. In spite of all, I am determined to speak to him."
p449-450 LETTER To Mme Guérin, THE CARMEL, August 23, 1888. On news received "yesterday evening" of the death of "Monsieur X...." (name withheld). "in moments of great sadness, we must turn our eyes to heaven instead of weeping. All the saints rejoice because Our Saviour has now one more added to His elect ... they wonder that we can call death that which is really the beginning of life; they consider us to be in a narrow tomb." "This morning, she is quite absorbed in the thought of the death of saints."
p450 LETTER To Mme Guérin, November 18, 1888 (her aunt's feast-day). Footnote: alludes to the first attack of her father's illness. "Alas, it is not joy He has sent for some time past; it is the Cross, the Cross alone He has given us to rest on.... Oh, if no one else were to suffer but myself, that would be of little consequence; but I know how greatly our trial affects you also. I would wish, on your feast-day, to relieve you of all sadness, to take upon myself all your grief." "suffering is the best that He can give us, and that He gives it only to His chosen friends."
p450 LETTER To Mme Guérin, December 28, 1888: New Year wishes for 1889. "When I think that your little niece will soon be nine months in the Carmel, I cannot get over my astonishment. How quickly life passes; already I am sixteen years on earth. Soon we shall be re-united in heaven." (cont. p451, Psalmist quote).
p451 (Dec. 28, 1888 cont.) Quotes Psalm: "A thousand years are in the eyes of the Lord but as yesterday which is already gone. What rapidity! Oh, I want to work well while the day of life still shines, for afterwards will come the night when I shall be unable to do anything." "Pray for your little niece ... that she may not abuse the graces showered on her by God in the little valley of the Carmel."
p451 LETTER To Mme Guérin, January 2, 1889: thanks for the wreath sent for her reception of the habit (Clothing, Jan. 10, 1889). "Your little niece is filled with joy.... But a mother can read the heart of her child." "The lilies are delightful; one would say that they had just been plucked. How good of my little cousins to send them to me. It will be a great joy for me, the day of my reception, to think that it is they who have adorned me to go to my divine Fiancé." "Our mother thinks the wreath very beautiful. Never have I seen flowers that pleased me so much; lilies are so pure. ... to have them in my hair is not sufficient; it is the heart that Jesus always regards.... Pray that your little niece may be as well adorned interiorly as she is exteriorly."
p451-452 LETTER To M. and Mme Guérin, December 30, 1889: "Your Benjamin wishes you, in her turn, a happy new year. As each day has its last hour, so each year sees the arrival of its last evening." "if His Hand has held out to us the chalice of bitterness, His Divine Heart has sustained us in our trial" (footnote: her father's sad illness). "The first day of the year contains for me a host of memories. Once again, I see papa showering his caresses upon us.... He was so good!.... This dear father has received the recompense of his virtues. God has sent him a trial that is worthy of him."
p452 LETTER To Mme Guérin, November 17, 1890: "How many memories does not November 19 hold for me! ... because it was the feast-day of my beloved auntie. Now, with the passing of the years, the little birds have grown, they have spread their wings, and have flown from the dear nest of their youth." "poor indeed myself, but having for Spouse a very rich and powerful King, I charge Him to bestow bountifully on my dear aunt the treasures of His love, thus repaying her for all the maternal care with which she surrounded my early years".
p453 LETTER To Mme Guérin, August 10, 1893 (footnote: at the château de la Musse). "every line that you have written has revealed your heart to me; it is a heart as tender as that of the tenderest of mothers." "I cannot tell you the happiness I feel in realizing that my beloved father is in your midst, surrounded with tenderness and care. God has done for him what He did for His servant Job; having humiliated Him, he now showers favours upon him".
p453 LETTER To Mme La Néele (cousin Jeanne Guérin, married to Dr. La Néele — "Francis"), October 22, 1893: "Our mother St Teresa was of so grateful a disposition, that, as she was wont to say pleasantly, 'her heart could be won with a sardine.' What would she have said, had she known Francis and Jeanne?" St Teresa also had a sister named Jeanne who watched over her nephews; "without forgetting the good St Anne, I turn to St Teresa to obtain through her intercession that I also may be an aunt"; prays God send Jeanne "a holy family that will give great saints to the Church.... The long delay does not discourage me, for I know that Rome requires a long time in order to make saints."
p454 LETTER To Mme Guérin, November 17, 1893 (feast-day): "When I am before the Tabernacle, I can say nothing to Our Saviour but this: 'My God, Thou knowest that I love Thee.' And I know that Jesus is not wearied by my prayer". "the good God has infused into the hearts of mothers something of the love with which His own Heart overflows." "in the eternal Fatherland, I shall be able to say to my dearest auntie many things that human language cannot convey."
p454-455 LETTER To M. and Mme Guérin, December 29, 1893: New Year. Quotes St Teresa: "My God, I do not wonder that Thou hast so few friends, so badly dost Thou treat them!" "My dear father's illness is to me an evident proof of it. This cross was the greatest I could imagine; but ... Our Saviour has willed to sweeten by the hands of our dear ones the chalice of grief". "The hour for matins has come; pardon this wandering letter, and such scribbling ... consider only the heart of your child."
p455-456 LETTER To Mme Guérin, July 16, 1896: "I can find nothing to speak of save affection for my dear ones ... and this, far from being new, is as old as myself." HEALTH: "if I tell you that I am in wonderfully good health, you will hardly believe me; so I will let the doctor who examined me yesterday speak. He declared that 'I looked well....'" Still thinks she will soon "go and join the little angels in heaven" — because of Abbé Lechêne (text "Lechéne"), who preached that day in the Carmel chapel on the Order's origin, compared the nuns to Elias against the priests of Baal, and declared "a period similar to that of Achab's persecution was about to begin." "It seemed to us as though we were already hastening to martyrdom. What happiness, dear auntie, if all our family were to enter heaven on the same day. I seem to see you smile." Footnote: the saint herself underlined the repetitions.
p456-457 LETTER To Mme Guérin, November 16, 1896 (feast-day): sad to confide feelings "to a lifeless pen"; cannot find expressions; quotes a poet (Boileau): "Thoughts well conceived find utterance clear, / For words to clothe them readily appear"; consoled by "the learned Father Faber": earthly words cannot express the heart; "full hearts are those most shut up within themselves." "the last and least of your nieces was not so in affection and gratitude ... only in age and wisdom." "I shall soon be nine years in the house of the Lord ... I am yet only at the bottom of the ladder. That does not discourage me; I am as merry as the grasshopper. Like her, I sing always, hoping, at the end of my life, to share the riches of my sisters who are far more generous than the ant. Ah, I have indeed been born under a lucky star". "a poor grasshopper who has nothing but her songs (and she cannot now sing except in her heart, for there is little melody in her voice)". "so many things that have neither rhyme nor reason".
p457-459 LETTER To M. and Mme Guérin, July 16, 1897 (written in pencil; this is the letter answered by M. Guérin's reply quoted p384): "I am very happy to prove to you that your little Thérése has not yet left this exile ... your joy will be greater still when instead of reading a few lines written by my trembling hand, you will feel my soul near yours." Promises graces to "my little cousin Jeanne and her dear Francis; I shall choose the most beautiful cherub in heaven and ask God to give it to them"; if not heard, Jeanne will be "mother of many children" = souls won by her "cheerfully accepted sacrifice" (childlessness). Communion that morning (July 16, feast of Our Lady of Mount Carmel) made "triumphant by your flowers"; "my dear little sister, Marie de l'Eucharistie" (cousin Marie Guérin) sang before Communion a couplet Thérèse composed for that morning, and after Communion a couplet from "Vivre d'amour": "To die of love, O martyrdom most sweet." "I cannot tell you how beautiful and clear her voice was ... in order to please me, she had promised not to weep". "True I am like a lark except when the fever attacks me; happily, it only comes at night, when larks sleep with heads hidden beneath their wings." "the only joy on earth is in the accomplishment of His will." "Adieu, my dear ones; only in heaven shall I tell you of all my affection for you; my pencil cannot convey it now."
p459-460 LETTER to Mère Saint-Placide, her former School-Directress (Benedictine Abbey, Lisieux); undated (from context late 1888, before her Clothing; signed "Soeur Thérése de l'Enfant Jésus, Post. carm. ind."). Reply to a circular to past-pupil Children of Mary announcing the fête of the twenty-fifth anniversary of the Association. "was it not in that blessed chapel that the Holy Virgin deigned to adopt me for her child on the beautiful day of my first Communion, and on that of my reception into the congregation of the Children of Mary?" "the signal grace of my religious vocation was implanted that happy day when, surrounded by my good teachers, I consecrated myself to Mary at the foot of her altar". On her father's illness: "For some days, I feared that God would take him from me; but Jesus ... for the moment has restored him to health, for my reception." Waiting for "Monseigneur" to fix the date of the Clothing after her acceptance "at the Chapter". Asks to be remembered to her teachers, "in particular to the Mother Prioress", and to her companions.

p466 VII. MILITARY SERVICE OF CAPTAIN BOUREAU (great-grandfather; maternal grandfather of Louis Martin): Jean-Nicolas Boureau enlisted at seventeen in 1791 as sergeant in the army of Nord; sub-lieutenant the next year; captain at twenty; served in the army of the Côtes de Brest; resigned 1796 for health; resumed service in the Grand Army in 1812; taken prisoner by the Prussians in Silesia on 19 August 1813; his son Jean-Prosper, aged 12½ years, also prisoner, died there 21 September 1813. 1816-1817 served in the royal army at Lyons with Captain Martin; discharged 1817. Nicolas-Jean Boureau, captain, living in this parish, rue Vaubecourt n° 4, with his wife and two daughters, led a life dictated by the principles of honour, wisdom and religion".
