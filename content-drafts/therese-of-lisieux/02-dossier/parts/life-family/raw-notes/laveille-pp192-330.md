# Laveille 1928 English Life — research notes, PDF pp. 192–330 (book p. = PDF p. − 20)

## Ch. VII: The Carmel of Lisieux — Thérèse as postulant (PDF pp. 192–)
p192 Carmel location: near centre of old Lisieux, not far from church of St Jacques, narrow street partly bordered by old timber-framed ("wood-stayed") houses. Chapel later embellished and enlarged in her honour; cloister unchanged since her time.
p192 Building: red brick, "austere and geometrical" architecture, small slate-roofed dome. Garden narrow, closed in between "the muddy waters of the Orbiquet river" and neighbouring properties; impression of smallness.
p192 Foundation 1838: in a small thatched house lent to them in the Chaussée de Beuvillers; first four Lisieux Carmelites, trained and governed by two nuns from Poitiers; extreme poverty. Footnote: see "The Foundation of the Carmel of Lisieux, and its Foundress, Rev. Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse", Central Office, Lisieux.
p192-193 First lodging: little more than two miserable garrets, a poor oratory, and a room eighteen feet square divided into three compartments; no basic furniture. A box in the kitchen = cupboard; a chimneyboard in a corner = larder; stone floor used as serving-table. Too few plates: dishes washed during dinner for reuse at same meal.
p193 Mère Elizabeth de Saint-Louis (Prioress) and Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse moved the community to an old house in the rue de Livarot (OCR "Liverot"), later replaced by present convent. More hardship: "more than once the sisters had to dine on fried leeks."
p193 Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse (one of the two Poitiers nuns) chosen Prioress after Mère Elizabeth's death; long government; described as of "consummate humility" [Laveille comment on her virtue]. New postulants brought material prosperity.
p193 Mère Aimée de Jésus (future Prioress of Carmel of Coutances) governed Lisieux 3 years during the temporary deposition (required by rule) of Mère Geneviève; in 1858 built first large wing of present convent.
p193 Mère Marie de Gonzague, who later succeeded Mère Geneviève, in 1876 began the building that gave the convent its final form.
p194 Entry date fixed: April 9, 1888. Last days at Les Buissonnets with father, Léonie and Céline.
p194 Footnote: Léonie had come home after a trial of religious life in the Novitiate of the Poor Clares; "her delicate health made it impossible for her to continue."
p194 Scene (lamb): M. Martin, returning from a country walk, brought Thérèse a lamb one day old, "all white and frizzly"; Céline and Thérèse delighted; lamb died the same day.
p194 Quote (Thérèse, letter to sister Marie, on the lamb): "We should not become attached to anything on this earth, not even to things most innocent, for they fail us at the moment we least think. Only the eternal can fully content us." (no footnote)
p194 Evening of April 8, 1888: last family dinner at the old oak table: father, three sisters (Léonie, Céline, Thérèse), M. and Mme Guérin with Jeanne and Marie Guérin. Room details: two armchairs generally used by her parents on winter evenings; the hearth. [Laveille comment: "veritable sanctuary", mute objects expressing grief]
p195 Quote (Thérèse): "Then, just when one would wish to be forgotten, words of the deepest tenderness are on all lips, as though to make the sacrifice of separation more keenly felt." — Histoire d'une Ame, ch. vii, p. 116.
p195 Scene April 9, 1888 (Thérèse): "That morning, after a last glance at Les Buissonnets, the charming nest of my childhood, I departed for the Carmel. I assisted at holy Mass, surrounded, as on the eve, by my dear ones. At the moment of Communion, when Jesus descended into their hearts, I heard nothing but the sound of weeping. As for me, I did not shed tears; but as I walked on before the others to the door of the cloister, my heart beat so violently that I asked myself whether I was not about to die. Ah, what a moment! What agony! One must have experienced it in order to understand." — Histoire d'une Ame, ch. vii, p. 116.
p195 Scene: "I embraced all, and knelt before my father to receive his blessing. He too knelt and, weeping, he blessed me." Then the doors closed; embraced by Marie and Pauline and her new community. — Histoire d'une Ame, ch. vii, p. 116 (p196 fn).
p195 Quote (Léonie, witness): "I witnessed the departure of my little sister for the Carmel ... and I was particularly struck by her strength of soul. She alone was calm. ... I told her to reflect well before entering religion, adding that my experience had shown me that the life demands many sacrifices and should not be lightly undertaken. The reply she gave me and the expression of her countenance showed that she was ready for every sacrifice, that she would accept all with joy." — Deposition of Sœur Françoise-Thérèse (Léonie) at the Process of the Ordinary, p. 211 (checked on page image).
p196 Her cell: cold bare walls; first-hour words (Thérèse): "Now I am here for ever."
p196 Scene / conflict: Canon Delatroëtte (ecclesiastical Superior of the Carmel), with M. Martin present and the enclosure door wide open, said dryly: "Well, my Reverend Mothers, you can now chant a Te Deum. As the Bishop's delegate, I present to you this child of fifteen whose entrance you have desired. I trust she may not disappoint your hopes, but I remind you that if it should turn out otherwise, the responsibility will be yours alone." All the Sisters "chilled". — fn: [Deposition] of Mère Agnès de Jésus at the Apostolic Process, p. 162 (OCR of fn garbled; "Mère Agnès de Jésus at the Apostolic Process, p. 162" legible).
p197 Quote (novice mistress Mère Marie des Anges): "From her entrance, the Servant of God surprised the community by her bearing which was marked by a certain majesty that one would not expect in a child of fifteen." Majesty "did not exclude either simplicity or a charming readiness to render service"; "she set herself to every duty with an admirable grace." — Deposition of Mère Marie des Anges at the Apostolic Process, p. 184.
p197 Quote (Mère Agnès): "At her entrance, the Sisters, aware of her youthfulness, expected to see a child. Her presence, however, inspired them with respect; they admired her dignified yet modest bearing and her air of deep resolve."
p197 Conflict: Sœur Saint-Jean de la Croix had opposed entry of so young a postulant; later told Mère Agnès: "I thought that you would soon be sorry for all you had done to give us your little sister. I said to myself, 'They will both be disappointed.' How deceived I was. Sœur Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus is extraordinary; she is an example to us in everything." — Summarium of 1914, p. 679 (2831).
p197-198 Carmelite rule (general): Roman Breviary; word of God on lips "six and often seven hours a day" (office, reading, meditation). Motto of Carmel: "Zelo zelatus sum pro Domino Deo Exercituum."
p198 Rule: continual fast from September 14 until Easter, plus Church fasts, every Friday of year and many vigils; perpetual abstinence (from meat) except in sickness. Profound silence except two hours daily recreation. Work in cell or assigned office. Clothing: coarse serge. Bed: "a hard palliasse supported on three planks". Voluntary penances allowed. Never excuse oneself even if unjustly accused; absolute obedience.
p199 Mère Marie de Gonzague had governed for two years (since 1886) at Thérèse's entry, in a fourth term of three years, succeeding Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse, "still living but weighed down with infirmities".
p199 Mère Marie de Gonzague: born at Caen, 1834 (fn); daughter of a noble Calvados family; initiative, administrative ability, "a great heart and a charming personality", careful early education. Defects: "Very impressionable, and of somewhat distrustful character, predisposed to melancholy, she did not always possess that even-balanced judgement which makes rule beneficial and inspires subordinates with confidence."
p199-200 Although she had desired Thérèse's entry, she judged so premature a vocation needed special testing; with "a certain natural impulsiveness not always held in check, she made the child's early religious life painful."
p200 Quote (Thérèse): "From the first, my way was strewn with more thorns than roses. Bitter dryness was the daily food of my soul. Then the Saviour permitted that I should be very severely treated by our Mother, even unconsciously; I could never meet her without receiving some reproof." — Histoire d'une Ame, ch. vii, p. 117.
p200 Scene (cobweb): Thérèse left a cobweb in the cloister; Mère Marie de Gonzague said before the whole community: "It can be easily seen that our cloisters have been swept by a child of fifteen. Too bad! Go, sweep away that cobweb, and learn to be more careful in future." — HA ch. vii, p. 117.
p200 Thérèse: in rare hour of direction with the Prioress, "I was reprimanded nearly all the time"; distressed she did not understand how to correct her faults, "for example, my slowness and insufficient assiduity in the various duties." — HA ch. vii, p. 117.
p200 Thérèse: believed the Mother wanted her to work during free time usually given to prayer, so she "plied my little needle without raising my eyes; but no one ever knew of it". — HA ch. vii, p. 117.
p200 Scene (weeding): as postulant the novice mistress sent her at half-past four in the afternoon to weed in the garden; nearly always met Mère Marie de Gonzague, who said once: "After all, this child does absolutely nothing. What sort of novice would she be who must be sent out every day for a walk?" Thérèse: "And it was thus that she acted towards me in all things." — HA ch. vii, p. 117.
p201 Treatment lasted "long years", scarcely relaxed "even during her last illness" [Laveille].
p201 Testimony: R. P. Godefroy Madelaine (fn: once Prior of Premonstratensian Abbey of Mondaye, Calvados; vice-provincial of his order in France; later Abbot of Saint-Michel de Frigolet): "Mère Marie de Gonzague ... confided to me that, in order to exercise Sœur Thérèse's virtue, she had studiously sought to try her by affecting towards her a certain indifference and severity. She has, moreover, testified to me that this apparently harsh treatment had certainly been very painful to the Servant of God, but that no pain had ever caused her to deviate in the least from perfect obedience." — Deposition of R. P. Godefroy Madelaine at the Apostolic Process, p. 739.
p201 Thérèse felt at first "drawn by a sympathy so soon checked" toward the Prioress.
p201 Mère Marie des Anges, mistress of novices: "good and pious religious of superior education"; knew Thérèse from age nine visiting Pauline in the parlour. Fn: of the de Chaumontel family, old Christian Calvados family, device "Douceur et discrétion"; died 1924 aged seventy-nine.
p201-202 Quote (Mère Marie des Anges): "From her entrance, Thérèse grew in grace and wisdom before God and before the community by a very constant correspondence with Divine grace. ... Even quite lately a venerable and holy nun said to me in referring to Thérèse's novitiate: 'In truth, we had never seen anything like it.'" — Deposition at the Apostolic Process, p. 215.
p202 Fault/conflict: Mère Marie des Anges, not understanding Thérèse's grace of interior silence, gave her in early months "long exhortations" (monotonous), hindering her prayer. Thérèse at first found it hard to express her feelings to her.
p202 Thérèse told Mère Marie des Anges of the pain caused by the Prioress's severity, without complaint. Quote (Mère Marie des Anges): "I see her still, as she came one day to confide to me the grief of her heart, without, however, letting the least murmur of complaint escape her. She discerned the providence of God regarding her soul, and smiled in spite of everything." — Deposition at the Process of the Ordinary, p. 665.
p202-203 Health/humour: novice sometimes "physically weakened by the austerities"; mistress allowed her extra sleep but was "often absent-minded": forgot it for whole weeks, then made her rest fifteen consecutive days without need; the Prioress, not seeing her at morning meditation, blamed Thérèse, "who did not then know whom to obey."
p203 Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse (former Prioress, foundress) now confined to bed, "a prey to cruel suffering"; had prophetic visions but did not foresee Thérèse's destiny; "frightened by the hardihood of her ideas, and disconcerted her by certain remarks"; but consoled her in exterior trials. Fn: see biography "La Révérende Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse", p. 88.
p203 Louis Martin's illness (fn): "One day ... when M. Martin was in a condition which was most painful to the hearts of his daughters, Mère Geneviève, then an invalid, sent for Thérèse and her sisters, and calmed their fears by repeating to them a word which Our Saviour had said to her, and which was verified on the following day." — Ibid. (biography of Mère Geneviève), p. 89. [extraordinary: prophecy reported by the biography]
p203 Abbé Youf, chaplain to the Carmel of Lisieux: true piety, austere virtue; spiritual activity restricted by ill-health; "It was impossible to obtain from him direction properly so called."
p204 Abbé Youf spoke highly of her; favoured "as far as discretion permitted, her desire for daily Communion."
p204 Before entry Thérèse had no real spiritual director; after entry, "trials of darkness and aridity". She sought counsel from priests coming to the monastery: chaplain (feeble support) and, at rare intervals, the retreat preacher.
p204 Père Blino SJ judged rash her ardour for self-immolation.
p204 Scene/quote (Mère Agnès): "Several confessors and preachers of retreats ... succeeded in frightening her or paralysing her ardent desires. 'Father, I want to become a saint,' she said one day to R. P. Blino; 'I want to love the good God as much as St Teresa did.' 'What pride and presumption!' he replied. 'Confine yourself to the correction of your faults; see that you offend the good God no more, make each day some little progress, and moderate your rash desires.' 'But, Father, I do not think that these desires can be called rash, since our Saviour has said, "Be ye perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect...."' But the priest was not convinced." — Summarium of 1919, p. 248 (605).
p204-205 Père Almire Pichon SJ: born at Carrouges, near Alençon; eminent retreat preacher; fn: by 1915, four years before his death (i.e. died 1919), he had given 1,015 Retreats. Sent to Canada 1886; back in France 1887 for some months (Thérèse then 14); director of her sisters; she told him her wish for religious life; "probably the only ecclesiastic to encourage her project of entering the Carmel without delay, even if ... she must have recourse to the Sovereign Pontiff."
p205 1888: Pichon preached the Exercises (retreat) at the Lisieux Carmel; Thérèse then in aridity, asking herself "if she were worthy of love or hatred"; he restored peace.
p205 Quote (Thérèse): "R. P. Pichon was himself surprised, two months after my entrance, at the work of God in my soul; he thought my fervour quite childlike and my way very sweet. This little conference with the good Father might have brought me great consolation but for the extreme difficulty which I felt in laying open my heart." — HA ch. vii, p. 119.
p205 Scene: general confession to Pichon, who said: "In the presence of God, of the Blessed Virgin, of all the Angels and Saints, I declare that you have never committed a single mortal sin. Render thanks to the Saviour who has given you this grace gratuitously without any merit on your part." — HA ch. vii, p. 119.
p206 Thérèse: "The fear of having stained the white robe of my baptism had caused me much suffering". Pichon: "My child, may Our Saviour be always your Superior, and your Novice Master." Then his superiors sent him to Canada; she received "but one letter a year". — HA ch. vii, p. 119.
p206 Quote (Pichon deposition): "It was easy to direct that child; the Holy Spirit led her, and I do not think that I ever had, either then or later on, to warn her against illusion. ... What struck me during that retreat were the spiritual trials through which God made her pass. I had then a very strong impression that the good God willed to make her a great saint." — Summarium of 1919 (362 and 364).
p206 Later another retreat priest understood her perfectly, but help was passing (unnamed here).
p206-207 Quote (Céline / Sœur Geneviève de la Sainte-Face, later her novice): "she studied the Sacred Scriptures in order to know the character of the good God. The different senses of Scripture distressed her. 'If I had been a priest,' she said, 'I would have diligently studied Greek and Hebrew, in order to know the Divine thought as God deigned to express it in our human language.'" — Testimony of Sœur Geneviève de la Sainte-Face at the Apostolic Process, p. 330.
p207 Quote (Sœur Marie de la Trinité, novice under Thérèse): "she interpreted the books of Holy Scripture with unheard-of facility. One would have said that these Divine books had no meaning hidden from her". — Testimony at the Apostolic Process, p. 702.
p207 Quote (Thérèse): "How happy I was when, officiating at the Divine Office, I said the prayers out loud in the centre of the choir. I reflected then that the priest recited the same prayers at Mass, and that, like him, I had the right to pray aloud before the Blessed Sacrament, and to read the Gospel when I was First Chantress. I can say truly that the Divine Office has been, at the same time, my joy and my martyrdom, for I had a great desire to recite it without fault, and yet, in spite of all my application, I made mistakes." — Unpublished notes.
p207 Studied St Teresa and St John of the Cross (esp. the latter); loved his lyrics.
p208 Knew the Imitation of Christ by heart. Constant companion: Père Surin, "The Foundations of the Spiritual Life, drawn from the Imitation of Christ". Also Mgr de Ségur, "Piety and the Interior Life".
p208 Object: after entry she found the four Gospels in one volume small enough to carry near her heart; did so until death. During temptations against faith "she wrote the entire Credo with her blood in this book, as counselled by one of her confessors."
p209 Quote (Thérèse to the Mother Prioress): "It is the Gospels, which, above all, occupy my mind during mental prayer. From them I draw everything necessary for my poor little soul. I ever discover there new lights, hidden and mysterious meanings." — HA ch. viii, p. 146.
p209 Quote (Thérèse): "When I read certain treatises where many obstacles to perfection are shown, my poor mind grows tired very quickly; I close the learned book which wearies my head and dries up my heart, and I take instead the Holy Scripture. ... perfection seems easy to me; I realize that it is sufficient to recognize one's nothingness, and to abandon oneself as a child in the arms of God." — Letter published at end of HA, p. 370.
p209 [Laveille comment] "Little Way" has purely evangelical origin (divine sonship).
p210 Relations with her sisters in Carmel: never sought the company of Sœur Agnès de Jésus (Pauline) or Sœur Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie); at recreation sat beside whoever came first, or someone who seemed forlorn; if a sister was sick, did not visit until other nuns had gone first.
p210-211 Scene (last illness): asked, if one of her sisters were ill, "Would you have gone to the infirmary during recreation?" Thérèse: "No, I would have gone straight to recreation without making any inquiry; but I would have done it quite simply, so that no one would notice the sacrifice I was making." — Deposition of Mère Agnès de Jésus at the Process of the Ordinary, p. 570. Fn: R. P. Auriault SJ, witness on her doctrine at the Process of Beatification, called these acts "magnificent".
p211 Parlour: except for Léonie and Céline (to console their loneliness), she let visitors talk, scarcely spoke, and was first to leave at the set time.
p211-212 Letter to Céline at Les Buissonnets, May 8, 1888: "You are right, life is often oppressive and sad; it is hard to begin the day's work, especially when Jesus hides Himself from our love. ... Céline, have no fear; He is there, quite near. He is looking at us. It is He who asks of us this suffering, these tears.... Let us rise above passing things, let us hold ourselves aloof from earth; high up the air is so pure! Jesus may hide Himself, but one divines His presence...." — Letter of May 8, 1888, end of HA, p. 315.
p212 Mère Marie de Gonzague "declared freely" Thérèse was a treasure for the Carmel, "the best of the best; an angel." (no fn)
p212 Clothing (taking of the Habit): January 10, 1889 — not at the normal time (after six months as postulant). Fn: delay "attributed to the opposition of M. Delatroëtte, who did not cease to put forward the extreme youthfulness of the postulant".
p212-213 Pre-clothing retreat in aridity. Letter to Sœur Agnès de Jésus: "...In my soul's intercourse with Jesus there is nothing, nothing but dryness and sleep! Since my Well-beloved wills to remain asleep, I will not prevent Him ... I am happy, yes, truly happy to suffer... He well knows that if He sent me even one ray of earthly happiness, I would become attached to it with all the energy, all the power of my heart's affection, and He refuses me this ray. ... And even when I shall have nothing to give Him, as this evening, I will offer Him that nothing...." — Letter published at end of HA, p. 342.
p213 Louis Martin: first attack of paralysis shortly before Thérèse left for Carmel (i.e. before April 1888); recovered almost completely.
p213 Quote (Louis Martin, letter to a friend, day after entry): "Thérèse, my little Queen, entered Carmel yesterday. God alone can claim such a sacrifice, but He helps me so powerfully that, in the midst of my tears, my heart abounds with joy." (no fn)
p213-214 Scene (Louis Martin's self-offering): he came to the Carmel and said to his three daughters: "My children, I have just returned from Alençon, where I received, in the church of Notre-Dame, such wonderful graces and consolation that I made this prayer: 'My God, it is too much; yes, I am too happy. It is not possible to get to heaven in this manner; I want to suffer something for Thee ...' And I offered myself ..." He did not finish; daughters understood he offered himself as victim. (no fn)
p214 "Shortly afterwards, a second attack of paralysis seemed to indicate that the holocaust had been accepted." (date not given; before clothing Jan 1889)
p214 Clothing day Jan 10, 1889: Thérèse crossed the cloister threshold for last time in bridal robes. Clothing (dress): at M. Martin's wish, a dress of white velvet, bordered with swansdown and enriched with point d'Alençon lace; long fair hair in natural curls on shoulders; white lilies only ornament.
p214 Quote (Thérèse): "Papa was waiting for me at the enclosure door. Advancing towards me, while his eyes filled with tears, he pressed me to his heart, saying: 'Ah, here is my little Queen.' Then he gave me his arm, and we made our solemn entrance into the chapel. This day was his triumph, his last feast on earth." — HA ch. vii, p. 124.
p214-215 Céline confided to Louis she too would enter Carmel; Louis: "Let us go together before the Blessed Sacrament to thank the Saviour for the graces that He showers on our family, and the honour He has done me in choosing spouses from my house. Yes, the good God greatly honours me in claiming my children. If I possessed anything better, I would hasten to offer it to Him." Thérèse: "This better thing was himself. ..." — HA ch. vii, p. 124.
p215 Snow "little miracle": Thérèse loved snow ("The first time her infant eyes beheld the earth, snow was its raiment"); weather extremely mild that day; after the church ceremony, nuns led her in procession to choir. Thérèse: "The moment I entered the cloister, my eyes instinctively turned to my dear little Jesus, who was smiling on me amidst flowers and lights. Then turning towards the quadrangle, I saw it all covered with snow. What tenderness on the part of Jesus! ..." — HA ch. vii, p. 124. Fn: "my dear little Jesus" = statue of the Child Jesus in the cloister, of which Thérèse had charge. Community and others spoke of the "little miracle". [extraordinary; reported by Thérèse and community]
p216 Louis Martin: second attack of paralysis "had given cause to fear some brain trouble"; third attack came "a month after her reception" (i.e. about Feb 1889) and "realized their gravest fears".
p216 Quote (Thérèse to Mère Marie des Anges, at earlier threats): "I suffer much, but I can still suffer more." After the third attack Thérèse wrote: "Ah, I did not say then that I could suffer more. Words could not express our agony; I shall not try to describe it. ..." (no fn)
p216 [Laveille comment] "That keen intellect darkened, that tender affection wrecked, perhaps irremediably".
p216 KEY: "On February 12, 1889, M. Martin left Lisieux to receive special treatment in a Home chosen by his family. He remained there three years." NOTE: Laveille does not name the Bon-Sauveur asylum or Caen here; euphemism "a Home". Léonie and Céline now "the two young girls now alone in the world".
p216 Family quoted Victor Hugo, "A Villequier" (lines on bringing God the fragments of a broken heart) — per Laveille "Thérèse and her sisters repeated" them.
p217 Quote (Thérèse): "The three years of my father's martyrdom seem to me the dearest and most fruitful of our life; I would not exchange them for the most sublime ecstasies." And, of talks with Céline in the parlour: "As formerly at Les Buissonnets, we lifted not alone our eyes but also our hearts beyond space and time, and, in order quickly to enjoy eternal happiness, we chose suffering and contempt here below." — HA ch. vii, p. 126.
p217 Letter to Céline, January 1889: "We have now nothing further to hope for on this earth; the fresh mornings are gone, naught is left to us but suffering. Oh, what an enviable lot! The Seraphim in Heaven are jealous of our happiness." ("fresh mornings" = St John of the Cross). — Letter of January, 1889.
p217 Letter to Céline, February 28, 1889: "Céline, far from making any complaint to Our Saviour of the cross He has sent us, I cannot comprehend the infinite love that has urged Him to deal thus with us. Our father must be greatly loved by God, since he has so much to suffer. What a delight to share in his humiliation!" — Letter of February 28, 1889.
p218 Quote (Thérèse): "I found no consolation, either from heaven or from earth; and, nevertheless, amidst these waters of tribulation, for which I had so longed and prayed, I was the happiest of creatures." — HA ch. vii, p. 127.
p218 Postulants under heavy trial were sent to Sœur Thérèse for consolation; even before profession she was charged with encouraging one of her companions (sign of Mère Marie de Gonzague's secret esteem).
p218 Profession delayed: at end of her year's novitiate (Jan 1890) "she was not called to make her vows; the still inflexible ecclesiastical superior [Delatroëtte] opposed it."
p218 Prayer (Thérèse): "I no longer ask Thee to let me make my profession; I shall wait as long as may please Thee; but I must not allow my union with Thee to be delayed by any fault of mine, so will devote all my care to preparing for myself a robe enriched with diamonds and every precious stone. When Thou dost find it rich enough, I am sure that nothing will prevent Thee from taking me as Thy spouse." — HA ch. vii, p. 127 (Ibid.).
p219 Novitiate companion: a young lay-sister "of rather difficult character"; Thérèse chose her company by preference and gained influence for good. (unnamed)
p219 Sœur Saint-Pierre: old lay-sister, made "very exacting" by infirmity; had to be led each evening to the refectory. Thérèse hesitated to volunteer, then did, and "succeeded, though not easily, in getting her services accepted." Signal: at end of prayer the old sister shook her hour-glass = "Let us go now."
p219 Scene (Thérèse): "Summoning all my courage, I rose, and quite a ceremony commenced. Her seat had to be removed and carried in a certain way, and above all there was to be no hurry. Then we set out. One had to follow the good sister, supporting her by her girdle. ... 'You go too fast, I shall break my bones,' she would say; and if I then tried to lead her more slowly, 'Now you are not following me; I do not feel your hand; you are loosening your hold; I shall fall.... Ah, I was right in saying that you were too young to take care of me.'" In refectory: helped into place, sleeves turned up "always in a certain way"; Thérèse also cut her bread for her unasked; "after all my little services I gave her my sweetest smile". — HA ch. x, p. 193. Afterward the invalid "never ceased to extol Thérèse's charity."
p220 As postulant she liked having "things that were nice" and everything at hand for work (love of beauty and order). Quote: "Jesus suffered this patiently, for He does not wish to disclose all to the soul at once; He ordinarily gives His light little by little."
p220-221 Scene (lamp): one evening after Compline, Great Silence begun, her little lamp missing from its shelf (a Sister took it by mistake); she spent an hour in darkness though she had planned much work. "And, amid the exterior darkness, my soul was filled with Divine light."
p221 Object: a large, much-damaged jug was substituted (probably by the Prioress's order) for her own; she accepted it joyfully; came to prefer "the ugliest and least serviceable utensils".
p221 Rule on never excusing oneself — fn: "Regulations or Points of Observance taken from those which the first Spanish Nuns had introduced into France", p. 35.
p221 Scene (vase): a little vase left behind a window was broken; novice-mistress blamed Thérèse for not putting it in place and reproached her for failing in order; Thérèse, not responsible, said nothing, "kissed the ground, and promised to be more careful in future."
p222 Profession delayed "eight long months added to the usual term of the novitiate".

## Ch. VIII: Profession — Céline's entrance (PDF pp. 223–)
p223 Profession date: September 8, 1890. Profession retreat "a retreat of great aridity" (Thérèse).
p223-224 Letter to Mère Agnès de Jésus days before profession (tunnel image): "Before setting out, my Betrothed asked me through what country I wished to journey ... I replied that I had but one desire—to reach the summit of the mountain of Love. ... p299 Letter to Roulland March 19, 1897: "I do not desire that you should ask God to deliver me from the flames of purgatory. St Teresa said ...: 'What matters it to me to remain in purgatory till the end of the world, if, by my prayers I save a single soul....' ... I want to save them even after my death. ... 'My God, permit my sister still to make Thee loved.'" — Summarium of 1919, p. 648, § 1920.
p299 Letter to Roulland July 14, 1897: "I shall be of much more assistance to you in heaven than on earth.... I quite count on not remaining inactive up there. My desire is to labour still for the Church and souls. I ask this of the good God, and I am certain He will hear me." — Letter of July 14, 1897.
p300 Last letter to Roulland, August 14, 1897: "On the eve of appearing before God, I understand more than ever that there is but one thing necessary, to work for Him alone ... Jesus wants to possess your heart completely; for that, you must suffer a great deal. ... I do not die, I enter into life ...; everything that here below I cannot tell you, I will make you fully understand from Heaven above." — Letter of August 14, 1897.
p300 Fn: Père Roulland spent twelve years in China, then recalled to the Foreign Missions' Seminary as Procurator of the Society; "lately" sent with companions to Dormans (Marne) to serve the "Souvenir" chapel built in memory of the battle of the Marne.
p300 Abbé Bellière: young cleric of the White Fathers (Society of Missionaries of Africa); still at the Seminary, not yet subdeacon, when Mère Agnès de Jésus, then Prioress, assigned Thérèse as his spiritual helper; aged twenty-one; zeal for Africa but grief at leaving family. Laveille: exchange over "the two last years of her life".
p301 Correspondence began October 1896, when Bellière, tortured by approaching departure, asked prayers for his "mother". Fn: Abbé Bellière lost his mother a few days after his birth; called his aunt, Mme Barthélemy, "mother" for her care.
p301 Letter to Bellière: "Monsieur l'Abbé, your part is in truth noble, since the Saviour has chosen it for you ... A saint has said: 'The greatest honour that God can confer on a soul does not consist in giving it a great deal but in asking from it a great deal.' ... martyrdom of heart is no less fruitful than the shedding of blood, and from now onward, that martyrdom is yours." — Letter cited in Appendix to HA, p. 364.
p302 Prayer she asked Bellière to say daily: "Merciful Father, in the name of Thy sweet Jesus, of the Holy Virgin and all the saints, I ask Thee to inflame my sister with Thy spirit of Love, and to give her the grace to make Thee greatly loved." — Appendix to HA, p. 365. She called him "her brother".
p302-303 Letter to Bellière: "You have promised to pray for me all your life. Undoubtedly it will be longer than mine, and you cannot sing like me: 'My exile will be short; that hope is mine.' ... You must think me strange, Monsieur l'Abbé. Perhaps you regret having a sister who, it seems, wishes to enjoy eternal repose while leaving you to work alone. ... if in heaven I could work no more for His glory, I should prefer exile rather than the Fatherland. ... Then there will no longer be grille or enclosure, and my soul can fly to you in your far-off missions. Our rôles shall remain the same. For you the weapons of an apostle, for me prayer and love." — Partly unpublished letter, February 24, 1897.
p303 She had never seen Bellière. Letter: "I have had the same thought as your director. You cannot be a saint by halves. You must be one entirely or not at all. ... Do not imagine that you will frighten me by speaking of your 'best years wasted.' ... you have left all to follow Him, and in the golden days of life, at eighteen years." — Partly unpublished letter, June 21, 1897.
p303 "Ah, my brother, since it has been granted me too, to understand the love of the Heart of Jesus, I avow that it has banished from my heart all fear. ... When we cast our faults with filial confidence into the devouring furnace of divine love, how should they not be consumed for ever?" — Letter, June 21, 1897. Soon after, Bellière asked her to obtain for him the grace of martyrdom.
p304 Warning to Mère Agnès before death: "Later on a great number of young priests, learning that I was given as spiritual sister to two missionaries, will ask the same favour at this Carmel. This may become a danger. Only by prayer and sacrifice can we be useful to the Church. Correspondence should be rare, and should not be permitted at all to those who would become preoccupied by it, believing that they would accomplish marvels, while in reality they only harm their own souls, and perhaps fall into the subtle snares of the demon. What I say to you, Mother, is very important; do not forget it when I am gone." — Summarium of 1919, p. 562, § 1553.
p305 Pope Pius XI, discourse on the Decree "De Tuto", March 19, 1925: "The spirit of our time is ... that of movement, of continuous and hurried action. ... Now, consider the Venerable Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus, a true flower of love come from heaven to earth to astonish both earth and heaven. ..."

## Ch. XI: Suffering; poverty, chastity, obedience; love of the Cross (PDF pp. 306–)
p306-307 [Laveille comment] long quotation from Mgr Gay, "Life and the Christian Virtues", t. II, p. 312, on suffering and illusions of prosperity.
p307 Letter to Sœur Agnès de Jésus, 1887 (age fourteen): "It is indeed true that the drop of gall should be mixed with every cup we drink. But I find that thorns help greatly in detaching us from earth; they make us look upwards, beyond this earth."
p307 "Oh, no, never do our sufferings make Him happy; but this suffering is necessary for us; He then sends us sorrow while, as it were, turning away His Face." — l'Esprit de Ste Thérèse, p. 107. "It costs Him much to make us drink at the source of tears ... temptation must purify us as gold in the furnace." — Ibid., p. 108.
p307 Before entry: "little crosses of her life at school, her troubles of conscience, too, which were often severe"; at Carmel "violent temptations against faith".
p308 "Trial had matured and fortified my soul to such a degree that nothing here below could any longer distress it." — HA ch. ii, p. 21.
p308 Louis Martin's illness: letter to Céline, July 14, 1889: "How has Jesus acted to detach our souls from everything created? Ah, He has stricken a heavy blow, but it is through love ... God is admirable, but He is above all lovable." — Letter of July 14, 1889.
p309 Privations "not imposed by the rule itself, and which could have been spared her by more attentive charity on the part of others" (neglect in community). Mère Agnès distressed at this negligence; Thérèse: "Do not torment yourself on my account, I beg of you; I am already too well cared for."
p309 Cold: intensely sensitive; admitted in last illness she had suffered at Carmel from cold "even to death".
p309-310 One of her sisters: "When the weather was very severe, the Servant of God, having been benumbed with the cold during the whole day, would go to the community-room for a few moments after Matins to warm herself. But in order to reach her cell she had to walk about fifty yards in the cloisters, in open air; then go up the stairs and along an icy corridor ... when she lay down on her paillasse wrapped in two poor blankets, her sleep was broken and unrestful. She often spent the entire night shivering with cold and unable to sleep. If she had told this to her novice-mistress during her first years at the Carmel, she would have obtained relief immediately; but she preferred to accept this severe mortification uncomplainingly, never mentioning it until she lay on her deathbed." — HA ch. xii, p. 233. (Only the community-room was heated.)
p310 Badly fitting habit; asked if vexed: "Not the least. No more than if it were that of an inhabitant of China two thousand leagues away." [humour]
p310 Objects: all her religious life she used a little lamp whose wick could only be raised with a pin. On Céline's entry she gave Céline her own good inkstand and holy water font, replacing them with old disused ones she fetched from the garret. Used pens long after they were worn out. Autobiography manuscript written in "two series of miserable exercise-books". Poems scribbled on old envelopes or waste paper; gave them away, kept no copy. — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 402, § 1018.
p311 Last illness: asked not to be given costly remedies; refrained from asking for iced water or grapes offered her. Slept on hard paillasse in her cell (far from community) as long as allowed, glad not to disturb others with "her frequent attacks of coughing" as she would in the infirmary.
p311 Céline (then assistant infirmarian): "After the cauterizing needle had been applied—one day I counted five hundred of these applications—she went at night to lie on her paillasse. Not having permission to give her a mattress, I had no other resource but to fold a large blanket in four and place it under her sheet." — Summarium of 1919, p. 394, § 1001. [medical treatment: pointes de feu / cauterization]
p311 Gathered used matches and scraps of paper; "time does not belong to us." Sisters at recreation sometimes gave out her thoughts as their own; she was silent: "the thought did not belong to herself but to the Holy Ghost."
p312 Chastity: Thérèse: "I have always been ill at ease in my body; even when still very young, I felt embarrassed by it." One could confide any temptation to her without troubling her. — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 398, § 1007. Guardians: Blessed Virgin and St Joseph; prayer at Our Lady of Victories before Italy trip.
p312 Scene (Boulogne, Italy pilgrimage Nov 1887): on alighting from the train pilgrims were surrounded by a crowd of boisterous students; one stared at Thérèse "with effrontery", then "seized hold of her, and in a moment lifted her across the track"; she recommended herself to the Virgin and "turned on him such a look that he went away dumbfounded with confusion." — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 632, §§ 1869, 1870.
p313 Scene (pilgrimage): a young man pilgrim "of perfect manners and education" showed her "rather too marked attentions"; she answered with "the coldest reserve". Alone with Céline: "Oh, it is indeed time that Jesus took me away from the poisoned breath of the world. I feel that my heart would easily let itself be taken captive by affection, and where others have perished, I would perish too, for every creature is weak, myself in particular." Autobiography: "I can claim no merit in not surrendering to the love of creatures since I was preserved only by the great mercy of God." — Summarium of 1919, p. 400, § 1013.
p313 She testified she was free of "the slightest interior suggestion against chastity". Letter to a young girl in the world: "Pure hearts are often surrounded by thorns.... But lilies in the midst of thorns are beloved by Jesus. Happy those who have been found worthy to suffer temptation."
p314 Obedience — a contemporary: "Never have I seen her in the slightest degree unfaithful to the rule. ... She left everything at the first sound of the bell, even if in the middle of a conversation however interesting. If engaged in sewing, she immediately laid her needle aside, not even finishing the stitch she had commenced." — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 480, § 1297.
p314 Scene: her sister Marie, while the bell rang, wanted to finish writing a thought Thérèse gave her; Thérèse: "It would be far better to lose that and act with regularity. If we but knew all that regularity means."
p314 Sœur Marie des Anges: "One day in winter when, profiting by the authorization of custom, she had taken off thick woollen stockings, which were wet through, to let them dry at the fire during recreation, there came a message that the bell was ringing in the sacristy, of which she then had charge. Simply putting on her hempen sandals, which we call alpargates, she went through two cloisters without a thought of the risk she ran in thus exposing herself to the icy-cold blast." — Ibid., p. 678, § 2042.
p315 HA: "How happy are simple religious. The will of their superiors being their only guide, they are sure of never deceiving themselves...."
p315 Sœur Marie du Sacré-Cœur: after a preached retreat Thérèse accused herself of having looked at an illustrated sheet of a catalogue of novelties. Marie said looking at pictures was not forbidden; Thérèse: "That is true; but the Father has told me that it is more perfect to deprive oneself even of this. Yet that sight of the world's vanities did but raise my soul to God. Now, however, when I find any such prints, I no longer look at them."
p315-316 Marie du Sacré-Cœur: Prioress permitted Mère Agnès to speak to Thérèse occasionally; Thérèse, lacking permission to reveal her own thoughts, only listened to "her little mother" and gave none of her own; would not ask permission: "we must not seek permissions which lessen the sacrifices of the religious life, for then it would become a merely natural existence without merit." — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 698, § 2043.
p316 KEY conflict: after Père Alexis's first interview (1891 retreat), "by a regrettable abuse of authority, Mère Marie de Gonzague forbade her ... to go back again, as others did, for the purpose of speaking more at leisure on the affairs of her soul." Thérèse suffered in silence.
p316 She asked to stay in the novitiate beyond the normal term chiefly to practise obedience — under Mère Marie de Gonzague as both Prioress and novice-mistress, "the superior who had treated her with so little consideration".
p316 Mère Marie de Gonzague made little regulations of her own, forgot to enforce them "through lapse of memory"; Thérèse kept observing them until expressly revoked.
p316-317 Scene (daily sickness report): Thérèse: "Our mistress had commanded me, when I was a postulant, to tell her whenever I felt sick. This happened every day, and the command became for me a real torture. When the attack of pain came on, I would have preferred a hundred strokes of the lash to the obligation of making it known; nevertheless, I told it each time through obedience. Sœur Marie des Anges, who no longer remembered the order she had given, would say to me on these occasions: 'My poor child, you will never have health sufficient to follow the rule; it is too severe for you.' And she went for remedies for me to Mère Marie de Gonzague, who, astonished and dissatisfied on hearing of my daily reports, replied sharply: 'This child is continually complaining. ... If she cannot bear her pains and aches, her place is not amongst us.' Notwithstanding all, I continued for a long time through pure obedience to confess this trouble at the risk of being sent away, until at last, the good God ... permitted that I should be relieved of the obligation". — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 518, § 1393. [health: daily attacks of pain as postulant; nature not stated]
p317 Histoire d'une Ame written only under formal command of her Prioress. To a young nun who planned to write her vocation story: "Beware of doing anything of the sort. You cannot write it without permission, and I advise you not to seek that. As for me, I would not have wished to write anything about my life had I not received an express command, a command that I would not have sought. It is more humble to write nothing about self. ..." — Ibid., p. 724, § 1393 (as printed).
p318 Last days, burning with fever: her sister Marie wanted to remove the sheet over her feet to cool her; Thérèse: "I would indeed like it, but it is perhaps not permitted. Ask our Mother." Kept the heavy woollen coverlet, though its weight oppressed her, because Mère Marie de Gonzague once told the strong, healthy nuns it was laudable to bear it in summer heat. — cf. Summarium of 1919, p. 588, § 1677.
p318 Fn: "This is a cause of trouble to souls. Many imagine that to please God they must give themselves over absolutely to exaggerated practices of mortification. Then the demon deceives them, and leads them into certain dangerous illusions such as preoccupation about self, pride, etc." (Thérèse, on lives of saints stressing extraordinary penances)
p319 Mgr Gay: "If a man has not suffered for Jesus and with Jesus, he cannot be sure that he loves Jesus." — De la vie et des vertus chrétiennes, t. ii, p. 327.
p319 Scene (last year of life, garden at recreation, with Mère Agnès): "I have been struck by a passage in the life of Blessed Henry Suso concerning bodily penances. He had practised frightful penances, which had ruined his health, when an angel appeared and told him to desist, adding: 'You have till now fought only like an ordinary soldier; I am going to make you a knight.' ... Well, Mother, the good God has not willed that I should fight like a common soldier; I received at once a knight's armour, and I set out to war against myself in the spiritual domain by renunciation and little hidden sacrifices. I have found peace and humility in this obscure combat where nature has no place."

## Ch. XII: Idea, advantages and necessity of the "Little Way" (PDF pp. 322–)
p322 [Laveille comment] plan of chapter; praise of Benedict XV for her doctrine.
p323 Only in 1895 did she begin to reveal the secret of the Little Way (HA ch. IX, addressed to her Mother Prioress).
p323 Quote (Thérèse, lift image): "You know, Mother, that I have always longed to be a Saint. But, alas! I have always felt, when comparing myself with the Saints, that there exists between them and me the same difference as we see in nature between a mountain with its summit hidden in the clouds and the grain of sand trodden under the feet of passers-by. ... We live in a century of inventions. The trouble of walking upstairs exists no longer; in the houses of the rich a lift replaces the stairs. I, too, would like to find a lift to raise me to Jesus, for I am too little to ascend the steep steps of perfection." Scripture found: "Whosoever is a little one, let him come to me" (Prov. ix 4); "As one whom the mother caresseth, so will I comfort you; you shall be carried at the breasts, and upon the knees they shall caress you" (Isaias lxvi 13). "Thine arms, O Jesus, form the lift which will raise me to heaven. For this I have no need of becoming greater; on the contrary, I must remain little and become even smaller."
p324 End of lift passage: "O my God, Thou hast surpassed my hopes, and I will sing Thy mercies: 'Thou hast taught me, O God, from my youth ...'" — HA ch. ix, pp. 153-154. Laveille adds Matt. xviii 3 ("unless you be converted, and become as little children ...").
p324-325 [Laveille comment] long analogy: father's and mother's love grows with the child's weakness; God as father/mother (Isaias xlix 15).
p326 Fn, Thérèse's definition: "To be little means not to attribute to self the virtues that one practises, believing oneself capable of anything; it means recognizing that the good God places this treasure of virtue in the hand of His little child to be used by him when he has need of it; but always it is God's treasure. In fine, it means not being discouraged about our faults, for children fall often, but are too small to do themselves much harm." — cited in Summarium of 1919, p. 260.
p326 "In order to be raised to heaven in the arms of Jesus, I need not become greater; I must, on the contrary, remain little and even become smaller." — HA ch. ix, p. 154.
p326-327 "What pleases Jesus in my little soul is to see me love my littleness and poverty, to see the unquestioning hope I have in His mercy." — Sixth letter to Sœur Marie du Sacré-Cœur. "It is Jesus who has accomplished everything in me; I have done nothing but remain little and weak." — Spirit of St Thérèse. HA p. 81: "Because I was little and weak, Jesus gently stooped down to me, and instructed me in the secrets of His love".
p327 "Even amongst the poor the little child is given what is necessary. But when it has grown up, the father is no longer inclined to continue his help, and says: 'Work now; you are able to support yourself.' Well then, it is so that I may never hear such words that I have not wished to grow up, feeling incapable of gaining for myself life eternal ... I have always therefore remained little, occupying myself solely in gathering flowers of love and sacrifice and offering them to the good God for His pleasure." — Conseils et Souvenirs.
p328 Poem "My Song of To-day": "What matters it, my Lord, if the future sombre be? To pray Thee for the morrow—ah no, not thus my way; Preserve my heart unstained, protect me lovingly Just for to-day."
p328 "Now I have received the grace of being detached from the things of heart and spirit as well as from the goods of earth." — HA ch. x.
p329 "To love Jesus, to be His victim of love, the more weak and miserable we are, the better disposed are we for the operations of this consuming and transforming love.... The sole desire of being a victim suffices; we must, however, be always willing to remain poor and weak. Herein lies the difficulty, for where are the truly poor in spirit to be found? ... let us love our littleness, let us love to feel nothing. ... He will transform us into flames of love." — Sixth letter to Sœur Marie du Sacré-Cœur.
p329 With St John of the Cross: "From the good God we obtain all that we hope for." — HA ch. xii, p. 240. To those stressing God's justice: "It is because He is just that God is also compassionate and full of tenderness, slow to punish and abounding in mercy, for He knows our frailty; He remembers that we are but dust." — Sixth letter to the missionaries.
p330 "the fault thus cast with filial confidence into the furnace of love is immediately and wholly consumed." — Fifth letter to the missionaries.
p330 Late in life, in distress: "I turn to God and to all the saints, and I thank them in spite of everything. I believe they wish to see how far my confidence will go." — HA ch. xii, p. 236.
p330 "The good God has always come to my assistance; He has led me by the hand from my tenderest years; I count upon Him ...." — Ibid., p. 237.
p330 God "would have but to take a soul in His arms and raise it up to Him in order to clothe it with His infinite merits and make it holy." — HA ch. iv, p. 55. "If weak and imperfect souls like mine felt what I feel, not one of them would despair of reaching the summit of the mountain of love." — HA ch. xi, p. 209.
