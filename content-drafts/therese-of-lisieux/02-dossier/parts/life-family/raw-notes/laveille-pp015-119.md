# Laveille notes (PDF page = book page + 20)
p15 Foreword: died 30 Sep 1897; a companion wondered what could be said in death notice; coffin taken to cemetery "accompanied by a few relatives". Canonization 17 May 1925: >4,000 priests, 60,000 faithful; almost a million spectators in Rome (Laveille's claim). "hurricane of glory" = Pius XI to Bayeux pilgrims.
p16 Laveille: Histoire omitted traits "probably through humility"; process acts "form two large volumes".
p17 Laveille himself was a member of the 1887 Rome pilgrimage. Prioress (her sister) charged him; family "marks of her protection".
p18 Every page revised by theologians and "the three Carmelite sisters"; 3rd French ed.: 30,000 copies sold in a few months; appendix documents on ancestors.
p21 Paternal ancestors from Athis-de-l'Orne (Domfront district); farmers named Martin since 16th c.; record from 2 Apr 1692 baptism of John (Jean) Martin. Pierre-François Martin bapt. Athis 16 Apr 1777 (grandfather). Source note: Abbé Madeline booklet.
p22 Parents lived near church; then "la Quentinière". P-F went to Alençon for first military training; Empire campaigns; 1823 captain 19th Light Infantry at Bordeaux. Old house rue Servandoni; 22 Aug [1823] wife Marie-Anne-Fanny Boureau bore Louis-Joseph-Aloys-Stanislas, third of five. Father absent (Spain expedition, Duke of Angoulême, for Ferdinand VII). Private baptism; solemn ceremony 28 Oct 1823, St Eulalie, Abbé Martegoute (prison chaplain). Abp d'Aviau du Bois de Sanzay attested 14 Jan 1824. Footnote: siblings: Pierre (navy, lost in shipwreck young); Marie elder sister married d. aged 26 in 1846; Françoise/Fanny d. 1853 aged 27 after marriage; Sophie godchild and favourite, d. aged 9.
p23 Archbishop "probably" said "Rejoice, for this child is one of the predestined!" (legend-ish, "probably"). Register copy: "born the twenty-third of the month of August last" -> CONFLICT with 22 Aug in text. No. 3 rue Servandoni; godfather Léonce de Lamothe, godmother Ernestine Beyssac. Cross of St Louis at 47 during Spanish campaign; conferred 20 Aug 1824 by Charles X.
p24 Retired to Alençon (education). Discharge at Strasbourg 1830. Cousins at Rennes watchmakers; Louis learns there. Mother's letter 25 Aug 1842: "you are the dream of my nights"; "Remain always humble, dear son" (unpublished letter, family archives).
p25 Strasbourg: friend of family clockmaker; Louis studied cathedral clock mechanism. Father's feast letter (bouquet from Sophie; "clink glasses in sprinkling the bouquet").
p26 Sept 1843 Louis (about 20) climbs to Great St Bernard monastery, partly on foot, partly stage-coach.
p27 Augustinian canons; Prior asks if Latin finished; no -> "Return to Normandy... when you have completed your humanities". Studied Latin under Curé of St Leonard, Alençon; illness stopped him.
p28 Paris (probably completed apprenticeship); back to Alençon, lived with parents rue du Pont-Neuf; to 35th year unmarried though mother wished. Zélie Guérin, b. Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon (Orne), asked to enter Hôtel-Dieu of Alençon (Sisters of St Vincent de Paul). Forebears hid clergy in Revolution; her father's uncle a priest hidden. Isidore Guérin (grandfather) b. Saint-Martin-l'Aiguillon 6 Jul 1789; army 6 Jun 1809; Wagram; Oudinot division; Vittoria to Toulouse; foot gendarmerie 1 Oct 1816, mounted 1 Jun 1823; Cie de la Vendée; from 23 Feb 1827 Cie de l'Orne at Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon; left 10 Sep 1844; d. Alençon 3 Sep 1868.
p29 Children: Marie-Louise (Visitation nun, died Le Mans), Zélie, Isidore (medical attraction). Zélie pupil of Dames de l'Adoration (Perpetual Adoration) Alençon; Superioress of Hôtel-Dieu said God had other designs. Father bought 1843 house No. 42 rue Saint-Blaise. 8 Dec 1851 interior voice: "Have Alençon point lace made".
p30 Point d'Alençon: hand-spun linen thread; pieces 20–30 cm joined; industry started under Colbert c.1664 (30 Venetian workers). Zélie specialised in assembling pieces; workers at home; she took orders, designs, supervision; 500 francs a metre not unusual. Louis's family in rue du Pont-Neuf, parish St-Pierre-de-Montsort.
p31 Met young man on bridge of Saint-Léonard; interior voice "This is he whom I have prepared for thee". Married 13 Jul 1858, Notre-Dame Alençon. Louis proposed continence on wedding evening; after a year (per family records) renounced. Established rue du Pont-Neuf. Watchmaking + jewellery + lace.
p32 Louis lifted drunk man in street, carried his tool box, led him home; railway station epileptic—took hat round for money. Mass daily 5.30 am together; fasts kept.
p33 Closed shop on Sundays despite loss; friend: "Leave the side-door open"; Louis: "I prefer to draw down on my house the blessing of God." Captain Martin's Our Father moved others to tears. Zélie reading life of Mme Acarie: "All her daughters Carmelites! Is it possible that a mother may have so great an honour?" (Apostolic Process, Mère Agnès). Louis monthly nocturnal adoration.
p34 Louis fished the Sarthe; eel and trout sent to Poor Clares of Alençon. Zélie nursed servant with articular rheumatism night and day; appeared before magistrate after rescuing a child from two old women exploiting her. Nine children; all named Marie: Marie-Louise, Marie-Pauline, Marie-Léonie, Marie-Hélène. Prayed to St Joseph for "a little missionary" (letter of Zélie to Marie and Pauline 17 Jan 1875).
p35 Marie-Joseph-Louis died 5 months after birth; Marie-Joseph-Jean-Baptiste died 8 months later [at 8 months]. Ceased supplications for a missionary. Marie and Pauline boarders at Visitation, Le Mans (aunt). Zélie expanded lace business.
p36 Zélie: "I seek no other recreation than to sit at my window putting together the pieces of my Point d'Alençon" (letter to sister-in-law 28 Sep 1872). Isidore as young medical student drawn to "dissipations of Paris"; Zélie letter (1 Jan 1863): husband "has given me an account of some of his own experiences... If you but knew through what trials he has passed!"; asks him to visit Notre-Dame-des-Victoires daily for a Hail Mary as New Year's gift.
p37 "I have received from her favours known only to myself". Isidore became chemist (pharmacist) at Lisieux, married Mlle (Céline) Fournet. Most of Zélie letters to sister-in-law. Story of rich childless couple's new house in Alençon.
p38 Letter to brother 28 Mar 1864: true happiness not here; "good fortune carries with it a danger-signal"; the couple fell into unprotected trench in their new garden. Hélène put with a wet nurse; letter 23 Apr 1865: "she smiled at me so sweetly that I thought I was looking at an angel".
p39 Hélène died of consumption aged five, 22 Feb 1870 (footnote) [CHECK: text p41 says 23 Feb; footnote says 22 Feb 1870]. Captain Martin d. 26 Jun 1865 at 1 pm, in 89th year; letter 27 Jun 1865: "He died like a saint". Two sons born and taken. Her father d.; letter 3 Sep 1868: "His tomb will be quite close to that of my two little Josephs." 3 Masses.
p40 1 Nov 1868 to brother: "For myself I do not fear purgatory; to suffer seems quite natural to me." Léonie frail constitution and education difficulties. Visitation sister threatened with phthisis. Marie gentle reserved, timid; Pauline gracious, bent for learning, exuberant.
p41 Marie offered dental operation for grandfather ("the poor papa"); dentist never saw child so brave; "What a pity! the poor papa would no longer have been in purgatory" (letter Jan 1869). Hélène d. 23 Feb [1870] (4th child). Sœur Marie-Dosithée letter 23 Feb 1870: "this great saint whom you have so much desired".
p42 Zélie to sister-in-law (who lost a child, letter 17 Oct 1871): "Everyone said: 'Better they had never been born.' I could not agree"; "we shall meet again in heaven". Marie-Dosithée 28 Oct 1868: "What a valiant woman she is!" Start of 1870 Louis handed watchmaking business to a nephew (stayed rue du Pont-Neuf until July of next year) to help lace business. Prussian invasion of Alençon: nine German infantry soldiers billeted in rue du Pont-Neuf house.
p43 "They have reduced the house to a deplorable condition... everyone is in tears except myself." Louis "if he were free he would soon be enlisted amongst the francs-tireurs"; war ended before call-up. After peace moved to rue Saint-Blaise house (inherited from grandfather Guérin) near Notre-Dame; ground floor two windows, three arched windows 2nd floor; small; separated by garden trellis.
p44 Marie-Céline born 1869; Marie-Mélanie-Thérèse born 1870, died. "Four of my children are already in their eternal home..." (HA intro p.xxxi). Resignation taken by strangers for coldness. Six years joy in this house. Birth 2 Jan 1873 "towards midnight"; Marie and Pauline asleep in 2nd-floor room. Footnote: Zélie singing seemed to hear the unborn child singing.
p45 Louis: "My children, you have a little sister."
p46 Godmother Marie; godfather Paul-Albert Boul (son of Louis's friend), delayed; baptism afternoon 4 Jan [1873] Notre-Dame.
p47 Abbé Lucien-Victor Dumaine baptized (friend; later Vicar-General of Séez; witness at process; wrote "Tinchebray et sa région"). Carried home by servant Louise [Marais]. Mother nursed herself; strength failed; child declined.
p48 Doctor: only chance a healthy wet nurse. Rose Taillé, "little Rose", farmer's wife, had nursed a Martin child before. Letter to sister-in-law March 1873: "How long the night seemed!"; set out at daybreak to Semallé "almost two leagues"; husband away; met two men "Even though they should kill me, I do not care"; nurse would stay 8 days then take Thérèse home.
p49 Zélie knelt before St Joseph statue; child appeared dead in nurse's lap; then opened eyes and smiled. Farm description (Laveille's own; thatch, stables, one-storey 3 openings); Rose had a year-old child and three others.
p50 Not 3 weeks at Semallé, new attack. Letter to Lisieux relations 30 Mar 1873: fever, throat and chest; "I doubt whether we shall be able to bring her up... I have already suffered much during my life." Fire had damaged part of M. Guérin's pharmacy. Marie had typhoid fever at Le Mans; weeks ill at Alençon; Zélie at bedside day and night while lace orders poured in.
p51 Zélie to Mme Guérin: "life is not all strewn with roses"; to sister-in-law 13 Apr 1873: "sometimes remain on my feet the whole night". 5 May [1873] to Pauline: Louis goes on pilgrimage to the ridge of Chaumont for Marie, fasting there and back, six leagues on foot. 20 Apr [1873]: "The nurse brought our little Thérèse here today; she is quite well and strong."
p52 Sunday: Rose arrived unannounced with four children at 11.30, put baby in Zélie's arms, went to Mass; baby screamed; Louise sent to fetch nurse; "the babe would not have died from crying"; Zélie "had a pain in my back for the remainder of the day" (letter to Pauline 5 May 1873). Rose put Thérèse on straw in the bottom of her wheelbarrow to go to the fields; carried in her apron at milking; "browned by the sun".
p53 Letter to Pauline 22 May [1873]: Thérèse cried when left; Louise took her to market where Rose sold butter; stayed "selling butter with all the women until midday"; weighs fourteen pounds. 30 Nov 1873 to Pauline: stands by chair; "continually smiling; she has the expression of one who is predestined."
p54 11 Jan 1874 letter to brother/sister-in-law: "since Thursday been able to walk alone". Returned to Alençon 2 Apr 1874. 1 Jun 1874 to sister-in-law: "Of all, except my first child, she is the most robust... a tiny mouth, which I particularly admire." May 1873 Louis to Notre-Dame de Chartres in thanks for Marie's cure.
p55 Oct 1873 Louis on diocesan pilgrimage to Lourdes, brought back two large pieces of stone from the rock of Massabielle. Zélie 1 Nov 1873 to Marie and Pauline: "I earnestly wish to become a saint. It will not be easy; a great deal yet remains to be 'hewn' off, and the wood is hard as stone." Louis called her his "Queen".
p56 June 1874 Louis built a swing in the garden for Céline and Thérèse; letter 25 Jun 1874: "She acts just like an older child... we tie a cord in front". Zélie to Mass 5.30 am; left Thérèse asleep in her bed with cradle alongside; one day forgot; found her asleep sitting on a chair by the bed.
p57 "Her guardian angel was there..." At 18 months: strokes face wanting "a scarf pin"; cries if mother not in garden. 8 Nov 1874 (22 months) letter to sister-in-law: "she prattles from morning till night... says her prayers like a little angel".
p58 Letter 14 Mar 1875: ran out in torrents of rain toward church wanting to go to Mass; tears lasted a good hour. "Why do you not say your prayers, papa? Have you already been to church?" "Mama, I am going to be very good."
p59 Cut forehead on table leg (feared scar). A painting shows the family evening scene in the rue Saint-Blaise sitting room [a later painting; Laveille describes it; not evidence]. Father "already white hair".
p60 Visits to Lisieux: play with Jeanne and Marie Guérin; New Year gifts exchanged. April 1875 train to Le Mans Visitation to see sister (phthisis).
p61 Letter 29 Apr 1875: cried at the grille "convulsed with silent weeping"; answered "as if she were undergoing an examination"; to the Superioress: "Mother, will you come home with us?" Louis inclined to spoil her (Zélie).
p62 Prayers at bedtime; father helped, not sure what "Grace" was for. "Oh, how I wish you would die, my poor little mother" ... "that you may go to heaven" (letter to Pauline 5 Dec [1875]). Marie taught Céline after leaving school; Céline 3 years 8 months older. By end Dec 1875 Thérèse knew almost all her letters (letter 23 Dec 1875).
p63 22 Mar 1877 to Pauline: "more intelligence than I noticed in any of you". Marie's note: Thérèse lay down outside the door she could not open; "Thérèse, you are grieving the Little Jesus" — never repeated (unpublished note of Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur).
p64 Zélie to Pauline 5 Dec 1875: "You are my true friend". Marie-Dosithée 1875 to Isidore: "Léonie ... somewhat difficult to manage, but she has a heart of gold... great force of character". Céline 1875 "very gentle ... if God leaves her with us" (Nov 1875; Céline delicate constitution).
p65 Zélie reading life of St Chantal (5 Dec 1875); 16 Jan 1876 to Pauline: "I do nothing but dream of the cloister and solitude... I would now wish to live to a great age, so that I might retire into solitude when all my children have been brought up."
p66 HA ch.i p.8: "my earliest memories are of smiles and tender caresses". Marble slab on house front: "Louis Martin, Manufacturer of Point d'Alençon" [rue Saint-Blaise].
p67 Louis did business outside, deliveries to distant places, accounts; never on credit; paid workers regularly; refused speculation: "speculation is a slippery incline". Zélie at Lisieux eight months before death "to undergo an operation for an incurable disease" [consultation with surgeon; Dec 1876] refused evening meal because Ember Day fast. [Laveille source not given]
p68 Hélène had told a small untruth; Zélie, fearing she was in purgatory, heard from the Virgin statue: "She is here at my side." Footnote: statue given to Louis before marriage by a pious lady of Alençon called "the saint". Diabolic attack: before Thérèse's birth, Zélie alone felt an enormous weight like a wild beast's claw on her shoulder (unpublished letter of Mme Martin).
p69 HA ch.i p.8: "No one can imagine how I loved papa and mama... I was very demonstrative". Dialogue (letter to Pauline 29 Oct 1876): "if I was not good I should then go to hell. But I know what I would do, I would fly up to you in heaven, and you would hold me close in your arms. How then would God be able to take me away?"
p70 Céline: "How can God be in a little host?" T: "That is not so wonderful, since God is all-powerful." (letter to Pauline May 1877). Letter 4 Mar 1877: Céline and Thérèse inseparable; sits 2–3 hours at lessons stringing pearls, cries when needle unthreaded, dares not disturb Marie.
p71 4 Mar 1877: recites verses; poem "Sweet little child with locks of gold, Where, think you, hath the Lord his Home?"; raises eyes to heaven at last line.
p72 HA ch.i p.11 love for godmother Marie. Letter to Pauline 21 May 1876: refused rose because Marie had forbidden cutting; "flushed crimson"; "No, they are Marie's."
p73 HA: "Of what are you thinking?" "Of Pauline." "I also will be a religious" — first memory, from age two. Léonie delicate, studied in Alençon; sang Thérèse to sleep in evenings while others walked.
p74 Léonie's First Communion: Zélie dressed a poor child as companion (custom among families in easy circumstances at Alençon), given place of honour at dinner (HA p.12). Letter 8 Oct 1876: Céline "exceptionally good disposition". Rose Taillé gave Thérèse a bantam cock and hen; gave cock to Céline.
p75 Letter to Pauline 8 Nov 1876: slept with Céline: "Do leave me here, Louise; you see that we two are like two little white chicks that cannot be separated." Blessed bread game: Céline cut bread, said Ave Maria over it (HA ch.i p.14).
p76 Zélie: on stairs "Mama! Mama!" at each step; won't move if no reply "Yes, my little child!" Rode on father's foot; Louis: "Well, after all, is she not the Queen?"
p77 Toys: carriages, dolls, ribbons; bonbons from uncle/aunt; preferred aunt's present "to all the lands" of a rich proprietor. Loved wild flowers.
p78 "Pavillon": small house on outskirts of Alençon with large garden (strawberries, currants, geraniums, roses) where Louis took Céline and Thérèse. Month of Mary altar; Marie to Pauline: "Mama is so very particular about it, more particular than the Blessed Virgin herself"; hawthorn branches to the ceiling. Statue = the one that smiled later.
p79 Marie: "this little 'bouquet'". Céline pestered father; T.: "Oh, we need not flatter ourselves with the notion that papa will bring us there every day!" papa laughed (letter Oct 1876).
p80 Zélie to Pauline 14 May 1876: Céline "inclined to virtue"; "As to the 'little ferret' ... so heedless ... a remarkable intellect and a heart of gold ... absolutely frank"; "Mama, I have pushed Céline once and I hit her once, but I will not do it again." HA ch.i p.10: on swing refused father "Come yourself, papa!"; Marie: "Bold little one!"; cries of contrition; ran upstairs to be reconciled.
p81 Letter to Pauline 13 Feb 1877: pretended sleep, "I don't want anyone to look at me"; came downstairs barefoot tripping on too-long nightdress: "Mama, I have been naughty; forgive me." Tore corner of wallpaper; "Tell papa quickly that I have torn the paper"; stood "like a criminal awaiting sentence".
p82 Broke a vase: "when I earn money of my own, I promise I will buy you another"; Zélie: "I shall have to wait some time for it!" (21 May 1876 / 14 May 1876 letters).
p83 "The Cross has never, from my cradle, ceased to accompany me." Marie brought from Visitation a "practice" chaplet with movable beads; Céline and Thérèse counted "practices"; neighbour leaned out window then asked maid what "practices" were.
p84 HA ch.i p.16: silent when accused unjustly. Zélie: "put her hand in her pocket a hundred times a day to move forward a bead". Grandmother Mme Martin (Fanny Boureau) took her wild flowers for a little altar; Thérèse gave them one by one, tears held back (source: Carbonel, children's life — secondary).
p85 HA ch.i p.15: Léonie's basket of dolls' things: Céline chose silk braid; Thérèse: "I choose everything" and took basket and doll; later "My God, I choose all! I do not wish to be a saint by halves."
p86 Letter 24 Feb 1877 from Visitation Le Mans: Sr Marie-Dosithée died that morning; said "I have no other thoughts but of love, trust, and abandonment" (cited by Zélie to brother 26 Feb 1877). Zélie: as a young woman struck breast against table corner; permanent swelling; fibrous tumour; 16 years; Dr Prévost at Alençon: "It is useless; I give it to please patients."
p87 Isidore took her to a Lisieux doctor who said too late. 28 Jan 1877 to sister-in-law: "my life is not so precious." 10 May 1877: "I have given up in good earnest my point d'Alençon and now begin to live on my income... it has cost me dear." 13 May 1877 to Pauline: confidence came 10–15 days after aunt's death.
p88 Diocese of Angers pilgrimage to Lourdes 18 Jun 1877 with Marie, Pauline, Léonie; letter 29 May 1877: "The trouble and expense are considerable". Heat, food, lodging. Letter 24 Jun 1877: "immersed four times in the piscina... above my shoulders... over a quarter of an hour"; "I will make you happy, not in this world but in the next." Zélie sang hymns home while daughters silent.
p89 Louis, Céline, Thérèse waited at station. Louis surprised she came back happy. To Pauline 25 Jun 1877: "whether you are still wroth with the Blessed Virgin because she has not made you 'leap for joy'... Do not hope for much happiness on this earth". Thérèse still cried remembering being left at Alençon two years before when others went to Lisieux.
p90 Letter 8 Jul 1877: "my Thérèse; I assure you that she will succeed." June–July intolerable suffering. 15 Jul 1877: "I am trying to prepare for death. I must not lose a moment". First Friday of August: last Mass at parish church; each step like a stiletto in her neck; pains down right side. Céline and Thérèse taken daily to a friend's house.
p91 HA ch.ii p.19: "like two poor little exiles"; Mme X [HA anonymises; lady] "Well, my little ones, you shall say them" left them alone; "Ah, that is not what mama would have done"; apricot: "We will not eat it, we will give it to mama." Marie staged a home prize-giving at start of holidays.
p92 Marie letter 9 Aug 1877 to aunt: room decorated with vinca garlands and roses; two armchairs for M. and Mme Martin as "presidents"; girls in white got books and wreaths. Zélie to brother 16 Aug 1877 (last): "My strength is at an end... God wills that I find my resting-place elsewhere". 26 Aug: Viaticum; Louis himself accompanied the priest to and from the church; Extreme Unction; HA ch.ii p.20: "I see again the spot where they placed me on my knees, I hear once more the sobs of our poor father."
p93 Death 28 Aug 1877, half an hour after midnight; Laveille says "forty-six years" [she was born 23 Dec 1831 -> aged 45; CONFLICT]. Louis lifted Thérèse: "Come and kiss your dear mother for the last time"; she kissed icy forehead silently; alone, saw coffin in corridor, examined it. Funeral Notre-Dame. HA: "The whole five of us... The maid...: 'Poor little children, you have no longer a mother.'" Céline to Marie: "It is you who will be mama for us now." Footnote: daughters remember serene expression.
p94 Thérèse to Pauline: "As for me, Pauline will be my mama." Footnote: dying Zélie had taken Pauline's hand and kissed it. Family went out only to cemetery. Louis wound up business; refused Alençon friends' advice; Zélie's mute look to Mme Guérin, who promised to be a mother.
p95 Louis asked Isidore to find a house; 10 Sep [1877] residence found; first fortnight of November took daughters to Lisieux. Footnote: after Louis's burial at Lisieux 1894, Isidore moved Zélie, the dead children, and grandparents (M. Guérin, Mme Martin) from Alençon to Lisieux cemetery. Les Buissonnets red brick.
p96 Lisieux: cathedral, wood-carving, factory chimneys smoke; children's first impression sadness; Thérèse "Children love change". Guérin house at corner of Grande-Rue and Place Thiers [pharmacy].
p97 Isidore: medium height, keen look, decisive speech; pharmacy flourishing; "formidable polemical opponent of the Church's enemies". Mme Guérin (Céline Fournet) relation of Thomas-Jean Monsaint, priest massacred at the Abbaye 2 Sep 1792.
p98 Les Buissonnets reached from Pont-l'Évêque road on east of Lisieux by winding path; midway up a hill; lawn with trees in front; garden behind with ivy wall. HA ch.ii p.22: "my happy disposition changed completely... timid and shy and sensitive to excess; a look sufficed to make the tears flow".
p99 Louis had visited in September. Marie letter 16 Nov [1877]: "We are installed in Les Buissonnets... Only the staircase, and also the approach to the house, leave something to be desired." Old building, low ceilings, far from church. Louis called the path "the way to Paradise". Louis arrived 30 Nov [1877]. Céline and Thérèse had ground-floor room opening on back garden. Footnote: pilgrims see her bed, objects, toys in glass gallery (formerly Léonie's tiny room).
p100 During illness Marie took her to her own room, front left, two large windows (now oratory) — where the Virgin smiled. Céline and Léonie day-boarders at the Benedictine convent. Marie and Pauline kept house and taught Thérèse. At ~3.5 she stopped learning to read for a time; first word read unaided "cieux" (heavens); ran to father in the "Belvedere" (room at top of house). Louis prematurely grey.
p101 Louis's routine: daily Mass at Cathedral [Saint-Pierre], gardening, reading in Belvedere, second daily visit to Blessed Sacrament with Thérèse — first visit to Carmel chapel this way. Walks as reward; Pauline cancelled walk if work poor; Louis always ratified. Footnote (Carbonel): thirst sacrifice "to save a sinner", Pauline then brought drink.
p102 Fishing on banks of the Touques with father; own little line; sat in grass; HA ch.ii p.24: military band music; "Earth seemed to me a place of exile". Storm: saw thunderbolt fall in a meadow, enchanted (HA p.25). Gave alms to old men with money father entrusted.
p103 Poor came every Monday to Les Buissonnets; Thérèse opened gate; "Pauline, it is a poor old crippled man..." Morning offering words mama taught: "My God, I give Thee my heart..." HA ch.ii p.26. To servant Victoire: "You know well, Victoire, that this offends the good God."
p104 HA ch.ii p.30: Orion's belt looked like a "T": "See, papa, my name is written in heaven!" asked father to lead her while she looked up. Christopher Columbus presentiment quote (HA ch.ix/?). After walk wrote Pauline's exercises.
p105 Evenings: dining room round table and oak chairs (still there); draughts; Marie/Pauline read Dom Guéranger's Liturgical Year; Louis sang with "fine voice"; night prayers; "had but to look at him to realize how the saints pray." In bed: "Have I been good today? Is the good God pleased with me?" — otherwise night in tears.
p106 Sunday High Mass at Cathedral, chapel on Epistle side; moved during sermon; Isidore Guérin in churchwarden's pew called her his "little ray of sunshine". First sermon understood: on the Passion, aged 5.5. Sunday evenings at Guérins, each sister in turn.
p107 Isidore sang "Barbe-Bleue" (Bluebeard) in a stentorian voice with her on his knee — joy mixed with fear. Corpus Christi: flower-girl; threw petals high to touch the monstrance (HA ch.ii p.28). May altar on chest of drawers with wax vestas as candles; Victoire the only congregation; Memorare.
p108 Old lady: "That little one is an angel... if she lives, you will find that she will be spoken of later on as a saint." A woman working at Les Buissonnets begged a lock of her hair. Thursday holiday games with Céline; Céline taught her dolls; "My dear child, go and kiss your aunt."
p109 First confession at age six to Abbé Ducellier, parish priest of Saint-Pierre (d. 1917 as arch-priest), grave, reserved; asked Pauline if she should tell confessor she "loved him with all her heart".
p110 Had to stand in the confessional; passed rosary through grating to be blessed; under a street lamp: "I want to see what a blessed rosary is like." Age 7 attended Céline's First Communion preparation (Céline day-boarder at Abbey) until told too young. Christmas Eve to Marie: "I would slip in amongst the others... I am so small that no one would notice me."
p111 Aug 1878 (nearly six): Trouville with Guérins, father and sisters; first sight of sea. Louis tall, snow-white hair, face still young; lady: "What a pretty little girl!"; Louis signed not to compliment her. Promenade des Planches; Roches Noires near Jetée des Anglais. Footnote: family friend: "she has heaven in her eyes."
p112 HA ch.ii p.35: sat with Pauline on a rock; golden path of the sun on the sea = grace; "my own heart as a frail little barque with snowy white sail". Pauline never went back on a decision; sent her in the dusk to fetch objects from dark corners to cure fear.
p113 Thimble and father's tumbler both full — Pauline's lesson on degrees of glory. Louis first went to 6 am Mass with the poor ("God's favourite children"), daughters persuaded him to 7 am, "with regret". Walked home silent: "I continue to commune with our Lord." Persuaded Isidore to found Nocturnal Adoration society with clergy of Saint-Pierre, Lisieux.
p114 Unsigned fragment attributed to Louis (Carmel archives): men "make as much effort to preserve their life on the eve of death as if they had yet many hundred years to live"; quotes Abbé de Rancé on the ship and Providence.

Prophetic vision at age six [Laveille; plan says 1879/1880] while father at Alençon on business: about 2–3 pm, sunny, at window overlooking garden, before the laundry:
p116 man dressed like papa, as tall, but "very bent and aged", head covered with thick veil; walked slowly; she cried "Papa! Papa!"; he went toward clump of fir trees; vanished. Marie and Pauline in adjoining room felt fear; Marie: "Why do you call papa thus, my little one, when he is at Alençon?"; suggested maid with apron over head; Victoire said she had not left kitchen; searched behind trees; "think no more about it" (HA ch.ii p.32).
