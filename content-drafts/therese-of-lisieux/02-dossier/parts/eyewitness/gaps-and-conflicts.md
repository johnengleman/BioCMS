# Gaps and conflicts — eyewitness family

Collected from each extraction batch. Card numbers are the final F-EW / M-EW numbers.

## From batch A

- **Pauline is both witness and editor.** She admits the HA text was changed (F-EW-1897), and says Thérèse gave her blanket permission to edit (F-EW-1423). Only she reports that permission. She also calls the changes "of little importance". Treat her HA-based statements with care.
- **Much of her "testimony" is quotation of HA.** She says Thérèse told her everything later written down, and quotes MS A/C at length as testimony. This blurs what she saw and what she read. The Pranzini story, the Rome audience and the Abbey years she refers to Céline or Marie.
- **Her notebook affected Thérèse.** Pauline admits her note-taking tired Thérèse and "paralysed her openness" (F-EW-1581). Thérèse feared her merit was lost because Pauline wrote it down. This matters for the reliability of the "Last Words".
- **Dates in the NV notebook do not always match the editors' DE references.** The Hanoi saying sits under "15 mai" but is tagged DE 15-6; items under "15 mai" are tagged DE 9-5; the "4 juin" entry is tagged DE 6-4; there are retro-entries "Du 21 (oublié)" and "Du 16 juillet (oublié)". The notebook was edited after the event.
- **Dying moment: two versions.** Oral: "I saw her close her eyes; she sighed several times and gave her soul to God" (f. 246v). NV: "As soon as it [the ecstasy] ended, she breathed her last" (f. 292v). The oral text says "a sister" carried the candle; the NV names Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie.
- **Body after death.** Supple limbs until burial (f. 292v) vs. coffin closed on 3 Oct because of "some signs of decomposition" (f. 246v). Both from Pauline; she does not reconcile them.
- **Cure date.** Pauline gives 10 May 1883 for the smile of the Virgin; the standard date is 13 May 1883 (Pentecost).
- **Publication date.** Pauline: October 1898; editors: 30 Sept 1898.
- **Origin date of MS A.** Pauline: early 1895; editors: Dec 1894.
- **Taxil / Diana Vaughan.** Pauline says Thérèse saw through the hoax before its exposure (F-EW-992). She does not mention that Thérèse wrote for "Diana" or that her photo was used at Taxil's April 1897 conference. This looks like a softened account. Check other witnesses.
- **Gonzague.** Pauline is critical ("gaps and faults", "harmful influence", "suffered much from this prioress", a prioress elected "despite notable faults"). But she also reports Gonzague's deathbed trust in Thérèse, and that Gonzague showed her off at the parlour. The 1896 prioress who "had notable faults" is not named; the identification with Gonzague is by context.
- **Dissenters named or hinted.** (1) a long-professed nun who later left the Carmel: "easy to be a saint with everything"; (2) a lay sister, dead by 1910: the "circular" remark, but at other times called her a saint; (3) a lay sister: "not even a good nun"; (4) a sister who stared laughing every evening; (5) some sisters who did not think her dying; (6) Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix before 1888; (7) two or three Carmels and one prioress who saw pride in HA. Pauline discredits dissenter 1 ("poor judgment", left the order). None except Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix is named.
- **"All except one or two" admired her** (f. 240v) sits uneasily with the list of critics above.
- **Pauline's own failures, freely admitted:** she did not read MS A for months; she did not believe the wound of love; she joined in refusing Thérèse's profession in 1890; she tried to get Thérèse to say edifying words to the doctor; she received confidences Thérèse said she should refuse.
- **Friction inside the Martin family:** Marie opposed her entry; Marie was hurt that Thérèse preferred a nun Marie disliked; Thérèse felt her sisters tried to "influence our mother" about her rest; she warned all three about talking after the parlour; "I don't like what comes from my family".
- **Unnamed people** the merger may want to identify from other files: the old lay sister she guided (died 1895); the "abominable" sister; the critic at the funeral; the first infirmarian whose handling "could have had grave consequences"; the neurasthenic sister; the unbelieving relative by marriage; the tempted seminarian; the director who called her proud.
- **Expected but absent:** almost nothing about her painting (only the 28 May painting request), Joan of Arc plays, her poems' circumstances beyond "Vivre d'amour", the Bellière/Roulland letters' content, Dr La Néele (not mentioned at all), and the laundry or portress work in detail. The Abbey years are deferred to Céline and Marie.
- **Procedural note:** the judges asked leading questions about neuropathic temperament, delirium, why she left school, and whether the superior objected because two sisters were already inside. These show the tribunal testing the "Martin clan" problem.

## From batch D

- **Mental prayer as a child:** Marie says she refused even a quarter of an hour (PA 7 [566]); Céline says Marie allowed a quarter of an hour, spent behind the bed curtains (PA 8 [691]).
- **Baby on the chair:** Marie says Thérèse fell from her cradle; Céline says she was left in the mother's big bed without the cradle nearby (PA 7 [560]; PA 8 [776]).
- **Father's veiled figure:** Marie says the meaning was understood when Thérèse retold it in Carmel; Céline says it was revealed only after the father's death (1894). Marie says "dark cloth", Céline "thick veil".
- **Cousin Marie Guérin:** Marie places Thérèse's refusal to go to the enclosure door at the cousin's profession examination; Céline places it at her clothing. These may be two occasions.
- **First haemoptysis:** Marie gives Holy Thursday 1896; Céline gives Good Friday at [741], Holy Thursday at [782], and "4 April" (wrong date) at [782].
- **Time and manner of death:** Marie gives 7:15 p.m. and says she did not speak from five o'clock except the last words. Céline gives 7:20 p.m. and reports words during the agony ("the cup is full", "allons... allons..."). Marie adds the candle test. Céline adds the last look for herself, and says Gonzague sent the community away.
- **Funeral crowd:** Marie says the crowd was "numerous" (explained by the family's local fame). Céline says many priests came but the procession of faithful was "very small".
- **Remains in 1910:** Marie says a brick-lined vault; Céline says a lead coffin. These are probably compatible.
- **"You are nursing a little saint":** Marie heard it with all three sisters present. Céline is unsure whether she heard it or only heard of it. Elsewhere Thérèse told Céline "I am not a saint" [787].
- **Origin of the last Marian poem:** Marie says it was written "at my request"; Céline quotes Thérèse's "I still have something to do before I die".
- **Date of the "beautiful death" saying:** Marie gives 22 August; the editors' reference points to DE 4 June.
- **Age of drawing refusal:** Marie says about 10; Céline says 9. Céline's age at the cure: 13 at [637], 14 at [777].
- **Mother Marie de Gonzague:** Both sisters, especially Céline, describe jealousy, "frightful scenes", neglect in the illness, refusal of Dr La Néele and of morphine injections. The editors explicitly warn that pp. 649 and 741 must be corrected against Guy Gaucher, and note that Gonzague herself kindly named Céline infirmary aide. Céline also records Gonzague's praise (none like her; wanted her last look to be for Gonzague) and her remorse after the death. Treat the blame as partisan family testimony.
- **Coaching / shaping:** Marie swears no one influenced her, yet her 13 March 1915 letter (editors' note) says the sisters would "set right" the Roman advocate's portrait. Céline's deposition was prepared "to the smallest detail" from notes (editors). Both reject the Articles' extraordinary phenomena (Céline: Articles 34, 216, 239–242, 244). Both repeat several of the same stories and sayings (three minutes without thinking of God; the Virgin helps at once; the 80 years; die in Gonzague's arms), mostly from Mother Agnès's notebook (DE).
- **Contrary opinion:** Both say they know of no opinion against her holiness, yet both report hostility: the lay sister who burned the sandals; Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul "irritated by her virtue"; Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph's insults; an older nun calling her irregular; Fr Blinot calling her aspirations presumption; the chaplain's "very dangerous state"; community jealousy of three Martin sisters. No witness here says "what will we write in her circular".
- **Dissent about the Martin "clan":** Céline admits the community's jealousy of the three sisters and Thérèse's efforts to hide the family tie ("forgive us for living under one roof"; photographs; "do not lead the family life"). Marie shows her own jealousy of Pauline and of a nun.
- **Expected but absent:** Neither names Dr de Cornière's diagnosis in detail. Neither mentions the Diana Vaughan photo and letter Thérèse sent in 1896 (Céline mentions only the rejection of the book). Neither discusses the nights of 1896–97 in the cold cell except in passing. Léonie's view is absent. Marie does not name the sister who burned the alpargates or the neurasthenic sister. Céline gives no detail of what Thérèse "reproached" Gonzague with.
- **Changes vs 1910 (flagged by the witnesses):** Marie: mother's birthplace; adds her feeling of Thérèse's soul the day after death. Céline: father's name, mother's birthplace, return from the wet-nurse (2 April 1874), and a clarification that "no spiritual director" meant no habitual direction. Céline updates offering counts (26 → 321 plaques).

## From batch G

- Pichon's claim that the teenage Thérèse told him "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth" word for word, several times, before Carmel (PO-10 [553r]) is flagged by the editors with "? ? ?". The saying is dated 17 July 1897. Likely a false memory.
- Pichon's dates are unreliable: he met the family "about 1880/1881" (editors: 17 April 1882); the 1888 retreat "in autumn" (editors: May 1888). He speaks of "a few letters" from her; she wrote that she sent him twelve for each of his. His two accounts of his 1909 cure differ (Mass after 6–7 days vs 4 days). Thérèse said he "treated me too much like a child" (editors, DE 4 July 1897).
- Jeanne La Néele contradicts herself: 1911 — she never suspected Thérèse's holiness and thought she entered Carmel to join Pauline; 1917 — she saw "quite extraordinary" perfection already in childhood.
- Jeanne (not expansive, spoke little, rarely played) versus Marcelline Husé (very joyful and expansive at home and with the maid). Different settings; both may be true.
- The 1883 illness: Marcelline's 1910 version (fright, hallucinations several times a day, lucid) is stronger than her 1917 version (feverish, nervous, "not delirium"). Jeanne gives the most physical detail (rotating movements) and says Dr Notta would not name it; in 1911 she says the doctor called it St Vitus' dance. Isidore Guérin blamed the devil. Marcelline dates the cure "Sunday 10 May" (the Sunday was 13 May 1883). Neither Jeanne nor Marcelline heard of an apparition at the time; Pichon says the sisters' letters told him of the smile of the Virgin "at that very time".
- School: "best marks" (Marcelline) vs "a little weak in arithmetic and spelling" and "classmates who equalled her" (her own teacher). The "persecution" at school is confirmed but played down by her teacher as "exaggeration".
- Why she left school: headaches (Jeanne, Marcelline), health given by her father (Sr Saint-André), health plus scruples (Sr Saint-François). The nuns insist she was not sent away, which suggests a rumour.
- Frequency of her post-school visits (1–2 vs 2–3 a week) and time in the chapel gallery (1–1.5 vs 1–2 hours) differ between Benedictine witnesses. Who spoke at her Child of Mary admission differs (Sr Sainte-Placide vs "the president of the council").
- Marguerite Leroy's ages and dates differ between 1911 and 1917 (six years older / aged 14; 1882–84 / 1881–83; Thérèse aged 9 / about 10). She honestly doubts whether the "I hide by my bed and think" saying was told to her or only retold in the community. Sr Saint-François says it was told to her.
- Roulland's letter count differs (about eight in 1911, about six in 1917).
- Dumaine's slips: Zélie died "1887" (1877); Domin: the Martins came to Lisieux "1887" (1877); Ducellier: returned "1889" and "two years after her death" (1899).
- The task list calls PA-01 "Mgr Lemonnier, bishop". The file is Fr Armand-Constant Lemonnier of La Délivrande, retreat preacher, not the bishop.
- The PA-25 file also contains two co-witnesses ex officio (Sr Jeanne-Marie de l'Enfant-Jésus; Pierre Derrien, the Carmel's sacristan) and the start of the editors' introduction to Fr Godefroy Madelaine's Namur process; it breaks off mid-sentence. Both co-witnesses were Carmel insiders or employees, relevant for weighing their graces.
- Sober testimony on death: Jeanne La Néele, Domin and Maupas all saw nothing extraordinary at the body, funeral or burial. No perfume or light at death is reported in this batch.
- Expected but absent: no witness here gives her last words or the last hour; Dr de Cornière appears only via a letter; Dr La Néele's own observations of her illness are only summarised by his wife.
- Several witnesses say they were not influenced (standard question); Jeanne adds "it would have been useless". No clear sign of coaching, but many "I only confirm Story of a Soul" answers.

## From batch J

- **Dating of Céline's text.** The page calls it Céline's old notes used for the Processes, but the text as published cites events up to 1954. It is a late, edited compilation (with bracketed doctrinal notes, e.g. against quietism, and notes framing sayings as "not for everyone"). Treat wording as Céline's mature, edited memory, not a 1910 note. Book page numbers appear in parentheses.
- **Editorial softening.** Several bracketed notes restrict Thérèse's sayings (repugnance for meals "not part of the Little Way"; "passer dessous" meant only for novices; "no latitude" not a universal rule). These show the editors managing her image.
- **Céline's own doubts.** Céline admits she once suspected Thérèse did not suffer as much as believed (F-EW-2422), found her too severe about the dance (F-EW-675), and thought her obedience to a sister "too blind" (F-EW-1359).
- **Cool or hostile views recorded:** family (Guérins implied) thought her "insignificant" and poorly educated (F-EW-598); some called her ideas "presumption" (F-EW-2376); an older sister found her "irregular" for taking remedies (F-EW-1164); Sr Marie des Anges said she "does nothing but complain" (F-EW-597); "Th. de J." said she had "nothing rare" (F-EW-600); a sick "Mère" deliberately harassed novices (F-EW-957); relatives criticised Louis's 10,000-franc altar (F-EW-592).
- **The Process.** Céline says the Promoter of the Faith warned that "a way" would sink the Cause, and she refused to change (F-EW-1888). She also says supernatural gifts were "very rare" (F-EW-2360). This conflicts with any later hagiography stressing marvels.
- **Community tension.** Marie writes of Pauline's "years of martyrdom" from the jealousy of an unnamed "Mère***", and of the "pearl" crushed "because of the diamond" (F-EW-367). The name is hidden. Also "a great trial… Heaven alone has its secret" (undefined).
- **Care of the sick.** Céline calls the care "primitive" (no mattress; F-EW-1367) and says it was "too late" to act on Thérèse's advice to infirmarians (F-EW-1361). This supports other criticisms of Gonzague's nursing regime, though Céline also shows Gonzague ordering a foot-warmer (F-EW-1160).
- **Date conflict.** Marie says Louis woke her "about eleven" to announce Zélie's death; the usual time is after midnight, 28 Aug 1877. Marie also says she was "13" at Thérèse's birth (she was 12).
- **Joan fire date.** Céline does not date the fire. Marie's text shows the Joan plays were Jan 1894 and Jan 1895; the fire is traditionally the 1895 performance.
- **Shower of roses.** Marie gives a precise trigger (Aloysius reading, her week as reader). Céline says the promise was made many times in many forms.
- **Expected but absent:** Céline's text names almost no difficult sisters (all are "une Soeur", "une Mère ancienne"). No doctor is named in the pointes-de-feu scene. Marie's notebook says almost nothing about Thérèse in Carmel beyond entry, the Joan play and the roses; she defers childhood illness to her deposition. Marie never mentions the Offering refusal (known only via Céline, F-EW-1033).
- **English availability.** Much of Céline's book was translated in 1959 ("A Memoir of My Sister St Thérèse"); several last-illness sayings are in "Last Conversations". I marked those "partial". Marie's 1909 notebook is, as far as I know, not in English.

## From batch L

- De Teil gives 22 as her age when she took charge of the novices; Laveille reports an old nun remarking on her being "only twenty". Standard date: Feb 1893 (age 20). Check depositions.
- Boulogne (LAV p. 292) is probably a mistranslation of Bologna (the pilgrims passed through Bologna). Verify in Céline's deposition.
- The glass-of-water story (D4): Dolan asks about Marie; the sisters answer "in order not to disturb Céline". Céline slept in the cell by the infirmary. The story is weakly attested.
- Two versions of Mother Geneviève's plea to Delatroëtte (infirmary vs community room).
- Number of witnesses at the Ordinary Process: 45 (De Teil 1912) vs 48 (Carmel site).
- The name of the lay sister cured at the dead body (M-EW-032) and of the kitchen Sister (F-EW-1218) are not in these books.
- Madame Tifenne vs Céline's portraits: open conflict about her looks.


## From batch N (gap fill)

New conflicts and open points found while adding cards.

- **Bishop Giannattasio and the words "My way is sure".** He corrected Article 145b: he knew the phrase when he posted the alms, though not when he first had the idea (F-EW-2471). The folios [1320]–[1323r] fall between the two parts of his text on the Carmel page and are not in our extract.
- **The Gallipoli sums.** The bishop counts the 1911 envelope as 500 livres sent and 300 livres found (two of 100 and two of 50). Earlier cards give the same total. He did not verify the 1910 surplus sums himself (F-EW-2460).
- **Mère Agnès's 54 cases.** Item 6 (Frère Paul, Rogersville) may or may not be the same as M-EW-077 (a Canadian boy). Not checked. The doctors' certificates are quoted second-hand.
- **Marie-Madeleine's boils.** Three versions: Céline (a veil believed to be Thérèse's), Marie des Anges (Céline tested a veil, about thirty boils), Marie-Madeleine herself (35 boils, two novenas, no veil). See M-EW-193, M-EW-194, M-EW-203.
- **Kitchen reservoir.** Isabelle says four and a half jugs and Lent 1910; Marie-Madeleine says four jugs and summer 1910 (F-EW-2507).
- **Session numbers of Isabelle's deposition.** The editors write 52–53; the headings on the same page say 62–63.
- **Léonie's entry date.** 28 Jan 1899 (Léonie) against 29 Jan 1899 (editors).
- **Dr La Néele's birth year.** 1858 (Piat, 18 Oct 1858) against 1855 (editors of the miracle depositions); death 19 Mar 1916 in both.
- **Marie Guérin's death hour.** The circular puts it at the consecration of a Mass that the chaplain said he would offer at nine; Piat gives 10.15 a.m. (F-EW-2579 and the Piat card F-EW-2653).
- **Marie-Élisabeth's birth year.** 1860 in Piat-type text, 1861 in the page heading (F-EW-2558).
- **The Guérins and the Cause.** Piat says Isidore Guérin did not encourage the Cause and doubted it (F-EW-2658). No letter is quoted, so this is Piat's judgement only.
- **Left unread on purpose (size).** Circulars and biographies of Mère Agnès (about 330k), Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (about 330k plus 105k), Sr Geneviève (about 300k, 235k, 173k), Mère Geneviève the foundress (194k), Gonzague biography read earlier. Marie de l'Eucharistie's "Souvenirs" and Marie's nineteen health bulletins are cited by Piat but are not on the site.
- **Not verified against images.** Nothing was checked against page images. All French wording comes from the Carmel site's text.
