# Batch I — community pages: notes préparatoires, témoignages, souvenirs, circulars

Base URL for all sub-pages: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/ (each Source line gives the full path). All these texts are "facts-only" under the rights rule; quotations are kept short.

Dating note: several "notes préparatoires" were written in answer to Bishop Lemonnier's request of 15 Oct 1907 (feast of St Teresa) for written impressions (see I-F120). Others (Marie des Anges, Marthe de Jésus, Thérèse de St Augustin "notes") speak of the 1914–18 war, of Benedict XV and of 1912 statistics, so they were written c.1913–1916 for the Apostolic Process.

## A. Cool, critical or dissenting voices

### I-F01 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus saw "nothing to put her on the altars"
- Claim: In a letter to the bishop, Sr Thérèse de Jésus wrote that she had never seen anything in Thérèse that could justify raising her to the altars. She described her as a good child with an easy character, who had imperfections "like everyone".
- When / where: letter dated 9 Nov 1907, answering Bishop Lemonnier's inquiry of 15 Oct 1907; Lisieux Carmel period 1888–1897
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus (choir nun of Polish origin, later left the community); Mgr Lemonnier, bishop of Bayeux
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de Jésus, fellow nun for 9 years 6 months; her own observation
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-therese-de-jesus/temoignage-de-soeur-therese-de-jesus/ (letter extracts)
- Original words: « Je n'ai jamais rien vu qui puisse la faire mettre sur les autels. »
- Translation: "I never saw anything that could get her put on the altars."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — the Carmel prints the letter text itself
- Notes: The page says she did not testify at the process because she had left the community by then. The Carmel prints only "extracts"; we do not know what was cut.

### I-F02 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus: an ordinary, never-overloaded day
- Claim: The same nun described Thérèse's life as plain routine: she got up, filled her small day, and was never overburdened. She said she loved her "because of her youth", and that she was a kind child who did not hurt anyone and liked to help.
- When / where: letter of 9 Nov 1907; about 1888–1897
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de Jésus, own observation
- Source: same URL as I-F01
- Original words: « Se levant le matin, remplissant sa petite journée, jamais surchargée. »
- Translation: "Getting up in the morning, filling her little day, never overloaded."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — direct text
- Notes: Directly contradicts witnesses who stress her heavy work and hidden suffering (e.g. Marie des Anges, I-F71).

### I-F03 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus: a child "fêted, pampered, adulated"
- Claim: She wrote that she saw a child who was celebrated, spoiled and flattered, always put on a pedestal, and that the prioress (Mère Marie de Gonzague) was infatuated with her.
- When / where: letter of 9 Nov 1907; 1888–1897
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de Jésus, own observation
- Source: same URL as I-F01
- Original words: « J'ai vu une enfant fêtée, choyée, adulée, toujours mise sur le pinacle. La mère prieure en raffolait. »
- Translation: "I saw a child who was fêted, pampered, adulated, always set on a pedestal. The mother prioress doted on her."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — direct text
- Notes: Contradicts Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Marie des Anges and Thérèse de St Augustin, who say Gonzague was cold and severe to her (I-F40, I-F41, I-F44). Marie des Anges herself admits that "several sisters" believed Thérèse was spoiled (I-F43).

### I-F04 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus: the Martin sisters treated her as a "phoenix"
- Claim: She wrote that Thérèse's own sisters treated her like a phoenix, kept paying her compliments and telling her she was a saint. She privately called them "imprudent", and said Thérèse was an "incensed" child.
- When / where: letter of 9 Nov 1907; 1888–1897
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus; Martin sisters in Carmel (Mère Agnès, Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur, later Sr Geneviève)
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de Jésus, own observation
- Source: same URL as I-F01
- Original words: « Les soeurs de la petite Thérèse la considéraient comme un phénix... Ah! je me disais: les imprudentes. »
- Translation: "Little Thérèse's sisters regarded her as a phoenix... Ah, I said to myself: the imprudent women."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — direct text
- Notes: Contrasts with Marthe, Marie de Jésus and Thérèse de St Augustin, who say Thérèse avoided her sisters at recreation (I-F75, I-F160).

### I-F05 — Her cut hair kept as relics during her lifetime
- Claim: Sr Thérèse de Jésus said that when Thérèse's hair was cut, it was kept like relics. She concluded that it is easy to be amiable when one is adulated.
- When / where: letter of 9 Nov 1907; probably at hair-cuttings in Carmel, 1888–1897 (undated)
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus; unnamed sisters who kept the hair
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de Jésus, own observation (she does not say who kept the hair)
- Source: same URL as I-F01
- Original words: « Quand on lui coupait les cheveux on les conservait comme des reliques. Il est facile d'être aimable quand on est adulée. »
- Translation: "When her hair was cut, they kept it like relics. It is easy to be pleasant when one is adulated."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high for the statement; medium for the fact — no other witness here confirms who kept the hair
- Notes: A strong sign that a cult of Thérèse existed inside the Martin circle before her death.

### I-F06 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus: Polish origin, left before the process
- Claim: Sr Thérèse de Jésus was of Polish origin. From 1899 she prayed for a Polish translation of Histoire d'une âme. She did not testify at the process because she had left the community.
- When / where: 1899 (Gonzague letter to the Carmel of Przemyśl, 9 Aug 1899); left before 1910
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus; Mère Marie de Gonzague; Carmel of Przemyśl
- Witness: modern Carmel editors
- Source: same URL as I-F01, introduction
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — editors' note citing a dated letter
- Notes: So her cool opinion came from a nun who still supported the spread of the book in 1899.

### I-F10 — Sr St Jean de la Croix opposed her entry, then changed her mind
- Claim: Sr St Jean de la Croix opposed Thérèse's entry because she thought her too young to understand Carmelite life. After the entry she said she was astonished and that Thérèse "outdid them all". This older nun later asked Thérèse for spiritual advice.
- When / where: 1887–1888 and after
- People: Sr St Jean de la Croix
- Witness: modern Carmel editors quoting the nun's words (source of the quotation not given)
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-st-jean-de-la-croix/temoignage-de-soeur-st-jean-de-la-croix-sur-therese/
- Original words: « Soeur Thérèse de l'Enfant-Jésus est extraordinaire, elle nous en remontre à toutes. »
- Translation: "Sister Thérèse of the Child Jesus is extraordinary; she shows us all up."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — quotation not referenced
- Notes: Same phrase "elle vous en remontre à toutes" is put in Abbé Youf's mouth by Marie des Anges (I-F95). Possibly a stock community phrase.

- Witness: Thérèse's own letter, cited by editors
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-st-vincent-de-paul/temoignage-de-soeur-st-vincent-de-paul/
- New to English readers: partial — the letter is in English editions, but the name link is rarely made
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### I-F13 — "Not only not a saint, not even a good religious"
- Claim: Late July 1897, Thérèse had an overwhelming disgust for food. Sr St Vincent de Paul brought her a cup of meat juice, which Thérèse refused. Back in the kitchen, the lay sister said that Thérèse was not a saint and not even a good religious.
- When / where: end of July 1897; infirmary and kitchen
- People: Sr St Vincent de Paul; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès in her Ordinary Process deposition (as cited by the editors); she heard it reported
- Source: same URL as I-F12
- Original words: « Sœur Thérèse de l'Enfant-Jésus non seulement n'est pas une sainte mais elle n'est même pas une bonne religieuse. »
- Translation: "Sister Thérèse of the Child Jesus is not only not a saint, she is not even a good nun."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium — second-hand, but reported by a sworn witness
- Notes: Thérèse de St Augustin reports a very similar phrase from an unnamed nun, relayed to Thérèse on her deathbed (I-F23). They are probably the same remark.

### I-F14 — Sr St Vincent de Paul's sneer at the laundry: "a good-for-nothing"
- Claim: A kitchen sister who disliked Thérèse spoke of her with contempt. When she saw her coming to the wash, she said: look at her coming, she's in no hurry, when will she start washing? She is a good-for-nothing. Thérèse, who heard or read it in her eyes, entered the laundry and gave her a lovely smile.
- When / where: undated (1890s); laundry (buanderie)
- People: "Sr St V...." (almost certainly Sr St Vincent de Paul); Thérèse
- Witness: Sr Marie-Madeleine du St-Sacrement (novice), heard/saw it
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-marie-madeleine-du-saint-sacrement/notes-preparatoires-de-sr-marie-madeleine/
- Original words: « Voyez-la venir, elle ne se presse pas ! Quand va-t-elle commencer à laver ?.. C'est une bonne à rien ! »
- Translation: "Look at her coming, she's in no hurry! When is she going to start washing? She's a good-for-nothing!"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — eyewitness; name abbreviated in the source
- Notes: The name is written "Sr St V....". The identification is ours but very likely.

- People: Sr Marie-Philomène de Jésus (entered at 45, older novice when Thérèse arrived)
- Witness: Sr Marie-Philomène, own observation

### I-F23 — "We can't even say she is a good religious" — reported to her on her deathbed
- Claim: A sister visiting the sick Thérèse told her how little she was loved and valued. She reported overhearing one nun say to another: I don't know why people talk so much of Sr Thérèse; she does nothing remarkable; one does not see her practise virtue; one cannot even call her a good religious. Thérèse told Sr Thérèse de St Augustin this made her very happy.
- When / where: summer 1897, infirmary
- People: an unnamed visiting sister; two unnamed nuns; Thérèse; Sr Thérèse de St Augustin
- Witness: Sr Thérèse de St Augustin, heard it from Thérèse
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-therese-de-st-augustin/souvenirs-dune-sainte-amitie/ §13; also notes préparatoires, HUMILITE
- Original words: « elle ne fait rien de remarquable, on ne la voit point pratiquer la vertu, on ne peut même pas dire que ce soit une bonne religieuse »
- Translation: "she does nothing remarkable, one never sees her practising virtue, one cannot even say she is a good nun"
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — two texts by the same witness agree
- Notes: The "Souvenirs" add that the visiting sister told Thérèse "how little you are loved and appreciated here". The notes say only that the words "were reported" to her. Compare I-F13.

## B. Mère Marie de Gonzague and Thérèse

### I-F28 — Gonzague's first verdict: "everything is perfect"
- Claim: Shortly after the entry, Mère Marie de Gonzague wrote to Mme Guérin that she would never have believed such advanced judgment at 15, and that there was nothing to say to her: everything was perfect.
- When / where: letter of 17 May 1888
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mme Céline Guérin
- Witness: Gonzague's letter, cited by editors
- Source: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/mere-marie-de-gonzague/mere-marie-de-gonzague-et-therese/
- Original words: « pas un mot à lui dire, tout est parfait »
- Translation: "not a word to say to her, everything is perfect"
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Editors say the prioress was deliberately severe with her, following the formation methods of the time.

### I-F29 — Gonzague's notes of Dec 1888–Jan 1889
- Claim: Gonzague sent Thérèse affectionate notes. She called her "my benjamin, my little grain of sand" and "the child of my tenderness", told her not to give way to sadness over the delayed clothing, and joked that a humiliation was worth "all the treasures of Panama".
- When / where: notes of Dec 1888 (10 and 13 Dec) and 7 Jan 1889
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Thérèse
- Witness: Gonzague's own notes, cited by editors
- Source: same URL as I-F28
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The "Panama" joke refers to the Panama scandal then in the news. She also advised her to make a "little parcel" of her miseries and place it in the Heart of Jesus.
