## Part 1 — Witness 3, Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin)

### C-F01 — Editors: Marie's role and later life
- Claim: The editors say Marie was Thérèse's godmother, her "angel" in the novitiate, the Carmel's provisoire (in charge of food and meals) from 1894 to 1933, and later crippled by joint rheumatism; she died 19 Jan 1940. In January 1908 she gave Mère Agnès a report on Thérèse's virtues that Bishop Lemonnier had asked for.
- When / where: 1860–1940; Lisieux Carmel
- People: Marie Martin (Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur); Mère Agnès; Mgr Lemonnier
- Witness: editors' note (Teresianum 1973), not testimony
- Source: PO 3, editors' introduction, before f. 304r
- Original words: —
- Translation: —
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — editorial fact summary
- Notes: The editors call her deposition "sober and very studied". Her 1908 written report means she had prepared a text before she testified.

### C-F03 — Marie started the writing of Manuscript A
- Claim: One winter evening after Matins, Marie, Thérèse, Céline and Mère Agnès (then prioress) were warming themselves together. Thérèse told two or three stories from her childhood. Marie then pushed Agnès to order Thérèse to write down her childhood memories, instead of only writing little poems for the sisters. Agnès hesitated, then agreed and told Thérèse to give her the story for her feast day.
- When / where: winter, probably 1894–95 (Céline was already in Carmel); Lisieux Carmel, after Matins
- People: Marie; Thérèse; Sr Geneviève (Céline); Mère Agnès (Pauline), prioress
- Witness: Marie — SAW and TOOK PART
- Source: PO 3, f. 304v–305r, S22, 6 Sept 1910
- Original words: « Vous verrez, c'est un ange qui restera pas longtemps sur la terre, nous aurons perdu tous ces détails »
- Translation: "You will see, she is an angel who will not stay long on earth; we will have lost all these details."
- New to English readers: partial (the request is known; this scene and Marie's words are not in Story of a Soul)
- Confidence: high — Marie's own act
- Notes: Céline (C-F120) says Agnès then put the finished notebook aside without reading it.

### C-F05 — Manuscript C was Agnès's idea; Manuscript B was Marie's

### C-F06 — Gonzague had the manuscript altered so it seemed all addressed to her
- Claim: When the manuscript was printed, Mère Marie de Gonzague, then prioress, wanted it to look as if the whole text had been written to her. So some forms of address and some private family details were scratched out. Marie says this did not change the substance, and the original text was restored after Gonzague's death.
- When / where: 1898 (first edition); restoration after Gonzague's death (Dec 1904)
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Marie
- Witness: Marie — first-hand (she lived through it)
- Source: PO 3, f. 305v, S22, 6 Sept 1910
- Original words: « voulut qu'il semblât lui avoir été adressé tout entier »
- Translation: "wanted it to seem that it had been addressed entirely to her"
- New to English readers: partial (known to scholars; not in the standard life)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Céline (C-F124) agrees and adds that Gonzague signed the manuscript and that Marie herself did the restoration.

### C-F08 — The parents' early "Mass of the poor" and frequent communion

At Lisieux, Louis received communion four or five times a week. Zélie kept Lent without the permitted relaxations.
- When / where: Alençon and Lisieux, 1860s–1880s
- People: Louis Martin; Zélie Martin
- Witness: Marie — saw it as their daughter
- Source: PO 3, f. 306v, S22
- Original words: « parce qu'ils disaient que c'était la messe des pauvres »
- Translation: "because they said it was the Mass of the poor"
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Céline (f. 335v) adds that both parents fasted all Lent and said the new relaxations "were not made for good Christians".

### C-F15 — "Practices" counted on a homemade string of beads

Her practices were mostly giving way to her sister, which cost her effort, because she was then very headstrong.
- When / where: c. 1877, Alençon/Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Céline; a neighbour (unnamed)
- Witness: Marie — SAW

- New to English readers: partial (the beads are known from other sources; the neighbour is new)

## Miracles and extraordinary events — Marie (PO 3)

### C-M01 — Cure of Thérèse by the Virgin's smile (Buissonnets, May 1883)
- Period: during life
- What was reported: After the "poison" crisis, Marie knelt with her sisters before the statue of the Virgin and prayed three times. At the third time Thérèse stared at the statue, her look radiant "as in ecstasy", for four to five minutes; two tears fell; then she looked at Marie tenderly. From that moment no trace of the illness remained. She told Marie she had seen the Virgin herself.
- The person helped: Thérèse, aged 10
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie (eyewitness); Léonie and Céline present
- Status: sworn testimony at the Ordinary Process; not an approved canonization miracle
- Source: PO 3, f. 311v–312r and 323r, S23 and S25
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: hallucinations, falls from bed, head-banging, loss of voice, showers prescribed; doctor said "science is powerless"
- Doubts / other versions: Céline dates the cure 10 May (usual date 13 May 1883). Marie calls the illness diabolical; Céline notes religious objects never frightened her, unlike usual demonic cases.

### C-M02 — Violet perfume in Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph's cell (1 Oct 1897)
- Period: after death
- What was reported: On the evening of Friday 1 Oct 1897, the day after the death, Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph found her cell filled with a scent of violets so strong she thought someone had left a bouquet and searched everywhere for it.
- The person helped: Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph, a sister to whom Thérèse had shown great charity
- Witnesses and reporter: reported by Marie (not present)
- Status: sworn testimony, hearsay
- Source: PO 3, f. 330v, S26
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: none
- Doubts / other versions: Céline says perfumes began for her only in 1908 and "had not occurred in the 11 years after the death" — at least for her (C-M09).

### C-M03 — Prophetic vision of her father (c. 1879)
- Period: during life
- What was reported: About age six Thérèse saw her father, then away, walking in the garden bent with age and head covered with a kind of veil. Marie heard her call "Papa, papa". Ten years later, in the first attacks of his cerebral illness, Louis covered his head with his handkerchief.
- The person helped: none (prophecy)
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie heard the cry; Céline also testifies to the handkerchief
- Status: sworn testimony; interpretation by the family
- Source: PO 3, f. 322v–323r; PO 4, f. 394v–395r
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: Louis's "paralysie cérébrale"
- Doubts / other versions: Marie dates the vision 1879, "ten years before the event".

### C-M04 — Fruit from Brest, as promised for the blackbirds
- Period: after death
- What was reported: The year after her death a case of pears and a basket of strawberries arrived, sent by the superior of the Hospital of Brest, whom the Carmel did not know, "to please Sr Thérèse". The gift came again every year.
- The person helped: the Carmel (Marie, provisoire)
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie
- Status: sworn testimony; presented as a fulfilled promise
- Source: PO 3, f. 323v–324r, S25
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: none
- Doubts / other versions: The Brest superior's motive is given as her own words; natural explanation possible (the first edition had appeared in 1898).

### C-M05 — The cauldron that filled itself (Sr Jeanne-Marie)
- Period: after death
- What was reported: A lay-sister novice, Sr Jeanne-Marie, tired and overworked, asked Thérèse to help her. The cook needed a completely empty cauldron filled. Sr Jeanne-Marie, on a ladder cleaning windows, at first thought of leaving it; then came down, cleaned it, and poured in the first jug. Four jugs were needed; when she came back with the second, the cauldron was full.
- The person helped: Sr Jeanne-Marie de l'Enfant-Jésus
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie (hearsay); Céline also reports it (C-M11)
- Status: sworn testimony, hearsay
- Source: PO 3, f. 331r, S26
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: none
- Doubts / other versions: Céline: filled "when she had poured barely a quarter". Consistent with Marie's "one jug of four".
