## A. Jeanne Guérin, Mme La Néele (PO-20, 28 March 1911; PA-23, 12 March 1917)

### G-F01 — Jeanne admits she did not see Thérèse's holiness in life
- Claim: Jeanne says that in the first years after Thérèse's death she did not expect the spread of "the supernatural". This spread showed her a holiness she had not known was so great.
- When / where: 1897–c.1905, Caen/Lisieux
- People: Jeanne Guérin, Mme La Néele (1868–1938), first cousin
- Witness: Jeanne herself, about her own view
- Source: PO-20, Jeanne Guérin, [1145v], Session 71, 28 March 1911
- Original words: « une sainteté que je ne savais pas si grande »
- Translation: "a holiness that I did not know was so great"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — direct sworn statement
- Notes: In PA-23 [1357] she says the opposite in part: already in childhood she thought Thérèse's perfection "quite extraordinary". The two statements do not fully agree.

### G-F02 — Jeanne says Thérèse sends her crosses, not consolations
- Claim: Jeanne prays to Thérèse but says Thérèse sends her crosses rather than consolations. In 1917 she says this makes her hesitant to ask Thérèse for graces; she prays the Magnificat at the tomb instead.
- When / where: 1911 and 1917, Lisieux cemetery
- People: Jeanne La Néele
- Witness: Jeanne herself
- Source: PO-20 [1145v], Session 71, 28 March 1911; PA-23 [1350] and [1360], Session 69, 12 March 1917
- Original words: « comme elle ne m'a envoyé que des croix, cela me rend hésitante »
- Translation: "since she has sent me nothing but crosses, that makes me hesitant"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — said twice, at both processes
- Notes: At [1360] she adds that each time she called on Thérèse in temporal troubles she saw "an increase of crosses and trials". Context: Jeanne never had the child Thérèse promised to obtain (editors' note, PA-23).

### G-F03 — Thérèse promised Jeanne a child; none came
- Claim: Thérèse wrote to Jeanne hoping God would send her a child (1891), and in mid-October 1895 said she had "intimate confidence" a little angel would come. Jeanne stayed childless. The editors say Thérèse promised to get this grace from heaven, and it never came.
- When / where: 17 Oct 1891; mid-Oct 1895; letters to Caen
- People: Jeanne and Dr Francis La Néele
- Witness: editors' note (PO-20 intro; PA-23 intro)
- Source: PO-20 editors' introduction; PA-23 editors' introduction
- Original words: « tu recevras un centuple plus abondant, un petit ange viendra réjouir ton foyer »
- Translation: "you will receive a hundredfold more; a little angel will come to gladden your home"
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — letters quoted by editors
- Notes: The editors say Thérèse also urged total abandonment to Providence (letter of 16 July 1897 to M. and Mme Guérin).

### G-F11 — The 1883 illness: symptoms in detail, and Isidore Guérin blamed the devil
- Claim: The illness began at the Guérins' house, while M. Martin was away, with violent trembling first taken for fever. Then came depression and a half-hallucinated state: objects and people's postures looked frightening to her. At its peak there were motor fits with rotating movements of the whole body she could never have done when well. Dr Notta would not name the illness. Isidore Guérin, a pharmacist, could not believe it was natural and saw the devil's action.
- When / where: spring 1883, Guérin house, Lisieux
- People: Dr Notta; Isidore Guérin; Louis Martin (absent)
- Witness: Jeanne, SAW it
- Source: PA-23 [1352–1353], Session 69, 12 March 1917
- Original words: « des mouvements rotatoires de tout le corps, dont elle eût été absolument incapable en état de santé »
- Translation: "rotating movements of the whole body that she could never have done in good health"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — detailed eyewitness
- Notes: Isidore argued that a nervous illness left to run its course would have left traces later; none appeared. Jeanne cannot remember whether anyone spoke of an apparition of the Virgin at the time; she only later often heard she was cured by the Virgin.

### G-F15 — School life was painful; the school had little influence
- Claim: At the Benedictine Abbey school Thérèse was a good, obedient pupil, kind to classmates, but did not join their games willingly and kept a reserve. Boarding-school life, in contrast to home, was rather painful for her. Jeanne thinks Pauline formed her, and the school had little influence.

- Witness: Jeanne, fellow pupil
- Source: PA-23 [1353–1354]
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Jeanne noticed "nothing very particular" at her First Communion.

### G-F17 — Jeanne thought Thérèse entered Carmel to rejoin Pauline
- Claim: When Thérèse entered Carmel at 15, Jeanne privately thought she went there to join Pauline, whom she loved so much. Her parents did not agree with her; they said the child was predestined.
- When / where: April 1888
- People: Isidore and Céline Guérin

- Source: PO-20 [1147r], Session 71
- Original words: « je pensai au fond de moi-même qu'elle entrait dans ce couvent pour y rejoindre Pauline »
- Translation: "deep down I thought she was entering that convent to join Pauline"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — a candid, dissenting view
- Notes: In PA-23 [1354] she says she was not surprised by the vocation, only by how early she entered. She learned of the decision from her father, whom Thérèse came to consult.

- Witness: Jeanne
- Source: PO-20 [1147r]; PA-23 [1355]

- Claim: Isidore Guérin was a pharmacist; his wife was Céline Fournet. Their daughters were Jeanne (b. 1868) and Marie (b. 1870, later Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie, d. 1905). They employed a young maid-nursemaid, Marcelline Husé, from 1880. Thérèse came to their house daily; the two families met every Thursday and Sunday. Les Buissonnets was about 1 km away.
- When / where: 1877–1888, Lisieux
- Witness: Jeanne La Néele; Marcelline Husé
- Source: PO-20 [1144v]; PA-23 [1349], [1351]; PO-08 [510v]
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Family and social world.

### G-F184 — Opposition: Abbey nuns who saw self-love and "susceptibility"

## Gaps and conflicts

- Pichon's claim that the teenage Thérèse told him "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth" word for word, several times, before Carmel (PO-10 [553r]) is flagged by the editors with "? ? ?". The saying is dated 17 July 1897. Likely a false memory.
- Pichon's dates are unreliable: he met the family "about 1880/1881" (editors: 17 April 1882); the 1888 retreat "in autumn" (editors: May 1888). He speaks of "a few letters" from her; she wrote that she sent him twelve for each of his. His two accounts of his 1909 cure differ (Mass after 6–7 days vs 4 days). Thérèse said he "treated me too much like a child" (editors, DE 4 July 1897).
- Jeanne La Néele contradicts herself: 1911 — she never suspected Thérèse's holiness and thought she entered Carmel to join Pauline; 1917 — she saw "quite extraordinary" perfection already in childhood.
- Jeanne (not expansive, spoke little, rarely played) versus Marcelline Husé (very joyful and expansive at home and with the maid). Different settings; both may be true.
- The 1883 illness: Marcelline's 1910 version (fright, hallucinations several times a day, lucid) is stronger than her 1917 version (feverish, nervous, "not delirium"). Jeanne gives the most physical detail (rotating movements) and says Dr Notta would not name it; in 1911 she says the doctor called it St Vitus' dance. Isidore Guérin blamed the devil. Marcelline dates the cure "Sunday 10 May" (the Sunday was 13 May 1883). Neither Jeanne nor Marcelline heard of an apparition at the time; Pichon says the sisters' letters told him of the smile of the Virgin "at that very time".
- School: "best marks" (Marcelline) vs "a little weak in arithmetic and spelling" and "classmates who equalled her" (her own teacher). The "persecution" at school is confirmed but played down by her teacher as "exaggeration".
- Why she left school: headaches (Jeanne, Marcelline), health given by her father (Sr Saint-André), health plus scruples (Sr Saint-François). The nuns insist she was not sent away, which suggests a rumour.
- Frequency of her post-school visits (1–2 vs 2–3 a week) and time in the chapel gallery (1–1.5 vs 1–2 hours) differ between Benedictine witnesses. Who spoke at her Child of Mary admission differs (Sr Sainte-Placide vs "the president of the council").
- Marguerite Leroy's ages and dates differ between 1911 and 1917 (six years older / aged 14; 1882–84 / 1881–83; Thérèse aged 9 / about 10). She honestly doubts whether the "I hide by my bed and think" saying was told to her or only retold in the community. Sr Saint-François says it was told to her.
- Roulland's letter count differs (about eight in 1911, about six in 1917).
- Dumaine's slips: Zélie died "1887" (1877); Domin: the Martins came to Lisieux "1887" (1877); Ducellier: returned "1889" and "two years after her death" (1899).
- The task list calls PA-01 "Mgr Lemonnier, bishop". The file is Fr Armand-Constant Lemonnier of La Délivrande, retreat preacher, not the bishop.
- The PA-25 file also contains two co-witnesses ex officio (Sr Jeanne-Marie de l'Enfant-Jésus; Pierre Derrien, the Carmel's sacristan) and the start of the editors' introduction to Fr Godefroy Madelaine's Namur process; it breaks off mid-sentence. Both co-witnesses were Carmel insiders or employees, relevant for weighing their graces.
- Sober testimony on death: Jeanne La Néele, Domin and Maupas all saw nothing extraordinary at the body, funeral or burial. No perfume or light at death is reported in this batch.
- Expected but absent: no witness here gives her last words or the last hour; Dr de Cornière appears only via a letter; Dr La Néele's own observations of her illness are only summarised by his wife.
- Several witnesses say they were not influenced (standard question); Jeanne adds "it would have been useless". No clear sign of coaching, but many "I only confirm Story of a Soul" answers.

## Batch summary

- Fact cards: G-F01 to G-F202 (202)
- Miracle/grace entries: G-M01 to G-M21 (21)
- Scenes: G-S01 to G-S12 (12)
- Quotes: G-Q01 to G-Q14 (14)

Five best finds:
1. Valadier (PO-11): Pranzini could not have "seized" the crucifix because his arms were tied; he begged for it and kissed it (chaplain Faure's first-hand account).
2. Dr de Cornière, after reading "Vivre d'amour", said he would never cure her: "a soul not made for earth" (Marie Guérin's letter, PO-20).
3. Named dissent: Benedictine mistresses who saw self-love or "susceptibility", one who rejected "mystical holinesses", a mistress who criticised the Martins' upbringing, a Fall River nun who found her piety "childish and superficial", and Lemonnier's report that Gonzague opposed the Martin family and Mère Agnès.
4. The school scene "Thérèse has no conscience!" followed by 15 days of tears and "I have sinned and made someone sin" (PO-30); plus her teacher's note that she was weak in spelling and arithmetic.
5. Pichon's doubtful claim that she said "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth" to him as a teenager (editors flag it), and Thérèse's own remark that Pichon "treated me too much like a child".
