## Part 1 — Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (PA witness 7)

### D-F01 — Marie thought the Roman Articles made Thérèse too "saintly" and planned to correct them
- Claim: On 13 March 1915, before testifying, Marie wrote to Léonie that the Roman advocate had failed to draw a portrait simple enough, and that the sisters would "put it right", since each saint must resemble herself.
- When / where: letter of 13 March 1915; Lisieux Carmel to Léonie at the Caen Visitation
- People: Marie Martin; Léonie Martin (Sr Françoise-Thérèse, Visitandine; the editors first wrote "Poor Clare", then corrected); the postulator's advocate in Rome
- Witness: editors' note (quoting S. Piat, "Une âme libre, Marie")
- Source: PA 7, Marie du Sacré-Cœur, editors' introduction
- Original words: « L'avocat de Rome n'a pas su faire un portrait assez simple, tout en étant le portrait d'une sainte. Nous saurons bien le remettre au point »
- Translation: "The Roman lawyer did not manage to paint a portrait simple enough while still being a saint's portrait. We will be sure to set it right."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — letter quoted by the editors
- Notes: Shows the Martin sisters consciously shaping the testimony against the official Articles. At [556] Marie swears "no one influenced me in preparing my testimony". Compare Geneviève, who also rejects Articles 239–242 and 244 as exaggerated (see Part 2).

- Source: PA 7, [558], Session 22
- New to English readers: partial

- When / where: August 1877, Alençon; later remark in Carmel, undated
- People: Thérèse; Marie; Zélie
- Witness: Marie, saw it and heard Thérèse say it
- Source: PA 7, [561], Session 22

### D-F15 — Dr Notta: "not hysteria"

### D-F16 — Symptoms of the 1883 illness
- Claim: The smallest objects looked like horrible monsters to her and she screamed. She was often pushed by an unknown force to throw herself head first from her bed onto the tiled floor. Other times she hit her head violently against the bed's wooden frame. Sometimes she tried to speak to Marie but only mouthed the words without sound. Between crises she was exhausted.
- When / where: Buissonnets, April–May 1883
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, saw it
- Source: PA 7, [563], Session 22
- New to English readers: yes (Story of a Soul gives only the hallucination of monsters)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: At [604] Marie adds that she never hurt herself in these blows.

### D-F17 — The strange kneeling posture, yet always covered
- Claim: Several times Thérèse suddenly knelt without using her hands, rested her head on the bed and tried to bring her feet forward. In this posture she should have been uncovered, yet she always stayed modestly wrapped. Marie took this as heavenly protection.
- When / where: Buissonnets, spring 1883
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, saw it
- Source: PA 7, [563], Session 22; again [604], Session 25
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high for the observation; interpretation is Marie's
- Notes: Geneviève describes "strange gymnastics" with modesty never offended.

### D-F19 — Marie's account of the cure by the Virgin
- Claim: Marie and her sisters knelt before the statue of the Virgin. Marie repeated the same prayer three times. At the third, Thérèse fixed the statue with a radiant, ecstatic look for four or five minutes. Two large tears fell; then she looked tenderly at Marie, cured. Alone later, Marie asked why she wept; after hesitation Thérèse said it was because she no longer saw her.
- When / where: Buissonnets, 10 May 1883
- People: Thérèse; Marie; Léonie; Céline
- Witness: Marie, saw it and heard Thérèse's explanation
- Source: PA 7, [563]–[564], Session 22
- Original words: « C'est parce que je ne la voyais plus »
- Translation: "It is because I could not see her any more."
- New to English readers: partial (Story of a Soul tells the cure, not the tears or this exchange)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: See D-M02.

### D-F20 — Uncle Guérin's advice not to thwart her, and Marie ignored it
- Claim: After the cure, Isidore Guérin, pharmacist, told Marie to take great care never to thwart the child. Marie says she did thwart her when needed, and nothing bad ever followed. No trace of the illness ever returned; she was neither impressionable nor nervous.
- When / where: Lisieux, after May 1883
- People: Isidore Guérin; Marie; Thérèse
- Witness: Marie, heard the advice; her own behaviour
- Source: PA 7, [564], Session 22
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Counters any "nervous" reading of her later life.

### D-F21 — Thérèse heard everything during the crises
- Claim: Marie is certain Thérèse kept the use of her higher faculties. Thérèse later told her she heard and understood all that was said around her, and that during the last great crisis, which lasted about one hour, she never stopped praying inwardly to the Virgin.
- When / where: 1883; told later, undated
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, observed it and heard Thérèse say it
- Source: PA 7, [564]–[565], Session 22
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high

### D-F22 — She wanted to run to the bishop in the street
- Claim: Meeting Bishop Hugonin in a Lisieux street, the child wanted to run to him and ask leave to make her First Communion early, because the rule made her wait a year for a matter of two days (born 2 January, cut-off 1 January).
- When / where: Lisieux street, c. 1882–83
- People: Thérèse; Mgr Flavien Hugonin, bishop of Bayeux and Lisieux
- Witness: Marie, saw/heard it
- Source: PA 7, [565], Session 22
- New to English readers: yes

- When / where: Buissonnets, 1884
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, her own decision
- Source: PA 7, [566], Session 22

### D-F24 — Scruples, and prayers to her dead brothers and sisters
- Claim: Scruples began during the retreat for her second solemn communion at the Abbey (1885). Marie calmed her; the "faults" were trifles. When Marie entered Carmel (15 Oct 1886), Thérèse had no one to confide in and prayed to her four siblings in heaven, and soon found peace. Before Carmel she took Communion three or four times a week.
- When / where: 1885–1886, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, saw it
- Source: PA 7, [566]–[567], Session 22
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: At [599] Marie adds she followed Marie's advice exactly in the time of scruples.

### D-F25 — Marie would gladly have blocked Thérèse's entry
- Claim: Opposition: When Thérèse, at 14, announced in the parlour her wish to enter the next year, Marie left it to Pauline to encourage her. For her part, Marie would willingly have put obstacles in the way, because of their father and of the child's age, but her conscience stopped her, so she said nothing. Uncle Guérin and Canon Delatroëtte opposed it "with all their strength".
- When / where: Carmel parlour, 1887
- People: Marie; Pauline (Mother Agnès); Isidore Guérin; Canon Jean-Baptiste Delatroëtte, ecclesiastical superior
- Witness: Marie, her own attitude
- Source: PA 7, [567], Session 22
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Dissenting family attitude, candidly admitted.

### D-F26 — Mother Marie de Gonzague was cold to the postulant
- Claim: Opposition: Marie says the prioress, Mother Marie de Gonzague, showed little sympathy for the young postulant, often scolded her, or paid her no attention. The prioress was used to being flattered by everyone; Thérèse did not seek her favour that way, so she went unnoticed, or received only coldness.
- When / where: Lisieux Carmel, from 1888
- People: Mother Marie de Gonzague, prioress; Thérèse
- Witness: Marie, saw it (in the community)
- Source: PA 7, [568], Session 22
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium-high — Marie's judgment, consistent with Geneviève
- Notes: Marie also lists "almost constant dryness" as a hidden trial.

### D-F27 — "Papa le bon Dieu" and "a baby who is an old man"
- Claim: Near the end of her life Marie heard Thérèse call the Virgin "maman" and God "Papa le bon Dieu". When Marie asked, "So you are a baby?", Thérèse became grave and said yes, but a baby who thinks a great deal, a baby who is an old man.
- When / where: infirmary, 1897
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Marie, heard it
- Source: PA 7, [572], Session 23, 21 July 1915
- Original words: « Oui... mais un bébé qui en pense bien long! Un bébé qui est un vieillard »

- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Also: « Laissez faire 'Papa le bon Dieu', il sait bien ce qu'il faut à son tout petit bébé. »

### D-F29 — "When I am in heaven I will make you change your mind" (daily Communion)
- Claim: Opposition: Some time before her death, Thérèse told Mother Marie de Gonzague, who opposed daily Communion, that from heaven she would make her change her mind. Marie says this happened. Marie also thinks Thérèse told the sisters there would be a change in Church practice on Communion.
- When / where: Carmel, 1897
- People: Thérèse; Mother Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Marie, "I remember perfectly" (the first); "I think I remember" (the second)

- New to English readers: yes

### D-F30 — The prioress refused a substitute doctor
- Claim: Opposition: A month before her death Thérèse had a very painful crisis. The community doctor was away, and the prioress refused to let another doctor in. When the sisters complained, Thérèse told them not to murmur, since God allowed "our Mother" not to give her relief.
- When / where: infirmary, about 30 August 1897
- People: Thérèse; Mother Marie de Gonzague; Dr de Cornière (absent, not named)
- Witness: Marie, heard it
- Source: PA 7, [574], Session 23
- Original words: « Ne murmurez pas contre la volonté du bon Dieu: c'est Lui qui permet que notre Mère ne me donne pas de soulagement »
- Translation: "Do not murmur against God's will. It is He who allows our Mother not to give me relief."
- New to English readers: partial (Last Conversations)
- Confidence: high for the event as Marie saw it
- Notes: The editors of Geneviève's deposition warn that claims about Gonzague's conduct in the last illness should be checked against Guy Gaucher's work.

### D-M01 — The baby found on a chair (Thérèse, Alençon, 1874)

Marie: she had fallen from her cradle and could not have climbed the chair herself; the mother saw God's intervention. Céline: Zélie had left her in the big bed and forgot to bring the cradle close to stop her falling; she found her on a chair and could not understand how.
- The person helped: Thérèse, about 18 months (Marie)
- Witnesses and reporter: Zélie Martin (not a witness here); reported by Marie and Céline from family tradition
- Status: sworn testimony at the Apostolic Process (family tradition, not eyewitness); not an approved miracle
- Source: PA 7, [560], Session 22; PA 8, [776]–[777], Session 37
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: none
- Doubts / other versions: The two sisters disagree on whether she was in a cradle or in the mother's bed. Marie attributes it to her guardian angel.

### D-M02 — Cure by the Virgin's smile (Thérèse, Buissonnets, 10 May 1883)
- Period: during life
- What was reported: After weeks of terrors, convulsive movements and delirium, Marie, Léonie and Céline prayed before the statue of the Virgin. At Marie's third prayer Thérèse gazed at the statue with a radiant, ecstatic look for 4–5 minutes, shed two large tears, and was cured instantly and permanently. She told Marie she wept "because I could not see her any more". In 1897 she said: today it is the statue; then it was not the statue.
- The person helped: Thérèse, aged 10
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie (present, led the prayer); Céline (present, then 13–14: "I had no doubt the Virgin had appeared to her"); Dr Notta, who said "not hysteria"
- Status: sworn testimony at the Apostolic Process; not one of the approved canonisation miracles
- Source: PA 7, [562]–[565], [604], [615], Sessions 22, 25, 26; PA 8, [637]–[638], [777], Sessions 27, 37
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: daily showers prescribed; hallucinations; head-banging; muteness; about six weeks; no relapse; "science powerless"
- Doubts / other versions: Marie and Céline both call the illness diabolic in origin. Céline suggests grief at losing her "mothers" to Carmel may have contributed.

### D-M13 — Missionary favours (China, Ivory Coast, Tanganyika, 1910–1912)

Van Aken, White Father at Tabora (December 1910), put her picture in huts and catechism halls; after his catechists prayed to her for the country's conversion, pagans came "in whole crowds", including from unvisited, formerly hostile villages; "pessimists" doubted it would last.

## Gaps and conflicts

- **Mental prayer as a child:** Marie says she refused even a quarter of an hour (PA 7 [566]); Céline says Marie allowed a quarter of an hour, spent behind the bed curtains (PA 8 [691]).
- **Baby on the chair:** Marie says Thérèse fell from her cradle; Céline says she was left in the mother's big bed without the cradle nearby (PA 7 [560]; PA 8 [776]).
- **Father's veiled figure:** Marie says the meaning was understood when Thérèse retold it in Carmel; Céline says it was revealed only after the father's death (1894). Marie says "dark cloth", Céline "thick veil".
- **Cousin Marie Guérin:** Marie places Thérèse's refusal to go to the enclosure door at the cousin's profession examination; Céline places it at her clothing. These may be two occasions.
- **First haemoptysis:** Marie gives Holy Thursday 1896; Céline gives Good Friday at [741], Holy Thursday at [782], and "4 April" (wrong date) at [782].
- **Time and manner of death:** Marie gives 7:15 p.m. and says she did not speak from five o'clock except the last words. Céline gives 7:20 p.m. and reports words during the agony ("the cup is full", "allons... allons..."). Marie adds the candle test. Céline adds the last look for herself, and says Gonzague sent the community away.
- **Funeral crowd:** Marie says the crowd was "numerous" (explained by the family's local fame). Céline says many priests came but the procession of faithful was "very small".
- **Remains in 1910:** Marie says a brick-lined vault; Céline says a lead coffin. These are probably compatible.
- **"You are nursing a little saint":** Marie heard it with all three sisters present. Céline is unsure whether she heard it or only heard of it. Elsewhere Thérèse told Céline "I am not a saint" [787].
- **Origin of the last Marian poem:** Marie says it was written "at my request"; Céline quotes Thérèse's "I still have something to do before I die".
- **Date of the "beautiful death" saying:** Marie gives 22 August; the editors' reference points to DE 4 June.
- **Age of drawing refusal:** Marie says about 10; Céline says 9. Céline's age at the cure: 13 at [637], 14 at [777].
- **Mother Marie de Gonzague:** Both sisters, especially Céline, describe jealousy, "frightful scenes", neglect in the illness, refusal of Dr La Néele and of morphine injections. The editors explicitly warn that pp. 649 and 741 must be corrected against Guy Gaucher, and note that Gonzague herself kindly named Céline infirmary aide. Céline also records Gonzague's praise (none like her; wanted her last look to be for Gonzague) and her remorse after the death. Treat the blame as partisan family testimony.
- **Coaching / shaping:** Marie swears no one influenced her, yet her 13 March 1915 letter (editors' note) says the sisters would "set right" the Roman advocate's portrait. Céline's deposition was prepared "to the smallest detail" from notes (editors). Both reject the Articles' extraordinary phenomena (Céline: Articles 34, 216, 239–242, 244). Both repeat several of the same stories and sayings (three minutes without thinking of God; the Virgin helps at once; the 80 years; die in Gonzague's arms), mostly from Mother Agnès's notebook (DE).
- **Contrary opinion:** Both say they know of no opinion against her holiness, yet both report hostility: the lay sister who burned the sandals; Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul "irritated by her virtue"; Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph's insults; an older nun calling her irregular; Fr Blinot calling her aspirations presumption; the chaplain's "very dangerous state"; community jealousy of three Martin sisters. No witness here says "what will we write in her circular".
- **Dissent about the Martin "clan":** Céline admits the community's jealousy of the three sisters and Thérèse's efforts to hide the family tie ("forgive us for living under one roof"; photographs; "do not lead the family life"). Marie shows her own jealousy of Pauline and of a nun.
- **Expected but absent:** Neither names Dr de Cornière's diagnosis in detail. Neither mentions the Diana Vaughan photo and letter Thérèse sent in 1896 (Céline mentions only the rejection of the book). Neither discusses the nights of 1896–97 in the cold cell except in passing. Léonie's view is absent. Marie does not name the sister who burned the alpargates or the neurasthenic sister. Céline gives no detail of what Thérèse "reproached" Gonzague with.
- **Changes vs 1910 (flagged by the witnesses):** Marie: mother's birthplace; adds her feeling of Thérèse's soul the day after death. Céline: father's name, mother's birthplace, return from the wet-nurse (2 April 1874), and a clarification that "no spiritual director" meant no habitual direction. Céline updates offering counts (26 → 321 plaques).

## Batch summary

- Fact cards: 326 (D-F01–D-F110 from Marie, including addendum cards D-F104–D-F110; D-F111–D-F326 from Céline, including addendum cards)
- Miracle / extraordinary entries: 22 (D-M01–D-M22)
- Scenes: 22 (D-S01–D-S22)
- Quotations: 34 (D-Q01–D-Q34)

Five best finds:
1. Céline: the prioress refused Dr La Néele out of jealousy of the family, and morphine had to be given "by stratagem" because Gonzague found relief shameful for a Carmelite (D-F242, D-F243). The editors flag both claims for correction.
2. Mother Marie de Gonzague later wept before a child portrait of Thérèse and said: "I alone know what I owe her... all she reproached me for... but so gently!" (D-F322).
3. Céline: "I would rather she were not beatified than give a false portrait". Marie's letter planned to "set right" the Roman Articles. Both sisters rejected the claims of heavenly odours and ecstasies (D-F277, D-F1, D-F247, D-F146).
4. Vivid human details: the sisters told her she was "not pretty at all"; she liked a peach's velvet skin; her one self-reproach was a sniff of eau de Cologne; she kept nail parings and eyelashes for Céline; she dipped her pens in milk; the discipline at 350 strokes per Miserere (D-F120, D-F227, D-F283, D-F255, D-F232).
5. Candid details of death and burial. The body showed "signs of decomposition" by 4 October. The funeral procession was "very small". The famous epitaph came from a workman smudging Agnès's first inscription. Marie tried to keep the patched sandals, and a lay sister burned them as "filth" (D-F95, D-F313, D-F64).
