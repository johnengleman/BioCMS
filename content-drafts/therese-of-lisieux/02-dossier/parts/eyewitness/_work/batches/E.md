## A. Sr Marie de la Trinité (PO 17 and PA 21)

### E-F01 — Marie de la Trinité's history before Lisieux (editors' note)
- Claim: Marie-Louise Castel, 13th of 19 children, born 12 Aug 1874 at Saint-Pierre-sur-Dives, raised in Paris. She entered the Paris Carmel of avenue de Messine on 30 Apr 1891 (name Agnès de Jésus), took the habit 12 May 1892, and had to leave on 8 July 1893 for health reasons. She entered Lisieux as a postulant on 16 June 1894, first as Marie-Agnès de la Sainte-Face, then renamed Marie de la Trinité in 1896. She was later the first archivist of the monastery and handled the sale of books about Thérèse. She died 16 Jan 1944.
- When / where: 1874–1944; Paris, Lisieux
- People: Marie de la Trinité
- Witness: editors' note (Teresianum), not testimony
- Source: PO 17 introduction; PA 21 introduction
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — editorial biography
- Notes: Her father Victor Castel was a schoolteacher ("instituteur", PA p. 1216).

### E-F02 — Why she was given to Thérèse: a nun under suspicion (editors' note)
- Claim: Fr Piat (quoted by the editors) says her unconventional tone, "street-urchin" manners and her failed first attempt at Carmel made the community distrust her. For this reason she was put under Thérèse, who for a time called her her "only novice". Piat lists a flighty mind, morbid emotionality and disconcerting changeability.
- When / where: 1894–1897, Lisieux
- People: Marie de la Trinité; Thérèse; Fr S.-J. Piat (1964 book)
- Witness: editors' note quoting Piat
- Source: PO 17 introduction (Piat, Sainte Thérèse de Lisieux à la découverte de la voie d'enfance, c. 7, p. 172)
- Original words: « ses allures gavroches »
- Translation: her street-urchin ways
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — a modern author's summary, not a witness
- Notes: MT herself confirms the hostility: see E-F83.

### E-F03 — Thérèse appointed as her "angel" and quasi-mistress
- Claim: When MT entered, the prioress Mère Agnès gave her Thérèse as her "angel" (the Carmel custom for teaching the outward rules). Agnès also told MT to take Thérèse's advice for her formation "as if she were novice mistress". MT sought her conversation and says their relations were "very intimate" for three years and some months.
- When / where: from 16 June 1894, Lisieux
- People: MT; Mère Agnès de Jésus (prioress); Thérèse
- Witness: MT, direct experience
- Source: PO 17, f. 1066v, Session 64, 13 Mar 1911; PA 21, p. 1216, Session 61, 22 Sept 1916
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — first-hand, consistent in both processes
- Notes: PO says Agnès charged Thérèse "above all to correct us of our faults".

### E-F04 — Thérèse stayed in the novitiate by her own request
- Claim: Thérèse had been professed four years in 1894 and should have left the novitiate the year before (nuns stayed three years after profession). Mère Marie de Gonzague, then novice mistress, told MT that Thérèse had asked to stay "out of humility". Agnès agreed because she hoped Thérèse would influence the novices. In 1896 Gonzague became prioress again but kept the novice-mistress title herself and left Thérèse as her auxiliary until her death.
- When / where: 1893–1897, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mère Agnès
- Witness: MT, partly heard from Gonzague
- Source: PO 17, f. 1067r; PA 21, p. 1217–1218
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: PA p. 1266: MT adds that Thérèse made this request when Gonzague was novice mistress, so she chose to stay dependent on Gonzague "to which she certainly could have no natural attraction". MM (E-F144) and MJ (E-F168) give different accounts of when Thérèse began to guide novices.

- When / where: c. 1894–1897
- People: Thérèse
- Witness: MT, saw it

- Confidence: high

### E-F06 — Thérèse kept the Rule while the community was in disorder
- Claim: MT says the community was "in disarray" because of Mère Marie de Gonzague's "regrettable government". Thérèse could have let herself go "like the others" and done things any old way, but she did not. MT applies to her the line of Ecclesiasticus about the one who could have broken the law and did not.
- When / where: 1894–1897, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: MT, observation
- Source: PO 17, f. 1068r, Session 64; PA 21, p. 1218–1219
- Original words: « la communauté étant en désarroi de son temps, à cause du gouvernement regrettable de mère Marie de Gonzague »
- Translation: the community was in disarray in her time, because of the regrettable government of Mother Marie de Gonzague
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — clear, repeated statement; hostile to Gonzague
- Notes: PA p. 1272 adds that parties had formed under Gonzague's influence; charity, regularity and silence were poorly kept. MM says the same (E-F147).

### E-F08 — Scolding over a badly made bed
- Claim: Thérèse saw that the cover of MT's bed was crooked and scolded her hard. She said this showed MT was hardly united to God, and asked what MT had come to Carmel for. As soon as MT admitted the fault, Thérèse's tone softened and she spoke of faith and love.
- When / where: undated, novitiate, MT's cell
- People: Thérèse; MT
- Witness: MT, experienced it
- Source: PO 17, f. 1068r–v; PA 21, p. 1220
- Original words: « Si vous aviez fait le lit de l'Enfant Jésus, est-ce ainsi que vous l'auriez fait? »
- Translation: If you had made the Child Jesus's bed, is this how you would have made it?
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: PA version is sharper: "it would have been better for you to stay in the world, to be useful there at least by some outward works."

### E-F09 — "You will find happiness when you stop looking for it"
- Claim: When MT brought her troubles, Thérèse said the main cause was that MT looked at things too much from the earthly side and sought her own satisfaction. She said happiness comes when one no longer seeks it. In PA she adds: "Believe me, I have tried it."
- When / where: undated
- People: Thérèse; MT
- Witness: MT, heard it
- Source: PO 17, f. 1068v–1069r; PA 21, p. 1220–1221
- New to English readers: partial (in Counsels and Reminiscences)
- Confidence: high

### E-F10 — Thérèse gave her a St John of the Cross text on spiritual love
- Claim: MT noticed that the more she loved Thérèse, the more she loved God, and when her love for Thérèse cooled her love for God cooled too. One day Thérèse gave her a holy picture with a text of St John of the Cross on the back about spiritual love of a creature. MT thought Thérèse had read her mind.
- When / where: undated
- People: Thérèse; MT
- Witness: MT
- Source: PO 17, f. 1068v; PA 21, p. 1240
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: MT also says Thérèse never tried to win her heart "in a natural way".

### E-F11 — "The wolf": the novices' nickname for Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Claim: The novices called Mère Marie de Gonzague "the wolf" ("le loup"). MT sometimes used it. When Gonzague was prioress (from March 1896), Thérèse always rebuked her for it. In PA, MT gives Thérèse's reason: the nickname was acceptable when Gonzague was not prioress, but now she held "the sacrament of authority".
- When / where: 1894–1897, Lisieux
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; MT; the novices; Thérèse
- Witness: MT, heard it
- Source: PO 17, f. 1069r; PA 21, p. 1219
- Original words: « C'était bon quand elle n'était pas prieure […] à présent qu'elle a le sacrement de l'autorité, nous devons la respecter. »
- Translation: That was all right when she was not prioress; now that she has the sacrament of authority, we must respect her.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — both processes; PA gives the nickname's origin ("between novices")
- Notes: The PA wording implies Thérèse tolerated the nickname before 1896. The PO wording says only that she rebuked it "when Gonzague was prioress".

### E-F14 — "Follow me, I am carrying Jesus"

### E-F15 — Her tears won a change in the communion custom
- Claim: On a communion day, when Thérèse was very ill (PA: very tired), the prioress ordered her to take a remedy before Mass. The house custom was that this meant losing communion. Thérèse burst into tears and pleaded so well that she could take the remedy after Mass. From that day the custom of losing communion in such cases was abolished.
- When / where: undated, probably 1896–1897 (PA names Gonzague as prioress)
- People: Thérèse; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: MT; not clear if she saw the plea or heard of it
- Source: PO 17, f. 1069v–1070r; PA 21, p. 1229
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — MT does not say how she knew
- Notes: PO says only "our mother prioress"; PA names Gonzague.

### E-F17 — The blind Spouse who cannot count
- Claim: MT asked if Jesus was displeased with her miseries. Thérèse said Jesus has one "infirmity": he is blind and does not know arithmetic. If he could see clearly and count, he would annihilate us. A great sinner who dies in an act of love is received at once.
- When / where: undated
- People: Thérèse; MT
- Witness: MT, heard it
- Source: PO 17, f. 1070r; PA 21, p. 1236
- New to English readers: partial (in Counsels and Reminiscences)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: See E-Q02. The editors note MT was good at arithmetic ("douée pour le calcul"), which may explain the image.

### E-F18 — MT wrote most of "Conseils et Souvenirs"
- Claim: The judges asked why her answer matched word for word the "Conseils et souvenirs" printed in Histoire d'une âme (1899 edition). MT said most of that section came from notes she herself wrote from memory, and she used the same notes for her deposition. In PA she says she wrote them right after Thérèse's death, for her own edification, well before any process; she knows them by heart and "cannot say them otherwise".
- When / where: notes from Oct 1897; asked in 1911 and 1916
- People: MT; the tribunal
- Witness: MT, about her own writing
- Source: PO 17, f. 1070v, Session 64; PA 21, p. 1235, Session 62
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Important for source criticism: many MT "quotations" are not fresh memory in 1911/1916 but recitations of her 1897 notes, already published. Both tribunals noticed.

Thérèse replied quickly that one must believe the Pope first. But she added that she would ask God's leave to come back at once and warn MT if she had misled her.
- When / where: undated

### E-F22 — Hot-water bottle and iodine in a fever

She said nothing to the nurse but complained to Jesus that she was burning and was given more heat, and wished for half a glass of water. A few minutes later the infirmarian came back, unexpectedly, with a cool drink.
- When / where: undated; PA places it "in her last illness"; infirmary or cell
- People: Thérèse; an unnamed infirmarian
- Witness: MT, heard it from Thérèse

- Confidence: medium — second-hand from Thérèse; PA says the nurse "was mistaken"

### E-F31 — The trying first portress
- Claim: For two or three years Thérèse was second portress under an elderly first portress. MT describes her as a very good nun but painfully slow, full of manias, able "to make an angel lose patience". In PA MT adds she talked all day in sermons that were "real charades". When MT answered her impatiently, the old sister said Thérèse had never spoken to her like that. Thérèse told MT: be gentle, "she is ill", and imagine having to listen to her all day as I do.
- When / where: c. 1894–1896, the turn / portress duty
- People: Thérèse; unnamed elderly first portress; MT
- Witness: MT, experienced part and heard Thérèse's reply
- Source: PO 17, f. 1075v–1076r; PA 21, p. 1246–1247
- Original words: « jamais sœur Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus ne m'a parlé comme vous le faites! »
- Translation: Sister Thérèse of the Child Jesus has never spoken to me the way you do!
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: MT says (PA) this patience alone would earn canonization. The sister is not named; merger may identify her from other sources.
