# Batch A — PO witness 1: Mère Agnès de Jésus (Pauline Martin), Ordinary Process 1910

Source file: `wit/PO-01-T-moin-1-Agn-s-de-J-sus.txt`. Web page: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/naissance-dune-sainte/les-proces-la-sainte-de-therese/le-proces-ordinaire/les-temoignages-du-proces-ordinaire/

Witness label used below: "Mère Agnès (Pauline Martin), elder sister, Thérèse's prioress 1893–96, prioress again in 1910". Abbreviation: PO 1 = Ordinary Process, witness 1.

Structure of the deposition: oral answers in Sessions 5–9 (12–19 Aug 1910, f. 135r–176v) and Sessions 14–21 (27 Aug–5 Sept 1910, f. 208v–302r). In Session 19 (2 Sept 1910) she handed in a signed notebook, "Physionomie morale de sœur Thérèse… pendant sa dernière maladie" (f. 247r–292v). This notebook is an early form of the "Novissima Verba" / "Derniers Entretiens". Cards from it say "NV notebook". Much of it exists in English as "Last Conversations", so those cards are marked "partial".

Note on folio typos in the file: "[947r]" = 247r; "[351v]" = 251v; "[218v]" inside the 20 Aug entry = 281v; "[274i-]" = 274r.

## Fact cards

### A-F01 — Editors: Pauline gave her testimony in two blocks, interrupted for a Scottish priest
- Claim: Mère Agnès testified in sessions 4–9 (12–19 Aug 1910) and 14–21 (27 Aug–15 Sept 1910). The tribunal interrupted her so that Fr Thomas Nimmo Taylor, a Scottish priest leading a pilgrimage in Lisieux, could testify at once.
- When / where: Aug–Sept 1910, Bayeux/Lisieux diocesan tribunal
- People: Mère Agnès; Fr Thomas Nimmo Taylor
- Witness: editors' note (Teresianum 1973)
- Source: PO 1, editors' introduction
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — editorial fact
- Notes: The session headers in the file start at "Session 5" on 12 Aug and end at "Session 21" on 5 Sept, not 15 Sept.

### A-F02 — Pauline admitted that Histoire d'une âme was edited, and the tribunal reacted
- Claim: Asked if the printed Histoire d'une âme matched the autograph, Pauline said there were changes "of little importance": short cuts of intimate family details, removal of one or two pages she judged less interesting to outsiders, and retouches that Mère Marie de Gonzague demanded so that the whole text seemed addressed to Gonzague. The judges then ordered an authentic copy of the autograph to be added to the Acts.
- When / where: Session 8, 17 Aug 1910
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague; the judges
- Witness: Mère Agnès, about her own editorial work
- Source: PO 1, f. 161v–162r, Session 8, 17 Aug 1910
- Original words: « exigea certaines retouches de détail dans les parties adressées à ses sœurs, afin que… le tout parût lui avoir été adressé à elle-même »
- Translation: "required some small retouches in the parts addressed to her sisters, so that… the whole would seem addressed to herself."
- New to English readers: partial (the edits are known to scholars; the courtroom moment is little known)
- Confidence: high — her own sworn words
- Notes: The editors say she said this "rather discreetly". She calls the changes minor. Modern critical editions show the changes were much larger. The authentic copy sits in vol. IV, f. 1404v–1634v, of the Process copy.

### A-F03 — Thérèse gave Pauline advance permission to cut or add to her text
- Claim: While writing Manuscript C in 1897, Thérèse told Pauline that anything Pauline chose to cut or add, Thérèse herself cut or added, and that she must have no scruple about it. On a day of many interruptions she said her pages on charity were badly written and that Pauline would have to retouch everything.
- When / where: June–July 1897, Lisieux Carmel (DE date 9 Aug)
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD Thérèse say it
- Source: PO 1, f. 157v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « tout ce que vous trouverez bon de retrancher ou d'ajouter au cahier de ma vie, c'est moi qui le retranche et qui l'ajoute »
- Translation: "whatever you think right to cut from or add to the notebook of my life, it is I who cut it and add it."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium — only Pauline reports it, and it justifies her own editing
- Notes: A self-serving statement from the editor of the text. See Gaps.

### A-F04 — The origin of Manuscript A: a winter evening, and Thérèse laughed
- Claim: One winter evening early in 1895 (the editors date it Dec 1894), Thérèse told childhood stories to Pauline and Marie. Marie said it was a pity these were not written down. Pauline, then prioress, ordered Thérèse to write her childhood memories. Thérèse laughed as if they were teasing her.
- When / where: winter 1894–95, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès (prioress); Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW and took part
- Source: PO 1, f. 155v–156r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « qui riait comme si l'on s'était moqué d'elle »
- Translation: "who was laughing as if we were making fun of her."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — eyewitness
- Notes: Pauline dates it "beginning of 1895"; editors' introduction says December 1894.

### A-F05 — Thérèse handed over Manuscript A kneeling, and Pauline did not read it for months
- Claim: On 20 Jan 1896 (Pauline's feast), during evening prayer, Thérèse knelt as she passed to her stall and gave Pauline the notebook. Pauline only nodded and put it on her stall unopened. She read it only after the spring 1896 elections. Thérèse never asked if she had read it. Once Pauline told her she had not had time; Thérèse did not seem hurt.
- When / where: 20 Jan 1896 and spring 1896, choir of the Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 156r–v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — eyewitness about her own action
- Notes: Pauline found the account incomplete; religious life was "barely sketched".

### A-F06 — Pauline's midnight visit to Gonzague to get Manuscript C written
- Claim: On the evening of 2 June 1897, near midnight, Pauline went to the prioress, Mère Marie de Gonzague. She said she could not sleep before telling a secret. She described Thérèse's childhood notebook as "nice" but of little use for the death circular, and suggested that Gonzague order Thérèse to write about her religious life. Next morning Gonzague gave the order.
- When / where: night of 2–3 June 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès; her own action
- Source: PO 1, f. 156v–157r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « c'est gentil, mais vous ne pourrez pas en tirer grand' chose pour vous aider à faire sa circulaire après sa mort »
- Translation: "it's nice, but you won't get much from it to help you write her circular after her death."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — first-person account
- Notes: Pauline says she feared Gonzague would not value the text as she did. She hid from Gonzague that she had already read it closely.

### A-F07 — Thérèse thought the notebook too fine and wanted to crowd the lines
- Claim: Pauline chose an ordinary notebook for Manuscript C. Thérèse found it too beautiful and feared a fault against poverty. She asked if she should at least write the lines close together to save paper. Pauline told her to space the lines and write large because she was too ill. Pauline showed the notebook to the tribunal.
- When / where: June 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW and HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 157r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — eyewitness, object shown in court
- Notes: —

### A-F08 — Thérèse wrote Manuscript C with constant interruptions
- Claim: Thérèse wrote at one go, without crossings-out, but infirmarians and novices kept coming and going to profit from her last days. She said she did not know what she was writing. She stopped writing early in July 1897. The last page is in pencil because she was too weak.
- When / where: June–early July 1897, Lisieux Carmel (garden and cell)
- People: Thérèse; infirmarians; novices
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW and HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 157r–158r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « Je ne sais pas ce que j'écris. »
- Translation: "I don't know what I'm writing."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — eyewitness
- Notes: In the NV notebook (15 June) she says she tried not to become impatient when disturbed while writing on charity (f. 253v–254r).

### A-F09 — Pauline warned Thérèse the manuscript might be burned
- Claim: Pauline told Thérèse that the manuscript could be used for publication, and also that the prioress might burn it. Thérèse answered that it would not matter; God would use other means.
- When / where: summer 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague (the "notre mère" meant)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 158v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910; again f. 239r, Session 18, 1 Sept 1910
- Original words: « Oh! mais qu'est-ce que cela ferait; c'est que le bon Dieu ne voudrait pas se servir de ce moyen »
- Translation: "Oh, but what would that matter? It would mean God did not want to use this means."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — told twice, consistent
- Notes: Pauline states it was never the custom at Lisieux for nuns to write autobiographical notes for their circulars (f. 158v).

### A-F10 — Pauline's account of how Histoire d'une âme was published
- Claim: Pauline says she proposed the publication. Gonzague sent Pauline's copy to Fr Godefroy Madelaine, Premonstratensian prior of Mondaye. On his report Bishop Hugonin gave the imprimatur on 7 March 1898. The copy was printed after some retouches Fr Godefroy indicated. Pauline gives October 1898 as the publication date.
- When / where: 1897–1898, Lisieux / Mondaye / Bayeux
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague; Fr Godefroy Madelaine; Bishop Flavien Hugonin
- Witness: Mère Agnès; first-hand
- Source: PO 1, f. 159r–v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — first-hand
- Notes: The editors' introduction gives 30 Sept 1898. Pauline says "October 1898". Elsewhere (f. 294r) she says Gonzague "had the manuscript printed".

### A-F11 — First copies went only to Carmels, as a death circular; Pauline shipped them
- Claim: The book was first sent only to Carmels, in place of the usual death circular. Carmels lent it and requests poured in. Pauline, not prioress, was put in charge of shipping. She had at least five orders every day.
- When / where: 1898–1899, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Mère Agnès; first-hand
- Source: PO 1, f. 294r–v, Session 19, 2 Sept 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### A-F12 — Print and mail figures up to 1910
- Claim: By 1910 there were 62,815 copies of the full Life printed and 80,000 of the abridged Life; sold: 45,715 full and 56,405 abridged. From July 1909 to July 1910 the Carmel received requests for 183,348 pictures and 36,612 "souvenirs", and 9,741 letters. The daily average of letters had reached 50. Records were kept from January 1909 on the advice of Mgr de Teil.
- When / where: 1898–1910, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie-Ange de l'Enfant-Jésus; Mgr Roger de Teil (vice-postulator)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; from the Carmel's account books
- Source: PO 1, f. 294v–295r, Session 19, 2 Sept 1910
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — figures from ledgers
- Notes: She says there was "no propaganda", only ordinary bookseller notices (f. 295r).

### A-F13 — Pauline's view of Gonzague's faults: why Thérèse was made "assistant"
- Claim: When Pauline became prioress in 1893, she felt bound by custom to name the outgoing prioress, Mère Marie de Gonzague, novice mistress. But Gonzague had "gaps and faults" besides real qualities. Pauline added Thérèse as her helper to offset Gonzague's harmful influence.
- When / where: 1893, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès; her own decision
- Source: PO 1, f. 153v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « à de réelles qualités, se joignaient en elle des lacunes et des défauts dont j'espérais contrebalancer l'influence fâcheuse »
- Translation: "alongside real qualities, she had gaps and faults whose unfortunate influence I hoped to counterbalance."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — sworn first-hand statement
- Notes: A frank criticism of Gonzague in open court. When Gonzague became prioress again in 1896 she kept the novice-mistress role herself and kept Thérèse as helper (f. 153v–154r).

### A-F14 — Thérèse was 20 when she began helping with the novices
- Claim: Thérèse was given charge of the novices "as an auxiliary" in 1893, at age 20, by Pauline as prioress. Gonzague confirmed this in 1896. She kept it until her death.
- When / where: 1893–1897
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Mère Agnès; first-hand
- Source: PO 1, f. 153r–v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Pauline says novices spoke more freely against her because she was not the titular mistress and was younger than several of them (f. 154r–v).

### A-F15 — "Some by the skin, some by the wing-tips": her joke about novices
- Claim: Thérèse told Pauline, joking, that she had to take some novices "by the skin" and others "by the tips of the wings". She corrected without fear, did not ask questions to satisfy curiosity, and did not try to win their hearts.
- When / where: 1893–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; the novices
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 154r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « Il y en a que je suis forcée de prendre par la peau, et d'autres par le bout des ailes. »
- Translation: "There are some I have to grab by the skin, and others by the tips of their wings."
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — quoted by a close witness
- Notes: Pauline says she wrote down some sayings "at once" (f. 154r).

### A-F16 — After a humiliating talk with a novice, her face was flushed
- Claim: Pauline met Thérèse just after a novice had spoken to her very humiliatingly. Her face was flushed. Pauline asked if she was more tired. Thérèse said she was very happy: she had thought of Shimei cursing David, and that God had told the sister to say these things. That morning she had wanted to be humiliated.
- When / where: undated (1893–97), Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; an unnamed novice ("sœur ***")
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW and HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 154v, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial (the scene is in HA ch. 12)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The novice is not named.

### A-F17 — Gonzague on her deathbed in 1904: "my little Thérèse will get me my salvation"
- Claim: In 1904, seven years after Thérèse's death, Mère Marie de Gonzague told Pauline on her deathbed that no soul in the house had been as guilty as she, yet she trusted God and "my little Thérèse" to obtain her salvation.
- When / where: 1904, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mère Agnès
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 154v–155r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- Original words: « pas une seule âme ici ne s'est rendue coupable comme je l'ai été et cependant j'ai confiance en Dieu et en ma petite Thérèse »
- Translation: "not a single soul here has been as guilty as I have, and yet I trust in God and in my little Thérèse."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — reported by Gonzague's rival and successor; no other witness named
- Notes: Pauline used it to show that Thérèse behaved with respect toward Gonzague.

### A-F18 — Offices she held; she wanted to be infirmarian but never was
- Claim: Thérèse held most house offices in turn: sacristan, portress (portière), refectorian, linen-keeper (lingère). She never held the infirmarian's office, which she wanted very much.
- When / where: 1888–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 155r, Session 7, 16 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: See A-F44 and A-F63 for her wish to nurse difficult patients.

### A-F19 — A "current of relaxation" in the Carmel when Thérèse entered
- Claim: Pauline states that when Thérèse entered, the Carmel had a current of relaxation to reverse. Several nuns were regular, but "a fairly large number" let abuses creep in. Thérèse never joined the groups that gathered round the prioress after a parlour visit to hear the news, and never listened to talk against charity.
- When / where: from 1888, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; the community; the prioress (Gonzague, not named here)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 162v, Session 8, 17 Aug 1910
- Original words: « il y en avait d'autres et en assez grand nombre qui se laissaient aller à des abus »
- Translation: "there were others, and a fairly large number, who let themselves slide into abuses."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — sworn statement by a prioress about her own house
- Notes: The deposition does not list the abuses.

### A-F20 — After bad family news at the parlour, she went straight back to community duties
- Claim: When the sisters learned painful news at the parlour, for example about their father's health, Thérèse did not stay to find comfort in talk with her sisters. She went back at once to the community exercises. Pauline says she was far braver than Marie and Pauline in these family griefs.
- When / where: 1888–1894, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Louis Martin
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 162v, Session 8, 17 Aug 1910; also f. 224v, Session 16
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### A-F21 — People blamed the daughters' entry for Louis Martin's illness, even at recreation
- Claim: Opposition: During Louis Martin's illness (paralysis, then cerebral troubles; he had to be treated in a mental-health house), tactless people said in front of Thérèse that his daughters' entry into Carmel, and above all the youngest's, had caused it. Visitors said it at the parlour. In the community it was often discussed at recreation in front of the Martin sisters. Marie and Pauline were crushed; Thérèse stayed calm.
- When / where: 1889–1892, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Louis Martin; unnamed nuns and visitors
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW and HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 224v, Session 16, 30 Aug 1910
- Original words: « Même dans la communauté on s'entretenait souvent devant nous, dans les récréations, de ce sujet si désolant »
- Translation: "Even in the community people often talked in front of us, at recreation, about this so painful subject."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — first-hand
- Notes: Thérèse noted 12 Feb 1889, the day her father entered the special institution, on a picture where she kept dates of her main graces (f. 224v).

### A-F22 — She avoided her own sisters at recreation and sought those less friendly to her
- Claim: Thérèse did not seek the company of her blood sisters. At recreation she chose nuns who were less sympathetic to her, and often those who were alone or neglected.
- When / where: 1888–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Sr Geneviève
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 151r, Session 6, 13 Aug 1910; f. 217r, Session 15, 29 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### A-F23 — Marie complained that Thérèse loved a nun Marie disliked more than herself
- Claim: Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur told Pauline several times of her surprise, and even hurt: Thérèse seemed to love a certain nun, whom Marie found very unpleasant, more than Marie, who had been like a mother to her.
- When / where: undated, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin); Thérèse; an unnamed nun
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD Marie say it
- Source: PO 1, f. 217r, Session 15, 29 Aug 1910
- Original words: « On dirait qu'elle aime plus que moi… cette religieuse qui me déplaît tant. »
- Translation: "You'd think she loves that nun I dislike so much more than me."
- New to English readers: yes

- Notes: The nun is not named.

### A-F24 — The wreaths at Mère Geneviève's coffin
- Claim: When the foundress Mère Geneviève died, families and workmen sent many wreaths. While Thérèse arranged them around the coffin, a watching sister said angrily that she put her own family's wreaths in front and the poor people's bouquets behind. Thérèse thanked her and asked for the moss cross sent by the workmen, to put it in front. The sister later admitted that from that day she regarded Thérèse as a saint.
- When / where: Dec 1891, choir of the Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; unnamed sister ("sœur…"); Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse (deceased)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD the reply herself ("j'entendis")
- Source: PO 1, f. 216v–217r, Session 15, 29 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial (story is in HA ch. 12 but without Pauline as ear-witness)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The critic is not named.

### A-F25 — Thérèse defended a nun Pauline found "abominable"
- Claim: About one nun whose conduct Pauline found especially blameworthy, Thérèse said she felt deep compassion: the sister was not responsible, she was ill in mind and suffered a great deal morally. With such an illness and "so badly made a mind", Thérèse said she would do no better and would despair.
- When / where: undated, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; unnamed nun
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD
- Source: PO 1, f. 216v, Session 15, 29 Aug 1910
- Original words: « si vous la connaissiez comme moi, vous verriez qu'elle n'est pas responsable de tout ce qui nous paraît si abominable »
- Translation: "if you knew her as I do, you'd see she is not responsible for everything that seems so abominable to us."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Pauline also mentions antipathy, jealousy and wounding behaviour from "certain nuns" (f. 216r).

### A-F26 — Years of leading an old, crabby lay sister
- Claim: An old, infirm, sour-tempered lay sister (died 1895) was hard to guide between exercises because of her odd character and roughness. Thérèse offered herself for this task and did it for years, until she won the trust of the sister, who first received her very badly. She said she led her with the care she would give to leading Our Lord.
- When / where: c. 1889–1895, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; "notre sœur X", an old lay sister
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW, and HEARD Thérèse
- Source: PO 1, f. 212v–213r, Session 14, 27 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial (MS C tells it; "for years" and "died 1895" are added)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Pauline does not name her. (Other sources identify Sr Saint-Pierre; this file does not.)

### A-F27 — "Dérange-toi": the one rude word, at about age three
- Claim: At about three, Thérèse told her father too freely "Dérange-toi" ("move out of the way"). He corrected her and made her feel the fault. She never did it again; her words to him were always respectful after that.
- When / where: c. 1876, Alençon
- People: Thérèse; Louis Martin
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW or heard in the family (she does not say which)
- Source: PO 1, f. 140r, Session 5, 12 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — family memory, 34 years later
- Notes: Pauline says the father loved her but did not spoil her. The mother told him: "Tu la perdras!" ("You'll spoil her!").

### A-F28 — "I'll ask Pauline's permission" — and tears when refused
- Claim: After her mother's death, Thérèse asked permission for everything. When her father invited her out, she always said she would ask Pauline. Her father encouraged this. If Pauline refused, Thérèse sometimes cried, because her father would have liked her company, but she obeyed without insisting.
- When / where: 1877–1882, Les Buissonnets, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Pauline; Louis Martin
- Witness: Mère Agnès; SAW
- Source: PO 1, f. 140v, Session 5, 12 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial (HA mentions the permission; the refusals and tears are added)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### A-F30 — A jealous schoolmate at the Abbey
- Claim: Thérèse later told Pauline she had suffered from a schoolmate's jealousy at the Benedictine Abbey, but she never complained at the time. She also tried to attach herself to a teacher as other girls did, failed, and later saw the failure as a grace.
- When / where: 1881–1886, Benedictine Abbey school, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; unnamed schoolmate
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD from Thérèse later
- Source: PO 1, f. 141r, Session 5, 12 Aug 1910

### A-F31 — Withdrawn from school because of health, not because of teachers
- Claim: Soon after First Communion Thérèse went through a crisis of scruples, and her health seemed worn out, so her father took her out of the school. Asked if the teachers disapproved or Thérèse disliked the school, Pauline said "Oh no", it was her weakness.
- When / where: c. 1885–86, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Louis Martin
- Witness: Mère Agnès; from the family (she admits her sisters know better)
- Source: PO 1, f. 141v–142r, Session 5, 12 Aug 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium — Pauline was in Carmel
- Notes: The judge's question suggests the tribunal suspected other motives.

### A-F32 — Scruples for a year and a half from about age 12
- Claim: Around age 12 Thérèse went through a year and a half of violent scruples about every detail of her conduct. As a small child she asked Pauline every evening whether God was pleased with her. Two moments gave her great joy: Fr Pichon SJ telling her, at about 15½, that she had never committed a mortal sin; and Fr Alexis (Recollect) at the 1891 retreat telling her that her faults did not grieve God.

### A-M01 — Vision of a veiled, bent man in the garden (Thérèse, Lisieux, c. 1879)
- Period: during life
- What was reported: At about 6–7, at about 2 pm, while her father was away for several days, Thérèse at a window saw in the garden, about 20 m away, a figure with her father's height and bearing, but bent as if by age, head covered by a veil of uncertain colour. - The person helped: none (premonition)
- Witnesses and reporter: Mère Agnès, present right after; Marie
- Status: sworn testimony at the Ordinary Process; not an approved miracle
- Source: PO 1, f. 233r–v, Session 17, 31 Aug 1910
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: Pauline says Thérèse was calm, balanced, "not imaginative"; the vision came three years before her childhood illness.
- Doubts / other versions: Told in MS A. The judge asked whether the sad meaning was only claimed after the father's illness; Pauline said Thérèse believed from the start it foretold something painful.

### A-M02 — The childhood illness and the smile of the Virgin (Thérèse, Lisieux, 13 May 1883)
- Period: during life
- What was reported: At 10½ she had crises of terror, horrible visions and an impulse to throw herself from bed head-first onto the floor. - The person helped: Thérèse
- Witnesses and reporter: Mère Agnès reports; she was in Carmel and knew it from the parlour and from Thérèse
- Status: sworn testimony; not an approved miracle
- Source: PO 1, f. 234r–v, Session 17, 31 Aug 1910

- Doubts / other versions: Pauline gives the date as 10 May 1883; the usual date is 13 May 1883 (Pentecost). Pauline defers to her sisters for details. ## Gaps and conflicts

- **Pauline is both witness and editor.** She admits the HA text was changed (A-F02), and says Thérèse gave her blanket permission to edit (A-F03). Only she reports that permission. She also calls the changes "of little importance". Treat her HA-based statements with care.
- **Much of her "testimony" is quotation of HA.** She says Thérèse told her everything later written down, and quotes MS A/C at length as testimony. This blurs what she saw and what she read. The Pranzini story, the Rome audience and the Abbey years she refers to Céline or Marie.
- **Her notebook affected Thérèse.** Pauline admits her note-taking tired Thérèse and "paralysed her openness" (A-F94). Thérèse feared her merit was lost because Pauline wrote it down. This matters for the reliability of the "Last Words".
- **Dates in the NV notebook do not always match the editors' DE references.** The Hanoi saying sits under "15 mai" but is tagged DE 15-6; items under "15 mai" are tagged DE 9-5; the "4 juin" entry is tagged DE 6-4; there are retro-entries "Du 21 (oublié)" and "Du 16 juillet (oublié)". The notebook was edited after the event.
- **Dying moment: two versions.** Oral: "I saw her close her eyes; she sighed several times and gave her soul to God" (f. 246v). NV: "As soon as it [the ecstasy] ended, she breathed her last" (f. 292v). The oral text says "a sister" carried the candle; the NV names Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie.
- **Body after death.** Supple limbs until burial (f. 292v) vs. coffin closed on 3 Oct because of "some signs of decomposition" (f. 246v). Both from Pauline; she does not reconcile them.
- **Cure date.** Pauline gives 10 May 1883 for the smile of the Virgin; the standard date is 13 May 1883 (Pentecost).
- **Publication date.** Pauline: October 1898; editors: 30 Sept 1898.
- **Origin date of MS A.** Pauline: early 1895; editors: Dec 1894.
- **Taxil / Diana Vaughan.** Pauline says Thérèse saw through the hoax before its exposure (A-F81). She does not mention that Thérèse wrote for "Diana" or that her photo was used at Taxil's April 1897 conference. This looks like a softened account. Check other witnesses.
- **Gonzague.** Pauline is critical ("gaps and faults", "harmful influence", "suffered much from this prioress", a prioress elected "despite notable faults"). But she also reports Gonzague's deathbed trust in Thérèse, and that Gonzague showed her off at the parlour. The 1896 prioress who "had notable faults" is not named; the identification with Gonzague is by context.
- **Dissenters named or hinted.** (1) a long-professed nun who later left the Carmel: "easy to be a saint with everything"; (2) a lay sister, dead by 1910: the "circular" remark, but at other times called her a saint; (3) a lay sister: "not even a good nun"; (4) a sister who stared laughing every evening; (5) some sisters who did not think her dying; (6) Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix before 1888; (7) two or three Carmels and one prioress who saw pride in HA. Pauline discredits dissenter 1 ("poor judgment", left the order). None except Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix is named.
- **"All except one or two" admired her** (f. 240v) sits uneasily with the list of critics above.
- **Pauline's own failures, freely admitted:** she did not read MS A for months; she did not believe the wound of love; she joined in refusing Thérèse's profession in 1890; she tried to get Thérèse to say edifying words to the doctor; she received confidences Thérèse said she should refuse.
- **Friction inside the Martin family:** Marie opposed her entry; Marie was hurt that Thérèse preferred a nun Marie disliked; Thérèse felt her sisters tried to "influence our mother" about her rest; she warned all three about talking after the parlour; "I don't like what comes from my family".
- **Unnamed people** the merger may want to identify from other files: the old lay sister she guided (died 1895); the "abominable" sister; the critic at the funeral; the first infirmarian whose handling "could have had grave consequences"; the neurasthenic sister; the unbelieving relative by marriage; the tempted seminarian; the director who called her proud.
- **Expected but absent:** almost nothing about her painting (only the 28 May painting request), Joan of Arc plays, her poems' circumstances beyond "Vivre d'amour", the Bellière/Roulland letters' content, Dr La Néele (not mentioned at all), and the laundry or portress work in detail. The Abbey years are deferred to Céline and Marie.
- **Procedural note:** the judges asked leading questions about neuropathic temperament, delirium, why she left school, and whether the superior objected because two sisters were already inside. These show the tribunal testing the "Martin clan" problem.

Five best finds:
1. Pauline's sworn admission that Histoire d'une âme was edited so that all of it seemed addressed to Gonzague, and the tribunal's order to file the autograph (A-F02, A-F03).
2. Pauline's open criticism of Gonzague ("gaps and faults", "harmful influence"; "she had suffered much from this prioress"), set against Gonzague's 1904 deathbed words (A-F13, A-F96, A-F17).
3. The dissenters inside the Carmel: "easy to be a saint when you have everything", "what will she write in her circular?", "not even a good nun", the nun who laughed at her bedside (A-F68–A-F70, A-F99).
4. Pauline's admission that her note-taking tired Thérèse and blocked her openness, and that Thérèse felt "harassed with questions like Joan of Arc" (A-F94, A-F95).
5. Candid bodily and domestic details: leftovers served to her, eating felt "shameful", the pin through her shoulder, the eau-de-Cologne bottle, the lip skin taken as relics, the crucifix pulled from her hands, and the coffin closed on signs of decomposition (A-F59, A-F57, A-F102, A-F100, A-F92, A-F93).
