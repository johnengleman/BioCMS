### L-F01 — Pilgrims were forbidden to speak to Leo XIII; she spoke anyway
- Claim: The vice-postulator's Articles state that the pilgrims were admitted to kiss the Pope's ring "with the express prohibition" to ask any favour; Thérèse asked, with tears, "encouraged by her sister", and rested her hands on the Pope's knees for a last plea.
- When / where: 20 Nov 1887, Vatican
- People: Leo XIII; Abbé Révérony (vicar general of Bayeux); Céline Martin
- Witness: De Teil (Articles), built on Story of a Soul
- Source: D3 art. 13, pp. 12–13 (PDF 26–27)
- New to English readers: no
- Confidence: high — agrees with her own account
- Notes: Adds nothing to her own account except the phrase "encouraged by her sister".

### L-F02 — Superior of the Carmel opposed; only Pauline encouraged at Carmel
- Claim: The Articles say that when she sought entry at 15 there was "a momentary opposition in the family", "little encouragement" at the Carmel except from Sister Agnès of Jesus, and opposition from the Carmel's ecclesiastical superior, who referred the matter to Bishop Hugonin.
- When / where: 1887, Lisieux
- People: Canon Jean-Baptiste Delatroëtte (superior; named in LAV); Mgr Hugonin
- Witness: De Teil
- Source: D3 art. 12, p. 11 (PDF 25)
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: See L-F46–F48 for the superior's words.

### L-F04 — Mother Geneviève's "spiritual bouquet"
- Claim: The foundress, Mother Geneviève of St Teresa, told her during a dark period: "serve God in peace and joy … our God is the God of peace." Thérèse took it as God-sent; the next Sunday Geneviève said she had had no revelation about her.
- When / where: 1889–1891, infirmary, Carmel of Lisieux
- People: Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse (1805–1891)
- Witness: Thérèse's own account, quoted in D3 and CF
- Source: D3 art. 19 p. 22 (PDF 36); CF PDF p. 53
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: CF adds that two old Sisters were at the bedside and that Thérèse had been "severely tried, almost to sadness" that day.

### L-M25 — Gallipoli: the 500 francs, 16 Jan 1910, and later sums
- Period: after death (modern)
- What was reported: The prioress, Mother Maria Carmela of the Heart of Jesus, of the very poor Carmel of Gallipoli (Apulia), dreamed that a Carmelite covered her with blankets, said she brought 500 francs to pay the community's debt (300 francs), took her ("bilocation will help us") to the turn-room and put the money in the cash box, said "I am … Sister Teresa of Lisieux", and "My way is sure". The notes (ten 50-franc notes of the Bank of Naples) were found. Later sums appeared monthly (25 lire in Jan, more Feb–Apr; a 50-franc note in May; 50 in June; a 5-lira note placed at a statue 16 July; a 100-franc note on 6 Aug, which the Bishop, who had lost one, returned); on 5 Sept 1910 she predicted that only her bones would be found at the exhumation. - Witnesses and reporter: Mother Carmela (letters 25 Feb and Sept 1910); the whole community; De Teil visited 22 Oct 1910 with Canon Cavallera; Bishop Gaetano Müller's canonical inquiry; Bishop Giannattasio testified at the Ordinary Process
- Status: canonical inquiry by the Bishop of Gallipoli; testimony at the Ordinary Process; not an approved miracle
- Source: D3 art. 143, 143a–d, pp. 185–200 (PDF 199–214); D6 pp. 333–340 (PDF 375–380)

## Gaps and conflicts
- De Teil gives 22 as her age when she took charge of the novices; Laveille reports an old nun remarking on her being "only twenty". Standard date: Feb 1893 (age 20). Check depositions.
- Boulogne (LAV p. 292) is probably a mistranslation of Bologna (the pilgrims passed through Bologna). Verify in Céline's deposition.
- The glass-of-water story (D4): Dolan asks about Marie; the sisters answer "in order not to disturb Céline". Céline slept in the cell by the infirmary. The story is weakly attested.
- Two versions of Mother Geneviève's plea to Delatroëtte (infirmary vs community room).
- Number of witnesses at the Ordinary Process: 45 (De Teil 1912) vs 48 (Carmel site).
- The name of the lay sister cured at the dead body (L-M02) and of the kitchen Sister (L-F43) are not in these books.
- Madame Tifenne vs Céline's portraits: open conflict about her looks.

### L-F147 — Last words and acts (as recorded in these books)
- Claim: Recorded last day (Prioress's Epilogue, i.e. Mère Agnès; and De Teil): morning, to the statue: "the air of earth is failing me"; 2.30 p.m. sat up: "the chalice is full to overflowing … I could never have believed it was possible to suffer so much … I can only explain it by my extreme desire to save souls"; then "all that I have written about my thirst for suffering is really true! I do not regret having surrendered myself to Love" (repeated several times); "Mother, prepare me to die well"; after Gonzague's answer on humility: "Yes, I feel it; my soul has ever sought the truth … I have understood humility of heart"; 4.30 agony began, she thanked the community with a smile, crucifix in her hands; at the Angelus she looked at the Virgin's statue; a few minutes after 7: "Mother, is it not the agony? … am I not going to die?"; "Ah, very well then … I do not wish to suffer less"; to the crucifix: "Oh! … I love Him! … My God, I … love … Thee!"; sank, head to the right; rose as if called, eyes fixed a little above the statue about the space of a Credo; died. - Confidence: high (Carmel's own account)
- Notes: Batches A–D give the sworn versions; compare exact French.
