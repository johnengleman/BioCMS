## PO-00 and PA-00 — editors' introductions (process facts)

### H-F01 — Only 37 of 48 Ordinary Process depositions are printed
- Claim: 48 witnesses were heard at the Ordinary Process, but the 1973 edition prints only 37 depositions. The editors left out 11 depositions that deal only with miracles.
- When / where: Ordinary Process, Bayeux/Lisieux 1910–1911; edition 1973
- People: modern editors; Claude Langlois (historian, author of the web introduction)
- Witness: editors' note (web introduction by Claude Langlois)
- Source: PO-00 intro (web introduction to the Ordinary Process testimonies)
- Original words: « Les éditeurs ont écarté, comme de moindre intérêt, onze dépositions concernant les seuls miracles. »
- Translation: The editors set aside, as of lesser interest, eleven depositions that concerned only miracles.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — explicit statement of the web editor
- Notes: Langlois says this "distorts reality a little", because the postulator chose witnesses to cover all questions. So the printed record under-represents the miracle side.

### H-F02 — Half the Ordinary Process witnesses never knew Thérèse
- Claim: The postulator chose witnesses to answer all three topics (virtues, fame of holiness, miracles). As a result, half of the witnesses had never known Thérèse.
- When / where: 1910–1911, Ordinary Process
- People: the postulator (Mgr Roger de Teil)
- Witness: editors' note (Langlois)
- Source: PO-00 intro
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — editor's statement
- Notes: Langlois says the Jesuit Auriault (doctrine), the convert pastor Grant, and the Bishop of Nardò (apparition with the words "ma voie est sûre") weighed as much for the cause as the Carmelite accounts.

### H-F03 — Which Carmelites did not testify
- Claim: Not all nuns who knew Thérèse testified. Marie de Gonzague and Marie de l'Eucharistie were dead. Two sick sisters left the community in 1909. Six others, old or little concerned, were not called. Besides her three sisters, only six Carmelites spoke of her life.
- When / where: 1910–1911, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague (d. 1904), Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie (Marie Guérin, d. 1905)
- Witness: editors' note (Langlois)
- Source: PO-00 intro
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The two sisters who left in 1909 are not named here.

### H-F04 — Two groups among the Carmelite witnesses, with rivalry
- Claim: Langlois sees two groups: the elder Martin sisters (Mère Agnès, Marie du Sacré-Cœur) and the novices (Marie-Madeleine, Sr Marthe, Marie de la Trinité). Sr Geneviève stands between. He sees a kind of competition among them.
- When / where: 1910–1911
- People: Mère Agnès de Jésus; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Sr Geneviève; Sr Marie-Madeleine du Saint-Sacrement; Sr Marthe de Jésus; Sr Marie de la Trinité
- Witness: editors' note (Langlois)
- Source: PO-00 intro
- Original words: « on sent parfois une sorte de compétition qui tient à des conflits de personnes (Sr Geneviève et Marie de la Trinité) »
- Translation: One sometimes feels a kind of competition, which comes from personal conflicts (Sr Geneviève and Marie de la Trinité).
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — a historian's interpretation, not testimony
- Notes: Langlois: Agnès claims the role of substitute mother and prioress to give the "legitimate" portrait. Marie de la Trinité claims priority for the young sisters' view. Geneviève's deposition is "essential, but too abundant, too sure", close to Agnès's aims. Marie du Sacré-Cœur speaks most soberly and least calculated.

### H-F07 — Mère Agnès briefed the witnesses on how the process worked

- Confidence: high

## PO-02 — Fr Thomas Nimmo Taylor (Scottish priest)

### H-F15 — Taylor's deposition interrupted Mère Agnès's
- Claim: The tribunal interrupted Mère Agnès's deposition so that Fr Taylor could testify at once. He had come to Lisieux with a group of British pilgrims.
- When / where: sessions 10–13, 20–26 August 1910, Bayeux/Lisieux tribunal
- People: Fr Thomas Nimmo Taylor; Mère Agnès de Jésus
- Witness: editors' note
- Source: PO-02 intro; deposition f. 179v–206v
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The edition omits f. 190r–202v (the Dorans cure).

### H-F16 — Who Taylor was
- Claim: Taylor was born 16 December 1873, studied at Saint-Sulpice, Paris (1891–1896) and the Institut Catholique (1896–1897). Cardinal Richard ordained him in Paris on 12 June 1897. He was a curate for three years, then for ten years a professor at the Glasgow major seminary (Scripture, Church history, and French).
- When / where: 1873–1910; Paris, Glasgow
- People: Fr Thomas Nimmo Taylor; Cardinal François Richard
- Witness: Taylor about himself
- Source: PO-02, f. 179v–180r, session 10, 20 August 1910
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: CONFLICT: the editors' intro says he was born in Glasgow and names his mother "Rosa Nimmo"; Taylor himself says born at Greenock (diocese of Glasgow), mother "Rose-Anne Nimmo". Editors add he was later parish priest at Carfin and a canon of the cathedral, and wrote "St. Teresa of the Child Jesus and Scotland" (St Peter's Magazine, 1947).

### H-F17 — Taylor first proposed the cause; Gonzague was cool
- Claim: According to the editors, Taylor seems to have been the first to launch the idea of a beatification cause. He spoke of it to Mère Marie de Gonzague. She was not enthusiastic and answered roughly: "But then how many Carmelites would one have to canonize!"
- When / where: before 1904 (Gonzague died 1904), Lisieux
- People: Taylor; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: editors' note, citing Stéphane-Joseph Piat
- Source: PO-02 intro
- Original words: « Mais en ce cas, combien de carmélites faudrait-il canoniser! »
- Translation: But in that case, how many Carmelites would have to be canonized!
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — second-hand, "à peu près" (approximately), via Piat
- Notes: Taylor himself, in the deposition, does not tell this Gonzague story; he tells the similar Blackrock story (H-F24).

### H-F18 — How Taylor learned of Thérèse
- Claim: Taylor never knew Thérèse. He first read Story of a Soul around 1901. He then wrote to the chief Carmelites of Lisieux and came four times to Lisieux to talk with them.
- When / where: c. 1901–1910
- People: Taylor
- Witness: Taylor about himself
- Source: PO-02, f. 180r, session 10, 20 August 1910
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: He spread her pictures and her Life in Britain, the British colonies, and the United States.

### H-F19 — What the Lisieux nuns stressed to Taylor
- Claim: Gonzague, Agnès, Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Geneviève and Marie de l'Eucharistie told Taylor chiefly of Thérèse's "purity", her patience in suffering, her trust in God, and her devotion to frequent communion. Taylor later explained that "purity" meant moral beauty and candour of soul, not only chastity.
- When / where: 1903 onward, Lisieux parlour and letters
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mère Agnès; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Sr Geneviève; Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie
- Witness: Taylor, heard it from the nuns
- Source: PO-02, f. 181r and 206r (correction after reading)
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: none

### H-F20 — Taylor's portrait of Mère Marie de Gonzague (1903)
- Claim: Taylor talked with Gonzague in 1903. He found her colder and less enthusiastic than the Martin sisters, "not so much a woman", with no exclamations in her speech. But at bottom she judged Thérèse the same: an extraordinary soul in holiness.
- When / where: 1903, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Taylor, SAW and heard her
- Source: PO-02, f. 181v, session 10, 20 August 1910
- Original words: « c'était une âme plus froide, moins enthousiaste; elle n'était pas tant femme. Il n'y avait pas d'exclamations dans son langage. »
- Translation: She was a colder soul, less enthusiastic; she was not so much a woman. There were no exclamations in her speech.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — first-hand impression, answer to a direct judge's question
- Notes: The judge asked specifically whether the non-relative Gonzague thought like Thérèse's sisters. Contrast with H-F17 (editors' report of her cool reaction to a cause).

### H-F21 — Growth of the cult at the grave (1910)
- Claim: Taylor visits the grave each time he comes to Lisieux. On his last visit (about half an hour) he saw two priests there, and several other people arrived. He heard that the pilgrimage grew daily, with many priests, among them missionaries of the Missions Étrangères.
- When / where: August 1910, Lisieux town cemetery, Carmelite plot
- People: Taylor
- Witness: Taylor SAW; the rest heard at Lisieux
- Source: PO-02, f. 182r, session 10
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: He was always accompanied by priests and religious except the first time.

### H-F22 — English translation (1901) and slow early spread
- Claim: Story of a Soul was translated into English in 1901. Its fame grew slowly at first, perhaps because the book was relatively expensive. It grew fast, especially in Scotland, after an extraordinary event at the Good Shepherd convent in London on 30 October 1908, which Taylor publicized.
- When / where: 1901–1910, Britain
- People: Taylor
- Witness: Taylor
- Source: PO-02, f. 182v–183r, session 10
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The London event is not described. See H-M02.

### H-F23 — Thanksgiving lists in The Glasgow Observer
- Claim: From April 1909 (24 April 1909) the Catholic weekly The Glasgow Observer printed weekly lists of thanks for favours. Numbers grew until the editor charged a fee; then they fell but never stopped. The issue of 16 July 1910 had six. Taylor counted 87 graces from April to July 1910, and more than 550 favours in total by August 1910.
- When / where: 1909–1910, Glasgow
- People: Taylor
- Witness: Taylor, from reading and counting
- Source: PO-02, f. 183r and 202v–203r, sessions 10 and 13
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Thanks came from Glasgow diocese mainly, and also from Ireland, England, the USA and New Zealand.

### H-F24 — Blackrock Carmel prioress laughed at the idea of a cause
- Claim: Around 1904, when Taylor spoke of Thérèse to the prioress of the Blackrock Carmel (Dublin), she laughed and said one could as well canonize all the Carmelites of her house. She later changed her mind before she died, as the next prioress told Taylor.
- When / where: c. 1904, Blackrock, Dublin
- People: prioress of Blackrock Carmel (unnamed); later prioress (former subprioress)
- Witness: Taylor heard her
- Source: PO-02, f. 184r, session 10
- Original words: « elle se mit à rire et me dit qu'on pourrait aussi bien faire canoniser toutes les carmélites de sa maison »
- Translation: She began to laugh and told me one might just as well have all the Carmelites of her house canonized.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Answer to Q28 on opposition.

### H-F25 — An Irish Carmelite at Lourdes distrusted Story of a Soul
- Claim: At the Lourdes Carmel, the only Irish nun (name forgotten) told Taylor that the book left her distrustful. As novice mistress she had known a Spanish novice who wrote very lofty things, then left and lost her fervour. Since then she distrusted "poet souls".
- When / where: August 1910, Lourdes Carmel
- People: an Irish Carmelite of Lourdes (unnamed)
- Witness: Taylor heard her
- Source: PO-02, f. 184r–184v, session 10
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### H-F26 — Archbishop Bourne delayed an indulgence because of the family's role
- Claim: Taylor asked Archbishop Bourne of Westminster for an indulgence for reading the English Story of a Soul, as some Portuguese dioceses had given. Bourne delayed. He had heard the affair was rushed, and that the family's part in it might compromise its success in Rome. Taylor thought this meant the Carmelite sisters.
- When / where: before August 1910, London
- People: Archbishop Francis Bourne; an unnamed intermediary priest
- Witness: Taylor, heard from the intermediary
- Source: PO-02, f. 186v–187r, session 11, 24 August 1910
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — second-hand report of the Archbishop's reasons
- Notes: On 8 August 1910 Bourne told Taylor he admired "this extraordinary soul" and promised a preface for the new English edition.

### H-F27 — Taylor on the surge of pictures and souvenirs
- Claim: Taylor said the Lisieux nuns never pushed him. When, three or four years earlier, pictures and souvenirs of Thérèse multiplied, he was at first surprised. He stopped being surprised when he saw how much people wanted them.
- When / where: c. 1906–1907 onward
- People: Taylor
- Witness: Taylor about himself
- Source: PO-02, f. 187v, session 11
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The judge asked directly whether his zeal was exaggerated; also whether the Lisieux Carmelites exaggerated. Taylor: "rather below the truth".

### H-F28 — Popular fame versus clerical fame

- When / where: 1909–1910, Britain
- People: none named

- Source: PO-02, f. 183r

### H-F32 — Thérèse foresaw pilgrimages and asked that her things be kept

- When / where: said 1897 (reported 1910)
- People: Thérèse; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; Sr Geneviève
- Witness: Taylor heard it from the two sisters

- Notes: The judge pressed on whether these were Thérèse's literal words; Taylor said "pilgrimage" may be the sisters' interpretation.

## PO-05 — Fr Élie de la Mère de Miséricorde OCD (Jérôme-Vincent Lanaro)

### H-F33 — Who Fr Élie was

### H-M37 — Grant's conversion: sense of her presence (Edinburgh, late 1909 – 1911)
- Period: after death
- What was reported: One evening late in 1909, fully awake, Grant felt an extraordinary sensation and told his wife: "That little girl is here… the Little Flower… I am sure she is in this room." The outer world seemed to vanish and he "saw" her inwardly. He seemed to hear: "This is how Catholic saints love Jesus; choose my little way." He answered he would try if she helped. In August 1910, amid rationalist doubts, he again felt her near and the words remained: "Can rationalism be true, and a life of such beauty and sweetness be a lie?" He bought her full Life in French in September 1910, on the very day a novena for him (unknown to him) ended. Later he heard: "Why do you ask me to pray for you and ignore the Blessed Virgin?"; he began to pray to Mary and felt an extraordinary love. At the end of March 1911, in a convent chapel during a novena to Thérèse, he felt Christ's presence in the tabernacle and told the nun: "Now I believe he is there."
- The person helped: Alexander James Grant
- Witnesses and reporter: Grant; his wife (present at the first); the nun who taught him French
- Status: sworn testimony at both processes; Langlois says it weighed heavily in the cause
- Source: PO-27, f. 1251r–1255v, session 80, 8 August 1911; PA-05, p. 312–314, 1915
- Medical or natural facts mentioned: first experience while ill with influenza
- Doubts / other versions: Grant says nothing in his upbringing or reading prepared him (he had read only St Teresa of Avila's Life and one 16th-century author, 30 years before). The 1915 account is shorter and more gradual; the 1911 account more visionary.
