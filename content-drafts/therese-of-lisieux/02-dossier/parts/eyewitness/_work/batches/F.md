## A. Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin (PO-13, PA-09)

### F-F01 — The witness is the nun Thérèse found antipathetic (editors' note)
- Claim: The modern editors state that Thérèse felt a strong natural antipathy for this witness. By her constant smiling kindness she made the nun believe the opposite. The unnamed sister of Manuscript C, fol. 13v–14r, is this witness.
- When / where: 1888–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin
- Witness: Editors' note (Teresianum 1976), not testimony
- Source: PA-09, editors' introduction
- Original words: « soeur Thérèse de l'Enfant‑Jésus éprouvait pour elle une forte antipathie naturelle »
- Translation: Sr Thérèse of the Child Jesus felt a strong natural antipathy toward her.
- New to English readers: partial (English readers know the Ms C story, not the name)
- Confidence: high — the editors state it plainly; the identification is traditional and also rests on TSA's own "Souvenirs"
- Notes: The witness herself never shows awareness of this. She says she had "a certain intimacy" with Thérèse (PO [577v]) and that "our souls understood each other" (PA [814]). The irony is useful for a writer. See also F-F155 (MdA's account of the same antipathy) and F-F52.

### F-F02 — TSA claims intimacy and personal memory only
- Claim: TSA says she lived near Thérèse "in a certain intimacy" from 1888 to 1897. She says she will use almost nothing from Story of a Soul or from other sisters. In 1915 she adds that "our souls understood each other."
- When / where: 1888–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: TSA; Thérèse
- Witness: TSA, about herself
- Source: PO-13 [577v], session 55, 14 Feb 1911; PA-09 [814], session 40, 2 Sept 1915
- Original words: « j'avais avec elle des relations assez intimes parce que nos âmes se comprenaient »
- Translation: I had fairly close relations with her because our souls understood each other.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high that she said it; see F-F01 for the irony
- Notes: She also says in PO that she will quote Thérèse's words "exactly" (PO [580r]).

### F-F04 — She carried the heavy statue of the Virgin of the Smile alone
- Claim: When the statue that had smiled at Thérèse in her childhood illness came to the monastery, no sister could carry it; all found it too heavy. Thérèse said it was not too heavy for her, seized it and carried it easily to the oratory meant for it.
- When / where: undated (after the statue came to the Carmel, probably 1894), Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; the community
- Witness: TSA, apparently SAW
- Source: PO-13 [579v], session 55, 14 Feb 1911
- Original words: « Elle n'est pas trop lourde pour moi »
- Translation: She is not too heavy for me.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — single witness; the date of the statue's arrival is not given
- Notes: Not repeated in PA-09.

### F-F05 — Prediction that Abbé Hodierne would become chaplain and give daily communion
- Claim: Thérèse told Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur that a time would come when Abbé Hodierne would be chaplain and would give daily communion. Asked why she thought so, she said she hoped he would come and "we will be very happy with him." At that time the chaplain, Abbé Youf, was in good health. Hodierne was named chaplain on 15 Oct 1897. His first sermon took the text "Come and eat my bread" and invited daily communion, unasked.
- When / where: undated (some years before 1897), Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin); Abbé Hodierne; Abbé Youf (chaplain)
- Witness: TSA, heard it from Marie du Sacré-Cœur before Thérèse's death
- Source: PO-13 [579r–579v], session 55, 14 Feb 1911
- Original words: « il viendra un temps où nous aurons peut-être pour aumônier monsieur l'abbé Hodierne, et lui, il nous donnera la communion tous les jours »
- Translation: A time will come when we will perhaps have Abbé Hodierne as chaplain, and he will give us communion every day.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — second-hand, but TSA says she heard it before the fulfilment
- Notes: MdA (PO-14 [603r]) confirms a prediction of daily communion without naming Hodierne. TSA writes that Youf's death came "several years after" the words were spoken; Youf in fact died in Oct 1897.

### F-F06 — The prioress obstructed daily communion after Leo XIII's decree (1915 version)
- Claim: Opposition: Thérèse badly wanted daily communion, which superiors then did not allow. When Leo XIII's decree (1891) took this right from superiors and gave it to the confessor, the Mother Prioress did not fully conform. She raised such difficulties that the confessor, out of prudence, did not use his authority. So Thérèse stayed deprived.
- Opposition detail: Mother Marie de Gonzague, prioress. Type: authority over sacraments (access to daily communion). Documented reason: none given; TSA says only that she did not want to leave the confessor full liberty.
- When / where: 1891–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; Mother Marie de Gonzague (prioress, not named in this passage but clearly meant); the confessor
- Witness: TSA, from her own observation of the community
- Source: PA-09 [817], session 40, 2 Sept 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — TSA's reading of community politics
- Notes: CONFLICT with MdA (PA-10 [872]), who says Abbé Youf "granted her this favour for several months." Also CONFLICT with TSA in 1911, who says only that daily communion "was not in use" (PO [579r]). TSA's 1915 version is much sharper about the prioress.

### F-F07 — TSA's reading of the "shower of roses" as daily communion
- Claim: TSA says the shower of roses Thérèse promised was, "without any doubt," the daily communion the community received right after her death and has had since without break.
- When / where: from Oct 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: TSA; the community
- Witness: TSA, her own interpretation
- Source: PA-09 [817], session 40, 2 Sept 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high that she held this view
- Notes: An unusual, local reading of the famous promise.

### F-F08 — Rising for early Mass in winter while ill
- Claim: In her last year, after nights without sleep and in pain, Thérèse still got up for an early Mass, even in the hardest cold, so as not to miss communion.
- When / where: winter 1896–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse
- Witness: TSA, SAW ("known to all the sisters")
- Source: PO-13 [578v], session 55; PA-09 [816–817], session 40
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: PA adds that she prepared for communion in union with the Virgin, asking Mary to "clothe" her with her dispositions.

### F-F09 — April 1895 prediction of death within two or three years
- Claim: In April 1895, in perfect health, Thérèse told TSA she would die soon, not in a few months but in two or three years; she felt her exile was nearly over. TSA later calls it a true prophecy; Thérèse died two years and five months later.
- When / where: April 1895, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; TSA
- Witness: TSA, HEARD Thérèse
- Source: PO-13 [580r] and [591v], sessions 55–56; PA-09 [844], session 42
- Original words: « Je mourrai bientôt. Je ne vous dis pas que ce soit dans quelques mois, mais dans deux ou trois ans »
- Translation: I will die soon. I do not say in a few months, but in two or three years.
- New to English readers: partial (known to specialists from TSA's "Souvenirs")
- Confidence: high — told consistently in both processes
- Notes: In PO she first said she knew no extraordinary fact in Thérèse's life, then corrected herself at the 30th question to add this prediction (PO [591v]).

### F-F22 — "Get out, then!": the prioress and the cough in the refectory
- Claim: Opposition: One day in the refectory Thérèse had a violent coughing fit. The prioress, tired of hearing it, said sharply: "Well then, go out, Sr Thérèse of the Child Jesus!" Thérèse left calmly. TSA was struck by her calm.

- When / where: undated, probably 1896–1897 (when she coughed), refectory, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; the prioress (Mother Marie de Gonzague, by context)
- Witness: TSA, SAW and HEARD

### F-F23 — Confession of the trial of faith: "I do not believe in eternal life"
- Claim: Thérèse confided to TSA, who was "strangely surprised": "If you knew what darkness I am plunged in. I do not believe in eternal life; it seems to me that after this mortal life there is nothing; everything has vanished for me; only love remains." She spoke of this as a temptation.
- When / where: 1896–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; TSA
- Witness: TSA, HEARD
- Source: PO-13 [583v–584r], session 55, 14 Feb 1911; PA-09 [835] (without the words)
- Original words: « Je ne crois pas à la vie éternelle; il me semble qu'après cette vie mortelle, il n'y a plus rien »
- Translation: I do not believe in eternal life; it seems to me that after this mortal life there is nothing more.
- New to English readers: yes — the bluntest wording of the trial in the processes
- Confidence: medium — single witness; the words are stronger than Thérèse's own written account; TSA insists her quotations are exact
- Notes: In PA (1915) TSA reports only that Thérèse confided "temptations against faith," without quoting these words. The softening may be deliberate.

### F-F24 — "She has no merit; she never had struggles"
- Claim: Opposition: TSA heard a sister say that Thérèse had no merit in practising virtue because she had never had struggles. In 1915 she adds that people "did not fear to say it loudly." About two months before Thérèse's death, TSA asked her directly. Thérèse said: "Oh, I have had them! I had a nature that was not easy; it did not show, but I felt it. Not one day without suffering, not one!" In 1915 TSA adds: "Ah, creatures! when they do not see, they do not believe!"
- Opposition detail: Unnamed sister(s) of the community. Type: belittling judgement. Documented reason (TSA): Thérèse's constant peace and ease made people think virtue cost her nothing.
- When / where: c. late July 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; TSA; an unnamed critic
- Witness: TSA, HEARD both the critic and Thérèse
- Source: PO-13 [584r], session 55; PA-09 [835], session 41
- Original words: « J'avais une nature pas commode; cela ne paraissait pas; mais moi, je le sentais bien »
- Translation: I had a difficult nature; it did not show, but I felt it well.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: A key "hidden temper" statement. Links with Léonie's remark that Thérèse wrote her nature was proud (F-F137).

### F-F25 — "No, no, let Him do it": refusing prayers for relief
- Claim: Opposition: Fearing sharper pain for her, TSA said she would pray God to soften it. Thérèse answered quickly: "No, no, one must let Him act." In 1915 TSA sets this after saying that in the last months the Mother Prioress "thought she ought to refuse to use the means of relieving her."
- Opposition detail: Mother Marie de Gonzague, prioress. Type: refusal of pain relief. Documented reason: none; TSA says she 'thought she ought to' refuse.
- When / where: summer 1897, infirmary
- People: Thérèse; TSA; the prioress (Marie de Gonzague)
- Witness: TSA, HEARD Thérèse; the prioress's refusal is her report of community knowledge
- Source: PO-13 [584r]; PA-09 [834], session 41
- Original words: « Non, non, il faut le laisser faire »
- Translation: No, no, one must let Him do it.
- New to English readers: partial (the prioress's refusal is new)
- Confidence: high for the words; medium for the refusal of relief
- Notes: CONFLICT on who blocked pain relief: TSA blames the prioress (PA [834]); MdA says God "permitted" that the doctor did not think of it (PO-14 [604r]) or "could not" give it (PA-10 [899]). See Gaps.

- When / where: from 1888, refectory, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; an unnamed sister
- Witness: TSA, observed
- Source: PO-13 [585r], session 55; PA-09 [836], session 41

### F-F29 — Deathbed joy at being called "not even a good religious"
- Claim: Opposition: Visiting Thérèse in her last illness, TSA found her radiant. Thérèse explained: a sister had visited and said, "If you knew how little you are loved and appreciated!" A few days earlier she had also heard a sister tell another: "I don't know why people talk so much about Sr Thérèse; she does nothing remarkable; we don't see her practise virtue; one cannot even say she is a good religious." Thérèse said: to hear on her deathbed that she was not a good religious — what joy!
- Opposition detail: Two unnamed sisters. Type: disparagement to her face and behind her back. Documented reason (TSA, 1911): the second spoke 'in a fit of bad temper.'
- When / where: summer 1897, infirmary
- People: Thérèse; TSA; two unnamed sisters
- Witness: TSA, HEARD Thérèse recount it
- Source: PO-13 [585r], session 55, 14 Feb 1911; PA-09 [842–843], session 42
- Original words: « on ne peut même pas dire qu'elle soit une bonne religieuse! »
- Translation: One cannot even say she is a good religious!
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: CONFLICT: in PO Thérèse "heard" the remark herself ("J'entendais aussi"); in PA the words "had been reported" to her. TSA says (PO) the speaker said it "in a fit of bad temper." The first remark ("how little you are loved") is dropped in PA. See F-F36 for the speaker's later fate.

- People: Thérèse; TSA
- Witness: TSA, HEARD
- Source: PO-13 [585v]; PA-09 [842]
- Original words: « un petit brin de mousse parmi les belles fleurs du bon Dieu »

- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-F31 — Community opinions: cold and proud, and jealousy of the four Martins
- Claim: Opposition: TSA heard differing opinions of Thérèse in her lifetime. Those who knew her best, especially her novices, admired her. For others she passed unnoticed because of her simplicity. Some judged her unfavourably: some accused her of coldness and pride. TSA thinks this was because she spoke little and stayed reserved, and perhaps because the presence of four sisters in the community stirred opposition or jealousy. She says the surviving opponents have all changed their minds.
- Opposition detail: Unnamed sisters. Type: accusation of coldness and pride; jealousy. Documented reasons (TSA's opinion): she spoke little and was reserved; four Martin sisters in one community stirred opposition or jealousy.
- When / where: 1888–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Thérèse; unnamed sisters
- Witness: TSA, HEARD these opinions
- Source: PO-13 [585v–586r], session 55, 14 Feb 1911
- Original words: « quelques-unes l'accusaient de froideur et de fierté »
- Translation: some accused her of coldness and pride.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — a sworn statement against interest
- Notes: CONFLICT with MdA, who said in 1911 she knew of no one with a different opinion (F-F79). In 1915 TSA says only "a small number" failed to see her virtue (PA [846–847]).

- Witness: TSA, about herself
- Source: PO-13 [586r], session 55
- Original words: « une charmante enfant et une excellente religieuse »
