## Fact cards

### B-F01 — Editors: longest deposition, long preparation, new emphasis
- Claim: The modern editors say this is the longest deposition of the Apostolic Process. They say it shows long, careful preparation, and that it stresses facts and words that she did not stress, or did not notice, in 1910.
- When / where: deposition 5–19 July 1915, sessions 11–21, Lisieux
- People: Mère Agnès; the Teresianum editors (1976)
- Witness: editors' note (not testimony)
- Source: PA 6, editors' introduction, before [340]
- Original words: —
- Translation: —
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — explicit editorial statement
- Notes: The editors say she used "Notes préparatoires" kept in the Lisieux archives. She chose facts so that the new deposition was not a copy of the 1910 one. This matters for any comparison between 1910 and 1915.

### B-F02 — Editors: three handwritten dossiers; the Gonzague memoir was later leaked
- Claim: She gave the court three documents in her own hand: (1) the setting of Thérèse's life in the Lisieux Carmel (mainly about Mère Marie de Gonzague); (2) the "Way of spiritual childhood"; (3) summaries of 54 miracle dossiers. The editors say the first was printed in full only in the 1920 Positio. Père Ubald d'Alençon published it too early, in the Barcelona review Estudis Franciscans, January 1926, and Mère Agnès suffered much from this.
- When / where: 1915; later 1920 and 1926
- People: Mère Agnès; P. Ubald d'Alençon; Mère Marie de Gonzague (died 1904, had living family)
- Witness: editors' note
- Source: PA 6, editors' introduction
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The editors also say that Benedict XV's speech on spiritual childhood (14 August 1921) drew on her "Voie d'enfance" paper.

### B-F03 — She swears no one told her what to say, and says her notes were made at the time
- Claim: She said she testified only for God's glory, would say what she knew personally, and that nobody had imposed on her what to say. She said Story of a Soul only helped her recall details she had herself witnessed. From June 1897 she wrote down at once all the words Thérèse said to her, and she quotes from those notes.
- When / where: 5 and 7 July 1915
- People: Mère Agnès
- Witness: the witness about herself
- Source: PA 6, [340]–[341], S11 5 Jul 1915; [396], S13 7 Jul 1915
- Original words: « personne ne m'a imposé ce que j'avais à dire »
- Translation: "no one imposed on me what I had to say"
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high — her own statement
- Notes: Compare with B-F20: the Gonzague memoir was corrected and signed by five other nuns before she read it in court. It is a group text, not only her personal memory.

### B-F04 — Baptism delayed two days for the godfather; mother anxious
- Claim: Thérèse was born 2 January 1873 but baptized only on the afternoon of 4 January, because the family waited for the godfather. Her mother was very upset by the delay and prayed that the baby would not die unbaptized.
- When / where: 2–4 January 1873, Alençon, rue Saint-Blaise
- People: Zélie Martin; the godfather (not named here)
- Witness: Mère Agnès, family memory (she was 11)
- Source: PA 6, [342], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: —

### B-F05 — Father closed his jewellery shop on Sundays despite lost sales
- Claim: Louis Martin closed his jewellery shop on Sundays. Friends called this excessive, because country people came to town on Sundays to buy wedding jewellery. They urged him to leave just a side door open. He answered that he preferred God's blessing.
- When / where: Alençon, before 1877
- People: Louis Martin; his friends
- Witness: Mère Agnès, saw and heard as a child
- Source: PA 6, [344], S11 5 Jul 1915

- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The 1910 deposition also mentions his strict Sunday rest.

### B-F08 — Why Zélie could not nurse: Pauline's theory of an old blow to the breast
- Claim: Zélie tried to breastfeed Thérèse, as she had tried with the others, and failed; she grieved much. Pauline supposes the cause was a blow to the breast in her youth, which also caused the cancer that killed her. Thérèse went to a wet nurse, Rose Taillé ("petite Rose"), at Semallé, and came home "blooming with health" on 11 April 1874.
- When / where: 1873–1874, Alençon and Semallé
- People: Zélie Martin; Rose Taillé
- Witness: Mère Agnès; her own guess ("je le suppose")
- Source: PA 6, [345]–[346], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial — the wet nurse is known; the blow theory is not
- Confidence: medium for facts; low for the medical theory
- Notes: —

- Claim: Zélie went to Lourdes in June 1877 to ask for her own cure and came back sicker. People heard her praying during nights of terrible pain. She died 28 August 1877.
- When / where: June–August 1877
- People: Zélie Martin
- Witness: Mère Agnès, present in the house

### B-F10 — After her mother's death: shy, cried easily, mostly from fear of giving pain
- Claim: After her mother died, Thérèse became very shy with strangers and so sensitive that a trifle made her cry. Pauline noticed that the cause of the tears was usually fear of having hurt her father, her sisters, or above all God.
- When / where: 1877–1886, Lisieux (Les Buissonnets)
- People: Thérèse; Pauline
- Witness: Mère Agnès, SAW
- Source: PA 6, [347], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial — adds the reason for the tears
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Pauline taught her at home until October 1881, when Thérèse became a day pupil at the Benedictine Abbey.

### B-F11 — Her 1883 illness: Dr Notta admitted he understood nothing; no trace in Carmel
- Claim: Pauline was already in Carmel. In the parlour Marie told her several times that Dr Notta admitted he did not understand the symptoms. Pauline adds that in Carmel no trace of these troubles ever came back; Thérèse was always calm, sensible and self-controlled. Pauline says "extraordinary symptoms" made people think the illness came from the devil.
- When / where: 1883, Les Buissonnets; later Carmel 1888–1897
- People: Dr Notta; Marie Martin (Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur)
- Witness: Mère Agnès; HEARD from Marie; she refers the court to Marie and Céline as direct witnesses
- Source: PA 6, [347]–[348], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial — Dr Notta's admission and her point about no relapse
- Confidence: medium — second-hand medical report
- Notes: The "no relapse" point looks aimed at the Promoter's likely doubt about hysteria.

### B-F12 — Scruples began at 12, lasted about 18 months; she was not like this before
- Claim: At 12, during the retreat before her second solemn communion, Thérèse became very scrupulous. Pauline says she had not known her this way; before, she was open and trusting and not troubled by small faults. After Marie entered Carmel (15 October 1886), Thérèse had no one to help her and prayed to her little brothers and sisters in heaven, and found peace. The trial lasted about a year and a half.
- When / where: 1885–1886, Lisieux
- People: Thérèse; Marie
- Witness: Mère Agnès (in Carmel then; partly from Thérèse's account)
- Source: PA 6, [348]–[349], S11 5 Jul 1915; also [405], S14
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Agrees with 1910 ("vers l'âge de 12 ans … un an et demi").

### B-F13 — She does not know why Thérèse left the Abbey school
- Claim: Asked why Thérèse left the Benedictine school at 13, Pauline said she did not know exactly; she thought it was because of her general health, and said Marie would know better.
- When / where: 1886
- People: Thérèse; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur
- Witness: Mère Agnès; admits ignorance
- Source: PA 6, [349], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high that she did not know
- Notes: Thérèse then finished her education at home with private lessons.

### B-F14 — Pauline admits she worked to bring Thérèse to Lisieux Carmel "for blessings"
- Claim: Asked if Thérèse chose Carmel to rejoin her sister, Pauline admitted that she herself did everything she could to draw Thérèse to the Lisieux Carmel. She says this was because she saw "a little saint who would draw great blessings on us". She believed Thérèse did not seek her company and would have gone to another Carmel if obedience had said so.
- When / where: 1882–1888
- People: Pauline; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès about herself
- Source: PA 6, [349]–[350], S11 5 Jul 1915
- Original words: « je voyais que c'était une petite sainte qui attirerait sur nous de grandes bénédictions »
- Translation: "I saw that she was a little saint who would draw great blessings on us"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — an admission against interest
- Notes: The judge asked a pointed question about family motives. This is a frank answer.

### B-F15 — Pauline sometimes objected too, under Marie's influence
- Claim: When Thérèse, at 14, spoke seriously of entering Carmel, Pauline was the only one who encouraged her. Marie said she was too young. Pauline says that she herself, influenced by Marie, sometimes raised objections too.
- When / where: 1887
- People: Pauline; Marie; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès about herself
- Source: PA 6, [350], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### B-F16 — Canon Delatroëtte's outburst in Mère Geneviève's infirmary
- Claim: On a great feast, the superior, Abbé Delatroëtte, entered the enclosure to visit the foundress, Mère Geneviève, in the infirmary. At Mère Marie de Gonzague's request, Mère Geneviève asked him before the whole community to let Thérèse enter at Christmas. He answered with emotion: was the community's salvation hanging on this child? She should stay with her father until she came of age. He said he had consulted God and did not want to hear of it again.
- When / where: autumn 1887, Lisieux Carmel infirmary
- People: Abbé Delatroëtte; Mère Geneviève de Sainte-Thérèse; Mère Marie de Gonzague; the community
- Witness: Mère Agnès, present (she was in the community)
- Source: PA 6, [350]–[351], S11 5 Jul 1915
- Original words: « Il n'y a pas de péril en la demeure. Qu'elle reste chez son père jusqu'à sa majorité. »
- Translation: "There is no hurry. Let her stay with her father until she comes of age."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high — eyewitness, direct speech
- Notes: The 1910 deposition says only that he found her too young.

### B-F17 — Pauline herself asked for the three-month delay after the bishop's yes
- Claim: On 28 December 1887 the bishop allowed immediate entry. Mère Marie de Gonzague still made Thérèse wait three more months. Pauline says the prioress was influenced by the superior's lasting displeasure, and "also asked by me", because Pauline feared the Lenten austerity for Thérèse's first weeks.
- When / where: January–April 1888
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Abbé Delatroëtte; Pauline
- Witness: Mère Agnès about herself
- Source: PA 6, [351], S11 5 Jul 1915
- Original words: « sollicitée aussi par moi, qui redoutais pour les débuts de Thérèse l'austérité du Carême »
- Translation: "urged also by me, for I feared the austerity of Lent for Thérèse's beginnings"
- New to English readers: yes — Story of a Soul does not say Pauline caused the delay
- Confidence: high — admission against interest
- Notes: In 1910 she spoke of "we at the Carmel" thinking it wise to wait. In 1915 she names herself.

### B-F18 — The superior's cold "Te Deum" at her entry
- Claim: On 9 April 1888, with the enclosure door wide open and Louis Martin present, Delatroëtte presented her: they could sing a Te Deum; he presented this 15-year-old child whom they had wanted; if she disappointed them, they alone would bear the blame. The whole community was "frozen".
- When / where: 9 April 1888, enclosure door, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Abbé Delatroëtte; Louis Martin; the community
- Witness: Mère Agnès, present
- Source: PA 6, [351]–[352], S11 5 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high
- Notes: 1910 gives the same speech but addressed to "Ma révérende mère" (singular). The 1915 version adds the open door, the father present, and the community's reaction.

### B-F19 — Delatroëtte later wept and called her an angel
- Claim: It took the superior several years to change his mind. Later Pauline saw him moved to tears when speaking of Sr Thérèse, whom he called an angel. Elsewhere she says he wept after hearing her speak of God and told the prioress she was an angel.
- When / where: early 1890s
- People: Abbé Delatroëtte
- Witness: Mère Agnès, SAW him in tears
- Source: PA 6, [352], S11 5 Jul 1915; [519], S20 16 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium-high
- Notes: She also says Thérèse never said a bitter word about him ([385]).

### B-F20 — The Gonzague memoir is a group document signed by five nuns
- Claim: Before reading her memoir on the community and Mère Marie de Gonzague, she said she had shown it to five sisters who knew the facts. They suggested corrections, which she made, and they signed the final text. She called it "a community document". Eight other nuns who knew Mère de Gonzague were not shown it, because she thought it painful to wake these memories; she was convinced they would agree. The signers were: Sr Marie des Anges, Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin, Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Sr Geneviève, Sr Marie de la Trinité.
- When / where: 6 July 1915
- People: judge-delegate Auguste Quirié (who asked); the five signers
- Witness: Mère Agnès; the signers' notes
- Source: PA 6, [356]–[357], [369]–[370], S12 6 Jul 1915
- Original words: « C'est donc comme un document de communauté que je communique au tribunal. »
- Translation: "So I give it to the court as a community document."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Three of the five signers are her own sisters. Sr Marie des Anges added that she was present at the 16 July 1867 incident and saw "many other things". Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin wrote that the text is "far from exaggerated".

### B-F21 — Portrait of Mère Marie de Gonzague: charm, instability, jealousy
- Claim: Marie Davy de Virville entered on 29 September 1860, aged 26, and became Sr Marie de Gonzague. She was tall, distinguished, with a very pleasant voice, pious and simple. But the memoir calls her "badly balanced": at times too gay, at times in black melancholy over nothing, with strange moods despite robust health. She did extraordinary penances and had "a heart of gold", but an often unconscious jealousy grew with the years and caused clashes and "terrible scenes".
- When / where: 1860–1904
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Mère Agnès and the five signers; SAW from 1882
- Source: PA 6, [358], S12 6 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes (in this detail)
- Confidence: medium — hostile group document, but by close witnesses
- Notes: She was sub-prioress from 8 July 1866 and prioress from 22 October 1874; the memoir says she held the office 21 years in all. The superior Abbé Cagniard put her in office early "to cure her odd temper" — the memoir calls this "a fatal error".

### B-F22 — 16 July 1867: the sub-prioress hid in the garden and tried to jump from a window
- Claim: After a fit of jealousy, sub-prioress Marie de Gonzague disappeared until night. Sisters found her crouched in a corner of the garden behind a ladder. Led to the prioress's cell, she was about to throw herself from the first-floor window when a lay sister caught her. A rumour spread outside that the Carmel's sub-prioress was mad; her family heard something. Mère Geneviève's prudence slowly stifled it.
- When / where: 16 July 1867, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mère Geneviève; an unnamed lay sister
- Witness: memoir (before Pauline's time); Sr Marie des Anges signed that she was present
- Source: PA 6, [359], S12 6 Jul 1915; [369]
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — Pauline HEARD it; one signer claims to have SEEN it
- Notes: Before Thérèse's time, but it frames the prioress she lived under.

### B-F23 — Admissions: she pushed through a hysterical nun and one who lied and stole
- Claim: As prioress she almost imposed her will in votes on candidates. She was won by outward charm, a fine voice, and affection shown to her. A "hysterical" nun was admitted through her insistence and made her weep much. Another, "with the same illness", indiscreet, with a mania for lying and stealing without noticing, was also received by her.
- When / where: 1874–1893
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; two unnamed nuns
- Witness: memoir
- Source: PA 6, [359]–[360], S12 6 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium
- Notes: The nuns are not named. The memoir says "one can guess what the formation of the subjects was".

### B-F24 — Flattery needed to be "in favour"; chaplain Youf on "politics"
- Claim: To be "in court" with Mère de Gonzague one had to flatter her or act like a diplomat. The chaplain of 25 years, Abbé Youf, said it was sad that souls who came to Carmel for simplicity had to "play politics" there. The memoir says secrecy and cunning were sometimes truly needed to avoid scandal.
- When / where: undated, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Abbé Youf
- Witness: memoir; Youf's words heard by the writer(s)
- Source: PA 6, [360], S12 6 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium
- Notes: See B-Q17.

### B-F26 — Daily Communion given, then withdrawn, because of the prioress's jealousy
- Claim: After the 1891 decrees took from superiors the right to regulate Communions, the confessor let some sisters communicate daily and others less often. Mère de Gonzague accepted the decree in principle but showed jealousy. Abbé Youf grew afraid and made the number of Communions the same for all again. Thérèse had hoped much from the decrees (she had prayed to St Joseph for them) and was deeply disappointed.
- When / where: 1891 onward
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Abbé Youf; Thérèse
- Witness: Mère Agnès, SAW; the disappointment from Thérèse
- Source: PA 6, [360], S12; [379], S12 6 Jul 1915; [389]
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium-high
- Notes: Youf later told Pauline: "When I think that I am not free to allow daily Communion to this so perfect nun" ([519]). See also the prediction B-M07.

### B-F27 — The prioress's cat: calf's liver, sweet milk, roasted birds, night searches
- Claim: Mère de Gonzague had a cat she fed with calf's liver and sweetened milk. If it caught a bird, the bird was roasted for it with a fine sauce. When the cat was lost, the prioress went looking for it in the evening, during the Great Silence, with the white-veiled sisters, calling it everywhere, even over the wall into a neighbour's garden. The whole community was upset.
- When / where: undated, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; the lay sisters
- Witness: memoir; the community SAW
- Source: PA 6, [360]–[361], S12 6 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium-high — a public, repeated scene
- Notes: The memoir calls it "ridiculous" and "a fault against poverty".

### B-F28 — A sick young sister treated in secret; "a sin to treat such illnesses"

- When / where: undated (a time when Gonzague was out of office)
- People: an unnamed young sister; Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: memoir
- Source: PA 6, [361], S12 6 Jul 1915
- New to English readers: yes

### B-F31 — Election "campaigns"; she steered votes to Pauline in 1893 to keep control

### B-F33 — 1896 election: seven ballots; slips with her name "lost" on purpose
- Claim: Mère de Gonzague worked against Pauline's re-election and won, but only at the seventh ballot, a "hard lesson" she suffered from all her life. After the election, some sisters deliberately mislaid voting slips with her name, so that the prioress would find them and not suspect them.
- When / where: 21 March 1896
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; unnamed sisters

### B-F34 — The prioress was shut out of the chapter for Céline's profession vote
- Claim: Early 1896, hoping soon to replace Mère Agnès, Mère de Gonzague tried to delay the professions of Sr Geneviève (Céline) and Sr Marie de la Trinité so she could receive them herself. The superior, Canon Maupas, said aloud that the prioress must present both. Mère de Gonzague went pale, then told her party she would allow Céline's profession but formally opposed Marie de la Trinité's, though the latter had two months more of novitiate. Using the rule that a sister may not vote for her sister, she put the prioress (Agnès) out of the chapter, presided over the three sessions herself, and at the end left Agnès standing at the back with the novices and lay sisters.
- When / where: January–February 1896, Lisieux Carmel chapter
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Mère Agnès; Canon Maupas; Sr Geneviève; Sr Marie de la Trinité
- Witness: Mère Agnès, SAW; about herself
