# Batch J — Céline's "Conseils et souvenirs" and Marie's "Souvenirs autobiographiques"

## Source keys used in this file

- **CS** = Sr Geneviève de la Sainte Face (Céline Martin), "Conseils et souvenirs d'une novice". URL: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-genevieve-de-la-sainte-face/conseils-et-souvenirs-dune-novice/ . The page calls it "old notes of Céline which she used for her testimonies at the Processes". The page gives NO date for the compiled text. Internal evidence: it cites Benedict XV's discourse of 14 Aug 1921, a note of 6 June 1934, Marie's death on 19 Jan 1940, Pius XII in 1937, and a papal message of 11 July 1954. So this compiled form dates from 1954 or later (Céline died 1959). Numbers in parentheses in the page text, e.g. "(172)", are page numbers of a printed book; I give them as "book p." Many passages carry bracketed notes by an editor (likely Céline herself or the Carmel); I mark those "editor's note".
  Sections: I Maîtresse des novices; II Pauvreté spirituelle, esprit d'enfance, confiance; III Amour de Dieu, union à Dieu; IV Charité fraternelle et zèle des âmes; V Fidélité, obéissance, pauvreté, mortification; VI Force dans la souffrance, sainteté et gloire; VII Dernière maladie.
- **MSC** = Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin), "Souvenirs autobiographiques de Marie du Sacré-Cœur", "Cahier de 1909", written for Mère Agnès (Pauline). URL: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-marie-du-sacre-coeur/souvenirs-autobiographiques-de-marie-du-sacre-coeur/ . Main notebook begun "on the evening of All Saints" (year not stated; probably 1 Nov 1908) and dated at its end 24 May 1909. "Feuilles ajoutées" (12 added pages) dated 29 June 1910. Written by Marie at age 48–50, 12–13 years after Thérèse's death, and just before the Ordinary Process opened (Aug 1910). She refers once to "ma déposition" for Thérèse's childhood illness.

Note on "New to English readers": the baseline is the standard Story of a Soul. Céline's book exists in an older English translation ("A Memoir of My Sister St Thérèse", 1959) and some last-illness sayings are in the English "Last Conversations". Where I know a saying is in Last Conversations, I mark "partial".

## Fact cards — Céline (CS)

### J-F01 — Thérèse ran the novitiate in practice from 1893, fully from March 1896
- Claim: After Mère Agnès became prioress (20 Feb 1893), she named Mère Marie de Gonzague novice mistress and quietly asked Thérèse (aged 20) to look after the novices. When Gonzague was re-elected prioress on 21 March 1896, she kept the novice-mistress title herself but, on Agnès's advice, left nearly all the work to Thérèse, who was mistress in all but name until her death.
- When / where: 1893–1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès de Jésus (Pauline); Mère Marie de Gonzague
- Witness: Céline's introduction (editorial framing, not a sworn statement)
- Source: CS §I, opening paragraphs
- New to English readers: partial (SoS mentions the novices, not the mechanics)
- Confidence: high — standard chronology
- Notes: The novices in 1893 were only two lay sisters, Sr Marthe de Jésus and Sr Marie-Madeleine du Saint-Sacrement; then Sr Marie de la Trinité (16 June 1894), Céline (14 Sept 1894), Marie Guérin / Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie (15 Aug 1895).

### J-F02 — Daily novice meeting, 2:30 to 3:00 after Vespers, only from March 1896
- Claim: Only after March 1896 did Thérèse gather the novices every day after Vespers, from 2:30 to 3:00. She gave no formal lectures. She read or had them read parts of the Rule, Constitutions or the "Papier d'exaction" (customary), explained, answered questions, corrected faults, and chatted about spiritual matters or current work.
- When / where: March 1896–1897, novitiate
- People: Thérèse; novices
- Witness: Céline, novice — SAW
- Source: CS §I, paragraphs 4–5
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Shows that the 1893–96 period was informal "discreet" care, not a daily class.

### J-F03 — Thérèse was never a capitular nun
- Claim: Although she should have left the novitiate three years after profession (Sept 1893), Thérèse stayed in it all her life. A common reading of the rules then allowed no more than two sisters of one family in the Chapter. Because Pauline and Marie were capitulars, Thérèse never had seat or vote in the conventual Chapter. She stayed with the novices as their "doyenne". Céline says Thérèse was glad to be counted "une petite".
- When / where: 1893–1897
- People: Thérèse; Mère Agnès; Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur
- Witness: editor's note in CS
- Source: CS §II "Humilité", bracketed note
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Related: she kept asking weekly permissions like a novice although she could have asked monthly (J-F71).

### J-F04 — At first she argued with novices; then she changed method
- Claim: At the start of her charge, when novices complained about each other, Thérèse tried to calm them by reasoning and by showing the other sister was not wrong. This led to long, useless discussions. She saw this quickly and changed her method: she made the novice look straight at her grievance.
- When / where: early in her charge (1893–95), novitiate
- People: Thérèse; Céline and others
- Witness: Céline — SAW / experienced
- Source: CS §I "Sa prudence"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: A rare admission that Thérèse's first approach failed.

### J-F05 — The wood-box story
- Claim: Céline complained that her work partner had not filled the wood box that week, though Céline always did it with care. Thérèse first agreed ("let us admit your companion is entirely in the wrong"), then led her to accept being wronged, then told her hidden virtues of the accused novice. If Thérèse knew the box had in fact been filled, she held back this news until Céline was at peace, and only then said simply "I know the box is filled".
- When / where: undated (Saturday), novitiate
- People: Céline; an unnamed novice companion
- Witness: Céline — experienced
- Source: CS §I "Sa prudence"
- Original words: « Je sais que le coffre est rempli. »
- Translation: "I know the box has been filled."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Céline says Thérèse sometimes let novices discover such things themselves, to show how many struggles rest on imagination.

### J-F06 — She asked God never to be loved "humanly" as mistress
- Claim: Thérèse told Céline in confidence that on taking charge of the novices she asked God never to be loved humanly, and that this was granted. Céline: "we loved her very much" but none had a foolish attachment; they went to her "from a need for truth".
- When / where: 1893 onward
- People: Thérèse; novices
- Witness: Céline — HEARD Thérèse
- Source: CS §I "Sa prudence"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — a confided statement; Céline's own framing
- Notes: Céline adds a footnote quoting Mère Agnès's process deposition (Summarium §1552) that Thérèse never sought popularity with novices.

### J-F14 — The "bonne volonté" story from Fr Surin
- Claim: When Céline was sad after Thérèse had shown her all her faults, Céline read a Gertrude passage on good will. Thérèse capped it with a story from the life of Fr Surin: during an exorcism the devils said they could overcome everything except "that bitch of good will". Thérèse told Céline she had "a little dog" that would lead her to Paradise.
- When / where: undated, novitiate
- Witness: Céline — HEARD

### J-F16 — Christmas 1887: in agony, yet pleased with her new blue hat
- Claim: On Christmas night 1887, when she had hoped to be in Carmel, Thérèse's soul was "in agony". Yet she later told Céline she was pleased to wear for the first time her pretty blue hat decorated with a white dove. Editor's note: a navy-blue wool toque with matching velvet, same cloth as her dress.
- When / where: Christmas 1887, Lisieux
- Witness: Céline — HEARD Thérèse tell it later in Carmel
- Source: CS §II "Un petit moyen"
- Original words: « j'ai cependant été contente d'étrenner mon joli chapeau bleu, orné d'une colombe blanche ! »
- Translation: "all the same I was pleased to wear my pretty blue hat with a white dove for the first time!"
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Thérèse used it to show how strange "the returns of nature" are.

### J-F22 — Céline versus the Promoter of the Faith
- Claim: At the Process, the Promoter of the Faith asked Céline why she wanted the beatification. She said: only to make the Little Way known. He replied that if she spoke of a "way", the Cause would certainly fail, as in similar cases. She answered: too bad; she would not drop the only point that mattered to her. The Cause did not fail. She says she felt more joy at Benedict XV's discourse on spiritual childhood (14 Aug 1921) than at the beatification and canonisation.
- When / where: Process (1910–1917); 14 Aug 1921
- People: Céline; the Promoter of the Faith (not named)
- Witness: Céline — her own account
- Source: CS §II "Esprit d'enfance"
- Original words: « Si vous parlez de voie, la Cause tombera infailliblement »
- Translation: "If you speak of a 'way', the Cause will certainly fall."
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — Céline's later memory of her own exchange
- Notes: Process fact. Shows pressure on witnesses and Céline's resistance.

### J-F41 — The Offering to Merciful Love: Céline was with her, 9–11 June 1895
- Claim: On Trinity Sunday, 9 June 1895, during Mass, Thérèse was inspired to offer herself. After Mass, moved, she led Céline (not knowing why) to Mère Agnès, who was going to the turn. Two days later, Tuesday 11 June, both knelt before the Virgin of the Smile (then in the work room beside Thérèse's cell) and Thérèse pronounced the Act for both.
- When / where: 9 and 11 June 1895, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Mère Agnès; Céline
- Witness: Céline — SAW, took part
- Source: CS §III "L'offrande à l'Amour miséricordieux"

- Confidence: high
- Notes: The same paragraph dates "Vivre d'amour" to adoration during the Forty Hours, Tuesday 26 Feb 1895, "composed in one go".

### J-F42 — Marie refused the Offering at first ("God would take me at my word")

## Gaps and conflicts

- **Dating of Céline's text.** The page calls it Céline's old notes used for the Processes, but the text as published cites events up to 1954. It is a late, edited compilation (with bracketed doctrinal notes, e.g. against quietism, and notes framing sayings as "not for everyone"). Treat wording as Céline's mature, edited memory, not a 1910 note. Book page numbers appear in parentheses.
- **Editorial softening.** Several bracketed notes restrict Thérèse's sayings (repugnance for meals "not part of the Little Way"; "passer dessous" meant only for novices; "no latitude" not a universal rule). These show the editors managing her image.
- **Céline's own doubts.** Céline admits she once suspected Thérèse did not suffer as much as believed (J-F138), found her too severe about the dance (J-F105), and thought her obedience to a sister "too blind" (J-F78).
- **Cool or hostile views recorded:** family (Guérins implied) thought her "insignificant" and poorly educated (J-F88); some called her ideas "presumption" (J-F52); an older sister found her "irregular" for taking remedies (J-F151); Sr Marie des Anges said she "does nothing but complain" (J-F76); "Th. de J." said she had "nothing rare" (J-F158); a sick "Mère" deliberately harassed novices (J-F91); relatives criticised Louis's 10,000-franc altar (J-F39).
- **The Process.** Céline says the Promoter of the Faith warned that "a way" would sink the Cause, and she refused to change (J-F22). She also says supernatural gifts were "very rare" (J-F23). This conflicts with any later hagiography stressing marvels.
- **Community tension.** Marie writes of Pauline's "years of martyrdom" from the jealousy of an unnamed "Mère***", and of the "pearl" crushed "because of the diamond" (J-F159). The name is hidden. Also "a great trial… Heaven alone has its secret" (undefined).
- **Care of the sick.** Céline calls the care "primitive" (no mattress; J-F137) and says it was "too late" to act on Thérèse's advice to infirmarians (J-F80). This supports other criticisms of Gonzague's nursing regime, though Céline also shows Gonzague ordering a foot-warmer (J-F40).
- **Date conflict.** Marie says Louis woke her "about eleven" to announce Zélie's death; the usual time is after midnight, 28 Aug 1877. Marie also says she was "13" at Thérèse's birth (she was 12).
- **Joan fire date.** Céline does not date the fire. Marie's text shows the Joan plays were Jan 1894 and Jan 1895; the fire is traditionally the 1895 performance.
- **Shower of roses.** Marie gives a precise trigger (Aloysius reading, her week as reader). Céline says the promise was made many times in many forms.
- **Expected but absent:** Céline's text names almost no difficult sisters (all are "une Soeur", "une Mère ancienne"). No doctor is named in the pointes-de-feu scene. Marie's notebook says almost nothing about Thérèse in Carmel beyond entry, the Joan play and the roses; she defers childhood illness to her deposition. Marie never mentions the Offering refusal (known only via Céline, J-F42).
- **English availability.** Much of Céline's book was translated in 1959 ("A Memoir of My Sister St Thérèse"); several last-illness sayings are in "Last Conversations". I marked those "partial". Marie's 1909 notebook is, as far as I know, not in English.
