# Miracle list — official documents, favours collections, after death (M-OM-)

Each entry is a **report**. Status labels: **approved** (for beatification or canonization) | **investigated** (canonical inquiry, not approved) | **recorded by the Carmel** (sent to or published by the Carmel of Lisieux, including accounts printed by Taylor from the *Shower of Roses*) | **popular report** (told by a writer or witness, not known to be in the Carmel collection).
Locators as in `sources.md`. Taylor 1924 = D6; Taylor 1926/30 = D7.

## A. The four approved miracles

### M-OM-001 — Healing of the seminarian Charles Anne, Lisieux / Bayeux, 1906
- Period: after death — modern (1906)
- What was reported: A young cleric of Lisieux, studying at the Bayeux seminary, was dying of tuberculosis. After a first novena (with a relic hung round his neck) failed, he held a relic of her and prayed silently in a surge of confidence; at once he sat up, the struggle for breath, pain and fever stopped, and he wanted to get up. Doctors called in haste found him well; Laveille says "new lungs" had formed "instead of the lungs which were consumed". His own account (Carmel) says he prayed with a sachet of her hair and improved "from the next day" (« Dès le lendemain ») after starting a second novena.
- The person helped: Charles Anne, native of Lisieux, aged 23 (Carmel; Taylor). Health failed from June 1904; collapse after a haemorrhage on 23 Aug 1906 (Laveille; Carmel: taken to bed 23 Aug 1906); large cavities in the lungs, burning fever, total aversion to food; death expected within days.
- His words (Laveille PDF 410): "Little Thérèse, you are in heaven; of that I feel sure. I am on earth where there is good to be done. You must cure me." Taylor (PDF 276): "I did not come here to die! I came to work for God! You must cure me!"
- Witnesses and reporter: the nursing sister, Sœur Marie Saint-Ignace (Clémence François) (Carmel); Dr Paul Loisnel (Carmel); first-person account by Anne dated 29 Jan 1907 (Carmel). Taylor heard the story from Anne himself in Rome in 1923; Anne and the old nun who nursed him attended the beatification. On 3–4 Aug 1917, during the Apostolic Process, Drs Adolphe-Jean Leprévost and Alexandre-Damase de Cornière (the Carmel doctor who treated Thérèse) examined him and found him in excellent health (Carmel).
- Status: **approved for beatification** — decree of 11 Feb 1923
- Source: AAS 15 pp. 169–170 (decree); AAS 17 p. 341 (bull); Laveille PDF 409–411 (cites Summarium of 1919 p. 989 § 2844); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 276; Taylor 1924 PDF 21; Carmel "Les miracles de la Béatification". Public-domain (AAS, Laveille, Taylor); unknown (Carmel).
- Official diagnosis: « tubercolosi florida, hemoptoica, in periodo cavitaria » — "florid tuberculosis with haemoptysis, in the cavity stage". Three experts appointed by the Congregation agreed it was an organic lesion that nature could not restore in that way (AAS 15 p. 169; AAS 17 p. 341). The bull calls it a miracle "of the same nature and earlier" than Sœur Louise's.
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: Taylor says the first novena was to Our Lady of Lourdes through her intercession, the second to her alone "in order that the saving of his priestly vocation might be manifestly due to her". Exact day of cure not given (between late Aug 1906 and 29 Jan 1907). Later life: priest; chaplain of the public hospital of Lisieux, serving 800 patients (Laveille PDF 410–411, 1925).
- Medical facts in the source: large pulmonary cavities; tubercle bacillus in sputum; a deep cavity in the right lung and damaged bronchi (Carmel); two doctors judged the case incurable (Carmel). Treatment named: none beyond nursing; prayer, relic.

### M-OM-002 — Healing of Sœur Louise de Saint-Germain, Ustaritz, September 1916
- Period: after death — modern (1916, during the war)
- What was reported: A Daughter of the Cross with a bleeding stomach ulcer received the last sacraments in 1915 and asked only for a good death. In early September 1916 another sister persuaded her to pray again. On the night of 10 Sep 1916 Thérèse appeared and said: "Be generous; you will soon be cured; I promise it." In the morning the three sisters sleeping in the infirmary found rose petals of different colours scattered round her bed. She grew worse; on the evening of 21 Sep, after a violent attack, she fell asleep and woke the next morning cured, hungry, asking to go to Mass; the superior sent her back to bed and gave her a big breakfast; she went back to her duties.
- The person helped: Sœur Louise de Saint-Germain, Congregation of the Daughters of the Cross, provincial house of Ustaritz (Basses-Pyrénées); a teacher attached to a Spanish convent; ill since her novitiate (1911–12, Taylor) or from 1912 (Laveille) / early 1913 after profession (Carmel).
- Witnesses and reporter: her own account (Laveille, citing *Journal des Pèlerins*, 5–18 Oct 1924); three infirmary sisters (petals); her superior.
- Status: **approved for beatification** — decree of 11 Feb 1923
- Source: AAS 15 pp. 169–170; AAS 17 pp. 340–341; Laveille PDF 425–426; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 276–277; Taylor 1924 PDF 21; Carmel "Les miracles de la Béatification".
- Official diagnosis: « gravissimo ulcere ventriculi, formae hemorragicae » — "a most grave ulcer of the stomach, haemorrhagic in form"; three experts agreed (AAS 17 p. 341).
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: **Date of cure:** Laveille and the Carmel: fell asleep evening of 21 Sep, woke cured 22 Sep 1916. Taylor: woke cured "on the morning of September 25". Taylor also says the last sacraments came "in 1915" and Laveille "at the beginning of 1915"; the Carmel says summer 1915. Taylor 1926/30 PDF 23: "a great picture in St. Peter's" showed the shower of roses at this cure (1925).
- Medical facts in the source: frequent haemorrhages; twice tried to return to teaching and was stopped by haemorrhages (Laveille); crisis on 14 Nov 1913 with 25 days of water only; recurrence 1915 with heavy bleeding, surgery thought necessary; 32 days on iced water only; ulcer running through the pylorus to the upper duodenum (Carmel). Taylor: certificate of her doctor "confirmed by an X-ray photograph and by Dr. le Bec of Paris, the famous surgeon, who wrote a remarkable paper to prove the supernatural character of the cure" (the Carmel page does not name Le Bec).

### M-OM-003 — Healing of Sister Gabriella Trimusi, Parma, June 1923
- Period: after death — modern (1923, after the beatification)
- What was reported: The bull says: she was 23 when she joined the Poor Daughters of the Sacred Hearts (mother house at Parma; called "Chieppine" by Taylor and Laveille); in 1913 her left knee began to hurt; doing housework, she used to break firewood across that knee; two doctors' remedies failed; after three years she was sent to "Medianum" (probably Milan) for heliotherapy, baths, blisters, injections; four years later her spine was affected; back in Parma several doctors judged the lesion tubercular; an X-ray of the knee showed periostitis of the head of the tibia; she was admitted to the public hospital and given radiation; while there she caught the "Spanish" influenza and the back pain grew worse. On 13 Jun 1923, on the advice of the ecclesiastical assistant, a novena began before a small picture with a prayer to Blessed Teresa; she prayed more for other sisters than for herself. The last day of the novena was the last day of the triduum for the beatification at the nearby Carmelite church; she went there with other sisters, came back slowly and painfully to the house chapel; when the superior told her to pray and go to her place, she knelt on the sick knee without any pain. After supper she went upstairs, took off her corset (« loricam ») in the first room, and cried out, "Sanata sum! sanata sum!" ("I am cured! I am cured!"). She returned to all her work.
- The person helped: Sister Gabriella (Gabrielle) Trimusi, Poor Daughters of the Sacred Hearts, Parma
- Witnesses and reporter: her superior and sisters; apostolic process at Parma validated 11 Jun 1924.
- Status: **approved for canonization** — decree of 19 Mar 1925
- Source: AAS 17 pp. 148–150 (decree; p. 150 image checked); AAS 17 pp. 342–343 (bull); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 279–280; Laveille PDF 438; Carmel "Historique" ("double tubercular disease").
- Official diagnosis: « a chronica arthrosinovite sinistri genu, simulque a chronica spondilite infimae spinae dorsualis, utraque naturae tubercularis » — chronic arthrosynovitis of the left knee and chronic spondylitis of the lower dorsal spine, both tubercular; "Koch's bacilli" first in the knee, then the spine (AAS 17 p. 149).
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: The cause's lawyer argued it counted as two miracles; the Congregation did not accept this (F-OM-068). Taylor: "arthritis in the knee, and … tuberculosis of the spine"; at the end of the triduum "she felt an irresistible impulse to discard all artificial support". Taylor dates the triduum "June, 1923". The bull does not give the exact day of the cure (the novena began 13 Jun 1923; nine days suggests about 21–22 Jun; not stated).
- Medical facts in the source: two doctors, then several; heliotherapy, baths, vesicatories, injections at "Medianum"; general remedies; X-ray; radiation in hospital; Spanish flu; the corset.

### M-OM-004 — Healing of Maria Pellemans at the tomb, Lisieux, March 1923
- Period: after death — modern (1923)
- What was reported: The bull, from her own account: in October 1909 she already had recognized pulmonary tuberculosis; later tubercular enteritis and gastritis appeared; she was treated at home, then in the sanatorium "La Hulpe". Back home, in August 1920 she went on pilgrimage to Lourdes without result. In March 1923 she joined a pilgrimage group visiting the Lisieux Carmel and, praying with confidence on the tomb of Blessed Teresa, at once recovered perfect health. Taylor: a young Belgian girl who came "in a dying state" a few days before the translation; "next day, March 24, at the grave … she was suddenly and completely restored"; the disease had for many years ravaged her.
- The person helped: Maria (Marie) Pellemans, Belgian ("young Belgian girl", Laveille and Taylor)
- Witnesses and reporter: her own narrative (bull); apostolic process at Mechelen (Malines) validated 11 Jun 1924; Canon Barette of Brussels preached on the cure at Lisieux in Sept 1925 (Laveille PDF 444).
- Status: **approved for canonization** — decree of 19 Mar 1925
- Source: AAS 17 pp. 149–150 (decree), 343–344 (bull, image checked); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 279; Laveille PDF 438; Carmel "Historique".
- Official diagnosis: « a chronica pulmonum atque intestinorum tuberculosi » — chronic tuberculosis of the lungs and intestines. Three doctors appointed by the Congregation gave favourable written opinions on both canonization cures (AAS 17 p. 344).
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: Date: "March 1923" (bull) and "March 24" (Taylor). The grave was then still in the cemetery (translation on 26 Mar). The plan asked "at her tomb, 1923, to verify": confirmed.
- Medical facts in the source: sanatorium La Hulpe; prior care at home; Lourdes pilgrimage 1920.

## C. Gallipoli and related (investigated)

### M-OM-007 — The 500 francs at the Carmel of Gallipoli, night of 16 January 1910
- Period: after death — 1910
- What was reported: The poor Carmel of Gallipoli (Apulia), in debt and at times skipping dinner, ended a triduum to the Trinity through her intercession on 16 Jan 1910. That night the prioress, ill and worried, felt a hand cover her; a voice said God uses those in heaven to help his servants and gave her 500 francs to pay the community's debt; told the debt was only 300, the voice said "the rest will be over and above". She was led (by "bilocation") down to the parlour by a young Carmelite shining with light, who put the money in a wooden box with the unpaid bill and said: "No, I am not our holy Mother! I am the Servant of God, Sœur Thérèse of Lisieux. To-day, in heaven and on earth, we keep the feast of the Holy Name of Jesus." When warned she might lose her way, she answered: "No, no, my way is sure, and I am not mistaken in following it." Next morning, urged by the two sacristans, the prioress opened the box and found 500 francs.
- The person helped: the Carmel of Gallipoli; Mother Maria Carmela of the Heart of Jesus, prioress
- Witnesses and reporter: the prioress's letter to Mère Agnès, 25 Feb 1910; a document signed by the whole community, a commission of priests and the bishop, sent to Rome; a canonical inquiry by Bishop Müller of Gallipoli, whose Jesuit president (superior of the diocesan seminary) at first called the affair diabolical and was won over.
- Status: **investigated by the Church** (diocesan canonical inquiry; reported to Rome; not an approved miracle)
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 375–377 (letters; account "drawn from the Articles for the Cause of Beatification, 2nd edition, July, 1911", PDF 381); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 437–438 (de Teil's version: 500 lire, "about £20"); Laveille PDF 413 (cites *Shower of Roses*, extract from vols. I–II p. 110); Carmel "Les miracles de Thérèse" (« ma voie est sûre, et je ne me suis pas trompée en la suivant »).
- Other sources, conflicts, doubts: currency: "500 francs" (Taylor 1924, Laveille) vs "500 lire" (de Teil via Taylor 1926/30); the lira equalled the franc then (Taylor 1924 note). De Teil says the apparitions were reported to Rome and "to a certain extent influenced" the cause, and that the Bishop of Nardò testified at Lisieux.

### M-OM-008 — Further money at Gallipoli, January–August 1910
- Period: after death — 1910
- What was reported: Each month-end the accounts showed unexplained surpluses (25 lire in January; varying sums Feb–Apr). In May she appeared and said a 50-franc note was in the box ("With God, to say is to do"); two sisters found three notes where there had been two. In June 50 lire. On the night of 15–16 Jul (eve of Our Lady of Mount Carmel) she promised 100 lire and gave a 5-lire note, placed at the foot of the Sacred Heart statue. The bishop said he had lost a 100-lire note in his accounts; on 6 Aug, eve of St Cajetan (his name-day), she appeared holding 100 lire, saying "The power of God takes away or gives with the same ease in matters temporal as in matters spiritual"; the note was found and sent to the bishop, who returned it. After that, alms came from outside and she brought no more.
- Reporter: Mother Carmela's letter, September 1910
- Status: **investigated** (same inquiry)
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 378–379; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 437 ("From January till August … mysteriously provided with money")

### M-OM-009 — Prediction at Gallipoli on the eve of the exhumation, 5 September 1910
- Period: after death — 1910
- What was reported: On 5 Sep 1910, eve of the first exhumation, of which the prioress knew nothing, Thérèse appeared, said only her bones would be found, and that her relics would work great wonders "as mighty weapons against the devil"; she revealed "great, though hidden, crosses" she bore on earth. Taylor: "They will find only my bones"; it was her wish that her dust be scattered to win love.
- Status: **investigated**
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 379; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 271, 437

### M-OM-010 — The bishop's sealed envelope, Gallipoli, 16 January 1911
- Period: after death — 1911
- What was reported: Mgr Giannattasio, Bishop of Nardò, put a 500-lire note and his card (« My Way is a Sure one: I am not mistaken … Gallipoli, January 16, 1910 ») in an envelope, sealed it inside a larger one, to be opened on 16 Jan 1911. The sisters had been making a novena for 300 lire to decorate their chapel. When opened (the seal intact) it contained, besides his note, two 100-lire and two 50-lire notes — 300 lire; one smelled of roses; the bishop exchanged it for another note. He saw in it a confirmation of "My way is a sure one" as her doctrine.
- Reporter: account in the Articles for the Cause (1911)
- Status: **investigated** (Bishop Müller's inquiry)
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 380–381; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 437–438

### M-OM-011 — The Bishop of Nardò's vanished offering, October 1911
- Period: after death — 1911
- What was reported: Mgr Giannattasio meant to send an offering for the cause in October 1911 (French notes and a gold 20-franc piece). He delayed; the notes disappeared; he said she had come to take what he should have sent; he put the napoleon aside and some days later it too had vanished.
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 383

### M-OM-012 — "Gallipoli": a voice tells a French Carmelite "You must make me known everywhere", 20 July 1910
- Period: after death — 1910
- What was reported: A French Carmelite, writing to an exiled nun in Belgium about Gallipoli, forgot the town's name; she asked Thérèse and heard a voice say "Gallipoli", then: "That is just what you must do. You must make me known everywhere. I desire to do so much good." She felt her presence without seeing her.
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 382; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 438

### M-OM-016 — The Eure family: a woman's tuberculosis (c. 1907) and her brother's fractured skull (22 Aug 1908)
- What was reported: The writer says Thérèse cured her of "tubercular disease" two years before (c. 1907). Her brother, 11, fell about twenty feet through a trap door into a cellar on 22 Aug 1908; the skull was fractured in several places; a doctor gave him two hours; a surgeon agreed. A novena began on 24 Aug (the Carmel's prioress joining); he was unconscious and delirious eight days, with four crises; on the ninth day he recognized everyone and was calm; he returned to school with no trace.
- Reporter: "A Priest" transmitting the sister's letter, March 1909 (Department of Eure)
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 401–402
- Medical facts: doctor and surgeon consulted; no treatment named.

- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 422; Taylor 1924 PDF 422 note

### M-OM-020 — Mrs Dorans, Glasgow, 27 August 1909 (cancer)
- Period: after death — 1909
- What was reported: A widowed mother of a large family, with a painful tumour of the left abdomen for years, was judged inoperable in the Glasgow Western Infirmary (April–May 1909) and sent home to die; for ten weeks she could keep no food. A Sister of Mercy proposed a novena to "the Little Flower" on Sunday 22 Aug 1909. On the Thursday night she slept for the first time in years; about 5.30 am on Friday 27 Aug she felt a gentle pressure on both shoulders and a warm breath, "knew that an invisible Presence was beside her bed", saw clearly, felt no pain, and found the tumour gone except a lump smaller than a marble, which was gone next day. She asked for a roll and a cup of tea. Her doctor, expecting to find her dying, said "If this is going to be permanent, it is nothing short of a miracle."
- The person helped: Mrs Dorans, parish of Our Lady and St Margaret, Glasgow
- Witnesses and reporter: Dr George Carmichael (her doctor, non-Catholic); Professor Gemmell (Chair of Medicine, Glasgow) diagnosed "Carcinoma of the sigmoid flexure of the colon" and called it "a large, tender, almost immobile tumour"; certificate of Dr Thomas Colvin, 14 Jul 1910 (examined her 21 Jun 1910); X-ray by Dr Riddell (c. July 1910) normal; examined for the Lisieux tribunal in August 1911 by Drs La Néele and de Cornière (abdomen normal); re-examined 17 May 1912 by Drs Alexander Rankin, Carmichael and Colvin; Colvin read a paper on the case at Plymouth, 26 May 1912.
- Status: recorded by the Carmel; **examined by the diocesan tribunal (1911)**, but not an approved miracle
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 396–401; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 422
- Conflict: Taylor 1926/30 dates the cure "August 27, 1910". Taylor 1924 (detailed, with certificates dated 1910) gives 27 Aug 1909. 1909 is clearly right.
- Medical facts in the source: hard tumour size of an orange in left lumbar region; chronic intestinal catarrh; swelling of legs; blood passed; emaciation; vomiting; operation judged certainly fatal; X-ray afterwards.

### M-OM-021 — New York: a woman trampled by a horse, 30 July 1909
- What was reported: knocked down and trampled by a restive horse; face bruised, head bleeding, ribs broken "and driven into the lungs", heart injured; confessed in the street; the ambulance doctor gave "one in a thousand"; after a nun's advice a novena began on 3 Aug and a relic was applied; she began to mend and on the last day "was saved".
- Reporter: "Sister M. A.", her sister, New York, 12 Aug 1909
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 402–403
- Medical facts: hospital; ambulance doctor.

### M-OM-022 — An aged priest's sight, Saint-Jean-de-Luz, 1909
- What was reported: A priest of 67 (in 1900) had cataract (confirmed by an assistant of the oculist Dr Abadie, Paris, Sept 1900); by 1908 he could not recognize people two yards away; in May 1909 an optician judged the right eye lost; on Palm Sunday 1909 he fell down the altar steps. The exiled Carmelites of Bordeaux at Zarautz (Spain), wanting his help with their bees, made a novena without telling him; he found he could read; a second novena (from 19 May 1909) with a relic applied daily let him read the smallest print and recognize people at 100 paces; a third novena in thanksgiving; he could again see bee eggs with the naked eye.
- Reporter: the priest himself ("Rev. Chas. W…", name garbled in the scan), 23 Jul 1910
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 405–407
- Medical facts: cataract diagnosis; no operation; no remedy used.

### M-OM-023 — Mgr Augouard and a missionary, French Congo, c. 1910
- What was reported: The Vicar Apostolic of Ubangi, in bed three months with painful rheumatism, read her Life, asked for cure or death, then withdrew the request and asked instead that a missionary with sleeping sickness be saved. The missionary, sent to France, was found at the Pasteur Institute free of any microbe and never again showed symptoms; the bishop himself was cured the day after he withdrew his petition. He made a pilgrimage of thanks to her tomb and asked the pope to introduce the cause.
- Reporter: Mgr Prosper Augouard, letter from Brazzaville, 10 Nov 1912
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Laveille PDF 411–412 (citing *Shower of Roses* for 1912, p. 107); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 452–453 ("She cured in 1909 Mgr. Augouard … together with one of his missionaries") — date conflict 1909/1910.
- Medical facts: Pasteur Institute examination of the missionary.

### M-OM-024 — "Marie", eleven, Loire-Inférieure: walking again, Easter week 1910
- What was reported: A frail pupil fell ill on 2 Jan 1910; her legs failed; three doctors treated weakness, then hip-joint disease; a specialist diagnosed paralysis of the spinal cord (a hump forming) and tried electric treatment twice, saying she might walk in a year. In the night between Wednesday and Thursday of Easter week she saw "a pretty little face" smiling: "You will soon be able to walk … this very day even!" At 3 pm a voice said "Walk!", and she ran to her mother. Three weeks later she made her First Communion; shown the book, she recognized the face.
- Reporter: the head mistress, 20 Jul 1910
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 403–405
- Medical facts: tonic, massage, treatment for hip disease, electricity.

### M-OM-025 — Charlotte, a penitent in a Refuge, March 1910

When she again wanted to leave, the illness returned; she stayed, said she saw Sœur Thérèse at her side, and died on 26 Sep 1910.
- Reporter: Charlotte's own letter (March 1910); letters of the Carmel of N. (April 1910) and of the Refuge superior (3 Jan 1911)
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 439–440

### M-OM-026 — A dying man of 35: apparition and conversion, March 1910

- Reporter: "Sister N.", September 1910
- Status: recorded by the Carmel
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 440–442

### M-OM-028 — Greenock: the Little Sisters of the Poor's rates, December 1910

### M-OM-029 — Canon Rossignol's peace before death, 16–18 January 1911
- What was reported: An octogenarian theologian (26 years professor at the seminary of Luçon), retired at Saint-Martin-de-Beaupréau near Angers, secretly haunted for over forty years by fear of God's judgment (he scourged himself until his walls were blood-spattered), said he saw her at his pillow on the morning of 16 Jan 1911, smiling, as if saying "I am seeing to it … all will come right". His fear gave way to joy; he died on 18 Jan 1911.
- Reporter: Fr Frappereau; Fr Arsenius, Trappist of Bellefontaine (his confessor); account in the Articles for the Cause; notes by de Teil
- Status: recorded by the Carmel (printed in the Articles for the Cause)

### M-OM-030 — "C. D.", 17, Laval: three apparitions, 2–4 February 1911
- What was reported: A girl with albuminuria, threatened with phthisis, took cerebral uraemia on 24 Jan 1911; on 1 Feb the doctor said meningitis had set in. ### M-OM-041 — Aline Poirson, Chaumont / Aurosay, 1 July 1912

- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 416–420
