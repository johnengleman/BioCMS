# Scenes — official documents, miracles, after death (S-OM)

Each scene has card numbers and one sentence on why it matters to the life. Every scene rests on the cards named. Scenes 1–18 came from the first pass. Later scenes are added at the end. Where a scene has two reports of one event, the card notes the conflict.

### S-OM-01 — The 1921 objection about her childhood illness
- Cards: F-OM-004, F-OM-005
- Why it matters: the Church's own lawyers asked whether her illness at ten made her holiness suspect, and the decree answered that her cheerful temper in Carmel was proof of greater virtue.
- Note: the brief of 1923 says her family thought the illness came from the devil (F-OM-004). The decree does not name the illness.

### S-OM-02 — De Teil's lecture and her joke about miracles
- Cards: F-OM-024, F-OM-025
- Why it matters: it shows her humor in life and links the future vice-postulator to her before her death.
- Note: two versions (Laveille gives her words; Taylor gives de Teil's).

### S-OM-03 — The Gallipoli night, 16 January 1910
- Cards: M-OM-007, M-OM-008, M-OM-009, M-OM-010
- Why it matters: a poor Italian Carmel reported the money, the voice and the words "my way is sure", and a bishop tested it with a sealed envelope.
- Note: status is "investigated by the Church"; not an approved miracle.

### S-OM-04 — The first exhumation, 6 September 1910
- Cards: F-OM-028, M-OM-009
- Why it matters: the tribunal opened the grave, found only bones and a palm that was still fresh, and this matched what the Gallipoli prioress said she had heard the day before.
- Note: the perfume is a witness report only.

### S-OM-05 — Mrs Dorans's night in Glasgow, 27 August 1909
- Cards: M-OM-020
- Why it matters: it is the best documented English-language cure that was not approved: named doctors, a Chair of Medicine, an X-ray and re-examinations in 1911 and 1912.
- Note: date 1909, not 1910 (conflict 9).

### S-OM-06 — "The fuss must stop" and the sturgeon
- Cards: F-OM-107, M-OM-048, F-OM-025
- Why it matters: an official in Rome warned that the cause would be halted, and de Teil's reply changed his mind, according to de Teil.
- Note: oral account through Taylor; no date.

### S-OM-07 — The soldiers' petitions and the trenches
- Cards: F-OM-037, F-OM-038, F-OM-035, M-OM-072, M-OM-075, M-OM-076, M-OM-077, M-OM-078, M-OM-079
- Why it matters: ordinary soldiers, not the Church, pushed the cause forward, and the Carmel says their 26 petitions helped win a dispensation.

### S-OM-08 — Charles Anne's cure
- Cards: M-OM-001, F-OM-108
- Why it matters: it is the first miracle the Church approved, and the cured man later stood in St Peter's at the beatification.
- Note: his own words differ in Laveille and Taylor; the day of the cure is not given.

### S-OM-09 — Sœur Louise and the rose petals
- Cards: M-OM-002
- Why it matters: it is the second beatification miracle and the origin of the "rain of roses" image in the official texts.
- Note: date of cure 22 Sep (Laveille, Carmel) or 25 Sep (Taylor).

### S-OM-10 — The mayor's six million francs and the translation of 26 March 1923
- Cards: F-OM-054, F-OM-055, M-OM-080, M-OM-081, M-OM-082, M-OM-083, M-OM-004
- Why it matters: it shows the civil resistance, then a silent procession of about 50,000 people, with the reported cures on the route.

### S-OM-11 — Beatification day, 29 April 1923
- Cards: F-OM-058, F-OM-059, F-OM-060, M-OM-084
- Why it matters: the lights, the *Te Deum* that Taylor calls a "mistake", and the pope who knelt alone in the evening show the pope's personal bond with her.

### S-OM-12 — Sister Gabriella and the end of the triduum
- Cards: M-OM-003, F-OM-068
- Why it matters: a nun with tuberculosis of the knee and spine ended a triduum without her support; the lawyer's claim of two miracles was refused.
- Note: the cry "Sanata sum!" listed in HANDOFF.md has no locator in the sources read (searched Taylor 1926/30 and Laveille text layers). Do not quote it until a source is found.

### S-OM-13 — Maria Pellemans at the grave
- Cards: M-OM-004, F-OM-081
- Why it matters: the last of the four approved cures came on the eve of the translation, and the preacher of the 1925 celebrations used it.

### S-OM-14 — Canonization, 17 May 1925
- Cards: F-OM-071, F-OM-072, F-OM-073, F-OM-074, F-OM-075, F-OM-076, F-OM-077, M-OM-085
- Why it matters: the radio, the first applause in a canonization, and the five roses reported by an eyewitness mark the largest public moment of her cult.
- Note: the crowd counts disagree (F-OM-073).

### S-OM-15 — 30 September 1925 at Lisieux
- Cards: F-OM-080, F-OM-081, F-OM-082
- Why it matters: the golden rose, the aviator's roses and the illuminated town brought her feast home to the place where she lived.

### S-OM-16 — Patron of missions (1927) and of France (1944)
- Cards: F-OM-084, F-OM-087, F-OM-091, M-OM-058
- Why it matters: a Carmelite who never left France became the equal of Francis Xavier by a decree that names her rain of roses "in the distant regions".

### S-OM-17 — The relics leave Lisieux (1945) and the world tours
- Cards: F-OM-092, F-OM-097
- Why it matters: relics that had stayed in one chapel for twenty years began to travel to the towns and countries that had prayed to her.

### S-OM-18 — The doctorate, 19 October 1997
- Cards: F-OM-098, F-OM-003
- Why it matters: the school chaplain's "little doctor" of about 1885 became the youngest Doctor of the Church 112 years later.
- Note: the bull's "little doctor" is the link; the writer must not claim the chaplain foresaw it.

### S-OM-19 — Two Carmelites who did not like the book, and changed their minds
- Cards: M-OM-051, M-OM-054
- Why it matters: Fr Raphael Kalinowski (who had said Polish could not carry the style) and a Spanish nun (who said she did not like her) each wrote a letter that tells how they changed; Taylor's title for this pair is "coals of fire".
- Note: the Kalinowski letter is dated 9 Oct 1902 by our check of the page image; the Spanish letter is 7 Apr 1909. Both are reports in Taylor.

### S-OM-20 — After canonization: the "rose-garden" saint and the critics
- Cards: F-OM-105, F-OM-106
- Why it matters: from 1923 a preacher (Père Perroy) and later a Capuchin writer argued over whether the roses gave a false picture of her; the argument over her image began at once.

### S-OM-21 — The second exhumation, 9–10 August 1917
- Cards: F-OM-114, F-OM-115, F-OM-116, F-OM-117, F-OM-039
- Why it matters: at 3.15 am, with 3,000 people keeping guard, a ribbon with her own promise came out of the coffin, and two doctors measured her bones.
- Note: from the Carmel's 1917 printed account (via a summary); check wording before quoting.

### S-OM-22 — The apostolic process in wartime Bayeux
- Cards: F-OM-109, F-OM-110, F-OM-111, F-OM-037
- Why it matters: her old confessors (Pichon, Lemonnier) and the convert Grant testified while the Belgian priest who edited her book could not travel.
