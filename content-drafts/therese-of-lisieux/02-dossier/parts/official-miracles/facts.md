# Fact cards — official documents, miracles, after death (F-OM-)

Chronological. Locators: see `sources.md` for the short names. AAS printed page = PDF page. "New to English readers" is judged against the stage 1 baseline (Wikipedia EN, Vatican 1997 biography, *Divini amoris scientia*, EWTN, Franciscan Media). Taylor and Laveille are English books, but they are out of print and the baseline does not use them, so their details count as new unless the baseline has them.

## Part A. The life as the official documents tell it (family, school, opposition, death)

### F-OM-001 — The bull names her parents and calls them "honest" and very devout
- Claim: The canonization bull says she was born on 2 Jan 1873 at Alençon, diocese of Sées, to Louis Stanislas Martin and Marie Zélie Guérin, "honest parents, outstanding in singular and constant piety", and was baptized on 4 Jan with the names Maria Francisca Teresia.
- When / where: 2 and 4 Jan 1873, Alençon
- People: Louis Martin; Zélie Guérin
- Source: bull *Vehementer exsultamus hodie*, AAS 17 (1925) pp. 337–338
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « Aloisio Stanislao Martin et Maria Zelia Guérin, honestis parentibus, singulari et assidua pietate praestantibus »
- Translation: "to Louis Stanislas Martin and Marie Zélie Guérin, honest parents, outstanding for a singular and constant piety"
- Other sources: Office lesson IV (AAS 20 p. 148) repeats "honestis parentibus". Taylor 1926/30 PDF 35: Louis gave up his jeweller's business in 1871 and retired to 42 rue Saint-Blaise, where she was born.
- New to English readers: no (the facts); yes (the official Latin wording)
- Confidence: high — official act.
- Notes: The bull does not name Louis's trade or Zélie's lace business.

### F-OM-002 — The official texts say she lost her mother at four years and seven months and was raised by Marie, Pauline and her father
- Claim: The bull says she was barely four years and seven months old when her mother died; that she "lost all sense of joy"; and that her education was entrusted to her elder sisters Marie and Pauline, under her father's watchful care.
- When / where: Aug 1877
- People: Marie Martin; Pauline Martin; Louis Martin
- Source: AAS 17 p. 338
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « magno dolore correpta, omnem iucunditatis sensum amisit »
- Translation: "seized by great sorrow, she lost all sense of joy"
- Other sources: Office lesson IV (AAS 20 p. 148) says "in her fifth year". Benedict XV (allocution, Taylor 1926/30 PDF 289) says witnesses affirm the loss "produced in her a seriousness and maturity of mind far in advance of her years".
- New to English readers: partial (baseline has the death; not this wording)
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-003 — The school chaplain called her "the little doctor"
- Claim: The bull says that at the Benedictine school in Lisieux, where she spent the whole day and came home at evening, she was younger than the other pupils but ahead of them, and the nuns' chaplain called her "teacher of religion" or "little doctor". It adds that she later knew the whole *Imitation of Christ* by heart.
- When / where: c. 1881–1886, Abbey of Notre-Dame-du-Pré, Lisieux
- People: the chaplain of the Benedictine nuns (not named)
- Source: AAS 17 p. 338
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « ut a sacerdote, earumdem monialium cappellano, ipsa "Religionis magistra" seu "parvus Doctor" appellaretur »
- Translation: "so that the priest who was chaplain of these nuns called her 'mistress of religion' or 'little doctor'"
- Other sources: The beatification brief (AAS 15 p. 202) says she learned the catechism so well "that she was called the little teacher" (« ut parva magistra appellaretur ») and that she retained the *Imitation* "to the letter" and knew the Scriptures well. Taylor's English of the bull (1926/30 PDF 307–308): "his 'theologian', or the 'little doctor'".
- New to English readers: yes — a striking detail given the 1997 doctorate
- Confidence: high for what the documents say; the documents do not name their witness.
- Notes: The documents do not mention the bullying at school that the baseline reports.

### F-OM-004 — The beatification brief says the family thought her childhood illness came from the devil
- Claim: The brief *Quod Ioannes* says that at about ten she fell gravely ill "with an illness whose nature escaped even the doctors", that her family held the opinion ("not without reason") that it was sent by the devil, who foresaw the harm she would do him, and that her sudden, wonderful cure after prayers to the Virgin confirmed that opinion.
- When / where: 1883, Les Buissonnets
- Source: AAS 15 p. 202
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « fuitque non inconsulta familiarium opinio, infirmitatem illam fuisse mali daemonis arte immissam »
- Translation: "and it was the not unreasonable opinion of her family that this illness had been brought on by the craft of the evil spirit"
- Other sources: The bull (AAS 17 p. 338) says a "mysterious and grave illness" tormented her for a long time in her tenth year, and she was freed, "as she herself related", by the Virgin, who appeared to her smiling, after a novena to Our Lady of Victories. Office lesson IV (AAS 20 p. 149) repeats this. See F-OM-005 for how the Congregation handled the illness.
- New to English readers: yes (the devil explanation in an official papal brief)
- Confidence: high for the text

### F-OM-005 — The heroic-virtues decree answers an objection based on her childhood illness
- Claim: The 1921 decree says that of all the objections raised against the cause, one deserved special notice because, if proved, it would touch "the whole substance of the cause": the illness she suffered at ten was said to have had so harmful an effect on her and her actions that it made her holiness suspect. The decree answers: the same sworn eyewitnesses who describe the illness also describe the sudden, complete cure by the Virgin, and the law requires that both statements be believed. If traces of the illness remained, then her gentle, humble, patient, cheerful and even temper in Carmel shows even greater virtue, because she had to fight harder.
- When / where: decree of 14 Aug 1921, Rome
- People: the Devil's advocate side ("quae obiecta ex adverso fuerant"); the Patron (lawyer) of the cause, not named
- Source: AAS 13 pp. 450–451
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « De illa enim sermo est aegritudine … quaeque in eam eiusque actiones adeo maleficam suam exercuisse vim, ut suspectam ipsius redderet sanctitatem »
- Translation: "The objection concerns that illness … which is said to have exercised so harmful a power over her and her actions that it made her holiness suspect."
- Other sources: none in this family. The Promoter of the Faith was Angelo Mariani (AAS 13 p. 452).
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Reactions and opposition. Type of conflict: canonical objection by the Promoter of the Faith's side. Reason: a nervous or mental illness could undermine proof of virtue. The decree does not name the illness.

### F-OM-007 — Official and papal texts on the opposition to her entry into Carmel
- Claim: (a) The brief says her father, though he had already given three daughters to God and felt the parting bitterly, did not refuse; but the Church authorities flatly refused to let a girl of barely fifteen take up so strict a life; she went to Rome with her father's pilgrimage, knelt before Leo XIII, who sent her back to her superiors; she bore this calmly until the bishop, "moved by such virtue", gave permission. (b) Benedict XV, in his 1921 allocution, adds that her uncle would not consent until she was seventeen, that the ecclesiastical superior of the Carmel wanted to wait until she was twenty-one, and that after the bishop agreed the prioress still delayed her entry four months; she complained against none of them.
- When / where: 1887–1888
- People: Louis Martin; Isidore Guérin (the "brother-in-law"); Bishop Hugonin of Bayeux; the ecclesiastical superior of the Carmel (Canon Delatroëtte, not named in these texts); the prioress (Marie de Gonzague, not named); Leo XIII
- Source: (a) AAS 15 p. 204; (b) Taylor 1926/30 PDF 290–291 (Benedict XV allocution, English)
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words (a): « ecclesiastici moderatores puellam vix quindecim annos natam se rigidissimae religiosae vitae posse mancipare omnino negarunt »
- Translation: "the Church authorities utterly refused that a girl barely fifteen should bind herself to the very strict religious life"
- Other sources: the bull (AAS 17 p. 339) says she met "many difficulties from the Church authorities" and that Leo XIII sent her back to her superiors.
- New to English readers: partial (baseline has the Rome audience; the pope's own list of objectors, with the ages 17 and 21 and the four-month delay, is new)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Reactions and opposition. Names in brackets are not in these sources.

### F-OM-009 — The documents link her religious name to "spiritual childhood"
- Claim: The brief says that at her profession (8 Sep 1890) she chose to be called "of the Child Jesus", as if to signify the kind of holiness she pursued, "the perfection called spiritual childhood". Benedict XV said that if the title had not fallen to her, her sisters would have given it to her, and quoted her: "I am called Thérèse of the Child Jesus because the Child Jesus is the master and model of Thérèse" (as he gives it).
- When / where: 8 Sep 1890
- Source: AAS 15 p. 204; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 292
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « Theresia a Puero Iesu maluit vocari »
- Notes: The baseline says she took the name on entering (1888/1889). The brief ties the choice to profession. This may be the brief's simplification. See gaps file.
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium

- When / where: 1893, Carmel of Lisieux
- People: Mère Agnès de Jésus (Pauline)
- Source: AAS 15 pp. 204–205

- New to English readers: partial

### F-OM-011 — Pius X praised the autobiography in a letter (two versions of the words)

### F-OM-012 — The brief says she was ill five months in bed and her bed became a "chair of holiness"
- Claim: The brief says that after her 23rd year she caught consumption of the lungs but did not relax the common rule until her strength failed; she lay "five months" in terrible pain with a cheerful face, and her sickbed was "a chair of holiness" for the whole community; she died on 30 Sep 1897 "refreshed by a heavenly vision".
- When / where: 1896–1897
- Source: AAS 15 p. 205
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « Ancillae Dei decumbentis lectulus religiosae familiae universae sanctitatis cathedra fuit »
- Translation: "the little bed of the handmaid of God, as she lay there, was a chair of holiness for the whole religious family"
- New to English readers: yes (wording)
- Confidence: high for the text

### F-OM-013 — The bull says she offered herself to Merciful Love two years before death, "as it is said" wounded by heavenly fire
- Claim: The bull says that, grieved to see God's love neglected, she offered herself as victim to Merciful Love two years before her death; "then, as it is reported, she was wounded by a flame of heavenly fire"; consumed by love she died repeating "My God, I love you", aged 24, on 30 Sep 1897.
- Source: AAS 17 p. 339; Office lesson VI (AAS 20 p. 149) has "ut ipsa refert" ("as she herself relates")
- Source type: official | Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « Tunc, uti fertur, caelestis ignis flamma vulnerata est … ferventissime ingeminans "Deus meus, Te diligo" »
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high for the text; note the careful "uti fertur" in the bull and "ut ipsa refert" in the Office.

### F-OM-014 — Her last words and last acts, as recorded by the Carmel (via Taylor) and by Laveille
- Claim: Taylor prints the Carmel's account (signed "The Carmel of Lisieux"): at 4.30 pm she entered her agony; at the evening Angelus she looked at the statue of the Virgin; a few minutes after 7 she asked, "Mother, is not this the agony? Am I not going to die?"; told it might last some hours, she answered "Very well … be it so … Ah! I do not wish to suffer less." Then, looking at her crucifix: "Oh! … I love Him! … My God, I … love … Thee!" These were her last words, at a quarter past seven, after "fully twelve hours" of agony. She then raised herself, eyes open and fixed a little above the statue, for about the space of a Credo, and died. Laveille gives the same last words and the time "about 7.20 p.m."; he adds that Sœur Geneviève wet her lips with a bit of ice and received "a look of infinite tenderness".
- When / where: 30 Sep 1897, infirmary, Carmel of Lisieux
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague (prioress); Mère Agnès; Sœur Geneviève (Céline); the community
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 265–266 (book pp. 239–240); Laveille PDF 396–399 (book pp. 376–379)
- Source type: eyewitness (Carmel account) printed in old-collection | Rights: public-domain
- Original words (French, given in the bull as Latin): « Deus meus, Te diligo » (AAS 17 p. 339)
- Other sources: The canonization homily (AAS 17 p. 213) says that shortly before death she could say "that she had given God nothing but love" (« se nihil Deo aliud, nisi amorem, dedisse »).
- New to English readers: partial (baseline has "My God, I love you")
- Confidence: high for the wording; the time differs by five minutes (7.15 vs about 7.20).
- Notes: This family has only the Carmel's printed account. The eyewitness family should hold the *Derniers entretiens* text.

### F-OM-015 — Signs reported at her death and while she lay in the choir
- Claim: Taylor (Carmel's account) reports that a palm branch was put in her hand; a lay sister who kissed her feet was cured of "cerebral anaemia"; one nun smelled violets; one felt a kiss; one saw a light in the sky; one saw a luminous crown rise and vanish. On Saturday and Sunday people filed past the grille and touched hundreds of rosaries and medals to her body; a child of ten smelled lilies though only artificial lilies were there.
- When / where: 30 Sep – 3 Oct 1897
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 267; Laveille PDF 399 (lay sister; perfume of lilies and violets)
- Source type: eyewitness (as reported by the Carmel) | Rights: public-domain
- Other sources: Laveille identifies the lay sister as the one who had scolded Thérèse over the wreaths round Mère Geneviève's coffin (1891).
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — community report; see M-OM-005.

### F-OM-016 — The funeral, the grave, and the words on the cross
- Claim: On Monday 4 Oct 1897 a "goodly number" of priests came to the Carmel chapel; the procession to the cemetery was small; she was put in a deep grave in a corner of the plot recently bought by the Carmelites in the Lisieux cemetery. A few days later a wooden cross was put up with her name and the words « Je veux passer mon ciel à faire du bien sur la terre ». Taylor adds that she was the first of her Carmel buried in this plot, "at the right-hand corner of the enclosure".
- When / where: 4 Oct 1897, Lisieux cemetery
- Source: Laveille PDF 400 (book p. 380); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 267–268, 271
- Source type: early-life | Rights: public-domain
- New to English readers: yes (the cross inscription and the plot)
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Taylor's note (PDF 271) says "Divine Providence made use of the bigotry of men" to allow the stream of pilgrims to her tomb. He does not explain. (Probably the laws that stopped burial inside the enclosure; not stated.)

### F-OM-017 — Her death changed the prioress Marie de Gonzague, according to Laveille
- Claim: Laveille says the community had been affected at times by "the unequable temperament" of Mère Marie de Gonzague, which explains trials Thérèse suffered from "two or three Sisters"; after her death the prioress became more even and gentle, and wept before a picture of Thérèse as a child, saying to Sœur Geneviève: "I alone can know what I owe her. Oh, what she said to me! … but so tenderly."
- When / where: after Sep 1897
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague; Sœur Geneviève
- Source: Laveille PDF 401–402 (book pp. 381–382), citing Summarium of 1919 p. 994 § 2854
- Source type: early-life (citing process testimony) | Rights: public-domain
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — rests on Céline's testimony as summarized.
- Notes: Reactions and opposition inside the community. Laveille names no sister who opposed her.

## Part B. The book, the first favours, and the start of the cause (1898–1914)

### F-OM-018 — *Histoire d'une âme* went out as her death notice, October 1898
- Claim: Laveille says Mère Marie de Gonzague accepted the idea of printing the *Histoire d'une âme* and sending it to all the Carmels as the usual biographical notice; it appeared in October 1898. Mère Agnès had urged it, following Thérèse's counsel; Père Godefroy Madelaine, prior of the Premonstratensians of Mondaye, revised the manuscript, took it to Bishop Hugonin for the imprimatur and wrote the letter of approval. Taylor adds that "a first attempt failed" before the imprimatur was obtained.
- When / where: Oct 1898
- Source: Laveille PDF 402 and note; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 443 note
- Source type: early-life | Rights: public-domain
- Other sources: Plan §9 (Carmel): 21 Oct 1898, 1,000 copies, paid by Isidore Guérin.
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-019 — Postulants drawn by the book: Marie-Ange, Isabelle, Thérèse de l'Eucharistie
- Claim: Laveille says postulants came to Lisieux from French provinces, then from Ireland, Portugal, Italy, Constantinople and Argentina; many were sent to other convents. Three who stayed became leaders: Mère Marie-Ange de l'Enfant-Jésus (Breton; drawn from a worldly life by the book; prioress for 18 months; persuaded Bishop Lemonnier in 1908 to support the cause; died of consumption on 11 Nov 1909 after seven years in the cloister); Mère Isabelle du Sacré-Cœur (poet, novice mistress, sub-prioress; died 31 Jul 1914 aged 33); Mère Thérèse de l'Eucharistie (formerly the Countess de la Tour d'Auvergne; sub-prioress).
- Source: Laveille PDF 402–405
- Source type: early-life | Rights: public-domain
- Other sources: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 420 note (garbled scan) also credits Mother Marie-Ange with bringing Bishop Lemonnier to submit the cause in 1908. Taylor 1924 PDF 421 calls her story "perhaps the fairest Rose".
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-020 — The first account of favours was added to the 1907 edition
- Claim: Taylor says that in the 1907 edition a short account of the most remarkable answers to prayer was added for the first time to her own pages. The Carmel says a long note of 1901 had grown to 60 pages of accounts by 1907 and became the base of the first *Pluie de roses* volume.
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 269–270; Carmel "Pluie de roses" page
- Source type: old-collection; official (Carmel) | Rights: public-domain; unknown
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-021 — The *Pluie de roses*: ten volumes, over 3,200 accounts, about 8,000 favours recorded
- Claim: The Carmel says the *Pluie de roses* appeared from 1907 to 1926 in 10 volumes (7 chronological, 2 thematic, 1 anthology), with over 3,200 accounts; the first printing (early 1910) was 8,000 copies, titled « Quelques-unes des grâces et guérisons attribuées à l'intercession de sœur Thérèse »; Rome stopped publication in 1914 when the cause was introduced; it resumed in 1920; during the war, typed fascicles circulated in hospitals and among military chaplains. About 8,000 favours were recorded at the Carmel between 1898 and 1925.
- Source: Carmel "Pluie de roses" page
- Source type: official (Carmel) | Rights: unknown (facts only)
- Other sources — conflict: Laveille (PDF 414 and note) speaks of "seven volumes" with "no less than 3,000 pages of close type" in 15 years, with the first facts going back to 1902; vol. VII covers beatification to canonization. Taylor 1926/30 PDF 273 also says "seven volumes". The difference is date: Laveille (1925) and Taylor (1927) wrote before the last volumes.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-022 — Bishop Lemonnier's first steps (1907–1908)
- Claim: The Carmel says that in 1907 Bishop Lemonnier invited the Carmelites to write down their memories of Sœur Thérèse and gave an imprimatur for a prayer for her beatification. In 1908 the young prioress Marie-Ange persuaded him to support the cause (F-OM-019).
- Source: Carmel "Historique" page; Laveille PDF 404
- Source type: official | Rights: unknown; public-domain
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-023 — Postulator and vice-postulator named in January 1909
- Claim: In early 1909 Fr Rodrigo of St Francis of Paula, OCD, Postulator General of the Discalced Carmelites in Rome, took up the cause, and Mgr Roger de Teil, in Paris, became vice-postulator (later Director-General of the Society of the Holy Childhood). De Teil died in Paris on 20 May 1922 "holding in his hand the picture of Sœur Thérèse".
- Source: Carmel "Historique"; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 270; Laveille PDF 413 note
- Source type: official; old-collection | Rights: unknown; public-domain
- Other sources: Taylor says de Teil died "in May, 1922" and met Taylor at Carfin before that.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-024 — Thérèse once heard the future vice-postulator speak, and joked about miracles
- Claim: Laveille says de Teil knew the Lisieux Carmel in her lifetime: he gave a talk there on the Carmelite martyrs of Compiègne and asked insistently whether the nuns had heard of miracles through their intercession. Coming out of the parlour Thérèse said: "How touching is the zeal of this postulator! How glad one would be to have miracles to make known to him!" Taylor gives de Teil's own version: he ended his lecture, "If any of you who are listening to me have the intention of being canonised, please have pity on the poor Vice-Postulator and work plenty of miracles!" — "And Sœur Thérèse, obedient child, did precisely as she was told."
- When / where: during her life, Carmel of Lisieux (year not given)
- People: Mgr Roger de Teil
- Source: Laveille PDF 413 note; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 421
- Source type: early-life (Laveille); popular anecdote told by de Teil at Carfin (Taylor) | Rights: public-domain
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — two different reports of one scene; the Laveille version gives her own words.

### F-OM-025 — De Teil: Carmelite causes were "hopeless" in Rome without cures; "money and miracles"
- Claim: De Teil told Taylor: "I knew that any Carmelite Cause was hopeless in Rome. The Congregation of Rites was weary of Causes in which it was easy to prove that virtue had been heroically practised, but where authenticated cures were invariably wanting." He took medical documents on several cures to Rome; officials said, "Well, at last, we have something substantial." And: "The two most important factors in a canonisation are money and miracles. Sœur Thérèse is providing us plentifully with both!"
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 420–421 (book pp. 390–391)
- Source type: popular (oral report of a participant) | Rights: public-domain
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium — second-hand report, but by a man who knew de Teil well.

### F-OM-026 — Process on her writings, 1910
- Claim: On 10 Feb 1910 the Congregation of Rites authorized the diocese to begin (the search for her writings). Bishop Lemonnier's mandate was read in all churches on 4 Apr 1910. The process on the writings ran 23 May – 12 Jun 1910 and heard 10 witnesses.
- Source: Carmel "Historique"; Laveille PDF 412–413 (authorization 10 Feb 1910); Taylor 1926/30 PDF 318 ("February 10, 1910 — Cause Begun (Writings)")
- Source type: official | Rights: unknown; public-domain
- Other sources: Taylor PDF 270 says "In March, 1910" the bishop ordered the search and "in the following July" set up the tribunal. Minor difference.
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-027 — The Ordinary (informative) Process: 93 sessions, 48 witnesses, 1910–1911
- Claim: The diocesan tribunal was set up on 3 Aug 1910; at the first session, 12 Aug 1910, she officially became "Servant of God"; there were 93 sessions and 48 witnesses; the process closed on 12 Dec 1911 in the chapel of the Bayeux seminary.
- Source: Carmel "Historique"
- Source type: official | Rights: unknown
- Other sources: Laveille PDF 420: first session at Bayeux in Aug 1910, ended Dec 1911. Taylor 1926/30 PDF 270–271: 11 witnesses were nuns of the Lisieux Carmel, 9 of whom had lived with her; 26 others, 16 of them eyewitnesses (total 37). Taylor was the first witness examined after Mère Agnès; he testified to devotion in English-speaking countries. Plan §9: 48 witnesses, 37 depositions printed (1973).
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high

### F-OM-028 — First exhumation, 6 September 1910: only bones; the palm intact
- Claim: On 6 Sep 1910, before Bishop Lemonnier and the tribunal, the grave was opened. As she had foretold, only bones were found; the thick habit had become thin; the palm placed in her hand in 1897 was found "fresh and intact". Several witnesses noticed a sweet perfume from the bones, and the earth that touched the coffin kept its fragrance for months. The remains were put in a new coffin in a cemented vault nearer the centre of the plot.
- When / where: 6 Sep 1910, Lisieux cemetery
- Source: Laveille PDF 399 note, 420–421; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 267 note, 271
- Source type: early-life | Rights: public-domain
- Other sources: Carmel "Historique" gives the date. Taylor 1924 PDF 379 (Gallipoli letter): on the eve, 5 Sep 1910, she told Mother Carmela they would find only her bones (M-OM-008). Taylor 1926/30 PDF 463: the palm was later kept in the Carmel sacristy.
- New to English readers: partial (baseline: "exhumation 1910")
- Confidence: high for date and bones; medium for perfume (witness reports)

### F-OM-029 — The "non-cultus" process, 1911
