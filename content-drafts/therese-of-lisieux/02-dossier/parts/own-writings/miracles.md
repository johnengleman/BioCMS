# Miracle list — own writings (M-OW)

Extraordinary events that Thérèse herself reports, or that her sisters recorded in her last months. This source family contains **no** approved miracle (the four approved miracles are after 1897; see other parts). Each entry is a report, not a finding. Card numbers refer to facts.md (this version).

### M-OW-01 — Vision of a veiled man like her father, c. 1879–1880
- Period: during life — modern (19th century)
- What was reported: At about six or seven, alone at a garret window at Les Buissonnets on a sunny afternoon, she saw a man dressed like her father, same height and walk but much more stooped, his head covered with a kind of apron of no clear colour, walk past her garden towards a grove and vanish. Her father was away at Alençon. The maid Victoire denied hiding under her apron; a search found no trace. Fourteen years later she and Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur read it as a picture of her father's illness (his "veiled face").
- The person helped: none; a sign concerning Louis Martin
- Witnesses and reporter: Thérèse alone saw it; Marie (in the next room) and Pauline heard her call and searched with her. Reported by Thérèse in Ms A, 1895
- Status: her own report; not investigated
- Source: Ms A 19v–21r (F-OW-028); public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: Marie's own account belongs to other parts. The interpretation came 14 years later, after the father's illness.
- Medical or natural facts: none in the source

### M-OW-02 — The smile of the Virgin and the cure of 13 May 1883
- Period: during life — modern
- What was reported: During a novena of Masses at Notre-Dame des Victoires (Paris) paid for by her father, on Sunday 13 May 1883, after weeks of an illness with trembling, apparent delirium, faints and terrors, she called "Mama" without recognising Marie. Marie, Léonie and Céline knelt and prayed before the statue of the Virgin in the room. Thérèse writes: "All at once the Blessed Virgin seemed to me beautiful... what went to the depths of my soul was the 'ravishing smile of the Blessed Virgin'." Two tears ran down her cheeks and "all my pains vanished". The cure worked "not all at once, but gently".
- The person helped: Thérèse Martin, aged 10
- Witnesses and reporter: Marie, Léonie, Céline present; Thérèse's account in Ms A (1895); she told Marie at once; the Carmelites questioned her soon after
- Status: family and Carmel tradition; the statue is venerated as the "Vierge du Sourire"; not an approved miracle
- Source: Ms A 27r–31r, 56v–57r, 86r (F-OW-038 to F-OW-042, F-OW-070); public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: She later feared she had "lied" about it and for four years suspected she had made herself ill on purpose (F-OW-041, F-OW-042). She does not say the statue moved. She blamed the illness on the devil. Dr Notta called the illness "very grave" and unknown in so young a child; no diagnosis is given in her text.
- Medical or natural facts: constant headache from late 1882; trembling fit at Easter 1883; treatment with blankets and hot bottles; Dr Notta consulted; she refused "very simple remedies" from fear

### M-OW-03 — Pranzini's "sign", 31 Aug 1887
- Period: during life — modern
- What was reported: She prayed and had a Mass said for the murderer Henri Pranzini and asked God for "a sign" of repentance. He did not confess, but on the scaffold he took the priest's crucifix and kissed the wounds three times (reported in *La Croix*, read by her the next day).
- The person helped: Henri Pranzini, executed in Paris
- Witnesses and reporter: newspaper report; Thérèse's account (Ms A, 1895); Céline shared the secret and the prayer
- Status: her own report of an answered prayer
- Source: Ms A 45v–46v (F-OW-064); public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: The press account is outside this source family (check the *La Croix* issue of 1 Sep 1887 in other parts).
- Medical or natural facts: none

### M-OW-04 — Her uncle's "inclination of heart", Oct 1887
- Period: during life
- What was reported: Her uncle said it would take "a miracle" to make him consent to her entering at 15. After three days of spiritual darkness and rain, he called her in and said he had asked God only for "a simple inclination of heart" and had received it. She writes: "for me the miracle was granted."
- The person helped: Thérèse (her vocation)
- Witnesses and reporter: Isidore Guérin; Thérèse's account
- Status: her own interpretation
- Source: Ms A 50v–51v (F-OW-065); public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: A change of mind, not a physical event. Listed because she calls it a miracle.
- Medical or natural facts: none

- Period: during life

- The person helped: —
- Witnesses and reporter: the community and town (per her); Thérèse's account
- Status: her own report; she calls it a "delicacy of Jesus"
- Source: Ms A 72r–72v (F-OW-086); public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: Weather records for Lisieux, 10 Jan 1889, not checked.
- Medical or natural facts: mild temperature and fine rain the day before and that morning (her words)

### M-OW-13 — A drink after a silent complaint, spring 1897

### M-OW-17 — The dead leaf that fell when she died, 30 Sep 1897
- Period: death
- What was reported: On 26 Sep she pointed to a dead leaf hanging on a spider's thread: "my life hangs by no more than a thread". Mère Agnès reports that the leaf fell on the evening of her death and that she kept it with its thread.
- Witnesses and reporter: Mère Agnès
- Status: recorded by Mère Agnès
- Source: CJ 26.9 (F-OW-327)

### M-OW-18 — The "ecstasy" at death and the supple body, 30 Sep – 4 Oct 1897
- Period: death
- What was reported: After her last words she fell back; the community returned and, per Mère Agnès, saw her face regain its colour and her eyes fixed upward "shining with peace and joy" for "about the space of a Creed", with no blinking when a candle was brought close; afterwards "a heavenly smile"; she looked 12 or 13; her limbs stayed supple until burial on 4 Oct.
- Witnesses and reporter: the community; Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie (candle); Mère Agnès (recorder); Sr Aimée de Jésus and Marie (laying out)
- Status: recorded by her sisters; later used in the process
- Source: CJ 30.9 (F-OW-334)
- Medical or natural facts: before death: rattle for over two hours, congested face, purple hands, icy feet, trembling, heavy sweating

- Source: LT 104 (fragments, 5–6 May 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-104-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-fragments-5-6-mai-1890/ ; public-domain (her words)
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: Thérèse herself declined any wish for Lourdes "ecstasies" (LT 106).
- Medical or natural facts the source mentions: "c'est enflé tout autour"; the uncle (a pharmacist) judged it dangerous.


## From the letters LT 182–266 and the Carnet jaune, July 1897 (M-OW-35 to M-OW-38)

Reports only. Each is her own account or a record by her sisters. None was investigated in these sources.

### M-OW-35 — Her father's "guarantee" of 29 July 1897 and the "passport for Heaven"
- Period: during life — July 1897
- What was reported: In a letter of 10 Aug 1897 she writes that she has "received my passport for Heaven", that her dear father obtained this grace, and that "on the 29th he gave me the guarantee that I would soon join him". The letter does not say what the guarantee was (a dream, a feeling, or a sign).
- The person helped: Thérèse
- Witnesses and reporter: Thérèse herself, writing to Abbé Bellière
- Status: her own report; not investigated
- Source: LT 263 (to l'abbé Bellière, 10 Aug 1897), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-263-a-labbe-belliere-10-aout-1897/ ; public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: 29 July is the anniversary of her father's death (29 Jul 1894, other cards). The Carnet jaune for 29 July records no such event.
- Medical or natural facts the source mentions: the doctor found the illness advanced "in two days" (30 July) and advised extreme unction.

### M-OW-36 — The novena of Masses at Our Lady of Victories (June–July 1897), and her sense that she would not be cured
- Period: during life — June–July 1897
- What was reported: In June 1897 the prioress had a novena of Masses said at Notre-Dame des Victoires for her cure. Thérèse writes that Our Lady of Victories "had already cured me in my childhood", but that she felt "the miracle would not take place" and asked instead that the Virgin console the prioress and make her consent to her death.
- The person helped: Thérèse (intended); the prioress (in her view)
- Witnesses and reporter: Thérèse, in two letters to Bellière
- Status: her own report; the novena was not answered by a cure
- Source: LT 244 (9 Jun 1897) and LT 253 (13 Jul 1897), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-244-a-labbe-belliere-9-juin-1897/ ; https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-253-a-labbe-belliere-13-juillet-1897/ ; public-domain
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: the childhood cure is the Virgin's smile of May 1883 (Ms A, baseline); the letters call the shrine "Notre-Dame des Victoires", not the statue of the smile.
- Medical or natural facts the source mentions: frequent haemoptysis from 6 July (editors' note); the doctor's shifting views (CJ 30–31 July).

### M-OW-37 — "The Thief is in my heart" and the "little thief" (25–31 July 1897)
- Period: during life — July 1897
- What was reported: She calls death "the Thief" who will come like a thief in the night. On 25 July she answers "Where is the Thief now?" with "He is here! He is in my heart." On 31 July she says she will be "a little thief" in Heaven and "take everything that pleases me", and after a large spitting of blood in the night she looked at the Virgin's statue and said she had thought "you were going to take me away".
- The person helped: Thérèse
- Witnesses and reporter: Mère Agnès, recording her words, July 1897 (notebook copied 1921–23)
- Status: recorded by her sister; not investigated
- Source: CJ 25.7.5; CJ 31.7.5–6, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/archive/cj-juillet-1897/ ; public-domain (1897 words); transcription unknown
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: the "Thief" image is from the Gospel (God comes like a thief); Novissima Verba has "thief" sayings.
- Medical or natural facts the source mentions: large haemoptysis in the night 30–31 July.

### M-OW-38 — Dream of being carried to the warming room between two torches (27 July 1897)
- Period: during life — July 1897
- What was reported: She told Mère Agnès with a laugh that she had dreamed that she was carried to the warming room (chauffoir) between two torches for "Our Father's" feast.
- The person helped: Thérèse
- Witnesses and reporter: Mère Agnès, 27 Jul 1897
- Status: recorded by her sister (a dream told as a joke); not investigated
- Source: CJ 27.7.3, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/archive/cj-juillet-1897/ ; public-domain (1897 words)
- Other sources, conflicts, and doubts: she also told a dream on 26 July of a bazaar with her father (CJ 26.7.1). Neither is presented by her as a sign.
- Medical or natural facts the source mentions: none.
