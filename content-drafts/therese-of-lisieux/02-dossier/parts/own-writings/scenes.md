# Scenes — own writings

The strongest scenes that her writings (and her sisters' 1897 records) support. Card numbers refer to facts.md in this version. Each has enough detail (who, where, what was said, what followed) to be told as a small story.

1. **The mother's death and "Pauline will be Mama!"** (Aug 1877, Alençon). F-OW-013, F-OW-014, F-OW-015, F-OW-016. The day at Mme Leriche's, the last kiss, the tall coffin lid, the choice of Pauline: the root of the "second period" of her life and of her bond with Pauline.
2. **Victoire, the bucket and "vous êtes une mioche!"** (c. 1879–81, Les Buissonnets). F-OW-021, F-OW-022. A funny, cross, very un-pious child, in scenes Pauline cut from the published book.
3. **The veiled man in the garden** (c. 1879–80). F-OW-028. A child's strange vision that the family later read as a sign of her father's mental illness.
4. **The 1883 illness and the smile of the Virgin** (Mar–May 1883). F-OW-038, F-OW-040, F-OW-041, F-OW-042. Fear that she would "go mad or die", "Mama... Mama...", the smile; then four years of torment that she had lied or faked it.
5. **Christmas night 1886: the shoes in the fireplace.** F-OW-059. Her father's irritated words (in the manuscript wording) and her choice not to cry: the "conversion" in one small domestic moment.
6. **Pentecost 1887: telling her father; the white flower.** F-OW-063. Father and daughter in the garden at sunset; his consent; the uprooted flower she kept in her Imitation and, ten years later, glued to a picture (F-OW-279).
7. **Bayeux, 31 Oct 1887.** F-OW-069. Rain, a funeral, the white hat, the huge armchair, "Ah, I see diamonds...", her hair put up to look older.
8. **The Colosseum and the audience with Leo XIII** (Nov 1887). F-OW-074, F-OW-076, F-OW-077. Climbing into the arena with Céline; then kneeling before the Pope against orders and being carried away by the guards; "the little ball".
9. **Entry to Carmel, 9 Apr 1888.** F-OW-082. The last dinner, the father kneeling to bless her, the dark choir, Mère Geneviève's eyes.
10. **Clothing day in the snow, 10 Jan 1889, and the 12 February that followed.** F-OW-086, F-OW-087. Her father's "last feast" and, a month later, his commitment to the asylum, "our great riches".
11. **Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come** (Sept 1890). F-OW-105 and F-OW-108. Her orphaned "wedding day".
12. **Leading Sr Saint-Pierre** (c. 1889–90). F-OW-088. The hourglass, "you're going too fast, I'll break!", the bread cut unasked, the distant music.
13. **The quarrel over the keys and flight to the stairs** (c. 1890–92). F-OW-092. Thérèse neither perfect nor meek: she resists, then runs away "without drum or trumpet".
14. **The novice guide: "the little hunting dog"** (1894). F-OW-179. From the letters: frank, funny, and class-conscious remarks the 1911 edition removed.
15. **The storm over Céline's entry** (Aug 1894). F-OW-183 and neighbouring letter cards. Family, priest and prioress opposition, in her own letters.
16. **Good Friday 1896 and the trial of faith.** F-OW-212, F-OW-213. The "stream bubbling to my lips", joy at the blood, and then "a wall that rises to the heavens".
17. **Writing Ms C among the haymakers** (June 1897). F-OW-238. A dying nun trying to write while lay sisters chat about "hay, ducks, hens".
18. **The white hen and her chicks** (7 June 1897). F-OW-241. She weeps at a hen sheltering its chicks: God "has hidden me entirely under his wings".
19. **The red balloon** (Aug 1897). F-OW-288. She tells a grieving novice to play with a balloon behind her bed and peeks and laughs.
20. **The black hole under the chestnut trees** (28 Aug 1897). F-OW-305. Her darkness named in one image, "but I'm at peace there".
21. **30 Aug 1897: wheeled to the choir door; the last photograph.** F-OW-307. Rose petals thrown at the grille; the doctor: "It's for soon."
22. **"If I had not had faith..."** (22 Sep 1897). F-OW-321. The hardest recorded sentence; needs its context and care.
23. **Her death, 29–30 Sep 1897.** F-OW-329 to F-OW-333 and the "after death" card. "I'll never know how to die!"; arms on her sisters' shoulders at 3 p.m.; the robins; the ice; "Oh! I love him... My God... I love you."

## Scenes from the letter review (LT 110–181), as proposed by that reviewer

1. **Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come** (Sept 1890). Cards: "Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come"; "The faded pink candles from Papa"; "EDIT: 1911 adds 'Papa ne viendra pas demain!'". Why it matters: it joins her father's illness to her profession, in her own tears, and shows how the 1911 edition sharpened the text.
2. **The little hunting dog and the rabbit** (July 1894). Cards: "The 'little hunting dog' and her novice"; "EDIT: 1911 turns one novice into 'my little rabbits'". Why it matters: it is the first picture of Thérèse forming novices, frank and funny, and a clear case of editorial rewriting.
3. **The storm over Céline's vocation** (Aug 1894). Cards: "Céline's vocation contested"; "The 'storm at Caen'"; "'After a death of 5 years'"; "Shopping list for Céline". Why it matters: opposition from a priest-director and from the La Néeles, secrecy towards Mère Marie de Gonzague, and grief for their father all meet in two letters.
4. **Léonie's return from the Visitation** (July 1895). Cards: "Léonie leaves the Visitation"; "Léonie in the parlour, crying". Why it matters: a small, human parlour scene: tears, a cousin who makes "stones laugh", and a meal in earthenware dishes.
5. **The postulant and the photographs at recreation** (Oct 1895). Cards: "'The old dean of the novitiate'"; "Flower patterns, not fish"; "Going to the laundry". Why it matters: it shows community life, humour and work (painting patterns, laundry), none of which survives in 1911.
6. **Uncle Guérin's Bluebeard** (childhood, told Dec 1892). Card: "Uncle Guérin, Bluebeard and 'Mirlitir'". Why it matters: a vivid, funny childhood memory known only from this letter.
7. **The painting that made her cry** (Jan–Apr 1894). Cards: "'The Dream of the Child Jesus'"; "She wept on the painting". Why it matters: her artistic effort, her disappointment and her spiritual dryness ("He almost always sleeps") in one concrete story.
8. **The profession delayed** (1889–90, told 1895). Card: "Profession delayed because the Superior found her too young". Why it matters: in her own words, a Church authority's objection and how she turned it into peace.

## Scenes from the letters LT 1–109 (card titles refer to headings in facts.md, block A, F-OW-335 to F-OW-433)

1. The audience with Leo XIII, told that night — cards "Pauline tells her to speak to the Pope", "Eve of the audience", "The audience with Leo XIII, told the same evening", "Conflict: the audience in the letter vs Story of a Soul". Why: the contemporary letter gives a plainer, sadder scene (an exhausted old Pope, Révérony speaking for him) than the famous Ms A version.
2. The uncle's first answer — cards "Her uncle's first answer: 'a public scandal'" and "Conflict: the uncle's refusal". Why: shows the real social objection (a child in Carmel would be a public scandal) in her words the same day.
3. The lamb in the snow — cards "The newborn lamb that died the same day" and "Edit: the lamb letter in 1911". Why: a tender father–daughter story with a clear ending, and a clean example of Pauline's edits.
4. Assisi carriage — card "Assisi: left behind, she rides in Révérony's carriage". Why: a small comic scene that also tests what Révérony promised.
5. The carp and the fruit avalanche — cards "Papa's carp" and "Alençon lace... an 'avalanche' of fruit and onions through the turn". Why: concrete, funny daily life in the Carmel, and her joke that the fruit got in more easily than she did.
6. Clothing gifts and the refectory — cards "Gifts for the clothing: a lily crown, a giant sugar stick, gingerbread" and "A melon, champagne and fireworks". Why: a 16-year-old's pride and the family's generosity just before the father's collapse.
7. Caen — cards "'Is it possible that I am writing to you in Caen?'", "New Year 1890: 'I still see Papa...'", "New Year's Eve 1889: 'now we are orphans'". Why: the family's grief over the father's confinement in her own words, much of it cut in 1911.
8. Advising a scrupulous cousin — cards "Scruples: advising Marie Guérin..." and "The prioress's verdict...". Why: a novice of 16 gives firm advice from her own experience; first strong statement of "confiance".


## Scenes from the letters LT 182–266 and the Carnet jaune, July 1897 (F-OW numbers refer to facts.md blocks C to J)

1. **Heaven at Céline's profession, told the night before** (23–24 Feb 1896). F-OW-438, F-OW-439, F-OW-440, F-OW-441. Why: a 3,000-word imagined court of Heaven in which the dead siblings and parents speak, and a "contract" in Jesus's voice: her humour, her saints, and her family in one letter.
2. **The lamb who told the Shepherdess it was God's will** (29 Jun 1896). F-OW-445. Why: the community's vote (seven ballots) and Thérèse's loyalty to Mère Marie de Gonzague, in allegory.
3. **A pall painted for a missionary** (23 Jun – 30 Jul 1896). F-OW-444, F-OW-450, F-OW-451. Why: how the Roulland link began: linens, a map of Sichuan on the workroom wall, a permitted photograph.
4. **The lobster in the Carmel kitchen** (early 1897). F-OW-476. Why: a short, funny story with a cook, a prioress and a community laughing to tears.
5. **The rebuke and the kiss: 28 May 1897.** F-OW-489, F-OW-490. Why: she loses patience, the severe sister embraces her, and she answers "Nobody, Lord"; the 1911 edition changes the scene.
6. **The envelope and the letter** (30 May 1897). F-OW-491. Why: Mère Agnès is hurt; Thérèse asks her sister not to believe what will be said after her death.
7. **9 June 1897: the farewell that came four months early.** F-OW-497, F-OW-498, F-OW-516, F-OW-504. Why: a letter written while the infirmarian was out, and how the 1911 edition moved it.
8. **17 July 1897.** F-OW-525, F-OW-526, F-OW-507. Why: the full text of "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth" and her letter of the same day.
9. **The bedside in late July** (24 – 27 July 1897): fruit for the Holy Family, "my telephone", the glass that looks like currant liqueur. F-OW-537, F-OW-546, F-OW-557. Why: small, vivid, funny details of the sickroom.
10. **30 July 1897: extreme unction.** F-OW-559, F-OW-516, F-OW-550. Why: her words on the day, the priest's "like a child just baptised", and the swallowed skins.
11. **Diana Vaughan, in her own play and after the hoax** (June 1896 – April 1897). F-OW-572, F-OW-578. Why: an unexpected episode in her world (the anti-Masonic hoax) with Joan of Arc in the middle.
12. **The last written words** (8 Sep 1897). F-OW-583. Why: "If I were Queen of Heaven" with the saxifrage flower from her father stuck to the picture.
