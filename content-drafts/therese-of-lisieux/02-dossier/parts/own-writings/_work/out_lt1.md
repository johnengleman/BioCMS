# St Thérèse of Lisieux — Letters LT 1 to LT 109 (April 1877 – July 1890): fact cards

Source file read in full: lt_part1.txt (Carmel of Lisieux archive transcriptions, French). All URLs follow https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/<file name without .html>/.
The source file holds the letter texts only. It has almost no Carmel editorial notes (one note at LT 40). So nearly every card below rests on Thérèse's own words.
Edition checks: "1911" = Histoire d'une âme 1911 edition (Gutenberg #36708), checked with chk.py and by reading its letter section. Taylor 1912 translated that edited text.
Nicknames used in the letters (from the letters themselves): "le Roi" / "mon Roi" = her father Louis Martin; "la Reine" / "Reine de France et de Navarre" = Thérèse; "le Diamant" = Marie (Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur; LT 58 calls the Diamant "ta grande", the eldest); "la perle fine" = Pauline (Sr Agnès de Jésus; LT 61 says the "perle fine" gave artist's advice); "l'agneau" = Pauline, "l'agnelet" = Thérèse; "Lion" = Marie (LT 75, 79, 91); "Louploup", "petit lutin", "Poupée" = her cousin Marie Guérin; "Lys-Immortelle" = Céline.
Abbreviation she uses after her name: "e.m." (enfant de Marie) in 1887; "p.c.ind." / "post.carm.ind." = unworthy Carmelite postulant; "nov.carm.ind." = unworthy Carmelite novice (from 10 Jan 1889).

### Her first letters: Pauline held her on her lap and guided her hand
- Claim: At age four Thérèse "wrote" her first letters with help. Pauline held her on her lap because she could not yet hold a pen-holder. In the next letter she says Marie will no longer guide her hand and she cannot write alone. In LT 1 she calls herself "a little imp who always laughs" and says she spends the day teasing her poor little sisters.
- When / where: 4 April 1877 and 12–17 April 1877, Alençon (the family still lived there; her mother died in August 1877).
- People: Louise Magdelaine (recipient, a girl she did not know); Pauline Martin (sister); Marie Martin (sister); "ma soeur Marie Aloysia" and "ma soeur Louise de Gonzague" of the Visitation; Jeanne and Marie Guérin (cousins).
- Source: LT 1 (to Louise Magdelaine, 4 Apr 1877), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-1-a-louise-magdelaine-4-avril-1877/ ; LT 2 (to Jeanne and Marie Guérin, 12–17 Apr 1877), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-2-a-jeanne-et-marie-guerin-12-17-avril-1877/
- Source type: own-writing (dictated / hand guided)
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « elle me tient sur ses genoux car je ne sais pas seulement tenir un porte-plume, elle veut que je vous dise que je suis une petite paresseuse, mais ce n'est pas vrai car je travaille toute la journée à faire des malices à mes pauvres petites soeurs enfin je suis un petit lutin qui rit toujours » ; « Marie ne veut plus me conduire la main et je ne sais pas écrire toute seule »
- Translation: "She is holding me on her knees because I don't even know how to hold a pen-holder. She wants me to tell you I am a little lazybones, but that is not true, because I work all day long at playing tricks on my poor little sisters. So I am a little imp who always laughs." / "Marie will not guide my hand any more and I do not know how to write by myself."
- Other sources: Ms A describes her as a lively, stubborn small child (baseline does not cover this letter).
- New to English readers: yes — "petit lutin qui rit toujours" is ABSENT from the 1911 edition; not in baseline.
- Confidence: high — exact text; note that the words were probably Pauline's pen with the child's dictation.
- Notes: The letter itself says Pauline told her what to say ("elle veut que je vous dise"). So this is a child's voice shaped by an older sister. She adds that she "knows no other people" than the two Visitation nuns.

- People: Pauline Martin (recipient); Céline Fournet Guérin, "ma tante"; Marie Guérin (cousin).

### Age seven: her own spelling, and she knows it is bad
- Claim: In a letter to Pauline written at her aunt's house, Thérèse admits she makes many mistakes and is "hardly skilful". The letter keeps her child spelling ("técrire", "baucoup", "conais", "gerre abile", "segon cienc", "himages"). She reports four good-marks (bons-points) the first day and five the second. While Marie does the accounts, she amuses herself hanging up pretty pictures her aunt gave her.
- When / where: 1 December 1880, Lisieux, at the Guérin house ("nous sommes chez ma Tante").
- People: Pauline (recipient); Marie (sister, doing the accounts); Mme Guérin (aunt); Louis Martin ("Papa").
- Source: LT 6 (to Pauline, 1 Dec 1880), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-6-a-pauline-1er-decembre-1880/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je fais baucoup de fautes, mais tu conais bien ta petite Thérèse et tu sait bien que Je ne suis gerre abile. [...] J'ai eu quatre bons-points le prmier Jours et le segon cienc. »
- Translation: "I make lots of mistakes, but you know your little Thérèse well, and you know I am not very clever at it. [...] I got four good-marks the first day and the second five."
- New to English readers: yes — the misspelled original is not in the 1911 edition or baseline.
- Confidence: high — the Carmel transcription keeps her spelling.
- Notes: Good evidence for her real childhood writing level at nearly eight. Compare the tidy spelling of LT 5 (1878), which may have been corrected by an adult.

### Age eight: roses thrown at St Domitia, and "show my letter to no one"
- Claim: Thérèse writes to Pauline, who is on holiday at Houlgate. She asks if Pauline rode a donkey. She thanks Pauline for giving her a holiday from lessons while Pauline is away. On St Domitia's day her aunt put a pink sash on her and she threw roses to St Domitia. She ends: "Show my letter to no one."
- When / where: about 4 July 1881; Thérèse in Lisieux, Pauline at Houlgate.
- People: Pauline (recipient, her teacher at home); Mme Guérin (aunt); Marie; Marie-Thérèse and "la petite Marguerite" (people with Pauline, not identified in the letter).
- Source: LT 7 (to Pauline, 4 Jul 1881), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-7-a-pauline-4-juillet-1881/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je te remercie bien de me donner congé pendant que Tu est à houlgat. [...] Si Tu savais le Jour de Sainte Domitia ma Tante ma mis une sinture rose et j'ai Jeté des roses à Sainte domitia. Ne montre ma lettre à personne. »
- Translation: "Thank you for giving me a holiday while you are at Houlgate. [...] If you only knew: on St Domitia's day my aunt put a pink sash on me and I threw roses to St Domitia. Don't show my letter to anyone."
- New to English readers: yes — absent from 1911; not in baseline.
- Confidence: high.
- Notes: Shows that Pauline was her teacher ("congé" = time off from lessons). Spelling still childish ("sinture", "houlgat").

### Age nine: her "confession" to Mère Marie de Gonzague — she talks back
- Claim: Writing to the Carmel prioress, Thérèse confesses that lately she always answers back when Marie tells her to do something. She repeats a family saying: when Pauline was small and made excuses to her aunt at Le Mans, the aunt said "As many holes, as many pegs". Thérèse says she is "even worse". She wants to put a pretty flower in each "hole" and offer it to the Child Jesus to prepare for First Communion. She signs "Thérésita".
- When / where: November–December 1882 (date uncertain in the source), Lisieux. Pauline had entered the Carmel in October 1882.
- People: Mère Marie de Gonzague (prioress of the Lisieux Carmel); Marie Martin (sister, in charge of her at home); Pauline; "ma tante du Mans" (Sr Marie-Dosithée, Visitation nun of Le Mans — the name is not in the letter).
- Source: LT 9 (to Mère Marie de Gonzague, Nov–Dec 1882), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-9-a-mere-marie-de-gonzague-nov-dec-1882/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Depuis quelque temps je répons toujours quand Marie me dit de faire quelquechose [...] elle lui disait : Autant de trous autant de chevilles, mais moi c'est bien pire encore. »
- Translation: "For some time now I always answer back when Marie tells me to do something. [...] she used to tell her: 'As many holes, as many pegs.' But with me it is even worse."
- New to English readers: yes — "autant de chevilles" ABSENT from 1911; not in baseline.
- Confidence: high.
- Notes: A child's admitted fault, in her own words. "Autant de trous autant de chevilles" means: for every fault, an excuse ready. The identification of the Le Mans aunt is from general family history, not from this letter.

### Preparing for First Communion: the little book of "practices" and the rosary of practices
- Claim: Pauline sent Thérèse a little handmade book for her First Communion preparation. Thérèse loved its picture of a dove giving its heart to the Child Jesus. She tries each day to do as many "practices" (small acts of virtue) as she can and says the short prayers "that make the scent of roses". She thanks Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin for a "chapelet de pratique" (a counting rosary for good acts) and for embroidering the cover of her book. She had not written earlier because she thought letters were not allowed in Lent.
- When / where: 1–6 March 1884, Lisieux.
- People: Pauline (Sr Agnès de Jésus, Carmelite); Marie (showed her the book); Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin (Carmelite of Lisieux); Mère Marie de Gonzague; Léonie and Céline.
- Source: LT 11 (to Sr Agnès de Jésus, 1–6 Mar 1884), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-11-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-1-6-mars-1884/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Tous les jours je tâche de faire le plus de pratiques que je peux [...] Remercie bien pour moi ma Sr Thérèse de St Augustin de son joli petit chapelet de pratique, et de m'avoir brodé la belle couverture de mon livre. »
- Translation: "Every day I try to do as many practices as I can [...] Please thank Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin for me for her pretty little rosary of practices, and for embroidering the lovely cover of my book."
- Other sources: Ms A mentions the book and the counting of sacrifices (partly in baseline as "preparation for First Communion").
- New to English readers: partial — the book is described in Ms A (Taylor 1912); the letter's details (embroidered cover, the nun's name) are not in baseline.
- Confidence: high.
- Notes: Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin appears here as a kind helper in 1884. The letter says nothing about any later relationship.

### May 1885: constant headaches before the second Communion retreat
- Claim: Her aunt asked for news of her health. Thérèse writes that she is better than on Sunday, but "I still have a very bad headache". She will enter retreat on Sunday evening; the Communion stays fixed on the 21st. She signs "enf[ant] des Sts Anges" (a member of the Holy Angels association at school).
- When / where: 10–17 May 1885, Lisieux.
- People: Mme Céline Guérin (aunt); Jeanne and Marie Guérin (Marie "finishing her cure").
- Source: LT 16 (to Mme Guérin, fragments, 10–17 May 1885), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-16-a-mme-guerin-fragments-10-17-mai-1885/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je vais mieux que dimanche, cependant j'ai toujours très mal à la tête. »
- Translation: "I am better than on Sunday, but I still have a very bad headache."
- Other sources: Ms A says her scruples began in the retreat before this second Communion (1885).
- New to English readers: yes — the headache report is not in baseline or 1911.
- Confidence: high for the words; the letter is fragmentary.
- Notes: A health detail between the 1883 illness and the 1885–86 scruples. The letter does not give a cause.

### The "little Queen" and her "Papa-King": Pauline's verses copied for her father's feast
- Claim: Her father was away on a journey on his feast day (St Louis, 25 August). Pauline had written verses for Thérèse to recite to him. Since she could not recite them, Thérèse copied them into her letter: "Le Souhaits d'une petite Reine pour la fête de son Papa-Roi" (sixteen quatrains). She signs "Ta Reine".
- When / where: 25 August 1885, Lisieux; Louis Martin travelling.
- People: Louis Martin (father); Pauline (author of the verses).
- Source: LT 18 (to M. Martin, 25 Aug 1885), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-18-a-m-martin-25-aout-1885/
- Source type: own-writing (letter) enclosing verses by Pauline
- Rights: public-domain
- Original words: « Mais je n'ai point d'aile qui brille / Je ne suis point un Séraphin / Je suis une petite fille / Qu'on tient encore par la main. »
- Translation: "But I have no shining wing, I am not a Seraph, I am a little girl who is still held by the hand."
- Other sources: 1911 (Pauline's biographical chapter) says her father called her "sa petite reine... de France et de Navarre".
- New to English readers: yes — the poem and "Papa-Roi" are ABSENT from 1911; the "little queen" nickname is partial.
- Confidence: high.
- Notes: The verses are Pauline's, not Thérèse's. Do not quote them as Thérèse's poetry. The letter shows the King/Queen game was in place by 1885.

### The swing and the rings: Marie fears she will become hunchbacked
- Claim: Marie was afraid Thérèse would become hunchbacked, so she asked Papa to hang rings and a swing. Thérèse prefers the swing; her hands are all red from the rings. The day before she spent the afternoon at Madame Maudelonde's with Céline and Hélène. Madame Papinot gave her a day off for the feast of Our Lady of Mount Carmel so she could attend the sermon.
- When / where: Thursday 15 July 1886, Les Buissonnets.
- People: Marie Guérin (recipient); Marie Martin; Louis Martin; Mme Maudelonde (a relative of the Guérins); Céline Maudelonde and Hélène Maudelonde (the letter says "Céline et Hélène"); Mme Papinot.
- Source: LT 20 (to Marie Guérin, 15 Jul 1886), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-20-a-marie-guerin-15-juillet-1886/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je viens de me balancer ; Marie a peur que je devienne bossue et elle a demendé à Papa de pendre les anneaux et la balançoire ; les anneaux me plaisent moins que la balançoire, j'ai les mains toutes rouges d'y avoir été. »
- Translation: "I have just been on the swing. Marie is afraid I will become hunchbacked, and she asked Papa to hang up the rings and the swing. I like the rings less than the swing; my hands are all red from them."
- Other sources: 1911 Ms A mentions a swing in an earlier childhood scene (a different episode).
- New to English readers: yes — this episode is not in 1911 or baseline.
- Confidence: high for facts; medium for identifying "Céline et Hélène" as the Maudelonde girls (the letter only gives first names).
- Notes: A concrete body/health detail at age 13: the family worried about her posture.

### Waiting for Marie's return: candles to the Virgin, a letter from "Monsieur Pichon", and the maid Félicité
- Claim: Marie was travelling with their father. The family worried that Marie would not know to meet "le Père" at Dover on his return. Céline sent letters to Dover and Calais poste restante. Thérèse says the Virgin "had a candle every day" and she prayed hard. A telegram arrived and relieved them. A letter from "Monsieur Pichon" to Papa was opened on Pauline's advice. Thérèse begs Marie not to bring her any souvenir. In a postscript: "Félicité ... has been in a charming mood since you left."
- When / where: Saturday 2 October 1886, 6 p.m., feast of the Holy Angels, Lisieux.
- People: Marie Martin (godmother, "Marraine"); Louis Martin; "le Père" (not named; a priest returning via Dover); "Monsieur Pichon"; Céline; Pauline (in the Carmel); Léonie; Félicité (household servant); the Guérins.
- Source: LT 21 (to Marie, 2 Oct 1886), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-21-a-marie-2-octobre-1886/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Tous les jours la Ste Vierge a eu un cierge et je l'ai tant priée et suppliée [...] Je t'en conjure ne me rapporte rien cela me fera vraiment de la peine. [...] Félicité te dit bien des choses elle est d'une humeur charmante depuis que tu est partie »
- Translation: "Every day the Blessed Virgin had a candle, and I prayed and begged her so much [...] I beg you, don't bring me anything back; it would really hurt me. [...] Félicité sends her regards; she has been in a charming mood since you left."
- New to English readers: yes — not in 1911 or baseline.
- Confidence: medium — the facts of the letter are clear, but who "le Père" is and the purpose of the trip are not stated.
- Notes: The letter does not say who "le Père" is (possibly Fr Pichon himself, but "Monsieur Pichon" writes separately, so it may be a different person). Marie entered the Carmel on 15 October 1886 (baseline); this letter is two weeks before. The line about Félicité's mood since Marie left may be a joke about Marie's strictness; the letter does not explain it.

### An April Fool to Céline
- Claim: On the eve of 1 April, Thérèse wrote Céline a two-line mock-solemn rhyme about her "Diadème" passing to Céline's head, then "Poisson d'Avril!" and the promise of a comb from the April fish.
- When / where: 31 March 1887, Les Buissonnets.
- People: Céline.
- Source: LT 22 (to Céline, 31 Mar 1887), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-22-a-celine-31-mars-1887/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je garde mon Diadème jusqu'à Demain matin / Mais après sur ta tête passera mon Destin / Poisson d'Avril !... / Demain tu auras un peigne que le poisson d'avril te donnera. »
- Translation: "I keep my Diadem until tomorrow morning, but after that my Destiny will pass onto your head. April Fool! Tomorrow you will get a comb that the April fish will give you."
- New to English readers: yes.
- Confidence: high for text; low for what the "diadème" refers to (not explained).
- Notes: Playfulness three months after the Christmas 1886 "conversion".

### "My sermons on death", "my eyes that fascinate you", and the silkworms
- Claim: To her cousin Jeanne, Thérèse drew a sailing boat herself because "the artist Darel" was not there. She jokes that Jeanne must be glad no longer to hear "my sermons on death", no longer to see "my eyes that fascinate you", and no longer to be pushed on the way to the Misses Pigeon. Eight of her silkworms have died; four remain. She blames Céline, who "gave them so much care" that they died "of grief or of sudden apoplexy". "The tall Englishwoman" has left. Papa will leave on Wednesday for a short stay in Alençon, so the aunt's invitation cannot be accepted.
- When / where: 27 June 1887, Les Buissonnets; Guérins at Trouville.
- People: Jeanne Guérin (cousin); "l'artiste Darel" (not identified); "la grande Anglaise" (not identified); "les demoiselles Pigeon" (not identified); Céline; Louis Martin; Mme Guérin.
- Source: LT 24 (to Jeanne Guérin, 27 Jun 1887), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-24-a-jeanne-guerin-27-juin-1887/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription/notes: unknown
- Original words: « Je pense que tu es bien contente de ne plus entendre mes sermons sur la mort, de ne plus voir mes yeux qui te fascinent et de ne plus être poussée en allant chez les demoiselles Pigeon... / J'ai à vous annoncer la mort de huit de mes chers vers à soie [...] Céline leur a prodigué tant de soins qu'elle est arrivée à me les faire mourir presque tous de chagrin ou d'apoplexie foudroyante »
- Translation: "I think you are very glad no longer to hear my sermons on death, no longer to see my eyes that fascinate you, and no longer to be pushed on the way to the Misses Pigeon... I must announce the death of eight of my dear silkworms [...] Céline gave them so much care that she managed to make almost all of them die of grief or sudden apoplexy."
- New to English readers: yes — "vers à soie" and "sermons sur la mort" ABSENT from 1911.
- Confidence: high.
- Notes: Shows her humour and self-mockery at 14, after the 1886 conversion. She mocks her own habit of preaching about death to her cousin.

### Léonie's last week at home before the Visitation; Céline's dead birds
- Claim: Léonie had two days left at the Buissonnets before entering the Visitation. Thérèse feels sorrow mixed with joy: she believes Léonie will be happy only there and will find at the Visitation "all that she lacks in the world". Céline is "in mourning" for her two cordon-bleu birds (finches); the male followed his mate the next morning, and their bodies are at the taxidermist. ### M-1 — Candles to the Virgin and the awaited telegram
- Period: during life — childhood (October 1886)
- What was reported: Thérèse lit a candle to the Virgin every day and prayed hard that Marie would learn in time to meet "le Père" at Dover. A telegram then came, which she took to mean Marie had met him.

- What was reported: Thérèse wrote that the Child Jesus "made me feel so clearly that he wanted me at Christmas". After the bishop sent her New Year gifts, she wrote: "So the Child Jesus did not deceive me! He said yes to me from his cradle."

### SCENES

1. The audience with Leo XIII, told that night — cards "Pauline tells her to speak to the Pope", "Eve of the audience", "The audience with Leo XIII, told the same evening", "Conflict: the audience in the letter vs Story of a Soul". Why: the contemporary letter gives a plainer, sadder scene (an exhausted old Pope, Révérony speaking for him) than the famous Ms A version.
2. The uncle's first answer — cards "Her uncle's first answer: 'a public scandal'" and "Conflict: the uncle's refusal". Why: shows the real social objection (a child in Carmel would be a public scandal) in her words the same day.
3. The lamb in the snow — cards "The newborn lamb that died the same day" and "Edit: the lamb letter in 1911". Why: a tender father–daughter story with a clear ending, and a clean example of Pauline's edits.
4. Assisi carriage — card "Assisi: left behind, she rides in Révérony's carriage". Why: a small comic scene that also tests what Révérony promised.
5. The carp and the fruit avalanche — cards "Papa's carp" and "Alençon lace... an 'avalanche' of fruit and onions through the turn". Why: concrete, funny daily life in the Carmel, and her joke that the fruit got in more easily than she did.
6. Clothing gifts and the refectory — cards "Gifts for the clothing: a lily crown, a giant sugar stick, gingerbread" and "A melon, champagne and fireworks". Why: a 16-year-old's pride and the family's generosity just before the father's collapse.
7. Caen — cards "'Is it possible that I am writing to you in Caen?'", "New Year 1890: 'I still see Papa...'", "New Year's Eve 1889: 'now we are orphans'". Why: the family's grief over the father's confinement in her own words, much of it cut in 1911.
8. Advising a scrupulous cousin — cards "Scruples: advising Marie Guérin..." and "The prioress's verdict...". Why: a novice of 16 gives firm advice from her own experience; first strong statement of "confiance".

### GAPS AND CONFLICTS

Silences in these letters
- No letter mentions the Christmas 1886 grace, Pranzini, or the Pentecost 1887 conversation with her father (both baseline from Ms A).
- No letter mentions her father's disappearance to Le Havre in June 1888 or the nature of his illness. LT 66 says only "très malade"; LT 82 names only "Caen"; the Bon-Sauveur asylum is never named. Words used: "malheur", "épreuve", "croix", "là-bas".
- The day of the clothing (10 Jan 1889) is not described after the event (no letter in this file tells what happened, e.g. snow, the father's presence).
- No description of her work (offices) in the Carmel in this period, beyond decorating the Child Jesus statue (LT 90, 96, 97), painting a picture (LT 61), making holy cards (LT 78) and cribs from moss (LT 62).
- No explicit mention of the delay of her profession (it took place 8 Sep 1890, after this file ends).

Conflicts with Ms A / 1911
- Uncle Guérin's first answer (8 Oct 1887): letter = affectionate, "sure of vocation", obstacle "le monde", age 17–18; Ms A = opposition "unless a miracle".
- The papal audience (20 Nov 1887): letter = Pope nearly unable to speak, Révérony answers, Pope says "Si le bon Dieu veut vous entrerez", she is passed into another room; Ms A adds Révérony's ban on speaking, Céline's "Parle!", guards lifting her, the Pope's hand on her lips.
- Assisi (24 Nov 1887): letter = Révérony "did not speak to me at all about my affairs"; Ms A = he talked of Carmel and promised to do all he could; LT 38 = he "promised me during the Rome journey" to speak to the bishop. The promise may have been given on another day; unresolved.
- Mère Marie de Gonzague: LT 57 (July 1888) = "what a treasure", brought the letter at 6 a.m.; LT 78 (Jan 1889) = "constantly interrupted... without joy"; LT 93 (July 1889) = "a deep knowledge of souls". Ms A (1895) describes severity toward her in her first years. The letters show both warmth and frustration.
- Her health in the first winter: LT 69 "they look after you so well... I always have to eat and warm my feet"; LT 58 "never eaten so much". These family-facing lines differ in tone from later accounts of cold and hunger in the Carmel (not in this file).

Pauline's edits in the 1911 edition (summary; each has a card)
- Redatings: LT 43 (Mar 1888) → "1887"; LT 55 (Jul 1888) → "1889"; LT 91 (May 1889) → "14 août 1889"; LT 92 (May 1889) → "1888"; LT 95 (1889) → "1891"; LT 54/103/106 (1888–1890) → "1892"; LT 109 (Jul 1890) → "1894".
- Composites: 1911 letters to Céline I, II, III, V, VI and to Mère Agnès II, VII each join two or three letters from different months.
- Changed key words: "faiblesse... toute sa confiance" → "toute ma force" (LT 55); "communier quand même" → "communier toujours" (LT 92); "chemin de la vertu" → "chemin du sacrifice" (LT 109); "chérubins" → "Séraphins" (LT 83); "Pauline" → "Maman" (LT 43).
- Added sentences not in the letters: "L'humiliation est la seule voie qui fait les saints" (1911 Céline IV vs LT 82); "A toutes les extases, je préfère le sacrifice" (vs LT 55); "Pour cela, il suffit de faire toutes nos actions par amour" (vs LT 91); "Tu voudrais devenir une sainte, et tu me demandes si ce n'est pas trop oser" (vs LT 65/107); "je me suis passionnée pour l'oubli" (vs LT 103); "mon cœur est déchiré" (vs LT 108).
- Cut material: complaints about the community (LT 74), the prioress (LT 78), bad priests (LT 94), the place Caen (LT 82), "the complement of his beautiful life" (LT 82), Léonie "plus malheureuse" (LT 83), the request to tear up her notes (LT 76), almost all humour and household detail.

Date and transcription problems
- LT 22–LT 26 and LT 31 are dated firmly; LT 29, 40, 43, 51, 52, 54, 64, 69, 81, 90, 95, 103 have "(?)" or ranges in the source.
- LT 31 / 31 A and LT 31 B: the source lists LT 31 B (14 Nov) before LT 31 (10 Nov).
- LT 90 (Apr 1889) says she has "beaucoup de mémoire pour les dates"; LT 98 (Oct 1889) says she never knows the date. A harmless self-contradiction.
- Unidentified names in the letters: "le Père" (LT 21), "l'artiste Darel", "la grande Anglaise", "les demoiselles Pigeon" (LT 24), "Monsieur de Virville" (LT 42), "M. Benoît" (LT 51), "la marraine qui plantait des verveines" (LT 62), "l'Intrépide N° 2" (LT 66), "Lolo" (LT 101), "Belloni" (LT 95), "Mlle Pauline" (LT 58, 83), "la Pott" (LT 50). A biographer should not guess these.
- LT 94's postscript and LT 105/LT 107 show the Carmel limited letters in retreats (Pentecost 1890) and that postage weight mattered.
