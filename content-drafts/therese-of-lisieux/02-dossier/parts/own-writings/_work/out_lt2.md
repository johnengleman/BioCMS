# St. Thérèse of Lisieux: letters LT 110–LT 181 (Aug 1890 – Nov 1895). Fact cards

Source file read in full: lt_part2.txt (letter texts only, with no Carmel editorial notes). Every card is based on Thérèse's own words. Where a card identifies a person or explains something that the letter does not state, the card says so.
URL pattern: https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/<slug>/
Edition check: the 1911 "Histoire d'une âme" (Gutenberg #36708) was checked with chk.py and by reading the matching 1911 letters. In the notes below, "1911" means that edition.

### Profession retreat: the "underground passage" and the honeymoon with no view
- Claim: In her retreat before profession, Thérèse told Pauline that Jesus had led her into an "underground passage", neither cold nor hot, with no sun, rain or wind. The only light came from the lowered eyes of Christ's Face. She and her "Fiancé" said nothing to each other. She could not see any progress. She said the road gave her no consolation, and yet it gave her all consolations because Jesus chose it.
- When / where: 30–31 Aug 1890, Carmel of Lisieux, during the retreat before her profession on 8 Sept
- People: Sr Agnès de Jésus (Pauline Martin), her sister, whom she calls "Petite Maman"
- Source: LT 110 (to Sr Agnès de Jésus, 30–31 Aug 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-110-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-30-31-aout-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Il m'a fait entrer dans un souterrain où il ne fait ni froid ni chaud, où le soleil ne luit pas et que la pluie ni le vent ne visitent pas »
- Translation: "He made me go into an underground passage where it is neither cold nor hot, where the sun does not shine, and where neither rain nor wind comes."
- Other sources: LT 111 (to Marie): "Son voyage de noces est bien aride… la nuit lui empêche de rien admirer". LT 112: "je suis dans un souterrain bien obscur". LT 115: "Mon âme est toujours dans le souterrain mais elle y est bien heureuse".
- New to English readers: partial. The 1911 edition prints it as "Lettre IVe" to Mère Agnès, but that letter is a composite of LT 110, LT 112 and LT 115, and it is reworded (see the edit card).
- Confidence: high. Her own text.
- Notes: The same letter also has practical questions: « Faut-il écrire à M. Le pelletier et à M. Révérony que je fais profession ?... » ("Should I write to M. Lepelletier and M. Révérony that I am making profession?").

### Pauline's little glass of wine, and permission from Mother Prioress
- Claim: Thérèse reminded Pauline to go to the cellar to take her "little wine". She herself was taking a "little wine" during the retreat. On 1 Sept she wrote that she would take hers, and that she would gladly have had it that morning, but she could not find "Notre Mère" (the prioress, whose permission she needed).
- When / where: 30–31 Aug and 1 Sept 1890, Carmel
- People: Sr Agnès de Jésus (Pauline); "Notre Mère", the prioress, Mère Marie de Gonzague (the letter does not give her name)
- Source: LT 110 (P.S.), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-110-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-30-31-aout-1890/ ; LT 112 (to Sr Agnès, 1 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-112-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-1-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Surtout n'oubliez pas d'aller dans la cave prendre votre petit vin » ; « (Je vais prendre mon petit vin, le même ce matin aurait été bien de mon goût mais je n'ai pas pu trouver Notre Mère). »
- Translation: "Above all, do not forget to go to the cellar and take your little wine." / "(I am going to take my little wine. The same this morning would have been very much to my taste, but I could not find Our Mother.)"
- Other sources: none in this file
- New to English readers: yes. "petit vin" is absent from the 1911 text, and the 1911 composite letter cuts the P.S.
- Confidence: high for the words. Medium for the reason (probably a tonic allowed by the prioress). The letter does not say why they took the wine.
- Notes: She links the wine to the "sweet wine of Engaddi" that she does not drink. This is a small detail of daily life and health in the Carmel.

### A letter for her sick father "who will not understand"
- Claim: Thérèse gave Pauline a letter she had written for their father. She asked Pauline to make a draft for her if it would not do, "but I really think he will not understand". On 4 Sept she sent "the letter from Rome" (the papal blessing) for Céline to take to him. She said, "if sometimes he understood, he would be so happy". She asked whether to send him her vows to bless, placed "in the middle of the crown".
- When / where: 1 and 4 Sept 1890. Louis Martin was then in the asylum at Caen (the Bon Sauveur). The letters say only "Papa".
- People: Louis Martin, her father; Pauline; Céline
- Source: LT 112 (1 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-112-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-1-septembre-1890/ ; LT 115 (4 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-115-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-4-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Je vous donne la lettre que j'ai écrite pour Papa ; si vous trouvez qu'elle ne peut pas aller, vous allez bien vouloir me faire un petit brouillon, mais je crois bien qu'il ne comprendra pas... » ; « Peut-être Papa ne comprendra-t-il pas, mais cela n'est pas difficile à emporter et si quelquefois il comprenait il serait si heureux. »
- Translation: "I am giving you the letter I wrote for Papa. If you think it will not do, please make me a little draft, but I really think he will not understand…" / "Perhaps Papa will not understand, but it is not hard to take there, and if sometimes he understood he would be so happy."
- Other sources: Ms A tells of the profession and her father's illness in general terms.
- New to English readers: yes. These passages are absent from the 1911 composite.
- Confidence: high
- Notes: The place (Caen) is not named in these two letters. LT 137 names "l'épreuve si douloureuse de Caen" (the very painful trial of Caen).

### Confession before profession: a "little review" with M. Youf
- Claim: Before profession, Thérèse was to see M. Youf the next day. He told her to make "a little review" only of the time since she entered the Carmel. She was very happy to have received absolution on Saturday. She did not understand her retreat and "thought of nothing".
- When / where: 1 Sept 1890, Carmel
- People: Abbé Youf (the file gives only "M. Youf". His role as the Carmel's chaplain comes from outside knowledge, not from this letter); Pauline
- Source: LT 112 (1 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-112-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-1-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Demain je vais trouver M. Youf ; il m'a dit de lui faire une petite revue seulement depuis que je suis au Carmel »
- Translation: "Tomorrow I am going to see M. Youf. He told me to make him a little review, only of the time since I have been in the Carmel."
- Other sources: none in this file
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high

### "Do you think Céline is really going to die?"
- Claim: Five days before her profession, Thérèse asked Pauline whether Céline was really going to die. She had promised Céline the day before to make profession "for both of us". She would not have the courage to ask Jesus to leave Céline on earth if that was not His will. She added: "love can make up for a long life".
- When / where: 3 Sept 1890, Carmel
- People: Céline Martin, who was ill at that time (the nature of the illness is not stated); Pauline
- Source: LT 114 (to Sr Agnès, 3 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-114-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-3-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Croyez-vous que Céline va vraiment mourir ?... Je lui ai promis hier de faire profession pour nous deux »
- Translation: "Do you believe Céline is really going to die?… Yesterday I promised her to make profession for the two of us."
- Other sources: 1911 "Lettre Ve" (1890) to Mère Agnès keeps the lines on love and the white robe but cuts the question about Céline.
- New to English readers: yes. The Céline passage is absent in 1911 ("vraiment mourir": ABSENT).
- Confidence: high for the words. The letter gives no diagnosis.
- Notes: A companion card covers her request to die on her profession day.

### Asking to die on profession day if she would offend God afterwards
- Claim: Thérèse asked Pauline to tell Jesus to take her on her profession day if she was going to offend Him after it. She wanted to carry to heaven "the white robe of my second baptism" without stain. She also wrote that she wanted, not "sensible" love, but love "felt by Jesus".
- When / where: 3 Sept 1890
- People: Pauline
- Source: LT 114, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-114-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-3-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « je ne demande pas de l'amour sensible mais seulement senti de Jésus »
- Translation: "I do not ask for love that I can feel, only for love that Jesus can feel."
- Other sources: 1911 Lettre Ve (1890) to Mère Agnès
- New to English readers: partial. It is in 1911 with a change: 1911 reads « pourvu qu'il soit sensible pour Jésus, cela me suffit » ("provided that it is sensible for Jesus, that is enough for me"). 1911 also adds « qui ne lui fassent pas de peine ».
- Confidence: high
- Notes: She signs this letter "le petit grain de sable" ("the little grain of sand").

### Profession practicalities: sewing, notices and the papal blessing
- Claim: Sr Thérèse de Jésus gave Thérèse a note asking her to do some work. Thérèse did not have patterns, and she thought "the linen and the Blessed Virgin" were more urgent. She asked Pauline whether to tell Abbé Domin (of the Abbey) and Mme Papinot about the profession. For Mme Papinot, she thought it better to wait until the veiling. She wanted to thank the "holy old man Simeon", whose letter (a papal blessing from Rome) had arrived.
- When / where: 1–4 Sept 1890
- People: Sr Thérèse de Jésus (a nun of the Lisieux Carmel); Abbé Domin (connected with "l'Abbaye" and "ces Dames"); Mme Papinot; "St Vieillard Siméon"
- Source: LT 112, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-112-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-1-septembre-1890/ ; LT 114, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-114-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-3-septembre-1890/ ; LT 115, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-115-a-soeur-agnes-de-jesus-4-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « Il me semble qu'il faudrait remercier le «St Vieillard Siméon» et lui dire que sa lettre est arrivée » ; « Je n'ai pas de modèles et puis il me semble que le linge et la Ste Vierge sont plus pressés »
- Translation: "I think we should thank the 'holy old man Simeon' and tell him his letter has arrived." / "I have no patterns, and it seems to me the linen and the Blessed Virgin are more urgent."
- Other sources: LT 121: « J'ai eu le bonheur de recevoir la bénédiction du Saint Père pour le jour de ma Profession. Le religieux qui me l'a procurée m'écrivait combien les ennemis de l'Eglise sont nombreux ». ("I had the happiness of receiving the Holy Father's blessing for my profession day. The religious who obtained it for me wrote how many the enemies of the Church are.")
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high for the words. Medium for linking "Siméon" in LT 114 with "the religious" in LT 121. The link is likely, but the letters do not state it.
- Notes: "St Vieillard Siméon" is in quotation marks in her text, so it is a nickname.

### The faded pink candles from Papa
- Claim: For her profession, Thérèse asked Marie to light the candles of the Child Jesus statue before she went to chapter. She did not use the new pink candles. She used the old ones, which had first burned on her clothing day, when Papa (who had given them) was present. Now "the pink colour has gone". She called herself "the orphan of the Berezina".
- When / where: 7 Sept 1890, eve of profession, Carmel
- People: Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur (Marie Martin, her godmother); Louis Martin
- Source: LT 116 (to Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur, 7 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-116-a-soeur-marie-du-sacre-coeur-7-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « elles ont commencé à brûler le jour de ma prise d'habit, alors elles étaient roses et fraîches. Papa (qui me les avait données) était là et tout était joie !... mais maintenant la couleur de rose est partie, y a-t-il encore pour l'orpheline de la Beresina des joies couleur de rose ici-bas ? »
- Translation: "They began to burn on my clothing day. Then they were pink and fresh. Papa (who had given them to me) was there and all was joy!… But now the pink colour has gone. Are there still rose-coloured joys here below for the orphan of the Berezina?"
- Other sources: 1911 Lettre Ve (7 Sept 1890) to Marie
- New to English readers: partial. The candles are in 1911, but 1911 cuts "l'orpheline de la Beresina" (ABSENT) and changes the addressee's words.
- Confidence: high
- Notes: "Beresina" refers to the 1812 retreat from Russia, used here as a family joke about being an orphan. The letter does not explain it.

### Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come
- Claim: On 23 Sept 1890, the eve of her public veiling, Thérèse wrote to Céline. The joy of seeing their father at the ceremony had been refused. Her tears were still flowing and she could "hardly hold my pen". She said "another" would explain the talk in "my uncle's parlour", but she could only speak "the language of Heaven". She quoted a letter from "the exiled Father": "Neither of your fathers will be there to offer you to Jesus."
- When / where: 23 Sept 1890, Carmel; the veiling was the next day
- People: Céline; Isidore Guérin, her uncle (his "parloir"); "le Père exilé" (identity not given in the letter, but likely Fr Almire Pichon SJ, then in Canada, from outside knowledge); Louis Martin
- Source: LT 120 (to Céline, 23 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-120-a-celine-23-septembre-1890/
- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown
- Original words: « je puis à peine tenir ma plume... et puis une autre te ferait beaucoup d'explications du parloir de mon oncle, mais ta Thérèse ne sait te parler que le langage du Ciel » ; « Ni l'un ni l'autre de vos pères ne sera là pour vous offrir à Jésus. »
- Translation: "I can hardly hold my pen… Another girl would give you many explanations about the talk in my uncle's parlour, but your Thérèse can only speak to you the language of Heaven." / "Neither of your fathers will be there to offer you to Jesus."
- Other sources: Ms A tells of her tears on the veiling day. 1911 Lettre IXe keeps the core of the letter.
- New to English readers: partial. The core is in 1911, but the uncle's parlour and the Pichon quotation are cut.
- Confidence: high for the words. The letter implies, but does not state, that the decision was made in the uncle's parlour.
- Notes: "Ni l'un ni l'autre de vos pères": her father and her director could not come.

### Formal letter after profession: "enemies of the Church" in Rome
- Claim: Thérèse wrote a formal letter to Sr Marie-Joseph de la Croix. She said she was now "all Jesus's" and had received the Holy Father's blessing for her profession. The religious who obtained it wrote how many the enemies of the Church were, and that in Rome "the struggle never stops for a moment" against the Pope. She asked for prayers for her father, "so tried by the cross and so admirable in his resignation".
- When / where: 28 Sept 1890, "Monastère du Carmel"
- People: Sr Marie-Joseph de la Croix (of another community; which one is not named); Louis Martin; Pope Leo XIII (as "le Saint Père")
- Source: LT 121 (to Sr Marie-Joseph de la Croix, 28 Sept 1890), https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/correspondance/lt-121-a-soeur-marie-joseph-de-la-croix-28-septembre-1890/

- Other sources: LT 114–115 (the "letter from Rome")

### "I do not see the Sacred Heart like everyone else"
- Claim: Writing to Céline, who was on a journey "là-bas", Thérèse said she did not see the Sacred Heart as everyone else did. She thought of her Spouse's heart as hers alone, as hers was His alone. She asked Céline to murmur her name "over there". She could not write to Léonie: "I don't even have time to reread myself". She signed "nov.carm.ind." - When / where: 14 Oct 1890. The journey's destination is not named; the Sacred Heart link suggests Paray-le-Monial, but that is not stated.
- People: Céline; Léonie; Marie (the letter was added to "la lettre de Marie")

- Source type: own-writing
- Rights: public-domain (her words); Carmel transcription: unknown

### Feast-day presents: heather pots and cakes from the Guérins
- Claim: For her feast (15 Oct), the Guérins sent Marie Guérin to wish her a happy feast. Jeanne and Marie sent two pots of heather, now placed near the Child Jesus. Her aunt sent "delicious cakes". Thérèse knew her aunt was ill. She also prayed to comfort her aunt for "the great emptiness" left by Jeanne's departure (Jeanne had just married).

### SCENES

1. **Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come** (Sept 1890). Cards: "Eve of the veiling: Papa will not come"; "The faded pink candles from Papa"; "EDIT: 1911 adds 'Papa ne viendra pas demain!'". Why it matters: it joins her father's illness to her profession, in her own tears, and shows how the 1911 edition sharpened the text.
2. **The little hunting dog and the rabbit** (July 1894). Cards: "The 'little hunting dog' and her novice"; "EDIT: 1911 turns one novice into 'my little rabbits'". Why it matters: it is the first picture of Thérèse forming novices, frank and funny, and a clear case of editorial rewriting.
3. **The storm over Céline's vocation** (Aug 1894). Cards: "Céline's vocation contested"; "The 'storm at Caen'"; "'After a death of 5 years'"; "Shopping list for Céline". Why it matters: opposition from a priest-director and from the La Néeles, secrecy towards Mère Marie de Gonzague, and grief for their father all meet in two letters.
4. **Léonie's return from the Visitation** (July 1895). Cards: "Léonie leaves the Visitation"; "Léonie in the parlour, crying". Why it matters: a small, human parlour scene: tears, a cousin who makes "stones laugh", and a meal in earthenware dishes.
5. **The postulant and the photographs at recreation** (Oct 1895). Cards: "'The old dean of the novitiate'"; "Flower patterns, not fish"; "Going to the laundry". Why it matters: it shows community life, humour and work (painting patterns, laundry), none of which survives in 1911.
6. **Uncle Guérin's Bluebeard** (childhood, told Dec 1892). Card: "Uncle Guérin, Bluebeard and 'Mirlitir'". Why it matters: a vivid, funny childhood memory known only from this letter.
7. **The painting that made her cry** (Jan–Apr 1894). Cards: "'The Dream of the Child Jesus'"; "She wept on the painting". Why it matters: her artistic effort, her disappointment and her spiritual dryness ("He almost always sleeps") in one concrete story.
8. **The profession delayed** (1889–90, told 1895). Card: "Profession delayed because the Superior found her too young". Why it matters: in her own words, a Church authority's objection and how she turned it into peace.

### GAPS AND CONFLICTS

- **No letters Nov 1891 – Apr 1892.** This file has nothing on the influenza epidemic (winter 1891–92) or on Mère Geneviève's death (Dec 1891), except that LT 134 (Apr 1892) calls her "notre Sainte Mère Geneviève" and sends a flower she held as a "relic".
- **Father's return (1892) and death (29 July 1894).** Only LT 138 (Nov 1892) mentions the return ("rappelant de son exil"). There is no letter between 18 July and 5–10 Aug 1894. The first letters about his death are LT 169 (19 Aug) and LT 170 (20 Aug). The time counts conflict: LT 169 says "after a death of 5 years" (from his 1889 collapse); LT 170 says "after 6 years of absence" (from her 1888 entry). Both can be true on their own terms.
- **Céline's entry (14 Sept 1894).** No letter in the file records the day. LT 169 is "the last time" she writes to Céline "in the world". LT 174 (Jan 1895) gives her religious name.
- **Fr Pichon's Canada plan.** It is not named anywhere in LT 110–181. LT 168 refers only to "le Père", whom Marie says "was wrong", and to waiting for "his letter". LT 120 quotes "le Père exilé" (1890). Do not state the Canada plan from these letters.
- **Céline's ball / social life.** Not described. LT 149 ("voix amies" warnings; "fleurs du chemin") and LT 167 P.S. ("ce que tu ressens pour les garçons") may relate, but they do not name any event.
- **Offering to Merciful Love (9–11 June 1895).** Not mentioned in any letter in this file. LT 176 (Apr) and LT 177–178 (July) are silent on it.
- **Joan of Arc plays (1894–95).** Not mentioned.
- **Bellière (Oct 1895).** Not mentioned. LT 180 (14–17 Oct 1895) and LT 181 (16 Nov 1895) say nothing about a missionary "brother".
- **Pauline's election (20 Feb 1893).** Covered by LT 140 (on the day itself). Mère Agnès's illness late in 1893 is covered by LT 153. The file does not name the illness.
- **Her jobs in the Carmel.** This file shows novitiate work ("petit chien de chasse", LT 167), painting (LT 156, LT 160, LT 162, LT 180 flower patterns to earn money), laundry (LT 180) and sewing (linen, LT 114). It does not name her official offices (sacristy, refectory, portress, etc.).
- **Health.** LT 167 (July 1894): "j'ai une santé de fer" (an iron constitution). LT 180 (Oct 1895): Dr La Néele "took trouble to find me remedies", with no illness named. LT 169's anti-haemorrhage water was requested by the infirmarian, and the letter does not say for whom. Do not connect these to her later TB without other evidence.
- **Identities not given in the file:** "Marguerite M." (LT 126), "le malheureux prodigue" (LT 129, almost certainly Loyson), the novice "ma fille" (LT 167), "le Père" (LT 168), "Notre Père Supérieur" (LT 176), "Notre Mère" in July 1891 (LT 129). Cards mark these as outside knowledge.
- **Transcription issues:** LT 148 « L'enfant de la Breuse Marg. Marie » (corrupt?); LT 180 « je suis obligé » (masculine); LT 138 « mon cher petite Père »; LT 169 « l'orage qui ronde à Caen » (for "gronde"). Check them against the manuscripts.
- **Unclear passages:** LT 172 "six de vos enfants" (six brides of Christ given by Mme Guérin; the count is unclear); LT 179 ("Demoiselle… Pauvre Monsieur"); LT 167 "j'ai passé par là l'année dernière… Le bon Dieu a voulu que je fasse mon sacrifice" (which sacrifice?); LT 157 Céline "sans asile" (why?).
- **1911 edition, summary of distortions found:** composite letters (Céline XI, XVI, XVII, XIX; Mère Agnès IV; Léonie I and II); wrong dates (LT 164 "pin" moved to Jan 1895; LT 167 lines moved to 19 Aug 1894; LT 180 moved to Aug 1895; LT 161 lines put under Oct 1893); added sentences (LT 120 "Papa ne viendra pas demain!"; LT 122 John of the Cross verse; LT 147 "table des cieux"; LT 165 "notre état nous faisait horreur"; LT 169 opening "le bon Dieu a exaucé mon vœu le plus cher"); Léonie hidden under the name "Sœur Françoise-Thérèse"; cuts of humour, family conflict, class remarks, food, money and her father's "humiliation". Taylor 1912 translated this edited text, so English readers have had the altered versions.
