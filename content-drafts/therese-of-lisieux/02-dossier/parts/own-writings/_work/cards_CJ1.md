### "I have lived so little"; if she had committed every crime
- Claim: "Alas, how little I have lived! Life always seemed very short to me. My childhood days, it seems they were yesterday." ### Her jobs in the Carmel, in her own list (told 13 July 1897)
- Claim: On entering: the linen room with the subprioress Sr Marie des Anges, plus sweeping the stairs and dormitory; asking for mortifications in the refectory cost her; she pulled weeds at 4:30, which displeased the prioress. After clothing (Jan 1889): the refectory until 18 (sweeping, putting out water and beer). Forty Hours 1891: sacristy with Sr Saint-Stanislas. From June 1892: two months without office (painted the angels of the oratory; tierce for the bursar). Then the turn (portress) with Sr Saint-Raphaël, keeping painting, until the elections of 1896, when she asked to help Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph in the linen room. People thought her slow and not devoted; Mère Agnès scolded her for a refectory tablecloth left unmended, "and I was wrong, it was time she lacked"; Thérèse cried without excusing herself. She hated spiders but cleaned the cobwebs from the dark hole of St Alexis under the stairs. Also (12 Jul): a violent inner struggle "with thoughts against authority" when told to prepare a night-light for Mère Marie de Gonzague's family, lodging unexpectedly with the turn sisters; she pretended it was for the Virgin and Child and made it spotless.

### Plans after death: baptising children, helping priests; "no roast larks"
- Claim: "Don't think that when I'm in Heaven I'll drop roast larks into your beaks... That's not what I had or wanted. You may have great trials, but I'll send you lights to value and love them." "Don't imagine I feel a lively joy at dying, like the joy I used to feel going for a month to Trouville or Alençon... Only one expectation makes my heart beat: the love I'll receive and that I'll be able to give. And I think of all the good I'd like to do after my death: have little children baptised, help priests, missionaries, the whole Church... but first comfort my little sisters." "God will have to do all my wishes in Heaven, because I never did my own will on earth." "Since my childhood God gave me the deep feeling that I would die young." Instead of wreaths: "buy three little heathen babies [to baptise]: a little Marie-Louis-Martin, a little Marie-Théophane, a little girl Marie-Cécile... and a little Marie-Thérèse."
