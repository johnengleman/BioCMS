# Digest P4 — 1889–1894 (clothing to Louis's death and Céline's entry)

Source: `02-dossier/_by-period/P4.md` (542 cards). Events: 134 (E-P4-001 to E-P4-134), in time order. Every card appears in the `Cards` line of at least one event. Cards were not changed.

## Period summary

### Turning points
- 10 Jan 1889: clothing (E-P4-005). 12 Feb 1889: Louis Martin enters the mental home at Caen (E-P4-009).
- 1889–90: profession delayed because of her youth (E-P4-025). 8 Sept 1890: profession (E-P4-037). 24 Sept 1890: veiling without her father (E-P4-042).
- Oct 1891: Fr Alexis frees her from fear, and Gonzague forbids a second interview (E-P4-046, E-P4-053).
- 5 Dec 1891: Mère Geneviève dies (E-P4-058). Dec 1891–Jan 1892: influenza epidemic; Delatroëtte is won over (E-P4-059).
- 10 May 1892: Louis returns to Lisieux (E-P4-009). 8 Dec 1892: she warns MJ and Marthe (E-P4-069).
- 20 Feb 1893: Agnès is elected prioress and Thérèse becomes her hidden helper with the novices (E-P4-072, E-P4-073).
- 29 Jul 1894: Louis dies at La Musse (E-P4-107). 14 Sept 1894: Céline enters (E-P4-113). Dec 1894: Ms A is asked for (E-P4-118).

### The five best scenes
1. E-P4-053 — The locked confessional: Gonzague forbids Thérèse a second interview with Père Alexis. She hears him pace in the sacristy and chooses silence.
2. E-P4-058 — Mère Geneviève's coffin: "you put your family's wreaths in front". Thérèse thanks the sister and puts the workmen's moss cross in front.
3. E-P4-069 — 8 Dec 1892: Thérèse lays Marthe's head on her heart and tells her the truth about her attachment to Gonzague (and warns MJ).
4. E-P4-037 — Profession day, 8 Sept 1890: the note on her heart, the forced prayer for Papa's cure, the prayer for Léonie, and Roulland's vocation the same day.
5. E-P4-065 — Sr Marie-Madeleine hides at the hour of direction: "I looked for you and couldn't find you." — "I was busy."
- Runners-up: E-P4-017 (Sr Saint-Pierre), E-P4-049 (the keys), E-P4-041 (avoiding her sisters), E-P4-020 (the pin), E-P4-022 (the dance), E-P4-087 (ready to write Gonzague "all her truths"), E-P4-125 (the shell of tears), E-P4-120 (hands on her knees in the frost), E-P4-042 (the mock invitation), E-P4-115 (Céline's inkwell).

### The five best "new to English" facts
1. Gonzague forbade Thérèse any second interview with Père Alexis in Oct 1891. Story of a Soul presents the retreat as pure grace (E-P4-053: F-EW-739, F-EW-741, F-EW-742).
2. During a "dreadful scene" against Mère Agnès, Thérèse left the dishes saying she would write Gonzague "all her truths" and leave the community rather than let her be damned. Agnès stopped her (E-P4-087: F-EW-847).
3. Louis Martin's committal in detail: his revolver, patient no. 14,449, and the interdiction of 8 Jun 1889 that he signed in tears. Also the prioress's recreation talk about straitjackets in front of the daughters (E-P4-009: F-LF-198, F-LF-199, F-EW-649).
4. The original mock wedding invitation names "Monsieur Louis Martin, owner and master of the Lordships of suffering and humiliation". The 1911 text cut that paragraph, and cut other lines of her letters (E-P4-044: F-OW-103, F-OW-104; E-P4-034).
5. Her first doubt about Heaven came in Oct 1891, five years before the famous trial of faith. Ms A says she wondered "sometimes whether there was a Heaven" (E-P4-046: F-OW-126). The 1911 text cut it.
- Also strong: Sr Marie-Madeleine's and MM's testimony that they fled her because she was "too perfect" (E-P4-065, E-P4-067); Roulland's vocation settled on her profession day (E-P4-037: F-EW-718).

### Open conflicts
1. When she began to guide the novices and whether she had a title: Feb 1893 (Agnès, Marie, MM), "from her profession" (MJ), 1894 (MT). Céline says the post was "taken away and given back every fortnight"; Marie is calmer (E-P4-040, E-P4-073).
2. Were the daughters blamed for Louis's illness at recreation and in the parlour? Mère Agnès says yes (1910 and 1915). Céline and Laveille give only hurtful remarks; the search for a source found none (E-P4-009: F-EW-642, F-EW-649, F-LF-201).
3. Daily communion after the 1891 decree: TSA says Thérèse stayed deprived; MdA and Marie des Anges say Youf granted it for months; Céline says only during the influenza (E-P4-047).
4. Sr Saint-Pierre: unnamed (Mère Agnès), named (others); dates "c. 1889–90" or "years, until 1895" (E-P4-017).
5. Date of the Alexis retreat (1891 or 1892) and the name Blinot/Blino (E-P4-046, E-P4-055, E-P4-033).
6. The critic at Mère Geneviève's coffin: an unnamed lay sister or Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul; different wording (E-P4-058).
7. Influenza: how many were well; Sr Madeleine; Thérèse's age ("scarcely nineteen" but she was 18 in Dec 1891) (E-P4-059).
8. The 1911 edition changes or redates many letters of this period (LT 74/76, 82, 91, 92, 109, 110–122, 142–147, 167–169). Quote the original letters, not the 1911 text.
9. Sr Aimée: "a little saint" (AJ) against "insignificant" or "spoiled" (MM), and her opposition to Céline (E-P4-026, E-P4-062, E-P4-109).
10. Snow at the clothing: Ms A and Laveille differ on the weather (E-P4-005).
11. The name "of the Child Jesus": at entry or at profession (E-P4-038). Holy Thursday 1896 is 2 April, not 4 April (E-P4-111).
12. The dance: date and who "forced" whom; Thérèse's tears (E-P4-022). The Jeanne La Néele death date (24 or 25 Apr 1938) (E-P4-134).

## Events

### E-P4-001 — 6–10 Jan 1889 — Letters during the clothing retreat: "the creatures repel me", the melon, "the Queen of France and Navarre"
- What happened: In her clothing retreat Thérèse wrote to Pauline (LT 76, 7 Jan 1889): « Ce matin j'ai eu de la peine chez ma Sœur St Vincent de Paul, je me suis en allée le cœur bien gros... » She notes Jesus changed the date: « Il a sans doute trouvé que la date du 9 était trop ravissante » (her entry had been on 9 April; clothing set for 10 January). She is "deprived of all consolation" but has great peace; the letter from Fr Pichon is heavenly, "but happiness? ... happiness is only in suffering". On 8 Jan (LT 78): « Chez Notre Mère, je suis continuellement dérangée, et puis quand j'ai un instant, je ne puis lui dire ce qui se passe dans mon âme, je m'en vais sans joie, après être entrée sans joie !... » She is "plunged in darkness". She asks Pauline for Indian ink and gold for her own little clothing holy cards, and to half-open "our door" at 6 a.m. To Marie (LT 79, 8 Jan): « Plus qu'un jour et je serai la Fiancée de Jésus, quelle grâce !... » To her father (LT 77, 8 Jan): a melon and champagne make her want to cry; for the "wedding" of the Queen of France and Navarre there will be fireworks paid by the King; "The little blond cockchafer has only to say thank you." Writing is not allowed in retreat, but "our Mother" allowed the note: "can one trouble one's peace by writing to a Saint?" She signs "La Reine de France et de toutes les Navarre". On her clothing day (LT 80, 10 Jan) she gave a keepsake to Sr Marthe de Jésus, her "dear little companion": « Demandez à Jésus que je devienne une grande sainte, je demanderai la même grâce pour ma chère petite compagne ! » She signs "Sœur Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus de la Sainte Face. nov.carm.ind."
- Cards: F-OW-400, F-OW-401, F-OW-402, F-OW-403, F-OW-437
- Best details:
  - « je m'en vais sans joie, après être entrée sans joie !... » — spiritual direction with the prioress (F-OW-401).
  - « le petit hanneton blond n'a qu'à remercier !... » — the "little blond cockchafer" to her father (F-OW-402).
  - « La Reine de France et de toutes les Navarre » (F-OW-402).
  - The date moved from the 9th: "too delightful" (F-OW-400).
  - She made her own clothing holy cards with ink and gold (F-OW-401).
  - The full name "de la Sainte Face" first appears in the letters on 10 Jan 1889 (LT 80) (F-OW-403).
- Evidence: Thérèse's own letters LT 76–80 (Carmel transcriptions). High.
- Conflicts: The letter does not say what happened at Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul's (F-OW-400). "Plus qu'un jour" on the 8th seems to count 9 Jan as the day before; the source does not settle it (F-OW-437). That the fireworks happened is not confirmed; the letters do not describe the day (F-OW-402). Her father was well enough to attend the clothing (LT 70) (F-OW-402). "Plus qu'un jour" and "Lion chéri" are ABSENT from the 1911 edition (F-OW-437). Her entry on "9 April" (F-OW-400): the card gives 9 April 1888; the clothing was 10 Jan 1889.
- New to English readers: yes.
- Scene potential: medium (the melon, champagne and "cockchafer").

- What happened: Thérèse lamented that her clothing was postponed by 24 hours. Gonzague reasoned with her that the date did not matter. On the same day, 7 Jan, she wrote to Agnès that she had been hurt that morning at Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul's and left with a heavy heart. The clothing took place on 10 Jan 1889. Further cards: In LT 76 (7 Jan 1889) Thérèse noted that Jesus had changed the date: « Il a sans doute trouvé que la date du 9 était trop ravissante », which lets her say he "wants nothing delightful for her".

### E-P4-003 — 10 Jan 1889 onward — Chastity: the lily, the reliquary of virgins only
- What happened: As a child she said she was "ashamed of her body", consoled only that Jesus had taken one. In letters she praised the "white lily" (virginity) against the "yellow lily", the sisters' code for marriage. At her clothing she chose only relics of virgins for her reliquary, even setting aside saints she loved more. She told Céline she never had temptations against chastity and always acted alone as if someone were present.
- Cards: F-EW-654
- Best details:

  - Relics of virgins only, "even setting aside saints she loved more" (F-EW-654).
- Evidence: Céline (heard, and saw the reliquary), PO 4. High.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes.
- Scene potential: low. It is character material.

- What happened: After her clothing, Thérèse was told to sit on her heels on the right side (Carmel custom). She always did so and never changed sides. When too tired she knelt instead. She was embarrassed when MT asked to learn "her method" and told her not to copy it.

### E-P4-005 — 10 Jan 1889 — Clothing day: white velvet, Alençon lace, and snow in the courtyard
- What happened: Her clothing came on 10 Jan 1889, later than the usual six months after entry; Laveille's footnote attributes the delay to Delatroëtte's continued opposition on grounds of her youth. Before it she made a retreat "in aridity" and wrote to Mère Agnès: "In my soul's intercourse with Jesus there is nothing, nothing but dryness and sleep!" At Louis's wish she wore a dress of white velvet edged with swansdown and trimmed with point d'Alençon lace, her long fair hair in natural curls, and white lilies as her only ornament. Louis waited at the enclosure door, moved to tears, "pressed me to his heart, saying: 'Ah, here is my little Queen'", gave her his arm and they entered the chapel together. "This day was his triumph, his last feast on earth." Céline told Louis she too would enter Carmel; he said: "Let us go together before the Blessed Sacrament to thank the Saviour… If I possessed anything better, I would hasten to offer it to Him." She had always wished nature to be white on her clothing day; the day was mild and grey with fine rain. The bishop intoned the Te Deum though a priest pointed out it was only for professions. Going back into the cloister she saw her "pink little Jesus" among flowers and then snow falling: the courtyard was white. "The whole town was astonished"; people found her "odd taste" for snow. The community spoke of "the little miracle". On 2 Jan 1889 she wrote to Mme Guérin thanking her for the wreath of lilies sent by her cousins. Further cards: On 2 Jan 1889 (LT 73, her 16th birthday) Thérèse wrote to Mme Guérin: her cousins' lilies for the clothing crown looked "just picked". She was happy to have "the enormous apple-sugar stick" to offer to "our Mother" and the big packet of gingerbread arrived: « j'ai été toute fière au réfectoire quand notre mère a dit à la Communauté qu'en l'honneur de mes 16 ans vous nous donniez ce régal ». The Mother and the community found the crown very beautiful. She prayed to be adorned inside as well as outside. On 6 Jan 1889 (LT 74), in her clothing retreat, she told Pauline that Jesus "riddles me with pin-pricks"; the little ball has little holes everywhere, which hurt more than one big one. With Jesus: "nothing, dryness! sleep!" But: « Mais les créatures !... Celles qui m'entourent sont bien bonnes, mais il y a je ne sais quoi qui me repousse !... » She is happy that Jesus sleeps: « il ne fait pas de frais pour me tenir conversation ». On her betrothal day she wants to "convert all the sinners on earth and save all the souls in purgatory"; "I know it is madness."
- Cards: F-LF-270, F-OW-086, F-OW-397, F-OW-398, F-OW-399
- Best details:
  - « le préau était blanc comme moi » (F-OW-086, Ms A 72r–72v): "the courtyard was white like me".
  - « Ah, here is my little Queen » (F-LF-270).
  - The dress used Alençon lace, the product of her mother's trade (F-LF-270).
  - Louis "had never been more handsome, more dignified" (F-OW-086).
  - « il y a je ne sais quoi qui me repousse !... » about the community (F-OW-398): an honest admission of difficulty in her first year.
  - The apple-sugar stick and gingerbread: "quite proud in the refectory" (F-OW-397).
  - « je vous EN PRIE, déchirez ces papiers après les avoir lus ! » (F-OW-399).
- Evidence: Ms A (own writing), Laveille (p.212–215, 451). High.
- Conflicts: Laveille says the weather was "extremely mild" that day; Ms A says mild and grey with fine rain (F-OW-086). No weather record was checked (F-LF-270). Snow: see M-LF-005 and M-OW-05. Louis's third attack came "a month after her reception" (LAV p.216). Also: 1911 "Lettre IIe (Janvier 1889)" to Mère Agnès merges LT 74 and LT 76. It removes the "repels me" line and replaces it with a neutral "Quelle différence avec celle des créatures!"; it also removes her pain at Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul's (LT 76), the postponed date of 9 January, the remark on "la lettre du Père" and her plea to tear up the papers (F-OW-399). "Sucre de pomme" is a Rouen sweet (F-OW-397); "sucre de pomme" and "pain d'épices" are ABSENT from 1911. Note the date: LT 76 is 7 Jan 1889, the day she was hurt at Sr Saint-Vincent's (F-EW-683) and the 24-hour postponement (F-EW-682).
- New to English readers: partial.
- Scene potential: high.

### E-P4-006 — Jan–Feb 1889 — "How well she looks" / "How tired you seem" / "Isn't she fat!"
- What happened: A few days after her clothing, one Sister told the prioress the novice looked well and would keep the Rule for years. Another came in and said she looked so tired that she might not keep it long. Thérèse, 16, learned the worth of human opinion. In a second version, Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul exclaimed near the kitchen: "what a picture of prosperity, how strong and fat this big girl is." Thérèse went away humiliated. Minutes later Sr Madeleine du Saint-Sacrement worried she was wasting away. Thérèse resolved to attach no importance to creatures' opinions.
- Cards: F-EW-680, F-EW-684
- Best details:
  - « Oh ! quelle figure de prospérité ! Est-elle forte, cette grande fille ! Est-elle grosse ! » (F-EW-684).
  - The two opposite remarks within minutes (F-EW-680, F-EW-684).
  - One biographer frames it as the orphan's resentment of the "little bourgeoise" (F-EW-684).
- Evidence: Thérèse's account (Ms A, told July 1897, DE / CJ), and the biographies of the two Sisters. High.
- Conflicts: Dating: "Feb 1889, when M. Martin entered the Bon Sauveur" (F-EW-684, Madeleine biography) vs "25 juillet" (one biography, F-EW-684) vs "a few days after her clothing" (F-EW-680). The place is the prioress's room (F-EW-680) or near the kitchen (F-EW-684).
- New to English readers: partial.
- Scene potential: medium. Funny and human.

### E-P4-007 — 23 Jan – 4 Apr 1889 — Letters to Céline at Caen: "not a Soldier but a General", "before dying by the sword, let us die by pin-pricks"
- What happened: With their father's illness weighing on the family, Thérèse told Céline (LT 81, 23–25 Jan 1889) that Jesus fell three times on the way to Calvary, so Céline may fall "100 times". She was "almost jealous" of Céline's trial. « Souffrir et être méprisé ! quelle amertume mais quelle gloire ! Voilà la devise du Lys-immortelle !... Tu n'es pas Soldat mais Général... » "What does it matter that paltry men cut the moulds growing on this mud?" On 28 Feb 1889 (LT 82): « Est-il possible que ce soit à Caen que je t'écrive ?... Je me demande si je rêve ou si je suis éveillée... » Jesus was giving Céline her "first novitiate", a novitiate "of sorrow". « ne trouves-tu pas que le malheur qui le frappe est tout à fait le complément de sa belle vie ?... » "It is Martyrdom beginning." On 5 Mar 1889 (LT 83) she thanked Jesus for placing "a lily near our dear father", a lily "that would rather die than abandon the glorious field": Céline staying at Caen. "Now we have nothing more to hope for on earth, nothing but suffering." At the end: « Pauvre Léonie, je l'aime bien aussi, elle est plus malheureuse que nous, Jésus lui a moins donné. » She adds: "Laugh in your turn at your poor Thérèse, who reaches her subject at the end of her letter." On 12 Mar 1889 (LT 84), her aunt forbade her to say thank you; she found herself suddenly "so rich" without having earned anything: « au lieu de gagner de l'argent je ne sais que le perdre ». She smiled that thanks to her relatives "it is I who will supply the fish for the whole Community". The same evening (LT 85): « Jésus a fait des folies pour Céline... Que Céline fasse des folies pour Jésus... L'amour ne se paie que par l'amour ». On 15 Mar 1889 (LT 86): Céline had written that she was Thérèse's "shadow"; Thérèse answered that Céline must be "the little shadow of Jesus": « Avant de mourir par le glaive, mourons à coup d'épingles ». On 4 Apr 1889 (LT 87): « Ta lettre a mis une grande tristesse dans mon âme !... Pauvre petit Père !... » "It is a moment between two eternities." She found "the secret of suffering in peace": peace is not felt joy; it is enough to will what Jesus wills. She copies Psalm 136 (the harps on the willows) and ends: "Look at Jesus in his Face... There you will see how he loves us."
- Cards: F-OW-404, F-OW-405, F-OW-406, F-OW-407, F-OW-408, F-OW-409, F-OW-410, F-OW-411
- Best details:
  - « Tu n'es pas Soldat mais Général... » (F-OW-404).
  - « Est-il possible que ce soit à Caen que je t'écrive ?... » (F-OW-405): the only letter to name Caen as the father's place.
  - « Avant de mourir par le glaive, mourons à coup d'épingles » (F-OW-410).
  - « Pauvre Léonie... Jésus lui a moins donné » — a frank, somewhat condescending judgement (F-OW-407).
  - « au lieu de gagner de l'argent je ne sais que le perdre » (F-OW-408).
  - « Jésus a fait des folies pour Céline... » (F-OW-409).
  - The Holy Face devotion grows exactly during the father's confinement from Jan 1889 (F-OW-411).
- Evidence: Thérèse's own letters LT 81–87 (Carmel transcriptions). High.
- Conflicts: LT 82 does not name the asylum (Bon-Sauveur) nor describe his illness; see Gaps (F-OW-405). 1911 prints LT 82 as "Lettre IVe, 28 février 1889": it removes "Est-il possible…à Caen", the "complement" sentence, changes "bien aimé de Jésus" to "bien aimé de Dieu" and adds "Quelles délices d'être humiliées avec lui! L'humiliation est la seule voie qui fait les saints, je le sais" (not in LT 82 as transcribed; may come from another letter, not checked) (F-OW-406). 1911 merges part of LT 83 into "Lettre IIIe (Janvier 1889)", changes "Les chérubins" to "Les Séraphins", and the Léonie sentence is ABSENT (F-OW-407). 1911 "Lettre Ve (12 mars 1889)" cuts the "folies pour Jésus" passage (F-OW-409). 1911 moves the "peace" passage of LT 87 into "Lettre Ve" and cuts the opening about the father (F-OW-411). The date of LT 81 is uncertain. LT 81 implies people were speaking against the family ("chétifs hommes", "méprisé") but gives no facts (F-OW-404). The amount and purpose of the aunt's gift are not stated (F-OW-408). "Le rêve de ma Céline" is not explained (F-OW-410). The letter does not say what news Céline sent (F-OW-411).
- New to English readers: yes for most; partial for the first lines of LT 81 and LT 82 (in Taylor).
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P4-009 — 12 Feb 1889 onward — Louis Martin enters the mental home; the daughters are hurt by careless talk
- What happened: Louis Martin's paralysis grew worse shortly before Thérèse's clothing. Then cerebral troubles followed, and he entered a special mental hospital on 12 February 1889 (the Bon Sauveur at Caen is not named by Céline). Céline calls it a five-year "humiliation". The daughters had for him "not only love but a cult". Thérèse kept the date on a holy picture. Tactless people blamed the daughters' entry into Carmel, above all the youngest's, in the parlour and at recreation. The prioress herself spoke at recreation about asylum regimes and straitjackets in front of them. Further cards: On Tuesday 12 Feb 1889 Louis was taken to the Bon-Sauveur asylum at Caen, at his family's request, so they could take him out at will. Céline wrote to Pauline Romet on 18 Feb 1889: "the greatest of trials"; "in his imagination he saw terrible things, carnage, battles; he heard the cannon and the drum". An attempted theft in the town confirmed his fears; he wanted to carry his revolver "in case of danger", saying he "would not wish to harm even a cat". She wrote: "perhaps we should have waited and tried, before acting, every means to take it from him". The asylum doctor said "paralysie générale, tout le système nerveux est attaqué". In town he was called "le saint Patriarche". The admission certificate was signed by Dr Notta. Louis was patient no. 14,449, in the "Saint-Joseph" section with about 500 men judged calm or semi-calm; the asylum held about 1,700. The register reads "Manifestations délirantes avec détention d'arme". On 8 Jun 1889 lawyers came to the Bon-Sauveur to have him sign the papers of his legal "interdiction", telling him it was at his children's request. He wept, said his children were abandoning him, and signed. Céline calls this the sharpest wound. Céline and Léonie lodged with the Sisters of Saint Vincent de Paul in Caen until 14 May 1889; the Guérins lived at "La Maison Sauvage" (25 Mar–7 Jun 1889), then Les Buissonnets, then La Musse in July; in autumn 1889 all moved to 19 rue Paul-Banaston. After that the sisters visited Caen every week "without fail". At her first visit Louis had good days; when doctors said they would cure him, he said he did not wish it: the trial was a mercy, to expiate his pride. He returned to Lisieux on 10 May 1892 after more than three years. Mme Guérin: "his poor legs can no longer carry him. He had to be carried into the carriage. He wept all the time." He was taken to Carmel on 12 May 1892 and knew everyone; at parting he raised his eyes and said "In heaven". The family placed a marble ex-voto under the Holy Face in the Carmel chapel: "Blessed be the name of the Lord". ### E-P4-012 — 1889–1890 and c. 1892–1895 — Thérèse's letters to Marie Guérin; the grille joke
- What happened: Piat says the correspondence began in banalities and ended in an intense dialogue in which Thérèse's mastery of soul guidance became evident. In May 1889 the Guérins, with Léonie and Céline, visited the Paris Exposition. Marie, lost in the crowd, saw evil everywhere and dared not receive Communion; she wrote that Paris was "a real hell". Thérèse's reply of 30 May 1889 (LT 92): "You have not committed the shadow of a sin. You must despise all these temptations… When the devil has succeeded in keeping a soul from Communion he has won everything… and Jesus weeps." Piat says Pius X later expressed wonder at its eucharistic doctrine. LT 93 (14 Jul 1889): thorns can save a soul. Marie's reply of 23 Jul 1889 shows she had taken the religious name "Marie du Saint-Sacrement" and made a monastic "cell" at La Musse. LT 109 (27–29 Jul 1890): "I know no other means to perfection but love." Another letter (c. 1893–95) compares Marie to a village girl asked in marriage by a powerful king who dares not accept: "if you are nothing, do you forget that Jesus is all?" Thérèse said she was "weak, very weak" and knew only one means to perfection: love. One day when Marie complained of missing Céline, who was on pilgrimage to Paray-le-Monial, Thérèse gently reproached her for lack of detachment. Marie protested and called her "heartless". Thérèse, who "mimed to perfection", put on her most offended look and shut the grille in her face, then reopened it a moment later, laughing aloud at her visitor's stunned face. Further cards: LT 92 (30 May 1889): Marie Guérin, tormented by scruples, had stopped going to Communion on Ascension day and the last day of the month of Mary. Thérèse: « Vous n'avez pas commis l'ombre d'un péché ». « Elle aussi a passé par le martyre du scrupule mais Jésus lui a fait la grâce de communier quand même, alors même qu'elle croyait avoir fait de grands péchés [...] Ce qui offense Jésus, ce qui le blesse au cœur c'est le manque de confiance !... » "Receive Communion often, very often... that is the only remedy." She dreamed that night of trying to console Marie, in vain. She suggested a candle at Notre-Dame-des-Victoires and that Marie "make up" the two missed Communions. LT 109 (27–29 Jul 1890): the village girl; « je ne connais pas d'autre moyen pour arriver à la perfection que «L'Amour»... Aimer. » « Jésus est malade et il faut remarquer que la maladie de l'amour ne se guérit que par l'amour !... » She said Jesus "consents to lodge in a dirty, dark little hovel". « ta petite Thérèse ... est faible et bien faible ». ### E-P4-017 — c. 1889–1890 — Leading the crabby lay sister (Sr Saint-Pierre) to the refectory

### E-P4-078 — 1893–1897 — Gonzague: esteem, jealousy, and "her little hunting dog"
- What happened: The memoir admits Gonzague's jealousy was not aimed at Thérèse. She showed her much trust, gave her part of her authority over the novices, made her a confidante at the end of her life, and praised her to family, retreat preachers and missionaries, for example in a letter to Père Roulland dated 11 November 1897. Pauline adds: "and she was sincere." But Gonzague would share authority with no one. Thérèse often stirred her jealousy and had to hide constantly to do her work. No permission given in a good moment could be relied on: her friendly face could turn dark within the hour. Gonzague used Thérèse in the novitiate and called her "her little hunting dog". When Thérèse's influence became too real, or her own moods troubled her, she took offence and treated her harshly. MT says Gonzague told her several times that she would pick Thérèse as prioress without hesitation: "She is perfect in everything; her only fault is having her three sisters." Gonzague had worked to obtain the bishop's leave for the four sisters to enter, then was the first to resent them. Thérèse, young and a novice, threatened her less, so Gonzague admired her maturity. Lemonnier concludes from his talks that Gonzague had "some opposition" to the Martin family and especially to Mère Agnès, but deep esteem for Thérèse's virtue. Further cards: The novices called Gonzague "the wolf" (le loup); MT sometimes used it. When Gonzague was prioress again (from March 1896), Thérèse always rebuked it: it was acceptable when Gonzague was not prioress, but now she held "the sacrament of authority". MT says the community was "in disarray" because of Gonzague's "regrettable government". Thérèse kept the Rule anyway, and told MT to act as if the perfection of the Order depended on her. Disputes were "sometimes very stormy"; when things became too poisoned it was Thérèse who restored peace with tact. TSA speaks of "the sad government" of Gonzague, "gatherings" of gossiping nuns in the cloisters, and names her as "the cause of this disorder". Thérèse forbade her novices any criticism of the prioress. Thérèse suffered to see Gonzague blind to her faults and tried "all means" to make her see the truth, though she loved "this unhappy soul".

### E-P4-082 — 1893–1897 — How she treated the novices: firm, funny, and never a favourite
- What happened: Thérèse told Pauline, joking, that she had to take some novices "by the skin" and others "by the tips of the wings". She corrected without fear, asked no questions to satisfy curiosity, and did not try to win their hearts. She was very kind but very firm and "let absolutely nothing pass". She could not bear importance given to childish sufferings. She asked God that her novices never love her humanly, and Céline says the prayer was answered. She loved all novices without self-seeking and showed preference for none. Her novices were difficult: one wild and closed, one unintelligent and without a vocation for Carmel, one so hard to form she stayed only thanks to Thérèse. Some older nuns came to her secretly for advice, "like Nicodemus". A novice told her to use gentleness, not force; she answered with the image of a surgeon and a screaming child. After a humiliating talk with a novice, her face was flushed. She told Pauline she was happy: she had thought of Shimei cursing David and that God had told the sister to say it. She would not let novices criticise sermons or priests. Further cards: Ms C: Mère Marie de Gonzague told her "Feed my lambs"; Thérèse said she was "too little" and asked the prioress to keep the novices herself; the prioress kept them but sent Thérèse to guide them. ### E-P4-083 — 1893–1897 — Poems and "Récréations pieuses"; never a copy, composed at work
- What happened: Besides her life and letters, she wrote pious poems, some to express her own feelings, others at a sister's request, for a profession or anniversary, and "Récréations pieuses", short plays for the community's private feasts. She spent her free periods and Sundays writing poems for sisters and never refused one. She never copied them for herself, though she wanted a copy. She composed the poems while working during the day and waited for the free evening time to note them. The sisters learned this only at the end. Céline said she could easily have got permission to write by day; Thérèse said she had carefully avoided permissions that would make religious life easy. ### E-P4-086 — c. 1893–1896 — Retreats with Marthe and with MJ: an hour a day, shared penances, the Sunday list
- What happened: During her annual retreats, when nuns love complete solitude, Thérèse let the lay novice Sr Marthe ask the prioress to make her retreat with her. She spent a full hour each day with this "poor little unintelligent sister". To encourage her to do certain humiliating penances in the refectory, she did them with her. MJ also asked to make her retreat with Thérèse. For three years Thérèse agreed, letting the date of her own profession retreat pass to wait for MJ; MJ learned later this was a great sacrifice. To encourage MJ, Thérèse made small sacrifices with her, marked daily; on Sundays they laid the list at the feet of the Blessed Virgin. Thérèse needed no such devices herself. She wrote to Céline on 23 July 1893 that she had to keep a "rosary of practices" out of charity and was "caught in nets I do not like". This was to spur on the lay-sister novice companion, who could believe she was the one stimulating Thérèse. Further cards: Céline testified (Summarium 1919 p. 373 §951, via Laveille) that to help a lay-sister novice, Thérèse pretended she herself needed a complicated system of counted "practice beads" though she disliked such methods; she wrote to Céline (23 July 1893): "I am obliged to have 'practice beads' … as though entangled in the meshes of a net". ### E-P4-101 — 1893–1897 — What Lemonnier, Youf and Faucon said of her teaching
- What happened: Lemonnier preached the annual retreats at Lisieux Carmel in 1893, 1894 and 1895 and heard Thérèse in confession and direction. He also heard Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Mère Agnès, and, outside, Céline and Léonie. He spoke daily with the chaplain. Thérèse told him she was novice mistress only as an auxiliary; he thought it was because of her youth. Novices told him they trusted her direction. Superiors sent sisters in trouble to her. In life she was seen as a privileged, very fervent model nun, but Lemonnier would not say she was then regarded as "a saint" worthy of the altars. Her fame came partly from talks with the Carmelites but mostly from Story of a Soul. Abbé Youf told him the clarity, depth and theological soundness of her teaching were extraordinary and humanly inexplicable in a young nun with no special study. Youf said she did much good by her virtue "joined to a habitual liveliness and good humour". Faucon heard nuns, especially her novices, say she enlightened them, removed doubts, seemed to read their souls and "had an answer for everything". They missed her greatly after her death. Further cards: Céline says Mother Hermance passed her little notes for Thérèse during her illness. The chaplain Abbé Youf valued and trusted her. Canon Delatroëtte seemed to esteem her greatly. The sacristan-gardener recognised her under the veil by her grave bearing. Abbé Faucon, replacing the sick chaplain, heard her confession shortly before her death and came out very moved, saying she was "confirmed in grace". Dr de Cornière and the turn sisters, who watched her at Mass on Sundays, were edified by her patience and heavenly smile.

### E-P4-102 — 1893–1897 — The little way taught in daily direction: sayings to Céline, Marie-Madeleine and Marthe
- What happened: Thérèse told the novices: recognise your faults, but see God's gifts too. She said whatever might happen she would tell them the truth; she preferred anything to leaving a soul in ignorance. ### E-P4-132 — 1894–1897 — MT: "her life never left the ordinary"; the secret of reading souls
- What happened: MT says Thérèse had none of the miracles, ecstasies and gifts usually admired in saints. Her life "never left the ordinary", which is her special mark and makes her imitable by all. God made her feel he wanted it so, to make her a model for souls in the dark night of faith. MT often felt Thérèse read her soul. Asked how, Thérèse said in PA: "I do not have that gift at all, but here is my secret": she never corrected without first asking the Blessed Virgin what would do the novice most good. She was often surprised at what she said without reflecting. MT wanted to find some imperfection in her to excuse herself and never did; what is said of Thérèse now, even by her sisters, is below what she saw. MT knew only the poems and short plays for community feasts; she did not know Thérèse was writing her story. When Thérèse refused to see her at a bad time, MT sulked all day. In the evening, as she prepared to reproach her, she was seized by a feeling that made her apologise; she understood she was dealing with "a saint particularly loved by God".
- Cards: F-EW-944, F-EW-945, F-EW-946, F-EW-947, F-EW-948
- Best details:
  - « sa vie, ici-bas, ne sortit pas de l'ordinaire; c'est là son cachet particulier qui la rend imitable et accessible à tous » (F-EW-944; the editors praise these pages as among the best of the Process).
  - « Je n'ai pas du tout ce don, mais voici mon secret » (F-EW-945).
  - Thérèse refused to see novices at inconvenient times (F-EW-948).
- Evidence: MT (PO 17, PA 21). High; F-EW-946 medium (partisan claim); F-EW-948 medium (subjective).
- Conflicts: MJ and MM also felt "read" (F-EW-2271, F-EW-763). MM tried to find a fault from a more hostile start (F-EW-759).
- New to English readers: yes.
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P4-134 — 1894–1938 (later) — Marie Guérin's letters and the Guérin family after Thérèse
- What happened: Piat says seventy-three letters from Marie to her cousin Céline Maudelonde survive, from 1894 to 24 Nov 1904. The replies give advice on sanctifying duties, using illness and trial. Marie's advice echoes Thérèse: "Do everything out of love"; "A pin-prick is often more painful than a lance thrust"; "Above all do not let the devil tighten your heart". Dr La Néele died without children on the feast of St Joseph, 19 Mar 1916. Jeanne La Néele died on 25 Apr 1938 under the roof of her adopted daughter at Nogent-le-Rotrou. The Guérin family monument at Lisieux bears the inscription "O Crux, Ave, Spes unica". Isidore (Brother Elie of the Blessed Sacrament) lies next to the Carmelite graves, where Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie, exhumed in 1923, rests in the vault once occupied by Thérèse. A granite cross marks the grave where in 1894 Isidore had gathered, beside Louis Martin's coffin, that of his wife and their four children who died young.
- Cards: F-EW-2646, F-EW-2662
- Best details:
  - The vault once occupied by Thérèse is now Marie de l'Eucharistie's (F-EW-2662).
- Evidence: Piat (1967). Medium-high.
- Conflicts: Dr La Néele's death 19 Mar 1916 confirmed; the birth-year conflict with the editors' 1855–1916 remains. Jeanne's death is 24 Apr 1938 in F-EW-695 but 25 Apr 1938 in F-EW-2662. The 1894 regrouping is the extractor's reading of "the coffin of his wife and their four children" as Zélie and the four Martin children who died young (F-EW-2662). Later than this period; kept for reference.
