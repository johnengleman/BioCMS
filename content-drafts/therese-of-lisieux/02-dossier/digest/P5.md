# Digest P5 — 1895–1896 (Manuscript A, the Act of Oblation, the plays, the missionary "brothers", the 1896 election, the first blood, the trial of faith, Manuscript B, the Taxil affair)

Source: `02-dossier/_by-period/P5.md` (331 cards, read in chunks). Events: 87 (E-P5-001 to E-P5-087), in time order. Cards that cover a whole span (1895–97) sit at the end of the nearest year. Every card of P5 appears in at least one event. Some cards support more than one event. The file also holds a few cards dated 1897 (Feb–Apr 1897); they are in E-P5-084 to E-P5-087.

## Period summary

### Turning points
1. Early 1895: Thérèse begins Manuscript A on Mère Agnès's order and hands it over on 20 Jan 1896 (E-P5-001, E-P5-032).
2. 9–11 Jun 1895: the Act of Oblation to Merciful Love, then the "wound of love" a few days later (E-P5-011, E-P5-015).
3. 21 Mar 1896: Gonzague is elected prioress on the seventh ballot. Thérèse stays a novice in status and keeps the novices without the title (E-P5-040, E-P5-002).
4. Night of 2–3 Apr 1896: the first coughing of blood. Easter 1896: the trial of faith begins (E-P5-041, E-P5-045).
5. 1896: Thérèse receives two missionary "brothers" (Bellière, Roulland) and writes Manuscript B, "my vocation is love", in Sept 1896 (E-P5-022, E-P5-047, E-P5-058).

### The five best scenes
1. E-P5-041 — the first coughing of blood: the lamp put out, Good Friday spent cleaning windows on bread and water.
2. E-P5-027 — the mussel shell for tears, the flick in the refectory, the cabbages upside down.
3. E-P5-032 — Ms A handed over kneeling, put down unopened by Pauline for two months.
4. E-P5-040 with E-P5-050 — Gonzague's seven ballots, and Thérèse's own "Legend of a little Lamb" written for her.
5. E-P5-060 — the linen-room nun who "had dark ideas", and the "poor clock that must be wound every quarter-hour".

Close runners-up: E-P5-005 (Joan at the stake), E-P5-066 (500 points of fire, the straw mattress), E-P5-072 (the top on Christmas night), E-P5-086 (her photo shown at Taxil's press conference), E-P5-049 (the lock of Roulland's hair, "a sin of envy").

### The five best "new to English readers" facts
1. Thérèse wrote Diana Vaughan into her own play (E-P5-048, 21 Jun 1896) as a real convert and "a new Joan of Arc". A photo of her as Joan was shown at Taxil's unmasking (E-P5-057, E-P5-086). Pauline and Céline both softened this (E-P5-020).
2. Pauline granted the Oblation "without enthusiasm", and Céline made the Act with her on 11 June 1895 (E-P5-011).
3. Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin's report of the bluntest wording of the trial: "I do not believe in eternal life" (E-P5-045, F-EW-1131), softened in TSA's 1915 deposition.
4. Gonzague forbade Thérèse to tell Pauline about Roulland. Thérèse barred her cell door and painted in the library with Pauline's brushes (E-P5-047).
5. In LT 182 (E-P5-036) the dead Martin siblings appear as "cherubs", one of them "the little thief who took my name". The 1911 edition cut the whole letter.

Other strong finds: the 1911 edition redated LT 180 and cut its humour (E-P5-021); Thérèse played Jesus for the lay sisters (E-P5-018); her father offered Jerusalem if she delayed entry (E-P5-068); a note of Gonzague's ("Let us be saints, but not put-on ones") (E-P5-016).

### People who matter in this period
- Mère Agnès (Pauline): prioress to 21 Mar 1896, orders Ms A, keeps quiet about it. Gonzague's rival.
- Mère Marie de Gonzague: re-elected 21 Mar 1896, novice mistress in title, harsh and caring by turns, jealous of the novices' trust.
- Céline (Sr Geneviève), Marie du Sacré-Cœur, Léonie: the three sisters who report most. Céline is also a novice and photographer.
- Marie de la Trinité (MT) and Marie Guérin (Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie): the two novices with the fullest testimony.
- Sr Marie de Saint-Joseph (linen room), Sr Saint-Vincent de Paul, Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin, Marie des Anges, Sr Saint-Stanislas: sisters who admire, oppose or serve her.
- Fr Lemonnier (theological check of the Act), Fr Godefroy Madelaine (trial of faith), Canon Maupas and Abbé Youf (superior and chaplain), Canon Delatroëtte (died 1895).
- Fr Adolphe Roulland and Abbé Maurice Bellière: the two missionary "brothers". Léo Taxil ("Diana Vaughan").
- Dr de Cornière and Dr La Néele: the doctors.

## Events

### E-P5-001 — Early 1895 (ordered), 20 Jan 1896 (handed over); Ms C ordered 2 June 1897 — Ordering and writing Manuscript A (winter 1895 – 20 Jan 1896)
- What happened: One winter evening early in 1895 Thérèse told childhood stories. At Sr Marie du Sacré-Cœur's prompting, Pauline, then prioress, ordered her to write her childhood memories for her sisters only. She wrote in her free time, in snatches, with no draft, and gave the notebook to Agnès on 20 January 1896. Agnès put it aside carelessly without reading it. Later the first part went to the house of M. Guérin. Ms C was ordered by Mère de Gonzague on 2 June 1897 and written in June 1897. After Thérèse's death Gonzague agreed to publish the texts only if altered so all seemed addressed to her; Marie du Sacré-Cœur restored the original in April 1910 and the 1914 edition restored the three-part distinction.
- Cards: F-EW-996, F-EW-1004, F-EW-999, F-EW-1011, F-EW-1013, F-EW-1032, F-EW-997, F-EW-993, F-EW-1017, F-EW-1076
- Best details:
  - First part written in two-sou notebooks "of the worst paper"; for the second part she was forced to space her lines in an imposed squared notebook; poems on old envelopes (F-EW-1011)
  - « qui le posa négligemment sans songer à le lire » (Agnès put the notebook down carelessly) (F-EW-999)
  - Marie says it was at her request that Agnès ordered it, "towards the end of 1895"; that in Sept 1896 Marie asked for the "little way", which became the end of the book (F-EW-1004)
  - Since the Carmel's foundation in 1838 no nun had written autobiographical notes for a circular; critics in other Carmels objected that so young a nun stated her views on perfection so firmly (F-EW-993)
  - Ms C written "at one go without erasures" amid constant interruptions; Thérèse stopped in early July 1897 (F-EW-996)
  - Before writing, c. 1895–96, she told MT privately of her prophetic childhood vision and of the Virgin's cure; the wording "statue disappeared" goes beyond her HA text (F-EW-1017)
  - She sang in her poems of heaven "simply what I want to believe" (F-EW-1076)
- Evidence: Mère Agnès (actor), Marie, Céline, MT, editors' notes; mostly high.
- Conflicts: Who asked: Agnès says she ordered it on Marie's prompting (F-EW-996); Marie claims the initiative (F-EW-1004); Céline says Thérèse foresaw publication for Ms C (F-EW-999, F-EW-1013). "End of 1895" (Marie) against handover of 20 Jan 1896. "No erasures" (Céline) is too strong. Céline says she wrote only for sisters and would not have written so simply if she had foreseen publication (F-EW-1013). The "sent to M. Guérin" date and reason are unknown (F-EW-997).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: high, because the manuscript's fragile origin (snatches, cheap paper, put down unread) is concrete.

### E-P5-002 — 1893–1895 (De Teil gives age 22; the usual date is 1893) — Charge of the novices without the title
- What happened: The prioress gave Thérèse charge of the novices without the title, when she was 22. After the re-election of 21 March 1896, Mère de Gonzague named no novice mistress and kept the office herself, helped as before by Thérèse. Thérèse kept this ill-defined role until her death. Gonzague read all novices' letters. Sr Cécile judges that among six novices of 1895 (average age 25) Thérèse truly made disciples only of Geneviève and above all Marie de la Trinité. After 21 March 1896, Thérèse began to gather the novices every day after Vespers from 2:30 to 3:00. She gave no formal lectures. She read or had them read parts of the Rule, the Constitutions or the "Papier d'exaction", explained, answered questions, and corrected faults. From March 1896 she did nearly all the work of novice mistress while Gonzague held the title, and she was herself still a novice in status. In Ms C, Thérèse writes that before she had charge of the novices she wished to tell a sister who did something irregular; now, when she sees an imperfect act, she sighs with relief.
- Cards: F-EW-990, F-EW-1081, F-EW-1039, F-EW-1016, F-EW-1014, F-EW-1022, F-EW-1061, F-EW-1184, F-EW-2432, F-EW-2609, F-OW-144
- Best details:
  - An old nun said disapprovingly that at "only twenty" she needed to direct herself before directing others (F-EW-990)
  - Novices trusted her because she showed no favouritism, though her novices included her sister Céline and her cousin Marie Guérin (F-EW-1016)
  - Sr Marie-Emmanuel told Céline she would want Thérèse as prioress "si elle avait plus de 22 ans" (F-EW-1014)
  - When Thérèse seemed to be or do something, Gonzague was hurt, humiliated her and got angry (F-EW-1081)
  - The 1893–96 period was informal "discreet" care, not a daily class (F-EW-1184 note)
  - Piat: Thérèse taught by small facts and remarks, "a mixture of simplicity and authority, familiarity and greatness" (F-EW-2609)
  - « Quel bonheur ! ce n'est pas une novice, je ne suis pas obligée de la reprendre. » (F-OW-144)
- Evidence: De Teil (official Articles), Mère Agnès, Céline, MT, Sr Cécile (modern judgment); medium-high. Also: Céline (saw, as novice), Fr Piat (modern); high. Also: Thérèse's own writing (Ms C 27v); high.
- Conflicts: Age and date: 1893 usually, but De Teil says age 22 (1895); another nun says "twenty" (F-EW-990). Gonzague's title: Agnès says Gonzague kept the office (F-EW-1081); Maupas believed Gonzague put Thérèse in charge (F-EW-1022). The start date: only after March 1896 for the daily meeting (Céline) against 1893 as the usual date for her charge of novices.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium, because it is a funny, honest line about correcting others.

### E-P5-004 — written Jan 1895 – Jan 1896 — Thérèse's own account in Ms A: why she wrote, dreams and her list of graces
- What happened: She wrote Ms A for Mère Agnès and first thought it would "distract my heart by occupying it with itself". Before writing she knelt before the statue of the Virgin. She writes "without worrying about style". She titled it "Springtime story of a little white flower written by herself". At the end of Ms A she listed the dates she counted as graces, from her birth (2 Jan 1873) to her offering to Love (9 Jun 1895).
- Cards: F-OW-209, F-OW-001, F-OW-210, F-OW-197

  - Her dreams: « j'attrape des papillons et des oiseaux comme jamais je n'en ai vus » (F-OW-210)

### E-P5-005 — 21 Jan 1895 (feast of Mère Agnès; date inferred, see conflicts) — Thérèse plays Joan of Arc at the stake and nearly burns
- What happened: On the prioress's feast, Thérèse played Joan of Arc at the stake in the community play. A careless act started a fire near her feet. Mère Agnès ordered her not to move while others put out the flames. She stayed calm and, as she said afterwards, offered her life to God. Carmel editors date RP 3, "Joan of Arc fulfilling her mission", to 21 Jan 1895, the feast of the prioress. Its subtitle is "The victories, the captivity, the martyrdom and the triumphs in Heaven of the Venerable Joan of France". Céline took the photographs in the sacristy courtyard between 21 Jan and 25 Mar 1895, with Thérèse still in costume.
- Cards: F-EW-1046, F-EW-1047, F-OW-567, F-LF-220
- Best details:
  - Flames "crackling at her feet"; she obeyed the order not to move and offered her life to God (F-EW-1047)
  - Told afterwards to the community that she had made that offering of her life (F-EW-1046, F-EW-1047)
  - The cast includes Joan, St Gabriel, St Michael, St Catherine, St Margaret, King Charles VII, La Trémouille, Regnault de Chartres, Massieu and "France"; the editors list fourteen tunes (F-OW-567)
  - The play opens at Chinon, with Charles VII "dressed simply like one of his favourites, without royal crown" (F-OW-567)
  - It is the longest of her plays (F-OW-567 note)
- Evidence: Céline, eyewitness (PO 4 and CS); high confidence. Thérèse's inner offering was heard later. Also: Carmel page on RP 3 (facts only), Laveille; high for date and cast.
- Conflicts: Neither card gives a date. The Jan 1895 date is the extractor's inference from the Joan-play chronology (F-EW-1047 note). The visiting saint in photo no. 14 is St Margaret (Carmel photos page) or St Catherine (other Carmel pages and Le Normand) (F-LF-220).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: high, because a vivid, dangerous moment with a clear act of obedience.

### E-P5-006 — 25 or 26 Feb 1895 (Forty Hours adoration) — "Vivre d'amour" composed, Feb 1895
- What happened: Thérèse composed the poem "Vivre d'amour" in Feb 1895, during adoration before the Blessed Sacrament. Céline says she wrote it in one go. Mère Agnès says she made 15 stanzas during the day and wrote them from memory in the 45-minute evening silence. Later, at the laundry, she asked Marie des Anges to have the sisters sing it. The Carmel editors date PN 17 to 26 Feb 1895. They say Thérèse conceived and memorised the poem in the three days before Ash Wednesday and wrote it down on the Tuesday evening. It has 15 stanzas and the tune "Il est à moi". Stanza 13 records the worldly reaction: "'Vivre d'Amour, quelle étrange folie!' me dit le monde". On 16 Jul 1897 Marie Guérin sang stanza 14 before her Communion.
- Cards: F-EW-1048, F-EW-1049, F-EW-1050, F-EW-1066, F-OW-568
- Best details:
  - Passing the Holy Face picture on the way from choir to refectory, she said a new stanza to it with love, and wept (F-EW-1048)
  - She asked Marie des Anges, who presided over the washing, to sing "Vivre d'amour" and to have the sisters present sing it (F-EW-1050)
  - Composed "in one go" (F-EW-1049, F-EW-1066)
  - « Mourir d'Amour, c'est un bien doux martyre / Et c'est celui que je voudrais souffrir » (stanza 14) (F-OW-568)
  - Stanza 12 compares her with Mary Magdalene, who breaks the jar and perfumes his Face (F-OW-568)
- Evidence: Mère Agnès (heard, told 5 Aug 1897), Céline (in the community), Marie des Anges (took part); high, except the date of the laundry scene (medium, inferred). Also: Carmel editors on PN 17 (facts only); high for the date given.
- Conflicts: Date: Céline's PO says 25 Feb 1895 (F-EW-1049); her Carmel notes say Tuesday 26 Feb 1895 (F-EW-1066). Marie des Anges says "after leaving the novitiate" (F-EW-1050), against witnesses who say Thérèse stayed in the novitiate all her life; Marie des Anges herself says she does not remember well. The editors: "conceived and memorised in three days, written down on the Tuesday evening". Mère Agnès: 15 stanzas made during the day and written from memory in the 45-minute evening silence (F-EW-1048). Céline: 25 Feb (PO) and 26 Feb (CS), "in one go" during adoration.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium, because of the small human moments (weeping before the Holy Face; the laundry song).

- What happened: In April 1895, in perfect health, Thérèse told Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin that she would die soon. She said it would be not in a few months but in two or three years. Saint-Augustin later called it a true prophecy: Thérèse died two years and five months later.
- Cards: F-EW-1053, F-EW-1025

  - Related: on a free day Thérèse, joyful, said she knew what her death would be. Told "you'll die of the chest", she said: "Oh, that's not it. What happiness — my blood will flow." There was much talk of massacres then (F-EW-1025)

### E-P5-011 — 9 Jun 1895 (Trinity Sunday); made with Céline on 11 Jun 1895 — The Act of Oblation to Merciful Love, 9–11 June 1895
- What happened: On Trinity Sunday, during Mass, Thérèse felt urged to offer herself as a victim to merciful Love, pained that God's love was scorned. After Mass she drew Céline along, silent and seeming beside herself, to find the prioress, Mère Agnès. She asked leave to offer herself, with Céline. Agnès, in a hurry, allowed it "without enthusiasm". Two days later, Tuesday 11 June, both knelt before the Virgin of the Smile and Thérèse pronounced the Act for both. She wrote the text and carried it always on her heart. Additional details from the early Life and Thérèse's own text. The thought came at Mass: "shall Thy Justice alone receive victims of holocaust? Has not Thy merciful Love itself need of them?" After thanksgiving she asked Mère Agnès, "her face aglow". Lemonnier thought two requests daring (the stigmata shining in her glorified body; Christ's presence kept between Communions) but did not cut them. The Act was found after her death in a Gospel book she carried near her heart. The text of the Act (Pri 6) is preserved on two leaves. She asks that God be "himself my holiness". She writes: "I cannot receive Holy Communion as often as I would like, but, Lord, are you not All-Powerful? Stay in me as in the tabernacle." She thanks God "for having made me pass through the crucible of suffering". She writes: "in the evening of this life I shall appear before you with empty hands... I want to clothe myself with your own Justice." She asks to become "Martyr of your Love".
- Cards: F-EW-1055, F-EW-1057, F-EW-1063, F-EW-1064, F-EW-1065, F-EW-1066, F-EW-1060, F-EW-1061, F-LF-221, F-OW-197, F-OW-569
- Best details:
  - Her face was lit up; she seemed "on fire with love" (F-EW-1057)
  - "J'accédai à son désir, mais sans enthousiasme" (Agnès, F-EW-1057)
  - Thérèse "seeming beside herself and not speaking", she led Céline to the prioress (F-EW-1064)
  - "Notre Mère était pressée, elle ne sembla pas trop comprendre ce dont il s'agissait et permit tout" (F-EW-1064)
  - Thérèse, a little embarrassed, stammered her request; Céline does not know if she said "victim" (F-EW-1066)
  - On 11 June they knelt before the Virgin of the Smile, then in the work room beside Thérèse's cell (F-EW-1066)
  - Signed text in the Process: "Marie-Françoise-Thérèse de l'Enfant Jésus et de la Sainte Face, rel. carm. ind." (F-EW-1063)
  - She said on her deathbed that she often repeated her act of consecration (F-EW-1063)

### E-P5-041 — Night of 2–3 April 1896 (Holy Thursday to Good Friday) — Holy Thursday night 1896: the first coughing of blood

### E-P5-042 — April 1896 – June 1897 — Health and care in 1896–97: treatments, neglect and endurance
- What happened: After the two haemoptyses of 1896, the doctors La Néele and de Cornière said there was nothing serious. They ordered frictions and "pointes de feu". ### E-P5-045 — Easter 1896 – Sept 1897 — The trial of faith (Easter 1896 – Sept 1897) and the Creed in blood
- What happened: From Easter 1896 Thérèse suffered a temptation against the existence of heaven. She told no one, for fear of passing it on. She let slip to Céline: "If you spent only five minutes in the trials I endure!" She spoke of it to confessors; one made it worse by calling her state very dangerous. On the advice of a wise director she copied the Creed and carried it always, and she wanted to write it with her blood. Mère Agnès says she wrote it in her blood at the end of the small Gospel she carried on her heart. She said she sang about heaven only "what I want to believe". Marie never saw the trial and learned of it from the manuscript after the death. Fr Godefroy Madelaine (Godefroy) says he was the confidant of her soul during the trial (retreat of 1896). He was struck by the peace in the upper part of her soul amid anguish. In the confessional nobody could have guessed the crisis. He says the trial lasted 18 months, that she "believed herself damned", and that only the prioress and the confessor knew. She told TSA, who was "strangely surprised": "I do not believe in eternal life; it seems to me that after this mortal life there is nothing." Céline heard her ask anxiously, "Is there a Heaven? Talk to me of Heaven." The chaplain, Abbé Youf, nearly upset her by saying her state was "very dangerous". Thérèse's own account in Ms C: during the Easter season 1896 "Jesus made me feel that there really are souls who do not have faith". Her soul was invaded "by the thickest darkness", and the thought of Heaven became "only a subject of combat and torment". She uses a parable of someone born in a land of fog who has heard of a sunny country; the darkness, "borrowing the voice of sinners", mocks her: "Go on, rejoice in death, which will give you not what you hope for but a night deeper still, the night of nothingness." She writes "I do not want to write more; I would be afraid of blaspheming." She says she has made more acts of faith in one year than in her whole life, and sits "at the table of sinners".
