# Digest P1 — Ancestors, parents and the Alençon years (to 1876)

Source: `_by-period/P1.md` (204 cards: 105 F-EW, 86 F-LF, 4 F-OM, 9 F-OW). Events: E-P1-001 to E-P1-102. Every card is cited in at least one event. Events 001–078 are in time order. Events 079–102 are background cards (the Lisieux Carmel, witnesses, later context) that carry a P1 date because of a birth year or an entry date.

## Period summary

### Turning points
1. 1851–1858: Zélie hears "Have Alençon lace made" (E-P1-014) and, in spring 1858, "This is the one I have prepared for you" (E-P1-017). They marry at midnight on 13 Jul 1858 (E-P1-018).
2. 1867–1870: four of nine children die (E-P1-027, E-P1-028, E-P1-029, E-P1-031). Zélie's answer to grief is in E-P1-039.
3. 1870–1871: Louis leaves watchmaking for the lace business (E-P1-036), Prussians are billeted in the house (E-P1-037), and the family moves to rue Saint-Blaise (E-P1-038).
4. 2–4 Jan 1873: Thérèse is born and baptized (E-P1-041, E-P1-042). She nearly dies, and a wet nurse at Semallé saves her (E-P1-044 to E-P1-050).
5. Dec 1876: Zélie is told she has a fibrous tumour and that surgery is too late (E-P1-076). This ends the Alençon childhood.

### The five best scenes
1. E-P1-061 — "Oh, how I wish you would die, my poor little mother!" and the rolling tantrums (Zélie's letter of 5 Dec 1875).
2. E-P1-044 and E-P1-045 — the coffin letter, then Zélie walking alone at dawn to Semallé.
3. E-P1-053 — the toddler found asleep on a chair beside the bed.
4. E-P1-063 — tied into bed with cords that Céline knots ("Maman, je suis toquée!").
5. E-P1-076 — "None; it is to please the patients", and Louis putting his fishing lines in the attic.
Also strong: E-P1-042 (baptism delay), E-P1-058 (sugar rings), E-P1-064 (the cellar line), E-P1-074 (the "safe in your arms" and "two little hens" scenes), E-P1-031 (starved baby seen by ten-year-old Marie), E-P1-027 (white roses on the small coffin), E-P1-037 (nine German soldiers in the house), E-P1-011 (turned away for lack of Latin), E-P1-082 ("For Carmel, never!").

### The five best "new to English readers" facts
1. Zélie's "coffin letter" about the aunt's vow to St Francis de Sales, and "Faudra-t-il encore perdre celle-là ?" (E-P1-044).
2. The tantrum passage of 5 Dec 1875 and "tied into her bed", cut from the edited Life (E-P1-061, E-P1-063; F-LF-063, F-OW-004, F-OW-005).
3. The baptism was delayed 36 hours for a 14-year-old godfather, and the delay tormented Zélie (E-P1-042).
4. Louis was turned away from the Great St Bernard for lack of Latin, and Félicité Baudouin paid the first costs of his shop (E-P1-011, E-P1-012, E-P1-015).
5. Zélie's own cancer history and diagnosis in her words (E-P1-024, E-P1-076), and the ten-year-old Marie who saw the baby Mélanie-Thérèse starving (E-P1-031).

# PART 1 — Events in time order

## 1A. Ancestors and the two families (before 1858)

### E-P1-001 — 1692–1777 — The Martins of Athis-de-l'Orne
- What happened: Laveille says the paternal ancestors were farmers named Martin at Athis-de-l'Orne (Domfront district) from the 16th century. The first sure record is the baptism of Jean Martin on 2 Apr 1692. Grandfather Pierre-François Martin was baptized there on 16 Apr 1777. The family lived near the church, then at a farm called "la Quentinière".
- Cards: F-LF-001
- Best details: the 1818 marriage record names Pierre-François as son of "the late John (Jean) Martin and Marie-Anne Bohard of Athis" (F-LF-001).
- Evidence: later Life (Laveille 1925) that relies on a local booklet (Abbé Madeline) that the researcher did not see. Confidence: medium.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low — a genealogy line only.

### E-P1-002 — 1799–1830 — Captain Pierre-François Martin, career soldier
- What happened: Thérèse's paternal grandfather enlisted in the 65th Infantry on 26 Aug 1799 and rose from corporal (1800) to captain (1815, confirmed 1816). He served in the Army of the Rhine, at Belle-Île and Brest, in Prussia and Poland (1807), in France (1814), in the Morbihan (1815) and in Spain (1823–24). He moved through several regiments and the Strasbourg staff (1828) and retired on 12 Dec 1830. Charles X made him Knight of the Order of St Louis on 20 Aug 1824.
- Cards: F-LF-002
- Best details: Laveille says he got the Cross of St Louis "at the age of forty-seven during the Spanish campaign" (F-LF-002). The Carmel page gives his postings as Bordeaux, Avignon (1826), Strasbourg (1828) (F-LF-002).
- Evidence: official (service record printed by Laveille, Appendix VI). Confidence: high for dates as printed.
- Conflicts: one campaign date is printed "1779–1801", surely an error for 1799–1801 (F-LF-002).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-003 — 7 Apr 1818 — The paternal grandparents marry at Lyon
- What happened: Captain Martin, of the 42nd line regiment, married Marie-Anne-Fanie Boureau, daughter of Nicolas Boureau and Marie Nay, in the church of Saint-Martin d'Ainay, Lyon, on 7 Apr 1818 (civil marriage 4 Apr). A colonel, the Count de Labesse, was a witness.
- Cards: F-LF-003
- Best details: the register spells the bride "Marie-Anne-Françoise"; Laveille corrects it to "Fanie". Her brother Jean-Prosper, aged 12½, died a prisoner of the Prussians in Silesia on 21 Sep 1813 (F-LF-003).
- Evidence: official (parish register copy certified 1926). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-004 — 6 Jul 1789 – 3 Sep 1868 — Isidore Guérin senior, soldier and gendarme (Zélie's father)
- What happened: Isidore Guérin was born at Saint-Martin-l'Aiguillon (Orne) on 6 Jul 1789. He joined the army on 6 Jun 1809, fought at Wagram and then in Spain (Oudinot division) from Vittoria to Toulouse. He joined the foot gendarmerie on 1 Oct 1816 and the mounted gendarmerie on 1 Jun 1823. He served at Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon from 23 Feb 1827 and left the service on 10 Sep 1844. He married Louise-Jeanne Macé at Pré-en-Pail (Mayenne) on 16 Sep 1828. He retired to Alençon and died there on 3 Sep 1868.
- Cards: F-LF-011
- Best details: as a child his family hid his uncle, a priest, from Revolutionary pursuers (F-LF-011). To add to his pension he worked as a carpenter, and his wife briefly ran a café in rue Saint-Blaise (F-LF-011).
- Evidence: official and early-life (Laveille, from the Guérin family archives and the Ministry of War). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: the register spells the bride's name "Massé" (F-LF-011).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-005 — 22 Aug 1823 – 14 Jan 1824 — Louis Martin is born at Bordeaux and baptized
- What happened: Louis-Joseph-Aloys-Stanislas Martin was born at 3 rue Servandoni, Bordeaux, third of five children, while his father was on the Spanish expedition. He was baptized privately at birth and solemnly on 28 Oct 1823 at Sainte-Eulalie by Abbé Martegoute, a prison chaplain. Godparents were Léonce de Lamothe and Ernestine Beyssac. Laveille says Archbishop d'Aviau du Bois de Sanzay attested the certificate on 14 Jan 1824 and "probably on this occasion" told the parents: "Rejoice, for this child is one of the predestined!"
- Cards: F-LF-004, F-LF-005, F-EW-001
- Best details: the archbishop's words are family tradition only (F-LF-005). Mère Agnès says Louis was born in Bordeaux on 22 Aug 1823 and was a jeweller (bijoutier) who had retired by Thérèse's birth (F-EW-001).
- Evidence: official (register copy) and family tradition told in 1925; eyewitness family knowledge (Agnès, 1910). Confidence: high for the birth; low for the archbishop's words.
- Conflicts: Laveille's text says 22 Aug, but his printed register says "the twenty-third" (F-LF-004). All other sources give 22 Aug. The Carmel says baptism was "in October". F-LF-005 notes that another source (FND p.13) wrongly says the archbishop "was to baptise" Louis; he only attested the certificate.
- New to English readers: partial (the archbishop's words, the address and the godparents are new).
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-006 — before 1853 — All of Louis's brothers and sisters die young
- What happened: Louis's only brother Pierre, meant for the navy, was lost in a shipwreck while young. His elder sister Marie married and died in 1846 aged 26. His younger sister Fanny died in 1853 aged 27 after marrying. His youngest sister Sophie, his godchild and favourite, died aged nine. By 1853 Louis was the only surviving child.
- Cards: F-LF-006
- Best details: Pierre was 4 and Marie 3 when Louis was born; Anne-Fannie was born at Avignon in 1826 (F-LF-006).
- Evidence: early-life (Laveille, footnote) and Carmel essay. Confidence: medium.
- Conflicts: no source gives the date of Pierre's shipwreck.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-007 — 23 Dec 1831 — Zélie Guérin is born
- What happened: Azélie-Marie Guérin was born on 23 Dec 1831 and baptized the next day at Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon. Her parents were Isidore Guérin, gendarme, and Louise Macé. Her godfather was François-Michel Septier, brigadier of gendarmes, and her godmother was Marie Berrier, a cousin. She signed official papers "Azélie" but all her letters "Zélie". Her parents had no fortune.
- Cards: F-LF-012, F-EW-001, F-EW-023, F-EW-044
- Best details: the signature "Azélie" in official papers and "Zélie" in letters (F-LF-012).
- Evidence: official (baptism register copy, certified 1915) and witness statements of Pauline, Marie and Céline. Confidence: high for the year and the day in December; medium for the place.
- Conflicts: place of birth is Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon (register; F-LF-012, F-EW-023 "Saint-Denis (Orne)") or Gandelain near Alençon (F-EW-001, F-EW-044, Carmel short biography). Both may be true: born at Gandelain, baptized at Saint-Denis. Month of birth: F-EW-001 (Pauline, 1910) says 23 Nov 1831; F-EW-044 (Céline) and the register say 23 Dec.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-008 — 2 Jan 1841 – 11 Sep 1866 — Isidore Guérin junior: spoiled youngest, Paris student, pharmacist at Lisieux
- What happened: Zélie's brother Isidore was born on 2 Jan 1841 at the barracks of Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon. He grew up at Alençon, the spoiled youngest of three, ten years younger than his sisters Élise and Zélie. He left the medical career his family wanted for chemistry: training at Alençon, several years at the School of Pharmacy in Paris, a residency at Bicêtre, and a first-class pharmacist diploma in May 1866. In summer 1866 he took over the pharmacy of Pierre Fournet at Lisieux, and on 11 Sep 1866 he married Fournet's second daughter, Céline.
- Cards: F-EW-2581, F-LF-245
- Best details: Piat calls him a "handsome, turbulent young man" whose faith declined in the Latin Quarter, and reports a family whisper of a fight in which he barely escaped a sword thrust (F-EW-2581). His sister Élise told him, with amused indignation, that he "drank iniquity like water" (F-EW-2581). The Carmel study says he attended spiritualist séances in Paris (F-LF-245).
- Evidence: later Life and scholarship (Piat 1967, from family papers; Carmel study by Br Marc Fortin). Confidence: medium-high.
- Conflicts: none on this period.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — a wayward brother, a warning letter (see the 1 Jan 1863 event).

### E-P1-009 — 1 Jan 1863 — Zélie warns Isidore about Paris
- What happened: In a letter of 1 Jan 1863, Zélie warned Isidore, then a student in Paris, about its pleasures. She said her husband knew Paris and "has told me of some of his own experiences". As a New Year's gift she asked him to visit Notre-Dame-des-Victoires daily to say a Hail Mary.
- Cards: F-LF-019
- Best details: "If you but knew through what trials he has passed!" (Laveille's English, about Louis; F-LF-019). Laveille adds that Isidore became "one of the most practical and devoted Catholics of his time" (F-LF-019).
- Evidence: own writing (Zélie, CF 1). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — Zélie as a firm big sister.

### E-P1-010 — 1842 – c.1850 — Louis learns watchmaking at Rennes, Strasbourg and Paris
- What happened: Louis began watchmaking in 1842 at Rennes with his father's cousin Louis Bohard. In Sept 1843 he moved to Strasbourg and lived and worked with the Mathey family, friends of his father; he studied the mechanism of the cathedral clock. He finished in Paris, lodging with his maternal grandmother. He learned German at Strasbourg, drew well, and copied favourite literary passages into notebooks.
- Cards: F-LF-007
- Best details: his mother wrote to him at Rennes on 25 Aug 1842: "you are the dream of my nights" and "Remain always humble, dear son" (F-LF-007). In a letter of 1883 he recalled a prank trip from Paris to Strasbourg "some twenty-five years ago" to surprise "l'ami Aimé Mathey", pretending to look at a watch in his friend's shop window; he also gave the friend's baby daughter a silver rattle and regretted that Mathey was not a practising Catholic (F-LF-007).
- Evidence: scholarship (Carmel essay) and early-life (Laveille); Louis's own letter, 1883. Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — the prank at the shop window shows a playful young Louis.

### E-P1-011 — Sept 1843 (or c.1845) — Louis is turned away from the Great St Bernard hospice
- What happened: Louis, about twenty, went up to the hospice of the Augustinian canons at the Great St Bernard, partly on foot and partly by coach. The prior asked if he had finished his Latin. He had not, so the prior told him to go home, finish his humanities and come back. He studied Latin (and, says the Carmel, Greek) at Alençon under the curé of Saint-Léonard for over a year, then fell ill and gave it up.
- Cards: F-LF-008
- Best details: the prior's question, "Have you finished your Latin studies, my son?" (Laveille's English; F-LF-008).
- Evidence: early-life and scholarship. Confidence: medium — the dialogue is Laveille's reconstruction; the refusal for lack of Latin is in both sources.
- Conflicts: Laveille gives one visit in Sept 1843. The Carmel gives a first visit in Sept 1843 on the way to Strasbourg and a second visit, with intent to enter, about two years later (F-LF-008).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: high — a young man is refused by a mountain monastery because of Latin.

### E-P1-012 — 1850 — Louis becomes a master watchmaker at Alençon
- What happened: Louis returned to Alençon in 1850 and bought a watchmaking business. He opened his workshop in rue du Pont-Neuf (parish of Saint-Pierre-de-Montsort). His parents came to live with him. He later added a jewellery shop. He never opened on Sunday.
- Cards: F-LF-009, F-EW-019
- Best details: he kept coins in a dried orange peel on his workbench (F-EW-019). Mlle Félicité Baudouin, an elderly devout lady, paid the first costs of setting up his shop (F-EW-019, F-EW-018).
- Evidence: scholarship (Carmel) and eyewitness family knowledge (Marie, cahier 1909). Confidence: high for the date; medium for the Baudouin loan (family memory, "not documented elsewhere in these files").
- Conflicts: none on the date. Laveille says he lived at rue du Pont-Neuf until his 35th year (F-LF-009).
- New to English readers: partial; yes for the Baudouin loan.
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-013 — c.1850 — The Sisters of Charity refuse Zélie
- What happened: About 1850 Zélie asked to enter the Sisters of St Vincent de Paul who ran the Hôtel-Dieu of Alençon, to serve the sick and poor. The superior told her at once that God did not want her there. The Carmel suggests her poor health was the reason.
- Cards: F-LF-015
- Best details: she had been a pupil of the Dames de l'Adoration ("Picpus"), Alençon, from age 13 (F-LF-015).
- Evidence: early-life and scholarship. Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — two refused vocations (Louis and Zélie) lead to one marriage.

### E-P1-014 — 8 Dec 1851 – 1863 — Zélie's inner voice "Have Alençon lace made" and the start of the lace business
- What happened: On 8 Dec 1851 (Immaculate Conception), after a novena, Zélie heard an interior voice: "Fais faire du point d'Alençon". Marie's version: at 20–22 Zélie learned lace at a manufacturer's and left because her virtue was not safe there; on the feast, making her bed, she heard the words. The Carmel stresses "have it made", not "make it": she was to run a business. With her sister (the future Sr Marie-Dosithée) she began at once and found Paris houses to buy. Zélie opened her own "bureau" in 1853 at her parents' house, 42 rue Saint-Blaise. From 1856 she worked for the Paris house of Pigache. At the Alençon exhibition of 20 Jun 1858 Pigache's lace won a silver medal, and the jury credited "the intelligent direction of Mademoiselle Zélie Guérin". She went back to her own account in 1863, when Louis took over dealings with clients.
- Cards: F-EW-011, F-EW-012, F-LF-016, F-LF-018
- Best details: Zélie signed the first contract herself because her elder sister was absent, a sign, Marie says, since that sister later entered the Visitation (F-EW-011). Marie-Dosithée wrote on 10 May 1874: "our start in Alençon lace was made in a small way and in tears" (F-LF-018). Marie remembers Zélie working "ardently" at her lace while the maid took the children out (F-EW-012).
- Evidence: Marie's memory (heard from Zélie); Carmel essay that quotes documents; Laveille (family tradition from Zélie's account). Confidence: medium for the voice (later tradition); high for the business dates.
- Conflicts: date "c.1851–53" (Marie) against 8 Dec 1851 (Laveille, Carmel). Zélie's age: 20–22 (Marie) would put the learning c.1851–53 and she was 19–20 in Dec 1851. The "1863" in the short Carmel page is not a conflict: 1853 own bureau, 1856–63 for Pigache, 1863 own account again (F-LF-018). Marie's story that she left a manufacturer because her virtue was unsafe is not in the other cards.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: high — the voice on the feast day while she makes the bed.

### E-P1-015 — before 1858 — The Virgin statue: gift, garden bower, later "Virgin of the Smile"
- What happened: Louis had a large statue of the Virgin before his marriage. Marie says it was given by Mlle Félicité Baudouin, an old lady devoted to him who also paid the first costs of his shop. It stood in the Pavillon garden in a leafy bower (Marie), or in a garden alley (Marie, PA). After Zélie's death Marie insisted it come to Lisieux. It stood in the sick child's room in 1883 and, from 8 Jul 1897, in the infirmary facing her bed.
- Cards: F-EW-017, F-EW-018
- Best details: Zélie refused to let Marie take it from her room: "while I live this Virgin will not leave here" (F-EW-018). In May Zélie paid a woman to buy big hawthorn branches so the Virgin was "lost in flowers" (F-EW-018). Zélie had received "very great favours" at its feet (F-EW-017).
- Evidence: eyewitness (Marie, PA 1915 and cahier 1909). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: origin differs: Louis placed it in a garden alley (F-EW-017); Baudouin gave it (F-EW-018); Laveille's note (LAV p.68 fn., see the Pavillon event) says a pious lady called "the saint". F-EW-017 links it to the 1883 statue; F-EW-018 says it was in Zélie's room.
- New to English readers: yes for the Baudouin gift; partial for the rest.
- Scene potential: medium — hawthorn branches around the statue in May.

### E-P1-016 — 1857 onward — Louis buys "le Pavillon"
- What happened: In 1857 Louis bought "le Pavillon", a small hexagonal tower with a little garden in the Sénatorerie quarter of Alençon. There he read, prayed and kept his fishing tackle, and he put a statue of the Virgin in its garden. Later he took Céline and Thérèse there; the garden had strawberries, currants, geraniums and roses.
- Cards: F-LF-010
- Best details: letters CF 169 (22 Oct 1876) and CF 214 (15 Jul 1877) tell of outings to pick strawberries and redcurrants (F-LF-010).
- Evidence: scholarship (Carmel) and early-life (Laveille). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: the sources do not say the Pavillon statue and the house statue are the same; treat as unconfirmed (F-LF-010; see the statue event).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P1-017 — Spring 1858 — Zélie hears "This is the one I have prepared for you"
- What happened: In spring 1858, crossing the Saint-Léonard bridge in Alençon, Zélie (26) passed Louis (35), whom she did not know. She heard inwardly: "C'est celui-là que j'ai préparé pour toi". Louis's mother had wanted him to marry.
- Cards: F-LF-020
- Best details: the exact words and the bridge (F-LF-020).
- Evidence: later Life (Laveille) and Carmel; family tradition, not a dated document. Confidence: medium.
- Conflicts: none. The sources do not say how the two were introduced.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: high — a chance meeting on a bridge with an inner voice.

### E-P1-018 — 12–13 Jul 1858 — The midnight wedding
- What happened: Louis Martin, watchmaker, and Zélie Guérin, "manufacturer of Point d'Alençon", married at Notre-Dame, Alençon, on Tuesday 13 Jul 1858. The civil marriage was on the evening of 12 Jul. The church marriage took place at midnight "without any parade", with only the Alençon family and witnesses. The dean of Saint-Léonard, Abbé F. Hurel, gave the consent; Abbé Jamot was curé of Notre-Dame.
- Cards: F-LF-022
- Best details: the signatures include "F. Martin" (Louis's mother Fanie) and "Louise Guérin" (Zélie's mother) (F-LF-022).
- Evidence: official (register copy, certified 1922) and Carmel. Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes (the midnight hour)
- Scene potential: high — a wedding at midnight.

### E-P1-019 — 1858–1859 — The first months: brother and sister
- What happened: According to family records, Louis proposed on the wedding night that they live as brother and sister, and Zélie agreed. After about a year they lived as an ordinary married couple. Laveille says this came after he learned it went against what she felt was God's plan for her; the Carmel says after "a better understanding of Christian marriage".
- Cards: F-LF-023
- Best details: none beyond the story itself.
- Evidence: family tradition and Carmel essay. Confidence: medium.
- Conflicts: the two sources differ on the reason for the change (F-LF-023). Modern works give a confessor's advice; not in these sources.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low — sensitive; use carefully.

### E-P1-020 — 1858–1894 — The parents' daily religious practice
- What happened: From 1858 both parents went daily to the 5:30 a.m. Mass (the "workers' Mass") and received Communion four or five times a week, which was not usual. Late in life Louis received daily. They kept the Lenten fast and abstinence despite Zélie's weak health, prayed together and read Lives of the saints. Louis joined the St Vincent de Paul Conference and the monthly Nocturnal Adoration. He kept Sunday strictly and closed his jewellery shop that day at a real financial loss.
- Cards: F-EW-016, F-LF-024
- Best details: reading the life of Mme Acarie, Zélie said "All her daughters Carmelites! Is it possible that a mother may have so great an honour?" (F-LF-024, citing Mère Agnès in the Apostolic Process).
- Evidence: eyewitness (Agnès, PO 1910); early-life. Confidence: high (F-EW-016), medium (F-LF-024).
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P1-021 — 1868–1877 — Louis in the eyes of his parish priest: grave outside, gentle inside
- What happened: Abbé Dumaine says Louis married rather late, and his piety made people think he had vowed celibacy. He was "grave and a little melancholy outwardly, he was gentle and cheerful in private". Louis loved fishing, often with his parish priest, and usually sent his catch to the Poor Clares of Alençon. He belonged to the monthly Nocturnal Adoration. Dumaine knew Zélie less: reserved, a homemaker, very pious.
- Cards: F-EW-034

### E-P1-032 — 1866–1873 — Isidore's pharmacy at Lisieux: money, fire, the Fournets

### E-P1-044 — Jan – Mar 1873 — The baby nearly dies; Zélie cannot nurse her

### E-P1-064 — 7 – 14 May 1876 — "I love her more than all the others"; the little ferret

### E-P1-065 — 14 – 21 May 1876 — Alms to an old man; the broken vase; the confessed wallpaper; bonbons
- What happened: On a Sunday walk (14 May 1876) the family met a poor old man. Zélie sent Thérèse to give him a small alms. On 21 May a box of bonbons came from Lisieux for the baptism of little Hélène Maudelonde; Thérèse jumped and clapped. When the family spoke of a rich landowner and the maid Louise, "who always envies the rich", sighed "If only I had that!", the little girl declared she much preferred the box of bonbons.

### E-P1-071 — c.1876–1877 — Small acts of a small child: rebuking the maid, "my Mass", glitter, the horse

### E-P1-076 — 17 – 24 Dec 1876 — The diagnosis and the Lisieux surgeon
- What happened: In Dec 1876, urged by her family, Zélie consulted an Alençon doctor ("Dr X" in the published letters; Laveille names him "M. Prévost"). She told her family at once and regretted it: "it was a scene of desolation … poor Léonie was sobbing." ## 1E. The Carmel of Lisieux: foundation and community (1835–1877)

### E-P1-079 — 1835 – 1877 — The Lisieux Carmel: foundation, poverty and the Foundress
- What happened: The Carmel of Lisieux began through two Gosselin sisters, pupils at the Pont-Audemer Carmel, whose health barred them from entering; they decided to found a house at their own cost. Bishop Dancel approved it on 16 Dec 1835. Fr Pierre Sauvage found nuns at Poitiers. The Gosselins took the habit at Poitiers on 14 Apr 1837. Sr Elizabeth of St Louis (Prioress) and Sr Geneviève of St Teresa (Sub-Prioress) came with them and arrived at Lisieux on 16 Mar 1838 (Sr Saint-Joseph-de-Jésus says 15 Mar, at 4 a.m. in pouring rain). They lived first in a thatched house lent by Mme Le Boucher: two garrets for a dormitory, a room 18 feet square split in three, a box as sideboard, the floor as serving table, so few plates that they were washed during dinner. Townspeople were cold. Fr Nicolas Sauvage said Mass in a whisper "like the priests… in the Catacombs". In Sept 1838 they moved to the rue de Livarot. The chapel was blessed 6 Sep 1852. Marie de Gonzague built the second wing in 1876, with the infirmary and the cell Thérèse later used. Sr Saint-Joseph-de-Jésus had 44 "little novices"; "the forty-third of them will be called Thérèse Martin". Mère Geneviève (Clare Bertrand), born Poitiers 19 Jul 1805, entered 26 Mar 1830, was prioress several times. From 1849 she had an internal tumour. Fr Sauvage lost confidence in her business sense after a letter from Poitiers, opened too late, said not to name her prioress ("Geneviève has no capacity"); she repeated to Marie des Anges: "Geneviève is capable of nothing." She received the devotion to the Holy Face from Tours in 1847. She died 5 Dec 1891.
- Cards: F-LF-184, F-LF-185, F-EW-006, F-EW-2664
- Best details: the washed plates at dinner and the Mass in a whisper (F-LF-184). "Geneviève is capable of nothing" — a saint judged incapable (F-EW-006). When Thérèse entered on 9 Apr 1888 only two witnesses of the foundation remained: Geneviève (83) and Sr Saint-Joseph (79), who had been totally deaf for several years, so their relations were "mostly exchanges of smiles" (F-EW-2664). Her golden jubilee, 20 Nov 1889: "no High Mass, no sermon, no parlour visits" (F-EW-2664). Geneviève's friend Sr Mary of the Cross lost her reason after offering herself as a victim for the definition of the Immaculate Conception; the illness lasted 33 years (F-LF-185 note).
- Evidence: old collection (Carmel publication FND 1913, edifying tone), Laveille, Carmel biographies, Sr Marie des Anges (heard). Confidence: high for printed dates; medium-high for FND.
- Conflicts: Geneviève's priorate dates are inconsistent inside FND (elected 1842, 1845, 1851, 1860 as printed; F-LF-185). The chapel: "large wing built in 1858 and chapel blessed 6 Sep 1852" is printed in this order (F-LF-184). Community size: "twenty nuns" (LAV p.278) against eight professed in 1840 (FND p.10). Arrival day 15 Mar (Sr Saint-Joseph biography) against 16 Mar (FND, Laveille). The humiliation story is dated 1842 (F-EW-006).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — the rainy arrival at 4 a.m.

### E-P1-080 — 29 Sep or 29 Nov 1860 – 22 Oct 1874 — Marie de Gonzague enters and becomes prioress
- What happened: Marie-Adèle-Rosalie Davy de Virville, born Caen 20 Feb 1834, entered the Lisieux Carmel in 1860 (the cards give 29 Sep and 29 Nov), aged 26, and became Sr Marie de Gonzague. She was professed 27 June 1862. As a young nun she volunteered for Saigon but her superior prevented it (Thérèse recalls it in 1897, LT 221 and Ms C 9v–10r). She was sub-prioress from 8 Jul 1866 (to 1872) and prioress from 22 Oct 1874. She was tall, slender and distinguished, with a pleasant voice. A family portrait of about 1871 shows strong features, a prominent nose, fine eyes and very high eyebrows. She was pious, did extraordinary penances and "a heart of gold", but the memoir calls her "badly balanced", with black melancholy over nothing and a jealousy that grew with the years and caused "terrible scenes". The superior Abbé Cagniard put her in office early "to cure her odd temper"; the memoir calls it "a fatal error". As prioress she almost imposed her will on votes for candidates. She was won by outward charm, a fine voice and affection. A "hysterical" nun was admitted through her insistence and made her weep much, and another, indiscreet, with a mania for lying and stealing, was also received. She held the office 21 years in all (6 years sub-prioress, 21 years prioress by Sr Cécile's count). Only Thérèse spoke of Gonzague's "so loving heart" (LT 190).

### E-P1-082 — 11 May 1871 – 1 May 1877 — Julia Leroyer (TSA): "For Carmel, never!"
- What happened: Julia-Marie-Élisa Leroyer was born at Lisieux on 5 Sep 1856, the only child of a devout couple. At 14 she told a priest friend she wished to be religious; he named the Carmel last and she answered at once: "Oh, for Carmel, never!" Two days later she saw "a whole programme of a mortified, solitary life united to God" and understood it was her path. Her father said if the vocation came from God he would not oppose it; he died two months later, on 11 May 1871, in a smallpox epidemic. She left the Benedictine school to be with her mother, who after some years agreed to the entry on 1 May 1875. In her postulancy she took the prioress's arm saying "Oh how happy I am!", disliked her bed because it looked like a coffin, and turned her back on it. Every Sunday the dinner was cabbage, which her stomach rejected, so she stayed without food until evening; she dared not say so. She admitted she ran and even jumped in the cloisters. For her habit retreat she kept her eyes down so exactly that the prioress had to tell her "But Sister, look!" at a statue. She was professed 1 May 1877. Agnès's circular says she had "a certain sharp stiffness" and never opened up to Mère Geneviève; Thérèse said kindly: "Sister Thérèse of Saint-Augustin is a lily in a pot".

### E-P1-086 — 12 Aug 1874 – 1944 — Marie de la Trinité is born

## 1F. Witnesses and people born in this period (background cards)

### E-P1-087 — 1835 – 1911 — Abbé Claude-Marcel Weber
- What happened: Weber, born 25 April 1835 at Lorcelette (diocese of Metz), was ordained 11 April 1861 and left the ministry in 1873. He was twice suspended a divinis around 1873 (absolved 1874). Around 1884 a German civil court convicted him of immorality ("rightly or wrongly") and sentenced him to six years' prison. He tried the Cistercians at Lérins (nine months, 1899), left for health, and became a resident priest at Saint-Jean-de-Luz.
- Cards: F-EW-003

- Evidence: witness about himself (PO-19, session 69, 22 March 1911). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes

### E-P1-089 — 1845 – 1920 (missions 1867–1898) — Fr Élie Lanaro, missionary in India
- What happened: Fr Élie (Jérôme-Vincent Lanaro), born Savona 13 December 1845, was a Discalced Carmelite of Lombardy. He was a missionary in India (Mangalore, Quilon) for about 31 years (1867–1898), then secretary to two Carmelite generals in Rome (1898–1906). His mission was ordinary 1867–1885 but very fruitful in converting pagans and Protestants from 1886 to 1898. He believed an unknown Carmelite in Europe was praying for him. Reading Story of a Soul, where Thérèse dates her zeal for souls from 1886, he concluded it was she.
- Cards: F-EW-007, F-EW-030
- Best details: he wrote of his belief to Fr Alphonse a Mater Dolorosa (Ypres, later Bruges) and said the letter should be printed in a Belgian review (F-EW-030). Editors: he briefly joined Garibaldi's troops as a youth, knew Bl. Mariam Baouardy in India, and died 20 February 1920 (F-EW-007). He never knew Thérèse.
- Evidence: witness about himself (PO-05, sessions 37 and 39, Oct 1910). Confidence: high for identity; medium — his own interpretation (F-EW-030).
- Conflicts: the editors' intro gives folios 443r–454r; the text starts at [434r]; one is a misprint (F-EW-007).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — a missionary sure a hidden Carmelite prayed for him.

### E-P1-094 — 1858 – 1905 — Dr Francis La Néele and the Guérin household later
- What happened: Francis La Néele was born on 18 Oct 1858 in Paris where his parents traded. - Cards: F-EW-2602

### E-P1-096 — 1865 – 1916 — Sr Marthe of Jesus
- What happened: Désirée-Florence-Marthe Cauvin, born 16 July 1865 at Giverville (Eure), lost her parents early and grew up in orphanages in Paris and Bernay. ### E-P1-099 — 1870 – 1893 — Isidore Guérin's later public life
- What happened: Isidore, a fervent Catholic notable by the 1870s, founded the Lisieux St Vincent de Paul Conference (1874), joined the parish council in 1874, founded a Catholic circle, promoted nocturnal adoration, protected the Christian Brothers and paid for a Calvary at the entrance of the town. Laveille says Lisieux was "formerly somewhat indifferent in spiritual matters, and perhaps a little too much given to pleasure". - Conflicts: French Wikipedia says he sold the pharmacy and retired in 1888; the Carmel study gives no date (F-LF-245).

## 1G. Later context: the pilgrimage world and the Church's records

### E-P1-100 — 1870 – 1893 — Italy and "the prisoner of the Vatican"
- What happened: Laveille says the 1887 pilgrimage came after the "spoliation of Italian convents" and the Pope's position as "virtual prisoner". Brennan (2000) says after 1870 French pilgrimages to "Peter in Chains" combined prayer for the Pope with national penance for the defeat of France, and fast trains and cheap package fares opened mass pilgrimage. Louis's letter from Rome on 27 Sep 1885 lamented that the Holy Father was "so in captivity". La Croix published more than 140 articles on Leo XIII's jubilee in 1887.
- Cards: F-LF-259
- Best details: Louis's 1885 letter (F-LF-259).
- Evidence: later Life (Laveille) and scholarship (Brennan, *J. Eccles. Hist.* 51/4, 2000; Carmel jubilee page). Confidence: high.
- Conflicts: none. Belongs to a later period; kept here only because the cards are dated from 1870.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P1-101 — 1911 – 1925 — The canonization bull and process: what the Church record says
- What happened: The canonization bull says she was born on 2 Jan 1873 at Alençon, diocese of Sées, "to Louis Stanislas Martin and Marie Zélie Guérin, honest parents, outstanding in singular and constant piety". Office lesson IV (AAS 20 p. 148) repeats "honestis parentibus". At the apostolic process the Carmel lists: Lemonnier (1841–1917), Dumaine (1842–1926, who baptized her), Pichon SJ (1843–1919, family adviser from 1880), Auriault SJ (b. 1855) and Grant (1854–1917). Two Carmelites (Sr Geneviève of St Thérèse and Sr Madeleine of Jesus, 1875–1940) identified the bones; eight reliquaries were sealed by the bishop. At the canonization, the pope's voice was carried by wireless amplifiers "for the first time at a canonisation", followed by "a thunderclap of applause"; that evening the dome and colonnade were lit for the first time since 1870.
- Cards: F-OM-074, F-OM-110, F-OM-117
- Best details: the wireless amplifiers and the applause (F-OM-074). Pichon "guided by the Holy Spirit": he said he never needed to warn her against illusion (F-OM-110).
- Evidence: official (Carmel pages), eyewitness (Taylor 1926/30, Laveille). Confidence: medium (F-OM-110 and F-OM-117 are from summaries; check page wording before quoting).
- Conflicts: absent witnesses at the apostolic process: Godefroy Madelaine (Belgium, under occupation) and, per the process page, Thomas N. Taylor and the Bishop of Nardò (F-OM-110 note).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low. Out of period; kept for completeness.
