# Digest P3 — 1887–1888 (Pranzini, the fight to enter Carmel, Rome, entry, first months, Louis's first crises)

Source: `02-dossier/_by-period/P3.md` (392 cards, all read; every card ID appears in at least one event below). Events: E-P3-001 to E-P3-088. Card IDs are unchanged.

## Period summary

### Turning points
1. Late Jun – 1 Sep 1887: Pranzini, "my first child" (E-P3-008, E-P3-009).
2. 29 May 1887: she tells her father, and he gives her the white flower (E-P3-006).
3. Oct 1887: the opposition of the uncle, Canon Delatroëtte and the bishop (E-P3-013, E-P3-014, E-P3-015, E-P3-020).
4. 20 Nov 1887: the audience with Leo XIII, "you will enter if God wills it" (E-P3-034).
5. 28 Dec 1887 – 9 Apr 1888: the bishop says yes, the prioress makes her wait, and she enters (E-P3-040, E-P3-046).
6. 23–27 Jun 1888: Louis vanishes to Le Havre, the start of his decline (E-P3-055).
7. 1888–1890: severe formation under Gonzague and Marie des Anges, delayed clothing and profession (E-P3-069 to E-P3-073, E-P3-068).

### Five best scenes
1. E-P3-034 — the audience with Leo XIII, with the letter written the same evening as a counter-text.
2. E-P3-009 with E-P3-008 — the coin for a Mass, the birdsong at five o'clock, the crucifix, the girl hiding to cry.
3. E-P3-006 — Pentecost in the garden, the little white flower with its roots, still in the book.
4. E-P3-030 — the Colosseum: over the barrier while Louis calls, then proud.
5. E-P3-047 with E-P3-046 — the tearless girl, the kneeling father, the superior's cold "Te Deum" at the open door.
Also strong: E-P3-042 (snow on the lamb's grave), E-P3-020 (Bayeux, "I see diamonds", the giant armchair), E-P3-072 (moonlit cell, the balloon), E-P3-086 (knocking on the tabernacle while sweeping), E-P3-083 (the night-light "for the Virgin", not a grain of dust).

### Five best "new to English" facts
1. Pauline admits (1915) she herself asked for the three-month delay after the bishop's yes, and that she joined in the refusal of profession in Jan 1890 (E-P3-040, E-P3-068).
2. Gonzague to Pauline: "She is much prouder than you think, she needs constant humbling"; Gonzague later told Godefroy she feigned indifference and severity on purpose (E-P3-070, E-P3-071).
3. The same-day letters differ from Story of a Soul: LT 27 (uncle "very kind", only "the world" an obstacle) and LT 36 (Pope "so old you would think he was dead", no guards) (E-P3-013, E-P3-034), plus the 1911 edits (E-P3-042, E-P3-043, E-P3-056).
4. Abbé Valadier via Abbé Faure: Pranzini, arms tied, could not "seize" the crucifix but demanded it; La Croix's detail of the severed head lifted "by one ear" (E-P3-009).
5. Marthe saw Thérèse knock on the tabernacle: "Are you there, Jesus? Answer me, I beg you!"; and the herring heads, beans and two-glass cider bottle of the refectory (E-P3-086, E-P3-076).
Also new: the Bologna student and the young pilgrim (E-P3-027), "too jovial" (E-P3-024), Marie's fierce opposition (E-P3-016), "I'll be a saint when I'm in Carmel; meanwhile I won't bother" (E-P3-041).

## Events (in time order)

### E-P3-001 — 19 Mar 1887 — Louis Martin at the curé's table: "she already burns to follow them"
- What happened: At a clothing ceremony of one of his daughters (fits Marie's clothing), Louis Martin sat near Godefroy at the presbytery table of the parish priest of Saint-Jacques, who was also the Carmel's superior (Delatroëtte). Louis said two daughters' salvation was assured and a third, only 14, already burned to follow them. Godefroy adds that Louis did not seek marriages for his daughters but wished all to be consecrated to God.
- Cards: F-EW-411
- Best details: The words « j'en ai encore une qui n'a que 14 ans, et qui déjà brûle de les suivre » (F-EW-411). The host was the very man who would later refuse her (F-EW-411).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Godefroy, PO-24, 1911), dated. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Timing sits oddly with the usual story that Thérèse first told her father at Pentecost (29 May 1887; see E-P3-006); Louis already speaks of her wish in March. The card does not explain this.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — Louis proud at the very table of the man who will say No.

### E-P3-002 — 31 Mar – 18 Aug 1887 — Playful letters to Céline and the Guérin cousins (LT 22–26)
- What happened: In the months after the "conversion" of Christmas 1886, Thérèse (14) wrote jokey letters to Céline and to her cousins Marie and Jeanne Guérin, who were at Trouville. She mocks herself and teases the cousins about illnesses.
- Cards: F-OW-347, F-OW-348, F-OW-349, F-OW-350 (part), F-OW-351
- Best details: April Fool rhyme to Céline about her "Diadème" and a comb from "the April fish" (F-OW-347). Marie Guérin nicknamed "mon petit Louploup", told to eat "like a real wolf" (F-OW-348). To Jeanne: glad no longer to hear "my sermons on death" or see "my eyes that fascinate you"; eight silkworms dead because Céline's care killed them "of grief or sudden apoplexy" (F-OW-349). Céline "in mourning" for two cordon-bleu finches, bodies at the taxidermist (F-OW-350). To sick Marie: "why did you take your face to the sculptor again?" (F-OW-350); "I let my pen run like a little mad thing" (F-OW-351).
- Evidence: Own writing, letters written the same day. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes (Louploup, silkworms and "sermons on death" are absent from the 1911 edition, F-OW-348, F-OW-349).
- Scene potential: medium — shows a funny, self-mocking 14-year-old.

### E-P3-003 — 1 May – Jun 1887 — Louis's first stroke, and the Le Havre exhibition
- What happened: Céline wrote in 1909 that Louis's first "attack of paralysis" came on 1 May 1887: very violent, striking the whole left side, quickly recovered. A few weeks later he had two milder ones. Laveille adds a fly sting while fishing left a small growth on his neck that sometimes gave great pain. In June 1887 Louis took Léonie, Céline and Thérèse to the International Maritime Exhibition at Le Havre. Thérèse herself writes of "an attack of paralysis in the legs" in May 1887 and says he tired easily on the Rome trip.
- Cards: F-LF-162, F-OW-085 (part)
- Best details: Céline says he walked to forget a pain "enough to make one lose one's head" (F-LF-162). The neck growth from the fly sting (F-LF-162).
- Evidence: Own writing (Thérèse), eyewitness (Céline 1909), later Life (Laveille). Confidence high for Céline's statement.
- Conflicts: Céline: whole left side; Thérèse (Ms A 71v): legs. English Wikipedia says "two paralyzing strokes" in 1889, which conflicts with Céline (weak source) (F-LF-162, F-OW-085).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low — background for the later crises (E-P3-055, E-P3-063).

### E-P3-004 — 1887 — Study, the Imitation in her muff, Arminjon, and the belvedere talks
- What happened: After Christmas 1886 she felt "an extreme desire to know" and studied history and science alone, with fixed hours. She knew almost all of the Imitation by heart, carried it in her pocket in summer and in her muff in winter, and at her aunt's was made to recite chapters on demand. At 14 she read Abbé Arminjon's conferences on the end of the world (lent by the Carmelites) at her study window and copied passages on perfect love. Each evening she and Céline stood at the belvedere windows and talked of eternity; the age gap had vanished. Only Pauline encouraged her vocation; Marie thought her too young. Céline agreed to let her go first.
- Cards: F-LF-167, F-OW-061, F-OW-062, F-EW-388
- Best details: Céline had said Thérèse must grow "the height of a stool" to share her secrets; Thérèse used to stand on the stool (F-OW-062). The two compare themselves to Monica and Augustine at Ostia (F-OW-062). "To suffer and be despised" of John of the Cross often on their lips (F-EW-388). Confessor allowed Communion four times a week in May, a fifth on feasts (F-OW-062); LF-167 says "several times a week". Their aunt astonished she knew the Imitation "word for word" (F-EW-388). Louis had both girls learn housekeeping (F-LF-167). Laveille's Cauchon/Joan of Arc tradition about the apse chapel of Saint-Pierre (F-LF-167).
- Evidence: Own writing; eyewitness (Céline); later Life (Laveille). Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Communion frequency: "four times a week" (Ms A) vs "several times" (Laveille). Minor.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium — the belvedere at night, moonrise, hand in hand.

### E-P3-005 — 1887 (a Sunday) — Blood from the hand of Christ; "I thirst"
- What happened: Looking at a picture of Christ on the Cross, she was struck by blood falling from one hand with no one to gather it. She resolved to stand in spirit at the foot of the Cross to receive it and pour it on souls. The cry "I thirst!" rang in her heart, and she felt "devoured by the thirst for souls", first for great sinners.
- Cards: F-OW-060
- Best details: « je me sentais moi-même dévorée de la soif des âmes » (F-OW-060).
- Evidence: Own writing (Ms A 45v). Confidence high. She gives no date.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium — image, leads directly to Pranzini.

### E-P3-006 — 29 May 1887 (Pentecost) — Telling her father; the little white flower
- What happened: Thérèse chose Pentecost to tell her father she wanted to enter Carmel at Christmas, and had prayed to the Apostles all day. After Vespers she found him sitting in the garden at the back of Les Buissonnets, and sat beside him in tears. He drew her to his heart: "What is it, my little queen? Tell me." Walking with her head on his chest, she told him. He wept, said she was very young, listened, and said God did him a great honour by asking for his children. He plucked a tiny white flower from a low wall, roots and all, and explained how God had made it grow. She pressed it in her Imitation at "That we must love Jesus above all things".
- Cards: F-LF-168, F-OW-063, F-EW-383 (part), F-EW-413
- Best details: Flower "like a lily in miniature"; "it is still there, only the stem has broken near the root" (F-OW-063). A white marble group of the scene stands in the garden of Les Buissonnets (F-LF-168). Léonie was at home and noticed nothing (F-EW-413). Setting: Ms A says "by the cistern, in the sunset"; Laveille's sunset and bird detail is his embellishment (F-LF-168).
- Evidence: Own writing; eyewitness negative (Léonie); later Life. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Louis's age given as 63–64 (Laveille says 64); place "garden entrance" (Laveille) vs "cistern" (Ms A). Minor. See also E-P3-001 (father already spoke of her in March).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: high — father and daughter, a flower with its roots, still kept in the book.

### E-P3-007 — 31 May 1887 — Admitted as Child of Mary by an exception
- What happened: Thérèse was received among the Children of Mary at the Benedictine abbey on 31 May 1887, with Marie Domin. Admitting a girl who had left the school was against custom. The one presenting her said "we will not regret having her name on our lists", and she was admitted unanimously. Days later the secretary, Blanche, listed only the new members still in the house and forgot Thérèse; she noticed only after Thérèse's death and keeps a holy card signed "Thérèse, enfant de Marie".
- Cards: F-EW-414, F-EW-396
- Best details: Signature « Thérèse, enfant de Marie » on a surviving holy card (F-EW-396). The speaker is the headmistress Sr Sainte-Placide in PO-31 but "the president of the council" in PA-12 (F-EW-414).
- Evidence: Eyewitness testimony (Sr Saint-Jean l'Évangéliste, Sr Saint-André). Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Who spoke the words: headmistress (PO-31) vs council president (PA-12) (F-EW-414).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: low — small human slip.

### E-P3-008 — Late Jun – Aug 1887 — She resolves to save the murderer Pranzini
- What happened: Everyone spoke of the murder of two women and a girl in the rue Montaigu, Paris, by Henri Pranzini, who showed no repentance. Louis did not let his daughters read newspapers, not even La Croix, but news was discussed. Thérèse resolved to save his soul, offering "the infinite merits of Our Saviour", and asked Céline to have a Mass said, not daring to ask her confessor. She gave Céline the coin, blushing, without naming the intention. Céline guessed and joined her. Her prayer: she was sure God would pardon him even without confession, but asked for "a sign of repentance" for her own consolation. Against her habit she began looking for newspapers.
- Cards: F-LF-164, F-OW-064, F-EW-405, F-EW-416 (part)
- Best details: The coin handed over, blushing; relief when Céline said she had guessed (F-EW-405). "This is my first sinner" (F-LF-164). She says she did not think she was disobeying the newspaper ban (F-OW-064). Céline saw her look for newspapers "against her habits" (F-EW-416).
- Evidence: Own writing; eyewitness (Céline). Confidence high. Laveille dates the prayer "late June 1887" by his own reckoning.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: partial (the coin, the blush and Céline's part are new, F-EW-405).
- Scene potential: high — a timid girl, a coin, a Mass for a murderer.

### E-P3-009 — 31 Aug – 1 Sep 1887 — Pranzini's execution and the crucifix
- What happened: Pranzini was guillotined in Paris on 31 Aug 1887. The next day (1 Sep) Thérèse opened La Croix, which reported that just before the last stroke he asked for the chaplain's crucifix and kissed it three times. She was in tears, had to run away and hide, and wrote that she had "the desired sign" and that the lips of "mon premier enfant" were pressed to the sacred wounds. Later, Abbé Valadier (successor of the chaplain Abbé Faure who assisted Pranzini) told Céline and the process that Pranzini, arms tied behind his back, could not have "seized" the crucifix, but demanded it insistently and kissed it with passion.
- Cards: F-LF-165, F-EW-415, F-EW-416, F-OW-064
- Best details: The report: "two minutes to five, while the birds sang in the trees" (F-LF-165). He pushed away the priest; the executioner's assistant held the head by the hair and, in the report, lifted the severed head "by one ear" (F-LF-165). Valadier quotes Pranzini: « Monsieur l'aumônier, donnez-moi le crucifix » (F-EW-416). Pranzini "exchanged two words with the chaplain as the executioner seized him" (F-EW-416).
- Evidence: Press report (La Croix); own writing; later testimony (Valadier via Faure, third-hand for the scaffold words). Confidence high on the kiss, medium for exact words.
- Conflicts: Ms A says he "took the crucifix the priest held out" (F-OW-064); La Croix says he "asked for" it (F-LF-165); Faure/Valadier say he could not seize it (arms tied) and demanded it (F-EW-415). Céline's version has him "cry out" and "exchange two words" (F-EW-416). The substance (he kissed it) is agreed. Card F-EW-415 says this corrects Ms A 46r.
- New to English readers: yes (Valadier's confirmation; the hair and ear details, F-EW-415, F-LF-165).
- Scene potential: high — birdsong at five, the crucifix, a girl hiding to cry.

### E-P3-010 — 1887 — Why Carmel: to suffer, to hide, to pray for priests; the missionary annals
- What happened: Thérèse weighed being a missionary, a nursing sister, or a helper of priests, and an inner voice pointed to the contemplative life. Céline says she chose Carmel to suffer more and win more souls, because it is harder for nature to work without seeing fruit. She wanted "to become a prisoner as soon as possible" to give the freedom of heaven to souls. She read a few pages of an annal of missionary nuns, stopped and said her desire to be a missionary was already "so violent"; she wanted to be a Carmelite. She had heard that at her birth the parents hoped for "a little missionary". She took a maxim of John of the Cross about pure love without a director.
- Cards: F-LF-166, F-EW-392, F-EW-434
- Best details: « j'ai déjà un désir si violent d'être missionnaire, que serait-ce si je l'avivais encore... Je veux être carmélite » (F-EW-392). She gave the annals to Céline unread (F-LF-166).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Céline, three separate passages), later Life. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Setting of the annals: Buissonnets 1887 (F-EW-392), the Rome pilgrimage where a pilgrim lent them (F-LF-166, F-EW-434). She was "fourteen" in all three.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P3-011 — Jul 1887 – Apr 1888 — Léonie's second attempt at the Visitation
- What happened: Léonie left for the Visitation two days after 14 Jul 1887; Thérèse wrote she believed Léonie would be happy only there. In October Léonie asked Thérèse to pray to Margaret Mary and to look after her little Child Jesus statue. By 9 Apr 1888 Léonie was back home. Léonie told the process that early in 1888 a Benedictine nun of Lisieux received from Thérèse the reply that after her death Léonie would enter the Visitation, succeed and take Thérèse's name and Francis de Sales's; in 1915 she gives a different version (Thérèse told Marie du Sacré-Cœur she would make Léonie return and persevere).
- Cards: F-OW-350 (part), F-OW-355, F-EW-547, F-LF-186 (part)
- Best details: Thérèse "kissed his little foot" for Léonie; "his little hand seemed to bless you from afar" (F-OW-355). Léonie's religious name was indeed Françoise-Thérèse (F-EW-547).
- Evidence: Own writing; second-hand testimony (Léonie). Confidence high for letters, medium for the prophecy.
- Conflicts: Two versions of the prophecy differ in hearer, date and detail (F-EW-547). Laveille says Léonie came home after leaving the Poor Clares (F-LF-186), while Léonie's own testimony and other cards say her first Visitation attempt (see E-P3-046). The Visitation house is not named in LT 29 (Caen inferred).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: low.

### E-P3-012 — Autumn 1887 — Charity to the poor: the workwoman, two catechism girls, two orphans
- What happened: After Pranzini she tried to convert an irreligious workwoman who sometimes worked at the house, and taught catechism to two poor girls who listened eagerly (Céline). Marcelline took her to poor girls' homes; she was radiant when sent with alms. The family also took in two motherless little girls (the elder not yet six, relatives of the maid), and Thérèse minded them all day, promising Heaven's rewards instead of toys; the elder called her "la grande demoiselle".
- Cards: F-EW-390, F-EW-397, F-OW-068
- Best details: Later in Carmel Céline saw her slip medals into the linings of workmen's clothes (F-EW-390; see E-P3-097). As a small child she kept her cakes for the poor (F-EW-397).
- Evidence: Own writing (Ms A); eyewitness (Céline, Marcelline). Confidence high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: yes (medals; F-EW-390)
- Scene potential: medium — the little "big young lady" with two orphans.

### E-P3-013 — 8 – 23 Oct 1887 — Uncle Guérin says no, then yes
- What happened: As guardian, Isidore Guérin had to consent. On Sat 8 Oct 1887 he gave her a kind talk and said only "the world" was an obstacle: it would be a "public scandal", "a unique case in all France"; not before 17 or 18. In Ms A he is harsher: he forbids any talk before 17, says it is against prudence, that "everyone would talk", that only a miracle would change his mind. She endured three days of "night of the soul" (raining in torrents). On the fourth day, Sat 22 Oct, he called her to his study and gave way: he had prayed for "a simple inclination of the heart"; she was "a little flower God wanted to pick". On 23 Oct she wrote Fr Pichon that Papa agreed and her uncle wanted God's will. Piat says Isidore later revised her letter of petition to the bishop.
- Cards: F-OW-352, F-OW-353, F-OW-065, F-LF-169, F-EW-380 (part), F-EW-419, F-EW-420, F-EW-2590, F-EW-2096, F-OW-354
- Best details: "a unique case in the whole of France… almost a scandal" (F-LF-169). Carmelite life looks to the world like "a philosopher's life" (F-OW-065). "in all the grave circumstances of my life, nature was the image of my soul" (rain, F-OW-065). Céline prayed with her all day while she spoke to their father (F-EW-420). First letter to Pichon: "I am not like my sisters… Bless your second little Lamb" (F-OW-354). Uncle's remark on what he felt: "A miracle is no longer necessary" (F-LF-169).
- Evidence: Own writing (letter same day, and Ms A), eyewitness (Céline, Pauline), scholarship (Piat). Confidence high.
- Conflicts: LT 27 (same day): uncle "very kind", "very sure of her vocation", names only the world and age 17–18. Ms A (1895): "all possible opposition", "unless a miracle" (F-OW-353). Dates: Laveille says Jun 1887 (F-LF-169); letters give 8 Oct and 22 Oct (F-OW-352, F-OW-354). Duration: "three days" (Ms A, Pauline, Céline) vs "some weeks of anguish" (F-EW-420). Reason: age (F-EW-419), scandal (F-EW-420), public image (F-EW-419), the world (F-OW-352). Pauline answered the judge: "He never said that" about the two-sisters reason (see E-P3-018).
- New to English readers: yes (letter version, F-OW-352; "second little Lamb", F-OW-354)
- Scene potential: high — three days of rain, then the study door on Saturday.

### E-P3-014 — Sep – Oct 1887 — Canon Delatroëtte's outburst in the foundress's infirmary
- What happened: When the Canon visited the dying foundress Mother Geneviève in the infirmary on a feast, Mère Marie de Gonzague had her ask him to admit Thérèse. He burst out: "Again this young girl! … one would think the salvation of the community depended on the entrance of this child … Let her remain with her father until she has reached her majority." He said he had consulted God and did not want to hear of it again. Geneviève favoured the entry against him.
- Cards: F-EW-379, F-EW-385, F-EW-403, F-LF-170 (part)
- Best details: "Il n'y a pas de péril en la demeure" (F-EW-385). The 1910 deposition says only that he found her too young; the 1915 one adds the outburst (F-EW-385).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Mère Agnès, present); quoted by Laveille. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Two tellings: Geneviève asked him "in honour of this Feast" in the community room and he turned away (F-EW-379 note, CF p. 49); asked "before the whole community" to let Thérèse enter at Christmas (F-EW-385); the infirmary visit (F-EW-379, F-LF-170). Two tellings of the same request, or two requests. Card F-EW-403 (editors) says Geneviève was 82 at entry and opposed the Superior.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: high — an old dying foundress asks, the priest explodes.

### E-P3-015 — Oct 1887 — At the presbytery: a sharp "No", in torrents of rain
- What happened: Louis, with Céline, took Thérèse to Canon Delatroëtte. Céline admired how the shy girl explained her reasons. He received them very coldly, cut her short with "No", said there was no hurry, she could live a Carmelite life at home, "all would not be lost" if she did not take the discipline, and that he was only the bishop's delegate ("If he allows you to enter, I shall have no more to say"). She left in tears under her umbrella in pouring rain, and said she would go to the Holy Father if the bishop refused. Louis at once offered to take her to Bayeux. ### E-P3-018 — 1887–1888 — Why the authorities objected: the official picture
- What happened: Cards from many witnesses give the reasons: youth (all sources); Uncle Guérin (guardian; too young, public scandal); Canon Delatroëtte (not before 21; later resented steps taken over his head; hesitated again at profession); Bishop Hugonin (let her stay with her father); Révérony (joked "not yet fifteen years"); Leo XIII (deferred). Thérèse told Lemonnier the reasons were her youth and two of her sisters already in that Carmel; Pichon thought age and delicate health. The Church's own texts (brief, 1921 allocution) say: uncle would not consent until 17; superior wanted to wait until 21; after the bishop agreed the prioress delayed four months; she complained against none. Thérèse never spoke a bitter word against those who opposed her; she wept but did not lose hope.
- Cards: F-EW-378, F-EW-382, F-EW-393, F-EW-398, F-EW-399, F-EW-400, F-EW-406, F-OM-007, F-EW-2096
- Best details: Opposition lasted "several years" (F-EW-382). Canon Delatroëtte's "acid words" about Thérèse were put by Agnès into her testimony "to prove Thérèse had not been spoiled" (F-EW-406, Sr Cécile). In her letter to Roulland she calls the superior's refusal "prudent" (F-EW-400). No one in the family was surprised she became a nun, only that she entered at 15 (F-EW-398, Marcelline and Jeanne Guérin).
- Evidence: Mixed: official (AAS brief, Benedict XV), eyewitness, later Life. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Pauline's statement that Delatroëtte gave "only" youth as reason vs Thérèse to Lemonnier adding two sisters (F-EW-380, F-EW-382, F-EW-399). ### E-P3-023 — 7 – 9 Nov 1887 — Nobles, cards and "pharisees": Champagne to Milan

### E-P3-034 — Sunday 20 Nov 1887 — The audience with Leo XIII: "You will enter if God wills it"
- What happened: In steady rain, pilgrims gathered at 7.30 under Mgr Germain and Révérony; at 8 the Pope said Mass, deathly pale, trembling. In the audience chamber each pilgrim kissed his foot and hand. Révérony told the Bayeux pilgrims that no one was to speak to the Holy Father. Thérèse looked at Céline, who said "Speak!" Instead of kissing his hand she joined hers: "Most Holy Father, in honour of your Jubilee, allow me to enter Carmel at fifteen!" Révérony explained: the superiors were considering it. The Pope: "Well, my child, do what the superiors tell you." With her hands on his knees: "Oh Holy Father, if you said yes, everyone would agree!" He looked at her and said, stressing each syllable, "Allons… allons… vous entrerez si le bon Dieu le veut." Two noble guards and Révérony lifted her by the arms and carried her almost to the door; the Pope put his hand on her lips and blessed her. Céline asked a blessing for the Carmel; Révérony said crossly "The Carmel is already blessed"; the Pope repeated gently "Oh yes, it is already blessed." Louis, blessed earlier (hand on his head), met her at the door.
- Cards: F-LF-179, F-OW-076, F-OW-362, F-OW-363, F-EW-435, F-EW-436, F-EW-437, F-EW-426 (part), F-EW-399 (part), F-EW-400 (part), F-OM-007 (part), F-OW-359 (part)

### E-P3-041 — Jan – Apr 1888 — Three months of waiting: the temptation to soften, or to live seriously
- What happened: During the wait she says she decided to lead "a serious, mortified life": breaking her will, holding back a retort, small hidden services, not leaning her back (she did no penances and was "coddled in cotton wool"). ## Ongoing conditions of the first years in Carmel (1888–1890, some to 1897)

These events group the cards that describe repeated conditions or undated incidents of the postulancy and novitiate. Each is dated from its earliest possible start.

### E-P3-069 — 1888 — The cobweb, the weeding at 4.30, "this child does absolutely nothing"
- What happened: Gonzague treated the postulant severely: "she could never meet her without receiving some reproof". She left a cobweb in the cloister and, before the whole community, Gonzague said: "It is easy to see that our cloisters are swept by a child of fifteen… go and take away that cobweb." Marie des Anges sent the postulant every afternoon at 4.30 to weed the garden for fresh air. Meeting her there, Gonzague said: "this child does absolutely nothing. What kind of novice is this whom it is necessary to send every day for a walk?" Thérèse smiled and did not explain she was going to work; Marie du Sacré-Cœur was present once, watching whether Thérèse would excuse herself. The habitual reproaches were slowness and little devotion in her jobs; Thérèse sewed in her free time, eyes down, thinking the prioress wanted it. Later she thanked Gonzague for this "precious" education.
- Cards: F-EW-443, F-EW-444, F-EW-538, F-EW-570, F-EW-571, F-EW-606, F-LF-192
- Best details: « on voit bien que nos cloîtres sont balayés par une enfant de 15 ans !... c'est une pitié ! » (F-EW-571). Weeding "found hard but said nothing" (F-EW-538). Ms A said "I could not meet her without kissing the ground" (F-OW-084, see E-P3-070); the 1911 edition rewrites this and inserts the cobweb story from Ms C (F-OW-084). The remark that the child "does absolutely nothing" was heard "later" by Thérèse (F-EW-538).
- Evidence: Own writing (Ms A, Ms C quoted by De Teil), eyewitness (Marie des Anges, Marie du Sacré-Cœur), Sr Cécile. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: none serious. The daily 4.30 walk was imposed by Marie des Anges for fresh air before the 5 p.m. prayer (F-EW-570, F-EW-606). Thérèse calls Marie des Anges "subprioress" in her 1897 list (F-OW-257); other cards call her novice mistress.
- New to English readers: partial (the time and "does nothing" are new; Ms A mentions walk and remark, F-EW-538).
- Scene potential: high — cobweb before the whole community; the smiling postulant in the garden.

### E-P3-070 — 1888–1893 — Direction hour, severity, and Gonzague's explanations
- What happened: In her rare hours of direction with Gonzague (about an hour), Thérèse was "scolded almost the whole time"; what hurt most was not understanding how to correct her faults. Gonzague told the novice mistress a soul of this temper should not be treated as a child. She also said to Pauline (who complained her sister was badly cared for and always humiliated): "she is much prouder than you think and needs constant humbling; if it is about her health, leave it to us, it is none of your business." Marie says the prioress, used to being flattered, showed the postulant little sympathy or coldness. Marie des Anges observed that Thérèse fought not to become humanly attached to the prioress "whom she loved very much"; Thérèse also fought to invent permissions to visit the office for comfort and had to cling to the stair rail; when Marie du Sacré-Cœur (about to enter Gonzague's cell) signalled her to follow, Thérèse hurried downstairs. Gonzague often gave recommendations she herself forgot; Thérèse kept practising them.
- Cards: F-EW-458, F-EW-471, F-EW-474, F-EW-537, F-EW-569, F-EW-531, F-EW-493, F-EW-568, F-EW-533, F-EW-590, F-EW-607, F-EW-572, F-OW-084
- Best details: « Ce n'est pas une âme de cette trempe qu'il faut traiter comme une enfant » (F-EW-458). « Elle est beaucoup plus orgueilleuse que vous ne pensez, elle a besoin d'être constamment humiliée » (F-EW-471, absent from 1910). Marie: "Seeing her heroism, I confess I was ashamed when I compared myself to her" (F-EW-607). Marie says direction sessions were "rare" (F-EW-569). Thérèse: "I could not meet her without kissing the ground" (F-OW-084).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Pauline, Marie, Marie des Anges, MdA); own writing. Confidence high.
- Conflicts: Gonzague's reason to the novice mistress ("not a soul to treat as a child") vs to Pauline ("much prouder than you think") (F-EW-474 notes the comparison). Gonzague's own explanation (see E-P3-071): deliberate testing. Praise in letters (E-P3-052, E-P3-065) vs scolding. Several sisters wrongly thought Thérèse spoiled (F-EW-537).
- New to English readers: yes (Gonzague's "prouder", the direction hour details, the banister)
- Scene potential: high — the banister, and "prouder than you think".

### E-P3-071 — 1888–1897 — How Gonzague explained herself, and how others saw the prioress
- What happened: Gonzague told Godefroy that, to train Thérèse's virtue, she deliberately showed her a kind of indifference and "some show of severity", which was very painful to Thérèse in the first years; later Thérèse made it a joyful sacrifice. Once in the parlour Gonzague praised the young novice's generosity in front of her; Domin thought this imprudent and concluded her humility firm since the praise did not shake her. Marie says that in life few knew Thérèse but Gonzague, "while sometimes jealous of her", said there was none like her in the community. MdA says Thérèse, with "remarkable finesse of mind", saw the gaps in Gonzague ("this mother whom we loved in spite of everything"), wrapped her in tenderness, and after the painful March 1896 election wrote her a fine letter. As a young nun Marie du Sacré-Cœur poured complaints about a sister to Gonzague; Gonzague kept writing, then asked thrice, louder each time, "And you, Sister Marie du Sacré-Cœur?"
- Cards: F-EW-557, F-EW-561, F-EW-496, F-EW-539, F-EW-407
- Best details: « elle s'étudiait à l'éprouver, en affectant à son égard une sorte d'indifférence et quelque apparence de sévérité » (F-EW-557). « Et vous, ma soeur Marie du Sacré-Cœur ? » told laughing near age 80 (F-EW-407).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Godefroy hearing Gonzague, Domin, Marie, MdA). Confidence high; F-EW-407 medium (oral, late).
- Conflicts: Gonzague's claim of deliberate severity (F-EW-557) vs public praise before the novice (F-EW-561, Domin: contrasts); vs jealousy (F-EW-496).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P3-072 — 1888–1890 — The novice mistress Marie des Anges: absent-minded, holy, and hurtful by accident
- What happened: Marie des Anges (Jeanne de Chaumontel, 1845–1924) taught her the linen room and tried to form her by talking of God almost constantly, which tired Thérèse; her soul did not open. About two years later, on 2 Sept 1897 Thérèse told Agnès, God ended the trial and she could open her soul to her, who then consoled her. The mistress wanted to let her sleep longer but forgot for weeks, then suddenly ordered fifteen days' rest; the prioress, not seeing her at prayer, scolded her, and "the poor child did not know whom to obey". Editors: MdA was rather absent-minded and an involuntary cause of suffering; she wrote seven short notes to Thérèse (Oct 1888–Oct 1890). One evening in the postulancy MdA went to Thérèse's cell to console her in great sorrow: Thérèse was in a long nightgown, hair loose, sat on the straw mattress; moonlight lit her face; MdA felt she was speaking to a saint and held back tears. Thérèse: "I suffer much, but I feel I can suffer still more." Gonzague ordered Thérèse to show her Pichon correspondence only to the prioress; MdA, always losing pencil, pen or paper, often took things from Thérèse's writing case, seeing a started letter or one from Canada, causing pain to both. MdA laughed heartily when Thérèse doubted her vocation on the eve of profession. A 14 July a half-deflated balloon shaped like a naked dwarf drifted into the cloister garth; MdA took it for sorcery, beat it with a stick while it "bowed", cried for holy water; Thérèse laughed to tears. MdA once put out a petrol fire in the lamp room with her hands and admired Thérèse's calm at the start of a fire.
- Cards: F-EW-441, F-EW-532, F-EW-608, F-EW-609, F-EW-541, F-EW-611, F-EW-612, F-EW-613, F-EW-578, F-LF-194, F-EW-487
- Best details: The old sub-prioress Fébronie told her laughingly at recreation that she must not have much to tell her superiors because her soul was "extremely simple" (F-EW-578; Ms A 70v identifies the "good old mother" per the editor). Long exhortations early tired her (F-EW-441). Thérèse "came once to confide the pain the prioress caused her, without complaint, smiling" (F-EW-441). « Il y avait peut-être deux ans que j'étais ici, quand le bon Dieu fit cesser mon épreuve » (F-EW-608). ### E-P3-073 — 1888–1890 — "Tell me every stomach ache": the order she obeyed daily
- What happened: As postulant and novice, Thérèse was ordered by her mistress (Marie des Anges) to report every stomach ache. She had one every day; reporting was torture ("a hundred blows with a stick") and she came blushing. The mistress forgot her own order and said "This child does nothing but complain" or "you will never have the health to keep the Rule". Gonzague, asked for a remedy, said crossly: "this child is always complaining… if she can't bear her ills, let her leave!" Thérèse went on reporting at the risk of being sent away, until freed from the order. She did not say she was ill unless ordered: several times she went to the Office in such pain she thought she would faint; she told herself "If I fall, they'll see it!"

### E-P3-075 — 1888–1897 — Poverty: the lamp, the jug, the velvet, the pin, the pens
- What happened: As a postulant she liked neat things and having what she needed to hand. She lost this taste step by step. One evening after Compline her lamp was taken by mistake when she had much work; she was tempted to impatience, then rejoiced. Her pretty cell jug was replaced by a big chipped one; she came to love "the ugliest and least convenient objects". Later: when a sister edged her workbasket with attic velvet, she undid it and sewed it on inside out, "poorer and uglier"; a pearl-headed pin she broke off; she made a novice scrub linseed oil from her cell furniture. All her religious life she used a small lamp whose wick could be raised only with a pin. When Céline entered Thérèse gave her own decent writing case and holy-water stoup and took discarded ones from the attics. She used pens to the last, dipping them in milk in her illness "to make them soft again". She mended clothes until worn out, chose the ugliest tools, let others take needed tools without asking them back. She said "I'm like the truly poor; no point making a vow of poverty if you don't suffer from it." When forgotten at table she was glad, and if someone plagiarised her thought she said poverty forbade her to claim it back. She let others repeat her witty remarks as their own.

### E-P3-076 — 1888–1897 — Food: leftovers, beans, herring heads, a careless neighbour
- What happened: Eating was "shameful" to her as a child; in Carmel she went to the refectory "as to a torture". She never complained, so the sisters concluded she had no dislikes and served her the leftovers and scraps of earlier meals. In the kitchen: "Nobody would eat that — let's give it to Sr Thérèse of the Child Jesus, she never refuses anything." An omelette or herring cooked on Sunday reappeared until week's end. Céline heard in the parlour (from Pauline) that she sometimes had only "a few herring heads". On bean days no one knew they made her ill; she was told to eat everything served, was sick each time, and said nothing until the infirmary. She sat beside a sister who took nearly everything (MdA/TSA say through distraction) and went without necessities. She shared with her neighbour a cider bottle holding barely two glasses: she drank none so as not to deprive her neighbour, and took no water so as not to be noticed. Early in religious life she tried to make her food tasteless, later dropped this. MdA "often had a heavy heart to see this young, frail child denied the dietary relief she needed". Marthe, the cook, never knew her tastes; MJ served the dried-up leftovers (per Piat).

### E-P3-077 — 1888–1897 — Cold, posture, the discipline, "never complained"
- What happened: Only near the end did the sisters learn that cold made her suffer very much; she never rubbed her hands or said "it's very cold". MdA: "Ah, if I had known!" Laveille: she walked about fifty yards through open cloisters and an icy corridor, slept under two thin blankets and shivered all night. She never leaned back, never crossed her feet, sat straight, and did not wipe sweat because it "admitted one was too hot". Céline said instinct stiffens under the discipline to hurt less; Thérèse was stunned: she took it "to hurt myself" and wanted it to hurt as much as possible; tears came but she tried to smile. Requests for extra penances were refused because of her delicate constitution. Godefroy never heard of excess; he believed she "carried the germ of the illness for several years". The Carmelite rule as Laveille sums it up (hours of office, fasting from 14 Sep to Easter, coarse serge, "a hard palliasse supported on three planks"). She also refused dispensations in her youth.
- Cards: F-EW-452, F-EW-535, F-EW-490, F-EW-516, F-EW-491, F-EW-559, F-LF-190
- Best details: « c'était convenir qu'on avait trop chaud et une manière de le faire savoir » (F-EW-490). « je prends la discipline pour me faire du mal et je veux qu'elle me fasse le plus de mal possible » (F-EW-491). Alpargates and chilblains (F-LF-190). MdA learned only recently that she suffered cold "enough to die of" (F-EW-535).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Pauline, MdA, Céline, MJ, Godefroy); Laveille. Confidence high; F-EW-559 medium (opinion).

### E-P3-078 — 1888–1897 — At the bell, in silence, on the clock: her regularity
- What happened: At the first sound of the bell she left everything, even in the middle of a word or a stitch. Marie kept a note ending "I must leave you, 9 o'clock is strik…" (LT 49). She said it was better to lose a line and make an act of regularity. She was first in choir, where she received "the blessing of the community's angel". She was "a miser with her time": "time does not belong to us". She spoke low, never in cloisters or regular places, closed the door before the first word in permitted places, and refused to answer Marie-Madeleine in a forbidden place. She walked eyes down, never running, passing groups without stopping and told novices to do the same. She never asked for news and kept away when the prioress told something to a group. She would not ask for painting lessons or write poems without the novice mistress's permission; her first poem on "the virginal milk of Mary" brought great joy; once she came embarrassed because Gonzague wanted her to keep some correspondence to herself. Her handwriting displeased Mère Agnès; she proposed writing slanting backwards, was refused and applied herself; only in 1894 was she allowed.

### E-P3-079 — 1888–1897 — Charity toward the difficult: Marthe, the teasing lay sister, TSA, and others
- What happened: Thérèse chose recreation company among those left out or unfriendly. She sat daily beside a young lay-sister novice (probably Marthe de Jésus, not named in most cards) who enjoyed teasing her in bad taste and was "jealous of her virtue", listened humbly and gained great influence over her by gentleness. Marthe says she often tried Thérèse's virtue: "another sister in her place would have given me up, so unbearable was I"; once, cross, Marthe said hurtful things and Thérèse calmly asked her help with a job; Marthe helped grumbling and could not break her gentleness, then asked pardon. The editors say Thérèse felt a strong natural antipathy for TSA and by constant smiling kindness made her believe the opposite (the unnamed sister of Ms C 13v–14r). TSA says "our souls understood each other". Sr St Vincent de Paul (lay sister cook, 32 years older) caused early friction: "I had pain at Sr St Vincent de Paul's". She told a novice never to say no and to see God in each sister; she carried loads for lay sisters as for choir nuns; she hated joking lies and corrected novices for them yet was the charm of recreation. Marie-Philomène (entered at 45) says Thérèse never made her feel her lesser gifts, told her "God has fruits of every season" and that she was "too afraid of God". For a companion lay sister Thérèse pretended to need a daily programme of gifts to the Child Jesus, to stir her up. She never used scented flowers because perfume bothered an old mother, and took up the artificial rose the mother demanded she remove: "See, Mother, how well nature is imitated today…" She once was reproached for not greeting Mère Agnès after a retreat and Mère Geneviève scolded her: not true charity.

### E-P3-080 — 1888–1897 — She did not seek her own sisters, and the parlour rules
- What happened: In Carmel Thérèse showed no marked preference for her three sisters; at recreation she never sought them nor avoided them, and often chose a sister who was alone or neglected. Several nuns say so. She never confided her struggles to Pauline under Gonzague's rule, though she could have got leave; when Pauline became prioress in 1893 the sisters kept coming weekly, but Thérèse thought that seeking herself, and Pauline saw her least of all. After bad parlour news about her father's health she went straight back to community exercises. Auriault: she turned away from them rather than toward them. Aimée saw she was perfectly detached, except once, when one sister's sorrow seemed to affect her strongly ("barely an imperfection"). She kept every confidence, even from her sisters and from Mère Agnès as prioress. In the parlour a half-hour sand-glass ran; when the last grain fell she closed grille and curtain and left at once. She hardly spoke ("Is she there?"), and the family judged her "insignificant" ("she had entered too young; her education was cut short and she would feel it all her life"). Céline, still in the world, left the parlour sad; Thérèse asked Jesus to tell Céline what she meant; at the next parlour Céline reported inspirations that were exactly the advice. Flowers Céline sent she received at the turn but would not keep without the prioress's order; in illness: grapes were good "but I don't like what comes to me from my family". She told Marcelline "Love God very much, so as not to fear him too much: he is so good!" and warned Léonie against vanity in dress. Sr Marie de Jésus wrote everyone wanted a few minutes with her on talking days.

### E-P3-084 — 1888–1897 — Her offices, year by year; the infirmary she never got
- What happened: She held most house offices in turn. ### E-P3-085 — 1888–1897 — Guides and directors: none proper, Pichon, Youf, Blino, others
- What happened: Céline: "She had, properly speaking, no spiritual director." She consulted retreat preachers and extraordinary confessors but rarely found the light. Pauline: the first director barely heard her and left for Canada (Pichon), writing a few lines once a year; another, surprised by her aim at holiness, said it was pride to want to equal or surpass St Teresa; a third, in 1891, told her she did not offend God and could follow her way of trust. Laveille names Fr Blino SJ ("What pride and presumption! Confine yourself to the correction of your faults"). Youf had true piety but poor health; "it was impossible to obtain from him direction properly so called" but he favoured daily Communion as far as discretion permitted. Pichon later testified he never had to warn her against illusion. Pichon kept none of her letters (she wrote him twelve for each of his). A July/Aug 1897 letter to him on Psalm 22 was one where "my whole soul was there". Pichon: "love mixed with no fear"; Roulland: she drew confidence from God's justice; abandonment above the cult of suffering. Communities did not have daily communion; her greatest suffering was not being able to receive every day.
- Cards: F-EW-389, F-EW-450, F-LF-195, F-EW-551, F-EW-554, F-EW-510
- Best details: « Elle n'avait pas, à proprement parler, de directeur spirituel. » (F-EW-389). Priest not named for the "pride" rebuke (F-EW-450; Laveille names Blino, F-LF-195). Pichon born at Carrouges near Alençon; 1,015 retreats by 1915; died 1919 (F-LF-195). The editors note Pichon's "few letters" does not fit Thérèse's twelve-to-one statement (F-EW-551).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Céline, Pauline, Pichon, Roulland); Laveille. Confidence high; F-EW-450 medium (second-hand, priest unnamed).
- Conflicts: Pichon in 1915 regrets not keeping "the few letters" she sent (F-EW-551), while Thérèse says she wrote him twelve for each of his. The "pride" rebuke: unnamed (Pauline) vs Blino (Laveille from Pauline's Summarium). Retreat with Pichon dated differently (see E-P3-053).
- New to English readers: yes ("pride" rebuke)
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P3-086 — 1888–1897 — Prayer and devotions: dryness, the Holy Face, the tabernacle, wholesale for priests, Pranzini's Masses
- What happened: Pichon saw her led by dryness, not sweetness. Marie names "almost constant dryness". Thérèse told MdA her spiritual desolations "gaily", so that God would give consolations to souls. Marie de la Trinité's glass (a girl's cut-glass tumbler given as a lamp glass before the family's Holy Face image in 1887–88, which blackened, kept a brown triangle when cleaned and later burst from too strong a flame): in March–April 1896 Thérèse said this grace was of the same kind as "the prophetic vision I had about Papa" and that her own end would be like the glass, love's flame breaking her body (F-EW-410, high confidence; a fuller version of 1938 lacks the "Papa" sentence). The Divine Office was "my happiness and my martyrdom"; she kept a Holy Face picture in her breviary and choir stall; it hung on her bed curtains in illness. Her devotion to the Passion began at five; in Carmel she threw flowers at the crucifix in the courtyard, in illness covered her crucifix with rose petals, but refused to throw flowers at a person as a sign of affection. She chose Carmel for priests, "doing business wholesale, since through the head she reached the members" — told at her canonical examination before profession. Patron saints: Teresa, Francis de Sales, Martin, John of the Cross; friends: Cecilia, Joan of Arc, Théophane Vénard, Holy Innocents; a hymn on the responses of St Agnes. She made the Heroic Act; only special suffrages for Pranzini: "He is my child; I must not forget him now." She asked for money for feasts and birthdays to have Masses said for him, sometimes whispering to Céline "It is for my child". While sweeping the chapel as novices, Marthe saw Thérèse kneel on the altar, knock at the tabernacle: "Are you there, Jesus? Answer me, I beg you!" and rest her head on the little gilded door; she rose as if transfigured. Once she slipped medals into the lining of workmen's clothes. She wrote to Marie Guérin about scruples: "When the devil has succeeded in keeping a soul from Communion, he has won everything and Jesus weeps… What offends Jesus is lack of trust… Communicate often."
- Cards: F-EW-542, F-EW-534, F-EW-410, F-EW-500, F-EW-501, F-EW-486, F-EW-504, F-EW-593, F-EW-588, F-EW-503, F-EW-2570
- Best details: « Es-tu là, Jésus ? réponds moi je t'en supplie ! » (F-EW-588). « C'est mon enfant, il ne faut pas que je l'oublie maintenant » (F-EW-504). « faire le commerce en gros » (F-EW-486, Céline says each candidate answers what she wishes; this was her own). Saints who seem deaf are loved more (F-EW-501). Circular quotes the scruples letter; number and date not given (F-EW-2570).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Céline, MdA, Marthe, Marie); own letter. Confidence high; F-EW-588 high (eyewitness, unusual gesture).
- Conflicts: none. Céline's PO/PA versions of Pranzini Masses differ by attribution (birthday money vs aunt's gifts, F-EW-504 vs F-EW-593).
- New to English readers: yes (the tabernacle scene, medals)
- Scene potential: high — knocking on the tabernacle door while sweeping.

### E-P3-087 — 1888–1897 — Record, dates and objects: what Céline kept, the description, the writing materials
- What happened: Céline kept all Thérèse's letters as treasures, but admits affection had much to do with it; she did not suspect their future value. Laveille prints "from contemporary information" a description: "tall of stature and slight, with golden hair and grey-blue eyes… face fair as a lily". Hearing "extremely keen". Small Gospel volume near her heart until death; in temptations against faith she once wrote the Credo in her blood in it, as one confessor counselled. Two "miserable exercise-books" for the manuscript; poems scribbled on old envelopes.

### E-P3-088 — 1888–1890 — Guérin family: La Musse, rue Labbey, Marie Guérin's life
- What happened: In July 1892 Céline and Léonie rented 7 rue Labbey and lived there with Louis until his death; Le Juif family entered the Guérins' service in 1892 (Désiré Le Juif converted in 1893 before Céline's painting). Each summer from 1888 the Martins and Guérins spent months at La Musse; Thérèse never went. Laveille says she found it hard to keep worldly influence from "the hospitable and beautiful château". Marie Guérin (b. 1870) did not enjoy the family's new wealth; her squirrel letter to Léonie from a tree branch; at Jeanne's wedding on 1 Oct 1890 (Thérèse wrote the announcement of her own union with the divine Spouse). Marie made a retreat each year at La Musse, prayed four times a day in the woods, attended weekly sewing meetings for the poorest; Gonzague favoured her; Marie still called Thérèse "the little Queen"; wrote to Carmel in secret because her parents did not favour a tendency to mysticism.
- Cards: F-LF-211, F-EW-2601, F-EW-2603
- Best details: Squirrel mocks her; the Iton river "taciturn because it cannot reflect Léonie's face"; the horse Bichette raising its head to the loft window looking for "her amazon" (F-EW-2601). Marie put on the shimmering dress "without a look at the mirror" (F-EW-2601). Rule limit 21 nuns, by special leave 27; "five members of one family" (F-EW-2603).
- Evidence: Scholarship (Piat 1967), Carmel place pages, Laveille. Confidence medium-high.
- Conflicts: The Guérins sold La Musse on 11 May 1898 (Carmel place page) or in 1899 (Fortin) (F-LF-211). This card range extends far past 1890 (P4+).
