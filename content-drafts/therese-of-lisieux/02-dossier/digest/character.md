# Character digest (undated cards) — St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Stage 2b, character agent. Input: `_by_period/undated.md` (425 cards, F-EW-2027 to F-EW-2597, F-LF, F-OM, F-OW). Every card ID in the input appears at least once below. Cards that say the same thing are merged: the entry starts with all the card IDs ("A + B + C") and keeps the extra details of each. Dates in square brackets are approximate periods. Quotations are copied from the cards. Nothing is invented.

Witness initials are kept as the cards give them: MT (Marie de la Trinité), MJ (a lay-sister witness in PO 15 / PA 18), MM (Marie-Madeleine), MdA (Marie des Anges), TSA (Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin), AJ (Aimée), Godefroy (Fr Godefroy Madelaine). Source codes: PO = Ordinary Process, PA = Apostolic Process, MSC = Marie du Sacré-Cœur, CS = Céline's "Conseils et souvenirs", LAV = Laveille, D3/D4/D6 = books in the dossier, HA = Histoire d'une âme.

## Top of the digest

3. The mussel shell for a novice's tears (F-EW-2077).

7. Thérèse writes "Amour du lucre" on Céline's fingernail; the hen that tells the passers-by (F-EW-2356, F-EW-2357).

### Conflicts and cautions for the outline stage
- **Same saying, different hearers.** "Never three minutes without thinking of God" is reported by Marie (F-EW-2143, F-EW-2158, F-EW-2348), by Céline (F-EW-2375, and F-EW-893 outside this file), and by Geneviève. Either she said it twice or one sister borrowed the other's memory.
- **Fashion journal or catalogue.** Marie's two versions of the confessed picture: "an illustrated page of a catalogue of novelties" (F-EW-2039) and "a page of a fashion journal" (F-EW-2146, F-EW-2169). Same event. Geneviève says fashion-catalogue pictures were a "ladder" to God for her.
- **Gonzague.** The cards give a severe prioress (F-EW-2029, F-EW-2118 to F-EW-2125) and a beloved one ("we feared her like a storm without an umbrella", F-EW-2445). Editors warn that Geneviève's statements on the last illness and Gonzague's conduct then need correction against Gaucher (F-EW-2174).
- **The hardest sister** is unnamed on the cards. Tradition says Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin (TSA), but the file does not say so (F-EW-2131). The cards on the "melancholic sister" (F-EW-2222), the linen-room sister (F-EW-2266) and the sacristy sister (F-EW-2292) may be one case or several.
- **Jeanne Guérin softened.** Her first testimony: "At that time I would never have suspected she was so holy" (F-EW-2301). Her later testimony claims she saw extraordinary perfection already (PA-23 [1357], noted on the card).
- **Zélie.** Neighbours said "No point pitying Madame Martin"; Marie says the letters show her heart "was broken" (F-EW-2148).
- **Boisterous or solitary at play?** Sr Saint-André: she liked little noisy play and gave sermons at recess (F-EW-2307). Marcelline and Jeanne: no pleasure in noisy games, "play hermit" (F-EW-2310, F-EW-2034).
- **Ordinary or extraordinary.** Céline, Marie, Pauline and Godefroy insist that little in her outward life was extraordinary (F-EW-2140, F-EW-2176, F-EW-2188, F-EW-2360, F-EW-2433). Yet the same cards show harsh penances (F-EW-2199) and a hidden inner trial (F-EW-2274, F-EW-2311).
- **Wet stockings.** One event told by two witnesses with different tone: Marie des Anges (F-EW-2038) and MdA in her own process ("a rather vulgar trait", F-EW-2294).
- **Low-confidence or context cards.** F-EW-2052 (Pauline rang the bell: Dolan only), F-EW-2090 (butter, not about Thérèse), F-EW-2313 (general), F-EW-2325 (subjective), F-EW-2351 (no names), F-EW-2430 (vague "several").

## 10. Cause, canonization and afterlife (not character; for other agents)

(These undated cards are not character material. They belong to the miracles/canonization and afterlife agents. Kept here so nothing is lost. Details are exactly as on the cards.)
- F-OM-024 — During her life de Teil (future vice-postulator) gave a talk at Lisieux Carmel on the Carmelite martyrs of Compiègne and asked insistently whether the nuns had heard of miracles through their intercession; leaving the parlour Thérèse said: "How touching is the zeal of this postulator! How glad one would be to have miracles to make known to him!" (Laveille, PDF 413 note). Taylor gives de Teil's own version: "If any of you who are listening to me have the intention of being canonised, please have pity on the poor Vice-Postulator and work plenty of miracles!" — "And Sœur Thérèse, obedient child, did precisely as she was told." Medium (two reports of one scene). New: yes. [Humor]
- F-OM-025 — De Teil to Taylor: "I knew that any Carmelite Cause was hopeless in Rome. The Congregation of Rites was weary of Causes in which it was easy to prove that virtue had been heroically practised, but where authenticated cures were invariably wanting"; officials on seeing medical documents: "Well, at last, we have something substantial"; "The two most important factors in a canonisation are money and miracles. Sœur Thérèse is providing us plentifully with both!" Taylor pp. 390-391; medium.
- F-OM-107 + F-OM-134 — De Teil told Taylor that Mgr La Fontaine, Secretary of the Congregation of Rites (later Cardinal Patriarch of Venice), said too much was being made of this young Carmelite; unless it stopped the cause would be suspended: "The fuss must stop." De Teil: "We can cease our own efforts, but how are we to hinder Sœur Thérèse from advertising herself?" and told him the Recanati fish story (M-OM-048); La Fontaine then asked him to write to Mother Agnès for a favour for himself, it was granted, "and thenceforward there was no more question in Rome of too much fuss". Taylor PDF 438-440 (garbled in scan); medium; date c. 1910-1914 not stated. Card F-OM-134 wrongly says "the pope" (source: a Roman secretary).
- F-OM-038 — Soldiers in the First World War called her their "second angel guardian" or "war god-mother"; artillery officers named their battery after her; pilots named aircraft "Avion Sœur-Thérèse"; whole regiments consecrated to her; General Duplessis vowed a pilgrimage to her tomb; bullets stopped by relics, medals, pictures and booklets were sent to the Carmel; the chapel walls covered with Legion of Honour crosses, epaulettes and swords as ex-votos; one soldier: "True, we have Joan of Arc, but the little Sister is nearer to us." Laveille PDF 421-424; Taylor 1924 PDF 29 ("on either bank of the Somme"); S. Vogt analysed about a thousand files 1914-1918; high that reports were received. F-OM-123 (soldiers' gifts kept at the Carmel: banners, ex-votos, anonymous medals; headings only, low) and F-OM-124 (Vogt's study; "extraordinarily palpable" presence in the trenches; medium).
- F-OM-046 — Benedict XV (via de Teil to Taylor): not surprised France was proud of her daughter, but "the devotion of the English-speaking peoples to Sœur Thérèse proved to him clearly the finger of God was there", promised a speedy beatification; to a priest: "Pray to her; it is her special vocation to teach priests how to love Jesus Christ." Taylor PDF 271, 273; Laveille PDF 406; medium (second-hand).
- F-OM-056 — Relics divided after a second canonical recognition; postulator Fr Rodrigo took a portion to Rome; Taylor: one portion in the "magnificent Brazilian reliquary" in the marble shrine under the "Corpo Santo" and the other, in a rosewood casket, inside the recumbent figure; Laveille: the more important relics in the base of the shrine and the rest in the tinted marble figure "in her last sleep"; Brazil's gift by national subscription (Fr Rubillon SJ); no source names the sculptor. High.
- F-OM-059 — Pius XI chose her as "his patron and counsellor", recognized in her the "Star" of his pontificate: "She has in truth thrown light on questions that I was unable to solve", and in confidence, "These days I feel quite near me the presence of Blessed Thérèse." Laveille PDF 432; medium.
- F-OM-068 — The lawyer argued that Sister Gabriella's cure was two miracles (two organic diseases cured, like a man who falls and breaks bones in head and leg); consultors did not agree (no precedent confirmed by the Holy See) though it made the supernatural character "shine more"; a second cure (Maria Pellemans) was needed. AAS 17 pp. 148-149; high.
- F-OM-072 — Sebastiani: it could seem "new and wonderful" that she be made a saint only two years after beatification, but God had granted a great number of graces and prodigies "in this very two-year period"; Christ seemed to yield to his bride « quasi pueriliter blandienti » ("caressing him almost like a child") and fulfil her promise of a "mystic rain of roses". AAS 17 p. 209; high.
- F-OM-073 — Canonization numbers disagree: Taylor (present 6.30 am to nearly 2 pm): 33 cardinals, about 200 prelates, about 4,000 in the procession, 200,000 could not get in; Laveille: 34 cardinals, over 200 archbishops and bishops, 50,000 inside, in his preface "more than 4,000 priests and 60,000 faithful" and "almost a million spectators"; Carmel: 23 cardinals, 250 bishops, 50,000 faithful. Low for any single figure; 50,000 inside agreed by two sources.
- F-OM-075 — Pius XI thanked God that "the virgin whom, after beginning his pontificate, he first raised to the honours of the blessed, he should be the first to enrol among the saints"; Taylor adds "the first daughter of St. Teresa to receive the honour of canonisation" (medium: would exclude Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi, canonized 1669). AAS 17 p. 211.
- F-OM-076 — Canonization homily: she learned the Gospel teaching on childhood and lived it; doctrine from the catechism, asceticism from the Imitation, mystical theology from St John of the Cross; she candidly said before dying "she had given God nothing but love"; the "mystic rain of roses" proves she began her work at once in heaven; if spiritual childhood became general society would be reformed; he ended by making his own the prayer that ends her book. AAS 17 pp. 211-214; Taylor PDF 299-302; high.
- F-OM-077 — Taylor, present: after the homily, as the amplifiers stopped, five roses from a cluster of lights in the apse came loose, "describing a large curve in the air", and landed at the pope's feet. Taylor PDF 23-24; medium (one eyewitness; see M-OM-085).
- F-OM-085 — Bishop McGinley asked at the canonization that his new diocese (Monterey-Fresno) be dedicated to her; told "That is not the custom"; Pius XI: "We will think it over", and days later sent written permission; Taylor: it would have the first cathedral dedicated to her. Taylor PDF 466-467; medium.
- F-OM-088 — Martyrology: 30 September (third place) records her death at Lisieux; Pius XI enrolled her among the virgins, declared her patroness of all missions, feast kept on 3 Oct; the 3 October entry names her "whose birthday [into heaven]" is recorded on 30 Sep. AAS 20 p. 152; high; resolves F-OM-071 v. F-OM-079 (canonization formula named her dies natalis; bull and Martyrology fixed feast on 3 Oct; reason not stated).
- F-OM-112 — Mère Agnès presented to the apostolic process "Voie d'enfance spirituelle" (Sr Isabelle du Sacré-Cœur) and "Dans quel milieu sœur Thérèse de l'Enfant-Jésus s'est sanctifiée". Carmel; medium.
- F-OM-118 — Carmel page "Un miracle, c'est quoi ?": the medical commission asks whether the illness was incurable by the medicine of the time, whether healing was instant and complete, whether lasting; doctors say "extraordinary" not "supernatural"; the Church asks whether credible witnesses testify and whether the cure links to a named invocation (a modern rule, not 1920s wording; F-OM-136 marks this a "surprise"). Medium.
- F-OM-119 — Carmel "Comment lire un récit de miracle": three parts: the sick person and the request, the healing, a chorus of wonder; the mark of the form is crossing a limit thought uncrossable; the Church does not oblige belief in any given miracle. Medium.
