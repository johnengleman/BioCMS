## Part 1 — Period summary

### Turning points
1. 7 Mar 1898: Bishop Hugonin gives the imprimatur; Oct 1898: the book goes out in place of the death circular (E-P7-001, E-P7-004).
2. 1898–1910: the book spreads by mail, print and relics; letters grow from 5 orders a day to 500 a day in 1914–15 (E-P7-011, E-P7-051).
3. 1904–1905: Gonzague and Marie Guérin die trusting "my little Thérèse" (E-P7-034, E-P7-003).
4. 15 Oct 1907 to 8 May 1908: Bishop Lemonnier asks for written impressions; Marie-Ange asks him to open the Cause (E-P7-045, E-P7-047).
5. 1909–1911: Postulator and vice-postulator; the Ordinary Process (Aug 1910–Dec 1911); first exhumation 6 Sep 1910 (E-P7-058, E-P7-062, E-P7-067).
6. 1915–1917: the Apostolic Process in wartime; soldiers' petitions; second exhumation 9–10 Aug 1917 (E-P7-088, E-P7-093, E-P7-069).
7. 14 Aug 1921 heroic virtues; 26 Mar 1923 translation; 29 Apr 1923 beatification; 17 May 1925 canonisation (E-P7-104, E-P7-068, E-P7-105, E-P7-109).
8. 1927–1997: patroness of missions (14 Dec 1927), basilica (1929–1954), patroness of France (1944), relic tours, Doctor of the Church (19 Oct 1997) (E-P7-112, E-P7-116).
9. 2008–2015: her parents beatified and canonised (E-P7-117).

### Five best scenes
1. E-P7-034 — Gonzague on her deathbed in Dec 1904: "not a single soul here has been as guilty as I have", yet she trusts "my little Thérèse".
2. E-P7-046 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus writes to the bishop in 1907: "I never saw anything that could get her put on the altars"; the sisters treated her as a "phoenix".
3. E-P7-073 and E-P7-057 — Gallipoli: the shy prioress, the drawer with 500 livres, and the bishop's sealed envelope that comes back with roses on one note.
4. E-P7-069 — 10 Aug 1917: the white ribbon with her saying, "Je veux passer mon ciel…", found in the coffin; the soldier's helmet on the coffin.
5. E-P7-050 — Grant, the Presbyterian pastor: the newspaper notice in his pocket for a year; the photograph he cannot take down; the books she removes from his shelves. (Also strong: E-P7-072 Franco reads the laundry passage; E-P7-114 Léonie hears the basilica homily forget her; E-P7-108 Père Perroy defends her against the "rose garden".)

## Part 2 — Events

### E-P7-001 — 7 Mar 1898 — Bishop Hugonin gives the imprimatur for the manuscript
- What happened: Godefroy Madelaine told Bishop Hugonin of Bayeux about a manuscript by Sr Thérèse. Hugonin first objected that one must beware of women's imagination. On Godefroy's verbal assurance that it showed the marks of God's spirit and had no doctrinal error, he gave the imprimatur on the spot. In another deposition Godefroy says the bishop "made some objections" and had him write the report.
- Cards: F-EW-1781
- Best details: « il fallait se défier de l'imagination des femmes » — Hugonin's first reaction (F-EW-1781). Godefroy's letter of 8 Mar 1898 says "Yesterday I saw Monseigneur" (F-EW-1781). PA-26 says Hugonin had known Thérèse (F-EW-1781).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Godefroy, in the process, PO-24 and PA-26); confidence high.
- Conflicts: PO-24 says the imprimatur was given on the spot; PA-26 says the bishop made some objections and had Godefroy write the report (F-EW-1781). Not a real contradiction, but the two accounts differ in emphasis.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium — a bishop's doubt, then a quick yes; one line of dialogue.

### E-P7-002 — 1898 — Preparing the printed text: Mondaye revision and Gonzague's alterations
- What happened: The autobiography was printed in place of the usual circular letter, and was revised by the Premonstratensian fathers of Mondaye (Fr Godefroy Madelaine, not named by Céline). Mère Marie de Gonzague, then prioress, wanted the whole text to seem addressed to her. Some forms of address and some private family details were scratched out. Marie says the substance did not change. The original text was restored after Gonzague's death (Dec 1904).
- Cards: F-EW-1754, F-EW-1762
- Best details: « voulut qu'il semblât lui avoir été adressé tout entier » (F-EW-1754). Céline (F-EW-1247, outside this file) agrees, and adds that Gonzague signed the manuscript and that Marie herself did the restoration (F-EW-1754 note). Céline admits the text was "revised" (F-EW-1762).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Marie, Céline, in the process); confidence high. Marie's "substance unchanged" is the word of an interested party.
- Conflicts: none among these cards. Marie says the substance did not change; a reader must check this against the manuscripts (not in these cards).
- New to English readers: partial (known to scholars; not in the standard life)
- Scene potential: medium — a prioress with a pen scratching out "Mother" and family names.

### E-P7-003 — 1898–14 Apr 1905 — Marie Guérin (Marie de l'Eucharistie): "little victim", illness, death
- What happened: On 15 Aug 1898 Marie received as a feast gift one of the linens on which Mère Agnès had gathered the tears of the dying Thérèse. She kept as "Ultima verba" the talk by which Thérèse prepared her for her veiling. On 30 Sept 1897 Sr Marie des Anges took over the novices and kept them until April 1900. The 30th of each month became a "little feast": novice recreation, special meal, and at seven a little hymn by Mère Agnès sung to the Infant Jesus that Thérèse decorated with flowers. On 21 Nov 1898 Marie wrote to her mother that she asked Thérèse to take her by the hand again on the way of pure love, that every soul has a spark of that love; for bellows she used "acts and sighs of love"; "The fire keeps under the ashes". Around 1899 a sister tried to console her by saying the community regarded her as a "benefactress"; she said she would prefer humiliation to breaking the Rule. From 1899 she could not keep the Rule and called herself "dead weight". She joked (c. 1900–05): "Thérèse spends her heaven doing good on earth... I will look after only scrupulous souls". About 1900–03 she composed for recreation "La Vertu", a royal coach driven by Love, with two horses Joy and Generosity, whip Penance, brake Fear, lackey Charity, lanterns spiritual direction, cushion Peace, carpet Humility, roof Desire of Heaven, preceded by "an angel: Thérèse of the Child Jesus" and caught up "in a whirlwind of fire: merciful Love". On 19 Apr 1902 Mère Agnès succeeded Gonzague as prioress; Marie "trembled with joy". In July 1903 a dry cough revealed the return of tuberculosis, which lasted 21 months; her father brought a Paris eminence who tried repeated injections of horse serum; enormous abscesses tortured her and nearly killed her twice. She asked for a New Year's gift of a phonograph disc of her father singing the Bluebeard air that used to frighten little Thérèse. She wrote "I am not a soul to desire suffering, no, but to accept it when it comes"; her mother's last instruction to Pauline: "watch that Marie does not become proud!" On 15 Jan 1905 she wrote "I have just received Extreme Unction. I am very happy and in great peace"; on 10 Mar 1905 she called Thérèse "a true Guardian Angel". She said "I have her same abandonment... I entered the legion of little victims that she asked of God". Mgr Amette promised her that after this life she would have as much capacity to enjoy as she had had to suffer. On 14 Apr 1905 she entered her agony in the morning; the chaplain said he would offer her at the consecration as a little host; she asked "And after?" — "After, that is God's secret." Dr La Néele knelt weeping. A sister brought "the Virgin of Thérèse" (the statue that smiled): "How I love her!" She tried to remove a little flower from her crucifix: "No withered flowers for Jesus." Last words: "I am not afraid to die! oh what peace!... Oh how I would like to die of love!... My Jesus, I love you!" Piat gives 10.15 a.m., aged 34 years 7 months. Isabelle said nine years later: "When one has seen Sister Marie de l'Eucharistie die, one can no longer fear death." Pauline wrote to Léonie on 24 Apr 1905: "She died a saint's death... The whole town came to see her... the chapel was too small... Never for any ceremony had this been seen." More than twenty priests and a large crowd attended.
- Cards: F-EW-2642, F-EW-2644, F-EW-2576, F-EW-2569, F-EW-2575, F-EW-2649, F-EW-2650, F-EW-2651, F-EW-2652, F-EW-2580, F-EW-2579, F-EW-2653, F-EW-2654, F-EW-1823
- Best details: « Elle est ... » not applicable. The phonograph disc request (F-EW-2650, "a vivid detail of medicine in 1904 and of a phonograph"). The "Virgin of the Smile" statue in the infirmary (F-EW-2579, F-EW-2653). The joke shows "I will spend my heaven doing good on earth" was known and joked about before 1905 (F-EW-2569). Marie's "Souvenirs" of Thérèse are not in our sources (F-EW-2642 note). The chaplain is not named (Abbé Youf died Oct 1897) (F-EW-2579).
- Evidence: Circular by Agnès (9 Jun 1905), Piat (1967) quoting letters. Confidence high for circular words; medium-high for Piat.
- Conflicts: Hour of death: the circular says she died at the moment the chaplain consecrated at Mass (he said Mass at 9); Piat says 10.15 a.m. (F-EW-2653).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: high — the dying sister's "And after?" and the statue that smiled.

### E-P7-004 — Oct 1898 — Histoire d'une âme is published and first sent to the Carmels
- What happened: The book was sent first only to Carmels, in place of the usual death circular. Carmels lent it and requests poured in. Pauline, not the prioress, was put in charge of shipping, and had at least five orders every day. After the October 1898 publication letters began to arrive. The edition sold out at once and further editions followed "like a trail of powder".
- Cards: F-EW-1747, F-EW-1750, F-EW-1762
- Best details: "at least five orders every day" for Pauline (F-EW-1747). Céline says readers were drawn by her doctrine and way to God (F-EW-1762). Céline: it spread "like a trail of powder" (F-EW-1762).
- Evidence: Eyewitness (Mère Agnès, Céline, in the process); confidence high.
- Conflicts: none.
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium — Pauline at the parlour table with the day's orders.

Mère Agnès urged it, following Thérèse's counsel; Fr Godefroy Madelaine revised the manuscript, took it to Bishop Hugonin for the imprimatur, and wrote the letter of approval. Taylor adds that "a first attempt failed" before the imprimatur was obtained. The plan says 21 Oct 1898, 1,000 copies, paid by Isidore Guérin.

- Scene potential: low.

### E-P7-006 — 1898–1909 — Isidore Guérin: publisher of the book, benefactor, doubter, death
- What happened: After Mgr Hugonin's authorisation of 7 Mar 1898, Isidore Guérin paid the costs and advised the Carmel to entrust the "modest pages" to the Imprimerie Saint-Paul at Bar-le-Duc; he supervised and read proofs. The work was printed in 2,000 copies and came off the press on 30 Sep 1898 (official date; in reality 10 Oct), the first anniversary of her death, and was sent in place of the circular to all the Carmels. After his wife's death in 1900 he wrote: "God may try us... we will love him all the same"; "it is a bottomless abyss that you open in my heart". He told his daughters at his last parlour: "O my children, ask for love... and finally die of love". He agreed to take in Jeanne's household; Dr La Néele left his Caen practice and settled with his wife at rue Paul Banaston in 1900. Isidore became a Carmelite tertiary as "Brother Elie of the Blessed Sacrament" on 11 Sep 1901 and, hearing that the Carmelites of Mount Carmel needed offerings for a wall, was among the first to subscribe: "It is my duty; I am a son of Carmel." When Mère Agnès was re-elected on 6 May 1905 he rejoiced; he regarded himself as "the foster father of the Community", and closed the deficit of about ten thousand gold francs a year freely. In 1903 Agnès, with his help, prepared a fall-back position at Westmeerbeek in Belgium (province of Malines) in case of expulsion; Dr La Néele rented it and Isidore took the lease; it was never used. He did not support the effort to introduce the Cause: he was too prudent to risk failure that Norman clubs and salons might mock; the Story of a Soul and the first miracles filled Lisieux gossip and irritated him; for a time he thought of leaving town; under the La Néeles' influence he doubted "an affair of nuns". He took part in spreading devotion to the Holy Face (lamp in the cathedral of Saint-Pierre, family prayer); in 1902 he bought Paul Vignon's "Le Linceul du Christ" and lent it to Céline, who then put all her art into painting the Saviour's face; three years later he sent a full-size reproduction of the Shroud with the seal of the Archbishop of Turin and Baron Antonio Mansio. He said: "Oh my poor children, it is finished! I can no longer make my Way of the Cross: I weep too much." He died at home on Tuesday 28 Sep 1909 at 10.45 a.m., aged 68½, last words "Call me, Jesus!" The funeral on Fri 1 Oct filled Saint-Pierre; by his will he went in the poor man's hearse, without speeches. La Dépêche de Lisieux called him "the surest of friends, the most discreet of confidants".
- Cards: F-EW-2643, F-EW-2648, F-EW-2655, F-EW-2656, F-EW-2657, F-EW-2658, F-EW-2660, F-EW-2661, F-LF-244
- Best details: Isidore funded the first edition and read the proofs (F-EW-2643). Rented but unused Belgian property (F-EW-2657). Piat says Isidore did not know until Heaven that he had been right to call her "a privileged little flower" (F-EW-2659, see E-P7-047). The Shroud of Turin link to Céline's painting (F-EW-2660; see M-EW-041 and E-P7-035).
- Evidence: Fr Piat (1967) drawing on family letters and interviews with the sisters (medium-high); F-EW-2658 is Piat's characterisation, no letter quoted (medium). F-LF-244 from Carmel and FR Wikipedia.
- Conflicts: Tertiary date: 11 Sep 1901 (F-EW-2656) vs 5 Sep 1900 (F-LF-244). Print run/date of first edition: 2,000 copies dated 30 Sep 1898 (in reality 10 Oct) here vs two runs of 1,000 in 1898 (F-LF-242, see E-P7-026). Piat says a chapter of "Rain of Roses" was first added to the autobiography in 1907 (F-EW-2658). Isidore's date of death: 28 Sep 1909 (both).
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: high — the uncle who paid for the book and wanted to leave town when it made her famous.

### E-P7-007 — 1898–1925 — Pluie de roses and the letters of favours
- What happened: Laveille says letters of favours arrived by hundreds and the Carmel printed them in volumes, *Pluie de roses* ("Shower of Roses"): in fifteen years seven volumes, no less than 3,000 pages; the first recorded facts go back to 1902; vol. VII covers beatification to canonisation. The Carmel says a long note of 1901 had grown to 60 pages of accounts by 1907 and became the base of the first volume; Taylor says a short account of the most remarkable answers to prayer was added for the first time to her own pages in the 1907 edition. The Carmel says the *Pluie de roses* appeared 1907–1926 in 10 volumes (7 chronological, 2 thematic, 1 anthology), with over 3,200 accounts; the first printing (early 1910) was 8,000 copies, titled « Quelques-unes des grâces et guérisons attribuées à l'intercession de sœur Thérèse »; Rome stopped publication in 1914 when the cause was introduced, and it resumed in 1920; during the war typed fascicles circulated in hospitals and among military chaplains. About 8,000 favours were recorded at the Carmel 1898–1925. A White Father in Africa wrote (Dec 1910) that he put her picture in Christian homes and catechism schools, that natives asked who this little "bikira" (virgin) was, and pagans came "in compact crowds". Roulland testified she had "very remarkable influence in converting souls" in Japan, China and the Indies and that many Trappistine nuns in Japan attributed their vocation to her. From 1898 the Annals of the Propagation of the Faith recorded unhoped-for mission results. Laveille quotes the sceptic Maurice de Waleffe in *Le Journal*: "Her short and uneventful life was that of a lily which opens at morn and silently fades at eve… She has written a book… Here we have the real miracle." Thérèse was named "Strewer of Roses" and "little Queen of Alençon". Canadian dioceses loved her; in Protestant hospitals nurses passed her pictures bed to bed; a Liverpool factory-owner gave the book to each of his hundred workers and an annual paid eight-day holiday (Shower of Roses IV, Preface, p. 8).
- Cards: F-LF-246, F-OM-020, F-OM-021
- Best details: The "bikira" question (F-LF-246). The Liverpool factory-owner (F-LF-246). Waleffe: "Here we have the real miracle." (F-LF-246).
- Evidence: Laveille (1925, popular), the Carmel page, Taylor; medium-high.
- Conflicts: "Seven volumes" (Laveille, Taylor 1927) vs "ten volumes" (Carmel): Laveille and Taylor wrote before the last volumes (F-OM-021). First facts "1902" (Laveille) vs a note of 1901 (Carmel).
- New to English readers: partial
- Scene potential: medium — a White Father whose villagers ask who "the little virgin" is.

### E-P7-008 — 1898–1914 — Nuns drawn by the book; postulants from many countries
- What happened: Laveille names three Lisieux nuns who entered because of the book. Mère Marie-Ange de l'Enfant Jésus, of Breton origin, "rather egoistic character" with "genuine greatness of soul", was drawn from a worldly life, prioress for eighteen months, in 1908 persuaded Bishop Lemonnier to support the Cause, and died of consumption on 11 Nov 1909 after seven years in the cloister. Mère Isabelle du Sacré-Cœur, poet, mistress of novices, sub-prioress, died 31 Jul 1914 aged 33 (Laveille also has "aged 33" where E-P7-029 gives TB). Mère Thérèse de l'Eucharistie, born Countess de la Tour-d'Auvergne, sub-prioress, asked "How would our little Thérèse have acted?" A retreat priest told the prioress "it is easy to see that a saint has lived in your Carmel." Postulants came from French provinces, Ireland, Portugal, Italy, Constantinople and Argentina; many were sent to other convents. Taylor 1924 calls Marie-Ange's story "perhaps the fairest Rose".
- Cards: F-LF-247, F-OM-019
- Best details: Marie-Ange's "rather egoistic character" (F-LF-247). All three appear in Laveille only (F-LF-247 note).
- Evidence: Laveille, Taylor; medium-high.
- Conflicts: Isabelle's age at death: Pauline says aged 32 (E-P7-054), Laveille 33.
- New to English readers: yes
- Scene potential: medium.

### E-P7-009 — 1898–1911 — How the book travelled: Leo XIII, first translations, early readers, sales
- What happened: The Carmel sent Histoire d'une âme, with a chapter added on her illness and death, instead of the usual obituary circular; it was printed and dispatched at the end of 1898. Early readers wrote of something new: Fr Godefroy Madelaine (Mondaye, 8 Apr 1898) found "a theology to which the most beautiful spiritual books only seldom attain"; the Passionist Fr Louis-Théophile of Jesus-Suffering, aged 80 (Mérignac, 30 Nov 1898), called it "a new invention of sanctity … unknown until to-day"; Fr Le Doré, Superior General of the Eudists (24 Feb 1899), had priests, society ladies and novices read it. Cardinal Gotti presented a fine copy to Leo XIII on 30 Dec 1899; the Pope read it "for a notable time with marked satisfaction". On 19 Mar 1900 Gotti and the Carmelite General put her souvenirs in the postulation archive. First translations: Polish (Carmel of Przemyśl; imprimatur Posen 6 Dec 1901); English by Professor Dziewicki of Cracow University (request 29 May 1899, unpaid); Italian (two), Dutch (1904), German, Portuguese (Fr de Santanna SJ, Madeira 1905), Spanish (Mgr Polit), Japanese, Braille, Russian (Kiev, March 1910), Breton, Chinese (Shanghai Carmel), Armenian. By 1909 the full French edition reached 60,000 and the abridged 80,000; 112,000 pictures and 25,000 souvenirs were sent in 1909; 30 letters a day, then 100 a day by 1911; 60,000 souvenirs and 300,000 pictures in 1910; the 90th thousand of the large edition was printed early in 1911. On 25 Sept 1909 the Glasgow Observer had 21 weekly thanks like small ads, and announced it would charge for them.
- Cards: F-EW-2055, F-EW-2056, F-EW-2057, F-EW-2058, F-EW-2059, F-EW-2099
- Best details: Leo XIII read the book "for a notable time with marked satisfaction" (F-EW-2056). Fr Godefroy's 8 Apr 1898 letter: "a theology to which the most beautiful spiritual books only seldom attain" (F-EW-2099). "a new invention of sanctity" (F-EW-2099).
- Evidence: De Teil (D3), printed sources; high.
- Conflicts: The English translation is dated 1901 in F-EW-1796 (Taylor), while F-EW-2059 says Dziewicki was asked on 29 May 1899; letters per day in 1911 (100) agree with MT (E-P7-051).
- New to English readers: yes

### E-P7-030 — c. 1901–1910 — Taylor learns of Thérèse and spreads her in Britain; the English edition
- What happened: Taylor never knew Thérèse. He first read Story of a Soul about 1901, wrote to the chief Carmelites of Lisieux and came four times to talk with them. The book was translated into English in 1901 and spread slowly, perhaps because it was expensive. It grew fast, especially in Scotland, after an "extraordinary event" at the Good Shepherd convent in London on 30 Oct 1908, which Taylor publicised. He spread her pictures and her Life in Britain, the colonies and the United States. The Lisieux nuns told him chiefly of her "purity", her patience, her trust in God and her devotion to frequent communion. Taylor later explained that "purity" meant moral beauty and candour of soul, not only chastity. In 1906–07 pictures and souvenirs of Thérèse multiplied; Taylor was at first surprised, then stopped being surprised when he saw how much people wanted them. He says the nuns never pushed him. Asked whether his zeal or the Carmelites' was exaggerated, he said "rather below the truth" (F-EW-1826, PO-02 f. 187v).

### E-P7-048 — Jun 1908 to Sep 1910 — Céline's signs: the voice, the roses, the coffin plank
- What happened: In June 1908, on her great retreat for the anniversary of her profession, Céline had again given herself to Love and asked how she would know she was fully given. About 4 a.m. someone said in her ear: « On ne se livre à l'Amour qu'autant qu'on se livre à la souffrance. » She did not doubt it was the Servant of God. At the end of 1908 people began to speak of perfumes by which Thérèse made her presence known. Céline rebelled, calling it « grossier, parce qu'il frappait agréablement les sens ». One evening near the end of Nov 1908, entering "our cell, which is the very one Sister Thérèse occupied", she found the oratory filled with a scent of roses. She stayed unbelieving ("now they perfume everything; it may be a letter soaked in scent"), left without checking, and on the turn of the stairs felt "a breath" of roses. Then she believed, but it vanished at once. Similar events happened about fifteen times in the two years after; none in the eleven years after her death, only from 1908; she was never favoured when she wished, only when thinking least about it. After the exhumation of 6 Sep 1910 a board from the head of the coffin was said to have come loose. On 15 Sep 1910 the prioress sent a portress to gather wood fragments; she took a board from a hedge that looked like it. Marie de la Trinité came in for a parcel, knew nothing of the board, and was struck by a strong smell of incense; two other nuns (Marie de l'Enfant-Jésus, Thérèse de l'Eucharistie) smelled it too. The prioress and Céline, though warned, smelled nothing. Céline compared it with the authentic boards and found it "absolutely identical"; the portresses later learned a workman had hidden it in the brushwood to take it back; Dr La Néele recognised it as the board from the head of the coffin. Céline named two "most remarkable" facts: the cure of furunculosis of Sr Marie-Madeleine during a novena with a veil believed (not certain) to have been Thérèse's, and Sr Jeanne-Marie's empty reservoir found full when she had poured barely a quarter. Céline's deposition ended in session 35 (28 Sep 1910, f. [415v]); she signed "Sister Geneviève of Saint Teresa, R.C.I." Isabelle told the reservoir story in 1911 as: the stove tank holds four and a half jugs; Marie-Madeleine had emptied it; Jeanne-Marie emptied the little left, took the second jug, and as she began to pour the tank was full; "in 1910, about Lent, I think". Marie-Madeleine (PO 18) says summer of 1910 and four jugs; only about one jug had been poured in both.
- Cards: F-EW-2485, F-EW-2486, F-EW-2487, F-EW-2488, F-EW-2489, F-EW-2490, F-EW-2491, F-EW-2507
- Best details: "A surprise: a sister of Thérèse who was a sceptic about the perfumes" (F-EW-2486). ### E-P7-068 — 6 Sep 1910, 9–10 Aug 1917, 26 Mar 1923 — Three openings of the grave

### E-P7-069 — 1910 and 1917 — The Carmel's detailed accounts of the two exhumations and the doctors' report

### E-P7-078 — 20–21 Feb 1911 — Isabelle's deposition: the world's letters
- What happened: Isabelle never met Thérèse. She said she had no human motive. She would not testify about Thérèse's life; instead she made a special study of the many letters that reached the Carmel to establish her reputation worldwide, and would say what she saw of her influence in the monastery and herself. She hoped the beatification, once offered to the universal Church, would call Thérèse to save a multitude of souls, lead many into the highest ways of divine love, and renew the clergy, whom she protects "in a very particular manner". She copied the best letters, numbered them, and asked the tribunal to file the copy; the notaries Eucher Deslandes and Charles Marie checked them against the originals (f. 652r–1064r), in 28 groups; about 379 numbered letters. Letters from 1898 (nos. 1–4) call her « une âme angélique », « une âme séraphique », « petite sainte », and ask for relics, prayers and novenas. Orders claimed her: « Elle est ma petite Sainte » (nos. 18–30: Benedictines of Argentan, Good Shepherd, Augustinians of Auteuil, Poor Clares of Perpignan, Visitation of Boulogne, Sisters of Saint-Joseph of Laval, Thérèse Durnerin of the Amis des Pauvres). Seminarians said she lit and changed them (nos. 64–68; professors nos. 81, 89). About 1906 the major seminary of Nancy petitioned Mgr Amette to submit the Cause, two years before Lemonnier acted. She cited an article by the Marquise d'Auray (letter 378), and a bishop of Cuenca (Ecuador, "Polite [sic]") who wished to see her beatified in the preface to the Paris Carmelites' translation of St Teresa's works. She found Thérèse's thoughts "on almost every page" of the life of Elisabeth of the Trinity of Dijon. The Florence Carmel of Santa Maria Maddalena de' Pazzi made one of two Italian translations and sent copies to Italian bishops; Pius X wrote on 1 Nov 1910: the book gave "the sweetest fruit of joy" and the Lisieux virgin's soul "almost breathes" in it, urging the nuns « ne imitari pigeat quam celebrare delectat ». ### E-P7-110 — 17–18 May 1925 — The canonisation formula, the bull and the next day's audience
- What happened: After the triple petition by consistorial advocate Virginio Jacoucci and replies by Nicola Sebastiani, Secretary of Briefs, Pius XI, seated, declared her a saint and ordered that "the universal Church" keep her memory each year on her birthday into heaven, 30 September, "among the holy Virgins not Martyrs" (AAS 17 pp. 209–211). ## Part 3 — Testimony about earlier events

These cards are dated by the year of the deposition (1898 or later) but describe earlier periods. They are grouped by the period they describe, so the outline can use them there. Each item names its cards.

### 1877–1886 — childhood, family and school (the world before Carmel)
- T-P7-001 — Victoire Pasquer, the Martins' former servant, said to Pauline (parlour, months before Aug 1910) that Miss Thérèse was not ordinary: she had something none of the other sisters had, "like an angel". People in the street looked at the child in an unusual way (F-EW-1854). « Thérèse avait quelque chose que vous n'aviez ni les unes ni les autres: c'était comme un ange ». Also an elderly lady of Saint-Pierre parish who watched children at processions predicted Thérèse would be a saint if she lived (f. 240r–v). Witness: Pauline heard Victoire; medium (memory 25 years later).
- T-P7-002 — Marie's written note (1909) compares the 1883 illness to Job's trial and refers to "ma déposition"; suggests she had drafted her deposition by 1909 (F-EW-1852).
- T-P7-003 — Dumaine's date slips: Mme Martin died "in 1887" (should be 1877); older sisters "about 12 and 13" at Thérèse's birth (F-EW-1866).
- T-P7-004 — Céline (1915): as a child Thérèse charmed those who saw her; other children were as pretty, but her look had something Céline never saw elsewhere; people said "she had heaven in her eyes"; Céline called her "an angel incarnate". But Article 216 is exaggerated: « jamais il n'y a eu d'"odeurs célestes" émanant d'elle ». Correction of the Roman Articles (F-EW-1963).
- T-P7-005 — Two Benedictine nuns (PO-29, PO-30) said that as a pupil Thérèse had excessive sensitivity that made her grieve beyond measure; one or two suspected self-love: « ces chagrins faciles témoignaient d'un fond d'amour-propre ». Sr Saint-François says "many" took her sensitivity for self-love but saw a supernatural fear of giving bad example; they changed after reading her writings (F-EW-1918). Witnesses: Sr Saint-André, Sr Saint-François de Sales; high (two witnesses).
- T-P7-006 — Pichon (1915): apart from the Virgin's appearance ending the 1883 illness, he knew of no mystical states; he was told of the cure by the sisters' letters at the time (PA-03 [264]) and judged the illness "a nervous affection, very strange" that left no trace; Jeanne Guérin and Marcelline knew of no apparition at the time (F-EW-1966).
- T-P7-007 — Marie and Céline's corrections of family facts: Zélie Guérin born at Saint-Denis, not Gandelain; father's first name; Thérèse home from the wet-nurse 2 Apr 1874, not March (F-EW-1957, F-EW-1961). Dumaine's slips (see T-P7-003).
- T-P7-008 — Marcelline: Pauline was formed by her aunt (a Visitation nun) and her parents' example and formed Thérèse seriously "in learning and virtue"; Dumaine notes one sister became a Visitandine at Caen (Léonie) and three were Carmelites (F-EW-1968).
- T-P7-009 — Céline (1910): lived with Thérèse except 1888–94; Thérèse became holy "in a common way, without anything extraordinary or prodigious" (F-EW-1906).
- T-P7-010 — Céline at the Apostolic Process (1915–17): the years 1877–1886 were "a period of darkness… a veil thrown over those qualities". In the world Thérèse "passed unnoticed". She was excessively timid, hardly ever defended herself, had continual headaches, and cried at the least thing "and when she had been consoled, she cried for having cried". Céline never heard a sharp word from her (F-LF-126; LAV p.118–119; high for what Céline said).

### 1888–1897 — Carmel life and character (about Thérèse herself)
- T-P7-011 — Sr Thérèse de Jésus (letter, 9 Nov 1907): a good child, easy character, imperfections "like everyone"; a plain day, "never overloaded"; adulated by sisters and prioress; the sisters treated her as a "phoenix"; her cut hair kept like relics (F-EW-1833 to F-EW-1837). See E-P7-046.
- T-P7-012 — Sr Saint-Stanislas's sealed note (1906): she worked several years in the same offices as Thérèse, saw heroic virtue, never an imperfection, never a complaint, and admired her silent suffering over her father (F-EW-1825). Sr Saint-Stanislas was first infirmarian in 1897.
- T-P7-013 — Marie-Madeleine and Agnès on the novice-mistress arrangement, 1893–97: Thérèse unofficial mistress; Gonzague named "to have peace" or "by propriety" (F-EW-1890).
- T-P7-014 — Pauline on Thérèse's writing: "what do you want me to write that you don't already know?" (F-EW-1855, about Ms A–C, 1895–97).
- T-P7-015 — Aimée said, on the Cause, "what had she done?" and thought the sisters had pushed the Cause (F-EW-1827): a comment on Thérèse's life, not testimony of events.
- T-P7-016 — Céline (1910): Thérèse's only "fault" in charity was severity with novices, "a holy anger" without loss of self-control; "let nothing pass" (F-EW-1907).
- T-P7-017 — Céline (1915): her conformity to God's will showed on her face; she was always gracious and gay, so those not close to her could think she followed a sweet way; readers miss "the cross carefully hidden under the flowers" (F-EW-1962).
- T-P7-018 — Marie (1915): she knew by her own observation everything Thérèse wrote, except the trials against faith, which she learned only by reading the manuscript. Shows how hidden the trial was even from her godmother in the same house (F-EW-1956).
- T-P7-019 — Marie de la Trinité's circular: her father, Victor Castel, was a close helper of abbé Roussel, founder of the Auteuil orphan-apprentices; the circular says Thérèse heard of Auteuil through this family and prayed for it. He died in 1912 saying he was "in the company of little Sister Thérèse" (F-EW-1944). Medium.
- T-P7-020 — Marie du Sacré-Cœur (via MT, c. 1916): she wanted Thérèse's company, but « par fidélité, elle ne voulait pas me parler » (F-EW-1985). See F-EW-943 (Thérèse seemed to prefer other nuns).
- T-P7-021 — MT (1916): the Act of Offering was composed June 1895 and shown to her at the end of Nov 1895; Thérèse said God would not inspire the request to remain in her as in the tabernacle unless he meant to grant it (F-EW-1991).
- T-P7-022 — Pauline (1926, to Dolan): the sisters saw a perfect religious, "infinitely sweet and gentle"; she began recording her words when Thérèse said "My work will begin after my death" (F-EW-2031). High.
- T-P7-023 — Marie des Anges's notes: Thérèse was with her "nearly 4 years" in the novitiate (editors: she is mistaken); Marie des Anges was novice mistress 1886?–1891 (F-EW-1973).
- T-P7-024 — Mme Tifenne (1926) on Thérèse's looks in 1888: the pictures flatter her; arresting placid expression, large "mystic" eyes (F-EW-2000). Medium; one witness.
- T-P7-025 — Marie des Anges, circular: Thérèse called her "a true saint, the finished type of the first Carmelites" (Ms A phrase) (F-EW-1999).
- T-P7-026 — Pichon (26 Jan 1911): three features of her way: spirit of faith from childhood; trust and love, with no "sulking humility"; a "cult" of suffering and the cross far above her age (F-EW-2540; PO 10 [553r]). See E-P7-075.
- T-P7-027 — Giannattasio (1911): named kindness to her neighbour, patience with others' faults, steady piety; knew this only from the Life (F-EW-2452). Léonie: Thérèse was "a ravishing child" whom she loved as a small sister (F-EW-2510).
- T-P7-028 — Léonie (1915): saw Thérèse only from time to time at the parlour in the Carmel years; was not at the negotiations at Bayeux and Rome about her entry; Thérèse never confided her vocation thoughts to her (F-EW-2509).
- T-P7-029 — TSA (circular, 1929): she admired Céline's portraits of Thérèse; « Will one ever be able to render the angelic air of our holy little Thérèse! That heavenly look! » (F-EW-2554). See also T-P7-024.
- T-P7-030 — Piat: Thérèse prepared Marie Guérin for her veiling by a talk that Marie kept as "Ultima verba", Thérèse's spiritual testament (F-EW-2642). Marie's mother's last instruction to Pauline about Marie: "watch that Marie does not become proud!" (F-EW-2651).

### 1866–1904 — Mère Marie de Gonzague (about her prioress years and character)
- T-P7-031 — Godefroy on Gonzague's character (F-EW-1813). Asked by the judge, he said she seemed to have particularly sound judgment, wanted the good of her community, and seemed excellent in her outside relations. He could not judge her inside the cloister. Her many re-elections suggested the nuns approved. Evidence: Godefroy from letters and parlour talks; medium, admittedly external view. Contrast with Taylor's "colder soul" (E-P7-034).
- T-P7-032 — Gonzague and Mère Agnès "made each other suffer" (F-EW-1814). Gonzague confided to Godefroy that her character and Mère Agnès's did not naturally get on, and that they made each other suffer despite sincere mutual esteem. Original: « se faisaient souffrir l'une l'autre ». Said without bitterness. A rare admission from Gonzague's side; high confidence.
- T-P7-033 — Gonzague's Calvary in the cloister garth (F-EW-1819), where Thérèse so often "stripped her roses"; TSA also says Thérèse liked stripping roses "at the Calvary" (F-EW-1154). This gives a place for a known scene.
- T-P7-034 — Agnès at the Apostolic Process (1915): Superior Cagniard put Gonzague into offices straight after her novitiate, hoping to cure her "bizarre temper"; « Ce fut une fatale erreur ». Sr Cécile objects that Agnès was five years old in 1866. Hearsay; medium (F-EW-1975). Laveille (Summarium 1919 p. 498 § 1345) says Gonzague's "changeable character" and faults of government were seen by Thérèse more clearly than by others; Thérèse defended her authority and tried to console her when sisters silently reproached her (F-EW-2043; medium).
- T-P7-035 — Fr Madelaine (Apostolic Process p. 739): Gonzague confided that to exercise Thérèse's virtue she "had studiously sought to try her by affecting towards her a certain indifference and severity", knew it was painful, and saw no deviation from perfect obedience. Laveille: the treatment lasted "long years", scarcely eased even in her last illness, yet Gonzague called Thérèse "the best of the best; an angel". In 1904 she said "I have my little Thérèse to intercede for me. I am sure that I shall owe my salvation to her." (F-LF-193). Medium — second-hand report of a prioress's own explanation, after the fact.
