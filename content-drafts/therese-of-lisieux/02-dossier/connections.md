# Connections to other saints — merged (stage 2b)

This file collects every card and note about connections to other saints, from the four parts. It has four blocks: a lookup by person, the index cards written by the life-family and own-writings parts, the connection cards of official-miracles, and the connection notes found on eyewitness and life-family cards. Cards are copied without change except that only the claim, source, notes and a few labels are kept. Card numbers point to each part's `facts.md`. Canonization years that lie outside the sources are marked 'check'.

## Counts

- Life-family: index list (14 entries) and 13 cards with a connection note.
- Own-writings: 1 index card (F-OW-584) covering more than a dozen saints and holy persons.
- Official-miracles: 6 connection cards (F-OM-126 to F-OM-131).
- Eyewitness: 18 cards with a connection note (8 about saints, 10 about other Church figures or links).
- Cards that only mention saints inside their claims, without a connection note, are not listed. A later stage can search the facts files for a name.

## Connection cards of the official-miracles part

### F-OM-126 — Connection: Joan of Arc (patroness of France, and a cause "saved")
- Claim: In 1944 Pius XII made her secondary patroness of France alongside Joan of Arc (F-OM-091). Cardinal Touchet credited her with saving Joan's cause from interruption under Pius X (F-OM-062). Père Perroy set her "between Joan of Arc and Margaret Mary" (F-OM-106).
- Source: AAS 36 pp. 329–330; Laveille PDF 433, 436
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high for the 1944 act; low–medium for the Touchet claim.
- Notes: Connection card. Joan was canonized in 1920, five years before her.

### F-OM-127 — Connection: Francis Xavier (equal patron of missions) and Théophane Vénard
- Claim: The 1927 decree makes her patroness of all missions "equal in rank" with Francis Xavier (F-OM-087). Taylor says Maryknoll pushed vocations in 1926–27 "under the united patronage of the Little Flower and Blessed Theophane Venard", and that Fr Roulland met Taylor in the rue du Bac seminary where Vénard's relics lie.
- Source: AAS 20 pp. 147–148; Taylor 1926/30 PDF 452 and note
- New to English readers: yes (the Maryknoll pairing)
- Confidence: medium.
- Notes: Connection card. Vénard was blessed in 1909 (canonized 1988, outside these sources).

### F-OM-128 — Connection: Teresa of Avila and John of the Cross
- Claim: Taylor says she was the first daughter of St Teresa canonized "after three centuries and a half" (F-OM-075). Taylor prints her motto, taken from St John of the Cross: "Love is repaid by love alone" (Taylor 1926/30 PDF 318). Benedict XVI names John of the Cross among the saints he cites (F-OM-125).
- Source: Taylor 1926/30 PDF 20, 318
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium.
- Notes: Connection card. See F-OM-075 on the Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi qualification.

### F-OM-129 — Connection: Raphael Kalinowski, the Polish Carmelite (canonized in 1991)
- Claim: Fr Raphael of St Joseph (Kalinowski), Vicar Provincial at Wadowice, wrote in 1902 that her poem "To live by Love" calmed him in a night of trouble, after he had once said Polish could not carry her style (M-OM-051; W-OM-006). Taylor's note says he "died in the odour of sanctity in 1907", cause submitted.
- Source: Taylor 1924 PDF 393–394
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium.
- Notes: Connection card. His canonization in 1991 is outside these sources; check before the writer states it.

### F-OM-130 — Connection: Almire Pichon SJ and the Martin parents
- Claim: Pichon, the Jesuit who directed her, testified in the apostolic process on 23 Apr 1915 (F-OM-110). Her parents were beatified together on 19 Oct 2008 (Benedict XVI, F-OM-125).
- Source: Carmel witness page; Benedict XVI audience

### F-OM-131 — Connection: Cardinal Vico, papal legate, and the "guiding star" of two popes

## Cards with a connection note — eyewitness, about saints

### F-EW-2450 — "A new way" of holiness: Francis of Assisi, Francis de Sales, Teresa
- Claim: The bishop judged her holiness "among the most eminent in the Church" and "characteristic": in Providence's plan it traces a new way to perfection. Asked what "new" means, he said it is like a man of genius who gives a new way to grasp an unchanging truth. He named St Francis of Assisi, St Francis de Sales and St Teresa as "typical" saints who lit up one virtue of the Gospel by their example, not only their teaching. In his view Thérèse became a "new exemplary cause" of holiness. The one authoritative cause of all holiness stays the same.
- When / where: 21 Aug 1911, session 86
- People: Giannattasio; Francis of Assisi; Francis de Sales; Teresa of Avila
- Source: PO 33, [1311v]–[1312r], W1
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high
- Notes: Useful for the "connections to other saints" and "what set her apart" scope items. It is a bishop's theological opinion from 1911.

### F-EW-2457 — The apparition's caress and the first guess that it was St Teresa
- Claim: In the account the prioress gave, the visitor put her hand on the prioress's head and set the folds of her veil straight "as a caress". The prioress first thought she saw St Teresa, the foundress, and cried "O Santa Madre". The visitor answered: "I am not the Santa Madre; I am the Servant of God Sister Thérèse of Lisieux. Today is a feast in heaven and on earth." The witness adds that it was the feast of the Holy Name of Jesus (16 Jan 1910).
- When / where: 16 Jan 1910, Carmel of Gallipoli
- People: Mother Carmela; Thérèse (as reported); Teresa of Avila
- Source: PO 33, [1315v]–[1316r], W1
- New to English readers: partial — the story is known; the veil caress and the feast day detail come from this deposition
- Confidence: medium — second-hand
- Notes: Liturgy and connection-to-saints item.

### F-EW-2528 — Saints that Marie des Anges compared her with: Mechtild, Gertrude, Margaret-Mary

She quoted words of Jesus to St Mechtild (that it pleases him when men expect great things of him) as an explanation of the wonders Thérèse works. She also quoted a priest of Argentina (Barréro, 1914) who called the posthumous apostolate a renewed Pentecost.

### F-EW-2533 — Life of St Jane de Chantal read in the refectory (Dec 1893)
- Claim: Léonie quoted a letter of 27 Dec 1893 in which Thérèse said the community read the Life of Sainte Chantal in the refectory. She saw in it "the intimate union that has always existed between the Visitation and the Carmel", and blessed God for choosing these two orders for their family. She asked Léonie to pray for her during the month of the Child Jesus and to ask him "that I remain always small, quite small".
- When / where: 27 Dec 1893, Lisieux Carmel (letter LT 154)
- People: Thérèse; Léonie; Jane Frances de Chantal
- Source: PO 7, [495v] and [496r], session 43, W1
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: high that the witness quotes it
- Notes: Connections to other saints: Jane de Chantal, and (by the Visitation link) Francis de Sales. Léonie's religious name at the Visitation was Françoise-Thérèse (after Francis de Sales and Thérèse's own name).

### F-EW-2584 — Sr Marie-Dosithée's letters to Isidore: 'I go to God as to my Father' (1870s)
- Claim: Piat quotes letters of the Le Mans Visitandine Sr Marie-Dosithée (Isidore's sister) that he says already contain traits of the Little Way: 'I go to God just as I would to my Father, even with much more trust and abandonment'; 'God loves with an extremely tender love those who abandon themselves to him, and a mother does not have as much tenderness for her small child as the Lord has for the abandoned soul'; 'Our Lord wants no galley slaves in his service.' She sent him the Introduction to the Devout Life and the twelve volumes of L'Année sainte de la Visitation. For his 1875 pilgrimage to Lourdes she wrote that one need not be worthy to receive a miracle: 'the most miserable but with most trust' are heard. Her motto for him: 'I will do God's business and he will do mine.' Before dying she wrote a testament: God alone will be your reward; in prosperity keep your taste and household modest; share your abundance with the poor. Piat sums it up as 'spin the thread of little virtues', the line of the Doctor of Geneva.
- When / where: 1860s–1877, Le Mans / Lisieux
- People: Sr Marie-Dosithée (Élise Guérin); Isidore Guérin; Francis de Sales
- Source: Extract from Fr Stéphane-Joseph Piat OFM, biography of Marie Guérin (Office Central de Lisieux, 1967), on the Carmel site, https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-marie-de-leucharistie/biographie-de-soeur-marie-de-leucharistie/ — section 'À la pharmacie Guérin'; rights: facts-only
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: medium-high that the quotations come from letters; 'anticipating the Little Way' is Piat's view
- Notes: Connection to another saint: Francis de Sales (Salesian spirituality of the Visitation in Thérèse's own family). See M-EW-002 for Marie-Dosithée's recovery.

### F-EW-2666 — Thérèse's last 'pious recreation' for Sr Saint-Stanislas's golden jubilee (8 Feb 1897)
- Claim: The biography says the community celebrated Sr Saint-Stanislas's fifty years of clothing (15 Jan 1896), with verses by Thérèse ('Loved by all our hearts as a gentle gift of Heaven'). For her golden jubilee Thérèse composed her eighth and last 'pious recreation', 'Saint-Stanislas Kostka' (8 Feb 1897), a short play acted by the novices, in which she gives the young Jesuit saint her own feelings: a desire so great he could not be happy in Heaven unless it is realised: to work still for the salvation of souls in Paradise and return to earth to protect holy souls whose long career on earth will complete his own.
- When / where: 15 Jan 1896; 8 Feb 1897, Lisieux Carmel
- People: Sr Saint-Stanislas; Thérèse; the novices; Stanislaus Kostka
- Source: Biography of Sr Saint-Stanislas, section 'Avec Thérèse', https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/au-carmel-du-temps-de-therese/la-communaute/soeur-stanislas-des-sacres-coeurs/biographie-de-soeur-stanislas-des-sacres-coeurs/; rights: facts-only
- New to English readers: yes
- Confidence: high on the title and date (RP 8 given in the text)
- Notes: Connection to another saint: Stanislaus Kostka. The desire 'to come back to earth' is the source of the 'I will spend my heaven doing good' idea, expressed here through a saint in a play. See works.md for the eight pious recreations.

### F-EW-2517 — Benedict XV, as Archbishop of Bologna, on the little way (17 May 1913)
- Claim: Léonie cited a letter of 17 May 1913 written by the Archbishop of Bologna (later Pope Benedict XV, whom she calls "our Holy Father") on an Italian edition of the Life. In it, he said Thérèse seemed to want to persuade us of the ease of reaching Christian perfection, hence "her way of spiritual childhood". Nothing should be easier than childlike trust or total abandon in Jesus' arms. He hoped her example would help the faithful of his diocese, since by "holy simplicity" she reached the heights of perfection.
- When / where: 17 May 1913, Bologna; cited 14 Sep 1915
- People: Giacomo Della Chiesa (Benedict XV); Léonie (citing)
- Source: PA 11, [937]–[938], W2
- New to English readers: partial
- Confidence: medium — the letter text comes second-hand through Léonie
- Notes: Connection: a future pope who read her early. Benedict XV was pope when Léonie testified (1915).

- Notes: Connection: two popes. The edition's note (introduction) also picks up the Benedict XV line. See F-EW-1861 for other fame evidence in the same deposition.

### F-EW-2596 — Henry Chéron: the anticlerical who knew Thérèse as a child, and later built the road to the Basilica
- Claim: Piat's footnote says Chéron, later a mayor, deputy and minister, dropped his sectarianism and kept real esteem for Isidore. When Thérèse's star rose, he liked to recall the days when he met her with her cousin in the pharmacy on the Place Saint-Pierre. His goodwill to the Martin family and the Carmel never wavered. As mayor of Lisieux he eased the great events in her honour and the development of pilgrimages, and was responsible for the triumphal avenue leading to the Basilica. ### F-EW-2660 — The Guérin family and the Holy Face: cathedral lamp, family prayer, and the Shroud of Turin

### F-LF-173 — Paris to Milan: nobles, cards, and "pharisees"

### F-LF-182 — Home by Assisi and Florence
- Claim: The party left Rome on 24 Nov at 6 a.m. At an early stop Mgr Legoux came to their carriage and asked, "Well, how is our little Carmelite?" She saw that all the pilgrims knew her secret.
