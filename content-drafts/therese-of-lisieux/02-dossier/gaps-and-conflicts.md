# Gaps and conflicts — merged (stage 2b)

This file merges the four `parts/*/gaps-and-conflicts.md` files. The first section lists the conflicts and cautions that most affect the outline. The four full lists follow, unchanged, one part after another. Their headings sit lower. Card numbers point to each part's own files.

## What the outline stage must know first

1. **Pauline is both witness and editor.** She admits that Histoire d'une âme was changed (F-EW-1897). She says Thérèse gave her blanket permission to edit, but only she reports it (F-EW-1423). Every Thérèse quotation from the 1898 text is the edited text (F-LF-241). For exact words, use the manuscripts (own-writings part), not Laveille or Taylor.
2. **The last words rest on Mère Agnès's later notebook.** She wrote the Carnet jaune out in 1921–1923 from a destroyed notebook of 1904–1905. Four versions exist (306, 275, 362 and 714 sayings). She scratched out the end of a saying on 16 Jul 1897. Her own notebook-keeping tired Thérèse (F-EW-1581).
3. **The cure date.** The standard date is 13 May 1883 (Pentecost). Pauline gives 10 May. In Ms A 86r Thérèse writes 1889. Marie reports that Thérèse said she wept because she could no longer see the Virgin (Q-EW-039); Léonie saw nothing.
4. **The first blood.** Marie gives Holy Thursday 1896. Céline gives Good Friday and Holy Thursday in different places, and a wrong date. Life-family gives the night of 2–3 April 1896.
5. **The death.** Marie gives 7.15 p.m., Céline 7.20 p.m. Céline reports words during the agony that Marie does not. Céline says the last look was for her; Thérèse had promised it to the prioress. Pauline gives two versions of the moment of death and of the body (supple, or signs of decomposition).
6. **The funeral.** Marie says the crowd was numerous; Céline says the procession of faithful was very small. See scene 22 in scenes.md.
7. **Louis Martin.** Do not write 'a Home'. The Carmel names the Bon-Sauveur at Caen, 12 Feb 1889 – 10 May 1892 (F-LF-198, F-LF-210). Céline doubted the committal. Ms A never names the asylum. The meaning of the veiled figure was understood in Carmel (Marie) or only after his death (Céline). Do not say the town blamed the daughters' vocations for the illness (F-LF-265).
8. **The Taxil hoax.** Pauline says Thérèse saw through it before the exposure (F-EW-992). She does not mention that Thérèse wrote 'Diana Vaughan' into a play as a new Joan of Arc, or that her photograph was used at the press conference of 19 April 1897. The photograph shows Margaret or Catherine (sources differ).
9. **Mère Marie de Gonzague.** Ms C praises her care. The sisters' testimony reports jealousy, 'frightful scenes', refusal of Dr La Néele and of morphine, and neglect. The editors warn that pages 649 and 741 of Céline's deposition must be checked against Guy Gaucher. Gonzague herself named Céline infirmary aide and wept before a child portrait of Thérèse.
10. **Pichon's claim about 'my heaven'.** He says the teenage Thérèse told him 'I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth' several times before Carmel. The editors mark it '? ? ?'. The saying is dated 17 July 1897. His dates are also unreliable.
11. **Shaped testimony.** Marie swears that no one influenced her, yet a letter of 13 March 1915 says the sisters would 'set right' the Roman advocate's portrait. Céline's deposition was prepared from notes. The judges asked leading questions about neuropathic temperament. Both sisters know of no opinion against her holiness, yet report hostile nuns.
12. **The 1883 illness.** The 1910 and 1917 accounts of Marcelline Husé differ in strength. Jeanne La Néele gives the most physical detail. The brief of 1923 says her family believed the illness came from the devil (F-OM-004, F-OM-132).
13. **The 1911 edition of the letters.** It uses composite letters, wrong dates and added sentences, and cuts humour, family conflict, money, and class remarks. Example: the farewell letters of 9 June and 13 July 1897 are moved to 26 July and 14 August (F-OW-498).
14. **After-death sources.** Open items: the coffin of 1923 (mahogany with silver handles, or oak chest in a rosewood coffin lined with lead); Mrs Dorans's night (1909, not 1910); the day of Charles Anne's cure; Sœur Louise's cure (22 or 25 Sep); crowd counts at the canonization; the cry 'Sanata sum!' (no locator); the 1917 exhumation account (from a summary). Status of Gallipoli: investigated, not approved.
15. **Facts that rest on one source.** French Wikipedia claims used alone (flagged in life-family cards): Louis's gifts of 10,000 francs; Marie de Gonzague's wish to destroy the autographs; Isidore's sale of the pharmacy; the dates of Léonie's cause. Life-family lists 32 small date conflicts in a table (birth and death dates, houses, counts).
16. **Silences in her letters (LT 110–181).** They do not mention the Offering to Merciful Love, the Joan of Arc plays, Bellière, or the influenza of 1891–92. Do not state Pichon's Canada plan from these letters. Do not link 'iron constitution' (1894) to her later illness.
17. **Rights.** The Carmel transcriptions are 'unknown'. Taylor 1926/30 has an imprimatur of 1936, so its rights are 'unknown' until first publication is confirmed; facts there rest on the 1924 printing where possible. Downloads that need approval are listed in each part; none was made.
18. **Not read.** Each part lists sources it did not reach; see the four lists below.


## Part own-writings (F-OW)

### Original title: Gaps and conflicts — own writings

#### 1. Pauline's edits: the manuscripts against *Histoire d'une âme* (1898/1911)

Method: phrase search of the 1911 French edition (local D1a, Gutenberg #36708), which Taylor translated in 1912. "Cut" means the phrase is absent. Findings so far (Ms A mainly):

- **Re-addressing.** Ms A was written to Mère Agnès (Pauline) and Ms C to Mère Marie de Gonzague. The edited text presents the whole as addressed to "ma Mère" = Mère Marie de Gonzague, and rewrites Ms A 70v (the prioress "very severe, without her knowing it") as "that I should be treated by you very severely" (HA 1911 ch. VII).
- **Inserted material.** HA 1911 ch. VII adds, in Thérèse's first person, the cobweb reproach («On voit bien que nos cloîtres sont balayés par une enfant de quinze ans!») and a passage on being scolded in direction for slowness. **Neither is in Ms A or Ms C as transcribed.** (Source of the insertion: probably Mère Agnès's own memory/testimony; check other parts.)
- **Softened words.** Christmas 1886: her father's «Enfin, heureusement que c'est la dernière année!...» becomes a gentle sentence (F-OW-059). 19th birthday «fêté par une mort» becomes «attristé par la mort» (Ms A 79r). 12 Feb 1889 «la plus humiliante de toutes les coupes» loses "humiliating" (F-OW-087).
- **Cut altogether (sample):** tantrums («furies épouvantables», F-OW-004); «je suis toquée» (F-OW-005); «Victoire, vous êtes une mioche» (F-OW-021); the bucket and fireplace (F-OW-022); her father's fear she would "go mad" and the «rang d'oignons» line (F-OW-038); the scruple of having faked the illness (F-OW-042); Mme Papinau's cat and names (F-OW-053); «écriture de chat» (F-OW-049); the Bologna students (F-OW-072); women "excommunicated" in Italy (F-OW-079); Céline's boat «Je dors mais mon coeur veille» (F-OW-080); sleeping at prayer "for 7 years" (F-OW-094); "wondering whether there was a Heaven" in 1891 (F-OW-097); Sr Aimée de Jésus's objection "three of us are already too many" (F-OW-108).
- **Names removed:** Leriche, Ducellier, Grogny/Mme Monnier, Papinau/Cochain, "Sr A. de J.".
- **Added permission:** the 1911 text says she asked permission to touch the nail of the Cross; the manuscript has none and calls herself "too bold" (F-OW-079).
- The letters reviewer (LT 110–181) found the same pattern in the letters: composite letters, wrong dates, added sentences, and cuts of humour, family conflict, class remarks, food and money (see section 5).
- Not yet checked: Ms B and Ms C against the 1911 text, systematically. The rosary confession (Ms C 25v) needs a check.

#### 2. Reliability of the last words

- The Carnet jaune as we have it was written out by Mère Agnès in 1921–1923, from a "Gros carnet noir" of 1904–1905 (destroyed), itself from loose sheets (one survives). Four versions exist (1909: 306 sayings; 1910: 275; Novissima Verba 1927: 362; Carnet jaune: 714). Langlois (2000; essay on the Carmel site) judges most sayings unrewritten but warns about "strategic" sayings (her mission after death; her authority to publish). F-OW-223 (reliability card), F-OW-148 (roses).
- Direct evidence of editing: on 16 Jul 1897 Mère Agnès scratched out the rest of a saying ("Mère Agnès a gratté la suite !", Carmel note). See card "A saying scratched out by Mère Agnès".
- Mère Agnès's appendix "Remarque importante" claims Thérèse gave her special authority ("you alone know all the folds of my little soul", 16 Jul 1897).
- The most famous saying, "I want to spend my heaven doing good on earth" (17 Jul 1897), is **not yet carded** in this part (the notebook for 16–31 July remains to be carded; see HANDOFF).
- Last words: Mère Agnès and Sr Geneviève agree in substance («Oh ! je l'aime !... Mon Dieu... je vous aime...»). Céline has "le vase" where Agnès has "le calice", and adds «Je suis... je suis réduite...». Céline says Thérèse's last look was for her, though Thérèse had promised it to the prioress.

#### 3. Conflicts and tensions to record

- **Prioress and illness.** Ms C 27v praises Mère Marie de Gonzague's care ("no remedy seems too expensive"). The last-words records show: the 1896 haemoptysis hidden from Mère Agnès for over a year (by permission of the prioress); Thérèse's view that sisters "tried to influence Our Mother" about her duties (F-OW, 12 Aug); an infirmarian leaving door and window open; "no one thought to support her" on 2 Jul 1897; sisters who thought she was not really ill. Later accusations of neglect belong to other parts; record both sides.
- **Mère Marie de Gonzague and Mère Agnès.** The prioress forbade Thérèse for nearly two years to tell Mère Agnès about Fr Roulland (CJ May 1897).
- **Fr Pichon.** Ms A 70r praises him; CJ 4.7.4: "Fr Pichon treated me too much like a child."
- **Opposition to her entry at 15:** uncle Guérin (F-OW-065), Canon Delatroëtte (F-OW-066), the bishop's caution (F-OW-069), Révérony at the audience (F-OW-076), Mère Marie de Gonzague's delay to after Lent (F-OW-081); later the superior delayed profession "too young" (letters).
- **Opposition to Céline's entry (1894):** Sr Aimée de Jésus (F-OW-108); the La Néeles and a priest-director (letters, F-OW-183 area).
- **Dates.** Ms A 86r: Thérèse wrote "1889" for the smile of the Virgin (Carmel corrects to 1883). The flower anniversary: Ms A says Pentecost (29 May 1887); CJ 7.6.2 says "10 years today" on 7 Jun 1897 (Pentecost Monday 1897 by the liturgical calendar — check). LT 169 vs LT 170 count years since her father's collapse differently (5 vs 6).
- **Identifications made by editors, not by Thérèse:** the fellow novice of 1892 (Sr Marthe); the sister who "displeased me in everything" (Sr Thérèse de Saint-Augustin); "Sr A. de J." (Sr Aimée de Jésus); the preacher of 1891 (Fr Alexis Prou); the superior (Delatroëtte). Keep them labelled.

#### 4. Gaps

- **Silence on her father's illness.** Ms A never names the asylum or the diagnosis ("our bitterness of June, and above all of the 24th, 1888"; "the most humiliating of cups"). The only direct mention found is Céline's record of a joke: "the 'Bon Sauveur' will come and get you" (July 1897).
- **Silence on the Bologna "little adventure" with a student** (Ms A 59v): she does not say what happened.
- **No source found in her own writings** for: the Diana Vaughan / Léo Taxil hoax (1896–97) — check RP 7 and letters LT 182–266; the date Ms A was handed over (20 Jan 1896 is standard, not in her text); Céline's photographs of her (only 30 Aug 1897 and the "envelope" remark).
- **Carmel site transcription gaps:** Ms C 8v and 26v empty, 27r–27v start mid-sentence; Marie du Sacré-Cœur's notebook pages 8–12 empty; Ms A 59v duplicated.
- **Not read in this part** (see HANDOFF.md): letters LT 1–109 and LT 182–266; Carnet jaune 16–31 July 1897 (partly read, not carded after 16 Jul); the poems, plays and prayers (only titles and dates listed); school notebooks, images, "écrits divers".

#### 5. From the letter review (LT 110–181)

- **No letters Nov 1891 – Apr 1892.** This file has nothing on the influenza epidemic (winter 1891–92) or on Mère Geneviève's death (Dec 1891), except that LT 134 (Apr 1892) calls her "notre Sainte Mère Geneviève" and sends a flower she held as a "relic".
- **Father's return (1892) and death (29 July 1894).** Only LT 138 (Nov 1892) mentions the return ("rappelant de son exil"). There is no letter between 18 July and 5–10 Aug 1894. The first letters about his death are LT 169 (19 Aug) and LT 170 (20 Aug). The time counts conflict: LT 169 says "after a death of 5 years" (from his 1889 collapse); LT 170 says "after 6 years of absence" (from her 1888 entry). Both can be true on their own terms.
- **Céline's entry (14 Sept 1894).** No letter in the file records the day. LT 169 is "the last time" she writes to Céline "in the world". LT 174 (Jan 1895) gives her religious name.
- **Fr Pichon's Canada plan.** It is not named anywhere in LT 110–181. LT 168 refers only to "le Père", whom Marie says "was wrong", and to waiting for "his letter". LT 120 quotes "le Père exilé" (1890). Do not state the Canada plan from these letters.
- **Céline's ball / social life.** Not described. LT 149 ("voix amies" warnings; "fleurs du chemin") and LT 167 P.S. ("ce que tu ressens pour les garçons") may relate, but they do not name any event.
- **Offering to Merciful Love (9–11 June 1895).** Not mentioned in any letter in this file. LT 176 (Apr) and LT 177–178 (July) are silent on it.
- **Joan of Arc plays (1894–95).** Not mentioned.
- **Bellière (Oct 1895).** Not mentioned. LT 180 (14–17 Oct 1895) and LT 181 (16 Nov 1895) say nothing about a missionary "brother".
- **Pauline's election (20 Feb 1893).** Covered by LT 140 (on the day itself). Mère Agnès's illness late in 1893 is covered by LT 153. The file does not name the illness.
- **Her jobs in the Carmel.** This file shows novitiate work ("petit chien de chasse", LT 167), painting (LT 156, LT 160, LT 162, LT 180 flower patterns to earn money), laundry (LT 180) and sewing (linen, LT 114). It does not name her official offices (sacristy, refectory, portress, etc.).
- **Health.** LT 167 (July 1894): "j'ai une santé de fer" (an iron constitution). LT 180 (Oct 1895): Dr La Néele "took trouble to find me remedies", with no illness named. LT 169's anti-haemorrhage water was requested by the infirmarian, and the letter does not say for whom. Do not connect these to her later TB without other evidence.
- **Identities not given in the file:** "Marguerite M." (LT 126), "le malheureux prodigue" (LT 129, almost certainly Loyson), the novice "ma fille" (LT 167), "le Père" (LT 168), "Notre Père Supérieur" (LT 176), "Notre Mère" in July 1891 (LT 129). Cards mark these as outside knowledge.
- **Transcription issues:** LT 148 « L'enfant de la Breuse Marg. Marie » (corrupt?); LT 180 « je suis obligé » (masculine); LT 138 « mon cher petite Père »; LT 169 « l'orage qui ronde à Caen » (for "gronde"). Check them against the manuscripts.
- **Unclear passages:** LT 172 "six de vos enfants" (six brides of Christ given by Mme Guérin; the count is unclear); LT 179 ("Demoiselle… Pauvre Monsieur"); LT 167 "j'ai passé par là l'année dernière… Le bon Dieu a voulu que je fasse mon sacrifice" (which sacrifice?); LT 157 Céline "sans asile" (why?).
- **1911 edition, summary of distortions found:** composite letters (Céline XI, XVI, XVII, XIX; Mère Agnès IV; Léonie I and II); wrong dates (LT 164 "pin" moved to Jan 1895; LT 167 lines moved to 19 Aug 1894; LT 180 moved to Aug 1895; LT 161 lines put under Oct 1893); added sentences (LT 120 "Papa ne viendra pas demain!"; LT 122 John of the Cross verse; LT 147 "table des cieux"; LT 165 "notre état nous faisait horreur"; LT 169 opening "le bon Dieu a exaucé mon vœu le plus cher"); Léonie hidden under the name "Sœur Françoise-Thérèse"; cuts of humour, family conflict, class remarks, food, money and her father's "humiliation". Taylor 1912 translated this edited text, so English readers have had the altered versions.

Conflicts with Ms A / 1911
- Uncle Guérin's first answer (8 Oct 1887): letter = affectionate, "sure of vocation", obstacle "le monde", age 17–18; Ms A = opposition "unless a miracle".
- The papal audience (20 Nov 1887): letter = Pope nearly unable to speak, Révérony answers, Pope says "Si le bon Dieu veut vous entrerez", she is passed into another room; Ms A adds Révérony's ban on speaking, Céline's "Parle!", guards lifting her, the Pope's hand on her lips.
- Assisi (24 Nov 1887): letter = Révérony "did not speak to me at all about my affairs"; Ms A = he talked of Carmel and promised to do all he could; LT 38 = he "promised me during the Rome journey" to speak to the bishop. The promise may have been given on another day; unresolved.
- Mère Marie de Gonzague: LT 57 (July 1888) = "what a treasure", brought the letter at 6 a.m.; LT 78 (Jan 1889) = "constantly interrupted... without joy"; LT 93 (July 1889) = "a deep knowledge of souls". Ms A (1895) describes severity toward her in her first years. The letters show both warmth and frustration.
- Her health in the first winter: LT 69 "they look after you so well... I always have to eat and warm my feet"; LT 58 "never eaten so much". These family-facing lines differ in tone from later accounts of cold and hunger in the Carmel (not in this file).

Pauline's edits in the 1911 edition (summary; each has a card)
- Redatings: LT 43 (Mar 1888) → "1887"; LT 55 (Jul 1888) → "1889"; LT 91 (May 1889) → "14 août 1889"; LT 92 (May 1889) → "1888"; LT 95 (1889) → "1891"; LT 54/103/106 (1888–1890) → "1892"; LT 109 (Jul 1890) → "1894".

- Changed key words: "faiblesse... toute sa confiance" → "toute ma force" (LT 55); "communier quand même" → "communier toujours" (LT 92); "chemin de la vertu" → "chemin du sacrifice" (LT 109); "chérubins" → "Séraphins" (LT 83); "Pauline" → "Maman" (LT 43).

- LT 31 / 31 A and LT 31 B: the source lists LT 31 B (14 Nov) before LT 31 (10 Nov).
- LT 90 (Apr 1889) says she has "beaucoup de mémoire pour les dates"; LT 98 (Oct 1889) says she never knows the date. A harmless self-contradiction.
- Unidentified names in the letters: "le Père" (LT 21), "l'artiste Darel", "la grande Anglaise", "les demoiselles Pigeon" (LT 24), "Monsieur de Virville" (LT 42), "M. Benoît" (LT 51), "la marraine qui plantait des verveines" (LT 62), "l'Intrépide N° 2" (LT 66), "Lolo" (LT 101), "Belloni" (LT 95), "Mlle Pauline" (LT 58, 83), "la Pott" (LT 50). A biographer should not guess these.
- LT 94's postscript and LT 105/LT 107 show the Carmel limited letters in retreats (Pentecost 1890) and that postage weight mattered.

#### From batch A

- **Pauline is both witness and editor.** She admits the HA text was changed (F-EW-1897), and says Thérèse gave her blanket permission to edit (F-EW-1423). Only she reports that permission. She also calls the changes "of little importance". Treat her HA-based statements with care.
- **Much of her "testimony" is quotation of HA.** She says Thérèse told her everything later written down, and quotes MS A/C at length as testimony. This blurs what she saw and what she read. The Pranzini story, the Rome audience and the Abbey years she refers to Céline or Marie.
- **Her notebook affected Thérèse.** Pauline admits her note-taking tired Thérèse and "paralysed her openness" (F-EW-1581). Thérèse feared her merit was lost because Pauline wrote it down. This matters for the reliability of the "Last Words".
- **Dates in the NV notebook do not always match the editors' DE references.** The Hanoi saying sits under "15 mai" but is tagged DE 15-6; items under "15 mai" are tagged DE 9-5; the "4 juin" entry is tagged DE 6-4; there are retro-entries "Du 21 (oublié)" and "Du 16 juillet (oublié)". The notebook was edited after the event.
- **Dying moment: two versions.** Oral: "I saw her close her eyes; she sighed several times and gave her soul to God" (f. 246v). NV: "As soon as it [the ecstasy] ended, she breathed her last" (f. 292v). The oral text says "a sister" carried the candle; the NV names Sr Marie de l'Eucharistie.
- **Body after death.** Supple limbs until burial (f. 292v) vs. coffin closed on 3 Oct because of "some signs of decomposition" (f. 246v). Both from Pauline; she does not reconcile them.
- **Cure date.** Pauline gives 10 May 1883 for the smile of the Virgin; the standard date is 13 May 1883 (Pentecost).
- **Publication date.** Pauline: October 1898; editors: 30 Sept 1898.
- **Origin date of MS A.** Pauline: early 1895; editors: Dec 1894.
- **Taxil / Diana Vaughan.** Pauline says Thérèse saw through the hoax before its exposure (F-EW-992). She does not mention that Thérèse wrote for "Diana" or that her photo was used at Taxil's April 1897 conference. This looks like a softened account. Check other witnesses.
- **Gonzague.** Pauline is critical ("gaps and faults", "harmful influence", "suffered much from this prioress", a prioress elected "despite notable faults"). But she also reports Gonzague's deathbed trust in Thérèse, and that Gonzague showed her off at the parlour. The 1896 prioress who "had notable faults" is not named; the identification with Gonzague is by context.
- **Dissenters named or hinted.** (1) a long-professed nun who later left the Carmel: "easy to be a saint with everything"; (2) a lay sister, dead by 1910: the "circular" remark, but at other times called her a saint; (3) a lay sister: "not even a good nun"; (4) a sister who stared laughing every evening; (5) some sisters who did not think her dying; (6) Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix before 1888; (7) two or three Carmels and one prioress who saw pride in HA. Pauline discredits dissenter 1 ("poor judgment", left the order). None except Sr Saint-Jean de la Croix is named.
- **"All except one or two" admired her** (f. 240v) sits uneasily with the list of critics above.
- **Pauline's own failures, freely admitted:** she did not read MS A for months; she did not believe the wound of love; she joined in refusing Thérèse's profession in 1890; she tried to get Thérèse to say edifying words to the doctor; she received confidences Thérèse said she should refuse.
- **Friction inside the Martin family:** Marie opposed her entry; Marie was hurt that Thérèse preferred a nun Marie disliked; Thérèse felt her sisters tried to "influence our mother" about her rest; she warned all three about talking after the parlour; "I don't like what comes from my family".
- **Unnamed people** the merger may want to identify from other files: the old lay sister she guided (died 1895); the "abominable" sister; the critic at the funeral; the first infirmarian whose handling "could have had grave consequences"; the neurasthenic sister; the unbelieving relative by marriage; the tempted seminarian; the director who called her proud.

The Abbey years are deferred to Céline and Marie.
