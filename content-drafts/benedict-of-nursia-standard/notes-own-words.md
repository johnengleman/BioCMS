## Quotations (Rule). Latin exact from S1.

Verse numbers are in the standard RB numbering. "V" says what confirms the number. Where no V is stated, the verse is TO CHECK.

Q1. Prologue 1. "Obsculta, o fili, præcepta magistri, et inclina aurem cordis tui et admonitionem pii patris libenter excipe et efficaciter conple, ut ad eum per oboedientiæ laborem redeas, a quo per inoboedientiæ desidiam recesseras." Own English: Listen, my son, to the master's teaching, and turn the ear of your heart. Gladly welcome the advice of a loving father and carry it out, so that you return by the work of obedience to the One you left through the laziness of disobedience. Locator: Prol. 1 (opening; S1; S2 prints "Ausculta" in the Ferrariis text? not checked, S1 reads "Obsculta"). Public domain.

Q2. Prologue 45. "Constituenda est ergo nobis dominici scola servitii." Own English: We must therefore set up a school of the Lord's service. Verse 45 [S3, which quotes "school for the service of the Lord" as Prol. 45]. S2 prints "schola" and translates "school of the Lord's service". Public domain.

Q3. Prologue 45-46. "In qua institutione nihil asperum, nihil grave nos constituturos speramus; sed et si quid paululum restrictius ... non ilico pavore perterritus refugias viam salutis, quæ non est nisi angusto initio incipienda." Own English: In laying this down we hope to order nothing harsh or heavy. If something is a little strict, for good reason, do not run from the way of salvation in fear. It cannot begin except with a narrow gate. Locator Prol. 46-48 (verses TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q4. Prologue 49. "Processu vero conversationis et fidei, dilatato corde inenarrabili dilectionis dulcedine curritur via mandatorum Dei." Own English: As life and faith advance, the heart widens, and one runs the way of God's commands in the unspeakable sweetness of love. Locator Prol. 49 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q5. Ch. 4, tools of good works (opening, verse 1-2). "In primis Dominum Deum diligere ex toto corde, tota anima, tota virtute. Deinde proximum tamquam seipsum." Own English: First, to love the Lord God with the whole heart, whole soul, whole strength. Then, to love one's neighbour as oneself. 4:1-2 (standard; TO CHECK from S3, which gives only 4:21 and 4:62? no, 4:21 only). S2 lists these as tools 1 and 2.

Q6. Ch. 4:21. "Nihil amori Christi præponere." Own English: Put nothing before the love of Christ. Locator 4:21, confirmed by S3 ("whose love he must put before all else (4, 21; 72, 11)"); S2 lists it as tool no. 21. Public domain.

Q7. Ch. 4, a hospitality-and-mercy run. "Pauperes recreare. Nudum vestire. Infirmum visitare. Mortuum sepelire. In tribulatione subvenire. Dolentem consolari." Own English: Refresh the poor. Clothe the naked. Visit the sick. Bury the dead. Help in trouble. Comfort the grieving. Locator ch. 4 (verses about 4:14-19; TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q8. Ch. 4. "Non velle dici sanctum antequam sit, sed prius esse quod verius dicatur." Own English: Do not wish to be called holy before you are. First be what can truly be said. S2 counts it tool no. 61 (the 1907 count). Standard RB verse TO CHECK (about 4:62). Public domain. Good line, shows the man's honesty.

Q9. Ch. 4, last tool and the workshop. "Et de Dei misericordia numquam desperare." Own English: And never despair of God's mercy. Then: "Officina vero ubi hæc omnia diligenter operemur claustra sunt monasterii et stabilitas in congregatione." Own English: The workshop where we do all this carefully is the monastery enclosure and stability in the community. Locator 4:74-78 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q10. Ch. 7, humility. "Omnis qui se exaltat humiliabitur et qui se humiliat exaltabitur." (Luke 14:11, quoted at 7:1.) Then the ladder: "scala illa erigenda est quæ in somnio Iacob apparuit" (7:6). Own English: We must raise the ladder that Jacob saw in his dream. Our life in this world is that ladder, raised toward heaven by a humbled heart. The two sides are body and soul. Ch. 7 verse numbers TO CHECK. Public domain.

Q11. Ch. 7, first step. "Primus itaque humilitatis gradus est, si timorem Dei sibi ante oculos semper ponens, oblivionem omnimo fugiat..." Own English: The first step of humility is to keep the fear of God always before one's eyes and never forget it. Ch. 7:10. TO CHECK. Public domain.

Q12. Ch. 7, the twelfth step and the summit. "Ergo, his omnibus humilitatis gradibus ascensis, monachus mox ad caritatem Dei perveniet illam quæ perfecta foris mittit timorem ... non iam timore gehennæ, sed amore Christi et consuetudine ipsa bona et dilectatione virtutum." Own English: When all the steps are climbed, the monk will come to that love of God which, being perfect, casts out fear. He will keep all things no longer from dread of hell, but from love of Christ, from good habit, and from delight in virtue. Ch. 7:67-69. TO CHECK. Public domain. This line is the point of the whole ladder.

Q13. Ch. 7, fourth step (patience under injustice). "...tacite conscientia patientiam amplectatur et sustinens non lassescat vel discedat." Own English: In hard and unjust treatment, he quietly embraces patience in his heart, endures, and does not grow weary or leave. Ch. 7:35. TO CHECK. Public domain.

Q14. Ch. 7, seventh step. "Ego autem sum vermis et non homo" is Psalm 21/22:7, quoted by Benedict. Do not credit it to him as his own words.

Q15. Ch. 31, the cellarer (the monastery's steward). "Cellararius monasterii elegatur de congregatione sapiens, maturis moribus, sobrius, non multum edax, non elatus, non turbulentus, non iniuriosus, non tardus, non prodigus, sed timens Deum; qui omni congregationi sit sicut pater." Own English: Choose as cellarer a wise man of mature character, sober, not a big eater, not proud, not a troublemaker, not insulting, not slow, not wasteful, but God-fearing, a father to the whole community. 31:1-2 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q16. Ch. 31. "Omnia vasa monasterii cunctamque substantiam ac si altaris vasa sacrata conspiciat." Own English: Let him treat all the tools and goods of the monastery as if they were the sacred vessels of the altar. Locator 31:10 (TO CHECK). S2 index: "sacred vessels of the altar, p. 95". Public domain.

Q17. Ch. 31. "...cui substantia non est quod tribuatur, sermo responsionis porrigatur bonus, ut scriptum est: Sermo bonus super datum optimum." Own English: If he has nothing to give, let him give a kind word, as it is written: a kind word is better than the best gift (Sirach 18:17). Also: "nemo perturbetur neque contristetur in domo Dei" (let no one be troubled or saddened in the house of God). 31:13-19 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q18. Ch. 31. "Infirmorum, infantum, hospitum pauperumque cum omni sollicitudine curam gerat, sciens sine dubio, quia pro his omnibus in die iudicii rationem redditurus est." Own English: He cares with full attention for the sick, the children, the guests, and the poor, knowing he will give account for all of them on the day of judgment. 31:9 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q19. Ch. 53, guests. "Omnes supervenientes hospites tamquam Christus suscipiantur, quia ipse dicturus est: Hospis fui et suscepistis me." Own English: Let all guests who arrive be received as Christ, because he will say: I was a stranger and you took me in. 53:1 (first sentence of the chapter; verse number TO CHECK, standard numbering gives 53:1). S2 opens the chapter the same way ("Let all guests that come be received like Christ Himself"). Public domain.

Q20. Ch. 53. "inclinato capite vel prostrato omni corpore in terra, Christus in eis adoretur qui et suscipitur." Own English: With head bowed, or the whole body stretched on the ground, let Christ be adored in them, for Christ is who is received. 53:6-7 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q21. Ch. 53. "Pauperum et peregrinorum maxime susceptioni cura sollicite exhibeatur, quia in ipsis magis Christus suscipitur; nam divitum terror ipse sibi exigit honorem." Own English: Take the greatest care to welcome the poor and pilgrims, because in them Christ is received more; for the rich, fear itself wins them honour. 53:15 (TO CHECK). Public domain. Humor and edge: the "fear of the rich" line.

Q22. Ch. 53. "Aquam in manibus abbas hospitibus det; pedes hospitibus omnibus tam abbas quam cuncta congregatio lavet." Own English: The abbot pours water over the guests' hands. The abbot and the whole community wash the feet of all guests. 53:12-13 (TO CHECK). Public domain. The abbot himself.

Q23. Ch. 53. "Coquina abbatis et hospitum super se sit, ut, incertis horis supervenientes hospites, qui numquam desunt monasterio, non inquietentur fratres." Own English: The abbot's and guests' kitchen is separate, so that the brothers are not disturbed when guests arrive at odd hours, "who are never lacking in a monastery". 53:16 (TO CHECK). Public domain. Dry humor.

Q24. Ch. 72, good zeal. "Sicut est zelus amaritudinis malus qui separat a Deo et ducit ad infernum, ita est zelus bonus qui separat a vitia et ducit ad Deum et ad vitam æternam." (S1 has typo "vitia"; S2 Latin/English: "keepeth us from vice, and leadeth to God".) Own English: As there is a bitter zeal that separates from God and leads to hell, so there is a good zeal that separates from vice and leads to God and eternal life. 72:1-2 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q25. Ch. 72. "honore se invicem præveniant, infirmitates suas sive corporum sive morum patientissime tolerent, oboedientiam sibi certatim inpendant: nullus quod sibi utile iudicat sequatur, sed quod magis alio." Own English: Let them try to outdo each other in honour. Let them bear each other's weaknesses of body and of character with great patience. Let them race one another in obedience. Let no one follow what he thinks useful for himself, but what is useful for the other. 72:4-7 (TO CHECK). Public domain.

Q26. Ch. 72, last line. "Christo omnimo nihil præponant, qui nos pariter ad vitam æternam perducat." Own English: Let them put absolutely nothing before Christ, who will lead us all together to eternal life. 72:11, confirmed by S3. Public domain.

## Gregory on the Rule

Dialogues II.36 (S4 "Chapter XXXVI", old English): Benedict "wrote a rule for his monks, both excellent for discretion, and also eloquent for the style." And: "he may in the institution of that rule understand all his manner of life and discipline: for the holy man could not otherwise teach than himself lived." Quote from S4 is the 1608-style English; use our own wording (paraphrase): Gregory says the Rule is outstanding in discretion and clear in style, and that Benedict lived what he taught. The Latin ("discretione præcipuam, sermone luculentam") is NOT checked: listed under "to check". S5 (Ullathorne) says the same, paraphrase.

## Misattributions and popular lines (flag for the writer)

1. "Ora et labora." Not in the Rule. Kardong, quoted at S8: "Ora et Labora is found nowhere in the Rule of St. Benedict. Moreover, it appears nowhere in Benedictine history before the 19th century. The motto actually originates in a popular book on Benedictine life written by the German abbot, Maurus Wolter." Kardong is a second-hand source here; the Wolter book itself is not read. Rule 48 gives a three-part day: prayer, work, and sacred reading (S8). The Rule's own words are "Otiositas inimica est animæ" (48:1). Carlyle 1843 used "Laborare est orare" for the old monks (S9; Wikipedia is weak). Mark: popular motto, not Benedict's own.
2. "Ut in omnibus glorificetur Deus" (that in all things God may be glorified) is a real Rule line, 57:9, per S3 (Latin not copied from S1 here; check). "Pax" on the medal is not from the Rule.
3. "Nihil amori Christi praeponere" is true (4:21, 72:11).
4. The "Rule of St Benedict says monks should drink a hemina of wine" is true (ch. 40). Not a misattribution.
5. "Listen with the ear of your heart" is a true paraphrase of Prol. 1 ("inclina aurem cordis tui"). Any popular line the writer meets that is not in S1 (the full Latin Rule) is not from the Rule; search S1 before using it. "Omnia mensurate" (do all things with measure) is in ch. 31 and ch. 48. 
6. Ullathorne (S5) cites Pope Urban VIII as saying the Rule's author was "filled with the wisdom of all the Saints" and Cosimo de' Medici (Cosmo) on the Rule. Those are reported sayings; not checked at source.

## Medal of St Benedict, inscriptions and sources (S6, S7)

- Front: image of Benedict with cross in right hand and Rule in left. A cup and a raven by him. Words above: "Crux Sancti Patris Benedicti". Margin: "Ejus in obitu nro praesentia muniamur" (May we be strengthened by his presence at our death). [S6]
- Back: the cross bears letters C S S M L (Crux sacra sit mihi lux) down the upright bar and N D S M D (Non draco sit mihi dux) on the cross-bar. The four corners: C S P B. Around the rim: V R S N S M V - S M Q L I V B, for "Vade retro Satana, nunquam suade mihi vana. Sunt mala quae libas, ipse venena bibas." Top: PAX or IHS. [S6]
- Origin. Not from Benedict; no source says he made it. In 1647, in a witchcraft trial at Natternberg near Metten Abbey in Bavaria, the accused said they had no power over Metten because of the cross. The monks found painted crosses with the letters on their walls but had forgotten what they meant. A manuscript of 1415 at Metten showed Benedict with a staff topped by a cross and a scroll, with the words written out in full. [S6; S7 in more detail]. S7 says the Metten manuscript was published by Dom Bernard Pez in 1721 (Thesaurus Anecdotorum Novissimus, vol. 1) with an engraving.
- First papal approval: Benedict XIV, briefs of 23 December 1741 and 12 March 1742 [S6].
- Jubilee medal first struck in 1880 for the 14th centenary of Benedict's birth; Monte Cassino holds exclusive right to strike it [S6]. Conflict: S6 says "doubtful when the medal originated"; a web search result says the initials appear on medals at least since 1780 (the search summary, not read at source: "to check").
- Legend: S7 links the cross to Benedict's miracles in his Life by Gregory (poisoned cup, raven) and to later accounts. The verses are medieval, not from Benedict's lifetime. The cup and raven come from Gregory, Dialogues II.8 (TO CHECK in S4 chapter on the poisoned bread; S4 table of contents shows "St Benet commands the crow to carry away the poisoned loaf"; the cup is a separate incident).
