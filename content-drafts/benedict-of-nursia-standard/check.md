## The copy editor's nine items

1. Theoprobus and the tears (ch. "Saint Benedict Weeps Over His Abbey's Future"). The Latin of II.17 is "nec tamen, ut vir Dei consueverat, orando plangeret sed moerendo": he was not weeping in prayer, as he was used to, but in grief. The 1608 English says the reverse. Now: "Saint Benedict often wept in prayer, but this was different. These were tears of grief." The "That was strange in itself" line is gone. Note 71 gives the Latin; the Note on the Sources says we follow the Latin here.
2. "the son of a man of the court" now "the son of a town councillor". Latin "cuiusdam curialis filium"; Gardner p. 70 note. Note 53 updated.
3. "probably" in two dates. "He left Rome about the year 500." "About the year 542, he set out for the abbey." Gardner's "probably" (p. 51 note; pp. 73–75 note) is in notes 9 and 64 and in "A Note on the Sources".
4. Close translations without quotation marks. Marked as quotations: "To a soul that sees the Creator, all creation is small" (Latin "animae videnti Creatorem angusta est omnis creatura", note 88); "their souls were always one in God, and their bodies were not parted in death" (II.34, note 109); "the cross, the book, and the plow" (Pacis nuntius, note 107); "lived with himself" (II.3, note 30).
5. "who sees into every heart". It is in the 1608 English ("who beholdeth the hearts of all men") and not in the Latin. The Latin of II.3 says "in superni spectatoris oculis" ("under the eyes of the One who watches from above"), and later "ante oculos Conditoris". Now "in the sight of his Creator." Note 30 gives the Latin.
6. Titles for Constantine, Simplicius, Valentinian, Honoratus, Servandus. Left plain. No source in the folder or on the pages checked (Gardner, Alston, Ford, Wikipedia, the list of abbots of Monte Cassino) calls any of them a saint or blessed (`titles-pass.md`).
7. Church words. "Rule": "a short rule for the monks' daily life". "an order in the later sense" now "what we now call an order, a family of monasteries living under one rule". "consecrate the new church, which means to set it apart for God in a solemn service". "the sacred crypt, the chapel below the church where the holy relics ... were kept". "the Holy See, the Pope's own government in Rome". "relics" now "the remains of Saint Benedict, his holy relics".
8. "until the Lombard troubles of Saint Gregory's own time" now "until the Lombards invaded Italy in Saint Gregory's own time" (Gardner Introduction p. xx; Latin "usque ad haec Longobardorum tempora"). Note 15 updated.
9. Enfide. The Latin is "multisque honestioribus viris pro caritate se illic detinentibus": "se" is reflexive, so the good men themselves stayed there out of charity. The text is right. Unchanged.

## Other errors of fact found and fixed

- Nurse (ch. "A Student Leaves Rome to Seek God"). The line "With her he left the last of his father's house" is cut. No source says the nurse was from his father's house.
- "a thin rope" cut to "a long rope". The Latin says "longissimo fune"; "thin" is invented.
- "He was the son of a great man" (candle monk, II.20) now "the son of a town official". The Latin says "defensoris filius"; Gardner p. 80 note. Note 61 updated.
- "He had learned that no one can be forced into a way of life" now "He had found that he could not hold men to a way of life they did not want". The first line was Gregory's moral stretched past what he says.
- Rule spread. "no one in Gaul seems to have used it at all" went past Dudden, who says there is no sign that other monasteries in Gaul adopted it for nearly a hundred years (he allows the Maurus story may be true). Now "there is no sign that the monasteries of Gaul used it". Note 100 gives vol. 2, p. 98, note 3.
- "In 817 a council at Aachen enforced it across the empire". Alston says the abbots of the empire met at Aachen in 817 and passed laws for uniform discipline that were additions to the Rule. Now says that.
- Medal. "Monte Cassino alone has the right to strike it" rests on Ott (1912) and was stated as a present fact. Now "alone had the right", and "In 1880 the jubilee design ... was first struck" (Ott says it was first struck in 1880; it does not say Monte Cassino struck it).
- Pacis nuntius. "Pope Saint John XXIII had wished it before him" was wrong. The letter says Pius XII hoped God would bless, through Saint Benedict's prayers, the work of uniting Europe, and that John XXIII wished the same; it does not say they wished a patron. Rewritten. The petitioners were also cardinals and "insignes ex laicorum ordine viri"; now "Cardinals, bishops, heads of religious orders, and well-known laymen".
- Homily of 1964. "Brothers, let us weep" was cut from "let us weep with emotion and gratitude" and placed after the unheard plea, so it read as grief for the war. In the homily it follows "These walls speak. It is peace that has made them rise again." Now the quotations are whole and in the homily's order.
- Two monks' vision (II.37). "in his cell at the abbey" now "in his own cell"; Gregory says only "in cella".
- Note 11 "Prologue 1 and 4.21" now "Prologue 1–2 and 4.21" (the quoted lines run over verses 1 and 2). Note 99: the letter to Maximianus is on p. xix of Gardner's Introduction, not p. xx.
- Note 109 and the grave paragraph: the high-altar claim stays as "The monks of Monte Cassino say" (Gardner p. 94 note calls it "a little uncertain"; Fleury disputes the relics).
- "A Note on the Sources": added Gardner's "probably" for the two dates, and a sentence saying that at Theoprobus's tears we follow the Latin.
