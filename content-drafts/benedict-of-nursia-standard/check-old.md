# Check: Benedict of Nursia (standard, tier A)

Copies of the originals: `biography.html.before-check`, `summary.txt.before-check`. Lint: no MUST FIX. One soft CHECK remains (average sentence length 14.7). It was 15.0 before the check. The straight-sentence rules keep it low, and the quotations hold many short lines.

Raw sources used: Gregory, Dialogues II, 1608 English (Gardner 1911 OCR); Gregory's Latin from the Benedictine edition on archive.org (Opera omnia vol. 6, OCR, partial); the Latin Rule (Latin Library); Pacis nuntius (Latin), Fulgens radiatur (Latin, English); CE "Medal of Saint Benedict" and "The Benedictine Order"; Wikipedia "Fleury Abbey"; Subiaco Abbey "Work".

## Quotations
- Prologue quote (Rule): added the clause the Latin has and the text dropped ("dictante aequitatis ratione, propter emendationem vitiorum vel conservationem caritatis": "as fairness requires, to correct faults or to protect love"). Changed "narrow gate" to "narrow start" (Latin: angusto initio).
- Gregory on leaving Rome: "He wanted only to serve God, so he went looking for a place" followed the 1608 English, but the Latin says "soli Deo placere desiderans, sanctae conversationis habitum quaesivit". Now "He wanted only to please God, so he sought out a holy way of life". "The same pit" became "a terrible pit" (Latin: immane praecipitium).
- The other Gregory block quotes (poison speech, Totila, Scholastica, tower, death, Theoprobus, Gregory's letter and sources) match the 1608 English. The poison, Scholastica, Riggo and tower lines also match the Latin I could read (tower: "omnis mundus velut sub uno solis radio collectus", so the OCR repair of "one beam" is confirmed).
- Rule quotations (Prologue 1, ch. 4 tools, ch. 53, ch. 72, "Otiositas", ch. 31, 36, 40, 58, 64) match the Latin. Chapter 72 quote has its ellipsis in the right place.
- "this least little rule for beginners" (twice) changed to "this little rule for beginners". The Latin is "hanc minimam inchoationis regulam".
- Medal prayer: the notes' English came from Wikipedia. It now reads as our own translation of the Latin distich (confirmed in CE Ott). CSSML and NDSMD confirmed ("Crux sacra sit mihi lux", "Non draco sit mihi dux"). "Vade retro" distich confirmed.
- Writer's two flagged Rule lines. "Omnia mensurate" occurs twice in the Latin Rule: ch. 31 (steward) and ch. 48 (day's labor). The text now says so. "Ut in omnibus glorificetur Deus" is Rule 57.9, the end of the craftsmen's chapter, not the aim of the Rule. The text said "its aim is stated in one line". Fixed to say where it stands.
- Exhilaratus quote unchanged but the framing was wrong ("When he came before Benedict, the abbot looked at him": invented). Gregory has him bring two flasks, hide one, and Benedict warns him as he leaves.
- Women's-house quote: dropped "to me" (not in the text).

## Invented drama and wrong detail
- Opening: priest "in the hill country of Subiaco" changed to "a good way from Subiaco" (1608: "dwelling a good way off"); "hollow places in the rocks" to "hollow pits"; "No one should fast" to the young man should not fast.
- "a schoolroom in Rome ... walked out": invented. Now "gave up his books".
- Sieve: "still there in his own day, until the Lombard wars" contradicted itself. Now "hung there until the Lombard troubles of his own time". "A place where no one knew him": not in Gregory. Now "on alone toward Subiaco".
- Briers story: "years later" removed (Gregory: "afterward").
- Poison chapter: "his cave" changed to "the wilderness he loved". Gregory's gloss was misstated; now "the monks had plotted against Benedict and lived nothing like him". "Followed him back to Subiaco" removed (not in the card).
- Billhook: the Goth was a monk ("poor of spirit, that gave over the world, was received by the man of God", sent to clear a garden plot), not "a poor Goth with a simple heart". The Goth told Maurus, and Maurus told Benedict. Fixed. Goth still unnamed.
- Maurus rescue: "cloak" to "garment" (1608).
- Evitius and Tertullus cut (four new names in one paragraph; not needed).
- Peter's Scripture parallels: Peter the deacon and the apostle Peter were confused. Now clear.
- Florentius: "a messenger" to "sent word"; "each of the twelve houses" to "the houses and chapels" (Gregory: governors for the abbeys and oratories).
- Famine meal at "the gate" to "in sacks at his cell door" (1608).
- Candle monk: "young" and the setting fixed ("while he ate").
- Totila: "told him a third time" to "two or three times" (card: "twice or thrice"). "An old man helping him up" and "dust": invented, removed. "In the tenth year of his reign, in 552" mixed Gregory with Gardner; now Gregory's words and "historians place his death in 552".
- Servandus "woke in alarm": not in the text. Now "the cry troubled Servandus".
- Death: the two monks "neither knew what the other saw": not in the sources. Removed.
- Tower chapter ending: "God had made the world small" and "He did not have to leave the world to find God" contradicted the card (Gregory says the soul is enlarged) and were a moral. Replaced with the plain image. "More than forty years later" removed (no age is known).
- Chapter 13: "a pope who loved monks sat down with them", "the monks walked to Rome", "He had never met Benedict": none are in the sources. Cut. The Gregory of Tours sentence cut (it overreached Dudden and did not help).

## Absolute and overstated claims
- "Within three hundred years it was the rule of nearly every monastery": CE says "by the 9th century". Now "By the 800s".
- "Discretion, by which he meant that it asked for nothing extreme": put into Gregory's mouth. Now "discretion, or good judgment".
- Rule: "lived both of the lives that monks of his day lived", "a middle way, and it says so at the start": not in the sources. Reworded.
- "Pray and work ... came from a German abbot": the Subiaco page quotes Kardong. Now attributed to him.
- "The line that sums up the whole Rule": now "a line that comes back at the end of the Rule" (72.11). "His promise to stay" to "his life in the community" (stabilitas). "The old writers say wine" to "we read" (Latin "Licet legamus"). "Rich men need no special care" to "the fear they cause wins them honor". Guest kitchen is for "the abbot and the guests" (Latin).
- "A single question: does this man seek God?": ch. 58 lists several things to watch (seeks God, eager for the Work of God, obedience, hardships). Now one of the abbot's questions. "The Rule closes with a chapter on zeal": ch. 72 is second to last. Fixed.
- "He did not think much of his own powers" to "He did not claim such powers for himself".
- "Five hundred years after Christ" to "More than five hundred years". Archaeologists "just where Gregory said": now "matched Gregory's account".
- "At some point" for the Rule's writing ("somewhere in these years" implied a date).
- Tales: Lombard "so Gregory saw the end himself" to "Gregory says the story ended as Benedict foretold". "Carried that knowledge for the rest of his life": not in the sources. Reworded.
- Rule spread: "the way the book did, from Rome outward" is not in CE. Now "slowly at first". Benedict "never founded an order" to "did not found an order in the later sense" (CE). Aachen: "a council of abbots ... enforced it across the empire".
- Fleury: "about 1043 five monks wrote a book" is wrong. The Miracula grew over three centuries through five monks, and Andrew wrote about 1043. "Monte Cassino never accepted" to "deny it". "Both abbeys still say they hold Benedict" to Fleury "reputed". "Long before that" to "Meanwhile".
- Medal: "People remembered the two poisonings most of all" and "Out of that grew the medal" were stronger than the notes. Reworded. "On every medal today": CE shows the letters, not the words, on the cross face, and the rim prayer "Ejus in obitu" is on the jubilee medal only. Rewritten to match CE. Medal bird is a raven (CE).
- "Oldest calendars" to "early lists of saints". "Because the old date fell in Lent": causal link not in the notes. Removed. Lutheran and Anglican feast clause cut.
- Pius XII: the text said the 1947 encyclical called Benedict "father of Europe". Fulgens radiatur does not contain it in the Latin or English. The phrase comes from Paul VI's Pacis nuntius ("Europae patrem", citing AAS 39, 1947, p. 453). Moved to the Paul VI paragraph. The intro line "In 1947 Pius XII called Benedict the father of Europe" stays (Paul VI is the source).
- Pacis nuntius "made Europe with the cross, the book, and the plow": the letter says Benedict and his sons "brought Christian civilization" by them. Fixed.
- "His bones and his sister's lie under the high altar": one blog source, and Fleury disputes the relics. Now "Monte Cassino says".
- Last paragraph: the "words of his whole life" line moralized. Removed. The echo of the cave quotation stays.
- Note on the Sources: "every year in this life" to "in Benedict's own life", and a line that Gregory wrote the Totila and abbey prophecies after the events (moved here from the story).

## Settled points: all still right
No age at leaving Rome. Monte Cassino "528 or 529". Totila "probably 542". Poisoners' monastery unnamed in the story (the Note names the later tradition, Vicovaro). Goth with the billhook unnamed. Lombard sack "in the 580s". No 1220 canonization. Paul VI named Benedict patron of Europe in 1964 and came to Monte Cassino on 24 October 1964 (homily and travel page). John Paul II's 1980 letter set Cyril and Methodius beside Benedict.
