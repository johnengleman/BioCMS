## 1. The driving question

Saint Benedict wanted one thing: to please God alone and put nothing before the love of Christ [W-S1 p.51; R-Q6]. He thought that meant a cave. But the world kept climbing up to him (praise, the flesh, monks who wanted his name but not his way, a jealous priest, a king, his own sister), and his answer each time was to run or to hold the rule tighter. Grace widened him: he built a school where others seek God with him, he let his sister's love overrule his rule, and at last, alone at a tower window at night, he saw the whole world gathered into one ray of God's light [W-S1 p.97-98].

**Why it was beautiful to him (heard in ch.1, at the decision to leave Rome).** He left "wanting only to please God" [W-S1 p.51; check.md]. Saint Gregory says the Rule shows how he lived [W-S1 p.99], so the Rule speaks for him, as words he wrote years later: "Listen, my son … so that you return by the work of obedience to the One you left" [R-Q1]; "Put nothing before the love of Christ" [R-Q6]. Ch.7 plants "the heart widens … in the unspeakable sweetness of love" [R-Q4]; the tower (ch.11) pays it off: the soul that sees God is "enlarged" [W-S1 p.98].

**Famous** because his Rule became the rule of nearly every monastery in western Europe by the 800s [CE-BO], and because Saint Paul VI named him principal patron of all Europe in 1964 [C-S3].

**Set apart.** In an Italy torn by Gothic war, he wrote a rule that hopes to order "nothing harsh or heavy" and calls itself a little rule for beginners [R-Q3; R ch.73]. He had failed once as a strict abbot [W-S1 p.57], and his Rule tells the abbot to try to be loved more than feared [R ch.64]; Saint Gregory praised it for discretion [W-S1 p.99].

## 2. Portrait

A quiet, mild man with a sharp eye. To the monks who poisoned him he spoke "with a mild countenance and quiet mind" [W-S1 p.57]. He wept for his dead enemy and gave his dearest disciple a penance for being glad [W-S1 p.66-67]. He named hidden faults with dry humor ("Sign your heart, brother" [W-S1 p.80]); the Rule says guests "are never lacking in a monastery" [R-Q23]. His faults: he ran from trouble [W-S1 p.66-67], and he held to his rule against love until his sister's prayer overruled him [W-S1 p.94-95]. He refused credit for wonders: such miracles belong "to the blessed Apostles" [W-S1 p.93].

His life with God: he rose before the night prayers to pray at a window [W-S1 p.97]; in prayer he was usually sad but did not weep [W-S1 p.78]. Over a dead child: "Lord, do not look at my sins, but at the faith of this man" [W-S1 p.93]. His Rule: "never despair of God's mercy" [R-Q9].

## 3. The opening

**Hook: scene at the edge.** Keep the Easter cave (B paragraphs 1-5). It raises the question without spending it: a man who went to be alone with God calls a stranger's company his Easter.

> One Easter Sunday a priest had his feast ready when the Lord spoke to him: "You have prepared a good meal for yourself, while my servant in such a place is hungry." He took the food and searched the hills and hollows until he found a cave. A young man had lived there alone for three years and had lost count of the days. "Get up, brother, and let us eat," the priest said, "because today is the feast of Easter." The young man answered, "It is Easter for me, and a great feast, because God has shown me this much kindness, to enjoy your company today."

[W-S1 p.53-54; card 03.] The body does not retell it; ch.2 refers back in one clause.

**Thesis.** The young man was Saint Benedict, and he had gone into the cave to please God alone. He spent the rest of his life making a home where others could do the same. He wrote "this little rule for beginners" [R ch.73; check.md], which Saint Gregory praised for its discretion [W-S1 p.99]; by the 800s it was the rule of nearly every monastery in western Europe [CE-BO]; in 1964 Saint Paul VI named him patron of all Europe [C-S3]. Answered in ch.7, 13, 14, 15. The thesis and `summary.txt` must not tell the storm's outcome, Totila on the ground, the ray of light, or the death. (The current summary tells the storm and Totila.)

**The ending.** Feasts, medal, relics, and the Rule's spread go in ch.14, before the image. Closing image: 24 October 1964 on the rebuilt mountain. Saint Paul VI recalls the failed plea to spare the abbey ("Brothers, let us weep"), then: "These walls speak. It is peace that has made them rise again" [card 15]. His tears answer Saint Benedict's tears in ch.9. One quiet sentence may follow: Monte Cassino says brother and sister rest together under the high altar [C-S10]; Saint Gregory said their bodies were not parted in death [W-S1 p.96]. What he offers a reader today (a quiet life broken again and again, and a heart that grew wider each time) stays in the story. No moral; no second Easter quotation.

## 4. Chapters

"Pull" only where the story truly turns; other chapters end on their own image.

1. **A Student Leaves Rome to Seek God.** SC-01 (TP1). Pull: he slips away from his nurse, alone, toward Subiaco.
2. **Saint Benedict Lives Alone in a Cave.** SC-02, SC-04. Pull: a whole monastery comes to ask him for something he does not want to give.
3. **Monks Try to Poison Their New Abbot.** SC-05 (TP2). Ends: alone with himself under the eyes of his Creator.
4. **Saint Benedict Builds Twelve Monasteries at Subiaco.** SC-06. Pull: a priest nearby hears the talk and grows jealous.
5. **A Jealous Priest Drives Saint Benedict Away.** SC-07 (TP3). Pull: he does not go back; he goes on toward a mountain above Cassino.
6. **Saint Benedict Founds the Abbey of Monte Cassino.** SC-08. Plant Theoprobus, and Saint Scholastica's yearly visit to a house near the gate [W-S1 p.94]. Ends: the chapel of Saint John where Apollo's altar stood.
7. **Saint Benedict Writes a Rule for His Monks.** SC-09; the Prologue block ("school … heart widens") and a few lines only.
8. **A Gothic King Comes to Test Saint Benedict.** SC-10. Ends: the king on the ground, the monk lifting him up.
9. **Saint Benedict Weeps Over His Abbey's Future.** SC-11. Pull: his sister comes for her yearly visit.
10. **Saint Benedict's Sister Prays Down a Storm.** SC-12 (TP4). Ends on her body in his own grave. No pull, no hint: nothing stands in front of the climax.
11. **Saint Benedict Sees the World in One Ray.** SC-13 (TP5, climax). Ends on the image.
12. **The Death of Saint Benedict.** SC-14. Pull: the abbey still stands, and his tears have not yet come true.
13. **The Abbey Falls and the Monks Reach Rome.** Bridge. Ends on Saint Gregory's book.
14. **How Saint Benedict's Rule Spread Through the Church.** Bridge. Pull: in 1944 the abbey falls once more.
15. **Monte Cassino Is Destroyed and Rebuilt.** SC-15. Closing image.

## 5. The scenes

TP = turning point: slow it down and tell it from inside him, through his words and Saint Gregory's report of what he felt. "Inside" lists all the inner life the cards give.

### SC-01 — Leaving Rome; the broken sieve (Rome; Enfide, church of Saint Peter) — TP1
- What happens: He gives up books, house and money. His nurse follows; she weeps over a borrowed sieve; he prays with tears and it is whole. The town hangs it at the church door; he slips away from her in secret.
- Why it is here: the first great decision, where the reader hears the desire; first praise, and he runs.
- Have: card 01; W-S1 p.51-53; Latin wording, check.md. Inside: fear of "a terrible pit"; "wanting only to please God"; he chose hardships over praise. Then the Rule's voice [R-Q1; R-Q6].
- Loss: his father's house, and the nurse who "loved him tenderly". The notes do not follow her further; the parting closes her thread.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.51-53.

### SC-02 — Bread on a rope (Subiaco cave)
- What happens: Saint Romanus keeps his secret, gives him a monk's robe, and lowers bread on a rope with a bell. A thrown stone breaks the bell; Saint Romanus keeps serving.
- Why it is here: the solitude he wanted, on a thin thread. The day the bell breaks is the one concrete day of the three years. Saint Romanus's thread ends here, faithful.
- Have: card 02; W-S1 p.53 (with Saint Gregory's candle line).
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.53.

### SC-03 — Easter in the cave
- The opening only (card 03; W-S1 p.53-54). Need: nothing.

### SC-04 — The blackbird and the briers
- What happens: A black bird flies at his face; a woman's memory nearly drives him from the wilderness; he throws himself into briers and nettles, and never feels that temptation again.
- Why it is here: a trial of faith, told by himself. Plainly and briefly.
- Have: card 04; W-S1 p.55.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.55.

### SC-05 — The poisoned glass (a monastery nearby) — TP2
- What happens: Monks beg him to be abbot; he warns them, then gives in. They poison his wine; the glass breaks at his blessing. He forgives them and goes back to the wilderness.
- Why it is here: his first try at fatherhood fails; a way of life cannot be forced.
- Have: card 05; speech W-S1 p.56-57. Inside: his long refusal; "mild countenance and quiet mind"; Saint Gregory: had he stayed, he might have lost himself and still not won them [W-S1 p.57].
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.56-58.

### SC-06 — Saint Maurus runs on the lake (Subiaco)
- What happens: Noble families bring their sons. Saint Placidus falls into the lake; Saint Benedict knows it in his cell and sends Saint Maurus, who runs on the water. Each gives the credit away.
- Why it is here: the hermit is now a father. Opens his two closest disciples; Saint Placidus's thread ends here.
- Have: card 06; W-S1 p.60-65. The night climb with Saint Placidus for water [W-S1 p.62-63] may be one bridging sentence.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.60-65.

### SC-07 — The crow, the loaf, and the leaving (Subiaco) — TP3
- What happens: Florentius sends a poisoned loaf; the crow carries it off. He sends women to dance before the young monks. Saint Benedict leaves his twelve houses. Florentius dies; Saint Benedict weeps and gives Saint Maurus a penance for his joy.
- Why it is here: he gives up his life's work to keep his monks safe, and grieves for his enemy. Loss: the valley and his first monks.
- Have: card 07; W-S1 p.65-67. Inside: "far more sorry for him than grieved for himself"; fear for his younger monks; he saw the attack was aimed at him; "passing sorrowful".
- Relationship: Saint Maurus ends here, the last time the notes show them together.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.65-67.

### SC-08 — Apollo's mountain (Monte Cassino, about 528 or 529)
- What happens: Country people still worship Apollo. He breaks the idol, burns the grove, builds chapels of Saint Martin and Saint John, and preaches. The devil cries "Cursed Benedict, not blessed!"
- Why it is here: the home for his monks; the chapel of Saint John becomes his grave.
- Have: card 08; W-S1 p.68-69; chapels found after 1944 [C-S6].
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.68-69.

### SC-09 — "Sign your heart" (his table at Monte Cassino, evening)
- What happens: A great man's son holds the candle while he eats and thinks a proud thought. "Sign your heart, brother. What are you saying? Sign your heart."
- Why it is here: the Rule lived in one small scene; he taught what he lived [W-S1 p.99]. It replaces a list of Rule chapters.
- Have: W-S1 p.80-81; B ch.7 (setting fixed in check.md).
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.80-81.

### SC-10 — The king in borrowed shoes (Monte Cassino, probably 542)
- What happens: Totila sends Riggo dressed as king; Saint Benedict sees through it. Totila comes himself and falls to the ground; Saint Benedict lifts him, rebukes him, and foretells his end.
- Why it is here: the man who fled the world speaks to a king as a father.
- Have: card 09; W-S1 p.73-75; W-S1n p.264.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.73-75.

### SC-11 — Weeping over the abbey (his cell)
- What happens: Theoprobus finds him weeping and waits. God has given the abbey to the pagans; he could barely win the monks' lives.
- Why it is here: the cost of his life's work, seen ahead. Do not tell the Lombard sack here; it belongs in ch.13 (the current text tells it twice, copy-edit.md item 5).
- Have: card 10; W-S1 p.78.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.78.

### SC-12 — Saint Scholastica's storm, and her death (house near the abbey gate) — TP4
- What happens: At their yearly meeting she asks him to stay the night; he refuses. She bows her head on her hands and prays; the storm breaks; they talk until morning. Three days later he sees her soul rise as a dove and lays her in his own grave.
- Why it is here: love overrules his rule; "she could do more because she loved more".
- Have: card 11; W-S1 p.94-96. Inside: one thing he wanted and could not have; he wanted the clear sky to last; he stayed "against his will"; at the dove he thanked God with hymns.
- Loss at its date: three days after the storm; a few months before his own death [W-S4 vol2 p.168]; tradition 10 February 543 [W-S1n].
- Testimony: W-S1 p.94-95, in full.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.94-96.

### SC-13 — The tower window (Monte Cassino, at night) — TP5, CLIMAX
- What happens: Servandus is his guest. Saint Benedict rises before the night prayers and prays at the window. A light brighter than day shows him the whole world "under one ray of the sun", and the soul of Saint Germanus carried to heaven. He calls loudly for Servandus, who sees only a remnant of the light. Theoprobus's messenger finds the bishop dead at that hour.
- Why it is here: the answer to the driving question; the fullest scene in the life. The man who went into a cave to be alone with God sees the whole world in God's light. In the cave a guest brought him Easter; here he calls his guest up to see.
- Have: card 12; W-S1 p.96-98; Latin confirmed, check.md. Inside: "as he told it himself"; he wanted a witness. Saint Gregory's reading comes after the vision: the soul is enlarged and creation looks small [W-S1 p.98]. Theoprobus's thread ends here.
- Testimony: W-S1 p.97.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.96-98.

### SC-14 — Death standing in the chapel (Monte Cassino, about 543 or 547)
- What happens: He tells the day. Six days before, he has his grave opened, where his sister lies. Fever. On the sixth day his monks carry him to the chapel; he receives Communion and dies standing, held up by them, hands raised in prayer. Two monks see a road of lamps to heaven. Burial in the chapel of Saint John.
- Why it is here: the man who wanted to be alone dies held up by his brothers, in prayer. Short and quiet. No last words in the sources.
- Have: card 13; W-S1 p.99-100.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: W-S1 p.99-100.

### SC-15 — The walls rise again (Monte Cassino, 1944 and 24 October 1964)
- What happens: Bombs leave the abbey in ruins on 15 February 1944; the crypt survives [C-S6; C-S4]. Saint Paul VI consecrates the rebuilt church, speaks on the mountain, and names Saint Benedict patron of Europe [C-S3].
- Why it is here: the real case after the death; the closing image.
- Have: card 15.
- Need: nothing. Where to look: card 15 (vatican.va homily and travel page).

**Bridges (no cards).** Ch.12 end: in Saint Gregory's day Saint Benedict still worked miracles in the Subiaco cave; a woman who had lost her mind slept there and woke well [W-S1 p.100]. Ch.13: Lombards sack the abbey at night in the 580s and hold no monk [W-S1 p.79; card 14]; Pope Pelagius II houses the monks by the Lateran [CE-BO]; in 593 Saint Gregory writes the *Dialogues* from four disciples [W-S1 p.51; W-S1n].

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** the Rule, undated; "excellent for discretion" [W-S1 p.99]. Ch.7, 14.
- **What he changed:** unused in Gaul for nearly 100 years [W-S4]; Canterbury soon after 597; France by the late 700s; Aachen 817; by the 800s the only rule in western Europe except Scotland, Wales, and Ireland [CE-BO]. Ch.14.
- **Why he is famous:** the *Dialogues*, 593 [W-S1n]; Fleury's relic claim from the 800s [C-S7]; the medal (Metten 1647; approved 1741 and 1742; jubilee medal 1880) [R-S6; C-S8]; patron against poison [C-S5]. Ch.13, 14.
- **Path to sainthood:** cult at the cave in Saint Gregory's day [W-S1 p.100]; 21 March in early lists; 11 July since 1969; 14 March in the East [C-S5]; "father of Europe" (Pius XII, cited by Paul VI) and patron of Europe, 1964 [C-S3]; Saints Cyril and Methodius set beside him in 1980 [card FN-3]. Ch.14, 15.

## 8. What we leave out

- Rome prophecy to the bishop of Canosa: a second prophecy; miracles section.
- Raised child, water from the rock, billhook, famine meal, flask, monks who ate out: miracles section. His prayer over the child stays in the portrait only.
- Peter the deacon's Scripture parallels: commentary that stops the story.
- The four-witness block quote: one sentence at most.
- The long Rule tour (steward, guests, wine, ladder, tools): teachings section.
- Fleury dispute, Natternberg trial, medal letters: two sentences each at most.
- Parents' names, twin sister, Saint Maurus in France, 1220 canonization: late or unconfirmed.
- Evitius, Tertullus, Riggo's attendants: names not needed.
- Guérin's "tall and well proportioned": late, unsourced.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Saint Gregory gives no dates. Birth about 480; left Rome probably a few years before 500 [W-S1n]; Monte Cassino 528 or 529; Totila probably 542; death about 543 or 547. No age at leaving Rome.
- Keep Saint Gregory's order (storm, tower, death); invent no interval between them.
- Glass (monks) and loaf (Florentius) are two attacks. The bird is a crow; the medal shows a raven.
- Do not name the poisoners' monastery (Vicovaro is later tradition) or the billhook Goth.
- Lombard sack "in the 580s" (sources say 580, 581, 589).
- "Pray and work" is not in the Rule. "This little rule for beginners", not "least little".
- "Father of Europe" comes from Paul VI citing Pius XII, not from *Fulgens radiatur*.
- Relics: Monte Cassino says under its high altar; Fleury also claims them.
- Servandus, Theoprobus, Florentius and Riggo take no "Saint". Modernize all 1608 English.
