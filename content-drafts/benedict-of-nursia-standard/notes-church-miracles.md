## Identity facts for this focus
- Dates: Gregory gives NO birth or death dates. S2: "born at Nursia, c. 480; died at Monte Cassino, 543" and "Totila's visit to Monte Cassino in 543 is the only certain date we have". S5 gives 2 March 480 – 21 March 547 "according to tradition". CONTESTED (death 543 vs 547; 480 is inference).
- Canonization: S5 infobox says "canonized 1220 by Honorius III". Not found in any source I read in the text; treat as unverified (pre-congregation cult). Other: to check.
- Gregory's informants: Constantinus (next abbot), Valentinianus (Lateran abbey), Simplicius (third abbot), Honoratus (abbot of the first monastery when Gregory wrote) [S1 ch.2 end]. "The only authentic life" [S2].

## Miracles (Gregory II; S1 chapter numbers). Status GREGORY unless noted.
1. Sieve (Enside, nurse; S1 ch.3). Nurse borrowed a sieve, it broke in two; "with tears fell to his prayers"; "found it so whole that the place could not be seen where before it was broken". Townsmen hung it at church door "even to these very troubles of the Lombards". Cult object known only to Gregory.
2. Poisoned glass (S1 ch.9). Monks of a monastery whose abbot had died asked him to rule; he enforced regular life; they poisoned the wine; he made the sign of the cross and "the glass... brake in pieces, as though the sign of the cross had been a stone thrown against it". He: "Almighty God have mercy upon you... Go your ways, and seek ye out some other Father". Gregory does not name the monastery (S5 says some identify it with Vicovaro; not Gregory).
3. Poisoned loaf and the crow (S1 ch.15). Priest Florentius, envious, sent a poisoned loaf. A crow came daily to take bread from his hand. Benedict: "In the Name of Jesus Christ, our Lord, take up that loaf, and leave it in some such place where no man may find it." Crow hopped about and cried, "at length, with much ado" carried it off, came back after three hours. Gregory says "crow" (corvus); the "raven" of the medal and popular English is later. NOTE the cup and the loaf are two separate attacks with two separate villains; popular accounts merge them (medal shows both).
4. Florentius's seven naked dancing women (ch.16) drive Benedict from Subiaco; Florentius killed when his room collapses (ch.17); Benedict weeps, Maurus rejoices, Benedict gives Maurus penance. NEW-ish detail: Benedict "sorry for him than grieved for himself".
5. Water from the rock (ch.12). Three monasteries on a mountain; monks tired from carrying water. Benedict climbs at night with Placidus, prays, lays three stones; next day monks dig where three stones lie: "sweating drops of water", flows "even to this day".
6. Axe-head (billhook) from the lake (ch.13). A "poor Goth" clearing briars by the lake; iron slips off, "so deep, that there was no hope". Benedict puts the handle in the water; iron "by-and-by ascended from the bottom". "Behold, here is thy bill again, work on, and be sad no more." Gregory (and Peter) compare Elisha (ch.17).
7. Maurus walks on water (ch.14). Placidus falls into the lake, carried "so far as one may shoot an arrow". Benedict, in his cell, knows it; Maurus runs on the water, grabs him "by the hair of his head". Gregory: "A strange thing, and since the time of Peter the Apostle never heard of!" Benedict credits Maurus's obedience, Maurus credits the command; Placidus settles it: he saw "the Abbot's garment upon his head".
8. Debtor (ch.37; informant Peregrinus). An honest man owes twelve shillings (Latin solidi); Benedict has none; prays two days; on third day thirteen shillings are found on the chest of corn. (The "Gothic" man in the user brief belongs to #6, not here. In S1 the debtor is not a Goth.)
9. Totila test (ch.24-25). Totila sends Riggo in royal robes with three attendants (Vuldericus, Rudericus, Blindinus). Benedict: "Put off, my good son, put off that apparel, for that which thou hast on is none of thine." Totila falls prostrate; Benedict lifts him and prophesies: "Into the city of Rome shall you enter, and over the sea shall you pass: nine years shall you reign, and in the tenth shall you leave this mortal life." Totila "nothing so cruel" afterward. Date 543 per S2. Gregory's Rome prophecy to the bishop of Canosa: Rome will not be destroyed by strangers but "fall to decay of itself". Gregory says Honoratus did not hear it from Benedict himself.
10. Resurrection of a boy (ch.42). Countryman lays his dead son at the abbey gate, meets Benedict returning from the field: "Give me my son". Benedict: "such miracles are not for us to work, but for the blessed Apostles". Then lies on the body, prays "Behold not, O Lord, my sins, but the faith of this man". Child "began to tremble"; Benedict "gave it to his father, but alive and in health". Gregory notes it came by prayer, not by Benedict's own power.
11. Wall falling (ch.21). Devil overthrows a new wall; a young monk, son of a courtier, is crushed, carried in a sack "for the stones of the wall had not only broken his limbs, but also his very bones"; Benedict prays alone; "the very same hour" he is sound and returns to work. No tower collapse in Gregory: the tower is where Benedict sleeps and has the vision of Germanus (ch.45).
12. Vision of the world in one sunbeam and Germanus of Capua (ch.45, from "tower"): "the whole world, gathered as it were together under one beam of the sun". Messenger finds Germanus died at the same hour. S2: Urban VIII said Benedict merited to see God; St Thomas disputes. Status: GREGORY; theological comment CONTESTED (St Thomas vs Gregory/Bonaventure per S2).
13. Scholastica's storm (ch.43-44). She prays; "she lifted up her head and brought down the rain". He says "God forgive you, what have you done?" She: "I desired you to stay, and you would not hear me; I have desired our good Lord, and He had vouchsafed to grant my petition". Gregory's moral: "of right she did more which loved more". Three days later he sees her soul as a dove; buried "in that grave which he provided for himself". (NB S2 cites the Latin numbering, Dial. II, 33-35, which differs from S1's 43-45.)
14. Death (ch.47). Six days before, opens the sepulchre; fever; carried to the oratory, communion, stands held by disciples with hands raised, dies praying. Two monks see a road hung with tapestry and lamps from his cell eastward to heaven. Buried in the oratory of St John the Baptist.
15. Smaller, GREGORY: idol of Apollo smashed, oratories of St Martin and St John built (ch.18); stone the devil sat on (19); phantom kitchen fire (20); knows monks ate out (22,23); snake in the hidden flagon (28); handkerchiefs (29); proud thought at candle (30); 200 bushels of meal (31); Terracina vision (32); nuns absolved after death (33); buried boy cast out until communion laid on him (34); dragon (35); leprous boy (36); poisoned man healed by touch (38); oil flask thrown out the window unbroken and barrel of oil (39); devil with horn and mortar (40); Zalla's cords fall from the prisoner when Benedict looks (41); mad woman healed in his cave (48).
16. Prophecy of the Lombard sack of Cassino (ch.27): "scarce could I obtain of God to have their lives spared". Fulfilled per Gregory: monks escaped with lives. Cross-check S6: Lombards sacked c.570/581.
- Gregory on cult at the saint's own places: at the cave "even to this very time, worketh miracles, if the faith of them that pray requireth" (ch.47). No miracle after death is listed at the tomb in Gregory.
- Mark all of the above GREGORY. Church has never "approved" each one individually; the Church honors the Dialogues as the source (S2 calls it "the only authentic life"). Peter's comparison: Moses (water), Elisha (axe), Peter (Maurus), Elijah (raven) [S1 ch.17].

## Cult and relics
- Burial: Monte Cassino, oratory of St John the Baptist, with Scholastica [S1 ch.44, 47].
- Lombard sack c.570/581, monks flee to Rome [S6]. Petronax refounds c.718 [S6]; Saracens sack 883/884 [S6].
- Fleury claim (CONTESTED): the abbot Mommolus is said to have moved the relics from Monte Cassino to Fleury after the Lombard ruin; first reported in the ninth century by Adrevald of Fleury; Miracula S. Benedicti by five Fleury monks incl. Andrew of Fleury c.1043; Monte Cassino monks dispute it [S7]. S2: "There is some doubt whether the relics ... are still at Monte Cassino, or whether they were moved in the seventh century to Fleury"; Abbot Tosti decides for Monte Cassino. Fleury's reliquary was melted down by Protestant troops in the Wars of Religion [S7]. Fleury (St-Benoit-sur-Loire) is still reputed to hold relics; monks returned in 1944 [S7].
- Monte Cassino destroyed 15 Feb 1944 by Allied bombing; 230 civilians killed, no Germans inside when bombed [S6]. Pius XII, 21 Mar 1947: "Practically nothing else survived from the destruction except the sacred crypt in which the relics of the holy Patriarch are preciously kept." [S4 par.31, Vatican]. S10: relics of Benedict and Scholastica are under the high altar of the main church; effigy tombs in the crypt are cenotaphs (empty). Rebuilt with Einaudi's support in the early 1950s; Paul VI consecrated the rebuilt basilica 24 Oct 1964 [S6]. Archaeology after 1944 found traces of the oratories of St Martin and St John, matching Gregory (de Vogue, quoted in S6).
- Pius XII called him father of Europe (Pacis nuntius cites AAS 39 (1947) p.453 and says Pius XII called him "Europae patrem") [S3].

## Pacis nuntius, 24 Oct 1964 [S3, Latin, exact]
- Title: "SANCTUS BENEDICTUS ABBAS PRINCIPALIS TOTIUS EUROPAE PATRONUS ELIGITUR." Opening: "Pacis nuntius, unitatis effector, civilis cultus magister, maxime vero religionis christianae praeco et monasticae vitae in occidente auctor meritissimo Sanctus Benedictus Abbas praedicatur." (own English: Messenger of peace, builder of unity, teacher of civilization, above all herald of the Christian religion and founder of monastic life in the West.)
- "Cruce, libro, aratro" = by cross, book, plough. Pius XII called him "Europae patrem".
- Operative: "Sanctum Benedictum Abbatem totius Europae principalem apud Deum caelestem Patronum constituimus ac declaramus" (own English: we establish and declare St Benedict, Abbot, principal heavenly patron of all Europe before God), after consulting the Sacred Congregation of Rites. Given at St Peter's 24 October 1964, second year of the pontificate. Same day as the consecration of the rebuilt Monte Cassino basilica ("Montis Casini templum, anno MCMXLIV... eversum, sed invida pietate deinde refectum... hodie rursus consecramus"). CHURCH.
- Requested by cardinals, archbishops, bishops, superiors of orders, lay leaders from various parts of Europe. John XXIII had also wished it.
- 1980: John Paul II made him co-patron of Europe with Cyril and Methodius [S5; not read in a papal text].

## Feast days
- 21 March: pre-1970 Roman calendar; day of death in some manuscripts of the Martyrologium Hieronymianum and Bede [S5]. Always fell in Lent. 
- 11 July: since the 1969 reform (Roman calendar, memorial); from Gallic liturgical books of the late 8th century (Natalis S. Benedicti); origin uncertain [S5]. Lutherans and Anglicans also 11 July [S5].
- 14 March: Eastern Orthodox and Byzantine Catholic [S5, S9]. S9 synaxarion says Benedict "raised the dead, foretold the future" and tells his death from Gregory.
- Scholastica: feast not read in a source (to check; 10 February).

## Patronage
- Europe (Paul VI 1964, CHURCH) [S3]; co-patron 1980 [S5].
- Against poison; schoolchildren and students; speleologists/cavers; farmers (on Tenerife: patron of fields and farmers, big romeria) [S5 infobox and text]. Associations: poison, from the cup and loaf episodes (LEGEND-derived). Cave link: Subiaco cave (Gregory).
- Italian architects, peasants per S5 note (a "Little Book of Saints").

## Medal of St Benedict [S8]
- Origin: first date unclear. About 1647 a 1415 manuscript at Metten Abbey (Mettener Armenbibel, BSB Clm 8201) showed Benedict with cross-staff and scroll, which explained the initials. Medals struck in Germany; approved by Benedict XIV 23 Dec 1741 and 12 Mar 1742. The 1880 Jubilee medal (Desiderius Lenz, Beuron) for the 1400th anniversary is the popular design; inscription "EX SM Casino MDCCCLXXX". Initials of the exorcism formula on medals "at least since 1780". The formula appears in a thirteenth-century legend of the Devil's Bridge at Sens (S8). Status: sacramental, LEGEND/devotion, not a Gregory miracle.
- Reverse: Benedict with cross in right hand, Rule in left; poisoned cup (shattering) on one side, raven with loaf on the other; "Crux sancti patris Benedicti"; "Eius in obitu nostro praesentia muniamur!" (May we be strengthened by his presence at our death).
- Obverse: cross with C S S M L (Crux sacra sit mihi lux), N D S M D (Non draco sit mihi dux), C S P B in quadrants, PAX; around the rim V R S N S M V - S M Q L I V B = Vade retro Satana! Nunquam suade mihi vana! Sunt mala quae libas. Ipse venena bibas! ("Begone Satan! Never tempt me with your vanities! What you offer is evil. Drink the poison yourself!").
- 1959 Sacred Congregation of Rites allowed the medal in place of a True Cross relic for the Blessing of St Maurus; any priest or deacon may bless it [S8].

## Character (from Gregory's miracles)
- Humble in miracle: refuses to claim Maurus's walk; "such miracles are not for us" (S1 ch.14, 42).
- Grieves for enemies (Florentius); penance for the joy of Maurus.
- Strict yet gentle; can be overruled by his sister's prayer (ch.43). 
- Gregory: "he that, sitting still, did appease the fury of that cruel Goth" (Zalla, ch.41).

1. Nurse and the broken sieve at Enside; sieve hung over the church door.
2. Romanus lowering bread by rope with a bell; devil breaks the bell with a stone (ch.5).
3. Priest told in a vision to bring Easter dinner to Benedict, who did not know it was Easter (ch.6).
4. The poisoned glass shatters at the sign of the cross (ch.9).
5. The crow hops and cries, will not carry the loaf, finally does (ch.15).
6. Totila's trick: Riggo in royal shoes and robes (ch.24).
7. Maurus runs over the lake and seizes Placidus by the hair (ch.14).
8. Scholastica lays her head on her hands; the storm comes (ch.43).
9. Night at the tower window: the world in one sunbeam (ch.45).
10. Death in the oratory, held up by his disciples, hands raised (ch.47).
