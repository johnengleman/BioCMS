# Check of miracles.html (Benedict of Nursia, 43 accounts)

Original kept as `miracles.html.before-check`. Fixed in place. Lint: `--kind miracles` shows no MUST FIX (one CHECK line remains: average sentence length 11.6, because the accounts are short fact stories).

Sources read as raw text: Gardner 1911 (archive.org cu31924029222076, djvu text, Book II and notes), Luck 1880 (archive.org LifeMiraclesOfStBenedict, for the 48-chapter numbers), Catholic Encyclopedia 1907 and 1912 (New Advent), Guéranger 1880 (archive.org medalorcrossstb01gugoog), Wikipedia Fleury Abbey and Monte Cassino (raw wikitext). Gregory's Latin could not be reached (no usable online text found), so every Latin remark rests on the notes.

## Confirmed as the reviser wrote it
- Wall account: Gregory says the devil's fall of the wall killed ("slew") the young monk, and Benedict restored him in the same hour. Sack, stones, courtier's son all in text. No tower.
- Totila: Gardner's note says the visit was "probably" 542. Gregory gives no year.
- Billhook Goth: unnamed in Gregory ("a certain Goth"). Gardner footnote: Zalla is the Goth of ch. 31, not this one.
- Two flasks: text has "two wooden bottles, commonly called flagons".
- Meal in sacks at the cell door: text has "in sacks before his cell door".
- Fleury: Wikipedia says the Miracula were "developed over three centuries by five monks of Fleury, including Andreas (c. 1043)". Adrevald first reported the transfer in the ninth century. Monte Cassino disputes it.
- Canosa: Gardner's note: "Camisina" is Canusium, modern Canosa.
- Gardner dates the sack of Monte Cassino to 589. Scholastica 10 Feb 543 is tradition (Gardner note to ch. 33).
- Luck chapter numbers all checked against Luck's chapter titles. Two were missing and are now added: the monk at prayer (Luck 11) and "blessed and cursed Bennet" (Luck 18).
- Death-year range (543, about 544, 547) is in notes-witnesses.md. "Theologians debated" is in the Catholic Encyclopedia (Gregory and Bonaventure vs. Thomas; Urban VIII).

## Fixes
1. Zalla (ch. 31). The victim was "a poor country man", not a farmer. Now "a poor countryman". The old last sentence ("Gregory praises a man who stayed seated") hid that the man is Benedict, and the account lacked the story. Rewritten from the text: Zalla persecuted Catholic monks and priests, tortured the man for his money, drove him bound to the abbey, found Benedict reading at the gate, and the cords fell. Benedict had Zalla fed and told him to stop. Gregory says this was by power, not prayer.
2. Dead child (ch. 32). "A farmer" changed to "a countryman" (text: "a country man"). Note: biography.html line 167 also says "a farmer". I did not touch the biography.
3. Monk at prayer (ch. 4). The old text said only Benedict saw the boy until Maurus did. In the text Benedict asked Pompeianus and Maurus; they did not see him; after two days Maurus did, and Pompeianus did not. Rewritten to match. Heading changed ("healed" to "made steady"; text says "reformed"). Luck number added.
4. Kitchen fire (ch. 10). The monks did not dig on their own: Benedict ordered the digging, the idol was thrown into the kitchen, Benedict "bowed his head to his prayers". Fixed.
5. Stone (ch. 9). "Building the abbey" became "building the cells of the abbey" (text).
6. Rome prophecy (ch. 15). "Around the same time" is not in the text. Now: the bishop of Canosa was a friend of Benedict and spoke with him about Totila. Status line now says the 1608 English has "Camisina" and Gardner says Canosa.
7. Totila (ch. 15). Totila did not just "fall": he saw Benedict far off and did not dare come near, and Benedict spoke to him two or three times before he rose. Added.
8. Riggo (ch. 14). The attendants did not dare come near after they rose (text). Status line adds that the Catholic Encyclopedia says 543, and the source line adds it.
9. Aquinum (ch. 16). Added the bishop who sent him to the shrines and that he was brought to Benedict. "Died possessed" became "the devil came back and tormented him until he died" (text).
10. Theoprobus quotation (ch. 17). Now matches biography.html word for word: "All this abbey that I built, and everything I prepared for my brothers, God has judged will be handed over to the pagans, to be plundered and torn down. I could hardly win from God that the lives of the monks living in it then should be spared." Added that he asked why (biography: "asked him what was wrong"). Status line: "other sources give earlier dates" is now "Wikipedia gives about 570 and also 580". Wikipedia Monte Cassino added to the source line.
11. Snake (ch. 18). Exhilaratus was a lay brother sent with the flasks of wine; the flasks were wooden (text).
12. Handkerchiefs (ch. 19). The monk took them at the nuns' request, not "from them" as if stolen. Benedict's second question and the monk's fall at his feet are in the text and are added. Heading "took" changed to "kept".
13. Candle (ch. 20). "While he ate" changed to "at supper" (text).
14. Meal (ch. 21). "No one ever found out" is stronger than the text ("unknown to this very day"). Now: Gregory says God sent them, and no one knew who brought them, even in his day.
15. Terracina (ch. 22). The old text said Benedict sent monks to found it. The text: a good man asked for an abbey on his land; Benedict sent an abbot, a prior, and monks; he promised a day; he appeared in their sleep the night before; they waited; they came and asked; he answered with the quoted question. Rewritten from the text.
16. Valentinian's brother (ch. 13). The old text said "one of the four disciples who gave Gregory his facts" about Valentinian and left out the story. Gregory says "Valentinian the monk, of whom I made mention before" (the prologue source). Gardner says only "probably" the abbot of the Lateran. The text now follows Gregory. Added the companion who urged him three times, and Benedict's question.
17. Leper boy (ch. 26). Text: "his father's boy", hair fell off, body swelled. Old "a boy of his father's household" was a guess. Now close to the text.
18. Shillings (ch. 27). "Gregory had the story from Peregrinus" is too strong; the text says a disciple "used to tell" it, and Gregory then says he turns to "such things as I had from the mouth of his own scholars". Fixed in text and status. "On the chest where the abbey kept its corn" became "the abbey's chest, which was full of corn" (text). Added that Benedict had twelve given to the man.
19. Poisoned man (ch. 27/38). Added the many-colored skin and the touch. Status: told by Gregory from his disciples (text).
20. Devil as a doctor (ch. 30). Order of events fixed: he was going to St. John's chapel, the devil went on and entered an old monk drawing water, Benedict returned and freed him. The old text said "Later" and left out the chapel.
21. Florentius (ch. 8). Benedict "wept" changed to "grieved" (text: "passing sorrowful, and lamented much").
22. Fleury. Added who made the claim (Adrevald, Mommolus). "The monks of Fleury said" and "miracles done at his shrine" are not in the source and were cut. "Over three centuries five monks" kept.
23. Metten. "Some people" changed to "women" (Catholic Encyclopedia: "the accused women"). The 1415 manuscript: the CE says the words were written on "the staff and scroll". Guéranger says the manuscript was in the library of Metten monastery; the old text had it "at Metten" and now says only "a manuscript of 1415".
24. Priest at Easter (ch. 1). "In a vision" changed to "The Lord appeared to him" (text).
25. Glass (ch. 3). "Rule of the house" changed to "rule of monastic life" (text: "regular life should be observed").
26. Billhook (ch. 6). "Flew off" changed to "slipped off" (text).
27. Dragon (ch. 25). "In anger" added (text).
28. Dove (ch. 34). "Three days after the storm" changed to "after that visit". Gregory says the sister returned next day and "three days after" he saw the dove.
29. World in one ray (ch. 35). Servandus is "a deacon and the abbot of a monastery in Campania". Catholic Encyclopedia added to the source line (it carries the debate).
30. Lamps (ch. 37). Added the man at the top and the question the monks answered "no". "One in his own cell" made clearer.
31. Cave woman (ch. 38). Added that she wandered, left in her right mind, and stayed well until she died (text).
32. Enfide. Heading and text now say "Enfide" (the spelling of the biography and of Gardner's note). The anchor id was not changed.
33. Straight sentences: several bent sentences (two phrases before the subject, words between subject and verb) were rewritten. The only lint check still showing is average sentence length.

## Not changed, and open
- "Solidi" (Latin) in the shillings status line rests on the notes only. I could not reach the Latin. It is correct as far as I know, but I did not confirm it.
- The 1415 manuscript and the 1741/1742 approvals rest on the Catholic Encyclopedia (Ott 1912) and Guéranger. Both agree with the page.
- Gardner's text has no "Monte Cassino" in ch. 8 or ch. 10; Gregory writes "Cassino". The page's "Monte Cassino" is the usual name and matches the biography.
- The quotation of the Rome prophecy, the Totila prophecy, and the other quoted lines are in plain modern English and agree in meaning with the 1608 text. I did not find a quotation to cut.
- biography.html line 167 says "a farmer" for the dead child. The text says "a country man". Left for the biography's owner.
