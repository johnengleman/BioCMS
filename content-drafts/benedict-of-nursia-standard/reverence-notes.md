### God as the one who acts; prayer and thanks restored

- Opening: "Then God spoke to him in a vision" → "Then the Lord appeared to him and spoke." Ch. 1: "our Lord vouchsafed to appear unto a certain Priest ... and spake thus unto him."
- Sieve: "when he got up the sieve was whole" → "He rose from prayer, and God had made the sieve whole." Ch. 1: "with tears fell to his prayers; and after he had done, rising up he found it so whole." The Church's framing of God's work, per point 1 of the rule.
- Sieve, the townsmen's reason restored: "so that those who came after would know how greatly God's grace had worked in him from the start." Ch. 1: "to the end that not only men then living, but also their posterity might understand, how greatly God's grace did work with him upon his first renouncing of the world." "It hung there until" → "it was still there until" (same fact).
- Candle: "as an example to others" → "where it could shine and give light to the Church of God." Ch. 1: "that such a candle, set upon a candlestick, might shine and give light to the Church of God."
- Water from the rock: added Saint Benedict's reason, "because almighty God could bring water out of the top of that mountain" (ch. 5: "for almighty God is able to bring forth water in the top of that mountain"), and "God brought the water out of the rock" for "Water came out of the rock".
- Famine meal: "no one ever found out who had brought them" → "Saint Gregory says almighty God sent them, and no one knew by whose hands. The monks gave thanks to God." Ch. 21: "which almighty God sent them: but by whom, or what means, that is unknown to this very day: which miracle when the monks saw, they gave God thanks." (The old "no one ever found out" was also stronger than the source; the miracles checker had noted this.)
- Dead child: the father's plea and Saint Benedict's prayer restored in direct speech, and the order of acts (knelt and lay on the body, rose, lifted his hands). Ch. 32: "No, no, but he is dead: come for Christ Jesus' sake and restore him to life"; "he kneeled down and lay upon the body of the little child, and rising, he held up his hands towards heaven, and said: Behold not, O Lord, my sins, but the faith of this man, that desireth to have his son raised to life, and restore that soul to the body, which thou hast taken away. He had scarce spoken these words, and behold the soul returned back again, and therewith the child's body began to tremble ... Then he took it by the hand and gave it to his father, but alive and in health." Modern English: "He is dead. Come, for the sake of Christ Jesus, and bring him back to life." / "Lord, do not look at my sins, but at the faith of this man who asks for his son's life. Give back to this body the soul you have taken away."
- Saint Scholastica: "and she prayed" → "and she prayed to almighty God" (ch. 33: "she made her prayers to almighty God"). Added before Saint Gregory's lesson: "A woman's prayer had worked that miracle by the power of almighty God, Saint Gregory says." Ch. 33: "a miracle ... which, by the power of almighty God, a woman's prayers had wrought."
- The cave after his death: "miracles still happen ... in the cave" → "God still works miracles through Saint Benedict in the cave where he first lived. They happen in our own time, when the faith of the people who pray there asks for them." Ch. 37: "who also in that cave in which he first dwelled, even to this very time, worketh miracles, if the faith of them that pray requireth the same." "Woke up cured" → "woke up in her right mind" (ch. 38: "as sound in sense and well in her wits").
- Veneration paragraph: "the cave at Subiaco worked miracles" → "God worked miracles in his cave at Subiaco."

Protestant soldiers melted down the reliquary at Fleury in the French wars of religion, and the abbey of Fleury still says it holds his relics." Both claims kept as claims; "reputed" removed.

- Famine meal (ch. 21): "Saint Gregory says God sent them. No one knew who brought them, even in his own day" → "Saint Gregory says almighty God sent them, and no one knew by whose hands even in his own day. When the monks saw it, they gave thanks to God." Source quoted above.
