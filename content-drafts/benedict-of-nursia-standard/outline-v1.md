# Benedict of Nursia: scene outline (tier A)

## 1. The driving question

Benedict wanted to live alone with God, "dwelling with himself" under God's eyes [W-S1 p.57]. People would not leave him alone: shepherds, monks who wanted an abbot and then poisoned him, a jealous priest, noble families with sons, a Gothic king, and his own sister. Grace turned each of these people into his work: he stopped running and built "a school of the Lord's service" [R-Q2], where ordinary people seek God together, and at the end he saw the whole world in one ray of light [W-S1 p.97].

He is famous because his Rule became the basic rule of monks in the West, and because Paul VI named him patron of all Europe in 1964 [C-S3]. In a time of Gothic war and a falling Rome, he wrote a rule that promised "nothing harsh, nothing heavy" [R-Q3] and that Gregory praised for its "discretion" [W-S1 p.99]. Strict hermits and lax monks were both common in his day; he asked for measure, and he told the abbot to try "to be loved more than feared" [R-S1 ch.64].

## 2. Portrait

A quiet, mild man who could be very sharp. When monks tried to poison him, he answered "with a mild countenance and quiet mind" [W-S1 p.57]. He wept when his enemy died and gave Maurus a penance for rejoicing [W-S1 p.66-67]. He saw hidden faults and named them with dry humor: a monk's hidden wine flask holds a snake [W-S1 p.79]; a proud monk hears "Sign your heart, brother" [W-S1 p.80]. His humor shows in the Rule too: guests "are never lacking in a monastery" [R-Q23]; monks today "cannot be persuaded" to give up wine [R-S1 ch.40]. His fault was rigidity: he would not stay one night outside the abbey, and his sister's prayer overruled him [W-S1 p.93-95]. He ran from danger more than he fought it [W-S1 p.67]. He refused credit for miracles: "such miracles are not for us to work, but for the blessed Apostles" [W-S1 p.93]. What surprised people: a young noble in animal skins whom shepherds took for a beast [W-S1 p.54].

His life with God: three years of prayer alone in a cave; prayer at a tower window before the night office [W-S1 p.96]. His own words: "Put nothing before the love of Christ" [R-Q6]; "never despair of God's mercy" [R-Q9]; the monk runs God's way "in the unspeakable sweetness of love" [R-Q4].

## 3. The opening

Hook: scene at the edge (Easter in the cave).

> One Easter Sunday a priest in the hills east of Rome sat down to his feast. In a vision God told him that His servant was going hungry nearby. The priest took his food and searched the rocks until he found a cave. In it lived a young man who had been alone there for three years. The priest told him it was Easter, and asked him to rise and eat. The young man had lost count of the days. "I know that it is Easter with me," he said, "and a great feast, because God has let me enjoy your company today."

Sources: W-S1 p.54; "Rise up, brother, and let us dine" (W- notes, scene 3). Quote modernized from the 1608 English.

Thesis (after the opening, before chapter 1): This hermit spent the rest of his life making a home for other people. He wrote a short rule for monks, which he called "this least little rule for beginners" [R-S1 ch.73], and Gregory the Great praised its discretion [W-S1 p.99]. In 1947 Pius XII called him "father of Europe", and in 1964 Paul VI named him principal patron of all Europe [C-S3]. The biography answers this in chapters 7, 13, 14 and 15.

## 4. Chapters

1. **A Student Leaves Rome to Seek God.** SC-01. Ends: he slips away from his nurse.
2. **Benedict Lives Alone in a Cave.** SC-02, SC-03, SC-04. Ends: shepherds spread his name; monks come asking.
3. **Monks Try to Poison Their New Abbot.** SC-05. Ends: he goes back to the cave, but disciples follow.
4. **Benedict Builds Twelve Monasteries at Subiaco.** SC-06. Ends: a priest nearby starts to envy him.
5. **A Jealous Priest Drives Benedict Away.** SC-07. Ends: he walks south to a mountain with a pagan shrine.
6. **Benedict Founds the Abbey of Monte Cassino.** SC-08. Ends: the community grows and needs a rule.
7. **Benedict Writes a Rule for His Monks.** No full scene; the flask and the "Sign your heart" moments show the Rule lived. Ends: news of his gifts reaches a king.
8. **A Gothic King Comes to Test Benedict.** SC-09. Ends: he foretells Rome's decay, and then grieves over his own house.
9. **Benedict Weeps Over His Abbey's Future.** SC-10. Ends: his sister comes for her yearly visit.
10. **Benedict's Sister Prays Down a Storm.** SC-11. Ends: he lays her in his own grave.
11. **Benedict Sees the World in One Ray of Light.** SC-12. Ends: he tells his monks the day of his death.
12. **The Death of Benedict.** SC-13. Ends: his prophecy over the abbey waits.
13. **Monks Carry Benedict's Story to Rome.** SC-14. Ends: Gregory's book and the Rule begin to travel.
14. **How Benedict's Rule Spread Through the Church.** No full scene; relics, medal, feasts. Ends: the abbey falls once more.
15. **Monte Cassino Is Destroyed and Rebuilt.** SC-15. Closes the loop with the cave and the Easter guest.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — The broken sieve (Effide, St Peter's church)
- What happens: The young man has left Rome and its schools; his nurse alone follows. She breaks a borrowed sieve and weeps; he prays and it is whole. The town hangs it at the church door.
- Why it is here: he left study and wealth for God, yet his first act is mercy to a weeping servant; fame starts at once, and he runs.
- Have: leaving Rome, "learned ignorance … unlearned wisdom" [W-S1 p.51]; sieve [W-S1 p.52; C-S1 ch.3].
- Need: Gregory's exact words on why he left the schools; his age (W-S2 argues 19–20).
- Testimony: W-S1 p.51-52.
- Where to look: W-S1 prologue and ch.1.

### SC-02 — Bread on a rope (Subiaco cave)
- What happens: The monk Romanus keeps his secret, gives him a habit, and lowers bread on a long rope with a bell. A thrown stone breaks the bell.
- Why it is here: the solitude he wanted, and its cost.
- Have: W-S1 p.53-54; C-S1 ch.5.
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.1.

### SC-03 — Easter in the cave
- What happens: The opening scene, retold in chapter 2.
- Why it is here: his life with God.
- Have: W-S1 p.54; C-S1 ch.6.
- Need: the priest's full words.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.1.

### SC-04 — The blackbird and the briers
- What happens: A small black bird flies at his face; the memory of a woman returns; he throws himself naked into briers and nettles. He never felt that temptation again. He told this to his disciples himself.
- Why it is here: a trial of faith, from his own mouth.
- Have: W-S1 p.55; "by the wounds of his body…".
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.2.

### SC-05 — The poisoned glass
- What happens: Monks of a nearby monastery beg him to be abbot. He warns them; they insist. He enforces the rule; they poison his wine. He blesses the glass and it shatters. He leaves.
- Why it is here: his first failure as a father; he learns he cannot force a way of life.
- Have: words "Almighty God have mercy upon you…" [W-S1 p.57]; "Go your ways, and seek ye out some other Father" [C-S1 ch.9]; "dwelt with himself" [W-S1 p.57].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.3.

### SC-06 — Maurus runs on the lake
- What happens: Noble boys Maurus and Placidus join. Placidus falls into the lake; Benedict knows it in his cell. Maurus runs on the water and pulls him out by the hair. Each credits the other.
- Why it is here: the hermit is now a father of a school; obedience and humility in action.
- Have: twelve houses, nobles' sons [W-S1 p.60-61]; lake [W-S1 p.64-65; C-S1 ch.14].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.3 end, ch.7.

### SC-07 — The crow and the poisoned loaf
- What happens: The priest Florentius sends a poisoned loaf; Benedict orders his tame crow to carry it away. Florentius then sends women to dance in the yard to tempt the young monks. Benedict leaves. Florentius dies when his room falls; Benedict weeps and gives Maurus a penance for rejoicing.
- Why it is here: he gives up his own work rather than lose his monks; he grieves for his enemy.
- Have: W-S1 p.65-67; C-S1 ch.15-17. Women "naked" in Gregory; tell plainly and briefly.
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.8.

### SC-08 — The shrine of Apollo
- What happens: On the mountain above Cassino, country people still worship Apollo. He breaks the idol, burns the grove, and builds chapels of St Martin and St John. A voice cries "Cursed Bennet, and not blessed".
- Why it is here: the home he builds for his monks.
- Have: W-S1 p.68-69; archaeology found the two chapels [C-S6].
- Need: date of founding (528/529 is inference, W-S1n).
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.8 end.

### SC-09 — The king in borrowed shoes
- What happens: Totila sends his guardsman Riggo in royal robes. Benedict: "Put off, my good son…". Totila comes himself, lies on the ground; Benedict raises him and rebukes him.
- Why it is here: the man who fled the world now speaks to a king as a father.
- Have: W-S1 p.73-75; C-S1 ch.24-25; the only fixed date, 543 [W-S2; W-S3].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.14-15.

### SC-10 — Weeping over the abbey
- What happens: His friend Theoprobus finds him weeping bitterly. The whole abbey will be given to the pagans; he could barely win the monks' lives.
- Why it is here: a hard part; his life's work will fall, and he accepts it.
- Have: words [W-S1 p.78-79; C-S1 ch.27].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.17.

### SC-11 — Scholastica's storm
- What happens: At their yearly meeting near the abbey, his sister begs him to stay the night. He refuses. She prays with her head on her hands, and a storm breaks. They talk all night. Three days later he sees her soul rise as a dove.
- Why it is here: his rigidity is overruled by love; "she did more which loved more".
- Have: W-S1 p.93-96; C-S1 ch.43-44.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Gregory's whole chapter, W-S1 p.93-95.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.33-34.

### SC-12 — The tower window
- What happens: With the abbot Servandus as guest, he prays at night at a window. A light brighter than day shows him the whole world "under one beam of the sun" and a bishop's soul carried to heaven. The bishop died that hour.
- Why it is here: the answer to the driving question; the soul that sees God sees all creation as small.
- Have: W-S1 p.96-98; C-S1 ch.45; Benedict told it himself.
- Need: a clean text of the "one beam" line (OCR garbled).
- Testimony: W-S1 p.96-98.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.35.

### SC-13 — Death standing in the chapel
- What happens: He opens his grave six days before. Fever. On the sixth day monks carry him to the chapel; he receives Communion and dies standing, held by them, hands raised in prayer. Two monks see a road of lamps to heaven.
- Why it is here: the death; his last act is prayer among his brothers.
- Have: W-S1 p.99-100; C-S1 ch.47.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: W-S1 p.99-100.
- Where to look: W-S1 ch.37.

### SC-14 — The monks escape to Rome (589–593)
- What happens: Lombards sack Monte Cassino at night; no monk dies. Monks reach Rome; Valentinian runs the Lateran abbey. In 593 Pope Gregory writes Benedict's life from four disciples.
- Why it is here: the prophecy is fulfilled; this is how we know him.
- Have: W-S1 p.51, p.79; W-S1n p.xx (593; 589).
- Need: the sack date (589 vs c.570/581, C-S6); Gregory's letter to Maximianus.
- Where to look: Paul the Deacon, *Historia Langobardorum* iv.17; W-S4 vol1 p.321.

### SC-15 — Bombs and a new church (1944–1964)
- What happens: Allied bombs destroy Monte Cassino on 15 February 1944; the crypt with the relics survives. On 24 October 1964 Paul VI consecrates the rebuilt church and names Benedict patron of Europe.
- Why it is here: the real case for the ending; the house he wept over keeps rising.
- Have: C-S6; C-S4 par.31; C-S3.
- Need: whether Paul VI was at Monte Cassino that day; a named witness of the bombing.
- Where to look: C-S6 refs; C-S3; vatican.va homily of 24 Oct 1964.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** the Rule, date unknown; Gregory: "excellent for discretion" [W-S1 p.99]. Ch. 7, 14.
- **Changes:** the Rule as the basic rule of Western monks [C-S3]; no sign of it in Gaul for nearly 100 years [W-S4]. Ch. 14.
- **Fame:** Gregory's *Dialogues* 593 [W-S1n]; relics claimed by Fleury from the 9th century [C-S7]; medal from 1647 Metten, approved 1741/1742 [R-S6; C-S8]; patron against poison [C-S5]. Ch. 13, 14.
- **Path to sainthood:** early cult at the cave [W-S1 p.100]; feast 21 March in early martyrologies [C-S5]; 11 July since 1969; 14 March in the East [C-S5; C-S9]; patron of Europe 1964 [C-S3]; co-patron 1980 [C-S5]. Ch. 14, 15.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Gregory gives no dates at all. Death 543 (W-S1n, W-S2) or 547 (C-S5); say "about".
- Totila 543 is the only fixed point [W-S2]; Gregory implies before Rome fell (Dec 546).
- The poisoned cup (monks) and the loaf (Florentius) are separate attacks. The bird is a crow, not a raven.
- Gregory does not name Vicovaro; say "a monastery nearby".
- Do not name the billhook Goth "Zalla"; Zalla is the cruel Goth of ch.31.
- Totila and Rome prophecies were told after the events; Honoratus heard the Rome one from other monks.
- "Ora et labora" is not in the Rule.
- Relics: Monte Cassino and Fleury both claim them.
- C-S1 chapter numbers differ from W-S1; quote by W-S1 page. Modernize "Bennet" and all 1608 English.
