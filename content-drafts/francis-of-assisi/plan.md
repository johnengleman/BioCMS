# Plan: St. Francis of Assisi

## The driving question

In a Perugia prison around 1202, a rich merchant's son laughed in his chains and told the others, "I shall be worshipped by the whole world." He wanted glory, first as a knight. What stood in the way was in himself: he could not bear to look at lepers, and he was his father's son, made for trade and fine clothes. Grace turned him the other way. The Lord led him among the lepers, and what had been bitter became "sweetness of soul and body" (Testament). From then on he went down: naked before the bishop, begging, owning nothing, "subject to all", until on La Verna his body bore the marks of the Crucified and he died on the bare ground in a lent habit. The boast came true, by the opposite road.

The hook raises the question (the boast in chains). The Testament's leper sentence names the answer early (ch. 3). The ending resolves it: a pope who had been his friend kisses his tomb, Celano reports kings kissing his bolster, and eight centuries on a whole year is kept for him; but the last word is his own: "serve him with great humility."

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: the merchant's son of Assisi who gave up everything to live the Gospel in poverty, founded the Friars Minor, and is the first saint known to have borne the stigmata.
Set apart: he chose not a monastery but the road, the lepers, and begging, and wrote a Rule in which the brothers own nothing. He saw all creatures as brothers and sisters and praised God for them in the everyday Italian of Umbria.

## The hook

Type: paradox plus scene. The prisoner's boast and the hated knight he befriends; then the flash-forward to 1228 and Celano's "ran to kiss and worship the bolster"; then the question and the Testament's one-sentence answer. Answered in ch. 3 (lepers), ch. 12 (La Verna), ch. 14 (death), ch. 16–17 (canonization, legacy).
