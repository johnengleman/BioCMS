# Francis of Assisi — notes: church-miracles
Researcher: church-miracles focus. Date: 2026-10-01. Tier A.
Status key: CHURCH-APPROVED = named in the canonization process or papal act; EARLY LIVES = reported by Celano or Bonaventure only; LEGENDARY = later or devotional tradition; CONTESTED = doubted then or now.
Note: the Church "approved" Francis's cause as a whole in 1228. The papal bull cites "many brilliant miracles" but names none. So every named miracle below is EARLY LIVES unless marked otherwise. Celano says the miracles of 127-150 are those read before Gregory IX and proclaimed to the people [S1 §127].

## 1. Identity (only the parts I verified)
- Born Assisi, 1181 or 1182 (year uncertain); died Assisi, Saturday evening 3 Oct 1226 [S4]. Bonaventure: died "4th of October ... on the evening of Saturday", buried Sunday [S2 ch.15]. Conflict: 3 vs 4 Oct. The Vatican decree of 2026 uses 3 Oct 1226 [S9].
- Tradition: Catholic (Western). Feast 4 Oct. Bull Mira circa nos orders the feast "on the fourth of October" [S5 §9].
- Canonized by Gregory IX at Assisi, 16 July 1228, "in the second year of the pontificate" [S1 §126; S4; S5 header]. See conflicts below.
- Patron of Italy, with Catherine of Siena, 18 June 1939, Pius XII, apostolic letter "Licet commissa" [S6, secondary; no Vatican text read].
- Patron of those who promote ecology: John Paul II, Inter Sanctos, "Dado en Roma ... 29 de noviembre ... 1979" [S7]. Requested by Cardinal Silvio Oddi for the "Planning environmental and ecological Institute for quality life" [S7]. AAS 71 (1979) p.1509 [S8, search snippet; AAS not read].

## 3. Timeline (cult, process, relics)
- c.1219 Francis meets the Sultan; Celano says he was "most honourably received" and Lord reserved for him "an unparalleled favour" (the stigmata) [S1 §57]. No fire ordeal in Celano. Bonaventure has the fire challenge [S2 ch.9]. Mark: fire ordeal = EARLY LIVES (Bonaventure only).
- Christmas 1223 Greccio crib. Celano: "the third year before the day of his glorious decease" [S1 §84]. Vatican 2026 decree calls it the 800th anniversary of the first Nativity Scene [S9].
- 14 Sep (c.1224), La Verna: seraph vision and stigmata. Celano gives "two years before" death, no date [S1 §94]. Bonaventure gives the Exaltation of the Cross, early morning [S2 ch.13]. CE: "on or about" 14 Sep [S4]. Vatican 2026 decree: stigmata on Mount Verna "two years before his death" [S9].
- 3 Oct 1226: death at Portiuncula area; a brother (unnamed) sees his soul rise as a star on a white cloud [S1 §110]. NEW to English popular pages: Celano asks to have the Gospel of John read, "Six days before the Passover".
- 4 Oct 1226: body carried to Assisi in procession with olive boughs; stop at San Damiano so Clare and her sisters see him [S1 §116-117; S2 ch.15]. Laid first in San Giorgio [S2; S4].
- Elias's encyclical letter: announced the death and the stigmata to the order [S12]. Text not read.
- 1226-28: miracles at the tomb. Celano lists the first: a girl with bent neck, healed under the coffin on the day of burial [S1 §127].
- 1228 (spring-summer): Gregory IX leaves Rome (Roman revolt), goes to Rieti, Spoleto, visits the Poor Ladies, comes to Assisi; the cardinals examine miracles; a second meeting at Perugia; the cardinals say miracles are not needed to prove a life "which we have seen with our eyes, handled with our hands" [S1 §122-124]. Bonaventure: the Pope had the miracles examined "by those members of the College of Cardinals who were least favourable" [S2 ch.15].
- 16 Jul 1228 (Sunday): canonization at San Giorgio, Assisi. Sermon text "As the morning star amid vapour" (Ecclus 50). Subdeacon Ottaviano reads the miracles; Cardinal Rainerio Capocci preaches on them; Pope weeps; formula, then Te Deum [S1 §125-126]. Pope kisses the tomb.
- 17 Jul 1228 (? date to check): Pope lays first stone of the basilica. CE says "the day following" [S4]. Not in Celano; check.
- 25 May 1230: body moved from San Giorgio to the Lower Basilica in procession by Elias; buried "far down under the high altar" [S4; S11]. Bonaventure gives 21 May 1230 [S2]. Conflict.
- Lower Basilica finished 1230; Upper begun after 1239 and done 1253; both consecrated by Innocent IV 1253 [S11].
- Burial hidden afterwards. 12 Dec 1818: coffin found after a search of 52 nights (CE) [S4]. Pius VII reign: crypt built so the faithful can visit [S11]. Order keeps a special office on 12 Dec [S4]. NEW: need Vatican or Franciscan source for details of opening and the 1818 examination (not found).
- 18 Jun 1939: patron of Italy [S6]. 4 Oct 2025: Italian Parliament passed a law making 4 Oct a national feast [S6].
- 29 Nov 1979: patron of ecology [S7].
- 16 Mar 2013: Pope Francis explains his name. See Quotations.
- 10 Jan 2026 to 10 Jan 2027: Year of St Francis, set by Pope Leo XIV, 800th anniversary of death [S9].

## 4. Character (as shown in the miracle accounts)
- Hid the stigmata: Celano says "he concealed the stigmata most diligently" [S1 §95]. Hid them even from his brothers.
- His motto for hidden things: "I have hidden Thy words in my heart" Celano says he used this verse as a signal to send visitors away [S1 §96].
- Bonaventure: custom "Secretum meum mihi" (my secret is mine), broken only at Illuminato's urging [S2 ch.13].
- Humor in the plain wonders: bleating "Bethlehem" like a sheep; licking his lips at the name of Jesus [S1 §86].
- Spoke to swallows: "My sisters, the swallows, it is now time for me to speak too" [S1 §59].
- Spoke to the fire before the cautery: "O, brother fire ..." [S2 ch.5].

## 6. Scenes (max 10)
1. 4 Oct 1226: the funeral procession halts at San Damiano. The coffin is opened at the little window where the nuns take Communion. Clare comes "with her daughters to look upon the father that spake not to them" [S1 §116].
2. Same day, Assisi: the knight Jerome, "learned and prudent", pushes the nail-heads with his own fingers in front of the townspeople. Then he swears on the Gospels [S2 ch.15].
3. The white body: limbs soft "like those of an innocent child", black nails in white flesh like "little black stones in a white pavement" [S1 §113].
4. Pope Gregory's dream: Francis, stern, lifts his arm, shows the side-wound, and orders a vessel to be filled with its blood. The Pope holds it brimful [S2 ch.15].
5. July 1228: the Pope, weeping, preaches; subdeacon Ottaviano reads the miracles; the people are "greatly wearied by the suspense" before the decree [S1 §125].
6. Montenero: a crippled boy at the tomb is offered pears by a young friar who vanishes. The boy eats one and is healed [S1 §133].
7. Castel della Pieve: the deaf-mute boy whose tongue seemed cut off says "Saint Francis lives" and "I see Saint Francis standing up there" [S1 §147].
8. The bent beggar of Narni, told in a dream to find a bath, loses the way at night and a voice puts him right [S1 §135].
9. Ilerda: John, stabbed so deeply the pus put out six candles; Francis in friar's habit enters by the window and unwraps the bandages [S2 ch.15].
10. Rome: the matron sees stigmata appear on a painted portrait; then she doubts, and they vanish [S2 ch.15].

## 7. Miracles (one line each; status shown)
### A. In his life
- Fire softened for cautery of the face (eyes), "I have neither felt heat" [S2 ch.5]. EARLY LIVES (Bonaventure).
- Spring from a rock for a thirsty muleteer; "never was flowing water in that place before" [S2 ch.6 "Love of Poverty"]. EARLY LIVES.
- Water turned to wine at Sant'Urbano near Narni; he was cured by tasting it [S1 §61]. EARLY LIVES.
- Birds at Bevagna listen and spread wings; sermon to swallows at Alviano [S1 §58-59]. EARLY LIVES.
- Leveret at Greccio runs to his lap; tench near Rieti stays by the boat until he finishes praying [S1 §60-61]. EARLY LIVES.
- Falcon wakes him for the Office, calls him later when he is sick [S2 ch.8]. EARLY LIVES.
- Greccio crib, Christmas 1223: a man sees a lifeless child in the manger revived by Francis [S1 §86]. EARLY LIVES (single visionary). The hay heals animals and eases childbirth [S1 §87]. EARLY LIVES.
- Woman in labour near Arezzo is delivered after the horse-reins he had held are placed on her [S1 §63]. EARLY LIVES.
- Healing at Ascoli: bread he blessed heals the sick; clothes and cords heal [S1 §62-64]. EARLY LIVES.
- Stigmata, La Verna, c.1224 [S1 §94-96; S2 ch.13]. EARLY LIVES / CONTESTED (see section 8).
- Vision of the seraph and cross-sign for 40 days: Bonaventure adds that the snow and rocks did not chill him after he received the stigmata [S2 ch.13]. EARLY LIVES.
- Opening the gospel three times at the Passion (before the stigmata) [S2 ch.13]. EARLY LIVES.
- Death: soul seen rising as a star on a cloud by an unnamed brother [S1 §110]. EARLY LIVES.
- Body soft and white after death; stigmata seen by "more than fifty brethren together", Clare and sisters, and "an innumerable company of seculars" [S2 ch.13]. EARLY LIVES (Bonaventure's number).

### C. Bonaventure's stigmata-linked miracles (after death)
- Pope Gregory's dream of the side-wound [S2 ch.15]. EARLY LIVES (Gregory said to have told it "with many tears").
- Doubting preacher-friar sees Francis with muddy feet; washes them and touches the nail-places [S2 ch.15]. EARLY LIVES / LEGENDARY.
- Roman matron's picture: stigmata appear, then vanish [S2 ch.15]. LEGENDARY.
- John of Ilerda, Catalonia, healed after dagger wounds [S2 ch.15]. EARLY LIVES.
- Roger, canon of Potenza, doubts at a picture and receives an arrow-like wound in the palm; healed after confession; "authenticated by the Bishop's seal" [S2 ch.15]. EARLY LIVES. The Bishop's seal claim: Bonaventure only.

## 8. Other: stigmata, doubts, conflicts, relics, patronage
Stigmata, status:
- Celano 1 (1228-29) is the earliest narrative; says "marks of nails began to appear", right-side wound bled so that "his tunic and drawers were many times sprinkled", Elias saw it, Rufino touched it [S1 §94-95].
- Bonaventure (1260s) adds the date (Exaltation of the Cross), the six-winged fiery seraph, Illuminatus, Alexander (IV) preaching that he had seen the stigmata himself, Gregory IX's dream, Jerome [S2 ch.13, 15]. He says the brothers, cardinals, and sisters swore on oath [S2 ch.13].
- Bonaventure's stories themselves record doubt among friars and others: Gregory IX, the preacher-friar, Jerome the knight, canon Roger [S2 ch.15]. This shows doubt existed before and after 1228. CONTESTED then; CHURCH-APPROVED later by cult (not by a formal process that I read).
- The bull Mira circa nos does not use the word stigmata. I found no match for "stigma", "wound", or "cross" in the English text. (I searched the text for "stigma|miracle|wound|cross"; only miracle lines hit.) The text only says "many brilliant miracles" [S5 §7-8]. Surprise for the writer.
- Brother Leo's note on the Chartula blessing (CE says it is an account of the stigmata) [S4]. Text not read. Check.
- CE says the stigmata are "better attested than many another historical fact" [S4]; a 1913 Catholic view.
- Modern doubt: Wikipedia reports that "Most cases of stigmata have been the result of trickery and fraud" and that Herbert Thurston attributed them to suggestion [S11, secondary]. Not specific to Francis. Wikipedia also lists Ansbert of Rouen (d.695) as a possible earlier case [S11]. Need a Francis-specific modern source (Sabatier, Vauchez, Frugoni). Not read.
- Feast of the Stigmata (17 Sep) not verified; to check.
Conflicts between sources:
- Canonization day: Celano and the bull header give 16 July 1228. Bull dating clause: "fourteenth Kalends of August", Perugia [S5]. 14 Kal. Aug. equals 19 July in Roman reckoning (my own calculation, not from a source), at Perugia, so the bull was issued at Perugia, after the ceremony. Bonaventure 1868 text says "Sunday the 6th of July" [S2], likely a mistake in print or translation. Explain to the writer.
- Burial transfer: 25 May 1230 [S4, S11] vs 21 May 1230 [S2].
- Death: evening 3 Oct vs "4th of October" [S2]. Church date of the feast 4 Oct [S5].
Relics (not enough read):
- Body in the Lower Basilica crypt since 1230; found 1818; crypt built under Pius VII [S4, S11].
- Celano 1 notes the bolster (France) and the girdle relics [S1 §120].
- Not found in session: Franciscan relics at Santa Chiara (tunic, cord), at Greccio, La Verna; the Chartula. To check.
Ceremonies and people:
- Cardinal Rainerio Capocci preached the miracles, subdeacon Ottaviano read them [S1 §125].
- Bonaventure says Alexander IV said he saw the stigmata alive [S2 ch.13]. Alexander IV: check his 1255 bull (not read).
Pope's name 2013:
- Pope Francis: during the conclave Cardinal Claudio Hummes embraced him and said "Don't forget the poor!"; then he thought of Francis of Assisi, "the man of poverty, the man of peace, the man who loves and protects creation" [S10]. He also mentions Xavier and De Sales as people some thought of [S10].
- Pope Francis's 2013 inaugural homily of 19 March: my grep for "Francis" found nothing; skip.
Patronage extras:
- 2026 Vatican decree: 8th centenary of death; Year of St Francis 10 Jan 2026 to 10 Jan 2027, Pope Leo XIV [S9]. Also names the Portiuncola indulgence (2 Aug) granted by Honorius III [S9].
Image candidates: Giotto, cycle at Upper Basilica (stigmata, Gregory's dream; the dream is a Giotto scene, not checked in session). Public domain.
