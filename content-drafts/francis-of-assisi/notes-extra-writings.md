## 2. Corrections to the first-pass notes

- The Letter to Brother Leo is Epistola VI in Quaracchi (p.116), not Epistola V. Robinson also numbers it VI. [X1 PS p.116; X11 line "VI. To Brother Leo"]
- The Chartula pages are p.124-125 as before; Quaracchi title: "Chartula quam dedit fratri Leoni", with note "Cfr. II. Cel. 2, 18". [X1 PS p.124-125]
- Died on Saturday evening 3 Oct vs 4 Oct: Elias's own letter says "quarto nonas Octobris die Dominica, prima hora noctis precedentis" (4 Oct, a Sunday; first hour of the night before). Calc: 4 Oct 1226 Julian was a Sunday, 3 Oct a Saturday. So Elias dates the death to the night between Saturday and Sunday. [X2 PS p.150; calc]
- The Peace Prayer is not touched by this pass.

## 3. Gap 1: the Latin of the writings, checked against scans

All lines below match the printed page. Spelling and word order follow Quaracchi 1904. Variant letters (a), (b) in the printed text are footnote marks and I have left them out.

### Testament [X1, PS p.77, 79, 82]
Heading in print: "Testamentum S. Patris Francisci". Print p.77 (leaf n96), p.79 (n98), p.82 (n101), all PS.

- p.77: "Dominus ita dedit mihi fratri Francisco incipere facere poenitentiam, quia, cum essem in peccatis, nimis mihi videbatur amarum videre leprosos; et ipse Dominus conduxit me inter illos, et feci misericordiam cum illis. Et recedente me ab ipsis, id quod videbatur mihi amarum, conversum fuit mihi in dulcedinem animi et corporis." Footnote in print: "Cfr. I. Cel. n. 17". PS.
- p.77 end/p.78: "Et postea parum steti et exivi de saeculo." PS p.77 (the sentence is on p.77). Own English: And afterward I stayed a little while and left the world.
- p.79: "Et, postquam Dominus dedit mihi de fratribus, nemo ostendebat mihi, quid deberem facere; sed ipse Altissimus revelavit mihi, quod deberem vivere secundum formam sancti Evangelii. Et ego paucis verbis et simpliciter feci scribi; et dominus papa confirmavit mihi." PS.
- p.79: "Et nolebamus plus habere." / "Et eramus idiotae et subditi omnibus. Et ego manibus meis laborabam et volo laborare" PS. Own English: And we wanted to have no more. And we were uneducated and subject to all. And I worked with my hands and I want to work.
- p.79: "...recurramus ad mensam Domini petendo eleemosynam ostiatim." PS. Own English: ...let us go back to the Lord's table, begging alms door to door.
- p.82: "Et ego frater Franciscus, parvulus vester et servus, quantumcumque possum, confirmo vobis intus et extra istam sanctissimam benedictionem. Amen." PS. Own English: And I, Brother Francis, your little one and servant, confirm to you, as far as I can, inside and outside, this most holy blessing.
- p.82: "sed, sicut dedit mihi Dominus simpliciter et pure dicere et scribere regulam et ista verba, ita simpliciter et pure intelligatis et cum sancta operatione observetis usque in finem." PS (matches first-pass Quote 25).
- Also p.81-82 (OCR; p.82 first line PS): "quia haec est recordatio, admonitio et exhortatio et meum testamentum, quod ego frater Franciscus parvulus facio vobis fratribus meis benedictis propter hoc, ut regulam, quam Domino promisimus, melius catholice observemus." Own English: for this is a reminder, a warning, an encouragement, and my testament, which I, little Brother Francis, make for you, my blessed brothers, so that we may keep the Rule we promised the Lord more faithfully as Catholics. NEW: this is the "do not call it another Rule" passage. [X1 OCR p.81, PS p.82 start]
- Not in the scan check: "Salutationem mihi Dominus revelavit, ut diceremus: Dominus det tibi pacem" is on p.80 (OCR; line 2892-2893 prints "pacm" as OCR error). [X1 OCR]
- Date: Robinson says it was probably written at the Celle near Cortona in summer 1226, or dictated to Angelo Tancredi in the infirmary hut by the Portiuncula; Gregory IX cites it in *Quo elongati*, 28 Sept 1230, and says it was published only a few days before Francis died ("Circa ultimum vitae suae", Bullarium Franciscanum I p.68). [X11 p.79-80] Surprise: the text's 1226 date is thus a scholar's inference, not from the document.

### Last will for Clare, "Ultima voluntas" [X1 PS p.76; leaf n95]
- Heading in print: "Ultima voluntas, quam scripsit S. Franciscus S. Clarae." Text: "Ego frater Franciscus parvulus volo sequi vitam et paupertatem altissimi Domini nostri Iesu Christi et eius sanctissimae matris et perseverare in ea usque in finem. Et rogo vos, dominas meas, et consilium do vobis, ut in ista sanctissima vita et paupertate semper vivatis. Et custodite vos multum, ne doctrina vel consilio alicuius ab ipsa in perpetuum ullatenus recedatis." PS. Own English: I, little Brother Francis, want to follow the life and poverty of our most high Lord Jesus Christ and of his most holy mother, and to hold to it to the end. And I ask you, my ladies, and I give you counsel, that you live always in this most holy life and poverty. And guard yourselves well, that you never depart from it for the teaching or advice of anyone. NEW to popular pages: it is the shortest of his writings to Clare, and survives only because Clare's own Rule (bull of 1253) quotes it. [X1 PS p.76; X11 p.76-78 notes: Clare's Rule ch.6 says Francis "again wrote to us shortly before his death"; the 1253 bull was found in 1893 wrapped in an old mantle of Clare at Santa Chiara]

### Letter to Brother Leo [X1 PS p.116, leaf n135; Epistola VI]
Full text, exact: "F. Leo, F. Francisco tuo salutem et pacem. Ita dico tibi, fili mi, et sicut mater, quia omnia verba, quae diximus in via, breviter in hoc verbo dispono et consilio, et si te post oportet propter consilium venire ad me, quia ita consilio tibi: In quocumque modo melius videtur tibi placere Domino Deo et sequi vestigia et paupertatem suam, faciatis cum benedictione Domini Dei et mea obedientia. Et, si tibi est necessarium propter animam tuam aut aliam consolationem tuam, et vis, Leo, venire ad me, veni." PS.
- Own English: Brother Leo, your Brother Francis wishes you health and peace. I speak to you, my son, as a mother does: all the words we said on the way I settle briefly in this one word and counsel; and if afterward you need to come to me for counsel, this is my counsel to you: in whatever way seems best to you to please the Lord God and to follow his footsteps and his poverty, do it with the blessing of the Lord God and my obedience. And if you need it for your soul, or for some other comfort of yours, and you want, Leo, to come to me, come.
- Notes: the print has a variant mark (a) after "et" and (b) after "post"; the clause "et si te post oportet" is hard grammar, so Quaracchi marks it. "faciatis" is plural though Leo is singular. Robinson explains the odd greeting "F. Leo, F. Francisco tuo": dative, so Leo is the addressee. Autograph: Spoleto cathedral; lost, found 1895, given to Leo XIII, returned about three years later. [X11 p.130; X1 PS p.116]

### Praises of God and the Blessing, the Chartula [X1 PS p.124-125]
Heading: "Chartula quam dedit fratri Leoni", with "Laudes Dei", "Benedictio fr. Leonis", "Oratio". Full Latin of the Praises (PS p.124-125): "Tu es sanctus Dominus Deus solus, qui facis mirabilia. Tu es fortis. Tu es magnus. Tu es altissimus. Tu es rex omnipotens, tu Pater sancte, Rex caeli et terrae. Tu es trinus et unus Dominus Deus, omne bonum. Tu es bonum, omne bonum, summum bonum, Dominus Deus, vivus et verus. Tu es caritas, amor. Tu es sapientia. Tu es humilitas. Tu es patientia. Tu es securitas. Tu es quietas. Tu es gaudium et laetitia. Tu es iustitia et temperantia. Tu es omnia divitia ad sufficientiam. Tu es pulchritudo. Tu es mansuetudo. Tu es protector. Tu es custos et defensor. Tu es fortitudo. Tu es refrigerium. Tu es spes nostra. Tu es fides nostra. Tu es magna dulcedo nostra. Tu es vita aeterna nostra, magnus et admirabilis Dominus, Deus omnipotens, misericors Salvator."
- Blessing (PS p.125): "Benedicat tibi Dominus et custodiat te. Ostendat faciem suam tibi et misereatur tui. Convertat vultum suum ad te et det tibi pacem. Dominus benedicat, f. Leo, te." Print footnote: "Numer. 6, 24-26".
- Prayer after it (PS p.125): "Absorbeat quaeso, Domine, mentem meam ab omnibus, quae sub caelo sunt, ignita et melliflua vis amoris tui, ut amore amoris tui moriar, qui amore amoris mei dignatus es mori." Own English: Lord, I ask that the fiery, honey-sweet power of your love swallow up my mind away from all things under heaven, so that I may die for love of your love, you who agreed to die for love of my love. The print puts it under the Chartula heading; whether it was written on the same parchment I did not check.
- The old notes quoted "Dominus benedicat, f. Leo, te" with "te" written; PS confirms. Quaracchi's own cross-reference for the Chartula is "II. Cel. 2, 18", the scene where the parchment cures Leo's temptation. [X1 PS p.124]
- Leo's marginal note on the parchment (kept in the Sacro Convento) and the 1224 dating come from Robinson p.146-147, as in notes-own-words. Not re-checked.

### Canticle of the Creatures: strophes, with the evidence for dates
- The text I used before (it.wikisource "Cantico di Frate Sole", with the proofreading index "Cantico delle Creature.djvu") has the heading "Incipiunt laudes creaturarum quas fecit beatus franciscus ad laudem et honorem dei, cum esset infirmus apud sanctum damianum." Strophe order in that text: God's praise; brother sun; sister moon and the stars; brother wind, air, cloud; sister water; brother fire; sister mother earth; the strophe on forgiveness ("Laudato si' mi signore per quelli ke perdonano per lo tuo amore, et sostengo infirmitate et tribulatione. Beati quelli ke l'sosterrano in pace, ka da te altissimo sirano incoronati."); sister bodily death; closing "Laudate et benedicete mi signore et rengratiate et serviate li cum grande humilitate." I did not match the Wikisource page against a photograph of MS 338; the first-pass note calls it MS 338 on Robinson's authority, which Robinson states for his own translation (Assisi MS 338 fol. 33). [X11 p.151-153; Wikisource]
- Robinson: the Canticle "appears to have been composed toward the close of the year 1225 in a poor little hut near the Monastery of San Damiano". By tradition, two strophes were added later: the eighth (forgiveness) during the feud between the bishop and the magistrates, and the ninth (death) when Francis knew he was dying. [X11 p.150-151]
- The strophe-8 story in the Mirror of Perfection (c.1318): the Bishop excommunicated the Governor, the Governor forbade all trade with the Bishop, and Francis, sick, said it was "a great reproach for the Servants of God" that they hated each other. He added the verse on pardon and sent two friars to sing it in the Bishop's palace. The Governor "with joined hands and arms, as if at the Gospel of the Lord" heard it; then he said that "had any one slain my kinsman, or my son, him also I would forgive". [X12 ch. X, pp.149-151] LATE source; the Mirror names neither man.
- The strophe-9 story: "Since it pleases my Lord that I am so soon to die, call to me Brother Angelo and Brother Leo that they may sing to me of Sister Death." Before the last verse he added the Sister Death verse. [X12, "Sister Death"] LATE.
- Dates conflict: Robinson 1906 says close of 1225. The Wikipedia summary (facts only) says late 1224. The 2026 Vatican decree counts the Canticle among the 800th anniversaries of "recent years", next to Greccio (1223) and the stigmata (1224), which implies the Church marks it about 1225. [X10] Do not give a day. The Mirror says only that he made it "at the time the Lord had assured him of His Kingdom". [X12 p.177-178]
- Not read: the Latin Compilation of Assisi chapters on the Canticle, the best early source for dates. List as "to check".

## 4. Gap 2: Clare, San Damiano, the Process of 1253 and the Legend

### Legend of St Clare (ascribed to Thomas of Celano, c.1255-1261) [X5 pp.10-18]
Robinson's title page gives A.D. 1255-1261 and says the text is translated from the earliest manuscripts.
- Meeting: "Clare at once was desirous to hear and see him" and Francis, hearing of her fame, "was not less wishful to see her and hold converse with her". She slipped out of her father's house "accompanied by a single confidential companion" so the meetings would not be known. "To her his words seemed a flame and his deeds more than human." [X5 p.10-11; footnote 96: the companion is thought to be her aunt Pacifica Guelfucci]
- He is called "a most faithful bridesman" who persuaded her to keep her maidenhood for Christ. After that "Clare committed herself wholly to the guidance of Francis, considering him to be, after God, the director of her steps." [X5 p.11-12]
- Flight: "The solemnity of Palm Sunday was drawing nigh". Francis told her to go to the blessing of the palms "dressed out and adorned" and leave the camp on the night after. The bishop came down from the steps and put a palm in her hands because she stayed in her place. That night she left by a door that had been walled up with beams and stones and broke it open "with a strength that astonished herself". Friars keeping vigil with lighted torches received her at the Portiuncula, where "her tresses" were cut by the friars. [X5 pp.13-14]. Robinson's note: the night after Palm Sunday 1212 was 18 March; the Legend itself gives no year. [X5 note 101] Calc: 18 March 1212 Julian was a Sunday, so it agrees.
- Francis led her to the Benedictine nuns of San Paolo; her relatives came and she held the altar cloths and bared her tonsured head; after a few days at Sant'Angelo in Panzo she went "by the advice of Blessed Francis" to San Damiano. [X5 pp.15-17]
- The Legend links San Damiano to the crucifix voice: "This is that church for the repair of which St. Francis toiled so wondrously... in which, while Francis was praying, a voice coming down from the wood of the cross resounded". Clare stayed there forty-two years and made it the first house of the Poor Ladies. [X5 p.17-18]
- The Legend has no date for the move to San Damiano.

### Process of Canonization (24 Nov 1253 and following) [X6; Spanish translation, facts only]
- Innocent IV's letter to Bishop Bartholomew of Spoleto is dated "fifteen days before the Kalends of November", year 11 of his pontificate (18 Oct 1253); the bishop heard the first thirteen sisters on 24 November 1253 in the cloister of San Damiano, with Brother Leo, Brother Angelo of Rieti and Brother Mark among the witnesses present. [X6 lines 1-82] NEW: Leo and Angelo (Francis's companions) were in the room.
- Witness 1, Sister Pacifica di Guelfuccio: "by exhortation of Saint Francis" Clare began the Order; she was a neighbor and some relation, with only the square between their houses; she had gone overseas with Clare's mother Hortulana; about forty-two years since conversion; after three years in religion Clare accepted government of the sisters "at the prayers and insistence of Saint Francis, who almost obliged her". [X6 witness I, 2-6]
- Witness 1: Clare once fasted Monday, Wednesday and Friday, so "Saint Francis, together with the bishop of Assisi" ordered her to eat at least half a roll a day, about an ounce and a half. [X6 I.8]. Witness 2 adds that Francis ordered her to eat something each day and that she was present when he did it. [X6 II.8]
- Francis and Clare: Clare's vision, told by witnesses 3, 4, 6: she brought Francis a vessel of hot water and a towel up a high stair, "with such agility as if she walked on level ground"; he drew out his breast and said "Come, take and suck"; the milk was so sweet she could not tell it; what stayed in her mouth looked like clear gold and made her see herself as in a mirror. [X6 III.29, IV.16, VI.13] (Own paraphrase; the first witness to tell it is Filippa, who heard it from Clare.) Dream-vision, status: recorded in the Process.
- Christmas night of the year before her death (1252): Clare, too ill to go to Matins, heard the organs and the whole office from San Francesco as if present, and also saw the crib. [X6 III.30, IV.16] Tie to Greccio and to the basilica.
- Francis sent Brother Stephen, ill with madness, to Clare to make the sign of the cross over him; he slept, woke and left healed. [X6 II.15]
- Francis sent five women to the monastery; Clare took four and refused the fifth, saying she would not stay more than three years; she stayed about half a year (Gasdia, daughter of Taccolo). [X6 VII.15] Prophecy detail, status: recorded in the Process; Francis was still alive then.
- Witness 12, Beatrice, Clare's sister: Francis, hearing of her holiness, "often" came to preach to her; he cut her hair "before the altar, in the church of the Virgin Mary called the Portiuncula" and led her to San Paolo; later "Saint Francis, Brother Philip and Brother Bernard" took her to Sant'Angelo of Panzo and then to San Damiano. [X6 XII.2-5]
- Witness 17, Bona di Guelfuccio: she went secretly with Clare "many times" to speak with Francis; he always urged her to turn to Jesus Christ, and Brother Philip did the same; Clare was about eighteen years old; Bona was not present at the haircut because "she was in Rome for Lent", and Bona says she heard of it. [X6 XVII.3-5] CORRECTION: a web summary says Bona went with Clare to the Portiuncula on Palm Sunday. The Process says she did not. 
- Witness 16, the knight Hugolino di Pietro Girardone, says it was "public and known by all" that Clare entered religion by Francis's preaching and that he heard Francis cut her hair at the Portiuncula. [X6 XVI.3-6]
- Witness 18, Ranieri di Bernardo: relatives urged marriage because she was beautiful; she refused and preached contempt of the world to him. [X6 XVIII.2]
- The Process confirms the Legend on: Francis as the cause of Clare's call; the Portiuncula haircut; San Paolo; Sant'Angelo in Panzo; San Damiano; about forty-two years in religion.
- Dates inside the Process: "about forty-two years" (witnesses 1, 12, 17) counted back from 1253 gives about 1211; Robinson's note gives 18 March 1212. Mark as a one-year difference between "about" and a computed date. [X6; X5 note 101]
- Not read: the Latin Process (ed. Pennacchi/Lemmens), Clare's own writings, or Clare's Testament. The Legend's pages are in X5 as printed with his page headings; I did not view the scans of X5.

## 5. Gap 3: the Sultan meeting

### Jacques de Vitry (1220), two texts
- Letter VI (Feb/March 1220, Latin as printed on the Wursten page from the critical edition; English there is Tolan, copyrighted, so I only paraphrase): the founder of the Order crossed to the enemy camp, preached to the Saracens "for several days" with little success, and "Soldanus autem, rex Egypti, ab eo secreto petiit ut pro se domino supplicaret quatinus religioni, que magis deo placeret, divinitus inspiratus adhereret." Paraphrase: the Sultan asked him privately to pray that he might be moved to join the religion that pleased God more. [Wursten page (modern); Latin text reproduced from the Huygens edition] OCR/transcription issue: the Wursten page has "peti it", which I read as "petiit".
- Historia Occidentalis ch. 32 (Moschus, Douai 1597, printed fol. 353, scan PS leaf n429). The Latin as printed (long s regularized): "Vidimus primum huius ordinis fundatorem magistrum, cui tanquam summo Priori suo omnes alii obediunt, virum simplicem et illiteratum, dilectum Deo et hominibus, fratrem Francinum nominatum, ad tantum ebrietatis excessum et fervorem spiritus raptum fuisse, quod cum ad exercitum Christianorum ante Damiatham in terra Aegypti devenisset, ad Soldani Aegypti castra intrepidus et fidei clypeo communitus accessit. Quem cum in via Saraceni tenuissent, Ego sum Christianus, inquit, ducite me ad Dominum vestrum. Quem cum ante ipsum pertraxissent, videns eum bestia crudelis, in aspectu viri Dei in mansuetudinem conversa, per dies aliquot ipsum sibi et suis Christi fidem praedicantem attentissime audivit. Tandem vero metuens ne aliqui de exercitu suo verborum eius efficacia ad Dominum conversi ad Christianorum exercitum pertransirent, cum omni reverentia et securitate ad nostrorum castra reduci praecepit, dicens ei in fine, Ora pro me, ut Deus legem illam et fidem, quae magis sibi placet, mihi dignetur revelare." PS.
- SURPRISE: the 1597 edition prints "Francinum" (a misprint for Franciscum), and the Latin text carries Francis's reported words "Ego sum Christianus... ducite me ad Dominum vestrum" ("I am a Christian; take me to your lord"). Own English: I am a Christian; lead me to your lord.
- Detail NEW: the Sultan is called "bestia crudelis" who "turned to gentleness at the sight of the man of God"; Jacques gives the Sultan's motive for sending him back as fear that soldiers would convert. These are the oldest eyewitness lines (Jacques was bishop of Acre and was at Damietta). [X3]
- Same page: "Saraceni autem omnes praedictos fratres Minores tam diu de Christi fide et evangelica doctrina praedicantes libenter audiunt, quousque Mahometo, tanquam mendaci et perfido, praedicatione sua manifeste contradicunt. Ex tunc autem eos impie verberantes, et nisi Deus mirabiliter protegeret paene trucidantes, de civitatibus suis expellunt." PS. Own English: the Saracens gladly hear the Minor friars preach about Christ and the Gospel until they speak against Muhammad as a liar and traitor; then they beat them and nearly kill them and drive them from their cities.
- No fire ordeal, no named sultan, no Illuminatus in Jacques. The name al-Kamil is not in Jacques. [X3; Wursten page]

### Ernoul/Bernard the Treasurer (Old French, Mas Latrie 1871, ch. XXXVII, "De .II. clers qui alerent preeschier au Soudain") [X4 pp.431-435, OCR]
- The chronicle does not name Francis. It says two clerks in the host at Damietta asked the cardinal to let them go to the Sultan to preach. The cardinal refused to give permission "à essient" because he knew they would not come back; they said it would be no sin for him if they went; he said "gardés que vo cuer et vos pensées soient tousjours à Dame Diu." [X4 p.431-432] Paraphrase: guard that your heart and thoughts are always with the Lord.
- The Saracen watchmen took them for messengers or renegades and brought them to the sultan; asked if they wished to become Saracens, "sarrasin ne seroient il ja". They offered to prove the law of Muhammad false before his wisest men, and "faites nos les testes coper" if they failed. [X4 p.432-433]
- The Sultan's imams said he must cut their heads off by the Law. The Sultan then said: "je irai .i. poi contre le commandement, ne je ne vous ferai mie les testes colper, car malvais guerredon vous renderoie de çou que vous vos estes mis en aventure de morir pour m'ame". Paraphrase: he would go a little against the command, because it would be bad payment to those who risked death for his soul. [X4 p.434]
- He offered them great land and possessions; they refused; he had gold, silver and silk brought and they took nothing, asking only for food. He had them escorted safely back. [X4 p.434-435]
- Mas Latrie's footnote 2 on p.431 says the two clerks were Saint Francis and another; the summary says the sultan of Damascus, "Coradin". The text itself names neither. Mark as the EDITOR's identification (1871), not Ernoul's. Dates in the margin: 1219 and 1220. So Ernoul and Jacques disagree on which sultan: Jacques says "Soldanus Aegypti"; the editor says Coradin of Damascus.
- Source note: the Ernoul text is a continuation of William of Tyre in French, compiled by well-informed lay nobles in Outremer; the identification with Francis needs a modern scholar. I did not read Tolan or Frugoni.
- Pope Francis in *Fratelli tutti* (3 Oct 2020) writes that Francis went to Sultan "Malik-el-Kamil, in Egypt", a journey with "considerable hardship", and quotes the Earlier Rule: brothers among Saracens should not "engage in arguments or disputes". [X14] This is the Vatican's present naming of the Sultan. [X14 text via vatican.va; one short line only]

## 6. Gap 4: the Rule of 1223 and the bull Solet annuere

- Bull: Honorius III, *Solet annuere*, "given November 29, 1223" at the Lateran, "on the third day of the Kalends of December, in the eighth year of Our Pontificate". [X7] Calc: 3 days before the Kalends of December = 29 November (correct). Calc: 29 Nov 1223 Julian was a Wednesday.
- Opening: "The Apostolic See is accustomed to grant the pious desires and to share her kind favor with the upright desires of those petitioning her." The bull confirms "your rule, approved by Our predecessor, Pope Innocent" and quotes the Rule. [X7]
- Original: the 1223 bull is preserved at the Sacro Convento in Assisi; a copy sits in the papal register at the Vatican. Robinson says he translated the Rule "according to the text of the original Bull". [X11 p.64 note]
- Quaracchi has the Latin Rule (Regula II, 1223); the first-pass notes cite chapter 6 and chapter 10 lines. [X1]
- NAME TRAP: *Solet annuere* is also the name of Innocent IV's bull of 9 August 1253 confirming Clare's Rule. Robinson gives that date; Gilliat-Smith too. Do not confuse the two. [X5 Robinson's introduction ("9 August, 1253"); X13 ("9th of August, 1253"); X11 p.76 note 3 names the bull as Innocent IV's *Solet annuere*]
- Quaracchi also says Clare's rule appears in Gregory IX's *Cum omnis vera* (24 May 1239) and Innocent IV's bull of 13 Nov 1245 (printed as *Solet annuere*), per its footnote. I did not check the 1245 name. [X1 note p.170, OCR] Conflict flag.
- Francis in the Testament: "dominus papa confirmavit mihi" [X1 PS p.79]; Francis promises obedience to Honorius in chapter 1. [X7]

## 7. Gap 5: 1230, the translation, Brother Elias [X2 Wadding, PS leaf n248; OCR leaves around p.232-236]

- Before the translation Gregory IX gave the new church privileges. Wadding's register note: a bull to "the Minister of the Order and his brothers dwelling at the church of blessed Francis in the place called Collis Paradisi" ("Hill of Paradise", renamed from Collis Inferni). Its text says the church is subject only to the Roman pontiff and is "head and mother" of the Order. [X2 OCR p.233-234] The bull's own words, as printed: "Ecclesia ipsa nulli, nisi Romano Pontifici sit subjecta, vestri Ordinis ... caput habeatur et mater". OCR; not scan-checked.
- Wadding says the pope gave a sum of money for the building, gave San Giorgio (where Francis lay) to Clare and her sisters, and named Brother Simon Vereduccio as his procurator to build their monastery inside the walls. [X2 OCR]
- The translation (Wadding): "in profesto Pentecostes die xxv. Maii" ("on the eve of Pentecost, 25 May"). Calc: 25 May 1230 was a Saturday; Pentecost 1230 was 26 May. So the eve fits. The remains were lifted in a wooden chest on a decorated cart drawn by oxen covered in purple. The citizens of Assisi came in force, with guards and soldiers, "fearing that by some trick the treasure be stolen or reduced". [X2 OCR p.234]
- The Minister General and other friars were named the pope's commissaries for the translation; but "the rulers of the city took this task upon themselves", and when they reached the church they stopped and took the body, "in pride and tumult". The friars were not allowed to show the body due veneration, says the pope. [X2 OCR p.234-235]
- The bull Speravimus (Wadding prints the incipit as "Speravimus hactenus... ") [X2 PS p.235 for the end of the bull]. Latin as printed: "Cum enim beatum Franciscum glorificatum in caelis, clarificantes in terris ipsum adscripsimus catalogo Confessorum, in honorem eius Ecclesiam fundari volentes, de manibus nostris lapide ibi primario posito..." (OCR p.234). The bull blames the citizens for seizing the body "aufu sacrilego" and "in superbia et tumultu translationis mysterium damnabiliter prophanarunt". It puts the basilica under the bishop and chapter of Assisi as a penalty, forbids a General Chapter there, lays the place under interdict until satisfaction is made, and gives them fifteen days to send envoys. [X2 PS p.235 for the penalty; OCR for the first part]
- Date conflict: Wadding prints the clause "Datum Laterani xvi. Kalendas Junii Pontificatus nostri anno iv." (PS p.235). That would be 17 May 1230, before the translation, which cannot be right for a bull that punishes it. The Catholic Encyclopedia and Key to Umbria give 16 June 1230; 16 June is 16 days before the Kalends of July (calc), so the printed "Junii" is probably a misprint for "Julii". Mark this as my own inference. [X2 PS p.235; X8; CE Elias]
- Wadding on blame: "some of our friars throw all the blame on Elias"; he says much has been laid on Elias beyond the truth, and that Elias may have worked to hide the body from thieves, so that no one should know "the place where it was hidden, nor the little passage or small door by which one went down to the church below". [X2 OCR p.234-235] This is the source-level explanation for the 1818 search. LATE, partisan.
- Key to Umbria: the Pope was busy with peace talks with the Emperor and sent three cardinals; the early biographies "make no mention of anything untoward"; the friars may have been warned of a Perugian assault. All facts-only, secondary. [X8]
- Date conflicts for the translation: 25 May (Wadding, CE) vs 22 May (CE "three days before the appointed time" in the web summary I did not read raw) vs Bonaventure 21 May (first-pass notes). I could not resolve it.
- Elias: CE says he succeeded Peter of Cataneo as vicar-general (10 March 1221), announced Francis's death and the stigmata in a letter, buried him at San Giorgio, and planned the basilica on the Collis Inferni. The CE says the zealots of the Order turned against him; he was deposed in 1239. [CE Elias, newadvent.org/cathen/05382a.htm, via fetch_text]
- Gregory IX's *Quo elongati* (28 Sept 1230) gave the ruling on the Testament. [X11 p.79-80]
- Later legend in Wadding vol. II p.236-238: a letter, in the form of a story told by a bishop (Jacopo, bishop of Lacedonia/"Laquidonensis") in 1449, tells that Nicholas V went down to the tomb with Cardinal Astorgio and saw the body "standing upright, face to the west, eyes raised to heaven, hands hidden in the habit", with the wounds fresh. [X2 OCR leaf p.236-238] LEGENDARY, 15th century; the Key to Umbria site says a similar story about Sixtus IV "can probably be discounted". [X8] NEW to English popular pages. I did not read the whole story.

## 8. Gap 6: 1818

- After failed searches in 1755 and 1802, and a campaign in 1806 halted by the French in 1808, Pius VII allowed a new search in 1818. The friars had come back in 1814 and rumors said they had found the body. [X8]
- The dig began with the pope's consent on 5 October 1818 and the sarcophagus was found on 12 December 1818 "after some 52 nights of work digging away the solid rock". [X8; the dates and the 52 nights also appear in the first-pass notes from the CE and in the OFM Conv. page.] The first-pass search result had said "12 Dec 1818" only; the 5 Oct detail came from a search snippet and Key to Umbria only says 52 nights.
- Description: a travertine sarcophagus of the Roman period, with no original lid, inside an iron cage of two gratings joined by iron bars. Above it, a massive slab and two more slabs; about 4 meters below the church floor. [X8]
- What lay inside: the body "essentially intact" (Key to Umbria's wording; the official wording would be needed), with a rough stone as a pillow, a few coins dated 1181-1208, small beads from a necklace, and an old silver ring with a carnelian cut with a figure of Minerva (lost). [X8] NEW to English popular pages.
- Authentication: Pius VII named Bishop Francesco Maria Giampè of Assisi and the bishops of Nocera, Spoleto, Perugia and Foligno; he confirmed the relics in the bull *Assisiensem Basilicam* (1820). The first-pass search snippet says the brief is "Ex parte dilecti filii" of 9 Jan 1819; I did not read it. [X8; search snippet]
- Crypt built 1822 to a design by Pasquale Belli; remodelled 1925-32; the bones were wrapped in silk and put in a reliquary, and returned to the sarcophagus in 1824. In 1934 the remains of Leo, Masseo, Rufino and Angelo and the casket of Jacoba dei Settesoli were moved into the crypt. [X8]
- 1978: Paul VI allowed the examination; Prof. Nicolò Valentino Miani found a man about 1.57-1.58 m (5 ft 2 in) tall with foot deformities, and the remains were sealed in a nitrogen case; further checks 1994 and 2015. [X9]
- Source gap: the Vatican/Franciscan primary accounts of 1818 (the reports of the dig, the 1820 bull text) are not read. Cooper, "In loco tutissimo et firmissimo" (2005) and Brooke are named by Key to Umbria; not read.
- 2026: first public extended exposition of the remains, 21 Feb (case moved at 4 p.m. to the lower church) to 22 March 2026, authorized and blessed by Pope Leo XIV; nearly 400,000 booked as of the OFM page's writing; closing Mass 22 March by Cardinal Zuppi. [X9]

## 9. Gap 7: the stigmata, Elias's circular letter [X2 PS p.149-150]

- Provenance (Wadding): the copy of the letter, addressed to the Provincial of France, came to Wadding from the Recollect convent of Valenciennes in Belgium, where the originals are kept; Willem Spoelberch had copied it into his *Speculum vitae sancti Francisci*. [X2 OCR p.148]
- Address as printed (PS p.149): "Dilecto sibi in Christo Fratri Gregorio Ministro Fratrum, qui sunt in Francia, cum omnibus Fratribus suis, et nostris frater Elias peccator salutem." Own English: To Brother Gregory, minister of the friars in France, beloved in Christ, with all his friars and ours, Brother Elias, sinner, sends greeting. Wadding calls it the letter sent "to all provinces", and the Vatican decree of 2026 cites it as "Encyclical Letter of Brother Elias, to all the Provinces" (FF 311). [X2; X10]
- Opening: "Antequam loqui incipiam suspiro, et merito quasi inundantes aquae, sic rugitus meus, quia timor quem timebam, evenit mihi, evenit et vobis". Own English: Before I begin to speak, I sigh, and rightly: like flooding waters is my roaring, for the fear that I feared has come upon me, and upon you. [PS p.149]
- On the orphaning: "pupilli sumus absque Patre, et orbati lumine oculorum nostrorum". Own English: we are orphans without a father, robbed of the light of our eyes. [PS p.149]
- The blessing at the end: "antequam tolleretur a nobis tamquam alter Jacob omnes filios suos benedixit, et omnibus remisit culpas". Own English: before he was taken from us, like a second Jacob he blessed all his sons and forgave all the faults. [PS p.149]
- THE STIGMATA PASSAGE (PS p.149-150): "Et his dictis annuncio vobis gaudium magnum, et miraculi novitatem. A saeculo non est auditum tale signum, praeterquam in Filio Dei, qui est Christus Deus. Non diu ante mortem Frater, et Pater noster apparuit Crucifixus, quinque plagas, quae vere sunt stigmata Christi, portans in corpore suo: nam manus ejus et pedes, quasi punctiones clavorum habuerunt ex utraque parte confixas, reservantes cicatrices, et clavorum nigredinem ostendentes, latus vero ejus lanceatum apparuit, et saepe sanguinem evaporavit." Own English: And after these words I announce to you a great joy and a new miracle. Such a sign has not been heard since the beginning of the world, except in the Son of God, who is Christ the Lord. Not long before his death our Brother and Father appeared as one crucified, carrying in his body five wounds, which are truly the stigmata of Christ. For his hands and feet had, as it were, the marks of nails set in on both sides, keeping the scars and showing the blackness of the nails; his side seemed pierced by a lance and often gave off blood.
- On his body: "Dum adhuc vivebat spiritus ejus in corpore, non erat in eo aspectus, sed despectus vultus ejus, et nullum membrum in eo remansit absque nimia passione" (while he lived, no beauty, a face despised, no limb without great suffering). After death: "pulcherrimus aspectus est, miro candore rutilans, laetificans videntes. Et membra, quae prius rigida erant, facta sunt mollia nimis, sese vertentia huc atque illuc secundum positionem suam, tamquam pueri delicati." Own English: after his death his face became very beautiful, shining with wonderful whiteness and gladdening all who saw him; and the limbs that had been rigid became very soft, turning this way and that as they were placed, like those of tender children. [PS p.150] This agrees with Celano's "limbs soft like those of an innocent child" in the first-pass notes. Elias's letter is the earlier witness for it.
- The date line (PS p.150): "Quarto nonas Octobris die Dominica, prima hora noctis praecedentis Pater, et Frater noster Franciscus migravit ad Christum." Own English: On the fourth day before the Nones of October, a Sunday, at the first hour of the preceding night, our Father and Brother Francis passed to Christ. [PS p.150] Calc: "quarto nonas Octobris" = 4 October; "prima hora noctis" = about nightfall of 3 October. This settles the 3 vs 4 Oct puzzle: the letter says the night before Sunday 4 Oct.
- Closing prayer instructions: "Quilibet Sacerdos dicat tres missas, singuli Clerici Psalterium, Laici quinque Pater noster, Clerici dicant solemniter vigiliam in communi. Amen." Own English: Each priest is to say three Masses, each cleric a psalter, each lay brother five Our Fathers, and the clerics are to say the vigil office solemnly in common. Signed "Frater Elias peccator". [PS p.150]
- The Vatican decree of 2026 opens with Elias's line "Hold fast to the memory of our father and brother, Francis, for the praise and glory of Him who made him great among us and glorified him in the sight of the angels. Pray for him, as once he asked us, and pray to him that God may make us sharers with him of his holy grace." That is the Latin "habete memoriam Patris, et Fratris nostri Francisci, ad laudem et gloriam ejus, qui magnificavit eum inter homines, et coram Angelis glorificavit illum. Oretis pro ipso, sicut antea a nobis postulavit, et ipsum orate, ut Deus nos cum ipso efficiat suae gratiae sanctae participes" (PS p.150). [X10; X2 PS p.150]
- Elias's letter does not say "La Verna", the date 14 Sept, a seraph, or "two years". It says only "not long before his death". [X2 PS p.149] Surprise: the first public announcement of the stigmata (4-5 Oct 1226) gives no seraph, no mountain and no date.
- Elias's own character in Wadding (editor's comment): Wadding says that outsiders, who knew his heart little, thought Elias very pious; Luke of Tuy, a bishop returning from Jerusalem, called him "Virum sanctissimum, beatissimi Patris Francisci successorem". [X2 PS p.150] Mark: a 17th-century Franciscan editor's defense of Elias.
