## A. Letter of Chrysostom to Pope Innocent I (preserved by Palladius) [X1 ch.2]
Letter is quoted in full in Palladius. Written 404 after the first exile and second expulsion; carried by bishops Demetrius of Pisinum, Pansophius, Pappus, Eugenius and deacons Paul and Cyriacus. Also sent to Venerius of Milan and Chromatius of Aquileia. (Authenticity: Palladius is the only source for the text; Innocent's answer and Honorius's letters are also given by Palladius ch.3. GRADE: single partisan source for the text of all three; facts-only on whether wording is exact.)
- Theophilus avoids the church: "came off the ship, hurried past the porch of the Church, and went somewhere outside of the city to lodge." [X1 ch.2, p.11-12]
- Chrysostom's legal ground: he had "his own letter, in which he said that cases ought not to be taken beyond the boundaries [of a province]" (Theophilus's own letter, as Chrysostom cites it) [X1 ch.2, p.12]
- "we were not to come before a judge (we would have appeared ten thousand times before a judge!), but before an enemy and a foe" [X1 ch.2, p.14]
- Arrest: "Late one evening, when I was being escorted through the streets by the whole of the populace, I was arrested by the city governor's agent in the middle of the city, dragged away by force, and put on board a ship, which set sail by night" [X1 ch.2, p.15]
- Recall: "thirty bishops introducing us, and our most reverent king sending a notary"; Theophilus left "like a runaway slave"; "at dead of night he secretly flung himself into a boat" [X1 ch.2, p.15-16]. (Compare Socrates VI.16-17: crowd riot; Theophilus fled to Alexandria.)
- Easter 404 raid, in Chrysostom's own letter: soldiers invaded the church "on the Great Sabbath, when evening was fast closing in"; women "unrobed in readiness for baptism on that day, fled naked"; "the fonts were filled with blood, and the holy water dyed red from their wounds"; "the most holy blood of Christ was poured out upon the garments of the women"; "more than forty bishops, in communion with us, were driven out"; "like a barbarian man-hunting raid." Then "When day came, the whole city moved outside the walls, and kept the feast under trees and thickets, like sheep scattered abroad." [X1 ch.2, p.17-19] NEW: first-person account of the Easter raid.
- Closing plea: "Scythians and Sarmatians would never have decided a case after hearing one side only" [X1 ch.2, p.20]
- Statement of principle: if precedent stands, "Every one will expel his neighbour, and be expelled in turn." [X1 ch.2, p.20]

## B. Pope Innocent and the West (Palladius ch.3) [X1 ch.3 p.21-28]
- Innocent declared communion with both John and Theophilus, nullified Theophilus's judgment, called for a new synod of east and west bishops [p.21]
- Theophilus's minutes: condemned by 36 bishops, 29 from Egypt, 7 others [p.22]. Innocent: charges not serious, John absent.
- Innocent's words to Theophilus: unless a proper judgment confirms these childish proceedings, "it is impossible for us, without reason given, to separate ourselves from communion with John." Appeal to the canons of Nicaea "(for the Church of Rome accepts no other canon)" [p.22-23]
- After the burning of the church and Cucusus exile, Innocent sent letters of communion to John, "begging them with tears to be patient, as he could not help them" [p.23]
- Paternus, a "mannikin, ugly in appearance," presented charges that John set the church on fire; John made no defence; Innocent treated it "with contempt" [p.23-24]. (Conflict flag: the arson charge is known only from Palladius here; Socrates VI.18 says fire started 20 June 404, the cause was not found.)
- Edicts: any bishop not in communion with Theophilus, Arsacius, Porphyrius to be removed and property seized; house that hides a John-bishop or priest confiscated [p.24-25]
- Deaconesses of high rank brought before Optatus the prefect: "communicate with Arsacius, or to pay two hundred pounds in gold to the treasury." Torture: "ribs scraped upon the rack, and mutilated ears." [p.26]
- Honorius letter to Arcadius: third letter asking for a synod at Thessalonica; sent five bishops, two priests, one deacon of Rome [p.27-28]. (The embassy was jailed in Thrace, per other sources; not yet verified here.)

## C. The Asian bishops affair (Palladius ch.13-15) NEW detail, answering Theophilus's charge that John deposed "sixteen bishops in a single day" [X1 ch.13 p.116]
- Palladius's figure: six, not sixteen. "in the thirteenth year of the sixth indiction" (about Jan 400-401? NOT converted by Moore here; to check) the 22 bishops at Constantinople included Theotimus of Scythia, Ammon the Egyptian, Arabianus of Galatia [p.117]
- Eusebius of Valentinopolis in Kilbia made seven charges against Antoninus of Ephesus: melted Church plate, marble of the baptistery for his bathroom, Church pillars in his dining room, a murderer servant kept, sold land of Basilina (mother of Julian the emperor), took back his wife, sold consecrations [p.118]
- Scene: Eusebius followed John into church during the service and pressed a second document, "conjuring John by terrible oaths, nothing less than 'by the salvation of the king and queen.'" The people thought he was begging a reprieve from a death sentence. John took it, left the altar to Pansophius, withdrew; "he always objected to offering the sacrifice with a disturbed mind" [p.120]
- John to Eusebius: "I beg you not to bring a written charge against our brother Antoninus; we will set right the matters which have vexed you." [p.119]
- Trial in the baptistery; investigation to 2 p.m.; John offered to go to Asia himself; Antoninus got a message sent from the palace to keep him in the city, because Gainas was expected to cause disturbance [p.121-122]
- Delegates Syncletius, Hesychius, Palladius to Hypoepi; the parties made peace by bribe and oath; 40 days plus 30; Antoninus died [p.123-125]
- Ephesus asked John to come "with an awful adjuration" [p.125]. Winter, John seriously ill; storm from the north; ship ran under Mount Trito; "For two whole days they lay without food"; third day reached Apameia; walked to Ephesus [p.125-126]. NEW scene.
- 70 bishops gathered of Lydia, Asia, Caria; six accused who gave bribes confessed; John promised to petition the king to free them from civil duties; synod let them recover money from heirs of Antoninus and communicate "within the sanctuary" but not be priests [p.126-128]. Six replacements, unmarried. Process lasted "not a single day... but of two years" [p.129]
- After exile, the enemies brought back the deposed six and imprisoned John's appointee; the eunuch Victor, a tribune, made bishop [p.129-131]. Palladius's invective against Victor is partisan; facts-only.
- GRADE of "sixteen vs six": Palladius gives six, with signatures on file (22 and 70 bishops) [p.132]; Theophilus's number survives only through Palladius quoting it. Both partisan. Unresolved.

## D. Olympias in Palladius ch.17 [X1 p.150-153]
- Widowed before twenty months of marriage; Theodosius tried to marry her to his kinsman Elpidius, a Spaniard [p.150-151]
- Her answer to the emperor: "If my King had desired me to live with a husband, he would not have taken away my first" [p.151]; he held her property in trust until age thirty; she replied "you will do better yet, if you order it to be dispersed among the poor, and the Churches." Theodosius, back from the war with Maximus, returned control [p.151-152]
- Habits: no flesh food, seldom baths; "she enters the water in her chemise, because, they say, modesty forbids her to look upon herself." Chronic stomach troubles [p.152]
- Supported John: "She certainly spared him anxiety for his daily barley bread" [p.152]. Palladius: John accepted meat for each day as it came and "like a man ashamed of himself" when he ate [p.153]. She gave also to Nectarius, Amphilochius, Optimus (closed his eyes), Epiphanius of Cyprus [p.152]
- She sheltered the Nitrian monks (Tall Brothers) driven by Theophilus [p.150]; Palladius: "a manly woman received... a deaconess hospitably entertained" [p.150]

## E. Chrysostom's table and his reputation for pride [X1 ch.13, 19]
- Why he ate alone: clergy "expected lavish hospitality, and great style, punctually to the minute"; "It would have been absurd to waste the food of the sick or the poor on feasting the healthy." [ch.13 p.112]
- Palladius: "every one wanted to dine with him, either to get a blessing, or because he was poor, or because he was greedy" [p.112-113]
- Critic heard from a tanner: he rarely entered company except in Church and "chafed at lengthy interviews" [ch.19 p.165]. Palladius's retort: "a man who takes the stench of his workshop home to live with him." [p.165] (shows social class of critics.)
- Playful nicknames: "he playfully rallied them, by giving them nicknames expressing the opposite. For instance, he would call the teetotaller a drunkard, the man living in holy poverty covetous, the charitable man a thief." [ch.19 p.169] NEW humor detail, from a friend.
- Palladius predicts: "John will be honoured as a martyr, when those who are set against his good reputation are brought to dust" [p.168]
- Counter-critic in the Dialogue (Theodorus the deacon) says John did not "know how to use the opportunity"; Palladius answers with Baptist, Paul [p.160-162]

## F. Fate of John's adherents (Palladius ch.20) [X1 p.173-180]
- Cyriacus to Palmyra; Eulysius of Bostra to fort Misphas; Palladius to Syene (near the Blemmyans); Demetrius to the Oasis; Serapion's teeth drawn [p.174-175]
- Hilarius, old man, "for eighteen years had not tasted bread, but lived on nothing but herbs and boiled wheat" [p.175]
- Silvanus the bishop "supporting himself by fishing" at Troas [p.176]; Stephanus the ascetic held ten months for carrying the Roman letters; "had the skin most cruelly torn from his ribs and breast; I myself have seen the marks." [p.176-177]
- Eutropius the singer died on the rack; "burning oil-lamps placed against the bones" [p.177-178]; Palladius reports a vision of singers (legend/pious report).
- A servant of the priest Elpidius took 50 coins to kill John, caught with three swords, wounded seven; "the murderer was acquitted." [p.177] (single partisan source; unverified)
- Bishops lodged in inns with prostitutes or synagogues. Palladius quotes one bishop: "How many of these prostitutes... have been brought by the sight of us in this condition to the fear and thought of God" [p.178-179]
- Prophecy of Ammonius the monk: "there would first be a great persecution and schism among the Churches, but that the originators of it would come to a most disgraceful end" [p.146] Followed by a catalogue of illnesses of John's enemies (legend/partisan; one wrote his confession on a tablet because his tongue had swollen) [p.146-147]
- Date of Dialogue: written c. 408, narrated after Palladius's own exile; the speaker is a bishop (Palladius) to deacon Theodorus of Rome (the deacon is named; not confirmed here which Theodorus)

## G. Sozomen VIII (Hartranft; X2). Numbers are Hartranft chapters. Gaps VIII.19-21 filled.
- VIII.5 Macedonian woman and the stone: she was told by her husband to share his (orthodox) belief; at communion "held down her head as if engaged in prayer"; her maid slipped a bit of bread in her hand; "as soon as she had placed it between her teeth, it was converted into stone." She confessed to John, showed the stone "which bore the marks of her teeth." Sozomen: "he can inspect the stone in question; for it is still preserved in the treasury of the church of Constantinople." Status: miracle reported by Sozomen (c. 440s) with relic claim; sermon-hall detail in same chapter: crowds pressed so hard John "placed himself in the midst of them upon the platform of the readers, and, having taken a seat, taught the multitude." NEW scene (reader platform).
- VIII.6 Ephesus: John deposed THIRTEEN bishops "in Lycia and Phrygia, and others in Asia itself" and ordained Heraclides (a Cypriot, ex-deacon of John, ex-disciple of Evagrius). Sozomen also accuses Gerontius of Nicomedia: Ambrose of Milan's deacon who claimed to have seized a demon-ass; John expelled him. CONFLICT with Palladius (six; Theophilus claimed sixteen). Grade: Sozomen is not partisan but late (c.443-450); Palladius is partisan but a participant. Report all three numbers.
- VIII.8 Antiphonal hymns: Arians sang by night in porticoes; John had the orthodox do the same, with "silver crosses and lighted wax tapers"; the empress's eunuch Briso "was wounded on the forehead by a stone"; Arcadius stopped the Arian assemblies [X2 VIII.8; Socrates VI.8 also]. Scene.
- VIII.9 Olympias: John's advice "you have been bestowing wealth on the wealthy, which is as useless as if you had cast it into the sea." Critics' charge: "a hard, passionate, morose, and arrogant man"; Sozomen on eating alone: "by rigorous asceticism, rendered himself liable to pain in the head and stomach" [X2 VIII.9] (Sozomen's informant "a man of undoubted veracity").
- VIII.14-15 Epiphanius (402-403): Theophilus wrote Epiphanius to condemn Origen. Epiphanius landed at Hebdomos, prayed in the church, John "went out with all his clergy to meet him"; Epiphanius refused John's lodging. Theotimus of Scythia read a passage of Origen aloud to Epiphanius.
  - Eudoxia's sick son: she sent to Epiphanius for prayer; he replied the child would live if she avoided Dioscorus and companions. Empress: "If it be the will of God to take my son, His will be done. The Lord who gave me my child, can take him back again. You have not power to raise the dead, otherwise your archdeacon would not have died." [X2 VIII.15] NEW.
  - Long Brothers (Ammonius) to Epiphanius: "How is it, then, that you consider us to be heretics, when you have no proof as to what sentiments we may hold?" [X2 VIII.15]. Epiphanius left, died at sea.
  - Prophecy exchange (reported): Epiphanius "I hope you will not die a bishop"; John "I hope you will never return to your bishopric" [X2 VIII.15]. Socrates VI.14 gives John's reply as "Expect not to arrive at your own country." CONFLICT in wording; Socrates: "I cannot be sure that those who reported these things to me spoke the truth." Grade: legend/popular report; both authors flag uncertainty. Use as "it was said".
  - Epiphanius departing, per Sozomen: "I leave you the city, the palace, and the stage, for I shall shortly depart." [X2 VIII.15] (reported)
- VIII.16 John's sermon against women's vices; "I am not in possession of sufficient data to determine whether there was any truth in the current report that John delivered the discourse... with express allusion to the empress" [X2 VIII.16]. Cyrinus of Chalcedon abused John; Maruthas trod on his foot; gangrene, "expired soon afterwards in great agony". Socrates VI.15 has the same foot story and says Cyrinus called John "the impious," "the haughty," "the inexorable." (Maruthas of Mesopotamia = Maruthas of Martyropolis; check.) Legend or pious report; both historians repeat it.
- VIII.17 The Oak (Sozomen): held at the palace and church of Rufinus (consul), the Apostolium; Theophilus pardoned the Tall Brothers ("they used the words ... spare us"); Dioscorus died before; Ammonius died (Sozomen: his death "tended much to promote the success of the designs of Theophilus"). Theophilus wept for Ammonius: "there was not a monk to be found of more exalted character." John cited four times; he answered through Demetrius of Pisinus: he "did not choose to be considered insane, and to recognize his manifest enemies as judges." Deposed only for non-obedience. "a courier and a shorthand writer were dispatched from the palace."
  - The 47 charges: Sozomen and Socrates do NOT give a count; Socrates VI.15 says "a torrent of accusations ... many of which were ridiculous." The number is not in Socrates, Sozomen, or Palladius ch.2-3 (checked). Do not use 47 without a source. (Photius Bibliotheca cod. 59 gives the Oak charges; not yet fetched. To check.)
- VIII.18 Seditions: John left church "at noon, three days after his deposition" (secretly); Severian preached that John deserved deposition for pride; people howled at the palace; Eudoxia sent the eunuch Briso to Prenetus; "protested that she had taken no part in the machinations... but had, on the contrary, always respected him as a priest and the initiator of her children" [X2 VIII.18]. NEW detail: John baptized her children (as Eudoxia states; reported). John stopped near Anaplus, refused to enter until a larger synod; people "went to meet him, singing psalms ... many carried light wax tapers"; he spoke impromptu comparing Theophilus to the king of Egypt and Sarah (Abraham's wife) and had "to leave his speech half ended" for the applause [X2 VIII.18].
- VIII.19 Flight of Theophilus: Heraclides tried in absence; blood in street fights between Alexandrians and Constantinopolitans; Theophilus fled "at the commencement of the winter" with Isaac the monk; wind drove ship to Gera near Pelusium. Nilammon story (legend/pious report from a source "I have been informed"): hermit in cell with door "built up with stones" refused ordination; "Let us first engage in prayer"; died while praying; Theophilus outside called his name till the afternoon, stones removed, found dead. Then "Sixty bishops assembled together in that city, and annulled all the decrees of the council of The Oak." [X2 VIII.19] Serapion made bishop of Heraclea. NEW.
- VIII.20 Statue: "the silver statue of the empress, which is still to be seen to the south of the church opposite the grand council-chamber, was placed upon a column of porphyry on a high platform... with applause and popular spectacles of dances and mimes." John said it dishonoured the Church. Sermon "Herodias is again enraged; again she dances; again she seeks to have the head of John in a basin" [X2 VIII.20]. Leontius of Ancyra, Acacius of Berea arrived. Christmas: emperor "sent to acquaint John that he could not hold communion with him until he had cleared himself of the charges"; John "spiritedly replied that he was ready to prove his innocence; and this so intimidated his accusers that they did not dare to follow up the charges." Canon of Antioch (made by Arians against Athanasius) used to depose him. The sermon "Herodias" text is NOT in his extant homilies (spurious/attributed; see notes-own-words misattribution section if present). Grade: reported by two historians; no authentic text.
- VIII.21 Easter 404 (Sozomen): after Lent "on the same holy night" followers "were expelled from the church by the soldiers"; "The women wept and lamented, and the children screamed; the priests and the deacons were beaten, and were forcibly ejected from the church, in the priestly garments in which they had been officiating." Sozomen withholds details "lest my work should fall into the hands of the uninitiated." Next day the Paschal feast was in the "very spacious public baths called after the Emperor Constantius"; then the field Constantine enclosed for hippodrome games outside the walls; followers named "Johnites." CONFLICT: Palladius/Chrysostom's letter say baths of Constans (Palladius quoted in first pass: "baths of Constans"), Sozomen and Socrates say Constantius (Socrates VI.18 per first pass: Baths of Constantine). Three names for the baths: Constans (Palladius), Constantius (Sozomen), Constantine (Socrates, first pass). Grade: unresolved; do not name the emperor in text, say "the public baths".
  - Assassin plots (X2 VIII.21): a man "either possessed of a devil, or who feigned to have one" seized with a poniard; John sent bishops to free him "before he had been questioned by torture"; slave of Elpidius ran and stabbed three people, one died; people took him to the palace; "The prefect allayed the fury." Palladius (ch.20) says the same slave of Elpidius took 50 coins and "the murderer was acquitted". Two witnesses agree on the event, differ on the number.
- VIII.22 June 404: John "made no other censure than that, in being sent into banishment without a legal trial... he was treated more severely than murderers, sorcerers, and adulterers." "He was conveyed in a little bark to Bithynia." Fire: church doors barred, crowd inside shattered doors; "the church was suddenly consumed on all sides with fire"; senate house to the south burned; "from late afternoon until the morning" John was taken to Cucusus. Accusation each way (arson). Sozomen gives no date; Socrates 20 June [first pass]. Cause: unknown; Palladius says the arson charge was "palpably false". The 'fire from heaven' of the chapter heading is the NPNF editor's heading, not text.
- VIII.23-24 Persecution: Arsacius (brother of Nectarius) bishop; tribune with clubs; soldiers "pulled off the lobes of the ear with the earrings" (stripping ornaments); Nicarete of Bithynia (virgin, remedies for the poor sick, refused deaconess office pressed by John). Eutropius the reader torn with iron nails and torches; Sisinius dreamed a beautiful man at the altar of St Stephen: only one good man found, Eutropius [X2 VIII.24] (pious report; Palladius has the same martyr with singers). Olympias before the prefect: "My past life ought to avert all suspicion from me, for I have devoted my large property to the restoration of the temples of God." Then to the prefect: "you ought to appear in the place of the accuser and let another judge us." And "I will not hold communion with those from whom I ought to secede, nor consent to anything that is not lawful to the pious." Fined; she left for Cyzicus [X2 VIII.24]. NEW. Tigrius (barbarian eunuch, freed slave) racked.
- VIII.26 Innocent's letter to John (Sozomen preserves, translated Latin to Greek): "Let your conscience encourage your love, O most honored brother" and "God always tries the best of men to see whether they will continue in the height of patience". Letter to the clergy: "No accusation was brought, none was heard." "We have never known such audacities to have been done by our fathers." Canons: "those defined at Nicæa are alone entitled to the obedience and recognition of the Catholic Church." Brought by Germanus and Cassianus. NEW: Innocent's own letters, in Sozomen, are a second source beside Palladius. Palladius says the Roman reply to John was via Cyriacus; Sozomen says "by Cyriacus, the deacon" too. Agree.
- VIII.27 Hail then "four days afterwards, the wife of the emperor died." Sozomen: "by many regarded as indications of Divine wrath" [X2 VIII.27]; Socrates VI.19: "God only knows" (first pass). Exile: John "purchased the liberty of many captives from the Isaurian robbers" using money from Olympias; loved in Armenia and Antioch; visitors from Antioch and Cilicia "frequently sought his society."
- VIII.28 Innocent's embassy dismissed "ignominiously... as if they had invaded a foreign government" [X2 VIII.28]; Pityus decree; "Basiliscus, the martyr, appeared to him at Comani... and apprised him of the day of his death." Sozomen: "Being attacked with pain in the head, and being unable to bear the heat of the sun, he could not prosecute his journey, but closed his life in that town." (Differs: Palladius says he returned to the shrine and died after being taken ill; Sozomen says the town. Minor.)

## H. Socrates VI additions (X3), not in first-pass notes
- VI.9 Origin of the Theophilus trouble: the archpresbyter Peter, a Manichaean woman admitted to communion; Isidore the witness had been in Rome to reconcile Damasus and Flavian; Theophilus ejected both; Isidore with Dioscorus came to Constantinople. John "gave the men an honorable reception, and did not exclude them from communion at prayers, but postponed their communion of the sacred mysteries, until their affairs should be examined." [X3 VI.9] Theophilus then used Origen as cover. Key correction to "John gave them communion."
- VI.11 Severian of Gabala and Antiochus of Ptolemais: Antiochus "amassed a large sum of money" preaching and went home; Severian "exercised himself for the occasion, and having composed a number of sermons, set out for Constantinople." John went to Ephesus; Serapion remained, "continued seated" when Severian passed; Severian cried "If Serapion should die a Christian, Christ has not become incarnate." [X3 VI.11] Humor scene, NEW. Sozomen VIII.10: Eudoxia reconciled them (first-pass only titled; to read). Socrates: John deprived "many of the Novatians and Quartodecimans of their churches" at Ephesus.
- Socrates VI.11: Heraclides of Cyprus chosen when Ephesus was split (here Socrates says only one bishop of Ephesus had died and John appointed Heraclides). Socrates does not give a number of deposed bishops in VI.11; VI.15 says "Many of the bishops in Asia John had deposed."
- VI.12 Epiphanius ordained a deacon in John's church seven miles from the city; Socrates VI.12.
- VI.14 John's message to Epiphanius at the Church of the Apostles: "You do many things contrary to the canons, Epiphanius... Beware therefore, lest a tumult being excited among the people, you yourself should also incur danger therefrom." [X3 VI.14] NEW; Epiphanius "left the church".
- VI.15 Oak/Chalcedon: Theophilus "took up his abode at one of the imperial mansions called 'The Placidian.'" Sozomen (VIII.17) says he went to the palace; both say he skipped the church. Origen dropped: "no mention was now made of Origen, but all were intent on urging a variety of criminations, many of which were ridiculous." Charged "as participators": Serapion, Tigris, Paul the reader.
- VI.16 Recall: Briso found John "at Prænetum — a commercial town situated over against Nicomedia"; John stopped at Marianae. Theodoret V.34 (first pass) has Hieron. Sozomen VIII.18: Prenetus (Bithynia) and Anaplus. So two of three witnesses give Prenetus; Theodoret Hieron; resolve: first stop vs. last stop of journey (my inference; flagged).
- VI.17 Theophilus on Origen: "Origen's books are like a meadow enameled with flowers of every kind. If, therefore, I chance to find a beautiful one among them, I cull it: but whatever appears to me to be thorny, I step over, as that which would prick." [X3 VI.17] NEW quote, good humor.
- Socrates VI.13: Socrates defends Origen as an aside, saying he doubts Theophilus's real motive.

## I. More letters to Olympias (X5; NPNF1 v.9 trans. Stephens/Hall; ccel files npnf109.xvii.ii-viii; letter numbers below are the CCEL page order, not Migne). First pass used letters v, vi (first lines), vii. New here: the Caesarea scene in full, letter iv, viii.
Context: Chrysostom left Constantinople June 404; journey to Cucusus in summer 404 via Caesarea in Cappadocia (late 404); letters written from Cucusus 404-405 and later. Translator: the Schaff editor intro (modern comment, facts only).
- CAESAREA SCENE (letter vi, para 2-3) [X5 xvii.vi]. NEW; this is Chrysostom's own account of the road to exile:
  - Arrival "late one evening at Cæsarea... in the very height of a burning fever", "a dead man" (his words: "I was well nigh a dead man when I entered the city"). He lay at an inn at the edge of town. Then "the whole body of the clergy, and the people, monks, nuns, physicians" visited him. Officials and ex-governors came daily and "treated me as the apple of their eye." He suspects this envy made Bishop Pharetrius hostile (he says "I do not positively declare it but only suspect it to be the fact").
  - Isaurian raiders: news that Isaurians "in countless multitudes were overrunning the district of Cæsarea, and had burnt a large village"; the tribune and soldiers went out; "even the old men undertook the defence of the walls."
  - At dawn "a rabble of monks (for so I must call them, indicating their frenzy by the expression) rushed up to the house where we were, threatening to set fire to it." Imperial soldiers fled to him: "even if we are to fall into the hands of the Isaurians deliver us from these wild beasts." ## N. The homilies against the Jews: honest context (facts-only; secondary: Wikipedia "Adversus Judaeos" and Schaff Prolegomena; the Chrysostom text is not in NPNF; English translation is Harkins, FOTC 68, 1979, NOT public-domain; no quotes here)

## T. Graded conflicts (summary)
- Bishops deposed in Asia: Theophilus 16 (reported by Palladius), Palladius 6, Sozomen 13 (VIII.6). Palladius had records signed by 22 and 70 bishops [X1 ch.15 p.132]. Unresolved; Sozomen unspecific on "Lycia and Phrygia and Asia".
- Judges at the Oak: Photius 45 voted; Palladius quoting the minutes in Rome: 36 (29 Egyptian) [X1 ch.3 p.22].
- Recall: Prenetus (Socrates VI.16, Sozomen VIII.18), Hieron (Theodoret V.34). 
- Cause of the 404 fire: unknown; both parties accused each other [X2 VIII.22; X1 ch.3 p.23-24; Socrates VI.18].
- Baths of Easter 404: Constans (Palladius, first pass), Constantius (Sozomen VIII.21), Constantine (Socrates, first pass).
- Date of second exile: Socrates (first pass) puts the fire at 20 June; CE says "24 June, 404" he left. Sozomen has no date.
- Death: Socrates 14 Sept 407 (first pass); CE same; Palladius no date.
- Epiphanius prophecy: John's words differ in Socrates VI.14 and Sozomen VIII.15; both "reported."
- Olympias widowhood length: Palladius "not even twenty months" vs Schaff intro "about two years".
- Chrysostom's account of when Cucusus: exile letters say winter at Cucusus and later spring; the CE: journey of three months in 407 [Schaff Prolegomena].
