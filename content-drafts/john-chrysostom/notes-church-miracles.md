## Relics 438 (status split)
- 438 (Socrates: 27 Jan, 16th consulate of Theodosius, "thirty-fifth year after his deposition"): Proclus, with the emperor's permission, brought the body from Comana to Constantinople, carried it "in solemn procession through the city", placed it in the Church of the Apostles. Aim: reconcile the Johannites. NO miracle mentioned. Status: reported in early sources. [S1]
- Theodoret: crowd in boats covered the Bosphorus mouth toward the Propontis with torches; the emperor gazed on the bier, laid his head against it, "prayed for his parents and for pardon on them who had ignorantly sinned". Status: reported in early sources (Theodoret writes of the emperor praying for his parents; does not name Eudoxia, does not mention a letter). [S2]
- Later Orthodox tradition (OCA): Proclus sermon ("place does not hinder the miracles of the saint"); crowd demand; bearers could not lift the relics until the emperor wrote a letter to John asking pardon (edict instead of prayer); read at the grave, relics lifted easily; coffin in Hagia Eirene; body found incorrupt; emperor wept for his mother; people cried "Father, take up your throne"; Proclus and clergy saw John open his mouth and say "Peace be to all"; many sick healed at tomb. Status: legendary / late tradition (liturgical Life; no source cited in OCA text). [S3]
- Feast of the translation set in 9th century per OCA. [S3]

## Three Hierarchs (30 Jan)
- 11th c., under Alexios Komnenos (1081-1118): quarrel of Johannites, Basilites, Gregorites in Constantinople. John Mauropous, Metropolitan of Euchaita, saw the three first singly then together; they said "We three are one"; he set 30 Jan (Basil 1 Jan, Gregory 25 Jan, translation of Chrysostom 27 Jan). Status: Church-recognized liturgical tradition; vision known from the synaxarion (not from Mauropous's own text in what I read). Fountoulis does not give year 1084; WebSearch summary gave 1084 (to check). [S4]
- Chrysostom died 14 Sep 407, Elevation of the Cross; feast moved to 13 Nov. [S4]

## 2004 return
- JP II letter dated 27 Nov 2004: "some of their relics ... return to Constantinople"; "purify our wounded memories". Bartholomew: "an anomaly and an ecclesiastical injustice". [S5]
- 1204: Orthodox tradition says Crusaders removed relics (to check: not yet read in a source).

## Timeline (church-miracles focus)
- 407, 14 Sep: Death at Comana in Pontus, after forced marches in sun, rain, cold; led back to Comana and died some hours later. Last words "Glory be to God for all things" (Palladius xi, 38, as cited). Died on feast of the Exaltation of the Cross, so his feast moved to 13 Nov. [S7] [S4]
- 407, night before death (reported by Palladius, via Butler): at the chapel of martyr Basiliscus near Comana the martyr appeared and said "Be of good courage brother John; tomorrow we shall be together." John asked to stay till eleven; guards dragged him out, then brought him back; he changed all his clothes, putting on white, received communion. Status: reported in early source (Palladius, Dialogue; I read Butler's summary, not Palladius). [S6]
- 438, 27 Jan: Translation (see above). Socrates says 35th year after deposition (c.403/404 to 438). Catholic Encyclopedia: entombed in Church of the Apostles where Eudoxia was buried in 404, citing Socrates 7.45 and Constantine Porphyrogenitus. [S1][S7]
- c.9th c.: feast of the translation instituted (OCA). [S3]
- 1081-1118 (Alexios Komnenos): feast of Three Hierarchs, 30 Jan; Mauropous vision. Year 1084 is in WebSearch summary only; not verified in a read text. [S4]
- 10th c.: relics of Gregory Nazianzen brought to Constantinople; both lay side by side in Holy Apostles from the 10th c. [S8]
- 1204: Fourth Crusade; relics of Chrysostom taken to Rome, placed in old St Peter's (S8). Gregory's in Sta Maria in Campo Santo. 1626 Chrysostom's relics moved to altar in nave, Choir Chapel. 1580 Gregory's to Cappella Gregoriana. [S8] (Vatican side: wording in S5 says only "some of their relics ... return"; the Pope's text does not discuss 1204.)
- Head: Kantakouzenos gave skulls to Vatopedi (WebSearch only; not read). Head of Chrysostom brought to Russia from Vatopedi in 17th c., in Moscow Kremlin Assumption Cathedral until 1922, now Cathedral of Christ the Saviour. [S9] A skull is also claimed and venerated at Vatopedi (S10): so two heads/skull claims: contested/unresolved.
- 1821: Vatopedi tradition of "miraculous preservation" of the skull as monks carrying it from Athos to Crete were martyred (Abbot Ephraim, 2019). Status: recorded by monastery. [S10]
- 1568: Pius V recognized four great Eastern Doctors incl. Chrysostom. Status: Church-recognized (S12; check primary papal text, not read).
- 1969 calendar reform: Western feast moved from 27 Jan to 13 Sep (day before Exaltation) [S11, S12]; Western 27 Jan feast had been universal until 1969 (Mozart was named for it, S11).
- 2004, 27 Nov (JP II letter date; Bartholomew accompanied relics to Constantinople on 27 Nov per S8; handing in St Peter's 27/28 Nov, sources vary): relics (part) to Phanar; placed in St George cathedral at mid-point of north wall. New feast of the translation on 30 Nov (St Andrew). Cardinal Kasper led Vatican delegation. [S5][S8] JP II had apologized for Fourth Crusade in 2004 per S8. NEW detail: new hymns by Metropolitan Evangelos of Perge.

## Miracles and claims table
1. Basiliscus vision before death, 407. Reported in early sources (Palladius). [S6]
2. 438 translation, procession, torches on Bosphorus, emperor's prayer for parents. Reported in early sources. [S1][S2]
3. Relics too heavy to lift until emperor's letter of pardon. Legendary/late (liturgical Life). [S3]
4. Body found incorrupt on opening in 438. Legendary/late; Vatopedi abbot now speaks of incorruptibility of relic (ear). [S3][S10]
5. John opens mouth, says "Peace be to all", people say "Father, take up your throne." Legendary/late. [S3]
6. Healings at tomb after 438. Late tradition, general claim. [S3]
7. Paul whispering into his ear while writing, seen by Proclus three nights; the old balding man looks like icon of Paul. Legendary / pious tradition (Orthodox homiletic retelling; source of oldest version not read; do not assert as history). [S13]
8. Mauropous vision 11th c. Church-recognized liturgical tradition; historical dispute is real, vision legendary. [S4]
9. 1821 skull preserved. Recorded by monastery. [S10]
10. Incorrupt hand / right hand tradition: NOT FOUND in any source I read. Search returned the skull and ear (Vatopedi) only. Do not use unless later found. Contested/unknown.
11. Doctor 1568: Church-recognized. Patronage preachers, orators, speakers (and epilepsy, Constantinople): popular, from modern Catholic sites only (WebSearch summary; no official source); status popular.

## Conflicts and notes
- Socrates and Theodoret give an emperor's sober state act; miracles only in later liturgical Life. Writer must keep these apart.
- OCA says body brought to Hagia Eirene then Holy Apostles; Socrates says only Church of the Apostles.
- 2004 date: letter dated 27 Nov; Bartholomew in Phanar 27 Nov per S8 text; other sources say handing on 28 Nov.
- Orthodox claim (head Vatopedi vs Moscow head vs relics in Phanar vs Rome portion retained): relic distribution is complex; status contested.
- Dates of birth differ (c.347 vs 349); not my focus.
- Patronage: unsourced beyond websites.
