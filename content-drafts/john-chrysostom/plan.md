## The driving question

As a young man John feared the bishop's office more than anything. He believed it fed every "wild beast" of the soul, vainglory first, and he tricked his dearest friend so that he could escape it. His own nature was the danger: a hot temper, a tongue that spared no one, a body broken by his years in a mountain cave. Then he was kidnapped into the most exposed see in the empire, Constantinople, beside the court. Could a man like that keep his soul there, and keep faith with the poor? The court broke him: deposed, exiled twice, marched to death. The resolution comes in exile and at Comana: stripped of everything, he writes that no one can harm the man who does not injure himself, and he dies in white saying his everyday prayer, "Glory to God for all things." The empire that exiled him then brings him home by torchlight, and the emperor lays his head on the coffin.

The hook raises the question (how does a man who lost everything die giving thanks, and why did the man who ran from the episcopate end as its most honored teacher?). Chapter 15 answers it at Comana; chapters 16 and 17 complete it.

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: the greatest preacher of the early Church, called Chrysostom, golden-mouthed; one of the Three Hierarchs of the Orthodox Church and a Doctor of the Catholic Church; the Divine Liturgy bears his name.

Set apart: he told emperors, consuls, and empresses the truth to their faces from the pulpit, and he made the poor in the street an altar as holy as the one in church. And he was not a smooth saint: irritable, severe with clergy, eating alone, too trusting of the wrong men, and his own critics said so.

## The hook

Scene at the edge, then paradox. September 407: a sick prisoner, turned back to a martyr's shrine outside Comana, asks for white clothes, gives the rest away, receives Communion, and says his customary words. Three years before, he was archbishop of the capital; as a young man he had run from the episcopate in fear for his soul. How did he end in thanksgiving? Answered in chapter 15, completed in 16 and 17.

## Rules for this saint (from the task)

- No "skulls of bishops"; Acts Hom. 3 only. Paschal homily not his (one line). Liturgy: bears his name, not composed as it stands.
- No "hottest places in hell", no "Church is a hospital".
- 438: Socrates and Theodoret sober; the liturgical Life's miracles marked legendary; no incorrupt hand.
- Conflicts stated in the source note: Praenetum vs Hieron, who ordained him priest, Proclus vs Theodosius.
- Leave out: Libanius "women" remark, date of the epithet, baptism date, 1084, Pius V text, 1969 text, Kantakouzenos, patronage (or attribute to modern websites).
- Quotations from NPNF (New Advent or CCEL wording) with homily and section; Palladius (Moore 1921) only for witness lines.
