# John Chrysostom - witnesses notes (partial, saved early)

## Sources
- S1 Socrates Scholasticus, Church History VI.2-5, 15-21 (c. 440 AD, Greek; NPNF2 vol.2 trans. A.C. Zenos, 1890) https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/26016.htm  public-domain. Read in full.

## Timeline (from S1, S2 so far)
- Birth: native of Antioch, son of Secundus and Anthusa, noble family [S1 VI.3]
- Study: rhetoric under Libanius, philosophy under Andragathius; meant to practice law, turned to scripture and church [S1 VI.3; S2 VIII.2]
- Ascetic school under Diodorus and Carterius with Theodore (later of Mopsuestia) and Maximus (of Seleucia), fellow-students from Libanius [S1 VI.3; S2 VIII.2]
- Epistle to Theodore, who was about to marry and return to the city: Theodore repented, gave up property [S2 VIII.2] (paraphrase; this is the Ad Theodorum lapsum)
- Reader under Zeno; wrote Against the Jews as reader; deacon under Meletius (writes On the Priesthood, Against Stagirius, On Incomprehensibility, On Women who lived with Clergy); after Meletius died at Constantinople, John left the Meletians, joined no one, three years in retirement; presbyter by Evagrius successor of Paulinus [S1 VI.3] NOTE: conflicts with Palladius/Baring-Gould sequence; check.
- LIBANIUS remark: when dying, asked who should succeed him, "It would have been John, replied he, had not the Christians taken him from us" [S2 VIII.2] (Sozomen, c.443; not Libanius's own text; flag as reported)
- 397 (27 Sept): Nectarius dies; John summoned from Antioch; Asterius the general of the East has him get in his chariot, takes him fast to the military station Pagras, hands him to imperial officers, so Antioch would not riot [S2 VIII.2]
- Consecration 26 Feb (398) by Theophilus, who was forced: Eutropius told him to choose between ordaining John and facing trial on charges against him [S1 VI.2; S2 VIII.2]. Theophilus wanted his own presbyter Isidore (affair of the double letters to Maximus and Theodosius).
- Character issues as bishop: stern to clergy; Serapion the deacon: "You will never be able to govern these men, my lord, unless you drive them all with a rod." [S1 VI.4]. Ate alone; reasons: weak stomach/digestion or asceticism [S1 VI.4; S2 VIII.9]. Called hard, passionate, morose, arrogant [S2 VIII.9]. "he was inclined rather to anger than forbearance" [S1 VI.21]; early friend: "proneness to irritability, rather than to modesty" [S1 VI.3]
- Eutropius (399): after Eutropius's law removing sanctuary, he fled to church himself; John preached against him as he lay under the altar table; later beheaded [S1 VI.5]  (critics said John insulted the unfortunate)
- Olympias: Nectarius ordained her deaconess; John advised her to give wealth with care, "you have been bestowing wealth on the wealthy" [S2 VIII.9] (paraphrase)
- 403 Synod of the Oak: Theophilus arrives, no clergy meet him, Alexandrian grain sailors cheer; stays in the Placidian palace; synod at the Oak (Chalcedon); John cited four times, refused, demanded a general council; deposed only for refusing to obey summons [S1 VI.15; S2 VIII.17]. Exile decree; John surrenders himself at noon on third day, unknown to the crowd, fearing riot [S1 VI.15]. 
- Recall: riot of the people; Briso the eunuch finds him at Praenetum; he waits at Marianae until innocence admitted; crowd leads him to church [S1 VI.16]. Theophilus flees to Alexandria after a riot [S1 VI.17].
- Silver statue of Eudoxia; games; "Again Herodias raves; ..." sermon [S1 VI.18; S2 VIII.20]. Emperor will not take communion at Christmas until cleared; John said he could prove innocence; synod condemned him on canon of Antioch (Arian origin) for resuming seat; Easter celebrated by Johannites at the Baths of Constantine [S1 VI.18].
- 404: two months out of public; exile decree; fire at church on 20 June, spread to senate house; John taken away in custody to Cucusus [S1 VI.18; S2 VIII.22]. S2: he said he was treated more severely than murderers, sorcerers and adulterers. Taken in small boat to Bithynia.
- Death: died at Comana on the Euxine, 14 September (407) [S1 VI.21]
- Hail 30 Sept 404, empress died four days later; Socrates refuses to judge: "God only knows" [S1 VI.19]. Cyrinus of Chalcedon lost both feet (legend/popular judgment) [S1 VI.19]
- Doctrine criticism: "Approach, although you may have repented a thousand times" - Sisinnius the Novatian wrote against it [S1 VI.21]

## Added: S3 Palladius, Dialogue on the Life of St John Chrysostom (written c. 408; Greek; Moore trans. SPCK 1921, public domain) https://tertullian.org/fathers/palladius_dialogus_02_text.htm  Read ch. 5, 8-9, 11, 12 start. Palladius is bishop-friend of John (partisan; Theophilus portrayed as villain).
- Birth/youth [S3 ch5]: son of a military commander in Syria; one elder sister only other child; at 18 "revolted against the professors of verbosities"; Meletius noticed the lad, kept him near; John "admitted to the mystery of the washing of regeneration" (baptism, c. 368-372, date not given), three years attending bishop, then reader. NEW detail: Palladius does not name Anthusa.
- Mountains and cave [S3 ch5]: old man Syrus, four years with him; then alone in a cave two years, "for the greater part of which he denied himself sleep"; stomach and kidneys ruined by cold and no lying down; returned to Church because of ill health. (Socrates/Sozomen do not give this.)
- Deacon after "serving the altar for five years" (reader), by Meletius; presbyter by Flavian; twelve years a presbyter at Antioch [S3 ch5]. CONFLICT with S1 (Evagrius ordained him presbyter after three years retirement) - flag.
- Summons 397/398 [S3 ch5]: Eutropius had met John on royal business in the East; governor of Antioch summoned him to the martyr shrines outside the gate Romanesia, put him in a public conveyance with eunuch and guard (S2: general Asterius, station Pagras). Two witnesses agree on secret removal.
- Reforms [S3 ch5]: attacked "brotherly life" with introduced women; audited Church treasurer; moved bishop's household spending to hospital; built more hospitals with two priests, doctors, cooks; reviewed widows; night vigils; horse-racing and theatre crowd came to church.
- Oak and 403 [S3 ch8-9]: forty bishops with John in his refectory; summons from "the holy synod assembled at The Oak" [S3 ch8].
- 404 Easter: bishops of John's party beg emperor and Eudoxia in martyr shrines; Paul of Crateia said "Eudoxia, fear God, and pity your own children; do not dishonour the festival of Christ by the shedding of blood." Vigil held in baths of Constans; Lucius with 400 Thracian soldiers in second watch; baptistery blood; women stripped for baptism fleeing; next day about 3000 newly baptized in white seen from Pempton by the emperor [S3 ch8]. NEW for English pages.
- Exile [S3 ch11]: John, Cyriacus, Eulysius bound to Bithynia; sent to Cucusus "in the hope of getting him killed by the Isaurians"; a year there feeding the poor in a famine "not with corn, so much as with words"; moved to Arabissus; then rescript sending him to Pityus; escort hurried him "at forced speed", three months; one soldier kind, one savage, who preferred heavy rain and hot sun (paraphrase). 
- Death at Comana [S3 ch11]: lodged in shrine of martyr Basiliscus 5-6 miles outside town; martyr said "Be of good cheer, brother; to-morrow we shall be together." Next day pushed on 30 furlongs, taken ill, returned; asked for white clothes, changed everything to shoes, gave the rest away; Communion; last prayer "Glory to God for all things"; buried in Basiliscus's shrine; crowds came from Syria, Cilicia, Pontus, Armenia. Date 14 Sept 407 only in S1.
- Eating alone [S3 ch12]: no wine (blood to head; rose-water in summer), disordered stomach, forgot meals while absorbed in Scripture.
- Arsacius: "less power of speech than a fish, and of action than a frog" (S3 ch11) - Palladius's partisan jab.

## Added: S4 Theodoret, Ecclesiastical History V.28-36 (c. 449-450; NPNF2 vol.3 trans. Jackson, public domain) https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/27025.htm (Book V; ch.34-36 on John). Read.
- Gainas and the Arian church [S4 V.32]: John to Arcadius "attempt, sir, no such promise, nor order what is holy to be given to the dogs." Told Gainas to remember "what kind of raiment you wore before you crossed the Ister" (paraphrase of rebuke). Gainas later: John went as envoy to Gainas in Thrace; Gainas "placed his right hand upon his eyes, and brought his children to his saintly knees" [S4 V.33]. Parallel with S1 VI.5. NEW to many English pages.
- Theodoret V.34: first exile only as far as Hieron at mouth of the Euxine; "In the night there was a great earthquake and the empress was struck with terror"; Bosphorus crowded with couriers; people covered the Propontis with boats and torches [S4 V.34]. (Socrates and Palladius give Praenetum/Prametum instead - location conflict: Hieron vs Praenetum.)
- Theodoret: Cucusus "a petty and lonely town in Armenia"; Pityus; died at Comana; buried beside Basiliscus "for so the martyr had ordained in a dream" [S4 V.34]
- Western bishops cut communion until his name was in the diptychs; Alexander of Antioch first inscribed the name at Antioch [S4 V.34-35]. Atticus later inscribed it. NEW detail on rehabilitation sequence.
- Return of relics [S4 V.36]: Emperor Theodosius II; crowd turned the sea "into land" with boats, torches at Bosphorus mouth; the emperor laid his head against the bier, prayed for his parents "and for pardon on them who had ignorantly sinned" [exact words: "After first gazing upon the bier he laid his head against it, and prayed for his parents and for pardon on them who had ignorantly sinned"]. Year not given by Theodoret; see Socrates VII.45.
- Theodoret V.29: John sent monks with imperial edicts to destroy temples in Phoenicia, paid for by wealthy women [S4 V.29].

## Added: S5 Socrates VII.45 (same trans.), S6 Chrysostom On the Priesthood I.2-6 (c. 390s; NPNF1 vol.9 trans. Stephens, public domain; own words, a primary witness to Anthusa) https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/19221.htm ; S7 Letters to Olympias (404-407; NPNF1 vol.9 trans. Stephens) https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1916.htm ; S8 Smith, Dict. of Greek and Roman Biography "Chrysostomus, Joannes" (1870, public domain) https://cts.perseids.org/read/pdlrefwk/viaf88890045/003/perseus-eng1/C.chrysostomus_joannes_1 (read only the first lines; epithet gloss).
- Relics 438 [S5 VII.45]: Proclus, with emperor's permission, moved the body from Comana to Constantinople "in the thirty-fifth year after his deposition"; carried in procession through the city; placed in the Church of the Apostles; date given: "27th of January, in the sixteenth consulate of the Emperor Theodosius" (= 438; the year-conversion is from the consulate, mine, check). Effect: "the admirers of that prelate were conciliated" and returned to communion. Socrates credits Proclus; Theodoret [S4 V.36] credits Theodosius II (laid head on bier). Both agree on the torch-lit boats at the Bosphorus [S4]; Socrates does not mention boats. Legend flag: Theodosius praying for pardon for his parents is Theodoret only; later Church tradition adds words of the emperor (not read).
- Anthusa [S6 I.5]: John speaking: mother took him to private chamber, sat "on the bed where she had given birth to me" and wept to stop him leaving to live with Basil; father died soon after birth; she did not remarry; spent own dowry on his education and kept his patrimony; asked "wait for my death". Exact: "do not plunge me into a second widowhood; nor revive the grief which is now laid to rest: wait for my death". CAUTION: Palladius says only one elder sister; Chrysostom's text does not name Anthusa (Socrates names her). The Libanius "What women these Christians have" remark was NOT found in S6 or S1-S4; it is not verified. List below under Other.
- Basil/forced ordination ruse [S6 I.6]: friend Basil seized for ordination as John hid; John had promised to follow; bystanders said the hot-tempered one (John) had yielded mildly. This is the "flight from the episcopate" story; NEW as drama.
- Libanius [S2 VIII.2]: only source for "had not the Christians taken him from us" - Sozomen (c. 443), 40 years after Libanius died (c. 393-ish; date not read).
- Epithet [S8]: Smith's Dictionary says "Χρυσόστομος, (golden-mouthed, so surnamed from the power of his eloquence)". It gives no date. Websearch snippet (not read in full, do not use as fact): epithet comes in sixth century. Socrates VI.4 and Theodoret V.33 call him "teacher of the world", not "golden-mouthed" [S4]. Palladius never uses it in what I read.
- Letters to Olympias [S7] (written from Cucusus and the journey; Olympias's deaconess office confirmed by S2 VIII.9):
  * "Do not be anxious on my behalf, nor rack yourself with solicitude, on account of the severity of the winter, and the weakness of my digestion, and the incursions of the Isaurians." Armenia winter: kept a constant fire, screens round chamber, many rugs, "staying always indoors". Sent a remedy by Syncletion. 
  * Illness: "the last two months I have been no better than one dead, nay worse"; "perpetual vomiting supervening on headache, loss of appetite, and constant sleeplessness"; spring cured him.
  * Caesarea in Cappadocia (journey): arrived late one evening in burning tertian fever; clergy, people, monks, nuns, physicians visited; then Isaurians reported burning a village; at dawn "a rabble of monks" rushed the house threatening to burn it; bishop Pharetrius stirred them; a lady (named by Chrysostom as one who received him into her villa) hid him; at midnight the presbyter Evethius shouted the barbarians were at hand; torches put out; "the mule which carried my litter fell on its knees" and threw him [S7]. He asks Olympias to "rejoice at these things, to be glad, and leap for joy , and to glorify God who has counted me worthy to suffer such things."
  * Connection: Palladius ch11 says Pharetrius "conferred by letters with John's enemies" [S3]. The two agree in outline.
  * Letter 1 intro: "Come now let me relieve the wound of your despondency" - he writes to a depressed deaconess after the 404 persecutions (Letter 1 on despondency).
  * Olympias editor intro: born about 368, married Nebridius 384, widow ~2 years later, Theodosius wanted her to marry Elpidius [S7 intro, NPNF editor]; Palladius also [S3 ch17].
- Palladius ch17: Olympias "has entirely maintained the blessed John" asked; she abstains from flesh and seldom visits the baths (paraphrase).

## Added: Riot of the Statues 387 (Antioch)
- [S9 Chrysostom, Homilies on the Statues, NPNF1 vol.9, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1901.htm, public domain, headings read only] Hom. 2 was "Spoken in Antioch in the Old Church ... while he was a presbyter" on the calamity of the overthrow of the Statues of Theodosius. Hom. 3 is on the departure of Bishop Flavian on embassy to the emperor. Hom. 21 is on Flavian's return and the emperor's reconciliation with the city. [S9 headings]
- Theodoret V.19 [S4] tells the riot (tax; bronze statue of Placilla dragged; threat to reduce Antioch to a village) but does NOT mention John; its hero is the hermit Macedonius, who grabbed the commissioners' cloaks and made them dismount. So John's role in 387 rests on his own homilies; no early historian here ties him to it.
- John's year/status: homilies are said to be from while he was presbyter [S9 heading].

## Character (one line each)
- Hot-tempered young man: early friend said "proneness to irritability, rather than to modesty" [S1 VI.3]; in S6 I.6 bystanders called him "more hot tempered".
- Severe to clergy; harsh talk to the powerful; haughty in private to those who did not know him [S1 VI.3-5; S2 VIII.9].
- Ate alone; stomach, headaches, no wine; Palladius defends, enemies used it [S1, S2, S3].
- Free-spoken: S2 says he rebuked sinners "even in the churches" and fought injustice "as if they had been perpetrated against himself".
- Generous and gentle in practice: hospitals [S3], fed the poor in Cucusus [S3], advised Olympias to give wisely [S2].
- Used humor? "playful style" of his letters to friends per NPNF editor (https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1917.htm, Letter to some priests of Antioch, editor's note; not otherwise read).
- Cheerful under pain: asks Olympias to rejoice over the night flight [S7].
- Enemies' view: "the impious", "the haughty", "the inexorable" (Cyrinus [S1 VI.15]); Severian: haughtiness enough to depose him [S1 VI.16].
- Doctrine critics: Novatians and some friends blamed "Approach, although you may have repented a thousand times" [S1 VI.21].
- Socrates: John "pass[ed]" Novatian and Quartodeciman churches in Asia/Lydia, with violence, according to others [S1 VI.19].

## Miracles / reports (recorded by early witnesses; none officially assessed here)
- Vision of Basiliscus the night before death: "Be of good cheer, brother; to-morrow we shall be together." Palladius c. 408 [S3]; Theodoret notes burial beside the coffin "for so the martyr had ordained in a dream" [S4].
- Divine judgment stories (popular): Cyrinus of Chalcedon lost both feet; hail on 30 Sept 404; Eudoxia died four days later; Socrates declines to judge [S1 VI.19].
- Earthquake terrifies Eudoxia, causing first recall [S4 V.34]; Palladius says "a catastrophe in the royal palace" [S3 ch9].
- Fire of 404: S2 title says "by fire from heaven" but the text blames each party; the cause is disputed [S1, S2].
- No post-mortem miracles read in my sources.

## Other
- CONFLICTS: (a) place of first exile: Praenetum [S1, S3 spells Prametum] vs Hieron [S4]. (b) Ordinations: S1 says Evagrius made him presbyter after three years' retirement; S3 says Flavian made him presbyter after twelve years of work; S1 and S3 disagree on who. (c) Socrates's baptism/Meletius link not in S1; only in S3. (d) 438 relics: Proclus [S5] vs Theodosius II [S4]. (e) Cucusus year: S3 says a year there, then Arabissus, then Pityus; S7 letters show he wintered at Cucusus.
- Tzane/Pityus plan: The aim was Pityus on the Black Sea; he died at Comana [S1, S3, S4].
- John's letters to Olympias mention Maruthas the bishop and the affairs in Persia [S7 letter after Caesarea] - connection to Maruthas of Mesopotamia (S1 VI.15 says he stepped on Cyrinus's foot).
- Wider witnesses not read: Sozomen VIII.21 (assassination plots), the full 47 accusations list (Sanidopoulos https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2020/11/the-47-accusations-against-st-john.html), Florovsky essay (https://www.johnsanidopoulos.com/2021/01/the-life-works-and-thought-of-saint_27.html), Baring-Gould vol01 pp.492-512 (found, not read), Butler on Sanidopoulos.
- Images: none sought.
