## Quotations (all copied from fetched text; NPNF English)
Q1 Eutropius, Hom. I (On Eutropius, the eunuch, patrician and consul), sec. 1 [S4]: "“Vanity of vanities, all is vanity”—it is always seasonable to utter this but more especially at the present time." Scene: Eutropius, fallen minister, had fled into the Church for asylum. Also: "Where now are your feigned friends? where are your drinking parties, and your suppers?" and "the Church which you treated as an enemy has opened her bosom and received thee into it; whereas the theatres which you courted ... have betrayed and ruined thee." Also: "my wounds work health, but their kisses have produced an incurable disease." (Prov 27:6 echo). Rights: public-domain.
Q2 Matthew Hom. 50, sec. 4 [S5]: "Would you do honor to Christ's body? Neglect Him not when naked; do not while here you honor Him with silken garments, neglect Him perishing without of cold and nakedness." and "God has no need at all of golden vessels, but of golden souls." and "the church is not a gold foundry nor a workshop for silver, but an assembly of angels." (New Advent text: "For the church is not a gold foundry nor a workshop for silver, but an assembly of angels"). public-domain.
Q3 On the Priesthood III.9 [S2]: wild beasts of the clerical life: "Do you ask what those wild beasts are? They are wrath, despondency, envy, strife, slanders, accusations, falsehood, hypocrisy, intrigues ... love of praise, desire of honor (which indeed most of all drives the human soul headlong to perdition) ... contempt of the poor, paying court to the rich" public-domain. Strong, honest self-portrait of the pastor's fears.
Q4 On the Priesthood VI.4 [S2]: "At such a time angels stand by the Priest; and the whole sanctuary, and the space round about the altar, is filled with the powers of heaven, in honor of Him who lieth thereon." (He also reports a hearsay vision of angels around the altar "as one might see soldiers in the presence of their King".) NB: the NPNF footnote there cites the "Liturgy of St. Chrysostom" prayer. public-domain.
Q5 Letter to Olympias (NPNF letter "To Olympias", exile, id xvii.vii) [S3]: "And as the fire makes the piece of gold, when it is applied to it, of better proof: so also affliction when it visits golden characters renders them purer and more proven." and "No one will be able to do anything to thee of this kind which he will not find you have already abundantly made yourself undergo." public-domain.
Q6 Letter to Olympias (xvii.v) [S3]: "during the last two months I have been no better than one dead, nay worse." Winter in Cucusus: fire burning, "endured a most unpleasant amount of smoke," "perpetual vomiting supervening on headache, loss of appetite, and constant sleeplessness." Human, vivid. public-domain.
Q7 Last words (S1, ch. X): "Glory be to God for all things, Amen." Schaff: "His last words were his accustomed doxology" - SECONDARY, 19th-c. Schaff; the source for the last words is Palladius/ later Lives, not his own writing. Status: tradition; check in witnesses notes.

## Facts from S1 (Schaff), tagged
- Exile: Cucusus on border of Cilicia and Armenia; fever, headache; "242 extant letters are nearly all from the three years of his exile"; 17 letters to Olympias; 2 to Pope Innocent [S1 ch.X].
- Died 14 Sept 407 near Comana in Pontus, chapel of martyr Basiliscus, in the 60th year; "Clothed in his white baptismal robes, he partook of the eucharist" [S1 X]. Translation to Constantinople 27 Jan 438; Theodosius II and Pulcheria at Chalcedon asked forgiveness [S1 X].
- Appearance: "small stature ... emaciated frame, a large, bald head, a lofty, wrinkled forehead, deep-set, bright, piercing eyes, pallid, hollow cheeks, and a short, gray beard" [S1 X] (Schaff's description, source unstated; check against Palladius/ Byzantine descriptions).
- Preacher: audience applauded; he rebuked: "You praise what I have said ... but show your approbation by obedience; that is the only praise I seek." [S1 XIV] (Schaff gives it as quote with no locator; locator NOT confirmed; mark paraphrase-level until located).
- Character per Schaff [S1 XI]: irritable, suspicious, "easily deceived and misled by such men as Serapion", "harmlessness of the dove without the wisdom of the serpent." Quote from Homily 20 on Acts via Schaff.
- Olympias [S3 intro]: b. c.368, married Nebridius 384, widowed; Theodosius tried to marry her to Elpidius; Chrysostom warned her against indiscriminate giving; she fed him wholesome food; accused of the 404 fire.

## Quotations, continued (all copied from fetched text)
Q8 Statues Hom. II, sec. 1 (preached in Antioch, 387, after the statues of Theodosius were overthrown) [S6]: "The present season is one for tears, and not for words; for lamentation, not for discourse; for prayer, not for preaching." Then: "Who, beloved, hath bewitched us? Who hath envied us?" (sec. 2). He compares Antioch to Job on the dunghill. public-domain.
Q9 2 Cor Hom. 20, sec. 3 [S10]: "This altar is composed of the very members of Christ, and the body of the Lord is made your altar." and "you honor indeed this altar, because it receives Christ's body; but him that is himself the body of Christ you treat with contumely, and when perishing, neglectest. This altar may thou everywhere see lying, both in lanes and in market places". New Advent text. public-domain. (Pair with Q2: his famous altar-of-the-poor teaching.)
Q10 Acts Hom. 3 [S9, S8]: "I do not think there are many among Bishops that will be saved, but many more that perish: and the reason is, that it is an affair that requires a great mind." Greek (via S8, Buckton 1852): "οὐκ οἶμαι εἶναι πολλοὺς ἐν τοῖς ἱερεῦσι τοὺς σωζομένους..." Note: the Greek says hiereus (priests); NPNF renders "Bishops" and Chrysostom is addressing office-seekers. The popular "skulls" line is NOT his (see Misattributions). public-domain. Section number not confirmed in the page; New Advent page has no numbered section at that place.
Q11 Letter to Olympias (CCEL id xvii.vi, exile, Cucusus) [S3]: "Why do you lament? why do you belabour yourself, and demand of yourself a punishment which your enemies were not able to demand from you, having thus abandoned your soul to the tyranny of dejection?" Then he tells her: "Is it the desolation of this place which grieves you? Yet what can be pleasanter than my sojourn here?" and "perhaps it seemed good to God that I should be set to run the longer double course, in order that the garland of victory might be rendered more glorious." CONFLICT: in the other letter (xvii.v) he says he was "no better than one dead" for two months. He plays down his hardship to comfort her. public-domain.
Q12 Priesthood I.1 [S2]: "I had many genuine and true friends, men who understood the laws of friendship, and faithfully observed them; but out of this large number there was one who excelled all the rest in his attachment to me" (the Basil of the dialogue). public-domain. For the Basil identification see S1 ch. IV (read it before using; not checked here).
Q13 Priesthood VI.12-ish (CCEL iv.viii) [S2]: "I will now try and unveil to thee the storm of my soul" (he explains why he fled ordination, to Basil). Section number unconfirmed. public-domain.
Q14 Greek of Q3 [S11]: "Τίνα δέ ἐστι τὰ θηρία; θυμός, ἀθυμία, φθόνος, ἔρις, διαβολαί, κατηγορίαι, ψεῦδος, ὑπόκρισις, ἐπιβουλαί ..." (OCR; breathing marks garbled in source; verify with a clean edition before printing).

## Timeline / what the writings show
- c.370-374 After the Priesthood's account he avoids ordination: he tricked his friend Basil, who was seized for the bishop's office; Basil reproached him. [S2 Book I-II] (the treatise is a dramatized dialogue; date and historicity: S1 ch. IV, to check)
- Lent 387 Antioch: tax revolt, imperial statues pulled down; he preaches 21 homilies on the Statues; no. 21 on Easter after Flavian's return with pardon [S1 ch. VI, S6 preface table]. Schaff: "One of the results of those sermons was the conversion of a large number of heathens." (S1). NEW-ish detail: Homily 1 was a temperance sermon on 1 Tim 5:23 ("a little wine"), preached just before the riot [S1, S6 Hom I].
- 399 Eutropius flees to the church; Chrysostom preaches "Vanity of vanities" [S4]. Date 399 from intro: not checked.
- 404-407 Exile at Cucusus; letters to Olympias; treatise "No one can harm the man who does not injure himself", sent with a letter to Olympias [S1 X, intro to S "xvi.ii"].
- 14 Sept 407 death near Comana [S1 X].

## Character (own words show)
- Writes to a sick friend about drugs and vomiting; asks for more of Syncletion's remedy [S3, xvii.iv]. Humane, practical.
- Complains about the cold in Cucusus: "constant fire", screens, rugs, stays indoors [S3 xvii.iv].
- Admits fear of ambition: ranks vainglory the worst "rock" (skopelos) [S2/S11 III.9].
- Rebukes applause (S1 XIV, locator missing).
- Plain speaker to the powerful, loyal to the fallen (Eutropius).
- Schaff (S1 XI): irritable, suspicious, too trusting of Serapion; "harmlessness of the dove without the wisdom of the serpent." (19th-c. opinion)

## Scenes (max 10)
1. Eutropius, who had stripped the Church of asylum rights (check: Schaff intro, S4 xv.ii), hangs onto the altar while the crowd and soldiers wait; Chrysostom preaches over him (S4).
2. Antioch Lent 387: city stunned, baths closed, senate jailed, monks come down from mountains to plead [S6 preface sec. 16-17 table].
3. Cucusus winter: Chrysostom in one room with a fire and smoke, "no better than one dead", writes to Olympias [S3].
4. Death in white baptismal robes in Basiliscus' chapel [S1 X].
5. The Statues Hom. I: lamp-lit congregation told to take "a little wine" for the stomach: a sermon on moderation that he delivers just before the mob tears down the emperor's statues [S1 VI, S6].

## Misattributions and authorship (central for this focus)
M1 "The road to hell is paved with the skulls of bishops/priests": no source in his works. Nearest real line: Acts Hom. 3, "I do not think there are many among Bishops that will be saved, but many more that perish" [S9]; Notes & Queries 1852 (Buckton) and Pearse trace the "skulls" line to later paraphrase; Wesley's remark is the earliest link given by search result (not read in primary form). Status: bogus embellishment of a real line. [S8]
M2 Paschal (Catechetical) Homily "If any be devout and God-loving...": NOT by Chrysostom per scholarly consensus. S7: scholars have insisted "for centuries" the attribution must be mistaken; earliest surviving attribution to Chrysostom is Theodore of Stoudios (9th c.), who began its reading at Pascha; critical edition (CPG 4605) by Huggins 2021. A search summary adds that it may be a 6th-8th c. pastiche of genuine and spurious Chrysostom sermons (NOT verified in a read source). Wikipedia (read) says "Scholarship since Montfaucon has generally rejected the authorship of Chrysostom" but gives the text as Chrysostom's anyway; the Orthodox Church reads it as his. Use: "attributed to".
M3 The Divine Liturgy: S1 (Schaff, 1889): "there is no evidence that he composed a liturgy, nor is it probable"; Liturgy "is an abridgment and improvement of the Liturgy of St. Basil" (Schaff view; dated). S12: Barberini (8th c.) ascribes only two prayers to Chrysostom (prayer for the catechumens; prayer behind the ambo); whole Liturgy first ascribed in 11th-c. manuscripts; "Chrysostom did not compose the entire Divine Liturgy, not even ... the Anaphora." Priesthood VI.4 footnote in NPNF cites the Liturgy, but this footnote is the translator's, not Chrysostom's. Taft's 6-volume "A History of the Liturgy of St John Chrysostom" is the standard study; not read.
M4 "The hottest places in hell are reserved for those who in times of moral crisis maintain their neutrality": not seen in his works; search result did not trace it. NOT confirmed either way; list as "to check".
M5 "The Church is a hospital": search did not confirm for Chrysostom. To check.
