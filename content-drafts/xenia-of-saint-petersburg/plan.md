# Plan: St. Xenia of Saint Petersburg

## The driving question

A young wife of about twenty-six lost her husband, the court singer Andrei Fedorovich Petrov, without warning. She put on his clothes, answered only to his name, said that Xenia had died, gave away the house, and walked the streets of Petersburg for forty-five years with nothing. Why would a woman disappear into a dead man's name, and how did the widow the city took for mad become the one Petersburg runs to? The answer the story builds, from true facts only: she gave up her name, her house, her marriage, and every comfort for God, and the neighbours put it in one line, "Who does not belong to the world belongs to God." The resolution: the homeless widow with no children is asked, at her grave, for exactly what she gave away: homes, work, husbands, and children. Her gravestone asks to be remembered; the city has never stopped.

We never hear her explain herself. The narrator does not invent a motive. The story places the facts (sudden death, in later retellings without confession; her claim that he lived on in her; her night prayer) side by side and lets the reader see the pattern.

## Why famous / what set her apart

Famous: the fool for Christ of eighteenth-century Petersburg, whose grave at the Smolensky cemetery has drawn crowds of petitioners for two hundred years.
Set apart: she took on her dead husband's name and clothes and lived as a fool for Christ in Peter's rational, regulated capital, where the Church's own rules treated such people as frauds. She is one of very few women among the Russian fools for Christ, and the first woman glorified as a saint on Petersburg soil.

## The hook

Scene at the edge (1847 police gazette, the oldest layer): an old woman who walks out of town every night; police follow her into the open fields to find out what she is up to; they find her on her knees, bowing to the four quarters, all night; they go away satisfied. Then the broken picture: the city called her Andrei Fedorovich. Answered in chapter 6 (the night prayer and the bricks) and in chapters 3 and 4 (the name).

## Chapters

1. **Followed Into the Night Fields.** Hook, the name, thesis (thin record, what the city remembered). Pull: it began with a funeral.
2. **The Wife of a Court Singer.** What is known and not known: station unknown, husband's rank, house, no children (modern), the ball legend dismissed. Pull: he died suddenly.
3. **A Funeral in His Coat.** The uniform at the coffin; "not Aksinya Grigorievna but Andrei Fedorovich"; Q7. Pull: then she began to give it all away.
4. **Examined and Found Sane.** House to Paraskeva; relatives' petition; the officers' verdict; Peter's Regulation; fools for Christ. Block quote Q11. Pull: the street.
5. **The Tsar on a Horse.** Clothes rotting, red and green; kopecks; the market; children; bath; the boys and her one anger; Gaidukova's "mare's head". Block quote Q10 or Q2. Pull: but where did she go at night?
6. **Bricks on the Scaffold.** Return to the hook: night prayer, the police; the Smolensky church and the bricks. Pull: what she said by day began to come true.
7. **All Russia Will Bake Pancakes.** Elizabeth 1761 (Q4 block); Ivan Antonovich (Q5 block, Q6). Pull: in households too.
8. **The Nettle Will Soon Wither.** Krapivina (Q3 block), the pie, the mirror, the Okhta bride (Q9 block), the five kopecks and the fire, Paraskeva's son (modern). Pull: old age.
9. **No Worse Than a Bird.** Refusals of homes; sheepskin; death unknown; the dream (tradition); the tombstone (Q12 block). Pull: "remember my soul".
10. **Earth Carried From the Grave.** Pilgrimage by 1820; the mound, the slab, the box; Grebenka 1845 "Ась!"; the colonel's wife on the bridge; 1891 booklet; chapels; House of Industry. Pull: the Revolution.
11. **A Chapel Closed, Then Returned.** 1940, 1946-47, 1960/62, 1983, 1987; ROCOR 1978 (Q19 inline); Moscow 1988 (Q18 block). Pull: why people still come.
12. **Houses, Husbands, and Children.** Petitions mirror what she gave up; the disputed portrait; the 2023 monument; ends on the tombstone's request.
