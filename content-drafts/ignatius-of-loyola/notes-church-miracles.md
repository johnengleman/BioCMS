## 1. Identity (church side only)
- Born 1491, castle of Loyola, Guipuscoa; died Rome 31 July 1556, age 65 [S1 p.385-386, p.419; S5].
- Baptized Inigo; "Ignatius" taken later in Rome [S5].
- Tradition: Catholic (Latin). Tier A.
- Feast: 31 July [S1 header; S3 p.374 Roman martyrology wording]. Other calendars: not checked.
- Beatified by Paul V, 1609 [S1 p.421]; 27 July 1609 [S5]. Canonized by Gregory XV [S1; S3; S5; S7].
- CONFLICT on canonization date: 12 March 1622, "the day of St. Gregory the Great" [S3 p.374, Bartoli]; 12 March 1622 in a news report of the ceremony [S7]; "22 May, 1622" [S5, Catholic Encyclopedia]. Butler adds the bull was published only the year after, by Urban VIII [S1 p.421] (OCR damaged; reads "yea[r] ping following by Urban VIII"). Use 12 March 1622 for the ceremony. Check the bull date.
- Same ceremony: Teresa of Avila, Isidore of Madrid, Francis Xavier, Philip Neri [S7]. Late additions to a ceremony prepared for Isidore [S7].

## 3. Timeline (church and cult side)
- 1521, Pamplona: cannon shot breaks Ignatius's legs. Bone set again; fever. REPORTED, popular. [S1 p.387; S2 p.21-22]
- Eve of Sts Peter and Paul 1521: given up by doctors; he has received the sacraments; "in the middle of the night he began to grow better". [S2 p.22] Butler adds a dream of St. Peter touching him; Ignatius "ever after looked upon this recovery as miraculous" [S1 p.387]. The Autobiography (as edited) does not describe a dream. Ribadeneira: the Lord "gave him health miraculously, the glorious St. Peter appearing to him" [S4, Lib. V]. REPORTED.
- 1521, Loyola: a night vision of Our Lady with the Child; consolation and contrition; he "never felt the least motion of concupiscence" until August 1555 when it was written [S2 p.28]. Butler adds the house shook and a window broke, "a rent was made in the wall which remains to this day" [S1 p.389]. The shaking: REPORTED by Butler's late source; not in the Autobiography. Butler himself says it may be a sign of acceptance "or ... an effect of the rage of the devil".
- Mar 1522, Montserrat: vigil before Our Lady; sword and dagger hung at her altar [S5; S1 p.390]. Church of St. Paul and the river at Manresa: illumination (the "Cardoner" is not named in S2 text read).
- 1522-23, Manresa: visions. The "serpent shape with glittering eyes", later judged the devil's work [S2 p.41, 57-58]; the white rays at the elevation of the Host [S2 p.55]; the way God created the world, "a white object, with rays emanating from it" [S2 p.54-55]; the humanity of Christ seen "perhaps twenty or even forty times" at Manresa, once at Jerusalem, once at Padua [S2 p.56]; the illumination by the river: "all the divine helps received, and all the knowledge acquired up to his sixty-second year, were not equal to it" [S2 p.57]. REPORTED, by Ignatius himself.
- 1524, Barcelona: Lasano hanged himself; Ignatius cut the rope and prayed; the man revived, confessed, died. "regarded in the city as miraculous". REPORTED by Butler [S1 p.396]. Source behind it not named.
- 1520s-30s, Paris: the pond. Ignatius stood neck-deep in a frozen pond to turn a young man from sin. Butler [S1 p.400]. Popular. NEW detail for readers of the church sources only: mark pious story.
- 15 Aug 1534, Montmartre: vows of the first companions [S1 p.401; S5].
- 1537, near Rome, chapel at La Storta (named in S5): Ignatius sees the Father place him with the Son carrying the cross; the Son says "Ego vobis Romae propitius ero". REPORTED. Source chain: Laínez told it to the Fathers in Rome when general; Ribadeneira was present and records it [S1 p.402; S4 Lib. I]. Butler: "attested by others to whom the saint had discovered this signal favour". Ignatius's own reading, per S5: "I do not know whether we shall be crucified in Rome; but Jesus will be propitious" (S5 gives it in English; the source of that line is not given there). Not in the Autobiography text read.
- 27 Sep 1540: bull Regimini militantis Ecclesiae approves the Society [S1 p.403; S5]. 22 Apr 1541: profession at St Paul outside the Walls [S5].
- 1548: Paul III approves the Spiritual Exercises by the brief Pastoralis Officii [S1 p.395; S5]. CHURCH.
- 1544-45, Rome: Spiritual Diary. Mass entries: tears so violent he feared losing sight; sight of "a brilliant flame of fire" at Mass of the Holy Spirit; "I saw the Most Holy Trinity". Found in his hand after death [S1 p.414; S3 pp.308-309 (S9)]. REPORTED.
- Ribadeneira's own list of Ignatius's favours: Peter at Pamplona; Our Lady and the Child; Christ at Manresa, Jerusalem, near Padua; the notebook on the Trinity; and "aquella extasi de ocho dias" ("that ecstasy of eight days") [S4 Lib. V]. Butler's footnote also lists "a rapture in which the saint continued for eight days" [S1 p.419 note]. REPORTED. The ecstasy account itself: not located. Date and place not read.
- 1553, Ribadeneira reports a talk between Ignatius and a Dominican, Fray Reginaldo, about a nun in Bologna who had ecstasies, felt no fire, and had stigmata-like marks. Ignatius's answer: of what was said of her, nothing is less suspect than her prompt obedience. NEW. [S4 Lib. V]. This shows his test for ecstasy: obedience. Rights: paraphrase.
- Ignatius told Ribadeneira that grace sometimes overflows from the soul to the body, "but this happens very seldom, and to the great friends of God" (paraphrase of S4 Lib. V, same passage).
- 31 Jul 1556: death in Rome. He asked on 30 July for the last blessing and was told no danger was near; in the morning the infirmarian found him in peaceful prayer; the holy oils came too late [S5]. Butler: sent Polanco to ask the pope's blessing the day before; died saying the name of Jesus [S1 p.419].
- 1-2 Aug 1556: body shown two days; crowd. Cardinal pushes through; Roman nobleman Fabricius de Massini cannot get near; orders issued to stop people cutting off pieces of clothes or flesh as relics. Buried 1 Aug evening in a wooden coffin in Santa Maria della Strada, left of the altar [S3 p.369]. REPORTED by Bartoli.
- 1556, Rome, at the burial: Bernardina Nerucci's daughter (14, scrofula, given up by four physicians) is signed with a cloth worn by Ignatius by Fr. Cornelius Vischaven; healed at once before the crowd [S3 p.369-370]. REPORTED. First cure after death in S3.
- 31 Jul 1568: first translation of the body (to make room for the new church), ordered by Francis Borgia, general [S3 p.370]. Fr. Julius Mancinelli hears heavenly singing the night before [S3 p.370]. LEGEND/REPORTED, private vision.
- 19 Nov 1587: second translation to the new Church of Jesus (Gesù), built by Cardinal Alexander Farnese, ordered by General Aquaviva. Placed on the right of the high altar; stone: "Ignatio, Societatis Jesu Fundatori" [S3 p.371]. Butler gives 1587 only [S1 p.421]. The 1587 date: two sources agree. Stars like gold sequins seen about the relics by the Fathers [S3 p.371]. REPORTED.
- Seven votive lamps hung round the tomb by an unknown hand were taken down by the Society (public veneration was held back before the beatification) [S3 p.371].
- 1599: Cardinal Bellarmine preaches at the tomb on the anniversary; Baronius, who came for his master Philip Neri, kneels, kisses the ground, hangs a portrait over the tomb. Bartoli says Bellarmine concluded no requirement was lacking for canonization [S3 pp.371-372]. REPORTED.
- 1605: Paul V permits the canonical proofs to be gathered. 1609: complete [S3 p.373]. Bartoli adds that the bull of 1609 allowed an Office. Date and bull name not checked.
- 1609: beatification by Paul V [S1; S5 gives 27 July].
- Process figures, in the plea of the consistorial advocate Mons. Nicholas Zambeccari before Gregory XV, as given by Bartoli: "more than two hundred" miracles in the acts, over 1,600 witnesses examined on life and virtues [S3 p.373]. Petitioners listed there: Philip II and III of Spain, Sigismund of Poland, Empress Mary, Henry IV and Louis XIII of France, Maximilian of Bavaria, Emperor Ferdinand [S3 pp.373-374]. REPORTED (a advocate's speech, not an official count).
- Butler on the process: the report in the secret consistory before Gregory XV (by Cardinal Monte and other commissioners) names the light on his face at prayer on the word of St. Philip Neri and Fr. Oliver Manare; the cure of Bastida's falling sickness and a cook's burnt hand by blessing and prayer; the freeing of Pontanus from two years of temptation. "The miracles which are chiefly attended to in a canonization, are those which have been performed after the person's death", approved first by the Auditors of the Rota, then the Congregation of Rites [S1 p.419 note]. CHURCH (approved), per Butler.
- 12 Mar 1622: canonization by Gregory XV, St Peter's, with four others [S3; S7].
- Urban VIII enters him in the Roman martyrology. Wording given by Bartoli: "On the 31st of July is celebrated in Rome, the feast of St. Ignatius, Confessor, Founder of the Society of Jesus; illustrious for his holiness, his miracles, and his zeal in propagating the Catholic religion throughout the whole world." [S3 p.374 (translated)]. CHURCH.
- 1637: remains laid under the altar of his chapel; church "next to the Vatican" in size; remains in a silver shrine under the altar, statue above; the rooms in the professed house where he lived are chapels [S1 p.421]. Butler uses 18th-c. detail; the Catholic Encyclopedia names the altar designer as Pozzi [S5]. The later translation and the present urn: not read.
- 21 Jul 1773 / printed 16 Aug 1773: Clement XIV suppresses the Society (brief). S8 gives 16 Aug 1773 as the printed date. Brief name Dominus ac Redemptor is not in the pages read: check. Survival in White Russia; Pius VII brief Catholicae Fidei, 7 Mar 1801 [S8].
- 7 Aug 1814: Pius VII restores the Society by the bull "Solicitudo omnium ecclesiarum" [S8, spelled "Solicitudo"]. CHURCH.
- 3 Dec 1922: Pius XI, Meditantibus Nobis, to Fr. Wlodimir Ledóchowski, general, on the third centenary of the canonization of Ignatius and Francis Xavier. Ignatius declared heavenly patron of all Spiritual Exercises. The letter says it was done by an earlier apostolic constitution, "Summorum Pontificum": "S. Ignatium a Loyola omnium Exercitiorum Spiritualium caelestem Patronum iussimus." [S6]. CHURCH. The date of Summorum Pontificum is not in the letter: check.

## 4. Character (what the process and reporters saw)
- Prayer with light: Gonzales: "I always saw his face shining with an air so bright and heavenly" [S1 p.413-414]. Laínez compared him to Moses. Nicholas Lanoy saw "a fire flame on his head" at Mass. Philip Neri saw rays; Cardinal Taurusius of Siena published a certificate [S1 p.414]. Dr. John Petronius saw his dark room filled with light when Ignatius entered. REPORTED, process-type.
- John Pascal, boy at Barcelona: saw him in prayer "raised more than a foot above the ground", heard "O my God! O my Lord! O that men knew thee!" [S1 p.414]. This is the only levitation source read. Butler vol. 5 p.351 also says "St Ignatius of Loyola was sometimes seen raised in prayer two feet above the ground, his body at the same time shining with light" (Butler's list on elevations; local search hit). The height differs: a foot (vol.7) and two feet (vol.5). REPORTED, not in the process lists read. Bartoli: even in ecstasies "when his body was raised from the earth" he cried "Oh God! God infinitely good! How canst thou endure a sinner like me?" [S3 p.203].
- He asked that his body be thrown on a dunghill [S1 p.417]; wanted to be forgotten in life and "to be thrown like carrion to the dogs" in death [S3 p.203]. Contrast with his cult.
- Hid his favours: "scarce ever mentioned such things" [S1 p.416].
- Demons in the Modena exorcism call him "lame, bald, and almost blind" [S3 p.385]. Humor and spite in one line.

## 8. Other
- Butler's note defends Ribadeneira against Bayle: in his first Life (1572) Ribadeneira asked why Ignatius's holiness was less shown by miracles than that of other founders; he then wrote he judged many "in the open light"; the 1587 and later Latin abridged life added miracles because the earlier miracles had not yet been examined and approved; the Council of Trent forbade publishing miracles before approval [S1 p.419-420 note]. Quote in the note, as Butler gives it: "Though, when I first printed his life in 1572, I knew of some miracles of the holy father, I did not look upon them to be so verified ... as to think that I ought to publish them, which afterward, by the authentical informations taken for his canonization, were proved true by credible witnesses" [S1 p.419 note, quoting Ribadeneira's Spanish lives, 1604]. STATUS: CONTESTED early (Bayle), then answered. NEW to most English pages.
- Conflict: canonization date 12 Mar vs 22 May (above). Conflict: first burial/translation dates (S1 gives 1587 translation then 1637 altar; S3 gives 1568 and 1587; consistent but S1 skips 1568). Conflict: levitation one foot vs two feet (S1 vol.7 vs vol.5).
- Contested origin of the Exercises: Butler's note rejects a claim that the book was first written by García de Cisneros, abbot of Montserrat, citing Pinius [S1 p.393 note]. CONTESTED, answered by Butler.
- Pius XI treats the Manresa gift of the Exercises from Our Lady as "feruntur caelitus" (reported); the 1922 patronage extends to retreat institutes and sodalities. Patronage of soldiers and Basque country: not found in the sources read. Check.
- The rosary and the medal: no source read links either to a miracle of Ignatius. Bartoli line 16037 mentions the rosary in a post-death account (not read). Mark "to check", no claim.
- Place: Gesù, Rome; Butler's description of the silver shrine and statue is of 1700s [S1 p.421]. Image candidates: portraits from life of Ignatius and Philip Neri shown in the professed house [S1 p.421]; Baronius's portrait hung at the tomb in 1599 [S3 p.372].
- The Society in 1773 and 1814: S8 gives the suppression brief print date 16 Aug 1773, survival in White Russia, restoration bull 7 Aug 1814.
