# Ignatius of Loyola — notes, focus own-words (PARTIAL, saved early)

## 1. Identity (own-words agent; limited)
- Name: Ignatius of Loyola (Spanish Ignacio/Iñigo de Loyola; calls himself "the pilgrim" in third person). Founder of the Society of Jesus. Tradition: Catholic. Tier A.
- Birth: Rix 1900 note says "now commonly received" Dec 24 1491 (OCR misprints "1691"); Polanco thought 1495; Bollandists not settled [S1 ch.I note 1]. Page given: wounded at Pamplona 1521 [S1 ch.I n.1]. Marked: birth year disputed.
- Autobiography: dictated to Luis Gonçalves da Câmara, Sept 1553, resumed 9 Mar 1555, last part 22 Sep 1555 and following days; Gonçalves wrote the last part in Genoa [S1 Introduction, p.~13-14]. The Vatican MS is the dictation copy; the last part is in Italian in another hand [S1].
- Other facts (death 1556, canonization 1622, feast days): not read this session; other agents cover.

## 3. Timeline (Autobiography, S1 by chapter)
- to 1521: "Until his twenty-sixth year he was given up to the vanities of this world", loved feats of arms, desire of renown [S1 ch.I].
- 1521 Pamplona: he argued the commander out of surrender; confessed to a fellow knight, who confessed to him; a cannon ball broke one leg and hurt the other [S1 ch.I]. NEW detail: lay-confession note.
- 1521 Loyola: surgeons rebroke the leg; he said nothing, only clenched fists; near death, on eve of Sts Peter and Paul rallied [S1 ch.I]. Later cut the bone that stuck out below the knee, "that martyrdom", so his brother wondered [S1 ch.I].
- Convalescence: wanted romances; only Life of Christ and Flowers of the Saints (Spanish "Romance"). Daydreamed 2-4 hours on a great lady, then on "What if I did this thing which Blessed Francis did?" Noticed dry sadness after worldly dreams, joy after holy ones: "first exercise of reason on the things of God"; source of discernment of spirits [S1 ch.I].
- Vision of Our Lady with Child; loathing for past carnal deeds; never again consented "to the time this book was written, August 1555" [S1 ch.I].
- He copied extracts into a book of ~300 quarto leaves: Christ's words in red, Mary's in blue; looked at sky and stars for consolation [S1 ch.I]. Thought of becoming a lay Carthusian in Seville [S1 ch.I].
- He left Loyola on a mule; brother tried to keep him; he sent two servants away; Arantzazu night vigil; Navarrete/Nájera ducats paid debts and restored a Marian image [S1 ch.II].
- Road to Montserrat: argument with a Moor about Mary's virginity; he nearly stabbed the Moor; left it to the mule: mule kept the main road [S1 ch.II]. NEW to many English pages.
- Bought sackcloth, one hemp shoe (bad leg), staff, gourd [S1 ch.II].
- Montserrat: written general confession over 3 days; sword and dagger hung at the altar; night vigil on eve of Annunciation 1522; gave his clothes to a beggar; a man chased him thinking beggar a thief; tears of compassion [S1 ch.II].
- Manresa ~ 1522-23, about a year: begged, no meat or wine; let hair and nails grow; saw a shining "serpent-like" thing with eyes (later knew it a demon) [S1 ch.II, III]. Temptation "seventy years" and reply "Canst thou, O wretched creature, promise me even one hour of life?" [S1 ch.II].
- Scruples: general confession again and again; prayed 7 hours a day; thought of throwing himself from the cell window; cried "Lord, I will not do what offendeth Thee"; fasted a week until his confessor ordered him to eat; freed from scruples [S1 ch.III].
- Visions: Trinity as three keys; creation as white with rays; the Eucharist; Christ's humanity as a white body [S1 ch.III].
- The Cardoner: sat by the river near a church about a mile from Manresa; understanding "all made new"; "as if he had been made another man" [S1 ch.III]. Then saw the "serpent" before the cross, recognized a demon, drove it off with his stick.
- 1523 Barcelona to Gaeta, Rome, Venice, Jerusalem: refused a companion; left the ship-biscuit to confessor's judgment; Gaeta, plague; the villagers' attempt on a mother and daughter, his anger [S1 ch.III-IV]. At Mount Olivet he went back to see the footprint, a Franciscan dragged him back; he saw Christ walking above him [S1 ch.IV].
- The Franciscan Provincial sent him from the Holy Land, since many had been killed or captured [S1 ch.IV].
- Back via Cyprus, Venice Jan 1524, Ferrara: gave away all coins to beggars; arrested as spy near Genoa; stripped; told governor nothing "showing no deference"; governor: "This fellow is mad" [S1 ch.V].
- Barcelona 1524-26 grammar with Ardebalo; consolation blocked memorizing; made a promise to teacher [S1 ch.V].
- Alcalá Feb 1526: begging, hospital, Spiritual Exercises and doctrine given; Inquisition inquiries; Figueroa: "if we had detected any you would have been burnt." / "So would you, were you convicted of heresy" [S1 ch.V]. NEW dialogue.
- Paris: some who took the Exercises (Peralta, Castro, Amador) gave away all; Master de Govea threatened him with a public flogging ("Hall") at St Barbara; Rouen barefoot walk without food or drink, 14 leagues the first day [S1 ch.VII]. Plague house: touched the sick man's wound; his hand ached; forced his fingers into mouth [S1 ch.VII].
- Paris: won Peter Faber and Francis Xavier by the Exercises [S1 ch.VII].
- Loyola 1535 visit: taught children; got gambling stopped; a law about women "veiling" for priests; Angelus bell thrice a day [S1 ch.VIII].
- Venice 1537: ordained 24 June 1537 (S1 note); Vicenza abandoned house without door or window, 40 days of prayer, preached [S1 ch.VIII].
- La Storta (near Rome) late 1537: "God the Father placing him with Christ His Son" [S1 ch.VIII]. NB: only this brief line in Autobiography; the "I will be propitious to you in Rome" words are from Laínez, not read here.
- Rome 1538: persecution; Michael tried to assassinate (S1 note); Ignatius insisted on sentence; was cleared [S1 ch.VIII].

## Quotations (all English from S1-S3 are public-domain translations; original Spanish not checked in this session unless noted)
1. "Until his twenty-sixth year he was given up to the vanities of this world, delighting chiefly in feats of arms, and engrossed with an inordinate desire of acquiring renown." Autobiography ch.I, Rix 1900 [S1]. public-domain.
2. On the surgery: "neither for this, nor for anything else which he had endured or was to endure did he utter a single word, nor was any sign of pain wrung from him, beyond the vehement clenching of his fists." Autobiography ch.I [S1].
3. "What if I did this thing which Blessed Francis did? What if I copied Blessed Dominic in this?" Autobiography ch.I [S1].
4. Discernment found in daily life: his soul found "great delight in pondering worldly things, but when he desisted through weariness, he found himself sad and dry"; with holy thoughts "joy remained with him." Autobiography ch.I [S1]. (Close to the original; copy exact if used: "...he found himself sad and dry; whereas when he contemplated his departure for Jerusalem... joy remained with him.")
5. To the devil of the "seventy years": "Canst thou, O wretched creature, promise me even one hour of life?" Autobiography ch.II [S1].
6. Of Manresa moods: "like as when one snatches a cloak from off a man's shoulders, even so were all heaviness and sorrow taken away." Autobiography ch.II [S1].
7. In scruples: "Make haste to help me, Lord, for there is no help in man... Ah, if I knew where I might find it, no labour would seem great nor hard... had I to go after a dog's whelp and take my cure from him, I should do it." Autobiography ch.III [S1].
8. "Lord, I will not do what offendeth Thee." Autobiography ch.III [S1].
9. God dealt with him "as a master with a school-boy whom he teaches." Autobiography ch.III [S1].
10. On the Cardoner: "as much enlightened as if he had been made another man and another mind had been given to him." Autobiography ch.III [S1].
11. On a companion for Jerusalem: he would not take one, "not even if he were the son or the brother of the Duke of Cardona, for that he wished to have the three virtues of faith, charity, and hope." Autobiography ch.III [S1].
12. Alcalá: Figueroa: "No... if we had detected any you would have been burnt." Ignatius: "So would you, were you convicted of heresy." Autobiography ch.V [S1]. NEW.
13. To the Paris doctor: "It is because I do not speak to anybody on religious subjects. But we shall resume our former relations as soon as I have finished my course." Autobiography ch.VII [S1].
14. Plague: "If you have plague in your hand, then get it in your mouth also." Autobiography ch.VII [S1].
15. Spiritual Exercises, Principle and Foundation: "Man is created to praise, reverence, and serve God our Lord, and by this means to save his soul." Mullan 1914, First Week [S2]. public-domain.
16. Same: "...we want not health rather than sickness, riches rather than poverty, honor rather than dishonor, long rather than short life, and so in all the rest; desiring and choosing only what is most conducive for us to the end for which we are created." [S2].
17. Annotation 15: the director should stand "in the centre like a balance, leave the Creator to act immediately with the creature, and the creature with its Creator and Lord." Mullan [S2].
18. Contemplation to Gain Love, Note: "love ought to be put more in deeds than in words." Mullan [S2].
19. "Take, Lord, and receive all my liberty, my memory, my intellect, and all my will — all that I have and possess. Thou gavest it to me: to Thee, Lord, I return it! All is Thine, dispose of it according to all Thy will. Give me Thy love and grace, for this is enough for me." Contemplation to Gain Love, first point, Mullan p.119-120 [S2]. This IS Ignatius's own prayer (not a misattribution).
20. Rules for discernment, Rule 2 (Week 1): "It belongs to God our Lord to give consolation to the soul without preceding cause" Mullan [S2].
21. Rules for thinking with the Church, 13th: "the white which I see, is black, if the Hierarchical Church so decides" Mullan [S2]. Mark: often misquoted; context is obedience to Church teaching.
22. Constitutions, Proemium: "interior law of charity and love, which the Holy Spirit is wont to write and imprint upon hearts" (my English of: "intema Charitatis et Amoris illius lex, quam Sanctus Spiritus scribere, et in cordibus imprimere solet", Latin text, S4 Proemium §1), preferred "potius, quam ullae externae Constitutiones". public-domain.
23. Constitutions Part VI ch.1 §1 (obedience): "perinde ac si cadaver essent... vel similiter, atque senis baculus" (S4; footnote cite "P. VI. c. 1. § 1"). Our English: "as if they were a corpse... or like an old man's staff." Note: the "cadaver" line is in the Latin text; the Letter on Obedience gives the fuller explanation (see S5).
24. Letter on Obedience to the Jesuits of Portugal, 26 March 1553: "la obediencia, mas particularmente que en ninguna otra, me da deseo Dios Nuestro Señor de veros señalar" S5 vol-1553, p.186-187; cites St Gregory: "Obedientia sola virtus est, qua menti ceteras virtutes inseruntur". Editors: most famous letter; occasion: Portuguese Jesuits too attached to Simão Rodrigues, refusing other superiors [S5].

## Misattributions and nuances (flagged as asked)
- "Anima Christi": NOT by Ignatius. First half 14th century, indulgenced by John XXII in 1330; in a c.1370 British Museum MS; in Cardinal Peter of Luxembourg's prayer book (d.1387); on a gate of the Alcázar in Seville, time of Pedro the Cruel (1350-69). Ignatius uses it; "he only mentions it in the first edition of his Spiritual Exercises" and later editions print it in full [S6]. Our copies: S3 prints it as a "Prayer" before Part I and names it in the Colloquy (note 30). Rights: public-domain.
- "Take, Lord, receive": this is Ignatius's own, the offering in the Contemplation to Gain Love (S2 p.119-120; S3). Do NOT flag as wrong. Latin: "Suscipe". Common English hymn "Take, Lord, receive" is a later reworking.
- "Ad majorem Dei gloriam" (AMDG): Latin phrase appears in the Latin Constitutions, in the Proemium §2 and elsewhere ("ad majorem Dei gloriam in primis quaeritur"; "quae tota ad majorem Dei gloriam... instituta est") [S4]. The Spanish original says "mayor gloria de Dios" (not checked). Nuance: it is a recurring motto in the Constitutions, not a single slogan; the phrase "greater" is the point (discernment of the "magis"). Not checked: use before Ignatius.
- "Give me a child until he is seven (the first seven years and I will give you the man)": NOT said by Ignatius, per Geger SJ [S7]. Another site says it is Aristotelian; unverified.
- "Go and set the world on fire": not by Ignatius; circumstantial evidence points to Olivier Mannaerts crediting him with a saying of the time [S7]. "Laugh and grow strong": Francis Thompson's play on "Laugh and grow fat" [S7].
- "I have no need of prisons when I can show them the door": not by Ignatius [S7].
- Prayer "Lord, teach me to be generous... to give and not to count the cost": not by Ignatius, and "quite opposed to Ignatius' spirituality" (he urged rest, food, moderate penance) [S7]. Do not quote as his.
- "What more can I do for Christ?": Ignatius's Exercises ask "What ought I to do for Christ?" [S7]. The Mullan OCR did not return that phrase in my search; check the Colloquy before Christ Crucified, n.53 (not verified).
- Note on penance: the Autobiography has Ignatius himself admit the early extremes were unwise: at Manresa he stopped the severities, cut nails and hair [S1 ch.III].
- "Paul IV's changes" (1558): Constitutions closing note says Paul IV through Cardinal Caraffa required a three-year General and choir for the Hours; Congregation obeyed [S4, end]. NEW to many English pages.

## Character (one line each)
- Truthful to a fault: told the Guardian and Provincial plainly; "without in anything swerving from truth" with his brother [S1 ch.I].
- Stubborn will: cut the bone, ordered the surgery against advice [S1 ch.I].
- Unvain about hair: hair and nails neglected "for the same reason"; had been fastidious [S1 ch.II].
- Bad temper in defence of the weak: shouted at the villagers [S1 ch.IV].
- Reckless trust: walked into war camps against advice, gave all coins to beggars [S1 ch.V].
- Anxiety and scruples at Manresa: fears of suicide, long fasting [S1 ch.III].
- Cautious later: told Laínez, "We must of necessity proceed with caution; and we must not make the acquaintance of women, unless they be of very high rank" [S1 ch.VIII].
- Humble about his gift: God taught him "as a master with a school-boy" [S1 ch.III].
- Wanted trial: insisted on a sentence in Rome so the matter was cleared [S1 ch.VIII].
- Plain speech: addressed all as "You", no titles, until the Genoa governor [S1 ch.V].
