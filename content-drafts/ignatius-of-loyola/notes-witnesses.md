## Timeline (part 1, from S1)
- Until age 26: "given up to the vanities of this world, delighting chiefly in feats of arms" and "inordinate desire of acquiring renown" [S1 p.41]. NB the Rix text says "twenty-sixth year"; the editor's note (1) discusses it.
- 1521 Pamplona (Pampeluna): French besiege; the nobles want to yield on terms; he argues the town can hold a little longer; his arguments "prevailed over the opinion of all the other nobles" [S1 p.41-42]. Before the battle he confesses to a fellow noble, who confesses to him in turn [S1 p.42]. A cannon ball shatters one leg and wounds the other as it passes between them; the defenders then yield; the French treat him kindly, then carry him on a litter to Loyola 12-15 days later [S1 p.42-43]. (The date 20 May is NOT in S1; to check.)
- Loyola: doctors re-break the leg; "nor was any sign of pain wrung from him, beyond the vehement clenching of his fists" [S1 p.43]. On the eve of SS. Peter and Paul (28 Jun) he receives sacraments, doctors say he is dead by midnight if no change; devotion to Peter; he mends at that midnight [S1 p.43-44]. Bone overlaps below the knee, leg shortened, he asks surgeons to cut away the protruding bone for vanity; "he resolved to undergo that martyrdom"; his elder brother marvels [S1 p.44-45]. Then instruments stretch the leg for days; he cannot stand without pain [S1 p.45]. NEW (vivid): vanity as the motive for the second surgery.
- Convalescence reading: asks for chivalric romances ("lying and vain romances"); none in the house; given "The Life of Christ" and "Flowers of the Saints" (Flos Sanctorum), "both in the Romance language" [S1 p.45]. NEW detail kept in S1.
- Two kinds of thought: dreaming of an illustrious lady (not countess nor duchess, "her rank was higher", Spanish note) for 2-4 hours at a time; versus "What if I did this thing which Blessed Francis did? What if I copied Blessed Dominic" [S1 p.46-48]. Worldly thoughts left him "sad and dry"; holy thoughts left joy. "this was the first exercise of reason which he made on the things of God" [S1 p.48]. Origin of discernment of spirits [S1 p.48-49].
- Night vision: Our Lady with the Child; "a loathing seized him for the former deeds of his life"; never again consented to carnal desire up to 1555 [S1 p.49-50]. He dares not claim it was from God [S1 p.50]. His household sees the change [S1 p.50].
- Copies extracts into a book of about 300 quarto leaves; words of Christ in red, Mary's in blue; looks at sky and stars for consolation [S1 p.51]. Considers entering the Carthusians at Seville as lay brother, anonymous; sends a servant to Burgos to ask about the Rule [S1 p.51-52].
- Leaving Loyola: tells his brother he must go to the Duke of Nájera; brother suspects and walks him from room to room begging him not to ruin himself; Ignatius answers without a lie and is released [S1 p.52-53]. He leaves on a mule, scourging himself every night; another brother goes along to Oñate; vigil at Aránzazu; collects debts from the Duke's treasurer, pays creditors and restores a Madonna image; dismisses two servants [S1 p.64-65].
- Road to Montserrat: argues about Mary's virginity with a "Saracen" (Moor) who rides ahead; Ignatius wants to stab him; lets the mule choose at the fork; mule keeps to the highway [S1 p.66-68]. NEW/vivid.
- Buys sackcloth tunic, one hempen shoe (bad leg swollen at night), staff and gourd; his head full of "Amadis de Gaul", he plans a knightly night vigil [S1 p.68-69].
- Montserrat: written general confession over three days; mule given to confessor; sword and dagger hung at the altar; first time he had told anyone his desires [S1 p.69-70]. Vigil on the eve of the Annunciation, 1522 (24 Mar 1522): gives clothes to a beggar, kneels or stands all night with staff; takes Communion at dawn [S1 p.70]. A man runs after him: the beggar was accused of theft; Ignatius weeps for him [S1 p.71]. Goes to Manresa, not Barcelona, to avoid honour [S1 p.70-71].
- Manresa: begs bread; no meat or wine; lets hair and nails grow because he had been vain about them [S1 p.71-72]. Sees a shining serpent-like thing with eyes in the air, later recognised as a demon [S1 p.72, 92-93]. Temptation: "How can you possibly endure such a life as this through the seventy years yet before you?" He answers: "Canst thou, O wretched creature, promise me even one hour of life?" [S1 p.73]. Alternation of dryness and joy: "like as when one snatches a cloak from off a man's shoulders" [S1 p.74]. An old woman of Manresa: "O that Jesus Christ my Lord would one day show Himself to thee!" [S1 p.75]. Scruples; confessions repeated; no confessor frees him; he decides himself never to mention the past again [S1 p.75-86].
- Seven hours of prayer a day [S1 p.86]. Nights full of such consolation that he loses sleep; he doubts and decides to sleep [S1 p.86-87]. A vision of meat ready to eat after he had given up meat [S1 p.87]. "God wrought with him at this time, as a master with a school-boy whom he teaches" [S1 p.88].
- Trinity vision on the steps of the monastery (Dominican church), as "a triple plectrum, or of three spinet-keys"; he weeps all day [S1 p.88-89]. Creation vision: something white with rays [S1 p.89-90]. Eucharist vision at Mass [S1 p.90]. Sees the humanity of Christ as a white body [S1 p.90-91].
- THE CARDONER: walking to a church about a mile from Manresa (he thinks St Paul's) along the river; sits facing the deep stream; "the eyes of his mind were opened, not so as to see any kind of vision, but so as to understand and comprehend spiritual things" [S1 p.91-92]. Text says that if all the help received through life to age 62 were gathered "these all would appear less than he had been given at this one time" [S1 p.92]. He kneels at the cross and thanks God; sees the eyed thing again, now knows it is a demon; drives it off with his stick [S1 p.92-93]. NB: the Rix text does not name the river "Cardoner" in the main text (check note); the name is in the Spanish.
- Manresa illness, fever; cries to the noble ladies to call "O sinner" if they see him dying [S1 p.93-94]. Winter sickness; ladies nurse him; forced to wear shoes, cap, and two coarse grey garments [S1 p.94-95]. About a year at Manresa in all; he then stops the extreme severities and cuts hair and nails [S1 p.90].
- Barcelona, early 1523: refuses a companion "even if he were the son or the brother of the Duke of Cardona" because he wanted to rely on God only [S1 p.96-97]. Takes ship biscuit on his confessor's order; leaves leftover coins (Blancas) on a bench by the landing [S1 p.97-99]. 20+ days in Barcelona seeking spiritual people.
- Voyage to Gaeta in five days (storm); roads: plague region; night in a village inn where he rages when a mother and daughter pilgrims are assaulted, and no one dares touch him [S1 p.99-100]. Reaches Rome on Palm Sunday; leaves on the octave of Easter with Adrian VI's blessing; takes 6 or 7 gold coins, repents, gives them to beggars before Venice [S1 p.100-101]. A man flees him at dawn in terror of his "extremely pale and bloodless aspect" [S1 p.101].
- Venice: sleeps in Piazza San Marco; a rich Spaniard hosts him and introduces him to the Doge, who puts him on the governor's ship to Cyprus; fever on the sailing day, doctor: "he could certainly go if they wanted to bury him in the ship"; he rebukes shameful deeds aboard; fellow Spaniards warn he may be put off on an island [S1 p.102-105].
- Jerusalem: ride on asses; approach in silence; "more than mortal joy was shared by every one" [S1 p.105-106]. The Franciscan Guardian and Provincial order him to leave, with papal authority to excommunicate; he submits [S1 p.106-108]. He slips back alone to the Mount of Olives, gives the keepers a penknife and scissors to be allowed in, to see the footprints; a Franciscan servant seizes him by the arm in rage; Christ walks above him on the way back [S1 p.108-110].

## Timeline (part 2, from S1, S2, S3)
- 1491: born in Guipúzcoa; father Beltrán Yáñez de Oñaz y Loyola, mother María Sáez de Balda; "cinco hijas, y ocho hijos" and Íñigo "el postrero de todos" [S2 Libro 1 cap.1]. Conflict: Butler says "three daughters and eight sons" (youngest still Ignatius) [S3 p.385]. Thirteen = Ribadeneira's count. Ribadeneira says the family sent him young to the court of the Catholic Kings [S2]; Butler says he served as page to Ferdinand under the Duke of Nájera [S3 p.386]. Arévalo is NOT named in S1, S2 (read part), S3: to check.
- Before 1521: Butler: he hated gaming, mediated soldiers' quarrels, wrote verses, composed a poem in praise of St Peter [S3 p.386]. (Butler's own claim; source not given here.)
- 1521 Pamplona: Ribadeneira adds the second blow: a stone from the wall hit the left leg [S2]; Butler the same [S3 p.387]. Ribadeneira on the surgery: "ni mudó color, ni gimió, ni suspiró" (OCR garbled; paraphrase only: no change of colour, no groan, no sigh) [S2]. Date 20 May 1521: not in any source read. To check.
- Loyola: Butler adds the dream of St Peter touching him, and the room shaking with a crack in the wall on the night he vowed to Our Lady [S3 p.388-89]; S1 has neither. LEGEND flag: the shaking house is a later tradition (not in Ignatius's own account).
- 1522 (Lady Day): vigil at Montserrat [S1 p.70]; Butler names the Benedictine confessor Jean Chanones and says the sword hung on a pillar [S3 p.390]; S1 says the sword and dagger were "placed in the church, on the altar" [S1 p.70]. Conflict: altar vs pillar.
- Manresa cave: S1 does not mention a cave in the pages read; Butler says he hid in "a dark deep cave" in the "Vale of Paradise" half a mile away, was found half dead [S3 p.391]. Later tradition: flag as late source for the cave. Butler also says iron girdle, three disciplines a day, children throwing stones [S3 p.391].
- Manresa "teacher" line and the ten months: Butler says ten months at Manresa [S3 p.395]; S1 says "about a year in all" [S1 p.90].
- Barcelona 1524: Ardebalo master, Isabel Roser gives support [S1 p.125]. Studies grammar; "new lights" invade his memory work; he promises his teacher to keep attending classes if he can get bread and water, and the temptation ends [S1 p.125-126]. Butler: conjugating "amo" he could only think "I love God; I am loved by God" [S3 p.396] (late/popular).
- Alcalá 1526-27: studies Soto's "terms", Albert's physics, the Sentences; gives the Exercises and teaches doctrine; hosted at the new hospital [S1 p.127-128]. Diego de Eguía hands him bed-hangings and candlesticks to give to the poor [S1 p.128]. Toledo inquisitors come; the host warns that they are called "Sagati" and "Illuminate"; Vicar Figueroa finds no fault but orders dress: Ignatius and Arteaga in black, Calisto and Cáceres brown [S1 p.129-130]. Figueroa: "if we had detected any you would have been burnt"; Ignatius: "So would you, were you convicted of heresy" [S1 p.130]. Priest gives him the Host unwillingly after eight days [S1 p.130]. Second inquiry about a veiled married woman [S1 p.131].
- Alcalá prison 21 Apr to 1 Jun 1527: 42 days; no reason given for 17 days; cause: two noble women (mother and daughter, widows) who walked to see the Veronica at Jaén; asked if he kept the Sabbath [S1 p.136-138, Fita's note]. Companion Calisto locks himself in the prison to join him, then Ignatius gets him out [S1 p.138]. Sentence: wear scholars' clothes; not to teach the faith for four years until more letters [S1 p.139]. Fonseca, archbishop of Toledo, gives four gold crowns [S1 p.140]. Butler adds "he rejoiced in his jail...many called him another St. Paul in prison" [S3 p.398].
- Salamanca 1527: Dominicans at San Esteban question him; "you speak from the Holy Ghost" trap; he refuses to say more without his superiors [S1 p.141-143]. Prison: the two chained by the feet, chain 10-12 handbreadths [S1 p.144]. Bachiller Frías examines the Exercises; Ignatius defines mortal and venial sin; the judges find nothing to condemn but forbid defining mortal or venial sin for four years [S1 p.145-148]. Prisoners escape one night; Ignatius and his friend stay, doors open [S1 p.147]. 22 days; he decides to go to Paris [S1 p.148-149]. Mendoza (later cardinal of Burgos) visits him in prison: "there are not so many fetters, handcuffs, and chains in Salamanca, but I should wish to wear still more for the love of God" [S1 p.147].
- Paris, February 1528 (the text says "about February, in the year 1528 as he calculated"): lodges with countrymen; Montaigu; student at the humanities with boys; his money lent to a lodger who spent it; begs; hostel of St James; plans to serve in a college; yearly trips to Flanders (and once England) to beg [S1 p.157-160]. Gives the Exercises to Peralta, Castro, Amador; they give away their goods; Spaniards drag them out of the hostel with arms [S1 p.160-161]. Govea threatens "the Hall" (public flogging) [S1 p.161-162]; Butler adds Ignatius's appeal in Govea's chamber and Govea's public apology on his knees [S3 p.398-399] (Butler/Ribadeneira only; flag: from later Life, not Gonçalves).
- Walk to Rouen barefoot and fasting, to help the sick lodger who owed him money; fear until Argenteuil, then joy on the high ground; 14 leagues the first day [S1 p.162-163]. NEW (vivid): he forgave the debt by a 3-day fast.
- Paris: Inquisitor Ory; begins arts on St Remigius, 1 Oct (1529?); meets Faber and Xavier, "won over...by means of the Spiritual Exercises" [S1 p.166-167]. Plague house: he touches the sore of a sick man, fears plague, puts his fingers in his mouth: "If you have plague in your hand, then get it in your mouth also" [S1 p.168]. Butler adds the frozen pond and the pest-hospital stories (late) [S3 p.400].
- 15 Aug 1534 Montmartre: not in S1. Butler: seven (he lists Laínez, Salmerón, Bobadilla, Rodrigues, with Faber and Xavier) vowed in the crypt at Montmartre after communion from Faber; deadline 25 Jan 1537; "on the feast of the Assumption...1534" [S3 p.400-401]. S1 gives the plan: Venice, then Jerusalem; if blocked after a year, go to the Pope [S1 p.170-171].
- 1535: leaves Paris for Spain on doctors' orders (stomach pain every 25th day, up to 16-17 hours) [S1 p.169-170]. Home in Azpeitia: refuses his brother's house, goes to the public hospital; teaches children daily; his brother says "One is enough for me"; the poor, gaming, and women who "veiled" for priests [S1 p.176-178]. Butler: he publicly confesses the orchard theft and pays the man [S3 p.401] (Butler only).
- Return to Italy: loses his way on a cliff path by a river, crawls on hands and knees: "the severest bodily suffering he ever endured" (paraphrase) [S1 p.181]. Falls from a footbridge at Bologna; begs from gate to gate and gets nothing [S1 p.181].
- Venice 1536-37: gives the Exercises to Contarini, de Dotis, Rozes, Hozes; a trial in Venice that clears him; nine companions join him in January 1537 and nurse the sick [S1 p.182-183]. Ordained priest 24 June 1537 (editor's note), says first Mass Christmas 1538 at Santa Maria Maggiore (editor's note) [S1 p.186-187 notes]. Vicenza: ruined house with no door or window, straw bed, 40 days of prayer; then preaching in squares waving caps [S1 p.184-185]. Mortal-sick companion at Bassano: he runs while himself feverish, Faber cannot keep up; God certifies the man will live [S1 p.185-186].
- La Storta: S1 describes it without the name: in a church a few miles before Rome "God the Father placing him with Christ His Son" [S1 p.187]; Gonçalves reminds him Laínez recorded more; Ignatius: "Whatever Lainez may have said about it is undoubtedly true" [S1 p.187]. Butler adds the words of Christ, "Ego vobis Rome propitius ero", reported by Laínez to the Fathers with Ribadeneira present [S3 p.402]. Source status: early (Laínez via Ribadeneira); the Latin words come through Butler's note citing Bouhours.
- Rome 1538: Ignatius says "windows shut against them"; "we must not make the acquaintance of women, unless they be of very high rank" [S1 p.188]. Monte Cassino: 40 days giving the Exercises to Ortiz; vision of Hozes entering heaven [S1 p.188]. Michael the Navarrese and others spread charges; he demands a sentence from the governor and Pope Paul III at Tusculum; verdict in his favour [S1 p.189-191]. Thurston note: Michael tried to kill him (editor's claim, p.189 note).
- 27 Sep 1540 Regimini militantis: Butler says Paul III approved by bull on 27 September 1540 [S3 p.403]. Elected first general, accepts after his confessor's order; takes office Easter 1541 [S3 p.403]. Writes Constitutions in Spanish, Polanco puts them into Latin [S3 p.405].
- Constitutions at Mass: Ignatius told Gonçalves he made them by saying Mass daily and weeping; he spent 40 days on the question of revenues for churches; visions on file in a bundle of papers; refused to lend the papers [S1, Postscript p.195-196].
- Roman years: kitchen scullion, 46 days catechising children after election (Butler) [S3 p.405]; houses for converted Jews, women, orphans [S3 p.405-406]; colleges; Roman College [S3 p.409]; Ethiopian mission: he sent Gonçalves to the Portuguese embassy every third day for three months [S1 intro note, Thurston].
- Dictation of the Testament: Gonçalves da Câmara's first session 4 Aug 1553 in the garden; Ignatius told him of his own two-year fight with vainglory; Gonçalves recorded in September 1553 up to Manresa; Nadal arrives 18 Oct 1554 and insists; resumed 9 Mar 1555; the Pope Julius dies 23 Mar [S1 Introduction p.29-35]. Last dictation in the "red tower", 22 Sep 1555 and following days; Gonçalves finished at Genoa in Dec 1555 [S1 p.35-37].
- Nadal's preface: in 1551 Ignatius said "But just now, and I was higher than heaven!"; Nadal asked for an account as "a last testament"; Ignatius: "say three Masses over the matter, you and Polanco and Pontio" [S1 Nadal preface p.26-27]. Nadal pressed again in 1554 [S1 p.28-29].
- Death: Butler says the day before he died he asked Polanco to ask the Pope's blessing; next morning he raised his eyes and hands and died "on the last day of July, in the year 1556, the sixty-fifth of his age" [S3 p.419]. He was general 15 years, 3 months, 9 days [S3 p.418]. Polanco/Laínez accounts of the death not read: to check; the oft-quoted detail that he died without the sacraments is NOT in the sources read.
- Beatification 1609 (Paul V), canonization 1622 (Gregory XV; bull published 1623 by Urban VIII) [S3 p.421]. (Other agent covers this.)

## Character (witnesses)
- Vain young courtier: "delighting chiefly in feats of arms" and "inordinate desire of acquiring renown" [S1 p.41]. Fussy about hair and nails ("somewhat nice about the arrangement of his hair") [S1 p.72]. Cared about boots: the cut bone was for appearance; Butler says "that his boot and stockings might sit handsomely" [S3 p.388].
- Quick temper at 33: wants to stab the Moor [S1 p.67]; rages at the inn near Gaeta [S1 p.99].
- Scrupulous; near suicide during the scruples at Manresa ("a sudden impulse to put an end to it") [S1 p.86].
- Fear of vainglory: told Gonçalves he fought it two years and did not dare tell anyone he was going to Jerusalem [S1 p.30].
- Stubborn, literal-minded penitent: gives away six or seven gold coins; leaves coins on a bench at the pier [S1 p.98, 101].
- Humility with edge: answers inquisitors with irony ("So would you") [S1 p.130]; rebukes crews [S1 p.104-105].
- Teacher's habit: "keep the rule" and rebukes Gonçalves for approaching too close and for gazing at him; Thurston's note: the Saint rebuked Manare for gazing into his eyes and gave a penance for fifteen months [S1 Introduction p.35 note; S3 p.410].
- Gonçalves on his face: "I always saw his face shining with an air so bright and heavenly" [S3 p.413-414, quoting Gonzales]. Laínez compared him to Moses [S3 p.414].
- Never presumed on life: "How then? and you imagine you will live so long?" [S1 p.31].
- Rix/Tyrrell (1900) on his reserve: "that grey reserve", and the "stern serva regulam" Ignatius [S1 p.191 note]. Editor's opinion; use as opinion.

## Miracles (from witnesses; popular or recorded, not approved by me)
- Recovery at Loyola on the eve of Sts Peter and Paul: reported by Ignatius, S1 p.43-44; Butler adds the dream of Peter [S3 p.387]. Status: Ignatius's own report; popular elaboration.
- Bassano: knows a companion will live, tells Faber [S1 p.185-186]. Status: Ignatius's own report.
- Hozes entering heaven in vision [S1 p.188].
- Butler list of reported lifetime and post-death events: Bastida cured of the falling sickness; a cook's burnt hand cured; Pontanus freed of temptations; Isabel Rebelles (Barcelona, 1601) broken thigh healed; Anna Barozellona palsy [S3 p.419-420]. Status: examined for canonization per Butler (not verified here).
