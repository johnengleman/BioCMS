# Plan: St. Ignatius of Loyola (tier A)

## The driving question

Iñigo de Loyola wanted renown, and he had a will strong enough to get anything he wanted: he let surgeons saw off a bone below his knee, with no sound but clenched fists, so that his leg would look right. After his conversion that same will turned on God and nearly destroyed him: at Manresa he tried to out-penance St. Dominic and St. Francis, confessed the same sins over and over, and was tempted to throw himself from a window. What resolved it was not more willpower. God taught him "as a master with a school-boy" to read the movements of his own soul (dryness and joy, the first thing he noticed on his sickbed) and to hand his will over. The hook raises the question "what does God do with a will like that?"; the ending answers it with his own prayer, "Take, Lord, and receive all my liberty… and all my will," and with the man who asked to be thrown on a dunghill and whose body the crowds of Rome tried to cut pieces from.

## Why famous / what set him apart

Famous: founder of the Society of Jesus and author of the Spiritual Exercises, canonized in 1622 with Teresa of Avila, Francis Xavier and Philip Neri.

Set apart: he learned discernment from his own moods on a sickbed and turned it into a method that others could be led through, with the director standing "like a balance" between God and the soul. And a soldier who obeyed no one built an order whose whole law was obedience, while himself going to the bishops' courts and the Inquisition again and again and insisting on a verdict.

## The hook

Type: scene at the edge plus paradox. The sickroom at Loyola, 1521: the leg healed crooked, the bone jutting below the knee, the surgeons' warning, "that martyrdom", the brother who could not watch it. Then the turn: thirty-two years later the same man, now general of the Jesuits in Rome, dictated this story about himself in the third person and began with the blunt verdict "Until his twenty-sixth year he was given up to the vanities of this world." The question: what happens when a will like that turns toward God? Answered in "A Master and a School-Boy" (Cardoner) and fully in "Take, Lord, and Receive".

## Chapters (h2 headline; key scene; block quote; pull)

1. The Bone Below the Knee: the second surgery; dictation in Rome; thesis. BQ "Until his twenty-sixth year…". Pull: a castle in Guipúzcoa.
2. A Cannon Ball at Pamplona: the argument with the commander, lay confession, the ball. Pull: carried home on a litter.
3. Two Books in a Sickroom: clenched fists, eve of Sts Peter and Paul, the romances that were not there, sad and dry vs joy, Our Lady, red and blue ink, stars, the brother walking him room to room. BQ "What if I did this thing…". Pull: he rides away on a mule.
4. The Mule at the Fork: the Moor, the mule chooses; sackcloth and one shoe; vigil at Montserrat, beggar accused. Pull: he turns aside to Manresa "for some days" (stays a year).
5. One Hour of Life: seventy-years temptation; the cloak; the old woman; scruples, the window, the week of fasting. BQs seventy years; "dog's whelp". Pull: "Lord, I will not do what offendeth Thee" and the freedom that follows.
6. A Master and a School-Boy: Trinity as three keys, white rays, the river, "another man", the demon and the stick, nails and hair cut. BQ river. Pull: Jerusalem.
7. No Companion but God: Cardona refusal, coins on the bench, the inn near Gaeta, the doge, "bury him in the ship", Jerusalem, Olivet, the Franciscan's grip, Christ above. Pull: ordered home.
8. This Fellow Is Mad: the captain's taunt and the wreck, the spy arrest, "You", Barcelona grammar with boys, the promise to the teacher. Pull: Alcalá.
9. So Would You: Alcalá hospital, dyed clothes, Figueroa exchange, 42 days in prison, Salamanca dinner trap, chains, the open doors. BQ "We do not preach…". Pull: Paris.
10. A Beggar at the University: Montaigu, Flanders begging, Govea's "Hall", Rouen barefoot, the plague hand, Faber and Xavier; the Exercises explained. BQ Principle and Foundation. Pull: Montmartre vow and illness.
11. One Is Enough for Me: Azpeitia hostel and children, the cliff path, Bologna mud, Venice, ordination, Vicenza house without door, Bassano. Pull: road to Rome.
12. Placed With the Son: La Storta; "Whatever Lainez may have said"; windows shut; Michael and the demand for a sentence. Pull: the Society approved.
13. The General in the Kitchen: 1540, election refused, scullion, 46 days of catechism, houses, Constitutions with tears, the nun of Bologna test, the Letter on Obedience. BQ Constitutions Proemium (our English). Pull: what his inner life was.
14. Higher Than Heaven: Diary tears and flame, the light on his face, John Pascal, Nadal 1551, "Nothing", Gonçalves and the red tower, "Keep the rule". BQ Diary. Pull: "you imagine you will live so long?"
15. A Body for the Dunghill: 30–31 July 1556, the crowds, the cloth and the Nerucci girl, 1568 and 1587. Pull: the cause opens.
16. Take, Lord, and Receive: Ribadeneira's caution, 1609, 1622, martyrology, 1773/1814, 1922 patron of the Exercises; end on the Suscipe. BQ Suscipe.

## Open loops

1. The bone and the will (ch1) → answered at Manresa (ch5–6) and in the Suscipe (ch16).
2. "Sad and dry" vs joy on the sickbed (ch3) → becomes the rules for discernment and the Exercises (ch6, ch10).
3. Jerusalem: the plan from the sickbed (ch3) → thwarted (ch7), vowed again at Montmartre (ch10), finally replaced by Rome (ch12).
4. The story Nadal begged for (ch1 frame) → the red tower (ch14).

## Rules applied

Anima Christi, "set the world on fire", "Go, brethren, inflame the world", the seven-year-old saying and the "count the cost" prayer are not quoted as his. Shaking house, Manresa cave, Govea's apology, the frozen pond: told only as later tradition. Paraphrases (twenty or forty times; die for the mysteries) not in quotation marks. Canonization date conflict in the Note.
