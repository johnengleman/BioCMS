Status tags: CHURCH = stated in a papal or Vatican document. RAYMOND/PROCESS = reported by Raymond of Capua or Process witnesses. LEGEND = later or unsourced tradition. CONTESTED = sources disagree.
Rule used: no fact from memory. Items I could not source are in "Other: to check".

## Identity and church-level facts
- Born Siena 1347; died Rome 29 April 1380; body at Santa Maria sopra Minerva [S3 §2: "die undetricesimo mensis Aprilis, anno MCCCLXXX"; "in templo Sanctae Mariae supra Minervam"]. Nigi di Doccio letter 22 May 1380 gives the date "2Qth of April" [S2 p.281].
- CHURCH. Canonization: Pius II (Enea Silvio Piccolomini, a Sienese), bull Misericordias Domini, ceremony in St Peter's, 29 June 1461 [S4; S2 p.342]. Pius II wrote the bull and her Office himself [S3 §4: "ut ipse testatur, canonizationis Litteras decretales composuit"]. Bull text is in Laurent, Processus Castellanus, pp.521-530 [S4 note]. Bull does not name the stigmata; Pius II's Office hymn does ("Vulnerum formam miserata Christi exprimis ipsa") [S1 p.311 n.]. NEW for English pages: Pius II decided on the canonization during his 1459 stay in Siena [S2 p.341]; the ceremony is painted by Pinturicchio in the Piccolomini Library, Siena [S2 p.343].
- CHURCH. Bull sentence, as Paul VI cites it: "Nemo ad eam accessit, qui non doctior meliorque abierit. Doctrina eius infusa, non acquisita fuit; prius magistra visa est quam discipula" [S3 §4]. (S4 prints a shorter form.) Paraphrase: "No one came to her who did not leave wiser and better; her teaching was infused, not acquired; she seemed a teacher before she was a pupil."
- CHURCH. Urban VIII "vulnera, seu stigmata, Catharinae sua auctoritate probavit" [S3 §4]. Drane: he ruled them "not bloody, but luminous" and set her feast on 30 April in 1630 [S2 p.343-344]. Wikipedia says 1628 [S8]: CONTESTED date. Benedict XIII gave the Dominican Office of the Stigmata, 3 April [S2 p.344].
- CHURCH. John XXIII invited all the faithful to celebrate the fifth centenary of the canonization (1961) [S3 §4].
- CHURCH. Pius IX "turbatis temporibus, sanctum Petri Sedem in Catharinae tutela esse voluit" [S3 §4]. Secondary: decree of 13 April 1866 named her co-patron of Rome [S8]. The task text said "Pius IX 1866" for the feast: I found no source for that. Treat 1866 as a Rome patronage, not a feast change.
- CHURCH. Feast. Bull set the first Sunday of May; Urban VIII moved it to 30 April because 29 April held St Peter Martyr [S2 p.343]. Secondary: restored to 29 April in the 1969 calendar reform, when Peter of Verona went to local calendars [S8; a search summary]. Primary calendar text NOT read.
- CHURCH. Patron of Italy, with Francis of Assisi, "Patroni Primari d'Italia", by Pius XII motu proprio, brief dated 18 June 1939, "primo del nostro pontificato"; feasts in double rite of first class [S6]. Brief calls her "fortissima e piissima vergine", "decoro e difesa della Patria e della Religione" [S6; Paul VI repeats "patriae decus, religionis tutamen", S3 §4].
- CHURCH. Co-patroness of Europe, with Bridget of Sweden and Teresa Benedicta of the Cross, John Paul II, 1 Oct 1999 ("Given in Rome, at Saint Peter's, on the first day of October in the year 1999") [S5]. Earlier co-patrons: Cyril and Methodius, 31 Dec 1980, with Benedict [S5 §2]. JPII calls her ring of espousal at age twenty and "interior cell" [S5 §6]. He quotes her death words from Raymond: "Hold firm to this, my beloved—that I have given my life for the holy Church" (Raymond, Life, III, ch. IV) [S5 §7].
- CHURCH. Doctor of the Church. Chain: request of the Dominican Master General Aniceto Fernandez and many cardinals and bishops; Congregation for Causes of Saints plenary session 2 Dec 1969 on report of Cardinal Michael Browne; Paul VI approved 8 Jan 1970; proclaimed in St Peter's during Mass; letter dated 4 Oct 1970 [S3 §5-6, end]. Latin words of proclamation: "Certa scientia ac matura deliberatione deque apostolicae potestatis plenitudine Sanctam Catharinam, Virginem Senensem, Ecclesiae universalis Doctorem declaramus" [S3]. Earlier: 20 Dec 1966 session asked if women could be Doctors; Paul VI confirmed 21 Mar 1968 [S3 §5]. Paul VI says no woman held the title while they worked on it [S3 §5 "quo titulo ... decorata erat nulla"].
- CONFLICT: Vatican homily page header says "Domenica, 3 ottobre 1970" [S4]; the apostolic letter and its filename say 4 October 1970, which was a Sunday [S3]. Use 4 October.
- Paul VI on her graces: "mistico sposalizio e le sacre stigmate" named as exceptional graces, he does not dwell on them; he says the Doctor title rests on holiness (Pius II) and "peculiare eccellenza della dottrina" and "sapienza infusa" [S4].
- Paul VI on stigmata in S3 §2: 1 April 1375, while she preached for the crusade: "vulnera Christi, seu, ut dicunt, stigmata impressa sunt, quae videlicet, si beato Raimundo Capuano fides tribuitur ... 'in forma purae lucis pervenerunt ad manus, pedes, et cor eius'". The hedge "si ... fides tribuitur" ("if Raymond is believed") is worth noting.
- Paul VI on 1380 death prayer (Letter 371): "O Dio eterno, ricevi il sacrificio della vita mia in ... questo corpo mistico della santa Chiesa ..." [S4].
- Process: the "Processus Castellanus" (Process of Venice) began 1411 under Francesco Bembo, bishop of Venice (Castello). It examined friars charged with giving public honors to Catherine before canonization; they were cleared. The depositions were later used for canonization [S1 p.xii-xiii; Drane writes 1411]. Published Martene-Durand, Ampl. Coll. vi (7 witnesses only; the Siena MS has 24). Modern edition: Laurent 1942 (copyrighted; facts only).

## Timeline of miracle reports (Raymond/Process unless stated)
- c.1365-70 and after: abstinence. Raymond writes: "tempore quo ejus conversationis merui esse testis, nullo cibo aut potu nutriente vivebat" ("in the time I was witness of her life she lived with no food or drink as nourishment") [S7 §59]. He says physicians who were brought to her admitted it was beyond nature [S7 §58]. Critics said "non jejunabat, sed latenter optime comedebat" ("she did not fast but ate very well in secret") [S7 §172]. Raymond adds that he does not want readers to copy her [S7 §60]. Status: RAYMOND/PROCESS; CONTESTED by contemporaries.
- Lent-Ascension 1373: 55 days (Passion Sunday to Ascension) with only Holy Communion; Caffarini adds an angel bringing the Host in a veil on the Rogation days; Bartholomew's account of the eve of Ascension [S1 p.202-203]. Status: RAYMOND/PROCESS; angel detail LEGEND-level.
- Sept 1372 (Drane): Christ's absolution over her head; eight months of increasing difficulty eating [S1 p.201-202].
- 17-22 July 1370 (Drane's reconstruction): Communion on the day after St Alexis; Christ opens her side and takes her heart; Raymond: she told him she felt she had no heart; he mocked; she said she lived without it [S7 §179]. Two days later in the Dominican chapel (delle Volte) light, Christ with a red luminous heart: "Ecce, carissima filia, sicut pridie tibi abstuli cor tuum, sic in praesentiarum trado tibi cor meum, quo semper vivas" ("Behold, dearest daughter, as I took your heart the other day, so now I give you mine, that you may always live") [S7 §180]. Raymond: a scar remained at the place; her companions told him and many others they saw it; she confirmed it when he asked [S7 §180] NEW for English pages. After that she could no longer say "Lord, I commend my heart to you", and said "I give you your heart" [S1 p.109, S7 §180]. Thomas della Fonte dates it to St Margaret's day, 20 July 1370 [S1 p.109]. Status: RAYMOND/PROCESS; named in Pius II's poem [S2 p.343]; not defined by the Church.
- Right-hand nail (earlier than Pisa): Christ offers a nail, presses it into her right palm so it felt pierced; "ego jam habeo stigmata ejus in manu dextera; quod licet adsit invisibile, mihi est tamen etiam sensibile et continue afflictivum" ("I already have His stigmata in my right hand: invisible, but felt and constantly painful") [S7 §193]. NEW for English pages. RAYMOND.
- 1 April 1375 (4th Sunday of Lent), church of Santa Cristina, Pisa: after Raymond's Mass she knelt, rose, stretched out arms, face glowing, fell as if struck. She told him: "Noveritis Pater, quod stigmata Domini Jesu misericordia sua ego jam in corpore meo porto" [S7 §195]. Five bloody rays from the crucifix's wounds to hands, feet, heart; she asked that no mark show; the rays turned to light. Left side above heart, not right [S1 p.307-308; S7 §195]. A week later she felt strengthened; Raymond says the pain stopped after prayer [S1 p.309-310]. Visible marks: none in life; Prior Antonio of Siena claimed he saw five marks at her wake (letter to Raymond, via Lombardelli, via Drane) [S1 p.311]. Foot at SS Giovanni e Paolo, Venice and hand at S. Domenico e Sisto, Rome claimed to show stigma; Drane says a photograph shows a spot in the palm where light shines through [S1 p.311]. CHURCH: Urban VIII approved [S3]. CONTESTED: Franciscan-Dominican dispute (Drane mentions the controversy [S2 p.343]); date 1375 vs inscription confusion (Santa Cristina inscription gives a wrong Avignon year) [S1 p.310 n.]. Crucifix (Giunta Pisano) moved to Siena 1563, at night under armed guard [S1 p.310].
- 1374, plague in Siena. Matteo di Cenni, rector of the Misericordia hospital, sick with plague overnight; physician: "evident tokens of pestilential ague"; Catherine shouts from afar "Surgatis, Domine Matthaee, surgatis; quia non est tempus in lecto molliter quiescendi" ("Rise, Master Matthew, rise; it is no time to rest softly in bed"); fever and groin swelling leave at once [S7 Pars II §24-25; English S1 p.225-227]. They ate raw onions that morning. Raymond arrives after and she denies power: "Do you take me to be a God?" [S1 p.227]. Date: Leggenda Minore says 1373, Miracoli says 1374 (Sienese year starts March) [S1 p.226 n.]. Status: RAYMOND (eyewitness: he came to the door). Matteo was named rector 1 Aug 1373 [S1 p.225 n.]. Left in charge of the disciples with the "Bachelor" after her death (Nigi's letter) [S2 p.282].
- Same plague: Fra Santi (hermit) taken to the Misericordia and nursed by Catherine [S1 p.227-228]. Raymond himself and Catherine visited plague wards daily [S1 p.225]. Plague of 1374 context: she and companions nursed the sick; Raymond's own plague survival is in Legend (not read).
- Many other plague healings: only the two above read. Rest NOT read.
- 29 April 1380, Rome: Raymond at Genoa hears an interior voice while saying an Ave at the Virgin's image: "Fear not, I am here for your sake; I am in heaven for you" [S2 p.278, Raymond]. Thomas Petra's vision of a white procession about 30 days later, in the Process (Bartholomew Dominic's second deposition) [S2 p.278-279]. RAYMOND/PROCESS.
- After death: crowds touch the body; many cures in the church at the Minerva before burial [S2 p.276]. Burial in cypress coffin in the friars' cemetery, 2 May funeral paid by Urban VI; second funeral by the Senator John Cenci [S2 p.276]. Stephen Maconi took a tooth; another to Alexia, another to Neri di Landoccio [S2 p.277].
- Tooth miracle: Raymond (Pars III §392): young Nicolo "Cola", stepson of Cincio Tancancini of Rome, dying of quinsy; Alexia puts Catherine's tooth to his throat; abscess bursts; Raymond saw the young man; physician Master Jacopo di Santa Maria Rotonda told Raymond [S7 §392]. Drane says Cincio himself stood and attested; Raymond's text says the sick person was the stepson: CONFLICT, trust Raymond [S2 p.277; S7]. RAYMOND.
- 1381 (October, per Drane): Raymond opens the tomb; moves body to stone sarcophagus; Catherine had foretold this translation to him at Voragine on St Francis's eve [S2 p.282-283]. He separates the head and sends it secretly to Siena (Thomas della Fonte and Ambrose) with Urban VI's permission [S2 p.283, OCR bad]. NEW: Raymond fails to honor the head openly for years; later the public reception, first Thursday after preaching 23 April to 1 May, head received on Thursday 5 May (year c.1385-88, not fixed) [S2 p.284-286].
- Bread multiplied at San Domenico, Siena, on the day of the head's reception: bread for 5 fed 50 (Raymond, Pars II ch.11) [S2 p.289-290]. RAYMOND (he was present).
- 1468: head into silver bust. 3 Dec 1531: fire at San Domenico; Florentine lay brother Anselm saved the head, damaged [S2 p.290-291]. 19th c. crystal reliquary; thumb of right hand put in front of the silver bust in the annual procession [S2 p.291].
- 1430: Antoninus, prior of the Minerva, put the body in carved sarcophagus with wooden effigy (inscription "SANCTA CATERINA VIRGO DE SENIS ORDINIS S. DOMINICI DE PAENITENTIA") [S2 p.291-292]. 1487: right hand to S. Domenico e Sisto, a foot to SS Giovanni e Paolo in Venice [S2 p.292]. 1575 relic to Santa Caterina, Rome. 17 April 1855 Jandel opened the sarcophagus; bone and skin "like grey ashes" with gold thread sent to Stone and Bow; church reopened 3 Aug 1855; Pius IX consecrated altar 4 Aug 1855; tomb under the high altar; Translation feast set [S2 p.292-293]. Other relics: habit part at San Nicolo in Carcere, discipline and chain at Santa Cecilia, white shoe at Santa Maddalena (Gigli) [S2 p.293].
- Incorrupt head: Drane says only that an 19th-century scientific report (Tommaseo vol 1 App. XI) found the face "vigour and agreeable candour" despite time [S2 p.291 n.]. "Incorrupt" as a Church claim: NOT found. Mark popular/LEGEND. The head in the glass case is mummified; no source read for state.
- Pius II's poem names her espousal, exchange of heart, mystic death and return to life, stigmata [S2 p.343]. Mystic death (1370, four hours): NOT read in Raymond this session.

## Patronage statuses
- Italy (primary, 1939) CHURCH [S6]. Europe (co-patroness, 1999) CHURCH [S5]. Rome (secondary patron, 1866) secondary source [S8]. Doctor 1970 CHURCH [S3].
- Nurses, fire prevention, miscarriages, people ridiculed for piety, sick people: found only on Wikipedia list [S8] with no explanation; popular, no Church document found. Nurses link: her work in the Misericordia and with plague patients [S1 p.225-228, S7].

## Character notes useful here
- She denies miracle power ("Do you take me to be a God?") then smiles and promises [S1 p.227].
- After the cask-of-wine miracle she begs God to make the wine turn to dregs so people will stop talking; the wine turns to sediment (Legend Pt 2 ch ii) [S1 p.306]. Status RAYMOND.
- Doubts of her own confessor, Raymond: he mocked "I have no heart" [S7 §179].

## Scenes (max 10)
1. Pisa, 1 April 1375: Mass at Santa Cristina, Raymond sees her rise, arms out, then fall "as though she had received a deadly wound"; she calls him quietly and says she has the stigmata [S1 p.307-308].
2. Siena plague: she cries from the street "Surgatis, Domine Matthaee" while Raymond is at the doctor's; they meet at the gate; the table is laid with raw onions [S1 p.226-227; S7].
3. Chapel delle Volte, July 1370: she alone in the chapel, light, Christ with a red heart; a scar later seen by companions [S7 §180].
4. Raymond at the mocking talk: "I have no heart"; she insists she lives without it [S7 §179].
5. The Minerva before burial: Cardinals and plebs touch the body, tear bits of garment; Stephen Maconi lays her in cypress, kisses her [S2 p.276].
6. Genoa, 29 April 1380: Raymond packs his bundle, says an Ave, hears the voice [S2 p.277-278].
7. Alexia holds Catherine's tooth to the throat of the dying boy in Rome; the abscess bursts "as if a stone had fallen" [S7 §392].
8. Raymond secretly cuts off the head, two friars carry it in gilded copper to Siena; it sits in a sacristy chest for years [S2 p.283; OCR damaged].
9. 5 May procession at Siena, children in white with roses, all bells ringing; the bread multiplies at the friars' table that night [S2 p.285-290].
10. Fire at San Domenico, 3 Dec 1531: Brother Anselm in wet clothes rushes in and saves the head [S2 p.290-291].
