## Identity
- Caterina di Jacopo di Benincasa; Siena, Fontebranda district; father Jacomo di Benincasa, dyer; mother Lapa di Puccio di Piagente [S1 p.4-6].
- Family: five brothers named (Benincasa, Bartolommeo, Sandro, Niccolo, Stefano) and five older sisters (Niccoluccia, Maddalena, Bonaventura, Lisa, Nera) [S1 p.6-7].
- Twin sister Giovanna born with Catherine, died soon; a later Giovanna ("Nanna") born after, buried 18 April 1363 in San Domenico's Libro de' Morti [S1 p.7].
- Number of children: Raymond says Lapa had 25 children, Catherine NOT last; the Legenda minore makes Catherine youngest [S1 p.7 n.2, citing Legenda I.ii.1 §26]. So "23rd/24th child" is NOT in S1; check.

## Timeline (so far)
- Birth, Siena, Fontebranda; plague year 1347/1348 background [S1 p.4]. Date to check.
- Age 5: kneels to greet Mary on every stair of the house [S1 p.8, Legenda I.ii.2 §27].
- Age 6 (about 1353): walking down Vallepiatta with brother Stefano from sister Bonaventura's house, she sees Christ in papal robes and tiara enthroned over San Domenico with Peter, Paul, John; he blesses her; Stefano pulls her hand to bring her back [S1 p.8]. NEW detail: Stefano.
- Age 7 by her own later account: vows virginity to Christ [S1 p.9]. Gardner doubts the age.
- After age 12: sister Bonaventura persuades her at Lapa's urging to dye hair and dress up; she later grieves over it as a sin [S1 p.9]. Bonaventura dies August 1362; Catherine returns to austerity [S1 p.9].
- Family wants a marriage; Fra Tommaso della Fonte (a relative, her first confessor) tells her to cut her hair; family takes her room, makes her do the servant's work [S1 p.9-10].
- The "inner cell": she builds in her mind a cell she never leaves; imagines father as Christ, mother as Mary, brothers as apostles [S1 p.10, Legenda I.iv.5-6 §49-50].
- Dream of St Dominic offering the tertiary habit with a lily [S1 p.10-11].
- Father Jacomo finds her praying in Stefano's room with a white dove over her head; he frees her to serve God [S1 p.11]. 
- Takes the Mantellate habit (Sisters of Penance of St Dominic); the sisters at first refuse a maiden (all widows) until she falls ill and they see she is disfigured by illness; about early 1363 [S1 p.11-12].
- Vignone baths with Lapa: she scalds herself in the boiling water meditating on hell [S1 p.13].
- Three years of silence in the cell, speaking only to confessor; Mass only outing [S1 p.15]. Learns to read miraculously (Raymond) [S1 p.22].
- End of the three years: demon assault; Christ on the cross: "I was in thy heart" [S1 p.20-21, Legenda].
- Spiritual espousal on last day of carnival, with ring of gold, four pearls and a diamond, visible only to her; Gardner dates probably 1366 [S1 p.24-26, Legenda I.xii.1-2 §115]. NEW to some English pages: Mary takes her hand, David plays psaltery.
- Persecution by Mantellate sisters and friars: thrown out of church in ecstasy; a friar kicks her; another pricks her with a needle (feels pain after) [S1 p.50-51, Legenda III.vi §406-407, Processus Simone da Cortona].
- Early disciples: Alessa Saracini, Cecca Gori, her sister-in-law Lisa; Tommaso Caffarini (then ~17) and Bartolommeo di Domenico [S1 p.52-53].
- Summer to autumn 1370: heart exchange; invisible nail in palm; mystical "death" for about four hours on a Sunday; told to go out into the world [S1 p.82-83, Legenda II.vi §178-216]. Bartolommeo di Domenico: he was preaching in San Domenico when the rumour came; the cell was full; she revived before him [S1 p.83-84, Processus coll. 1332-1333].

## Quotations (so far; all English, from S1; originals not yet checked)
- Christ to her: "Thou art she who art not, and I am He who am." [S1 p.16, Legenda]. Italian original to find.
- "I was in thy heart." [S1 p.21].
- On hell: she would stand over its mouth to close it (S1 p.15 paraphrase of Legenda Prol. §15).
- When told men call her hypocrite: "They speak sooth, for, if the world knew me, it would stone me." [S1 p.51, Tantucci / Processus Barontus].

## APPEND 1 (batch 2: Gardner S1 and Fawtier S4)

### Source corrections
- S3 is by Augusta Theodosia Drane (not "McKenny"). Not yet read in detail.
- S4 Fawtier 1921 (French, public-domain) lists the whole dossier [S4 p.1-2]: minor Lives (Antoninus, Antonio della Rocca, Jerome of Prague, Massimino of Salerno, Caffarini); the Venice/Castellan Process; Caffarini's *Supplementum*; texts by William Flete; death texts (Transitus, Alcuni Punti, letters of Tommaso Petra and of Barduccio Canigiani); the anonymous Florentine *Miracoli* (Italian, 1374); fragments of the Latin *Miracula* of Tommaso della Fonte and Bartolomeo Dominici; Stefano Maconi's *Epitaphium*; Raymond's *Legenda Maior* [S4 p.1-2].

### Identity corrections and conflicts
- Birth: 25 March 1347, Annunciation, New Year by Sienese reckoning [S1 p.4]. This is Gardner's deduction, not a date in Raymond. Fawtier: Raymond never gives the birth date, only death at 33 in 1380; "33" is Christ's age and so suspect; the anonymous *Miracoli* give two contradictory chronologies (born 1347 by 'age 27 in 1374'; but about 1355 by other synchronisms); the Benincasa brothers' petition to Florence says they traded there for 28 years before 1370 [S4 p.136-139]. Treat 1347 as traditional, not proven. NEW.
- Raymond: Lapa bore 25 children; Catherine's twin Giovanna died; a later Giovanna was the last [S1 p.7 n.2, Legenda I.ii.1 §26]. Legenda minore calls Catherine the youngest. "23rd or 24th" is not found in S1 or S4; do not state it as fact. [Check Curtayne (S2 p.339 says 25 children, "hardly half" lived).]

### Timeline additions
- 1361-62: Bonaventura dies August 1362 [S1 p.9].
- Mantellate admission story (widows only; refused her as "too beautiful") is in Raymond (Leg. maj. §71-73); Fawtier: the Tertiary rule has no such rule (no beauty or widow condition; married women and girls on the lists), so the story is doubtful as to detail [S4 p.139]. Mark as legend-shaped detail. NEW.
- Persecuted in church: see above (Legenda III.vi.12-13 §406-407).
- Ecstasy-prick episodes: friar pricks her with a needle (Legenda III.vi). At Avignon, 1376, Dame Elys de Turenne, the Pope's nephew's wife, stabs her feet with a needle during ecstasy; Catherine felt the pain only afterward [S1 p.184; source of the Avignon story not yet seen]. NEW detail.
- 1370 autumn: Bartolommeo di Domenico heard a rumour of her death while preaching in San Domenico [S1 p.83-84, Processus coll. 1332-1333].
- Early conversions (1370-71): Andrea di Naddino Bellanti (blasphemer), Francesco Saracini (80, noble), Jacomo Tolomei (twice a homicide, "changed from wolf to lamb, from lion to watch-dog") [S1 p.84].
- 1374 May: she is summoned before the Dominican general chapter in Florence, with three women "who stay at her guard"; the anonymous Florentine hears of her and writes the *Miracoli* between her visit and her departure 29 June 1374 (finished 10 Oct 1374) [S4 p.92]. NEW (English pages seldom say that she was examined by the chapter in Florence, or that this source is a Florentine layman).
- 1374 Siena plague: she buries eight nephews, full of "holy joy" [S4 p.94-95 summary of Miracoli §25]; on 15 August 1374 she nearly dies herself [Miracoli §29]; 8 September 1374 eats a few fruits [Miracoli §30]. NEW.
- Miracoli §32: after drinking the pus of a nun's cancer, she is espoused that night with a ring (the Miracoli place a ring, no pearls given in S4 summary). Compare Raymond's espousal at carnival. Conflict of versions [S4 p.95]. NEW.
- 1375 Lent: the Pisan stigmata, Santa Cristina, fourth Sunday of Lent (Laetare) 1375, after Communion. Raymond and others saw her rise to her knees, arms stretched, then fall "as though mortally wounded". She says (Legenda): she saw the crucified Lord descend in light, five blood-red rays came to hands, feet and heart; she begged "let not the marks appear outwardly"; the rays turned to light. Raymond asks the next Sunday; she says pain now strengthens her [S1 p.133-135]. Fawtier: Raymond records a first stigma in the right hand earlier (§192), taken from Tommaso della Fonte's Miracula, but the Miracoli do not know it [S4 p.?? note 4 in ch. on birth]. The Pisan crucifix is shown in the Siena house/Sanctuary; Curtayne: a "strangely unmoved figure, inspiring fear" [S2 p.~7800 in text; modern].
- 1375: Gorgona island, Carthusians: she sees the sea for the first time; prior Bartolommeo Serafini; her mantle touches a suicidal young monk and frees him [S1 p.135].
- Giovanni Dominici (then a novice, later Cardinal of Ragusa) wrote of watching her speak to sinners at Pisa [S1 p.130].
- 1376 about April: Niccolo di Toldo of Perugia, young noble, sentenced to death in Siena for rash words against the State; he rages in prison, refuses confession; Catherine brings him peace; he dies with Christ's name and hers on his lips, she receiving his head in her hands. Witness: Fra Tommaso Caffarini, who was there (Processus col. 1266; Legenda minore pp.93-94; Raymond was absent and does not tell it). The letter to Raymond tells details [S1 p.210-211]. Gardner dates this (Siena, year 1376) "probably now". (To read the letter in Italian.)
- 1376, Avignon: she arrives 18 June 1376 [S1 p.180]. Two days later the Pope receives her. Florence's ambassadors disown her mediation. Raymond interprets for her. In one early interview she criticises the Curia's vices; the Pope listens in silence [S1 p.182, Legenda II.iv.7 §152, Processus col.1325]. Gregory asks the will of God about returning to Rome; she says "Who knoweth this better than your Holiness, who vowed to God that you would do this thing?"; he is amazed because no one else knew of his secret vow (made at the conclave) [S1 p.182-183]. NEW as to detail.
- At Avignon three prelates try to trap her with questions to charge her with heresy; the Pope's physician Francesco Casini tells Maconi "if they had not found this virgin Catherine had a solid foundation, she would never have made a more unfortunate voyage" [S1 p.183; Epistola Domni Stephani §22-24].
- Return: Catherine leaves Avignon, passes Marseilles, reaches Genoa early October 1376; Gregory sails from Marseilles/Genoa 29 October 1376 [S1 p.~7834-7911 lines; check pages].

### Death (Rome, 1380) [S1 p.348-352; Letter of Barduccio Canigiani to Suor Caterina Petribuoni; Legenda III.iv §360-365; Epistola Domni Stephani §7]
- 29 April 1380, Sunday before the Ascension, about midday ("the sixth hour"). Before dawn the spiritual family gathered; Giovanni Tantucci gave absolution a culpa et a poena; Extreme Unction from the Abbot of Sant'Antimo.
- Two hours of agony as if attacked by demons; she says "Peccavi, Domine, miserere mei", "Credo, credo"; once answers an accusation: "Never vainglory, but always the true glory and praise of Jesus Christ crucified" (Gardner's English of Barduccio).
- Then transfigured; sits up leaning on Monna Alessa; makes a general confession (accuses herself of negligence about souls and the Church); tells each child their task. To Stefano Maconi: obey and go to the Carthusians (he did) [S1 p.350].
- Her mother Lapa is present and asks her blessing; Barduccio: "verily, I tell you it was a sweet sorrow." Prayed for Urban VI as "true Sovereign Pontiff"; last words: "Father, into Thy hands I commend my soul and my spirit"; "Sangue, Sangue" cried many times [S1 p.351].
- Body carried by Stefano to Santa Maria sopra Minerva; exposed until evening of Tuesday 1 May; Tantucci tries to preach but the crowd drowns him; Urban VI and the Roman Senator Giovanni Cenci hold funeral rites [S1 p.352].
- Bartolommeo di Domenico had left Rome days earlier after asking for a sign; she was well the next day, but relapsed the day of his leaving (Processus coll.1358-1361) [S1 p.348-349]. 
- Relics: curtayne says her head is shown in San Domenico, Siena (silver reliquary), Raymond began his Legenda 1385 at the time the head was moved to Siena [S4 p.120-121, Leg. maj. §305].

### Sources on sources
- Legenda Maior: begun 1385 (when her head was taken to Siena, Raymond in Siena for the baths, at the request of her sons and daughters), second part under way 1390, finished June 1391 (letter), "published" in Venice about 1395 [S4 p.120, Leg. maj. §245, §305; Caffarini's Supplementum p.170]. Aim: canonization.
- Fawtier: the Legenda Maior and the *Miracoli* are independent of each other [S4 p.96]. The Miracoli contain accounts (Montepulciano: she bends to kiss St Agnes's foot and the foot rises to her mouth; Christ as a beggar takes her tunic; the inexhaustible wine cask) [S4 p.94].
- Venice/Castellan Process: on 3 May 1411, in SS. Giovanni e Paolo, the Dominican inquisitor Bartolomeo of Ferrara preached "Ergo evacuatum est scandalum crucis" mainly in her praise; the audience murmured; Caffarini preached next at SS. Apostoli on "Mihi autem absit gloriari nisi in cruce" (the motto of Francis); a complaint went to Bishop Francesco Bembo; on 26 May 1411 he cited both and an inquiry on her holiness began; depositions from 16 April 1412 to 20 July 1416 [S4 p.~chapter III, lines 1205-1235]. NEW. Fawtier's text gives another date: one deposition 30 Sept 1411 (conflict inside S4; check).
- Stigmata dispute: Caffarini in the Supplementum has a long treatise on her stigmata, ending with a Mass of the Five Stigmata and four sermons; argument: no one doubts the stigmata of the Dominicans Blessed Walter (Gautier) of Strasbourg and Blessed Helen of Hungary, who were not seen either; and he sets three Dominican stigmatics against the one Franciscan [S4 p.62-63 and ch. on Supplementum]. Caffarini's tone aims at the Franciscans [S4 p.?]. Fawtier: the Death texts (Barduccio) never mention stigmata or the mystic marriage [S4 lines ~4129]. The Sixtus IV decree (1472) is NOT yet confirmed in a source I read: "Other: to check".

### Late or legendary (marks)
- Mantellate admission "too beautiful, not a widow": Raymond only; contradicted by the rule (Fawtier).
- The pus drinking, the heart exchange, the spiritual espousal ring: Raymond/Caffarini; no direct trace in her own letters I read; popular and devotional. The espousal differs between Raymond (carnival, Mary, David, ring with four pearls and a diamond, invisible) and the Miracoli (ring on the night after the pus episode) [S1 p.25-26; S4 p.95].

## Dialogue and Schism (S1)
- Dialogue (Libro della Divina Dottrina): dictated at Siena, late summer to early autumn 1378 (Gardner), while in ecstasy before secretaries (Raymond, Legenda III.i.2 §352); in her last letter she calls it "il libro nel quale io trovava alcuna recreazione" [S1 p.353-354]. Treatises: introduction (ch.1-8), Discretion (9-64), Prayer (65-86), Tears (87-134), Divine Providence (135-153), Obedience (154-167) [S1 p.354 n.2].
- Gregory XI enters Rome 17 January 1377 [S1 line 8056]; leaves for Rome after the October 1376 sailing from Genoa [S1].
- 1377 spring-summer: peace missions among the Salimbeni at Rocca d'Orcia; band of disciples; 1377 Gaddo Accorimboni podesta of Gubbio, her letter to his prisoners Thursday of Holy Week [S1 p.210-212].
- Gregory dies 27 March 1378 [S1 line 10022]. Conclave April 1378: crowd shouts "A Roman, a Roman"; the aged Cardinal Tebaldeschi is dressed in papal robes and enthroned by mistake, raves of antipopes while Romans kiss his feet; the Archbishop of Bari (Urban VI) is the real choice [S1 p.~line 10470-10540]. NEW and vivid.
- Cardinals meet at Fondi, elect Clement VII 20 September 1378 [S1 line 10989]. Catherine writes to the Count of Fondi before the election [S1 line 11138].
- Ciompi revolt of Florence, 20-22 July 1378; she stays in the city until the treaty is signed; leaves about 2 August [S1 p.247-250]. Catherine is said to have been in great personal danger from the mob (Gardner; Raymond Leg. III.vi.35 §429).
- Called to Rome by Urban VI (autumn 1378) [S1 ch. XII-XIV; not read in detail].

## Character (witness testimony)
- Gardner: people called her "Eufrosina" for the joy she gave [S1 p.8; S6 p.10 "Eufrosina"].
- Bartolommeo di Domenico: in her presence "all carnal passion died away"; "always joyful and merry of countenance" [S1 p.53, Processus col. 1312].
- She laughs at moments of triumph: "Love, Love, I have conquered Thee with Thyself" [S1 p.15].
- Faults named by witnesses: severe fasting and her confessor ordering her to eat; she "chewed and spat out" (Gardner: masticated without swallowing); she wept over having dressed up at 12 [S1 p.9, 13].
- Raymond's own admission that she "lived without any nourishment" to him is a claim; critics at the time, including "men of repute in the spiritual life", cried out against it [S1 p.12].
- Critics: Mantellate and friars slandered her; Pisan scholars Giovanni Gutalebraccia and Pietro Albizzi came to test her [S1 p.50-51, p.130].

## Miracles (reported; status: recorded by hagiographers only, none approved by name in my sources)
