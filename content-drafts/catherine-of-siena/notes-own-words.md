# Catherine of Siena: notes, focus own-words

Letter numbers are TOMMASEO numbers (Tommaseo 1860), read from the printed headings in the OCR. Scudder (1905) prints no numbers, only addressees. Dialogue: Thorold's chapter numbers (to CXLVIII) differ from the Italian (to CLXVII) after about ch. CXXXV; both are given.

## Quotations (all copied from raw text read this session)
Rights for all: Italian and Thorold/Scudder English public-domain. "Own English" lines are our own rendering.

Q1. To Stefano Maconi, Tommaseo CCCLXVIII, 1378 (about Urban VI and the Company of the Virgin Mary in Siena). THE REAL "SET FIRE" LINE. S4 vol IV p.461.
  IT: "Se sarete quello che dovete essere, metterete fuoco in tutta Italia, non tanto costì."
  EN S1 (Scudder's letter to Stefano Maconi): "If you are what you ought to be, you will set fire to all Italy, and not only yonder."
  Own English: "If you are what you should be, you will set fire to all Italy, not only there." (costì = there, in Siena.)
Q2. To Gregory XI, CCVI (1376, urging his return to Rome from Avignon). S4 vol III p.~161.
  IT: "Siatemi uomo virile, e non timoroso."  EN S1 p.~133: "Be manly in my sight, and not timorous."
  IT: "Venite, venite, venite, e non aspettate il tempo, chè il tempo non aspetta voi."  EN S1: "Come, come, come, and do not wait for time, since time does not wait for you."
  IT: "Allora farete come lo svenato Agnello ... che con la mano disarmata uccise li nemici nostri" EN S1: the Lamb Slain "who without weapons in His hand slew our foes, coming in gentleness".
Q3. To Gregory XI, CCXVIII (the Tommaseo contents list gives "A Gregorio XI"). S4 vol III p.228.
  IT: "Parrai che stiate, benigno padre mio, siccome sta l'agnello nel mezzo de' lupi. Ma confortatevi, e non temete" ... "Su, padre, mandate in effetto il proponimento che avete fatto, dell'avvenimento vostro" EN S1 p.166: "Up, father, like a man! For I tell you that you have no need to fear." (Scudder prints alternate readings in brackets here; use the Italian and our own English.)
Q4. To Gregory XI, CLXXXV (about 1375-76). S4 vol III. 
  IT: "Oimè, oimè, dolcissimo Babbo mio! questa è la cagione, che li sudditi sono tutti corrotti di immondizia e di iniquità."  EN S1 p.119: "Oh me, oh me, sweetest 'Babbo' mine! This is the reason that all the subjects are corrupted by impurity and iniquity."
  IT: "Se la piaga, quando bisogna, non s'incende col fuoco, e non si taglia col ferro, ma ponesi solo l'unguento ... imputridisce tutto" EN S1: "If a wound when necessary is not cauterized or cut out with steel, but simply covered with ointment, not only does it fail to heal, but it infects everything."
  Scudder p.~125 notes her pet name "Babbo" (Daddy) for the pope.
Q5. To Urban VI, CCXCI (1378, first letter to the new pope; Tommaseo summary: "Raccomanda carità verso i sudditi"). S4 vol IV p.67.
  IT: "Oimè, babbo mio dolce, poneteci rimedio; e date refrigerio alli spasimati desiderii delli servi di Dio, che di dolore muoiono, e non possono morire"  EN S1 p.245: "Oh me, sweet my Babbo, bring us a remedy! And give refreshment to the desperate desires of the servants of God, who die and cannot die."
  Context (S1 p.241): the Prior of Gorgona wrote her "that our new Christ on earth is a terrible man" (not her words).
Q6. To Raymond of Capua, CCLXXIII (the execution of Niccolò Tuldo of Perugia). S4 vol IV p.~7-9; S1 p.112-113. Tommaseo note: the year is not known; Tuldo was condemned for speaking against the Sienese government.
  IT: "Confortati, fratello mio dolce; perocché tosto giungeremo alle nozze. Tu v'anderai bagnato nel sangue dolce del Figliuolo di Dio, col dolce nome di Gesù ... E io t'aspetto al luogo della giustizia."
  EN S1 p.112: "Comfort thee, sweet my brother; since we shall soon arrive at the Wedding Feast. Thou shalt go there bathed in the sweet Blood of the Son of God, with the sweet Name of Jesus, which I will never to leave thy memory. And I await thee at the place of justice."
  IT: "Aspettailo dunque al luogo della giustizia ... io mi posi giù, e distesi il collo in sul ceppo" EN S1 p.113: "I waited for him then at the place of justice ... I prostrated me, and stretched my neck upon the block". Then he came "like a gentle lamb" and said only "Jesus!" and "Catherine!"; "I received his head in my hands."
  Opening of the same letter (S1 p.110): "Up, up, sweetest my father! and let us sleep no more! ... I have just received a Head in my hands". Scudder calls it "one of the famous letters of the world".
Q7. To Monna Lapa, her mother, CCXL (from Avignon, 1376, before she returned). S4 vol III. 
  IT: "Con desiderio ho desiderato di vedervi madre vera non solamente del corpo, ma dell'anima mia"  EN S1 p.186: "With desire have I desired to see you a true mother, not only of my body but of my soul". She points her to Mary, who let the disciples go: "I want you to learn from her, dearest mother."
Q8. To Queen Giovanna of Naples, CCCXVII (1378). S4 vol IV p.215.
  IT: "Annegatevi un poco nel sangue di Cristo crocifisso. Quivi si dissolva la nuvola dell'amor proprio, e 'l timore servile, e 'l veleno dell'odio e del proprio sdegno."  Own English: "Drown yourself a little in the blood of Christ crucified. There let the cloud of self-love dissolve, and servile fear, and the poison of hatred and of your own disdain." (Scudder prints a different, longer letter to Giovanna; no English match for CCCXVII was checked.)
  Her usual opening (CCCXII, to the Queen, 1378): "Carissima madre in Cristo dolce Gesù. Io Catarina, serva e schiava de' servi di Gesù Cristo, scrivo a voi nel prezioso sangue suo" = S1 "I Catherine, servant and slave of the servants of Jesus Christ, write to you in His precious Blood".
Q9. To the Eight of War of Florence, CCXXX (1376, Avignon). S4 vol III p.280-281; S1 p.173-174. Tommaseo summary: she was welcomed kindly at court; the Eight must not make her look a liar.
  IT: "Carissimi padri e fratelli in Cristo Gesù ... con desiderio di vedervi veri figliuoli, umili e obbedienti al padre vostro"  EN S1: "Dearest fathers and brothers in Christ Jesus ... with desire to see you true sons, humble and obedient to your father". She also scolds them for taxing the clergy.
Q10. Dialogue ch. I (Thorold I, It. I). S2 p.19; S3.
  IT: "abituata e abitata nella cella del cognoscimento di sé per meglio cognoscere la bontà di Dio in sé"  EN S2: "remaining in the cell of self-knowledge, in order to know better the goodness of God towards her."
Q11. Dialogue, the Bridge: Thorold XXI = It. XXI. S2; S3.
  IT: "v'ho dato il ponte del mio Figliuolo, acciò che passando el fiume non annegaste. El quale fiume è il mare tempestoso di questa tenebrosa vita."  EN S2 (ch. XXI opening): "I have made a Bridge of My Word, of My only-begotten Son"; own English of the Italian: "I gave you the bridge of my Son, so that crossing the river you would not drown; the river is the stormy sea of this dark life."
Q12. Dialogue, the soul's words: Thorold CXXXIV = It. CXXXIV. S2 p.312.
  IT: "io so' colei che non so'. ... Però che tu se' solo colui che se'"  EN S2: "I am she who is not". God's words (Thorold CXIX, S2): "I am He who is, and they are they who are not". Italian of the latter: S3 has "solo Io so' Colui che so'" (chapter not matched).
Q13. Dialogue, closing prayer (Thorold CXLVIII, S2 p.358-359; It. CLXVII, S3).
  IT: "Tu, Trinità eterna, se' uno mare profondo, che quanto più c'entro tanto più truovo, e quanto più truovo più cerco di te."  EN S2: "Thou, oh eternal Trinity, art a deep Sea, into which the deeper I enter the more I find, and the more I find the more I seek"
  IT: "O abisso, o Deità eterna, o mare profondo! E che più potevi dare a me che dare te medesimo?"  EN S2: "Oh! Abyss, oh! Eternal Godhead, oh! Sea Profound! what more couldst Thou give me than Thyself"
  IT: "Sì come desidera il cervio la fonte dell'acqua viva, così desidera l'anima mia d'escire della carcere del corpo tenebroso" EN S2: "As the hart desires the spring of living water, so my soul desires to leave the prison of this dark body".

## Misattributions
- "Be who God meant you to be and you will set the world on fire": NOT FOUND in S1, S2, S3, or Tommaseo vols III and IV (searched "set the world", "meant you to be", "ablaze", "dovete essere"). The real source of the idea is Q1 (CCCLXVIII, to Stefano Maconi): "set fire to all Italy", it does not mention God's meaning for you. Mark the popular wording as a loose paraphrase, not a quotation.
- Vols I and II of Tommaseo (letters I-CLXX) were not searched for this line, though the line in Q1 is the well-known source.

## Character (from her own letters)
- Calls the pope "Babbo" (Daddy) and still tells him to be a man: "Siatemi uomo virile" [S4 CCVI; S1].
- Signs most letters "serva e schiava de' servi di Gesù Cristo" [S4 CCCXII].
- Dictated; her letters often end "Gesù dolce, Gesù Amore" [S4 CCCXVII].
- Writes tenderly to an anxious mother, and teaches her through Mary [S4 CCXL].
- Her own voice at an execution is intimate, practical, ecstatic: she kneels at the block herself before he comes [S1 p.113].

## Scenes
- Tuldo's execution, Siena: she promises to be there at dawn, takes him to Mass and Communion, waits at the block, takes his head in her hands [S1 p.112-113; S4 CCLXXIII].
- Avignon 1376: she writes the pope "Come, come, come" and tells the Florentine Eight not to make her a liar at court [S4 CCVI, CCXXX].
- 1378: new pope Urban VI; the Prior of Gorgona calls him "a terrible man", and she writes him anyway [S1 p.241].
