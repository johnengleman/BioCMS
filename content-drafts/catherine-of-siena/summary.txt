On the morning a young man was to be beheaded in Siena, a dyer’s daughter went ahead of him to the block and stretched her own neck on it while she waited. When he came, he laughed to see her and said nothing but “Jesus” and “Catherine,” and she received his head into her hands. As a girl, Catherine of Siena had asked for nothing but a small room and silence. Then she was sent out, to plague wards, to the pope at Avignon, to a city in revolt, and to a Church split between two popes. Did she lose the silence, or carry it with her?
