# Catherine of Siena: plan

Tier A. The three notes files hold about 9,800 words, but they are dense with scenes from her own letters, Raymond, Caffarini, Barduccio, and the papal documents. Target about 8,000-8,500 words of prose, the low end of tier A. The Rome years 1378-80 are thin in the notes and stay short. No padding.

## The driving question

As a girl Catherine wanted only to be alone with God: when her family took away her room to break her will, she built a cell inside her mind and never left it, and for three years she spoke to no one but her confessor. What stood in the way: God himself sent her out of the cell, into her family's kitchen, plague wards, prisons, the papal court at Avignon, a city in revolt, and the Great Schism. The question: how could a woman who wanted the cell live in the world's noise without losing God? The resolution: the "cell of self-knowledge" she carried everywhere ("I am she who is not"; God "He who is"), and a love for God that became, in the same act, a hunger for souls and for the Church. On her deathbed she says she has given her life for the Church; her last prayer of the Dialogue calls God a sea in which the more she finds the more she seeks.

## Why famous; what set her apart

Famous: the dyer's daughter of Siena who pressed Gregory XI to leave Avignon, a Doctor of the Church (1970), patron of Italy (1939) and of Europe (1999).

Set apart: a laywoman with no office, who dictated her letters, told a pope "Be manly" and called him "Babbo", Daddy, in the same letter. She went where others would not, to the condemned man's block and the plague bed, and took the outcast's head in her own hands.

## The hook

Scene at the edge: Siena, the scaffold. Niccolò di Toldo, a young Perugian condemned for rash words, comes "like a gentle lamb", laughs to see her, says only "Jesus" and "Catherine", and she receives his head in her hands ("Io voglio"). She had lain her own neck on the block first. Then: this woman had once begged for nothing but a small room and silence. How did she get here? Answered in "A Cell No One Could Take" (the inner cell), "Thirsty for Souls" (sent out), and resolved in "Blood, Blood" and the ending.

## Chapters

1. **I Have Received a Head** (hook, thesis). Quote: Toldo letter, our English. Pull: it began in a dyer's house in Fontebranda.
2. **The Dyer's Daughter of Fontebranda.** Family, Eufrosina, the stairs, the vision over San Domenico with Stefano, the loaf and the cave, the vow. Pull: her family plans a marriage.
3. **A Cell No One Could Take.** Bonaventura and the dyed hair; hair cut; room taken; the inner cell; Dominic's lily; the dove; Jacomo frees her. Pull: the habit of the Mantellate.
4. **Three Years Without a Word.** Mantellate (Raymond's account, attributed); Vignone; silence; learning to read; "she who is not" (block quote, Gardner's English of Raymond); food; the assault and "I was in thy heart"; carnival ring. Pull: sent out.
5. **Thirsty for Souls.** Household service, persecution in church, "it would stone me", the band, conversions. Heart exchange (block quote, our Latin translation). Pull: summoned to Florence.
6. **Plague Summer in Siena.** 1374 chapter at Florence; plague; eight nephews; Matteo and the onions; "Do you take me to be a God?"; near death. Pull: Pisa.
7. **Five Rays at Santa Cristina.** Stigmata scene (block quote of the pain, English Legend via Drane); Gorgona and the sea. Pull: Avignon.
8. **Come, Come, Come.** Letters to Gregory (block quote); Avignon; Florence disowns her; the secret vow; tests; the needle; letter to Lapa (block quote). Pull: Gregory sails.
9. **I Have Just Received a Head** returns briefly, then Rocca d'Orcia and the Gubbio prisoners; Gregory dies; the conclave; Urban VI; Florence and the Ciompi (block quote, letter to the Signoria). Headline: **A Mob at the Door in Florence.** Pull: home to dictate a book.
10. **A Bridge Over the Stormy River.** The Dialogue (block quote, Thorold XXI). Pull: Fondi and the Schism.
11. **Fire in All Italy.** Schism, Rome, the Queen, the Maconi letter (block quote, Scudder). Pull: winter of 1380.
12. **Blood, Blood.** The death (Barduccio, Gardner's English). Pull: the Minerva and the head.
13. **A Head Carried Home to Siena.** Funeral, tooth, Raymond's voice, the head, bread, Venice 1411, Pius II. Pull: the titles.
14. **A Teacher Before a Pupil.** Urban VIII, Pius IX, Pius XII, Paul VI, John Paul II; end on the sea prayer (block quote, Thorold CXLVIII = It. CLXVII).
