# Teresa of Avila: notes, focus WITNESSES

Researcher: witnesses agent. Tier A. All facts below come from texts read in this session. Quotations are copied from the OCR text with line-end hyphens joined. Spanish OCR may hide small accent errors: check against the scan before print.

## 1. Identity (witnesses view only; the first agent owns the full block)
- Teresa de Cepeda y Ahumada; "Teresa de Jesus" in religion. Born Avila, 28 March 1515 [S1 p.93 text and Quadrado quote p.92]. Died Alba de Tormes, Thursday 4 Oct 1582 in the old calendar, about 9 pm; the next day was counted as 15 Oct [S1 p.336-337].
- Feast 15 Oct (Roman Calendar) [S4 p.308]. Canonized 1622 under Gregory XV (named in the Bull, quoted in S1 p.338).
- Tradition type: Catholic only (playbook latin-iberia.md).

## 3. Timeline (one line per event)
- 1515, 28 Mar: born in Avila, to Alonso Sanchez de Cepeda and Beatriz de Ahumada; Beatriz was his second wife; he had 3 children by his first, 9 by his second [S4 p.309; S1 p.93].
- Age 6-7: reads saints' lives with brother Rodrigo (about 4 years older, per S1 p.96; the Life footnote says the ages were 7 and 11 [S2 ch.1 note]). They repeat "para siempre, para siempre" about eternity. [S1 p.96-97]
- About age 7: the flight toward the Moors. She and Rodrigo leave by the Adaja gate, cross the bridge, and an uncle meets them. Rodrigo blames his sister. [S1 p.96; S2 ch.1 and note; S4 p.309] Late-legend check: Bollandists doubt she meant to leave Spain [S2 note]. NEW detail for English pages: Gregory XV had the episode put in the canonization process, and the Breviary hymn of 15 Oct mentions it [S1 p.96 footnote].
- Same childhood: the two build little hermitages in the garden, but the walls fall down [S1 p.97; S4 p.310]. She keeps a picture of Christ and the Samaritan woman with the words "Domine, da mihi aquam" [S1 p.97].
- About 1528 (she says 12; the will of Beatriz is dated 24 Nov 1528, so nearly 14): mother dies; Teresa asks Our Lady to be her mother [S2 ch.1 par.7 and note; S4 p.309 says mother died at 33]. Conflict on age: see section 8.
- About 14 to 16: romances of chivalry, and the "light" cousin who shares vanities [S2 ch.2; S4 p.310]. Half-sister Maria de Cepeda marries in 1531 (Teresa 16, not 14 as she says) [S2 ch.2 note]. She is sent to the Augustinian nuns of Santa Maria de Gracia and leaves after about 1.5 years (not read here; to check).
- 1535, 2 Nov: takes the habit at the Incarnation; entered "con una contradiccion tan grande"; she says every bone seemed to separate [S1 p.106 and her Vida ch.4 quoted in the footnote; date proved by three early documents, S1 near p.110 footnote]. Dating conflict: some biographers say 1533/34 [S2 introduction].
- Soon after profession: grave illness; her father brings her to Avila, doctors give her up; on the night of 15 Aug she falls into a four-day coma. Wax on her eyes, grave open at the Incarnation, her father says "Esta hija no es para enterrar." A candle nearly burns the bed; brother Lorenzo falls asleep while watching. [S1 p.110-111; S3 Ana de la Encarnacion, p.22-23]. Late/legend flag: Ribera himself doubts the claim that she saw her foundations in the coma and says it was probably a representation she did not believe [S1 p.111].
- Paralysis: she is carried back to the Incarnation at Easter; "Estuvo tullida ocho meses" and the long illness lasted three years [S1 p.111-112]. Witness: Ana de la Encarnacion says she moved only one finger [S3 p.23].
- Healing through St Joseph (S1 p.112 begins the passage; I read only the start).
- Before 1554 (date of the Christ of the Wounds conversion not in the texts I read): she sees a statue of Christ most grievously wounded in the oratory and falls down in tears [S2 ch.9; S1 p.121]. Then she reads Augustine's Confessions and weeps at his conversion in the garden [S1 p.121-122; S2 ch.9 par.8]. The date 1554 is NOT confirmed here: only the Jesuit college at Avila dates to spring 1554 [S1 footnote]. Check.
- Confessor Banez: he treats her severely; she asks him not to be so free of fear [S3 p.7; quote 2].
- 1559 (the most probable date, per S1 footnote): the transverberation; a small angel with a golden spear [S1 p.139-140; S2 ch.29 par.16-17]. The editor of S1 says she was 44, so 1559. NEW: Banez wrote in the margin of her manuscript that the figure was rather a seraph [S2 ch.29 footnote].
- 1562, 24 Aug (St Bartholomew's Day): San Jose of Avila founded; Maestro Daza places the Blessed Sacrament; four novices clothed; witnesses Gonzalo de Aranda, Francisco Salcedo, the priest Julian de Avila, Juan de Ovalle, Juana de Ahumada [S1 p.? text after line 11117; footnote]. Banez testifies that the whole city and the religious orders opposed the house, and that he alone defended it before the city council [S3 p.8].
- Founding opposition at Medina: a preacher compares her to Magdalena de la Cruz, the fraud of Cordoba; Pedro Fernandez OP threatens to walk out [S3 Banez p.10-11]. NEW.
- 1567: General Rubeo visits Avila and orders her to found "tantos monasterios cuantos pelos tenia en la cabeza" (as Banez heard from Rubeo himself) [S3 p.8].
- 1567, Medina: she meets Fray Juan de la Cruz, then a student at Salamanca, who wants to be a Carthusian; she asks him to wait [S1 p.222 ("Libro III")]. Teresa's own plan: "dos piedras vivas" for the Descalzo house (Antonio de Heredia and Juan).
- 1568: Don Rafael de Avila Megia offers a house at Duruelo, "un portal razonable, y una camara doblada, y una cocinilla" [S1 p.222-223].
- 1569: Pastrana founded; Princess of Eboli, after a dispute, denounces the Vida to the Inquisition (per S1 footnote, p.1015 text; testimony of Ana de Jesus, 1575 at Veas). Banez takes the manuscript to the Inquisition himself. It stays 12 years in Inquisition hands [S1 intro]. Cardinal Quiroga in March 1580 tells Gracian he has read it: "no hay en el cosa que no sea muy buena" [S1 intro, quoting Gracian].
- 1575-1578: Nuncio Felipe Sega hostile; calls her "femina inquieta y andariega" (Yepes via S1 p.304); Descalzo leaders jailed or exiled [S1 p.305].
- 1580s: foundations travel in carriages; Veas 1575, Granada plan, Burgos last (July 1582) [S1 pp.320-334].
- 1581 Dec: John of the Cross arrives at Veas with mules to take her to Granada; she sends two nuns instead because Burgos is fixed [S1 p.320]. The Archbishop of Granada is struck by lightning in his house and gives the permit (Yepes via S1 p.321 footnote); late story: the lightning is told as providential, treat as a report.
- 1582, Sept: leaves Burgos, goes to Palencia and Medina, wants to go home to Avila, but Fray Antonio de Jesus carries her to Alba at the Duchess's request; she obeys [S1 p.333]. On the road near Penaranda she faints; the only food is figs [S1 p.333-334].
- 1582, 20 Sept (eve of St Matthew): arrives at Alba. "Valame Dios, que cansada me siento; mas ha de veinte anos que nunca me acoste temprano, sino ahora." [S1 p.334; quote 6].
- 1582, 29 Sept: after Mass on St Michael's Day she takes to her bed; a flow of blood [S1 p.334]. Duchess María Enríquez feeds her with her own hand [S1 p.334 footnote, via Francisco de Santa Maria].
- 1582, 3 Oct, 5 pm: asks for the Sacrament; says to the nuns "perdonenmele" for her bad example; sits up when the host enters; repeats "En fin, Senor, soy hija de la Iglesia" [S1 p.335].
- 1582, 4 Oct, 7 am to 9 pm: lies on her side with a crucifix, face glowing; dies about 9 pm [S1 p.336; S3 witness quoted p.336 footnote].
- Calendar: the next day is counted 15 Oct because 10 days were removed by Gregory XIII [S1 p.336-337 and footnote]. Teresa's age: 67 years, 6 months, 7 days; 47 years in religion, 27 at the Incarnation, 20 in the primitive Rule [S1 p.337].
- Body found whole and fragrant after about three years; the process question says an oil or balm soaks cloths touching it [S3 p.1512 line; witness article]. Popular, not examined here.

## 4. Character
- Humor and grace: Banez heard that people visited her at the Incarnation for her "buena gracia y donaire"; she wept over that all her life [S3 p.7].
- Charm in sickness: Ana de la Encarnacion says that during the eight months she was paralyzed she gave joy to all who tended her [S3 p.23].
- Humility with praise: she says she is ashamed because God took many years with her and shorter time with her young nuns [S3 Ana de la Encarnacion, same page, quote 5].
- Care about being fooled: she preferred learned men who were not great mystics, because spiritual men are easier to deceive [S3 Banez p.7; paraphrase].
- Fault she names herself: sin of vanity, romance reading, and a long "mixing" of God and the world; the Vida is her own record [S2 ch.2, ch.5].
- Critics: the nuncio's "femina inquieta y andariega" [S1 p.304]; the Medina preacher comparing her to a fraud [S3 p.10-11]; the Princess of Eboli's denunciation [S1 intro].
- On the road: she tells a nun in distress at the figs, "muy buenos son estos higos; muchos pobres no tendran tanto regalo" [S1 p.334; quote 7].

## 5. Quotations (original, own English, locator)
1. "Estuvo tullida ocho meses, y esta larga y penosa enfermedad la duro tres anos." Own English: She was crippled for eight months, and the long illness lasted three years. [S1 p.112, Ribera]. Public-domain.
2. "Por amor de Dios, Padre, que no este tan sin miedo, que me le hace tomar a mi; de nuevo mire, que no querria enganarle." Own English: For the love of God, Father, do not be so without fear, for you make me afraid; look again, I would not want to deceive you. [S3 Banez, p.7, sworn 16 Oct 1591]. Facts-only edition; original testimony PD.
3. "Esta hija no es para enterrar." Own English: This daughter is not for burying. [S1 p.111; also S3 Ana de la Encarnacion p.22 "no era hija para enterrar"].
4. "tantos monasterios cuantos pelos tenia en la cabeza." Own English: as many monasteries as she had hairs on her head. [S3 Banez p.8, reporting Rubeo]. Banez said he heard this from the General himself.
5. "lo que Su Majestad no ha acabado conmigo en tanta multitud de anos ... acabo con ellas en tres meses" (partial; text is OCR-damaged in this part). Own English: what God has not finished in me in so many years he finished in them in three months. [S3 p.11, witness text near line 3830]. Check the page in the book before use.
6. "Valame Dios, que cansada me siento; mas ha de veinte anos que nunca me acoste temprano, sino ahora." Own English: God help me, how tired I am; it is more than twenty years since I went to bed early, except now. [S1 p.334, Ribera].
7. "No tengas pena por mi, hija, que muy buenos son estos higos; muchos pobres no tendran tanto regalo." Own English: Do not worry for me, daughter; these figs are very good; many poor people will not have such a feast. [S1 p.333-334].
8. "Hijas mias y senoras mias, por amor de Dios las pido tengan gran cuenta con la guarda de la regla y constituciones, y no miren el mal ejemplo que esta mala monja las ha dado, y perdonenmele." Own English: My daughters and ladies, for the love of God I ask you to keep the rule and constitutions carefully, and do not look at the bad example this bad nun gave you, and forgive me. [S1 p.335].
9. "En fin, Senor, soy hija de la Iglesia." Own English: In the end, Lord, I am a daughter of the Church. [S1 p.335].
10. "Tengo yo de tener cosa propia? aqui no me daran un poco de tierra?" Own English: Must I have anything of my own? Will they not give me a little earth here? [S1 p.336]. Asked whether to move her body to Avila.
11. "Cuando estaba en el articulo de la muerte ... el rostro vuelto hacia las religiosas, muy bello y encendido ... que no parecia sino una luna llena" Own English: face turned to the nuns, very beautiful and glowing, like a full moon. [S1 p.336 footnote, María de San Francisco in the beatification process]. Partial paraphrase; exact original: "muy bello y encendido, con tanta hermosura, que me parecio no se la habia visto mayor en mi vida" and "que no parecia sino una luna llena".
12. "Veia un angel cabe mi hacia el lado izquierdo en forma corporal" ... "Veiale en las manos un dardo de oro largo, y al fin del hierro me parecia tener un poco de fuego" [Vida ch.29 as printed in S1 p.139 footnote]. Own English: I saw an angel beside me on the left side, in bodily form; in his hands a long golden spear with a little fire at the iron's tip. Rights: original text by Teresa PD. Use the Escorial manuscript for print: S2 note says the manuscript word is "cherubines", and Banez wrote "seraphines" in the margin.
13. "Representation of Christ most grievously wounded; and so devotional, that the very sight of it ... moved me" [S2 ch.9 par.1] (English, Lewis). Public-domain translation.
14. "I thought my heart was breaking. I threw myself on the ground beside it, my tears flowing plenteously" [S2 ch.9 par.1].
15. "It seems to me that I said to Him then that I would not rise up till He granted my petition." [S2 ch.9 par.3].
16. "When I read of martyrdom undergone by the Saints for the love of God, it struck me that the vision of God was very cheaply purchased" [S2 ch.1 par.4 (the passage beginning at the second tag found by grep)].
17. "Hija, a esta tuve por amiga viviendo en la tierra, y a ti te tengo por amiga ahora estando en el cielo." Own English: Daughter, I had her as a friend while she lived on earth, and I have you as a friend now, while in heaven. [S3 Yanguas, line 15130; Teresa's report of the Lord's reply when she envied Mary Magdalene]. Witness hearsay: Yanguas reports what Teresa said.

## 6. Scenes (max 10)
1. Two children and the Adaja bridge. An uncle catches them; Rodrigo blames his sister; the mother has had the house searched, afraid of the well in the house [S1 p.96].
2. The garden hermitages: stones piled for walls; they fall down [S1 p.97].
3. Fifteen of August: the coma. Wax on her eyes, the grave open at the Incarnation, her father's refusal, the candle that burns the bed [S1 p.110-111].
4. The statue of the wounded Christ in the oratory, and "I would not rise" [S2 ch.9; S1 p.121].
5. San Jose, 24 Aug 1562: four novices, one priest with the Sacrament, hostile town [S1 text after line 11117; S3 Banez p.8].
6. Banez at the Inquisition's door with her book [S1 intro; Banez' own words quoted there: "Yo lleve el libro, dice Banez, al Santo Oficio ... en Madrid"].
7. The road to Alba: a faint near Penaranda, figs for food, cabbage with onion, which her illness forbade [S1 p.333-334].
8. Alba, the Duchess feeding her; the spilled medicine that smells of "agua de angeles" [S1 p.334 footnote, quoted from María de San Francisco in the process].
9. 3 Oct, 5 pm: she sits up in bed when the host enters; "hija de la Iglesia" [S1 p.335].
10. 4 Oct: face like a full moon; Isabel de la Cruz healed of a headache by Teresa's hands before she dies [S1 p.336 footnote]; the room sister sees white-robed visitors (legend/vision, see section 7).

## 7. Miracles and reported wonders (what I read only)
- Her revival after four days: Ribera's own doubts on the revelations she is said to have spoken [S1 p.111]. Popular.
- Isabel de la Cruz: a headache and eye pain, cured when Teresa put her hands on her head before she died. Reported by María de San Francisco in the process; date 4 Oct 1582, Alba [S1 p.336 footnote]. Recorded in the process, status approved as testimony only.
- Catalina Baptista: lost sense of smell, recovered when she kissed Teresa's feet after death [same].
- The medicine spilled on the bed smelling of "agua de angeles" (Sept 1582, Alba) [S1 p.334 footnote, María de San Francisco].
- White dove from her mouth; star over the tower; white-robed visitors in her cell, reported by Ana de San Bartolomé and the infirmarian Catalina de la Concepcion [S1 p.337 footnote]. Late/legendary flavor: Ribera repeats them as reports. Popular.
- Archbishop of Granada struck by lightning and then allowing the foundation: reported by Yepes [S1 p.321 footnote]. Popular providence story.
- Body found whole about three years later with fragrance and oil: the Process question text, not a verified report [S3 line 1512].
- Revelation to Catalina de Jesus at Veas that Teresa had died of "ímpetu de amor" and her soul left [S1 p.337-338]. Reported; the Rota and the Bull echo it [S1 p.338].
- Nothing from the Congregation of Rites on these items was read here; the church-miracles agent owns the official side.

- Entry at the Incarnation: Ribera's edition footnote fixes 2 Nov 1535 on three documents; the Zimmerman note lists 1533, 1534, 1535 as opinions [S1 p.110 footnote; S2 introduction].
