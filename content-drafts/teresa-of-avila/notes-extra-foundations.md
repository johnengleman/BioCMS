## Fix of an earlier error (the "friendship" line)
- CONFIRMED: "no es otra cosa oración mental, a mi parecer, sino tratar de amistad, estando muchas veces tratando a solas con quien sabemos nos ama" is LIFE (Libro de la Vida) ch. 8, par. 5 (E2 vol 1; the text sits inside CAPITULO VIII, between the headings at OCR lines 8696 and 8976 of vol 1). It is NOT Camino de Perfección; notes-own-words Q11 and plan.md chapter 7 should say Life 8.5. Lewis tr. (E3, ch. 8 par. 7): "for mental prayer is nothing else, in my opinion, but being on terms of friendship with God, frequently conversing in secret with Him Who, we know, loves us." Lewis par. 7 = Spanish par. 5 (paragraph numbering differs). Accents confirmed in the Spanish: oración, parecer, amistad, sabemos.
- Also in that same sentence (Spanish): "nadie le tomó por amigo que no se lo pagase" = "no one ever took Him for a friend that He did not repay." Lewis: "no one ever took Him for his friend that was not amply rewarded".

## Dated timeline additions (1567-1569, Medina to Toledo) [E1 ch.9-15 text and Zimmerman notes]
- 1568, Lent: leaves Alcalá for Toledo with nuns Anne of the Angels and Antonia of the Holy Ghost, sends to Avila for four more; reaches Malagón before Passion Sunday [E1 ch.9 n.6].
- 1568, 11 Apr (Palm Sunday): Malagón founded; the townspeople meet the nuns in procession, the nuns "in our white mantles, with our veils over our faces" [E1 ch.9 §5]. Not quite two months there; leaves c.19 May (letter 18 May) [E1 ch.10 n.7]. Contract with Doña Luisa de la Cerda 30 Mar 1568 (Zimmerman). Doña Luisa lost six of seven children in infancy [E1 ch.9 n.2]. At Malagón she picks the convent site with the parish priest and mayor, and at one spot says "This must be left for the barefooted friars of S. Francis"; years later Franciscans built there [E1 ch.9 n.6, via Reforma]. Zimmerman: she returned 25 Nov 1579 and sat on a stone watching the workmen; her eighth and last visit; inauguration 8 Dec 1579; stayed to mid-Feb 1580 [E1 ch.9 n.3]. NEW.
- 1568, after Malagón, Teresa writes the final version of her Life and sends it to Juan de Ávila (via inquisitor Soto's advice); letters from Toledo 27 May and Avila in June [E1 ch.10 n.4]. Link to chapter on Inquisition.
- 1568, Aug: Valladolid. Don Bernardino de Mendoza had offered a house near the river, died suddenly without confession. Christ told Teresa "his salvation had been in serious danger" but he would be released from purgatory at the first Mass in that house. She arrives 10 Aug (St Lawrence), sees the house is damp and unhealthy, tired, hears Mass far off, secretly fills the walls with clay. At the first Mass (15 Aug, Assumption) she sees him beside the priest, "his face was bright and shining, his hands were joined" and he thanks her [E1 ch.10 §1-5]. Zimmerman notes she was one day off in the date she gives (the Sunday). Nearly all the nuns fell ill; Doña María de Mendoza took them in and gave a better house; they moved 3 Feb 1569, feast of St Blaise [E1 ch.10 §6-7, n.9]. NEW vivid.
- 1568 (en route Avila to Medina, Aug): visits Duruelo site offered by Don Rafael Mejía Velázquez, sends Julián de Ávila to Olmedo to the bishop [E1 ch.10 n.5, n.8].
- 1568, 30 Sep: John of the Cross leaves Valladolid, having received the new habit of the Reform from Teresa (likely at Medina in July or Aug; the date is argued); arrives at Duruelo end of Sept, spends the night in prayer, places the habit on the altar, blesses it, says Mass, puts it on: first friar of the Reform [E1 ch.14 n.1, citing Jerome of St Joseph]. Antonio de Heredia (age 58) arrives 27 Nov [E1 ch.14 n.2]. 28 Nov 1568 (first Sunday of Advent): first Mass in the "porch of Bethlehem"; the friars change names: Antonio de Heredia becomes Antonio of Jesus, John of St Matthias becomes John of the Cross, lay brother Joseph of Christ [E1 ch.14 n.4]. 
- Duruelo scene by Teresa, Lent 1569 on her way to Toledo: Fray Antonio of Jesus sweeping the church door, joyful; she says "What is this, father? what has become of your dignity?" He: "I execrate the time wherein I had any." Two merchants friends who came with her "did nothing but cry, there were so many crosses, so many skulls"; a small wooden cross by the holy water with a paper picture of Christ stuck to it, "it seemed to cause more devotion than if it had been made of some material most admirably fashioned"; the choir is the garret, friars stoop to enter; hay hermitages; they stay praying until Prime "having their habits covered with snow, but they did not know it" [E1 ch.14 §5-6]. NEW. Antonio's hour-glasses: he had only five, to keep the hours, "that amused me much" [E1 ch.14 §2].
- Teresa asked the Duruelo friars "not to be so severe with themselves" and they made light of her advice [E1 ch.14 §11]. She says this was "a much greater grace on His part than was that which He gave me in founding the houses of nuns".
- The Mancera well: Antonio taps with his stick, "Now dig here"; water rushes; later "We ask for water, Lord, but not for a flood" (Zimmerman n., from Reforma) [E1 ch.14 §9, n.]. Move to Mancera 11 Jun 1570 (St Barnabas) [E1 ch.14 n.].
- Zimmerman: sandals. 12 Dec 1576 she wrote to Fr Mariano against friars who claimed her authority for total barefootedness, saying she preferred talented, learned men to enter than that exaggeration frighten them away [E1 ch.14 n.6]. Character: against excess.
- 1569, Lent to 14 May: Toledo foundation. Left Valladolid 21 Feb 1569; on the way visits Duruelo; arrives Toledo on the eve of the Annunciation (24 Mar) with two nuns [E1 ch.15 §3 and nn.]. Merchant Martin Ramírez died; his heirs (brother Alonso Álvarez Ramírez and son-in-law Diego Ortiz) press conditions; permission blocked by the Toledo council (no archbishop: Carranza in prison from 1559, absolved 1576) [E1 ch.15 n.5]. She meets the administrator in a church near his house, 8 May, speaks "with a certain firmness of purpose" and gets the licence [E1 ch.15 §5-6 and n.9]. She has "three or four ducats": buys two pictures on canvas, two straw mattresses, and a blanket [E1 ch.15 §6]. 
- The helper is a poor student, Andrada, sent by the Franciscan Martín de la Cruz; her companions laugh; he finds the house next morning "he had the keys of it with him" [E1 ch.15 §7-8]. They take possession at night with a small elevation bell, clean all night "in great fear", then knock a door through a wall into a neighbour house of women who rose in terror; Mass said on St Boniface, 14 May 1569 by the Carmelite prior Juan Gutiérrez de la Magdalena, who later (provincial 1576) turned against her and presided the 1577 Incarnation election with 55 nuns excommunicated [E1 ch.15 §9-10 nn.]. NEW (the link to 1577 is Zimmerman's).
- Toledo: no furniture but two straw mattresses and a blanket; "on that first day we had not even a withered leaf to fry a sardine with" until someone laid a faggot in the church; when wealth arrived the sisters said, "What is the matter, mother? We do not seem to be poor any longer." [E1 ch.15 §13-14]. Teresa: "I was in pain when my poverty was ended." Good humour/character.
- Footnote anecdote (Zimmerman, from Oeuvres III): praying in the church of San Clemente in Toledo, a woman who lost a wooden shoe suspects Teresa of taking it and hits her on the head with the other; Teresa: "God bless this good woman! I had already enough headache without that." [E1 ch.15 n.10]. Source is a secondary footnote: mark as tradition.
- Christ's rebuke on noble birth: "How useless will birth and dignities be before the judgment-seat of God!" [E1 ch.15 §16].

## Timeline additions, 1569-1575 (Pastrana, Salamanca, Alba, Incarnation, Segovia) [E1 ch.16-21 with Zimmerman notes]
- 1569, Toledo (eve of Pentecost, 28 May; she left 30 May): a servant of the Princess of Eboli (Ana de Mendoza y de la Cerda, b. 29 Jun 1540, betrothed at 12 to Ruy Gómez de Silva, much older; ten children; lost her right eye, Zimmerman n.) arrives while Teresa is at table, "I could scarcely eat, so great was the joy of my soul" at her feast-day peace [E1 ch.17 §1, n.]. She refuses to go; then prays; Christ says "I was going for something more than for that foundation" and to take the rule and constitutions; she asks her confessor, hiding what she heard [E1 ch.17 §3].
- 1569, Madrid: lodges with Doña Leonor de Mascareñas (once governess of Philip II) at the Franciscan house; meets hermit Mariano Azaro (b. Bitonto, Naples; doctor of canon and civil law; at Trent; school friend of future Gregory XIII; jailed two years on a false murder charge, witnesses contradict each other "as it happened to the old men who accused S. Susanna") and the lay brother Fray Juan de la Miseria from the Tardón hermitage [E1 ch.17 §5-9 and nn.]. She shows Mariano the primitive Carmelite rule; "he told me he would think of it that very night"; next morning he is converted. Zimmerman: he read the rule aloud to Juan and cried "Brother John, we have found what we are seeking for; that is the rule we should keep." [E1 ch.17 n.]. Teresa: he was "amazed at the change so suddenly wrought in himself, especially by a woman". 
- Mariano's later fame in the Order: ordained 1574; first master of novices in Seville [E1 ch.17 n.]. He is the friar who sits beside Teresa as the portrait is painted (see portrait below).
- Fray Juan de la Miseria painted the only portrait of Teresa from life, in Seville (Gracián's Declamación, via Zimmerman): Gracián, as her superior, ordered her to sit "for her greater mortification". Teresa looked at it and said mirthfully "Fray Juan, God forgive thee! what I have had to suffer at thy hands and after all to paint me blear-eyed and ugly." (Fuente, quoted by Zimmerman). Still at the Seville convent, partly repainted [E1 ch.17 n.5]. NEW and vivid; mark as Zimmerman/Fuente quote, not her own writing. Juan died Madrid 1616, more than 100 years old [same note].
- 1569, Pastrana: three months there; the Princess insists on "certain things unbecoming our order"; Teresa decides to leave rather than consent; Ruy Gómez calms his wife [E1 ch.17 §12]. Zimmerman: the Princess brought an Augustinian nun, Catalina Machuca, and wanted her to take the Carmelite habit on the spot; Teresa refused and sought Báñez's opinion, who approved. The Princess wanted no endowment; Teresa refused because she doubted her generosity [E1 ch.17 n.14]. Also: the Princess found out Teresa had written the Life, insisted on reading it, "ridiculed the book; left it for her servants to read; and these... divulged its contents"; it led the Inquisition to demand the book (Reforma, via Zimmerman) [E1 ch.17 n.14, which also points to Relation 7.17].
- 9 Jun 1569: friars take civil possession of Pastrana; Sacrament reserved 13 June (true date) after Fray Antonio of Jesus arrives; Mariano and Juan receive the habit as lay brothers; Baltasar (Nieto) of Jesus joins (Zimmerman's long note: ex-Minim with a troubled record, later turned against Gracián and gave false evidence; died Lisbon 1589) [E1 ch.17 §13-14 nn.]. Rubeo's letters patent of 8 Aug 1570 limit the Discalced to houses they had and forbid taking Calced Carmelites of Andalucía [E1 ch.17 n.16]. Link to later war with the Calced.
- 1573, 29 Jul: Ruy Gómez dies in Madrid. The Princess in her grief insists on becoming a Carmelite nun at once (Zimmerman, from Reforma): she arrives at Pastrana with her mother at 8 am (Baltasar of Jesus had woken the nuns at 2 am); prioress Isabel of St Dominic says "The princess a nun? the monastery is lost."; she takes the lowest seat in the refectory, brings two novices and two maids, shouts "What have the friars to do with my monastery?" (Zimmerman n.), withdraws to a hermitage in the garden and receives visitors in a nun's habit [E1 ch.17 n.17]. Teresa wrote the Foundations text on this: "the devil, or perhaps because our Lord permitted it—His Majesty knoweth why—sent the princess here as a nun, in the tumult of her grief for her husband's death" [E1 ch.17 §14]. NEW for English readers: Teresa's version is mild; Zimmerman gives the sharp details.
- 1574, Holy Week: the Pastrana nuns leave at midnight with the corregidor's receipt for everything the Princess had given, escorted by Julián de Ávila, Antonio Gaytán and Fray Gabriel of the Assumption, and reach Segovia; at the moment of their danger on the road Teresa in Segovia says "Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana" (Reforma via Zimmerman). The bishop of Segorbe follows with a lawsuit threat; the receipt is shown and he says no more [E1 ch.17 n.19, 20]. Teresa: "I had the greatest joy in the world when I saw them in peace" [E1 ch.17 §15].
- 1570, 1 Nov (eve of All Souls at Salamanca): Foundation of Salamanca. Teresa travels with only one companion, Mary of the Blessed Sacrament; arrives at noon on All Saints' eve in the cold; student lodgers have to be coaxed out by Nicolás Gutiérrez (a ruined and cheerful "good man", six daughters later nuns); they enter at dusk; first foundation without the Sacrament; they sleep on straw under two borrowed blankets [E1 ch.18-19; ch.19 §2-4]. Scene: Mary says, "Mother, I am thinking, if I were to die now, what you would do alone."; Teresa: "Sister, when that shall happen I will consider what I shall do; now let me go to sleep." Teresa laughs: "at which I am ready to laugh when I remember it" [E1 ch.19 §5]. NEW to the biography (check what the entry has). The house is large, with many garrets; students are rumoured to be hiding; the bells for All Souls are tolling.
- Teresa on travel (ch.18 §4): snow all day, lost roads, fever; "I never refrained from making a foundation through fear of trouble, though I felt a great dislike to journeys, especially long ones"; "It is a special joy to me to see one church more, when I consider how many the Lutherans are destroying." [E1 ch.18 §4]. Good character line.
- 1571 (Zimmerman n.): Teresa and Prioress Agnes of Jesus ordered out of Medina before night by provincial Salazar under pain of excommunication (a family dispute over novice Isabel of the Angels' property); Teresa, very ill, obeys; visitor Pedro Fernández (Dominican) hears of her from Báñez, sends her back, and in 1571 names her prioress of the Incarnation (which she did not want); she formally renounced the mitigated rule at St Joseph's before taking office; nuns unwilling; she asks the visitor for two Reform confessors: John of the Cross and Germán of St Matthias [E1 ch.19 n.7]. NEW.
- 1571, Feb/Mar, Salamanca: lodged with the Count and Countess of Monterrey; Teresa prays for their dying small daughter; St Catherine of Siena and St Dominic appear and say the life is granted and the child must wear the Dominican habit for a year; she tells Báñez, who tells the parents; the girl later married the Count of Olivares (Reforma, via Zimmerman) [E1 ch.19 n.14]. Reported miracle, secondary source.
- 1573 Aug-29 Sep: Salamanca move to new house; they enter before dawn on St Michael's eve; heavy rain, leaking chapel; Teresa complains to Christ ("He must either not bid me to apply myself to these foundations or relieve us in our present necessity"); Nicolás calm: "God would send help"; sun shines on 29 Sept. Teresa: "I found I was very imperfect that day" [E1 ch.19 §8]. Zimmerman adds Anne of Jesus's version: when the sky cleared she said, "Your Reverence might very well have asked this favour a little sooner." Teresa looked at her smilingly and went to her room [E1 ch.19 n.]. NEW comic scene.
- 1571, 25 Jan (Conversion of St Paul): Alba de Tormes foundation, foundress Teresa de Layz (a baby alleged to say "Yes, I am one" when asked if she was not a Christian; St Andrew in a vision shows a meadow and a well; the same well in the court of the house in Alba) [E1 ch.20 §4, 7, 9, 12]. Teresa insisted on adequate endowment and on the monasteries having "no need for the nuns to apply to their kindred". Teresa admits "In the dates of the foundations I suspect I am occasionally in error" [E1 ch.20 §13].
- 1574, 19 Mar (St Joseph): Segovia. Teresa is then prioress of the Incarnation for three years under the visitor Pedro Fernández; Christ tells her to found; she asks him; he consents. She is ill six months; "my soul was in very great dryness and darkness; I had a fever upon me, and loathed my food" [E1 ch.21 §1-3]. She enters in secret the night before; the vicar-general arrives furious next day, forbids Mass, posts a constable at the door; John of the Cross (the "barefooted friar" said Mass; Zimmerman n.) and Julián of Ávila hid (Julián under the staircase per Ribera, via Zimmerman); the vicar-general is satisfied by nobles; no Sacrament for some months [E1 ch.21 §4-8, nn.]. Don Juan de Orozco, bishop's nephew, passes the house, sees the cross on the door, says Mass; she tells him "God had brought him to the house" and that she was his aunt's cousin [E1 ch.21 n.14]. Her three years at the Incarnation ended 6 Oct 1574; the nuns who had threatened rebellion re-elected her [E1 ch.21 n.15]. NEW: link of John of the Cross to the Segovia foundation.
- Teresa on her helpers: of Julián de Ávila and Antonio Gaytán, "their conversation on the journey was about God, for the instruction of those who travelled with us" [E1 ch.21 §5].

## Character lines (this block)
- She buys icons with her last ducats, not food: two pictures on canvas, two mattresses, a blanket (Toledo) [E1 ch.15 §6].
- Obedient nuns who threw themselves in a pond at a thoughtless remark of the prioress (Toledo); Teresa had to "watch what the prioress was saying" [E1 ch.16 §3]. Zimmerman: she herself first wrote that she made the remark ("spoke to me and I said to her") and corrected the MS [E1 ch.16 n.2]. Teresa's later letter to Mary of St Joseph (11 Nov 1576) against blows and pinching at Malagón: "they are not slaves" [E1 ch.18 n.4, Zimmerman]. Good evidence that she warned against excessive penance.
- To a young woman who proposed to bring her Bible to the convent: "No, you shall not come here; we do not want you or your Bible, for we are ignorant women, and do nothing but spin and obey." (Yepes via Zimmerman; the girl later fell into the Inquisition's hands) [E1 ch.16 n.]. Secondary report; mark as tradition and handle with care (it fits the time's suspicion of lay Bible reading, and Teresa's own wary public stance).
- Teresa on visions (ch.8 §3-4): Báñez told her not to treat a vision as a devil's picture with contempt: "it is right to venerate the image of our Lord wherever we may see it, even if the devil himself had been the painter—and he is a great painter" [E1 ch.8 §3]. Strong, quotable. "like the spider, the food of which is all turned into poison, and not like the bee, which turns it all into honey" [E1 ch.8 §4]. Spanish check pending.

## Timeline additions, 1575-1576 (Gratián, Veas, Seville, Caravaca) [E1 ch.22-27 with Zimmerman notes]
- 1575, Jan to Feb: Teresa at Valladolid on 4 Jan, then Avila; a messenger arrives from Doña Catalina de Otálora of Caravaca (three young women, after a Jesuit sermon, shut themselves in her house "determined never to quit it till a monastery should be built") [E1 ch.27 §1 and nn.]. Julián de Ávila and Antonio Gaytán go ahead and settle the deeds; the contract date is 10 Mar 1575; the licence came with a clause putting the house under the knights of Santiago; Teresa refused and wrote to the king, who ordered a new licence [E1 ch.27 §3-5]. The three girls stay shut in until the nuns arrive 18 Dec 1575; Sacrament reserved on 1 Jan 1576 (feast of the Name of Jesus), the foundress group sent by Gratián while Teresa was in Seville [E1 ch.27 §5-7 nn.]. Teresa never went to Caravaca herself.
- 1575, Feb 24 (St Matthias): Veas founded (foundress Catalina Godínez; Teresa tells the story of her conversion and her cure; Teresa arrives with six nuns from Malagón; Anne of Jesus prioress) [E1 ch.22 §4 and n.1 of ch.23]. While at Veas she receives word from the bishop of Avila and from the nuns that Inquisition officers are searching for the Life [E1 ch.23 n.1, citing Relation 7.8, 16]. NEW date link.
- 1575, April: Gratián (b. Valladolid 5/6 June 1545, son of Charles V's and Philip II's secretary Diego Gracián de Alderete; mother Juana Dantisco, daughter of a Polish ambassador who later became a bishop; third of twenty children, thirteen survived; MA divinity 1564, priest 1569; took the habit at Pastrana 25 Mar 1572, professed 28 Mar 1573) arrives at Veas. Zimmerman: Teresa "conceived a singular veneration and affection for him; for she understood that our Lord had chosen him to hold His own place with regard to her (Relation 6.1)". Teresa on first meeting: "he pleased me so much that I did not think that they who had spoken so highly of him really knew him at all." and "during those days I felt such exceeding comfort and happiness that I was in truth astonished at myself." [E1 ch.23 n.2, ch.24 §1]. NEW.
- Gratián's story, from Teresa: as a child he prayed at an image of Our Lady in Madrid, called her "his love", saw "her eyes sometimes... filled with tears over the many offences committed against her Son"; wanted the Jesuits but was told to wait a few days; sent to Pastrana to arrange a nun's reception, where the prioress told him to pray to enter himself; the nuns prayed with fasts and disciplines for his vocation [E1 ch.23 §4-9]. Teresa says: "for, though he overlooks no fault... he does it all with such winning sweetness that no one is able to complain of him" [E1 ch.23 §7].
- Zimmerman's long note on Gratián's later life (useful for the ending): general of the Discalced; expelled 17 Feb 1592 on falsified evidence; captured by pirates and taken to Tunis, worked among Christian slaves, freed after 18 months; Clement VIII said "this man is a Saint"; died Brussels 21 Sep 1614 [E1 ch.23 n.2]. His autobiography is *Peregrinaciones de Anastasio* (Burgos 1905) [same note].
- 1575, May: Gratián as superior orders Teresa to ask God whether Madrid or Seville next; answer Madrid; he nevertheless orders Seville; two or three days later he asks why she obeyed him rather than Christ; she says she "could not be so sure of any revelation as she was of his order"; he tells her to ask again, and the answer is Seville (Yepes via Zimmerman) [E1 ch.24 n.9]. NEW and key for character (obedience).
- Also on the road at Ecija she made the vow of obedience to Gratián (Zimmerman: Relation 6.3) [E1 ch.24 n.10].
- 1575, 18 May to 26 May: journey to Seville in covered carriages with six nuns, Julián de Ávila, Antonio Gaytán, a barefoot friar; "to go into them was like going into Purgatory" in the heat; "I could have ventured to go with them into the country of the Turks" [E1 ch.24 §3]. Teresa has a violent fever, falls into lethargy; they throw water over her face "so warm, because of the heat" it gave little relief; a shed-like room with no window; a bed "as if made of sharp stones"; she gets up and travels on [E1 ch.24 §4-5]. Crossing the Guadalquivir (near Espeluy) the ferry slips from its rope and drifts without oars; a ferryman's boy, ten or eleven, cries for his father; a nobleman sends help; the boat grounds on a sandbank; a man from the castle guides them at night [E1 ch.24 §6]. In Córdoba (Pentecost Monday) they hurry to hear Mass unseen; the church is full for the Holy Spirit's feast; the carriages are too big for the bridge gate and have to be cut with a saw; "it must have taken away my fever altogether"; a good man leads them through the crowd (later he inherited an estate, "which he thought was the reward") [E1 ch.24 §7-8]. They eat at noon "under a bridge" [E1 ch.24 §8]. Superb comic and dramatic scene; NEW to popular English.
- 1575, Thu 26 May: arrives Seville; Mariano had hired a house, but the archbishop Cristóbal de Rojas y Sandoval (b. 1502, once at Trent) had not given leave and "never sanctioned" a monastery of nuns founded in poverty; the friars had not told him; Teresa learns from Mariano; money: "we had but one coin remaining"; Antonio Gaytán's friend lends money; the archbishop permits Mass on Trinity Sunday (29 May) but no bell; "I had made up my mind... to go back with my nuns, with very little regret, to Veas" [E1 ch.24 §9-12 and nn.]. Teresa writes that she was torn between reasons: "I had always resolutely refrained... from making any foundations in Andalucia" [E1 ch.24 §2].
- 1575, Seville, June: the Calced prior Fray Miguel de Ulloa visits and asks to see the General's letter; Teresa shows it but refuses a copy (Antonio of St Joseph note) [E1 ch.24 n.21]. In the end the archbishop visits and tells her "to do what I liked, and as I liked" [E1 ch.24 §14].
- 1575, Aug: brother Lorenzo de Cepeda, 34 years in the Indies (b. 1519, left Spain 1540; treasurer to the Viceroy at Quito 1550; wife Juana de Fuentes died 1567; seven children) arrives at Sanlúcar [E1 ch.25 n.7]. Christ tells Teresa in prayer "I have heard you; let me be." (ch.25 §4). The first house was dropped because the seller made difficulties. Lorenzo, as surety, takes refuge in the Carmelite monastery after a notary's error in the deed (letters 29 Apr 1576) [E1 ch.25 §8 n.12]. The house on La Pagería, 6,000 ducats; the move by night and in fear "they thought every shadow they saw was a friar" (Franciscan neighbours were against) [E1 ch.25 §6]. Prior of the Carthusians of Las Cuevas, Don Fernando Pantoja (born Seville, over 80), a Carthusian since 1518, gives alms; Teresa gave him "a fragment of one of the thorns with which our Lord was crowned" (Zimmerman) [E1 ch.25 §8 n.13]. He died 8 Jul 1582, three months before her.
- 1576, 3 Jun (Sunday in the Ascension octave): solemn reservation of the Sacrament in Seville; the archbishop carries it; at the end he kneels and asks Teresa to bless him (Reforma via Zimmerman, her confusion) [E1 ch.25 §10-11 n.]. Powder explodes and flames go up the cloister but the silk hangings stay unsinged [E1 ch.25 §12]. Teresa leaves Seville next day, 4 Jun 1576, with Lorenzo and Fray Gregorio Nazianzen; in Malagón on 11 Jun; Toledo by 9 Aug, with Anne of St Bartholomew as companion and secretary [E1 ch.26 n.1]. Teresa: "with the exception of the foundation in Avila... I never had so much to endure anywhere as here, because my trials were for the most part interior" [E1 ch.26 §2]. Also, "I was so weak and cowardly... I felt our Lord was in some measure withdrawing His hand, that He might abide in Himself, and that I might see that any courage I had before was not mine." - Zimmerman: Beatriz of the Mother of God, the first Seville novice (b. c. 1550s, saw a mysterious old barefoot friar who said "Beatriz, God make thee strong", a vision of Elijah per her later admission), gave false evidence in 1577 against the prioress and against Gratián and was named prioress, and later asked pardon [E1 ch.26 n.8]. So the Seville house was used in the 1577-78 attack on Gratián. Prioress Mary of St Joseph deposed 1579 then reinstated [E1 ch.25 n.9].
- Teresa's prejudice: Zimmerman says she was Castilian "not without a certain prejudice against the people of Andalucia" (proverb "Jesús y Cruces y pedradas en los andaluces") [E1 ch.25 n.2]. Mild.
- Mary of St Joseph (Salazar), prioress of Seville: Teresa wrote her sixty letters; "second in importance only to those written to Father Jerome Gratian" (Zimmerman) [E1 ch.25 n.9]. Her Libro de las Recreaciones is the source for the Córdoba date.
- Mortification extremes: Zimmerman notes Teresa's 11 Nov 1576 letter to Mary of St Joseph forbade pinching and surprise blows at Malagón: "Evidently the devil, under pretext of perfection, teaches to lead souls to commit an offence against God" and "it is necessary to keep a vigilant eye on these childish prioresses" [E1 ch.18 n.4]. Zimmerman translation of the letter, not the Stanbrook version; check letter text before quoting.
- Teresa's rules for dowries and poverty (ch.27 §11): "never fail to receive those who come to be nuns merely because they are without worldly goods, if they are virtuous"; "my joy was not so great when I received those that brought much with them as it was when I received those who came for God's sake alone... I had fears about the former, while those who were poor cheered my spirit, and gave me so much pleasure as to make me weep for joy" [E1 ch.27 §11]. Good quote.
- Teresa's own account of the foundations (ch.27 §10, 13-14): "Where do you think a poor woman like myself, under obedience, without a farthing in the world, or any one to help her, found the means to do so great a work?" [E1 ch.27 §9]; "I see very clearly that the work was not mine, but God's" [E1 ch.27 §14].
