# Teresa of Avila — notes: church-miracles focus

Researcher: church-miracles agent, 2026-10-01. Tier A. Tradition: Catholic only (playbook latin-iberia.md).
Status labels: APPROVED (Church act), OWN (in her own writing), WITNESS (sworn or reported by people), LEGEND, CONTESTED.

## 1. Identity (church side only)
- Teresa de Ahumada y Cepeda; Teresa de Jesus; Teresa of Avila. Born Avila 28 March 1515 [S2; S6 p.309]. Died Alba de Tormes, night of 4 Oct 1582 [S2; S5 contents, p.585-604]. Age 67 [S2].
- Beatified 24 April 1614, Paul V [S3]. Canonized 12 March 1622, Gregory XV, St Peter's, with Ignatius, Francis Xavier, Isidore the Farmer, Philip Neri [S3; S4].
- Doctor of the Church 27 Sept 1970, Paul VI, first woman [S4; S2]. (Others in S2: Catherine of Siena, Therese of Lisieux, Hildegard.)
- Feast: 15 October, memorial, Roman Calendar [S3; S2]. Why 15 Oct and not 4 Oct: NOT READ (to check: Gregorian reform dropped 5-14 Oct 1582).
- Roman Martyrology text for 15 Oct is quoted in S2 and S3 (Latin-Italian official text).

## 3. Timeline (church-miracle focus)
- 1522-ish: child Teresa and brother Rodrigo set out for the land of the Moors to be martyrs; an uncle stops them on the bridge over the Adaja [S6 p.309]. NEW? no, widely known.
- Aug 1537: four days in a coma; grave dug at the Incarnation; her father refuses burial [S6 p.316]. Teresa says St Joseph later restored her health, after about three years paralysis [S6 p.317].
- 29 June 1557: Christ present "close by me" (feast of St Peter); not seen, felt [S1 ch.27 §3 and footnote date].
- Around 1559 (date uncertain): the angel with the golden spear [S1 ch.29 §16-18, editor's note on date].
- Before 1562: rapture lifts her body off the floor, in choir at communion and during a sermon [S1 ch.20 §4-7].
- 1562: first reformed house, St Joseph, Avila; later 16 more foundations [S2]. (24 Aug 1562 per S2.)
- 4 Oct 1582, about 9 pm: she dies at Alba, in the arms of Sister Anne of St Bartholomew [S5 p.603-604].
- Next day: body carried to the outer chapel; burial the day after, deep, with stones and mortar [S5 p.605-606].
- Nine months later (about July 1583): Gracian digs it up after four days of work; the body is whole [S5 p.606].
- 24 Nov 1585: second exhumation; body unchanged; Alba keeps an arm; body moved to Avila [S5 p.607].
- After pressure from the Duke and Duchess of Alba, the body goes back to Alba [S5 p.607].
- 1588: first edition of her works, edited by Fray Luis de Leon [S5 p.610].
- 1595-97: processes in Spain, sent to Rome by the Nuncio in 1597 [S5 p.610; S7 shows the 1595 hearings].
- 24 Apr 1614: beatified [S3]. 12 Mar 1622: canonized [S3].
- 1750: church at Alba enlarged; body re-vested, new silver shrine [S5 p.608].
- 1914: last opening before 2024 [S8].
- 27 Sept 1970: Doctor of the Church [S3; S4].
- 28 Aug 2024: tomb opened for study begun in 2022; first findings Oct 2024 [web search summary; S8 says only that results came; the Aug date is from a search snippet, not a page I read].

## 4. Character (as the Church side sees it)
- Obedience test: the confessors ordered her to make signs of scorn at a vision; she did, and Gregory XV praised this in the bull [S6 p.337-338]. She said she might be wrong about a vision but not about obeying [S6 p.338].
- She tried to hide raptures: she held floor mats to stay down [S7 line ~15100]; she asked God to stop public raptures and "have never been enraptured since" in public [S1 ch.20 §6].
- Humor and practicality in the kitchen: raptures at the frying pan [S5, see Miracles].
- Her last words: "I am a child of Thy Church" [S5 p.602]; Paul VI quotes "Finalmente, sono figlia della Chiesa!" [S4].

## 6. Scenes
1. 1537: a grave is dug at the Incarnation; her father says "This daughter is not for burying" (Spanish: "Esta hija no es para enterrar", Ribera, line 7834 of the 1908 text; paraphrase of a secondary edition, check before quoting) [S6 p.316 for the facts].
2. Choir at Communion: Teresa rises from her knees; she orders the nuns to keep silent [S1 ch.20 §5].
3. Sermon on the house feast, great ladies present: she throws herself down and the nuns hold her; the rapture is seen anyway [S1 ch.20 §5].
4. The kitchen: a sister finds her radiant, feet raised above the floor, still gripping the pan of frying fish [S5 ~p.247-248, citing Ribera; WITNESS/legend].
5. A witness (Maria de Jesus) says Ana de los Angeles saw Teresa lifted while praying, and Teresa clutched the floor mats so no one would see [S7 line ~15100].
6. Another witness (Maria de San Jose) says Maria Bautista saw her lifted from the floor several times and "enraptured with her pan in her hand" in the kitchen [S7 line ~18527].
7. The death night: Sister Anne changes her linen "even to her sleeves and coif" at dawn; Teresa smiles [S5 p.603].
8. Death: the cell glows, her face young, three light sighs at nine [S5 p.603-604].
9. Gracian and a friar dig four days to the coffin; wood split, full of earth and water; the body whole [S5 p.606].
10. The Alba tree: a tree by the chapel dead for years puts out leaves and flowers [S5 p.605; citing the bull of canonization, Boll. 1397].

## 7. Miracles (one line each)
In her life
- 1537, Avila Incarnation: apparent death, four days, grave dug; Teresa; Teresa's own Life and father's refusal; OWN/WITNESS (natural cause not excluded; she credits St Joseph for the later cure) [S6 p.316-317].
- 1537-1540: cure of the paralysis through St Joseph, per her own Life; OWN; Church used it as an attribute [S6 p.317; S2 says "vero miracolo" in a devotional text, not an official finding].
- 29 June 1557: Christ present, not seen; OWN [S1 ch.27].
- About 1559, Avila: the transverberation; Teresa; reported by Teresa in Life ch.29; OWN. Editor's note: she says "algunas veces" so it may have repeated; the Incarnation convent has a tradition of a second event during her priorship 1571-74 [S1 ch.29 note]. The Escorial manuscript reads "cherubines"; Banez wrote in the margin that "seraphim" seemed better and later editors followed him [S1 ch.29 footnote]. So "seraph" is an editor's change, not her word. The feast of the Transverberation: NOT READ (to check).
- Before 1562 and later, Avila / Valladolid: levitation. Her own account: OWN (ch.20). Witness accounts: Ana de los Angeles via Maria de Jesus [S7 l.15100]; Maria Bautista via Maria de San Jose [S7 l.18527]; WITNESS (second-hand in these testimonies). Kitchen scene: WITNESS/LEGEND [S5].
- Cross of stones: a cross which Christ handed to her seemed a cross of four great stones carved with the five wounds; only she saw it; she gave it to her sister Joan, who died at Alba; Yepes lists miracles worked by it (not read) [S6 p.337-338]. OWN + WITNESS.
- Lights/wax: Dicho of Juana del Espiritu Santo describes candles which weighed the same after burning, attributed to Teresa by the sacristan; WITNESS [S7 line ~14980-15000, one testimony; exact context to check].
After death
- 4-5 Oct 1582: two nuns see the soul rise, as a white dove and as a crystal globe; a third hears music and sees a procession of virgins; WITNESS (nuns' depositions) [S5 p.605, citing Boll. depositions].
- Alba, Oct 1582: the dead tree flowers; reported in the canonization bull per S5; WITNESS/APPROVED mention (I did not read the bull) [S5 p.605].
- Body: fragrance before and after death; flesh soft, limbs flexible [S5 p.605]. WITNESS.
- July 1583 (nine months): body found whole, flesh "white and soft", fragrance, "miraculous oil" on the limbs [S5 p.606]. WITNESS (Gracian); the oil is the part most often cited.
- 24 Nov 1585: unchanged again [S5 p.607]. WITNESS.
- Later openings to 1750 same report [S5 p.608]. The 1914 opening: still as before [S8]. 2024: examiners call the body "mummified" more than "literally incorrupt" [S8]. CONTESTED: "incorrupt" is a popular word. The radiographers also found severe spinal curvature [S8].
- The heart: removed by a lay sister; kept in crystal at Alba; wound shown by the seraph; three "thorns" from dust at the bottom, controversial [S5 p.607 footnote]. LEGEND/CONTESTED. Date of removal NOT READ.
- Apparitions: day after death, to the prioress of Veas; later to a young nun at Veas; to Gracian; to Teresita; WITNESS [S5 p.608-609].
- Miracles for the cause: S5 says many "wrought by relics" in quick succession and the process ended 1597 [S5 p.607, 610]; the specific miracles APPROVED for the beatification and canonization: NOT FOUND in my sources. The Dicastery page gives only the dates and popes [S3].

## 8. Other
Relics
- Main body: Alba de Tormes, Carmelite convent; heart and arm also there [S8; S5]. Alba kept an arm cut off in 1585 [S5 p.607]. "Severed arm" is therefore a Nov 1585 act by order of the Carmelite chapter, not a legend [S5 p.607].
- Left hand at Carmelite convent in Lisbon, "minus her index finger" [S8; S5 says "one of her hands"]. Right foot and jaw at Santa Maria della Scala, Rome; part of skull and teeth at San Pancrazio, Rome [S8]. Flesh and habit pieces in Madrid and elsewhere; blood and teeth as far as Puebla, Mexico [S8].
- Who cut each part and when, apart from the 1585 arm: NOT READ. Hand "Franco" story from my search: not confirmed, not used.
Patronage
- S2: Catholic writers; lace makers; invoked against heart disorders. The assigned "Spanish writers, headache sufferers, those in religious ridicule": NOT in S2/S3. Others unconfirmed: to check. (Butler p.316-317 notes headaches in her own illness.)
Doctor of the Church
- First woman, 27 Sept 1970, by Paul VI; he says it is a title of doctrine, not of hierarchical office [S4].
- Paul VI reasons: holiness proclaimed 1622; "eminence of doctrine" cited from Benedict XIV; the charism of wisdom; she is the teacher of prayer [S4].
- The Dicastery lists the title in her profile [S3].
