# Teresa of Avila — notes, focus: own-words
Researcher: own-words agent. Date: 2026-10-01. All text below was read in this session from raw OCR (archive.org via fetch_text.py) unless marked "web summary".

## 1. Identity (own-words view only)
- Teresa de Cepeda y Ahumada, "Teresa de Jesús" (Teresa of Avila). Born Avila, Wed 28 March 1515 [S1 preface]. Father Alonso Sánchez de Cepeda; mother Beatriz de Ahumada, his second wife [S1 preface]. 
- Died Alba de Tormes, night of 4 Oct 1582 (eve/feast of St Francis; the calendar jump made the next day 15 Oct) [S3 intro: "Between nine and ten o'clock that night"]. Feast 15 Oct (Roman calendar) [playbook latin-iberia]. Calendar-jump fact: not read, check.
- Tradition: Catholic only (playbook). Canonized 1622, Doctor 1970: given in the task, not read by me. Tier A.

## 3. Timeline (own-words angle)
- 1515, 28 Mar: born Avila [S1 preface].
- c.1522, age ~7: she and brother Rodrigo (name not read: "one of my brothers") plan to go to the Moors "begging our way" to be beheaded; stopped by "our father and mother" being the problem [S1 ch1 §4]. Children chant "For ever, ever, ever" over eternity [S1 ch1 §5]. NEW-ish vivid.
- c.1527, age ~12: mother dies; she asks an image of Our Lady "to be my mother" [S1 ch1 §7].
- c.1529: romances/chivalry, misled by a cousin (annals) [S1 annals]. A friendship that "distressed my father and sister" [S1 ch2 area, §5]. Chapter not confirmed.
- 1531: sister Maria's marriage; Teresa sent to the Augustinian nuns [S1 preface].
- 1533 (Lewis annals: 2 Nov enters Incarnation; 3 Nov 1534 profession). CONFLICT: modern lives date entry 1535; annals here say 1533. Flag for writer. She wrote that leaving her father's house felt "as if every bone in my body were wrenched asunder" [S1 ch4? §1].
- 1537-39: illness; four days' trance in father's house; paralysed over two years; "Cuando comencé a andar a gatas, alababa a Dios" (crawling, I praised God) [S1 annals; S2 vol1 Vida p.36]. NEW as a quoted line.
- 1541-: gives up mental prayer ~ (annals "1541 lukewarm"); 1542 Christ appears in the parlour "stern and grave" [S1 annals]. She says she gave up prayer for years (twenty years: not read, check).
- c.1554-55, Lent: picture of Christ "most grievously wounded" in the oratory; she throws herself down weeping [S1 ch9 §1]. Same period: reads Augustine's *Confessions*, given to her though she had not sought them [S1 ch9 §8].
- 1556-58: visions begin; first rapture 1558; vision of Hell 1558 [S1 annals].
- 1559: transverberation: angel with long golden spear [S1 §16-17; S2 vol1 p.234]. Annals date 1559. Note: Spanish editor/ Báñez in margin of the autograph changed "Cherubines" to "Serafines" [S2 vol1 p.234 note]. NEW detail.
- 1560: vow always to do the most perfect (Foundations intro) [S4 intro].
- 24 Aug 1562: St Joseph's, Avila founded; recalled to Incarnation, must defend herself before provincial [S4 intro; S1 annals].
- 1562 June: first draft of Life finished [S2 vol1 colophon: "Acabóse este libro en Junio, año de MDLXII"].
- 1573, 24 Aug: begins *Foundations* at Salamanca by order of Fr Ripalda SJ [S4 intro].
- Medina del Campo foundation (Foundations ch3): the Sacrament reserved behind a door with walls "level with the ground"; she saw "His Majesty in the street in times so full of peril because of those Lutherans"; night spent hanging borrowed tapestry, nails found "along the walls" [S4 ch3 §9-11]. Year not read (1567, from memory, unchecked).
- 1576, 9 Sep, Toledo: letter to Mary of St Joseph (Seville) with quinces and dogfish [S5 CXII].
- 1577, 2 Jun (Trinity Sunday) to 29 Nov: writes *Interior Castle*; four real weeks of writing; five-month break [S3 intro]. Vision on Trinity eve of a crystal castle with seven rooms (witness Diego de Yepes) [S3 intro]. Written amid crisis: Incarnation nuns elect her prioress, 55 electors "declared excommunicated"; "No trace of these trials is to be found" in the book [S3 intro].
- 1582, 3-4 Oct, Alba de Tormes: death (see Scenes) [S4 intro].

## 4. Character
- Self-judgment: "I have never found one [saint] in whom I can have any comfort" because they never relapsed; she did [S1 prologue].
- Honest about her failings, distrust of her own pen: "I could never make myself understood... but at the cost of many words" [S1 §25].
- Cheerful travel company: "not fond of sad people, nor was she, nor did she want her companions to be"; "Dios me libre de santos encapotados" [S2 vol2 appendix p.~301-302, Ana de San Bartolomé testimony; also S2 vol5]. This is witness report, not her written words.
- Tired by "personas encapotadas y oraciones estrujadas" per Ana de la Encarnación [S2 vol6 intro]. Witness.
- Humour about her own love: "At one time, I laugh at myself; and at another, I am very much distressed" [S1 §25, water-love passage].
- Practical: orders a nun to plant a cabbage "sideways" to test obedience [S4 early ch, §6]. NEW?
- Letters: dry wit with nuns: "The tunny remained at Malagon, and a good thing too!" [S5 CXII]. Tells brother-in-law: "God prefers your health and obedience to your penances" [S5 p.254, recipient and number: letter to a nun/man? heading unconfirmed].
- Her fear of pious excess: "From silly devotions, God deliver us!" [S1 §24 ch13?].
- Critics/admirers: not my focus; Inquisition seized rumours (S1 preface: book in servants' hands of Princess of Eboli, Inquisition began search).

## 5. Quotations (exact copies; rights noted)
Q1. "When I read of martyrdom undergone by the Saints for the love of God, it struck me that the vision of God was very cheaply purchased." Life, Lewis tr., ch1 §4. Public-domain. Spanish original in S2 vol1 (not copied).
Q2. "We had a pleasure in repeating frequently, 'For ever, ever, ever.'" Life ch1 §5 (Lewis). PD.
Q3. "When my mother died, I was about twelve years old - a little less... I went in my affliction to an image of our Lady, and with many tears implored her to be my mother." Life ch1 §7 (Lewis). PD.
Q4. "The pain I felt when I left my father's house was so great, that I do not believe the pain of dying will be greater - for it seemed to me as if every bone in my body were wrenched asunder." Life, ch4(?) §1 (Lewis). PD. Chapter unconfirmed.
Q5. "Cuando comencé a andar a gatas, alababa a Dios." Libro de la Vida, ed. Silverio 1915, vol 1 p.36 (her original). PD (Spanish). Own English: "When I began to crawl on all fours, I praised God."
Q6. "It was a representation of Christ most grievously wounded; and so devotional, that the very sight of it, when I saw it, moved me... I threw myself on the ground beside it, my tears flowing plenteously." Life ch9 §1 (Lewis). PD.
Q7. "At this time, the Confessions of Saint Augustine were given me. Our Lord seems to have so ordained it, for I did not seek them myself." Life ch9 §8 (Lewis). PD.
Q8. "I saw in his hand a long spear of gold, and at the iron's point there seemed to be a little fire... he seemed to draw them out also, and to leave me all on fire with a great love of God." Life, transverberation §17 (Lewis). PD. Spanish: "Víale en las manos un dardo de oro largo, y al fin de el hierro me parecía tener un poco de fuego." S2 vol1 p.234. PD.
Q9. "so surpassing was the sweetness of this excessive pain, that I could not wish to be rid of it." Life §17 (Lewis). PD.
Q10. "I pray God of His goodness to make him experience it who may think that I am lying." Life §17 (Lewis). PD.
Q11. "that no es otra cosa oración mental, a mi parecer, sino tratar de amistad, estando muchas veces tratando a solas con quien sabemos nos ama." Camino de Perfección, S2 vol3 (page not noted; it is a chapter on prayer, text read). Spanish original, PD. Own English: "mental prayer is, to my mind, nothing but friendly dealing, many times alone, with the one who we know loves us." Do NOT use Peers.
Q12. "From silly devotions, God deliver us!" Life §24 (Lewis; Spanish "de devociones a bobas nos libre Dios", S2 vol1). PD.
Q13. "A beginner must look upon himself as making a garden, wherein our Lord may take His delight, but in a soil unfruitful, and abounding in weeds." Life, water-garden passage §10 (Lewis). PD. Chapter not noted (ch11 from memory, unchecked).
Q14. "His Majesty has been to me a veritable Book, in which I saw all truth." Life §6 (Lewis). PD.
Q15. "Who can look upon our Lord, covered with wounds, and bowed down under persecutions, without accepting, loving, and longing for them?" Life §7 same chapter (Lewis; also in S3 scan p.300). PD.
Q16. "To make rapid progress and to reach the mansions we wish to enter, it is not so essential to think much as to love much." Interior Castle, Fourth Mansions p.33 of the scan (Stanbrook/Zimmerman 1921). PD. Spanish: "no está la cosa en pensar mucho, sino en amar mucho" (quoted in S2 vol4 editor note; the autograph text not copied).
Q17. "Love does not consist in great sweetness of devotion, but in a fervent determination to strive to please God in all things." Interior Castle, Fourth Mansions (Stanbrook 1921). PD.
Q18. "Both Martha and Mary must entertain our Lord and keep Him as their Guest, nor must they be so inhospitable as to offer Him no food." Interior Castle, Seventh Mansions §17 (Stanbrook 1921; section title "Both Martha and Mary must serve our Lord"). PD.
Q19. "Even if you are in the kitchen our Lord moves amidst the pots and pans, helping us both within and without." Foundations ch5 (Lewis 1913). PD. Spanish: "entre los pucheros anda el Señor, ayudándoos en lo interior y exterior." S2 vol5. PD.
Q20. "Nada te turbe, / Nada te espante, / Todo se pasa, / Dios no se muda, / La paciencia / Todo lo alcanza; / Quien a Dios tiene / Nada le falta: / Sólo Dios basta." Poesías IX "Eficacia de la paciencia", S2 vol6 p.~(ch. Poesías). Spanish original. PD. Own English: "Let nothing trouble you, let nothing frighten you. All things pass. God does not change. Patience gains everything. Whoever has God lacks nothing. God alone is enough."
Q21. "Pues que Dios está en mis manos / Y yo en las manos de Dios." S2 vol6 Apéndices, p.545, a poem in the appendix whose attribution is not established in my reading (editor's context: Apéndice, verses on divine love). Use with care; probably drop.
Q22. "Búscate en mí... Porque tú eres mi aposento, / Eres mi casa y morada." S2 vol6, Poesías VIII (heading "Vejamen": "Búscate en mí"). Spanish PD.
Q23. "Dios mío! what a sight is that soul which Thou givest up to suffer!" Foundations ch3 §11 (Lewis; exact: "O my God! what a sight is that soul which Thou givest up to suffer!"). PD.
Q24. Death words reported: "O my Lord, it is time to go... But I am a child of the Church." Reported in S4 intro (Zimmerman). Witness account, not her written words.
Q25. "The tunny remained at Malagon, and a good thing too!" Letter CXII, Toledo, 9 Sept 1576, to Mary of St Joseph (Stanbrook 1921). PD.

### Wrongly credited lines (flags)
F1. "Christ has no body now but yours" etc. I searched the full text of Life (Lewis), Interior Castle, Foundations (Lewis), the Peers Camino, letters vol 2, and BMC vols 1-6 for "no body", "no hands", "hands but", "eyes with which": zero hits. Wikiquote lists it as Disputed [S6]. Web summary (S7, not raw): attributed to Mark Guy Pearse (first part) and Sarah Eliza Rowntree (later addition) by Mary Luti. Wikiquote also cites Annie Johnson Flint's hymn "The World's Bible" with similar words (Flint 1866-1932) [S6]. Say: "not in any of her writings; no source before the 20th century found". To check: first print date.
F2. "More tears are shed over answered prayers than unanswered ones." Wikiquote [S6]: first credited to Teresa by Truman Capote, McCall's, Nov 1967 (Gloria Steinem interview); likely a variant of Wilde, *An Ideal Husband* act 2. Not found in her texts.
F3. "Let nothing disturb you" (Nada te turbe). Genuine, with a caveat. BMC editor Silverio: she kept these verses in her breviary when she died at Alba; Gracián wrote in it that the breviary was hers and signed it [S2 vol6 Poesías IX note]. The editor says the longer poem beginning "Eleva el pensamiento, / Al cielo sube" is "más bien" a gloss by a later Carmelite, not hers [S2 vol6 intro and Apéndice CXXI]. So: the 9-line letrilla is hers (per Silverio's edition; autograph status not checked); the long "Eleva el pensamiento" version is not. The English Longfellow version: not read.
F4. "God save us from sullen saints" (Dios me libre de santos encapotados). Comes from nun Ana de San Bartolomé's testimony [S2 vol2 appendix, vol5 text], not from Teresa's writings. Similar, authentic, from her own pen: Castle V, "muy encapotadas cuando están en ella" (S2 vol4 p.90 area).
F5. "Cuando perdiz, perdiz, y cuando penitencia, penitencia" ("when partridge, partridge; when penance, penance"): a Manzanares-area tradition told by the editor, not found in her writings [S2 vol5 note]. Mark legend.

## 6. Scenes (max 10)
1. Children at the Moors: two children begging their way to be beheaded; "For ever, ever, ever" repeated like a game [S1 ch1].
2. Twelve-year-old at an image of Our Lady asking her to be her mother [S1 ch1].
3. Leaving home: "as if every bone... were wrenched asunder" [S1].
4. Crawling out of paralysis on all fours, praising God [S2 vol1 p.36].
5. The wounded Christ in the oratory, she on the floor, "I would not rise up till He granted my petition" [S1 ch9 §1-3; "§3: I said to Him then that I would not rise up till He granted my petition"].
6. The angel with the golden spear: small, face on fire, "went about as if beside myself... only to cherish my pain" [S1 §16-18].
7. Medina del Campo night: tapestries nailed to bare walls, nails found along the walls, the Sacrament seen through chinks in a door [S4 ch3].
8. Toledo, June 1577: writing the Castle after Communion, face "resplendent"; a nun sees a trance that leaves a blank page filled with writing, which Teresa hides in the box [S3 intro, from Mother Mariana of the Angels].
9. Crystal castle vision on Trinity eve, told to Yepes who promised to say nothing until after her death [S3 intro].
10. Death at Alba: asks pardon for bad example, rises unaided at viaticum, "Can I have anything of my own? Will they not give me here a little earth?"; dies on Anne of St Bartholomew's shoulder after fourteen hours in ecstasy, between nine and ten at night [S4 intro].
