At seven, Teresa of Ávila slipped out of the city gate with her brother to be beheaded by the Moors, because the sight of God seemed cheap at that price. An uncle met them on the bridge. Years later, a charming nun whom visitors came to see, she gave up prayer for more than a year and struggled in it for years. Then she began founding convents, laughing at herself, while a preacher compared her to a fraud, the Inquisition took her book, and a papal envoy called her a restless gadabout. In 1970 she became the first woman Doctor of the Church. How? It began with one discovery: prayer is friendship.
