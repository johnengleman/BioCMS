# Check: St. Teresa of Ávila (fact check of 2026-10-01)

Originals kept as `biography.html.before-check`, `entry.json.before-check`, `summary.txt.before-check`.
Raw texts used: Lewis Life (1870) and Zimmerman Life, Foundations (1913), Interior Castle (1921), Stanbrook Letters I–IV, Ribera (Gili ed.), Silverio's Obras (BMC vols. 1–6), Procesos I–III, Vatican text of Multiformis sapientia and the Italian homily, Butler vol. 10 (earlier notes), Aleteia.

## Fixes in biography.html (mirrored in entry.json)

1. **Prayer given up "for years" (hook, ch. 4, ch. 6, ch. 7, summary, teachings, share text).** Teresa writes "a year and more without praying" (Life 7:17) and counts nearly twenty years "falling and rising" (Life 8). Now: gave up prayer for more than a year, struggled for years. Also removed "the parlour years" and the teaching claim that she "returned to prayer only after" the wounded Christ.
2. **Ch. 4, Báñez.** Added his own view (no faults beyond other good nuns, more prayer than most), so her harsh self-judgment reads as hers. "Wept over those years" now "wept over that" (the visitors).
3. **Ch. 4, Christ "in the parlour".** The parlour and the year come from the annals only; her text says she was with a person whose friendship was bad for her. Rewritten.
4. **Saints "in the lives of the saints".** Her words are "among the saints who were converted". Fixed.
5. **Ch. 5, "never been enraptured since … in public at least".** Her words are "I have never been enraptured since. It is true that it was not long ago." Quoted in full; "in public at least" removed.
6. **Ch. 5, "she thought" spiritual men easiest to fool.** This is Báñez's report. Attributed.
7. **Ch. 2, "Nobody carried her there".** Replaced with her own account (she talked a brother into becoming a friar; they set out early one morning).
8. **Ch. 3.** "Soon after her vows" (it was after Becedas and years later); wax "put on her eyes" (she found drops of wax on her eyelids); grave open "a day and a half"; Ana de la Encarnación "remembered" (she repeats what she heard: "esto oyó decir").
9. **Ch. 3 end.** "judged most harshly of all" softened.
10. **Ch. 8.** Cut the "three months" remark (Ana de la Trinidad, 1597, hearsay; the first draft had it as Ana de la Encarnación).
11. **Ch. 9, Medina scene.** Matched to Foundations ch. 3: the tapestries were the owner's steward's; the nuns saw Mass through the chinks of a door; walls "level with the ground" seen by daylight. Removed "seen from the street through the chinks".
12. **Ch. 9, Medina preacher.** Báñez had it from Pedro Fernández OP; Fernández objected. Attribution fixed.
13. **Ch. 9, "dos piedras vivas".** It is Ribera's phrase, not hers; also the Duruelo description is Ribera's.
14. **Ch. 10, hour-glasses.** Antonio's five hour-glasses were what he had gathered when he came to tell Teresa at Valladolid, not a Lent 1569 detail. Moved. Cut the Valladolid sickness, as part of the length trim.
15. **Ch. 11, Salamanca night.** Teresa and Mary were not "lying on straw" when Mary spoke; Mary kept looking about, afraid of hiding students. Rewritten from Foundations ch. 19. "Years later" corrected to "afterward".
16. **Ch. 11, Anne of Jesus and the rain.** The quote comes from a nineteenth-century life of her (Zimmerman's note), not her memory. Attributed. Name unified as Ana de Jesús.
17. **Ch. 11, "personas encapotadas y oraciones estrujadas".** Said by Ana de Jesús (Salamanca, 1597), not Ana de la Encarnación.
18. **Ch. 12, Princess of Eboli.** Ana de Jesús did not say the princess denounced the book. Zimmerman's note (the chronicle, Reforma) says the princess's conduct led the Inquisition to demand it. Rewritten. Ana de Jesús is now cited only for the messenger at Veas.
19. **Ch. 12, "twelve years".** Gracián says "casi doce": now "almost twelve".
20. **Ch. 12, Juan de la Miseria.** "Only portrait" removed (no source says "only"); the remark rests on Fuente via Zimmerman, so "she is said to have laughed".
21. **Ch. 13, Lorenzo.** "Royal treasurer" now "treasurer to the viceroy" (Zimmerman).
22. **Ch. 14, trance and "resplendent".** Said by Mary of the Nativity; Mariana of the Angels reports hearing the trance story from her. Fixed.
23. **Ch. 14, poem.** The lines quoted as "Búscate en mí … porque tú eres mi aposento" joined the Vejamen phrase to a different poem. Now the poem's own consecutive lines ("si hallarme quisieres, a Mí buscarme has en ti. Porque tú eres mi aposento…"; Poesías VIII).
24. **Ch. 14, Quiroga.** He spoke to Teresa, with Gracián present, not to Gracián alone.
25. **Ch. 15, Granada and Burgos.** "In December 1581 … mules" corrected to late 1581, at Ávila, "mounts" (Ribera); the Burgos foundation was April 1582 (Foundations ch. 31, first Mass 19 April), not July.
26. **Ch. 15, last letter.** "Last surviving" now "last dated".
27. **Ch. 15, road to Alba.** "Without having eaten" now "nothing to take for the road" (Ana); "dried figs"; the distressed nun is Ana de San Bartolomé. Cut "Teresita's brother … too short" in the length trim.
28. **Spanish accents.** Checked against the Gili text of Ribera and the Procesos. Fixed "mas ha de veinte años" to "más ha de veinte años". Spelling "fémina" (Ribera). "Vía un ángel" (Silverio's reading, as the BMC text cited).
29. **Ch. 16, first biographer.** Yepes (1587) came before Ribera (1590): "early biographer". Also fixed in "How This Life Was Researched" and the miracles intro.
30. **Ch. 16.** "As if she needed no one" (hook) cut; Isabel de la Cruz took Teresa's hands and laid them on her own head (María de San Francisco); "no defence … at the end" now "not recorded as defending".
31. **Ch. 16, "the year they gained the ten days".** Said by Leonor de San Gabriel (Sanlúcar process, 1596), who knew her at Seville, not "a nun of Alba".
32. **Ch. 17, Rótulo miracle.** "Given up for dead / came back to life" now "life despaired of"; the child "revived", as the article says.
33. **Ch. 17, relics.** 2024 findings were reported by two radiographers in an interview (Aleteia), not "examiners' X-rays"; the link of the curved spine to the one-finger months is dropped.
34. **Ch. 17, Gracián / Clement VIII.** "is reported to have said" (one editor's note).
35. **Ch. 17, Paul VI.** "We have conferred, or rather We have recognized" is from the homily, not the letter. Marked "in his homily".
36. **Note on the Sources.** Portrait remark, rain, "sullen saints" (Ana's own account), Capote ("earliest source we found"), "Christ has no body now but yours" ("we did not find it") now worded to what we found.
37. **How This Life Was Researched.** "More than twenty of her letters" now "about fifteen" (fifteen counted); "Twelve witnesses" dropped (not countable); "a 2024 report" now "a 2024 news report".

Length trim to stay under 10,000 words: cut the "young women … three months" paragraph, the Valladolid illness, the neighbours' doorway at Toledo, the set-task line, and one Teresita line. Lint: 10,000 words, no MUST FIX, no CHECK lines.
