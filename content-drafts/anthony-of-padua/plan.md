# Plan: Anthony of Padua (tier A)

## The driving question

Fernando of Lisbon wanted to give Christ his blood. When the relics of five friars killed in Morocco passed through Coimbra, he left a learned and comfortable life to become a Franciscan and die as they had died. God refused him: illness in Morocco, a storm that threw him onto Sicily, and an Assisi chapter where, of all the friars, he alone was asked for by no one. He hid his learning in a hermitage kitchen. Obedience, not ambition, forced him to speak at Forlì, and from then on he gave his voice instead of his blood, preaching until his body broke at thirty-six. The hook raises the question with the incorrupt tongue found in 1263 and the friar nobody wanted in 1221. The ending answers it: the martyrdom he asked for became a life used up in words, ending with "I see my Lord", and the Church kept, of all of him, the tongue.

## Why he is famous

The Franciscan preacher whom thirty thousand people stood silent to hear, canonized less than a year after his death, and invoked for lost things ever since.

## What set him apart

A canon who knew the Fathers by heart hid that learning in a kitchen until he was ordered to speak. His preaching was gentle and severe at once: he drew sermons from vines, spiders and cranes, and he called the rich men of his day clothed in the blood of the poor.

## The hook

Scene at the edge plus paradox: in 1263 the friars open his grave and find the flesh turned to dust but the tongue fresh and red; Bonaventure kisses it. Then the turn back to 1221: at the Assisi chapter, Anthony alone was asked for by no one. How did the friar no one wanted become the voice of Padua, and why did a man who wanted to die for Christ give him his voice instead? Answered in "Thirty Thousand and Not a Sound", "I See My Lord", and "The Tongue in the Dust".

## Chapters

1. **A Boy Beside the Cathedral** — Lisbon, the canons, the move to Coimbra; memory in place of books. Quote: none (inline "like another Abraham"). Ends: 1220, the martyrs' relics arrive.
2. **Five Friars Dead in Morocco** — Infante Pedro, the war-horse image, the begging friars, the canon's taunt. Block quote: "When you hear that I am a saint…". Ends: he asks to go to the Saracens.
3. **The Friar Nobody Wanted** — illness in Morocco, Sicily, the 1221 chapter, Gratian. Block quote: Rigauld on the palm of martyrdom. Ends: sent to Monte Paolo.
4. **The Kitchen Friar at Forlì** — crypt cell, pots and feet, the ordination meal. Block quote: Rigauld, "not of man, but of God". Ends: ordered to preach.
5. **A Heretic Thirty Years Blind** — Bologna and Francis's letter (disputed), Rimini, the poisoned dish, the fish and the horse as later stories. Block quote: the poisoned dish. Ends: France.
6. **Francis at the Door in Arles** — the vision, Monaldo. Ends: Limousin.
7. **"I Speak to Thee, Mitre-Bearer"** — Limoges, Brive, Bourges, the rain. Block quote: "Fear not…". Ends: the 1230 chapter calls him back.
8. **Sermons Written in Winter** — freed to preach, Ark of the Covenant, the vineyards. Block quote: the dove in the cleft. Ends: Lent 1231 begins.
9. **Cloth Dyed With Sweat and Blood** — usurers, rich men, priests. Block quote: the purple of the poor. Ends: the crowds outgrow the churches.
10. **Thirty Thousand and Not a Sound** — Lent 1231, the robber, the devil at his throat. Block quotes: the silence; the robber. Ends: the hill over Padua.
11. **A Cell in a Walnut Tree** — Camposampiero, Tiso, the cart. Ends: Arcella.
12. **"I See My Lord"** — death. Block quote: the children's cry. Ends: the city in arms.
13. **Four Days of Fighting** — the body, the bridge of boats, burial, the first cures, the candles. Ends: the envoys to Rome.
14. **A Saint Within a Year** — Spoleto 1232, the dream, the aunt in Coimbra. Ends: the grave opened again.
15. **The Tongue in the Dust** — 1263, 1981, Doctor of the Church. Block quote: Rigauld on the tongue.
16. **The Finder of Lost Things** — the stolen notes, the patronage, the ending on the dove's nest. Block quote: the dove (or Coleridge on the only grace he asked for himself).
