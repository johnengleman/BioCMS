# Plan: St. Sergius of Radonezh

## The driving question

A plowman walks a long way to see a prophet and finds a man in a patched robe digging a garden: "I came to see a prophet, and you show me an orphan." Both were true. Bartholomew, a ruined boyar's son who could not learn to read, wanted only God, a hut in the forest, and poverty. The world kept coming to him: hungry brothers who grumbled, a patriarch who wanted a common life, a metropolitan who offered him a gold cross and his see, a grand prince who asked whether to march against the Horde. How did the orphan become the "Hegumen of the Russian land" without ever stopping being the orphan? He let them come and stayed the servant: he built a porch for moldy bread, would not let a spring carry his name, told a father that his child had only been cold, and refused the gold. Grace answered his poverty with abundance: fresh loaves at the gate, and the Mother of God's promise that she would not leave his monastery.

The hook raises the question (prophet or orphan). Chapters 7 to 11 answer it in acts. The ending resolves it: the boy who could not read is the patron of students, and the monastery has outlived the Horde, the Soviet commission, and the war.

A thread of bread runs through the life: the blessed bread under the oak, the bear's crust, the moldy sieve, the loaves at the gate, the promise of abundance.

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: the founder of the Trinity monastery near Radonezh, called the Hegumen of the Russian land, who blessed Grand Prince Dmitri against Mamai.
Set apart: he joined the hardest poverty to a common life with no private property, and his disciples carried it into new monasteries. He hid every sign of grace he could, and asked witnesses to keep silent while he lived.

## The hook

Type: broken picture. The plowman at the fence gap, the brothers who want to throw him out, Sergius's bow to the ground, the prince whose guards push the peasant aside. Answered in "Moldy Bread and Fresh Loaves" (why he looked like an orphan) and "One Rule Is Missing" (the gold cross refused), closed in the last chapter.

## Chapters

1. **A Prophet or an Orphan?** Hook, thesis. Pull: three cries before his birth.
2. **The Boy Who Could Not Read.** Cries in the womb; fasting infant; school failure; the elder under the oak. Block quote: the elder's prophecy (S1, our English). Pull: "a dwelling of the Holy Trinity".
3. **A Boyar Hanged in Rostov.** Fasting boy and his mother; ruin; Radonezh; parents' death; inheritance to Petr. Pull: Stefan and the forest.
4. **Logs on Their Shoulders.** Hut and church; Trinity name explained; Stefan leaves; tonsure. Pull: alone at night.
5. **A Bear at the Stump.** Demons, wolves, the bear. Pull: men begin to come.
6. **A Knock on the Cell Window.** Warnings; twelve brothers; Theodore; night rounds. Pull: the bread runs out.
7. **Moldy Bread and Fresh Loaves.** Four days of hunger; Daniil's porch; the knock at the gate; the refused cloth. Pull: water.
8. **Do Not Call It My Spring.** The spring; the child who "was only cold". Pull: the father could not keep silent; a letter from Constantinople.
9. **One Rule Is Missing.** Common life; daughter houses; the angel at the liturgy; Alexis and the gold cross. Pull: Mamai.
10. **A Letter on the Road.** Dmitri; the letter (block quote, S4 public domain); the later Kulikovo tradition; Klyuchevsky (block quote, S4); Kuchkin. Pull: a visit of another kind.
11. **Do Not Be Afraid, Chosen One.** The Mother of God (block quote, S1); fire in the chalice; birds. Pull: the end approaches.
12. **A Face Not Like the Dead.** Nikon; last teaching; death 1392. Pull: thirty years in the ground.
13. **Water Presses My Body.** 1422; glorification; Maxim the Greek. Pull: 1919.
14. **A Lock of Hair, 1919.** Soviet opening; head tradition; Solikamsk; 1946.
15. **Hegumen of the Russian Land.** Feasts; patronage; ends on the boy, the bread, and the promise.
