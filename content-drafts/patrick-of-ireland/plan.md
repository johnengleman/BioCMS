# Patrick of Ireland: plan

## The driving question

Patrick was carried off from his father's farm at sixteen and spent six years as a slave in Ireland. He escaped, came home, and his parents begged him never to leave again. Then he went back, of his own will, to the people who had enslaved him, and stayed until he died, poor, in daily danger, and accused at home by his own elders and his dearest friend. Why? What stood in the way was everything: his family, his elders, his rank, his poor Latin, and his own sense that he was "a stone lying in deep mud". The resolution is his own: God lifted the stone, and he cannot keep silent about the gift. The Confessio, written to answer his accusers, turns out to be a thanksgiving.

The hook raises the question with the betrayal (an old fault confessed to a friend, used against him thirty years later). The ending answers it with the dream of the "We" (God taking his side), the Confessio as thanks, and the scribe's note at the end of the Book of Armagh.

## Why famous, what set him apart

Famous: he is the apostle and patron of Ireland, whose day, 17 March, is kept around the world.

Set apart: he is the only one of the early missionaries to Ireland whose own voice survives, in two short Latin texts. He was a former slave who went back to the land of his slavery, and he spoke out for slaves and captives, with a fierce letter to a Christian king's soldiers.

## Hook

Scene at the edge plus paradox: the friend who had told him "Look, you are to be given the rank of bishop" shames him in public, in his absence, with a fault from his boyhood; the old bishop, ashamed of his Latin, sits down to answer. Answered in "We Have Seen With Displeasure" and "This Is My Confession".

## Chapters

1. A Sin Confessed Thirty Years Before. The betrayal; thesis; the question. Quote: Conf. 1. Pull: back to the farm.
2. Taken From His Father's Farm. Family, the raid at sixteen. Quote: stone in deep mud (Stokes). Pull: the slave on the hill.
3. A Hundred Prayers a Day. Slavery, prayer, the dream. Quote: Conf. 16. Pull: "your ship is ready".
4. Your Ship Is Ready. The port; the captain says no; called back. Pull: the wilderness.
5. Twenty-Eight Days in a Wilderness. The taunt, the pigs, the honey, the rock, "Helias". Quote: the shipmaster. Pull: captured again.
6. The Voice of the Irish. Sixty nights; home; the parents' plea; Victoricus. Quote: Conf. 23. Pull: what happened next is a blank.
7. The Years He Left Blank. The Dicta; Muirchú's Gaul (marked); deacon, bishop; gifts with tears; the friend's words. Quote: Letter 10. Pull: he crosses.
8. Where No One Had Baptized. The mission as he tells it; the noble girl. Pull: the cost.
9. Fourteen Days in Irons. Money, kings, irons, fear. Quote: the shoe. Pull: the raid.
10. Chrism Still on Their Foreheads. The Letter to Coroticus. Quotes: Letter 3, Letter 17. Pull: the blow from home.
11. We Have Seen With Displeasure. The betrayal and the dream. Pull: he writes.
12. This Is My Confession. The Confessio as thanks; blood without a grave. Quote: Conf. 59. Pull: silence after him.
13. Fire on the Plain of Brega. Muirchú and the Tripartite, marked: Díchu, Miliucc, the Easter fire at the Graves of Fíacc's Men, Tara.
14. Two Oxen and a Grave. Muirchú's death; Downpatrick; 1185, 1186 (Cusack), 1315, 1538. Quote: Columba's prophecy.
15. On the Seventeenth of March. Martyrology 1846, Bede, John XXIII 1961, Nigeria, Orthodox; the colophon; the sun. Quote: Martyrology; Conf. 60.
Then A Note on the Sources (dates, Two Patricks, items not his), How This Life Was Researched, Sources.

## Open loops

1. What did the friend do, and how did Patrick bear it? (ch. 1, answered ch. 11)
2. Why did he go back? (ch. 1, answered ch. 6 and ch. 12)
3. "Even if I have no grave" (ch. 12, answered ch. 14: the oxen, Downpatrick, the doubted grave)
4. The boy who did not know God (ch. 2, answered ch. 15: the Martyrology and the colophon)

## Length

The evidence is thin: two short texts by Patrick, then Lives 230 years later. Target about 7,500 words. The later Lives get one chapter and are marked by source and date.

## Revisions by the second writer (2026-10-01)

- Kept the driving question, the hook, the order and the open loops.
- The snakes, the shamrock, the Breastplate, "Be Thou My Vision" and the "Prayers of Patrick" appear only in A Note on the Sources (caller's rule 2), not in the hook or the last chapter.
- Ch. 2: "I sold my noble rank" moves to ch. 7, where it marks the cost of going back.
- Ch. 13: the fire stays at the Graves of Fíacc's Men on the plain of Brega; Slane is named only for Ercc's relics. The Bachall Ísu is given as a Tripartite story. The Croagh Patrick Lent is given as a Tripartite story (the notes date it there, 10th/11th c., not 12th).
- Ch. 14: 1186 translation attributed to Cusack; 1185 find to Butler, Baring-Gould and Cusack; the staff's burning to the Annals of Loch Cé as Cusack gives it.
- Ch. 15: Orthodox calendar date left out (unchecked).
- Block quotes: Conf. 1, 12, 16, 23, 24, 29, 59; Letter 3, 17; the 1846 Martyrology; Columba's verse (11).
