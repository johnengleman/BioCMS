The Orthodox Church honors Saint John Maximovitch as a wonderworker. This list gathers the miracles his Church remembers as God’s works through his prayers, in his lifetime and at his holy relics. The people who were there told them, and they were written down by bishops and priests of his Church and in its booklets. Each account names its teller, its source, and its status.

“Recorded by his Church” means that a bishop, a priest, a booklet, or an official report of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia gave the account. “Eyewitness memoir” means that a person who saw it wrote it down under their own name. “Printed testimony” means a letter that the cathedral printed. “Popular account” means a story his people tell and his Church’s writers have set down, with no witness named. Questions of date and source are gathered in a short note at the end.

During Saint John’s life

Saint John’s swollen leg heals, Shanghai

A witness quoted in Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John told this story. Saint John’s leg swelled badly, and the doctors feared gangrene. They ordered him to the hospital. He refused, because he would not miss a service. The parish council pleaded with him for a long time until he agreed. He entered the Russian hospital on the morning before the feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross. By six o’clock that evening he had limped to the cathedral, and he served. In a day the swelling was gone.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by a witness in the Life of Saint John, which prints it among his Shanghai years and gives no year.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

The Holy Gifts and a woman with rabies, Shanghai

Olga Skopichenko told this story, and many people from Shanghai confirmed it. Mrs. Menshikova was bitten by a mad dog and came down with rabies. Saint John heard of it and came to the dying woman to give her Holy Communion. Then a fit of the disease seized her, and she spat out the Holy Gifts she had just received. The Holy Gifts are the Body and Blood of Christ, and they cannot be thrown away. Saint John picked them up and put them in his own mouth. The people with him cried out that rabies was terribly contagious. He answered them calmly that nothing would happen, because these were the Holy Gifts. And nothing did happen.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by Olga Skopichenko in the Life of Saint John, among his Shanghai years; no year given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco; John Sanidopoulos, St. John Maximovitch and the Woman Dying from Rabies.

Saint John recognizes a letter writer, Hong Kong

Lidia Liu told this story. Before she knew Saint John, she wrote to him. She asked for help for a widow with children and asked his advice on a spiritual matter of her own, and she got no answer. A year passed. Then he came to Hong Kong, and she stood in the crowd that met him at church. He turned to her and said that she was the one who had written him the letter. He had never seen her before.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by the eyewitness Lidia Liu in the Life of Saint John; no year given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Light surrounds Saint John preaching, Hong Kong

Lidia Liu also told what she saw at a church service in Hong Kong. A short prayer service called a moleben was sung. Then Saint John preached from a lectern. She stood beside her mother, and they both saw a light around him about a foot wide. It reached down to the lectern and lasted a long time. When the sermon ended she told a friend what they had seen, and the friend answered that many of the faithful had seen it. Lidia’s husband stood a little way off and saw it too.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by Lidia Liu in the Life of Saint John; no year given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A tulip-shaped flame enters the chalice, 1939

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John tells of a woman of his flock who began to lose her faith in 1939 after many hardships. One day she came into church while Saint John was serving the Divine Liturgy, the Church’s chief service. At the moment when the bread and wine are consecrated, she saw a small flame come down into the chalice, the cup of Holy Communion. It had the shape of a large tulip. After this her faith returned, and she began to repent of her faint-heartedness.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The woman is not named, and the Life gives no place.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Saint John foretells a death, Shanghai hospital

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John tells that Saint John was once asked to give Holy Communion to a dying man in a Russian hospital in Shanghai. He took another priest with him. When he arrived he saw a cheerful young man in his twenties who was playing a harmonica. The young man was to go home the next day. Saint John called him over and said that he wanted to give him Communion right then. The young man confessed his sins at once and received Holy Communion. The priest asked why Saint John had stopped with a healthy young man instead of going to the dying one. Saint John answered that the young man would die that night. The other man was seriously ill, but he would live many years. It happened just as he said.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The priest who went with him is the witness; neither man is named, and no year is given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A skull fracture patient survives, Shanghai

The Life of Saint John tells that L. D. Sadkovskaya loved horse racing. One day she was thrown from her horse. She hit her head on a rock and lost consciousness. The doctors found her skull broken in places, and pieces of bone pressed on the brain. Her pulse was almost gone. They said she would not live until morning, and that she would die on the table if they operated. Her sister heard all this and ran to Saint John in despair. She begged him to save her sister, and he agreed. He came to the hospital and asked everyone to leave the room. Then he prayed there for about two hours. After that he called the chief doctor and asked him to examine her again. Her pulse was normal. The doctor agreed to operate at once, but only with Saint John present. The operation succeeded. She woke, asked for a drink, and lived a normal life for many years. The Life counts this among the many miracles worked by his prayers.

Status: Recorded by his Church. The Life says the case was written down in the archives of the County Hospital in Shanghai; no year given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A seven-year-old girl survives a twisted bowel

The 1994 booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia tells of a seven-year-old girl in an orphanage. She fell ill one night with a fever and screamed with pain. At the hospital the doctors found a twisted bowel. They told her mother that the girl was beyond help and could not survive an operation. The mother went to Saint John that night. He prayed before the altar through the night, and she prayed on her knees. In the morning he blessed her and told her that her daughter would live. The surgeon told the mother that the operation had gone well and that he had never seen such a case. The booklet says that only God could have saved her.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the 1994 glorification booklet. Neither the girl nor her mother is named. The booklet prints the story in its section of miracles after its part on Shanghai, and it gives no year.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

A teacher with appendicitis sits up

The 1994 booklet tells of a former teacher at a commercial school. Doctors found his appendix badly inflamed and said he might die on the operating table. His wife asked Saint John to pray. He went to the hospital and laid his hands on the man’s head. He prayed for a long time and left. The next day a nurse told the wife that the appendix had burst in the night and that her husband was sitting up in bed.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the 1994 booklet. The man is not named, and the booklet prints the story in its section of miracles after its part on Shanghai. It gives no year.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

A dying woman receives Communion and recovers

The 1994 booklet tells of a woman who lay seriously ill in a hospital and asked for Saint John. Her doctor said that she was dying and that the archbishop should not be disturbed. The next day Saint John came anyway. He told her that she was keeping him from his prayers, because it was time for him to serve the Divine Liturgy. He gave her Holy Communion and blessed her. She slept, and then she got well quickly.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the 1994 booklet. The woman is not named, and no place or year is given.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

A possessed man dies at peace, Shanghai

A father told this story, and the 1994 booklet printed it. His son was possessed. He hated all sacred things, and he split icons and crosses into thin sticks. For several years the father brought him to Saint John. Saint John prayed over him with a cross or the Gospel laid on his head, and he told the father not to despair. One day the son asked to go to the mental hospital at Minkhon, outside Shanghai. He said that the Spirit of God would cleanse him there. Two days later the father was visiting him when Saint John arrived. The son ran to him and begged him to drive the evil spirit away. Saint John prayed over him and gave him Holy Communion. The son went home. The next day he received Holy Communion once more, and he died in peace.

Status: Recorded by his Church. The father’s account is printed in the 1994 booklet; neither man is named, and no year is given.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

Saint John walks Shanghai at night in wartime

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John says that it was very dangerous to walk the streets of Shanghai at night under the Japanese occupation. Most people made sure to be home by dark. Saint John paid no attention to the danger. He went on visiting the sick and the needy at any hour of the night, and no one ever touched him. Zoya Bogdanov lived in his orphanage as a child, and she remembered the ten o’clock curfew. He came to the children late at night. He was never stopped, while other people were shot and killed. “We thought they didn’t see him,” she wrote.

Status: Recorded by his Church, with an eyewitness memoir by Zoya Bogdanov in the 2019 booklet. No dates are given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco; Benevolent Memorial Fund of Saint Archbishop John, 2019, Saint John (Maximovich), Wonderworker of Shanghai and San Francisco: Memories of the Living Saint John and Testimonials.

A wounded woman receives Communion at night

The Life of Saint John tells of Anna Lushnikova, the voice teacher who taught Saint John to breathe and speak more clearly. He paid her 20 dollars at the end of each lesson. The Life says that in 1945 she was badly wounded and lay in a French hospital. One very stormy night she felt that she might die, and she asked the nurses to call Saint John so that he could give her Holy Communion. The nurses refused, because the hospital was locked at night. She kept calling on him. About eleven o’clock he appeared in her ward. She could not believe her eyes, and she asked him whether this was a dream. He smiled, prayed, and gave her Holy Communion. She grew calm and slept, and in the morning she felt cured. No one believed that he had come, because the hospital was locked tight. But the patient in the next bed said that she had seen him too, and a 20-dollar bill lay under Anna’s pillow.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The Life gives the year 1945 and says he was then in France; see the note at the end.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A screaming patient receives Communion, Philippines

The 1994 booklet tells of a hospital in the Philippines, after his people had left China. Saint John heard terrible screams from far down the corridors. A nurse said they came from a hopeless patient who had been moved away because she disturbed everyone. The nurse advised him not to go because the woman smelled bad. He said that did not matter. He went to her and laid a cross on her head and prayed. Then he heard her confession and gave her Holy Communion. She stopped screaming and only moaned softly. When he came back some time later, she ran out to meet him.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the 1994 booklet. The woman is not named, and no date is given.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

The Tubabao camp and the typhoons, 1949

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John tells that about 5,000 refugees from China lived in a camp on the island of Tubabao in the Philippines in 1949. The island lies in the path of the seasonal typhoons. The Life says the camp stood for 27 months. In that time a typhoon threatened it only once, and that typhoon changed course and passed the island by. When a Russian spoke to the Filipinos of his fear of typhoons, they answered that there was no reason to worry. They said the archbishop blessed the camp from four directions every night. After almost everyone had left and been resettled, a terrible typhoon struck the island and destroyed the camp.

Status: Popular account, printed in the Life of Saint John; see the note at the end.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco; Russian Wikipedia, in Russian, Иоанн Шанхайский и Сан-Францисский.

Saint John on the White House steps

The 1994 booklet says that Saint John sat for days on the steps of the White House in Washington. It says this won permission for more than 5,000 refugees to enter the United States, and it calls the case well known. The historian Matthew Namee tells how Saint John went to Washington in September 1949 and was invited to testify before a Senate committee. Saint John came back again and again until the bill passed in 1950.

Status: Popular account, printed in the 1994 booklet with no date; see the note at the end.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation); Matthew Namee, Orthodox History, 2 July 2025, When a god visited Congress.

A skull fracture vanishes from x-rays, France

V. D. told of his brother, who lay in a hospital in France with a head wound. He said that many people knew there was no need to ask Saint John to visit someone. The Lord Himself showed Saint John where to go and to whom. An x-ray showed a large fracture of his brother’s skull, and he was in critical condition. Saint John did not know the brother, yet he found him in the hospital, prayed over him, and gave him Holy Communion. The next x-rays showed no fracture at all. The brother recovered very fast, and the doctor was dumbfounded.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The witness gives only his initials, and no year is given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Reader sees Saint John above the floor, Paris

The 1994 booklet tells of a reader named Gregory at the Church of All Saints of Russia in Paris. A reader is a man in the first rank of church service who reads the Scriptures aloud at services. Gregory had finished reading the prayers and went to the altar for instructions. Through a half-open side door he saw Saint John standing in a bright light, about 30 centimeters above the floor. The booklet adds that a nun of the Lesna convent told of the same thing.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the 1994 booklet. The reader is named Gregory. The nun is not named, and no date is given.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation).

A painful hand heals after a letter

Anna Hodyriva told of her sister Xenia Yarovoy, who lived in Los Angeles and suffered for a long time with a painful hand. Doctors and home remedies did not help. At last she turned to Saint John and wrote to him in San Francisco. Some time went by, and the hand was healed. She began to forget the pain. Later she visited San Francisco and went to the cathedral for a service. At the end Saint John held out the cross for the people to kiss. When he saw her, he asked how her hand was. He had never seen her before.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by Anna Hodyriva in the Life of Saint John; no year is given for the healing. The cathedral visit fell in his San Francisco years, 1962 to 1966.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A teenager recovers from lung surgery, San Francisco

Sergei Bogatsky came to the United States with his family in 1963, at the age of fourteen, and met Saint John at the old cathedral on Fulton Street. A few months later he entered San Francisco General Hospital with a serious illness, and three months of medicine did not help. Saint John visited him there from time to time, as he visited many sick people, and the boy looked forward to his visits. The doctors decided to remove the upper part of his left lung. His parents were very worried, and they asked Saint John for his prayers once more. He told them that everything would turn out well. Bogatsky woke from the anesthesia in intensive care and saw Saint John standing at his bedside, holding his hand and smiling. He recovered completely, and 57 years later he had never had any trouble from that illness. He writes that he has no doubt the surgery and the recovery succeeded through Saint John’s prayers to God for him, and that he will always be grateful.

Status: Eyewitness memoir, written by Sergei Bogatsky for the 2019 booklet.

Source: Benevolent Memorial Fund of Saint Archbishop John, 2019, Saint John (Maximovich), Wonderworker of Shanghai and San Francisco: Memories of the Living Saint John and Testimonials.

A woman survives a car crash

Anna S. told what happened to her sister Musia. A drunk young man drove into the door on Musia’s side of their car. A lung was punctured and a rib broken, and her face was so swollen that her eyes could not be seen. When Saint John came to her, she lifted her eyelid with her finger and saw him. She took his hand and kissed it. She could not speak, but tears of joy ran down her face. He visited her several times, and she began to improve. One day he came into the ward and said that Musia was feeling very poorly now. He closed the curtain around her bed and prayed for a long time. While he prayed, two doctors told Anna that they could not promise her sister would live until morning. Anna thanked God that her sister survived the night and was completely cured. Her family believes that Musia was saved by Saint John’s prayers.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by Anna S. in the Life of Saint John, among his San Francisco years; no year or city is given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Saint John visits an injured man, 1963

Mrs. L. Liu told how her husband was badly hurt in a car accident in San Francisco in 1963. He suffered terribly. She knew the power of Saint John’s prayers. She thought that her husband would recover if the archbishop came. But she did not dare to ask, because he had so many troubles of his own just then. Two days later Mr. T. drove Saint John to the house. Saint John stayed about five minutes, and from then on she believed that her husband would get well. Her husband passed through a sharp crisis, then began to recover, and he lived four more years. Afterward Mr. T. told her what had happened in the car. He had been driving Saint John to the airport when the archbishop told him to go to the Lius’ house. Mr. T. objected that they would miss the plane and that he could not turn around. Saint John asked him, “Can you take the life of a man upon yourself?” So Mr. T. drove him there, and Saint John was not late for his plane.

Status: Recorded by his Church. Told by Mrs. Liu in the Life of Saint John, and Mr. T. confirmed Saint John’s words.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Saint John foretells his death in Seattle, 1966

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John tells that he foretold his own death to three people. In the spring of 1966 the manager of his orphanage spoke of a meeting planned three years ahead, and Saint John said that he would not be there then. In May he told a woman who had known him for twelve years that he would die soon, at the end of June, in Seattle and not in San Francisco. On the evening before he left for Seattle he served a moleben for a man, and then he told the man that he would not kiss the archbishop’s hand again. Four days later he served the Divine Liturgy and prayed three hours in the altar. That day was 2 July 1966, and he reposed in Seattle before the wonderworking Kursk Root Icon of the Mother of God.

Status: Recorded by his Church. The woman is not named; Metropolitan Saint Philaret called her testimony worthy of complete confidence.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

After Saint John’s death

Six days in an open coffin, 1966

The 1994 booklet says that Saint John’s body lay six days in an open coffin in hot summer weather. Yet there was not the least smell of decay, and his hand stayed soft. Father Peter Perekrestov tells that the funeral in San Francisco was put off until the bishops could gather. Two of them drove three days from Jordanville, almost 3,000 miles. He says the body was not embalmed, and it showed no sign of decay before or after the funeral service.

Status: Recorded by his Church in the 1994 booklet, with the account of Father Peter Perekrestov.

Source: The Solemn Glorification of St. John the Wonderworker of Shanghai in San Francisco, 19 June / 2 July 1994 (booklet of the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, in Russian; our translation); Archpriest Peter Perekrestov, How We Opened the Relics of Saint John Maximovitch.

A student dreams of Saint John in vestments

Bishop Alexander’s Life of Saint John tells that soon after Saint John’s death his former student Father Amvrosy P. saw a dream or a vision. Saint John stood in Easter vestments, full of light and shining, and censed the cathedral. He blessed the priest and spoke one joyful word: “happy.”

Status: Recorded by his Church in the Life of Saint John, which calls it a dream or a vision. The dreamer is named by initials.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A doubting deacon dreams of Saint John

The Life tells of Father Constantine Z., who had long been Saint John’s deacon and was now a priest. Lately he had been angry with Saint John and had begun to doubt his righteousness. Within forty days of the death, he saw Saint John in a dream. Saint John was all in light, and rays shone around his head so brightly that the priest could not look at them. His doubts were gone.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The dreamer is named by initials.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

An orphanage manager dreams Saint John is alive

The Life tells of a dream of M. A. Shakhmatova, the manager of his orphanage. In the dream a crowd carried Saint John in a coffin into Saint Tikhon’s Church. He came to life and stood in the royal doors of the altar, and he anointed the people. The royal doors are the central doors of the screen before the altar. He told her to tell the people that although he had died, he was alive.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. The dreamer is named by initials.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Pilgrims at Saint John’s tomb before 1993

Before the glorification, Saint John’s tomb under the cathedral was a place of prayer. Prayers for the dead were sung there almost every day. People who asked his help read the book of Psalms, called the Psalter, over his coffin. Father Peter Perekrestov took part in the opening of the tomb in 1993, and he tells what the years before it were like. People went to the tomb. At first they prayed for him, and then they began to pray to him and to leave him their lists of names. Perekrestov says that many miracles happened there. His veneration grew from the people up, not from the bishops down.

Status: Eyewitness memoir, from an interview. It names no single case.

Source: Archpriest Peter Perekrestov, How We Opened the Relics of Saint John Maximovitch; Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Rusted coffin lock opens as bishop sings, 1993

Three bishops signed a report dated 11 October 1993 on the opening of Saint John’s tomb in San Francisco. Fifteen clergy and helpers took part, and all had fasted that day. Some had gone to confession, and others had served the Divine Liturgy. At nine in the evening they sang a panikhida, a service for the dead. Then Archbishop Anthony bowed to the ground and asked everyone’s forgiveness. The metal coffin had rusted through in many places. Hieromonk Peter Lukianov had kept its key for 27 years, but the rust stopped him from opening the lock. A hieromonk is a monk who is also a priest. The clergy would not use force in such a sacred task. Archbishop Anthony sang the hymn “We have no other Help,” and almost at once the lock opened.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the official commission report. Father Peter Perekrestov remembers the moment a little differently; see the note at the end.

Source: report of the commission, 1993, English reprint, Uncovering of the Relics of St. John Maximovitch; Archpriest Peter Perekrestov, How We Opened the Relics of Saint John Maximovitch.

Commission opens Saint John’s coffin, 1993

At ten past ten that night Archbishop Anthony opened the coffin with trembling and the fear of God. The face was covered, and everyone looked first at the white hands. Archbishop Anthony read Psalm 50 and lifted the cloth from the face, and all of them saw that it was incorrupt, which means that it had not decayed. The skin was white, and the beard was whole. The white vestments had turned green. Both legs were dark, with a mark where an old wound had been. There was neither a sweet nor a foul smell, only a slight smell of damp earth or rust. When the clergy lifted the body, it was light and stiff, not fragile as they had feared. The report says that after the uncovering of the face there was “an extraordinary silence.” It says that everyone felt a reverent peace, and that no one cried out in surprise or hurried. No one wanted to leave the relics, and they wanted only to stand beside them, pray, and touch them. Each of them venerated the holy relics in turn.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the official commission report signed by Archbishop Anthony, Archbishop Laurus, and Bishop Kyrill.

Source: report of the commission, 1993, English reprint, Uncovering of the Relics of St. John Maximovitch.

A sick boy touches Saint John’s hands, 1993

The commission report says that Archbishop Anthony then told the others about the two-year-old son of a diocesan priest, Father Yaroslav Belikow. The boy was very ill, and father and son were waiting not far away to venerate the relics. They sent for Father Yaroslav, and he carried his son Vsevolod down into the crypt and touched him to Saint John’s hands. Father Peter Perekrestov was there, and he tells what came of it. The boy was healed. Perekrestov says the boy is now completely healthy, plays college rugby, and is much taller than Perekrestov himself. The clergy sealed the new coffin and left the crypt at a quarter past eleven, thanking God for His mercy.

Status: Recorded by his Church in the commission report, with the eyewitness account of Father Peter Perekrestov.

Source: report of the commission, 1993, English reprint, Uncovering of the Relics of St. John Maximovitch; Archpriest Peter Perekrestov, How We Opened the Relics of Saint John Maximovitch.

A friend’s leukemia is healed after prayer

Victor Boyton told of a friend from Russia, a Muslim, who had leukemia and was losing his sight. The doctors agreed that in three months he would be blind. Boyton put a photograph of Saint John beside his vigil lamp and began to pray for his friend every day. After a short time the friend was healed of the leukemia and began to see normally, and the eye doctors were amazed. From then on he led a normal life and read without trouble.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. No year or place is given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

A woman recognizes the monk of her dreams

Archpriest Stephan Pavlenko told of his brother Paul, who lived for some years in Vietnam during the war. Paul placed wounded and orphaned children in orphanages or hospitals. He grew close to a Vietnamese woman named Kim En, who also helped these children and later became his wife. He told her about the Christian faith and the lives of God’s saints. She told him that in her hardest times a monk had come to her in dreams, comforted her, and told her what to do. One Easter season Father Stephan sent his brother some books and journals. Paul showed them to Kim, and when she saw the cover of one journal she cried out that this was the monk who had appeared to her in her dreams. The cover showed a well-known photograph of Saint John. Kim was baptized into the Orthodox Church with the name Kyra.

Status: Recorded by his Church, in the Life of Saint John. No year is given.

Source: Bishop Alexander (Mileant), Life and Miracles of St. John (Maximovich) of Shanghai and San Francisco.

Healings with oil from Saint John’s lamp

The 2019 booklet printed in San Francisco tells that many letters addressed to Saint John come to the cathedral where his holy relics lie. These letters are private and are not read, and they are placed under his relics. A vigil lamp burns at the relics all the time, and every year thousands of people from all over the world ask for oil from it. The cathedral sends small bottles of it with a note that says it is olive oil from the lamp at his relics. Many people write back to tell of the help they received, and the booklet prints their letters: healings in cases of cancer, surgery, and stroke.

Status: Popular account. The booklet prints the letters and e-mails that the cathedral received.

Source: Benevolent Memorial Fund of Saint Archbishop John, 2019, Saint John (Maximovich), Wonderworker of Shanghai and San Francisco: Memories of the Living Saint John and Testimonials.

A young man’s vision clears after anointing, 2018

A writer who signed CK told this story. CK is a colleague of the young man’s father. In late October 2018 the 29-year-old son of an attorney had blinding headaches, nausea, and neck pain. The emergency room at UCSF ruled out meningitis and sent him home with pills for nausea. Then his vision began to fail. He saw double, and it grew so bad that he could barely see. A neurosurgeon’s scans showed pockets of fluid pressing on the spine and skull, and a spinal tap did not ease the pain. After almost a month, father and son were desperate. CK brought the father a vial of Saint John’s oil and an icon. The father is a Catholic, and he said that he believed in the prayers of the saints and would use it. CK told him to make the sign of the cross on his son’s forehead with the oil and ask Saint John to help. Two days after Thanksgiving the father did so. The next day his son called to say that his vision was beginning to clear, and the day after that it was fully clear. Then the son went to the neurosurgeon, who told him that he had no explanation for the change. After that the son went back to work. CK ended the letter with glory to God, who is wondrous in His saints.

Status: Printed testimony, in the 2019 booklet. CK signed with initials and a state.

Source: Benevolent Memorial Fund of Saint Archbishop John, 2019, Saint John (Maximovich), Wonderworker of Shanghai and San Francisco: Memories of the Living Saint John and Testimonials.

A note on these accounts

These accounts come from the people who knew Saint John and from his Church. A few points of date and source belong here rather than in the stories. The Life of Saint John places Anna Lushnikova’s night Communion in 1945 and says he was then in France. Other sources place him in China in 1945 and in France only from 1951. The Life says the Tubabao camp stood for 27 months and that a typhoon threatened it only once. Russian Wikipedia says Saint John himself stayed on the island about three months. We found no other record of the typhoon. The story of the White House steps rests on the 1994 booklet alone, with no date. Matthew Namee’s account of the Senate hearing of September 1949 does not mention the steps. The story of Saint John above the floor in Paris rests on the same booklet, which names the reader Gregory and not the nun. For 1966, Father Peter Perekrestov does not say that he saw the body himself. Perekrestov also remembers the night of 11 October 1993 a little differently. He says Archbishop Anthony read Psalm 50 and the lid then opened with ease. In the signed report the lock opened as he sang “We have no other Help.” The report does not say what became of the boy Vsevolod; Perekrestov told of his healing in a later interview. The report notes that the white on the hands was mildew, and that after they were washed the relics were a dark bronze color. Sergei Bogatsky had medicine and surgery, and he writes that the surgery and his recovery came through Saint John’s prayers to God. Each bottle of oil goes out with a note that says only that it is olive oil from the lamp, and the cathedral makes no other claim. The letters about the oil and the account by CK are the writers’ own testimony.
