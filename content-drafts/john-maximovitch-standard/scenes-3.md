# Scene cards, group 3: St. John Maximovitch (SC-09 to SC-12, FN-1 to FN-3)

Source keys S1 to S16 follow `notes.md`. New keys are listed in full at the end (S17 to S25).
Rights note: S1, S5, S7, Orthodox Word 1994 and the 1963 newspapers are not confirmed public domain. Each card gives facts in our own words plus at most one short quotation per source.

## SC-09 — His room near Paris, 1950s
- Status: full (one eyewitness source; the priest stays unnamed)
- When and where: 1950s, after he reached Paris on July 21, 1951 [S4]. Mrs. Chertkova says she visited "on several occasions" when he lived "in the Cadet Corps building near Paris" [S1]. The notes and S4 place that building at Versailles from 1952. The small cell was on the top floor [S1].
- Who was there: Mrs. E. G. Chertkova, a parishioner who tells it (first name not given) [S1].
- What happened, in order:
  1. The room held a table, an armchair, a few chairs, and icons and a lectern with books in a corner [S1].
  2. It had no bed. He did not lie down to sleep. He prayed leaning on a tall stick with a cross-bar on top, sometimes on his knees. She thought he then slept a little on the floor [S1].
  3. While she talked he seemed to doze. When she stopped he said at once, "Continue, I hear you" [S1].
  4. Her parish had no permanent priest. A visiting priest served Vespers in 45 minutes. The usual service took 2.5 hours. The people were shocked and told Vladyka, hoping he would correct the priest [S1].
  5. He smiled and joked about it. She says he taught them not to judge [S1].
- Words spoken: "How difficult it is to please you people. I celebrate too long, and he too short!" Speaker: St. John, as Mrs. Chertkova remembers it. English only; no Russian text found. [S1, "Paris"; a second copy at S17 reads "How difficult is to please you people"]
  plain: "It is hard to please you people. I serve too long, and he serves too short."
- Testimony (short, facts-only rule): the quotation above. Do not copy Chertkova's longer account.
- Around the scene: He served the Dutch and French in their own languages. He collected the lives of Western saints from before the schism and sent a long list to the Synod [S1][S4].
- "Saint John the Barefoot": S1 says a priest in "one of the Catholic churches of Paris" told his young people: "today there walks in the streets of Paris a Saint — Saint Jean Nus Pieds (Saint John the Barefoot)." A Russian magazine gives the same words: "ходит святой Иоанн Босой" ("Saint John the Barefoot walks"), after "по улицам Парижа" ("through the streets of Paris") [S18, "Владыка и дети" section]. Neither source names the priest, the church, or the date.
- What changed: nothing in the plot. The scene shows his prayer without a bed and his humor.
- Not in the sources: the priest's name; the date of the Vespers; what Chertkova's parish was; his tone of voice; any levitation (a separate claim, S7).
- Sources: S1, S4, S17, S18.

## SC-10 — San Francisco, spring to August 1963
- Status: thin on the court (no court record found; the outcome rests on ROCOR-friendly accounts). Full on the quarrel.
- When and where: Late April to August 1963; the San Francisco courthouse, Holy Virgin Cathedral parish.
- Who: St. John; Archpriest Nicholas Dombrovsky, named with him in the complaint; Bishop Savva and Bishop Nektary, who stood with him; Metropolitan Anastassy; Archbishop Anthony of Los Angeles, blamed by his supporters [S19][S20].
- What happened, in order:
  1. He arrived Nov 21, 1962. The cathedral works had stopped. The Synod sent him as a peacemaker [S1][S7].
  2. Late April 1963: the Synod called him to New York and recalled him. About 700 people met at the Russian Center, 2450 Sutter Street, and voted to petition for him [S19, Chronicle Apr 28].
  3. Newspapers say a petition from about a dozen parishioners (11 in the News Call Bulletin, "a dozen" in the Chronicle) called him "generally unstable", said he had not checked the cathedral finances, and said he had favored the Soviet church in Shanghai. His supporters called it lies [S19, Chronicle May 2; News Call Bulletin Apr 29, May 2].
  4. His own report (later printed): a court officer served him after evening services with a complaint by 16 members of the parish council and helpers. It named him and Fr. Dombrovsky. The charge against the former council: it caused losses. The charge against him: he covered up what that council had done and called elections to bring its members back. A court order forbade him to call the parish meeting or sign contracts [S20]. (Corrected 2026-10-05: an earlier reading said he "covered the losses".)
  5. The next day a judge lifted the order on the meeting. The meeting and election went ahead [S20].
  6. A later hearing let him and the two bishops wait in the court library. The decision was put off [S20].
  7. July 9, 1963: the San Francisco Examiner printed a photograph of him in court with Bishops Savva, Nektary and Archbishop Leonty [S20 caption].
  8. August 13, 1963: the Council of Bishops confirmed him on the see [S4].
- Conduct: In April he told followers to obey the Synod and not act in haste (paraphrase, News Call Bulletin Apr 29 [S19]). In his report he says he held back angry crowds [S20]. S1 and S7 say he answered slander "without complaint" [S1][S7].
- Outcome: S20 (1994) and S1/S7 say he and the council were cleared. No court record or 1963 newspaper report of the verdict found. S20 adds that charges kept coming until his death. S4 names Archpriest Grabbe and the Synod chancery as the force behind the case.
- Words spoken: from his report, translated: "he delivered to me a copy of a complaint of 16 members of the Parish Council and of their collaborators, against 16 members of the former Parish Council, and against ME and Archpriest Nicholas Dombrovsky." [S20, Part 3]
  plain: "He handed me a complaint. Sixteen members of the parish council and their helpers had filed it. It named sixteen members of the former council, Archpriest Nicholas Dombrovsky, and me."
- What changed: confirmed on the see, but a long strain of attacks followed.
- Not in the sources: the judge's name; the verdict date; a courtroom scene; what he said on the stand.
- Conflict to flag: S20 intro says "July 1963"; his report dates the service of papers to May 24 / June 6 (Old Style / New Style).
- Sources: S1, S4, S7, S19, S20.

## SC-11 — Seattle, June 30 to July 2, 1966
- Status: full
- When and where: He died July 2, 1966 (June 19 Old Style) in the parish house beside St. Nicholas Cathedral, Seattle [S1][S7].
- Why in Seattle: S7 says he went with the miracle-working Kursk Root Icon of the Mother of God. S21 (Perekrestov) agrees.
- Who: He; the icon; former servers who ran in when they heard a crash [S7]. A man for whom he served a moleben four days before [S1]. A woman of twelve years' acquaintance [S1].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Spring 1966: told the orphanage manager, "I will not be here then," about a meeting three years off [S1].
  2. May 1966: told a woman he would die "at the end of June ... in Seattle" [S1].
  3. The evening before he left, four days before his death, he told a man after a moleben, "You will not kiss my hand again" [S1].
  4. July 2: he served the Liturgy, then spent three hours alone in the altar [S1][S7].
  5. He visited spiritual children near the cathedral with the icon, then went to his room [S7].
  6. A crash was heard. Former servers ran in and saw he was dying. They put him in a chair. He died peacefully before the icon [S1][S7].
  7. His body lay six days in an open coffin in summer heat, with no smell of decay, and his hand stayed soft [S7]. The funeral waited for the bishops to come; two drove nearly 3,000 miles from Jordanville [S21].
- Words spoken (S1; short total under 40 words): "I will die soon, at the end of June—not in San Francisco, but in Seattle." / "You will not kiss my hand again."
  plain: "I will die soon, at the end of June. It will be in Seattle, not in San Francisco." / "You will not kiss my hand again."
- Russian (S7): "пред Чудотворной Иконой Богоматери предал душу свою Богу" = "he gave his soul to God before the miracle-working icon of the Mother of God."
- What changed: his tomb became a place of prayer in the cathedral crypt [S4][S21].
- Not in the sources: who found him first by name; the doctor; any last words; the time of day.
- Sources: S1, S4, S7, S21.

## SC-12 — Crypt, San Francisco, night of September 28 / October 11, 1993
- Status: full
- When and where: Evening of Sept 28 O.S. (Oct 11 N.S.), 1993. Sepulchre under the Cathedral of the Mother of God "Joy of All Who Sorrow" [S5]. Perekrestov gives "October 13" [S21]; the report says Oct 11.
- Who: Archbishop Anthony of Western America; Archbishop Laurus; Bishop Kyrill of Seattle; seven priests, three deacons, a reader and the custodian, 15 in all [S5]. Hieromonk Peter (Lukianov) kept the key [S5].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Sept 30 (N.S. date given as Sept 17/30): Anthony and four helpers lifted the 400-pound lid and found no odor [S5].
  2. All fasted. At 9:00 p.m. a panikhida was served. Anthony asked everyone's forgiveness [S5].
  3. The metal coffin was rusted through. Peter's key, kept 27 years, would not turn. Anthony sang "We have no other Help" and the lock opened almost at once [S5].
  4. 10:10 p.m. Anthony opened the lid. The hands looked white (the report says it was mildew). The face was uncovered: skin white, beard whole, vestments green [S5].
  5. The report: legs dark, a mark where an old wound had been; no sweet or bad smell; body light and stiff [S5].
  6. Priest Yaroslav Belikow brought his sick two-year-old son, Vsevolod, who was touched to the hands [S5].
  7. The group moved the body to a wooden coffin and left at 11:15 [S5].
- Words (S5, 11 words): "After the uncovering of the face, there was an extraordinary silence."
  plain: "After they uncovered the face, there was a great silence."
- What became of the boy: the report gives no result. Fr. Peter Perekrestov, an eyewitness, says later that the boy "was healed", now "completely healthy", and plays college rugby; he does not say when he spoke [S21].
- What changed: the Synod went on with the glorification set for July 2, 1994 [S5].
- Not in the sources: the boy's illness; his reaction; the weather; the crowd above.
- Sources: S5, S21. For the commission wording, write "found preserved, the commission reported."

## FN-1 — Orphanage
St. Tikhon of Zadonsk orphanage, Shanghai. Began with eight children [S1][S4]. Founding year: no primary source found. A Russian magazine says it ran 1935–1951 and held over 3,500 orphans, Russian and Chinese [S22]. Pravenc (vol. 23, Litvinova) says about 3,500 Russian and Chinese children, with no year [S23, text on page 468963]. S1 says about 1,500 and up to 100 at once. Write "about 3,500, by the Orthodox Encyclopedia" and "at first eight children," and give no start year, or say "from the mid-1930s [S22]".

## FN-2 — Moscow 2008
The Council of Bishops of the Russian Church, June 24, 2008, "Определение ... по докладу ... Ювеналия": "Прославить в лике общецерковных святых ... святителя Шанхайского и Сан-Францисского Иоанна (Максимовича; 1896-1966), установив днем его памяти 19 июня (2 июля)."
plain: Glorify among the saints of the whole Church Saint John, Archbishop of Shanghai and San Francisco (Maximovitch, 1896–1966). Set his memorial day at June 19 (July 2).
It follows a report by Metropolitan Juvenaly, head of the Synodal Commission on canonization. Source: S24. Use "June 24, 2008". S2's "July 2, 2008" is wrong.

## FN-3 — Crypt, 1966–1993
Thin. No named pilgrim or dated healing found for 1966–1993. Fr. Peter Perekrestov says people went to the tomb before glorification, prayed to him, left name lists, and "numerous miracles occurred", with none named [S21]. The nearest named case is Vsevolod Belikow, October 1993 (see SC-12) [S5][S21]. The Rose/Podmoshensky 1987 book was not available. Do not use Victor Boyton's story of his friend with leukemia; it has no date and no link to the crypt [S1].
