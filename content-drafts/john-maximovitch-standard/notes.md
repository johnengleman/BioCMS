# St. John Maximovitch, Archbishop of Shanghai and San Francisco: research notes (tier B)

Researched 2026-10-04. Every fact below has a source I read in this session. No fact comes from memory or from the older pilot notes. Items I could not confirm are in section 9 without a source key.

## 1. Identity

- Names: Mikhail (Michael) Borisovich Maximovitch, "Misha" as a boy [S1][S4]. Monastic name John, taken in 1926 in honor of his distant relative St. John of Tobolsk [S1][S4]. Russian: Иоанн Шанхайский и Сан-Францисский, Иоанн (Максимович). Also "John the Wonderworker" [S3]. Nicknames: Vladyka John; in Paris "Saint Jean Nus Pieds" (Saint John the Barefoot), as quoted by a Catholic priest [S1]. NOT John of Tobolsk (his relative, glorified 1916 [S7]).
- Born: June 4, 1896, Adamovka, Izyum district, Kharkov province (now Donetsk region, Ukraine) [S1][S3][S4]. S4 prints "4 (16) июня" in its lead; the infobox says 4 June. I could not settle whether June 4 is Old Style. Treat the day as Old Style likely; check before printing a Gregorian day.
- Parents: Boris Ivanovich Maximovitch (1871–1954), marshal of nobility of Izyum district; Glafira Mikhailovna Stefanovich-Sevastyanovich (d. 1952) [S4]. Parents lived in Venezuela in the 1950s [S4].
- Died: July 2, 1966 (June 19 Old Style), Seattle, Washington, at St. Nicholas Cathedral [S1][S4][S7].
- Burial: crypt chapel under the Cathedral of the Mother of God "Joy of All Who Sorrow," San Francisco [S2][S4].
- Tradition type: Orthodox only. Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia (ROCOR) saint, later recognized by the Moscow Patriarchate. No Catholic cult found.
- Glorification: ROCOR, July 2, 1994 (June 19 O.S.), San Francisco [S2][S3][S7]. ROCOR Synod set it in May 1993 [S4]. Universal veneration: the Moscow Patriarchate Council of Bishops, June 24–29, 2008 [S4]. CONFLICT: S2 (Sanidopoulos/OrthodoxWiki text) says "July 2, 2008." S4 cites the Moscow Patriarchate page of 24.06.2008. Use "June 2008" unless a patriarchia.ru page is read.
- Feast days: June 19 (O.S.) / July 2 (N.S.), the day of his death [S2][S4]. ROCOR keeps the Saturday nearest July 2 [S3 only]. Uncovering of relics: September 29 (O.S.) / October 12 (N.S.) [S4][S5]. OCA keeps July 2 (older pilot; not re-confirmed: OCA page fetch returned the wrong page).
- Titles: Bishop of Shanghai, vicar of Peking diocese (1934); Archbishop of Shanghai (1946); Archbishop of Brussels and Western Europe (1950/51); Archbishop of Western America and San Francisco (1962/63) [S4].
- Research tier: B.

## 3. Timeline

One line per event. NEW = I did not see it on the popular English pages I read (S2, S3, S16), only in S1/S4/S7/S9/S10.

- 1896 Jun 4: Born at Adamovka; baptized Michael after the Archangel [S1][S2]. Sickly child who ate little [S1]. Loved animals, especially dogs; avoided noisy games; often lost in thought [S7] NEW.
- Childhood: collected icons and religious and history books; read lives of saints most of all, and taught his brothers and sister their stories [S7] NEW. Played at monastery with toy soldiers dressed as monks and toy forts as monasteries [S7] NEW. Family summer estate was 12 km from the Svyatogorsk monastery on the Donets, where his parents often stayed [S7][S4] NEW.
- 1907–1914: Poltava Cadet Corps (Petrovsky) [S1][S4]. Age 13 (1909, the Poltava battle bicentenary): crossed himself while marching past the Poltava cathedral; classmates mocked him and the officers punished him; Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich turned the punishment into a commendation [S7] NEW (one source only).
- Age 15: his French Catholic governess was baptized Orthodox, after his example and prayers [S7] NEW. S3 says he "converted" her as a child; S7 gives 15.
- 1914: Wanted to study at the Kiev Theological Academy; his parents insisted on law; he obeyed [S7] NEW. Also S4.
- 1914–1918: Law at Kharkov University; read the lives of the saints more than he attended lectures, yet did well [S1]. Archbishop Anthony (Khrapovitsky) of Kharkov became his spiritual guide [S1][S4][S8].
- Kharkov years: refused a parish proposal to melt down a church bell; jailed by the Bolsheviks, released, jailed again, released when they saw he did not care where he was [S7] NEW (one source). Served in the provincial court under Hetman Skoropadsky and again under Denikin [S4].
- 1918: Graduated in law [S1][S2][S4].
- 1920 Nov: evacuated from Crimea to Constantinople; 1921 arrived in the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes with parents and two brothers [S4]. S1 says 1921 with parents, brothers and a sister.
- 1921–1925: Theology at Belgrade. Sold newspapers to live [S1][S4]. Fellow student N. Zernov remembered a heavy fur coat, old boots, a greasy notebook, and sleeping in lectures then writing again [S4, quoting Zernov] NEW.
- 1924: Reader, ordained by Metropolitan Anthony at the Russian church in Belgrade [S1][S4].
- 1925 Oct 13: Finished theology at Belgrade [S4]. Then religion teacher at the state gymnasium in Velika Kikinda [S4].
- 1926 (Entrance of the Theotokos feast, Dec 4 N.S.): Tonsured monk John by Metropolitan Anthony at Milkovo Monastery, then made hierodeacon [S1][S4].
- 1926 Nov 21 (O.S.): Ordained hieromonk; the date is Nov 21 in S1 and S2 and Dec 4 N.S. in S4 [S1][S2][S4]. The ordaining bishop was Gabriel (Chepur) of Chelyabinsk [S2][S4].
- 1927/28–1934: Taught pastoral theology and church history at the seminary of St. John the Theologian in Bitol; Bishop Nikolai Velimirovich said, "If you wish to see a living saint, go to Bitol to Father John" [S1]. S4 gives a different wording from Velimirovich ("he is an angel of God in human form") and S7 a third. Wrote articles against Sophiology (Fr. Sergius Bulgakov) [S4]. S3 says the Serbian Synod appointed him in 1929; S4 says 1927; S7 says 1928.
- Bitol: students noticed he never slept in a bed; he walked the dormitories at night, pulled blankets back over sleepers and blessed them [S1][S7].
- 1934: Summoned to Belgrade; told an acquaintance there was a mistake, he was not the Hieromonk John to be consecrated; next day he said "the mistake was bigger than I thought" [S1][S4]. He objected that he had a speech defect; they answered that Moses had one too [S1].
- 1934 May 20/June 3: ROCOR Synod named him Bishop of Shanghai, vicar of the Peking diocese [S8][S4].
- 1934 May 27/June 10: Consecrated bishop in the Holy Trinity Church, Belgrade, by Metropolitan Anthony [S8][S4]. S1/S2/S16 give "May 28." CONFLICT: S8 and S4 give May 27 O.S. = June 10 N.S. Use S8/S4.
- 1934 (consecration): Anthony's letter to Archbishop Dimitry of Hailar: "my soul, my heart" is Bishop John, a "miracle of ascetic firmness" [S1][S4][S8].
- 1934 Dec 4 (Nov 21 O.S. Entrance feast): Arrived in Shanghai, about 50,000 Russian refugees there; found the large cathedral unfinished and parishes in conflict [S1][S4][S8].
- 1935: St. Nicholas Church, the memorial to the Tsar-Martyr, finished under him [S4]. 1936: opened a mission house of the Peking Pokrovsky women's convent with 15 nuns [S4].
- Shanghai 1930s–40s: Founded the St. Tikhon of Zadonsk orphanage; began with eight children [S1][S4]. Total: about 1,500 [S1]; about 3,500 [S4]. CONFLICT: not reconciled.
- Shanghai: examined children on the Law of God in all Russian schools [S1][S10]. Visited prisons, the mental hospital outside the city, the sick every day [S1].
- 1937+: Japanese war; he served the Russian community through the occupation, ignored curfew, and declared himself temporary head of the Russian colony after two heads of the Russian Emigrant Committee were killed [S1].
- 1945–46: After the Red Army entered Manchuria, bishops in China accepted Patriarch Alexy of Moscow. John for a time commemorated both Patriarch Alexy and Metropolitan Anastasy of ROCOR [S4]. 1946 (decision of May 20 Synod at Munich; his decree June 20): Shanghai diocese created under him, with 12 priests and three protodeacons; Shanghai Russians split into about 10,000 under the Patriarchate and about 5,000 under him [S4]. Archbishop from May 10, 1946 [S4].
- 1949 Jan–May: Evacuation of Shanghai Russians to Tubabao, Philippines (IRO camp) [S4]. About 5,000 people [S1][S2]; 5,500 [S14]. NOTE: S1 says only one typhoon threat in 27 months; see section 7.
- 1949 (days before Easter): Arrived on Tubabao by jeep; went to the Theotokos cathedral, the St. Seraphim church and the St. Michael church, with bells and choirs at each; Knyazev's memoir [S4] NEW.
- 1949 Jul 12: Left Tubabao for Washington; founded the parish of St. John the Forerunner there [S4][S2].
- 1949 Sep 6: Arrived in Washington; a Senate subcommittee heard him with N. N. Alexandrov on the displaced persons bill [S6]. Bill stalled for months; he came back more than once [S6].
- 1950 Holy Thursday: Bill passed the Senate; House followed; Truman signed June 16, 1950 [S6][S3]. S3: provision for 4,000 Europeans in the Far East.
- 1950 Nov 27: ROCOR Council appointed him Archbishop of Western Europe [S4]. 1951 Jul 21: arrived in Paris, lived at Meudon, titled "of Brussels and Western Europe" because Paris Russian parishes belonged to the Constantinople exarchate [S4]. S2 and S3 say 1951; S6 says 1952–62.
- 1952: Moved to Versailles, lived in the Russian Cadet Corps building [S4]. S1 has the visit to his cell there (Chertkova).
- 1951–62: Took Dutch and French Orthodox groups under his care, served in Dutch and French [S1][S4]. Collected lives and images of pre-schism Western saints and sent the Synod a long list [S1][S4]. Made the Orthodox Church of France's first bishop Jean-Nectaire (Kovalevsky) and ordained its second [S2], with S2 not saying when for either. Ordained a Spanish priest for the Madrid mission [S4].
- 1953 Oct 21: Council gave him an added title, ruler of Russian Orthodox churches in China and the Philippines [S4].
- 1961 Dec: Consecrated the church of All Saints of Russia in Paris [S4].
- 1962 Nov 21: Arrived in San Francisco, 28 years to the day (O.S.) after his arrival in Shanghai [S1]. Ruling archbishop from August 14, 1963 [S4].
- 1962–66: Cathedral of the Mother of God "Joy of All Who Sorrow" completed [S1][S2][S4]. Summer 1963: lawsuit over cathedral money brought by his opponents; ROCOR Bishops' Council on Aug 13, 1963 confirmed him on the see [S4]. S1: all involved were exonerated. S4 names Archpriest Grabbe and the Synod chancery as opponents' backers. Not verified in court records.
- 1964: Helped prepare the glorification of John of Kronstadt and wrote an apolytikion for him [S2][S3][S11]. In 1964 he was nearly elected ROCOR first hierarch; he proposed Bishop Philaret and said he would withdraw if the vote went to Philaret [S4].
- 1965 Nov 12: Last visit to the Kennedy family in Oregon; confessed each of them; told the narrator to read the Gospel ten minutes a day [S10].
- 1966 spring: Told the orphanage manager "I will not be here then" about a meeting three years away [S1]. May 1966: told a woman he would die soon, at the end of June, "not in San Francisco, but in Seattle" [S1].
- 1966 Jul 2 (Jun 19 O.S.): Died in his room at St. Nicholas parish house, Seattle, in front of the Kursk Root Icon, after a liturgy and three hours in the altar [S1][S4][S7]. Body lay six days in an open coffin in summer heat with no smell; his hand stayed soft (S7 and S4, from witnesses) [S4][S7].
- 1966: Entombed in the cathedral crypt [S4].
- 1993 May 7 and 13: ROCOR Council voted to glorify him and set the date for July 2, 1994; only Archbishop Anthony (Sinkevich) spoke against [S4]. Metropolitan Vitaly said he had once held up the glorification but would not oppose it now [S4].
- 1993 Sep 17/30: Lid of sarcophagus lifted for a first check [S5]. 1993 Sep 28/Oct 11: Tomb opened, 10:10 p.m. coffin opened [S5].
- 1994 Jul 1–3: Relics moved to a wooden shrine and carried up to the cathedral; glorification led by Metropolitan Vitaly, with ten bishops and about 160 clergy; three miraculous icons present; about 700 communicants at the main liturgy; about 2,000 at the meal [S7].
- 2008 Jun 24–29: Moscow Patriarchate's Council of Bishops glorified him for the whole Church [S4].
- 2015 Jan 9: Metropolitan Hilarion of ROCOR answered a request from the cadet associations (S4 truncated; details unread) [S4].

## 4. Character

- Small and frail, near-child in looks, according to Metropolitan Anthony [S1][S8]. Zernov, a fellow student, recalled a heavy man with thick shoulders, swollen cheeks, red lips, reddish Little Russian mustache [S4, quoting Zernov].
- Speech defect: could not pronounce clearly; he took diction lessons in Shanghai from Anna Lushnikova and paid her 20 dollars each lesson [S1].
- Sleep: never in a bed after taking vows, 40 years by S1; a few hours kneeling, sitting, or leaning on a tall crutch-like stick with a cross-bar, or on the floor before the icons [S1].
- Food: once a day at 11 p.m.; nothing during the first and last weeks of Lent; in the rest of Lent and the Nativity fast, only altar bread [S1][S7].
- Cold water on his face, then to church in minutes when someone woke him [S1].
- Served the liturgy every day for the rest of his life; if he could not serve he received communion [S1].
- Barefoot or in sandals on bare feet in every weather; gave his shoes to the poor; cheapest vestments [S4][S1][S10].
- Sternness: strict about church life. At a Halloween ball at the end of October in San Francisco he walked in with his staff after the all-night vigil, said nothing, and made the full round of the hall; he preached the next day [S1].
- Gentleness and play with children: playfully rapped mischievous altar boys with his staff; bent to play with a small child in the middle of a service; sprinkled holy water into faces ("He squirts you") [S1].
- Humor with mild reproof: complained he served too long, the other priest too short [S1].
- Practical sense: made a boy eat sausage in Lent because parents asked (obedience first) [S1].
- Gave away the socks the girls knitted him, which they saw later on beggars [S10].
- Tasted the soup the orphans got, from the pot [S10].
- Critics in ROCOR: faults named in Shanghai were administrative and staffing mistakes; some disliked the length of his services; some rejected his handling of the new calendar and of Latin-rite parishes that joined Orthodoxy; some objected to his veneration of old Western saints; some did not trust his mild stance toward the Moscow Patriarchate clergy [S4]. His rival Archbishop Nikon (Rklitsky), with Archbishop Seraphim (Ivanov) writing to Metropolitan Anastasy that John could not hold a ruling see [S4]. Some supporters made it worse with an ultimatum [S4]. S4 is itself partisan toward John; its own source is a historian's review. Flag.
- In his own words he disliked party politics for clergy: a clergyman "does not dare turn into an ordinary political or public figure" [S8].
- Admirers named him a clairvoyant and healer; Catholic priests in Paris pointed to him [S1].

## 6. Scenes (20)

Each line: scene; where to go back to read it. "testimony" means the saint or a witness tells it at length. Rights for all scene sources are facts-only unless noted.

1. 1909, Poltava. A 13-year-old cadet turns toward the cathedral during a parade and crosses himself. The boys laugh; the officers punish; the Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich steps in. [S7, section "Детство и отрочество," paragraph beginning "Когда ему исполнилось 11 лет"] One source.
2. Kharkov, 1914–18. A law student reads saints' lives instead of attending lectures; the shrine of Archbishop Meletius; Archbishop Anthony takes him in. His own telling in 1934. [S1, "Childhood"; S8, paragraphs 3–4 of the address; S7] testimony (his own words).
3. Belgrade, early 1920s. The student sells newspapers; he comes late to lecture in the mud, in a fur coat and old boots, falls asleep and starts writing again. [S4, "Жизнь в Югославии," quoting N. Zernov's memoir; find the memoir itself.] Memoir is in copyright.
4. Bitol, 1927–34. Night walk through the dormitories: he lifts fallen blankets, covers boys, blesses them. A student's recollection (Archbishop Averky witnessed the effect on students). [S1, "Belgrade"; S7, "Эмиграция. В Югославии," student quote] testimony.
5. Belgrade, spring 1934. On a streetcar and then a second meeting, he tells a woman first that there has been a mistake, then that the mistake was bigger than he thought. He objects about his speech; he is told about Moses. [S1, "Belgrade"; S4, Shatilova account, Sremski Karlovci] testimony (Shatilova's words as retold).
6. Belgrade, June 10, 1934. Consecration, his address on shepherding, and Metropolitan Anthony's letter sending him "as my soul, as my heart." [S8, full page; S4] his own words.
7. Shanghai, December 4, 1934. A frail new bishop reaches a city of 50,000 Russians, an unfinished cathedral and quarrelling parishes. [S1, "Belgrade/Shanghai"; S4, "Архиерей в Китае"].
8. Shanghai slums at night. Marina Shakhmatova (with two bottles of Chinese vodka) walks behind him; he trades a bottle to a drunk guarding a garbage can where a baby cries. He comes back with two babies. [S6, quoting Podmoshensky; original is in Rose's 1987 book, in copyright] testimony. Mark: retold; check the book page.
9. Shanghai, 1930s. Bishop John gives Communion to Mrs. Menshikova, bitten by a mad dog; she spits the Gifts; he takes them into his own mouth: "Nothing will happen." Olga Skopichenko. [S13; S1, "Shanghai"] testimony.
10. Shanghai. The altar boy George Larin gives up bed and meals in Lent; the bishop makes him eat sausage and tells him obedience to parents matters more. [S1, "Shanghai"] testimony.

- Shanghai. A teacher with appendicitis; he laid his hands on him; the next day the appendix had burst in the night and he sat up [S7] recorded.

## 8. Legacy

- St. Tikhon of Zadonsk orphanage: founded in Shanghai, eight children at the start; later also in San Francisco; the 1953–54 letters are addressed to the San Francisco students [S1][S4][S9]. Foundation date in Shanghai not given in these sources. Number of children disputed (1,500 vs 3,500).
- Shanghai: St. Nicholas Church finished 1935 [S4]; cathedral of the Surety of Sinners Icon [S2] (S4 also lists it); hospital, homes for the elderly, a free canteen, a commercial school built with his blessing [S4]; Peking convent mission house 1936 [S4].
- Exodus of Shanghai Russians through Tubabao, 1949–51, and the lobbying that led to the amended law of June 16, 1950 [S6][S3]. For depth: John Dunlop, "Exodus" (2017) [S3 bibliography; not read].
- Parish of St. John the Forerunner in Washington, 1949 [S2][S4].
- Western Europe: veneration of pre-schism Western saints (Genevieve of Paris, Patrick of Ireland named [S4]); lists sent to the Synod [S1][S4]; support to French and Dutch Orthodox communities [S1][S2][S4]; Brussels church of Righteous Job [S4]; Paris church of All Saints of Russia, Dec 1961 [S4].
- San Francisco: finished the cathedral; helped by money pouring in after he arrived [S4]; his tomb is there [S2][S4].
- Prepared the glorification of John of Kronstadt (1964); wrote an apolytikion for him [S2][S11].
- Writings: "The Russian Orthodox Church Abroad: A Short History" (Holy Trinity Publications, 1997); "The Orthodox Veneration of the Mother of God" with a foreword by Fr. Seraphim Rose; articles against Sophiology in the 1920s–30s [S16][S4]. A report to the 1938 All-Diaspora Council of ROCOR on the Constantinople Patriarchate is listed [S16; not read].
- Why he became famous: Rose and Podmoshensky wrote "Blessed John the Wonderworker" (St. Herman Brotherhood, 1987; 3rd ed. 1998) from witness accounts [S3][S16]. The relics and a ROCOR Council vote (1993) followed [S4][S5]. People ask him for help in sickness, danger, and travel (the service says "hasten to the suffering" [S2]); S2 and S1 stress healing. Relics are in Spain, Serbia, Russia, Mount Athos, Greece, South Korea, Bulgaria, Romania, Canada, the UK, North Macedonia, and elsewhere [S3, from a Seattle Times item].
- Filipino municipality established a "St. John Maximovitch Day" (headline in search results from basilica.ro, not read) [lead only]. ROCOR mission chapel dedicated to him in Santa Maria, Davao del Sur, in 2013 [S14].

## 9. Other

Works and connections:
- Kinship to St. John of Tobolsk [S1][S4][S7]. Close friend Archbishop Tikhon (Troitsky) of San Francisco, his predecessor [S1][S2]. Teachers: Metropolitan Anthony Khrapovitsky; Bishop Nikolai Velimirovich [S1][S4]. Father Seraphim Rose wrote the main English biography [S3].
- Eyewitness names to follow in the Rose/Podmoshensky book: Marina Shakhmatova, Fr. Herman Podmoshensky, Hieromonk Peter Loukianoff (later Bishop Peter), Bishop Nektary, Mrs. Chertkova, Archpriest George Larin, Olga Skopichenko, Lidia Liu.

Surprises:
- A Western church-planter: he served in Greek, Chinese, French, Dutch, and English, in addition to Church Slavonic [S1].
- He allowed Latin-rite parishes that joined Orthodoxy to keep the Gregorian calendar, which angered some ROCOR believers [S4].
- He did not preach to the local Filipinos in the camp; no Filipino was baptized during his stay [S14].
- At first he kept both patriarch and ROCOR head in commemoration, so the Moscow link was not a clean break [S4].

Conflicts:
- Birth day: June 4 vs "4 (16) June" [S1/S3 vs S4].
- Consecration date: May 28 [S1][S2][S16] vs May 27 O.S./June 10 N.S. [S4][S8].
- Move to Western Europe: 1950 appointed, July 1951 arrival [S4] vs 1951 [S2] vs "1952–62" [S6].
- Moscow glorification: June 24–29, 2008 [S4] vs July 2, 2008 [S2].
- Orphans: 1,500 [S1] vs 3,500 [S4].
