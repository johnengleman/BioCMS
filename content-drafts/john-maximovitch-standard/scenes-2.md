## SC-05 — Shanghai back streets at night, 1930s
- Status: thin (one retelling; no original page, no year)
- When and where: night, slums of Shanghai. Year not stated. Namee places the story after the 1937 Japanese invasion; Podmoshensky gives no date in the passage [S6]. Do not print a year.
- Who was there: Archbishop John; Mrs. Marina Shakhmatova (ran the orphanage John set up); a drunk man in a doorway; a baby in a garbage can [S6].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Newspapers reported that dogs devoured baby girls thrown into garbage cans [S6].
  2. John told Shakhmatova to buy two bottles of Chinese vodka. She was horrified. He then made her go with him into the slums, where, she knew, adults were murdered [S6].
  3. They walked dark alleys at night past drunks, shady people, and growling dogs and cats. She carried the bottles [S6].
  4. A drunk man sitting in a doorway growled. A baby moaned from a nearby garbage can [S6].
  5. John went to the cry. The drunk growled a warning. John said, "Hand me a bottle." Without words, he raised the bottle and pointed at the can: a sale [S6].
  6. The drunk got the bottle. Shakhmatova saved the child [S6].
  7. "They say" he went back to the orphanage with two babies under his arms [S6]. This line is hearsay inside the retelling.
- Details: Chinese vodka; dark alleys; growling dogs and cats; Shakhmatova's dread [S6].
- Words spoken: "Hand me a bottle." Speaker: John. Retold in English by Podmoshensky; no original language found. Plain: same. [S6]
- Inner life: Namee adds that this fearlessness "had not been acquired without a deep inner struggle." That is Namee's remark, with no detail [S6]. paraphrase
- Testimony: Retold by Fr. Herman Podmoshensky from Shakhmatova's memory. Rights: facts-only. Short quote, 25 words: "the faint moan of a baby was heard from a nearby garbage can. When the Bishop hastened towards the cry, the drunkard growled in warning." [S6; same text on orthodoxethos.com]. Plain: A baby's weak cry came from a garbage can. The bishop hurried toward it, and the drunk man growled a warning.
- What changed: A baby was saved. Orphanage began with eight children [S1].
- Not in the sources: the year; the baby's sex or name; the street or district; what became of either baby; what John said to the drunk (the source says he spoke no words); whether this was the orphanage's first rescue; the vodka's price; any weather.
- Sources: S6; orthodoxethos copy (same text, no page given); S1 ("Beginning with eight children", orphanage later "some 1500 in all"). Tries used: S1, S6, search for a Russian text. Original in Rose/Podmoshensky 1987 not reachable online (in copyright). Needs the book page; ask the editor if a library copy is possible.

## SC-06 — St. Tikhon of Zadonsk orphanage, Shanghai, 1938–1948
- Status: full (Kennedy); letters mismatched (see below)
- When and where: Shanghai, the years Kennedy gives as 1938–1948. Places: St. Olga's Orphanage at the convent (third-floor mansion), the cathedral, John's cell, the street to St. Tikhon's orphanage [S10].
- Who was there: Tatiana Kennedy (Urusova), a girl at St. Olga's Orphanage under Abbess Ariadna; she was John's helper at services from age ten to sixteen; the other girls; a nun leading them [S10].
- What happened, in order:
  1. At Nativity, after the vigil at the convent, a group of girls led by a nun walked through the sleeping city to the cathedral with a burning Christmas star to greet John [S10].
  2. He received them in his room, "shining with joy." Each girl took a blessing and received a bag of sweets from large baskets in his cell [S10].
  3. They gave him woolen socks they had knit. Later they saw the socks on beggars [S10].
  4. From the third-floor windows they saw him walk in torrential rain, winter and summer, to St. Tikhon's orphanage, past a House of Mercy, a home for old women, and a prison. Sometimes he stopped at their convent on the way back; bells announced him [S10].
  5. Soaked, in sandals on bare feet, he went into the church and kissed the altar table and icons, then went up the back stairs [S10].
  6. The girls sang "Eis polla eti, Despota." He asked their patron saints, sometimes their Lives, and the day's Gospel. He asked if they had enough food [S10].
  7. In wartime the orphanage got soup from a public canteen. He tasted it with a spoon from the pot [S10].
- Details: strict at times; children ran to him in the street for a blessing [S10]. Rabies Communion: one sentence only, per outline [S13][S1].
- Words spoken: none quoted from him in this period.
- Inner life: none in the sources for these years.
- Testimony: Kennedy, S10 section "1938-1948. Shanghai," rights facts-only. One short quote, 11 words: "We children felt light and joyful with Vladyka." Plain: We children felt light and happy with him.
- What changed: the children trusted him as a shepherd, not a distant bishop.
- Not in the sources: the year of the star walk; the star's make; what was in the sweets; the soup's recipe; which winter; any word John said at the soup.
- Letters warning: outline's quotes 7–12 [S9] do not belong to this scene. The 1927 letter is to school pupils, before Shanghai. The 1952–54 letters go to the San Francisco orphanage. Use them in later chapters only. S9 gives English only (OrthoChristian); the Russian is printed in "Blessed John the Wonderworker" (1993) pp. 256–7 and "Vladyka John" (2008) pp. 30–31, which I did not find online. Short quote, 12 words, for ch. 11 if needed: "Remember: Whoever quarrels consoles the devil, and whoever makes peace with others helps Christ." Plain: Remember, the one who quarrels pleases the devil. The one who makes peace with others helps Christ. (1927 letter) [S9]. Own translation awaits the Russian text.
- Sources: S10, S9, S1 (eight children, 1500 in all; S4 says 3,500: attribute or give no total).

## SC-07 — Tubabao refugee camp, days before Easter 1949
- Status: thin (one retelling, memoir original not reached)
- When and where: Tubabao island, Philippines; "a few days before Easter 1949" [S4]. No day given. Evacuation from Shanghai ran from January to early May 1949 [S4]. John stayed about three months and left July 12, 1949 for Washington [S4].
- Who was there: John; Hieromonk Modest; Fr. Philaret Astrakhansky; Protodeacon Konstantin Zanevsky; the bishop's choir under G. Agafonov; Archpriest Afanasy Shalobanov; Fr. Nikolai Kolchev; Hieromonk Nikolai; Deacon Pavel Metlenko; choir under I. P. Mikhailov; Archpriest Matvei Medvedev; Fr. David Shevchenko; choir under M. A. Shulyakovsky; the crowd. Source: A. N. Knyazev, quoted in [S4].
- What happened, in order:
  1. John came by jeep and went straight to the Holy Theotokos cathedral. Clergy and the bishop's choir met him; the cathedral was crowded with people who revered him [S4].
  2. After a moleben and a cup of tea, he went to the St. Seraphim church. Bells rang; clergy and a choir met him there [S4].
  3. After a short service he went to the St. Archangel Michael church, where bells and another choir met him [S4].
  4. S4 calls his arrival an event of exceptional importance in the camp's spiritual life [S4].
- Details: jeep; bells; three churches; tea. Camp numbers: about 5,000 [S1][S2], 5,500 [S14]. Filipinos' remark, English: "your holy man blesses your camp from four directions every night" [S14]. The no-typhoon claim is "anecdotal" (S14) and a legend in the outline; leave out.
- Testimony: Knyazev via S4. Rights: facts-only (Wikipedia quoting a memoir). One quote, 37 words, Russian, then our English.
  Russian: "На Тубабао прибыл Владыка Иоанн Шанхайский на джипе и сразу проследовал в Свято-Богородицкий собор, где был встречен иеромонахом Модестом, иереем отцом Филаретом Астраханским и протодиаконом отцом Константином Заневским и архиерейским хором … Собор был переполнен почитателями Владыки."
  Our English: Archbishop John of Shanghai came to Tubabao in a jeep. He went straight to the Cathedral of the Mother of God. Hieromonk Modest, Fr. Philaret Astrakhansky, Protodeacon Konstantin Zanevsky, and the bishop's choir met him there. The cathedral was packed with people who honored him.
  (The ellipsis marks words that name the choir director, G. Agafonov.)
- What changed: his flock saw him in the camp before he left for Washington.
- Not in the sources: the date; time of day; his words or mood; whether he wept or blessed; weather; the jeep's driver; whether he came from Manila or from a ship.
- Sources: S4 section "Бегство из Китая. Тубабао," footnote 20: N. V. Moravsky, "Остров Тубабао. 1948–1951: Последнее пристанище российской дальневосточной эмиграции" (Moscow: Русский путь, 2000), p. 16. Knyazev is quoted there. The archived Word file is at https://web.archive.org/web/20220331234213/http://www.rp-net.ru/book/publications/island/Moravskiy_OstrovTubabao.doc . My tool returned binary text; I could not read it, and I did not download it. Knyazev's own memoir was not found online. Tries used: S4, web search, Wayback .doc. Ask the editor to open the .doc by hand for the full Russian text (p. 16) so we can translate it ourselves; rights of the 2000 book unknown. S14 (Filipino remark; 5,500).

## SC-08 — US Senate, Washington, September 1949 – June 1950
- Status: full for what the sources give; no hearing record found
- When and where: John arrived in Washington September 6, 1949 [S6]. A Senate Judiciary Committee meeting on the displaced-persons bill took place that day or just before his letter; Namee's wording is "Senate subcommittee" and the quoted letter says "Senate Judiciary Committee." Treat both as his own account [S6]. Left Tubabao July 12, 1949 [S4].
- Who was there: John; his translator, Nikolai Nikolaevich Alexandrov, who prepared "a very impressive report"; Senator William Knowland of California, who gave a report [S6].
- What happened, in order:
  1. John met congressmen and officials. He and the translator were invited to testify [S6].
  2. After Knowland's report, John was questioned in detail on the Shanghai Russian colony from 1920 on, and on the number of refugees on Tubabao [S6, quoting John's letter].
  3. The bill was opposed and stalled for months. Truman supported it; a bloc of senators did not. John made more trips to Washington [S6].
  4. The bill passed the Senate on Holy Thursday 1950, then the House [S6].
  5. Truman signed the amended Displaced Persons Act on June 16, 1950: H.R. 4567, Public Law 555 (81st Congress), 64 Stat. 219 [Truman 1950; S3 Wikipedia "Displaced Persons Act"].
  6. John was grateful to Knowland [S6].
  7. Washington parish of St. John the Forerunner founded during this stay [S4].
- Words spoken (his letter, English via Namee, short quotes, facts-only): "Today is a very important day in our case." Plain: Today is a very important day for our cause. And: "Senator Knowland was our best helper in the Senate… He conducted the last session like a guard standing at his post." Plain: Senator Knowland helped us most in the Senate. He ran the last session like a sentry at his post. Russian original of the letter not found (searched azbyka and the web; the 2011 Moscow collection "I Am Always With You in Spirit" may hold it; not online).
- Testimony: Truman's statement, public domain (US government), full text read, Truman Library, Public Papers 1950, item 167. Passage on the Far East, 53 words: "Special provisions are made for 10,000 war orphans from the free countries of Europe and for 4,000 European refugees who fled to the Far East to escape one form of totalitarianism and must now flee before a new tyranny." The statement does not name John or Shanghai. Plain: The law admits 4,000 European refugees who fled to the Far East from one tyranny and now must flee another.
- What changed: the law opened 4,000 places for Europeans from the Far East. Shanghai refugees on Tubabao could now enter the US (resettlement in US and Australia began 1951 [S14]). John got a new post, Western Europe, from the ROCOR Synod (appointed November 27, 1950 [S4]).
- Not in the sources: any hearing transcript or his spoken testimony; the committee's room; the senators who opposed; his dress or words to them; how many trips; that he sat on White House steps (one source only, legend). Do not say the 4,000 places were "for Russians"; Truman says "European refugees."
- Links: Truman statement does not credit John. The step from the 4,000 provision to Tubabao comes from S3/S6 and the notes; keep it attributed.
- Sources: S6 (Namee, 2025, facts-only); S4; S14; S3 (Wikipedia, "Displaced Persons Act" for the June 16, 1950 date and H.R. 4567); New: Harry S. Truman, "Statement by the President upon signing bill amending the Displaced Persons Act," June 16, 1950, English, https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/167/statement-president-upon-signing-bill-amending-displaced-persons-act , public domain. Not found in three tries: the 1949 Senate hearing record. Namee points to Dunlop, "Exodus" (2017), in copyright.
