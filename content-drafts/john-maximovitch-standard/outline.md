# Saint John Maximovitch — scene outline (tier A, revised 2026-10-07)

Keys: `notes.md` and `scenes-*.md`. Facts in `biography.html` are approved; do not bring back what the check logs removed.

## 1. The driving question

From the first days he was aware of himself, Saint John wanted "to serve justice and truth" [S8-ru ¶1]. The monastery and the church "drew me more" than law school, and when the Russian state fell he gave himself to God alone [S8-ru ¶4]. His own weakness stood in the way: a tongue that could not speak clearly and a deep sense of being unworthy [S1][S8]. Later a flock with no country, and opponents in his own Church who took him to court, stood in the way too [S4][S20]. Grace and his choices answered it: he obeyed when he was afraid, served the liturgy every day, went where his people went, answered slander without complaint, and reposed at prayer [S1][S7][S8].

Why it was beautiful to him: the spiritual life was "the science of sciences" [S8-ru ¶4], and a bishop "has no personal life" but gives himself "entirely to the cause of saving human souls and leading them into the Kingdom of Heaven" [S8]. The reader first hears this in ch. 2, at his first decision, in his 1934 words.

The great turn comes early (1934, ch. 4), so the later life has its own question. He now loves a people who have lost their country, and their children most of all. It costs him a bed, a home, his teacher and parents far away, and at last his good name among his own. Can a shepherd who keeps nothing for himself hold a flock that has lost everything?

Famous: the wonderworker of the Russian emigration, sought in sickness, danger, and travel [S2][S3].

Apart: many exile churchmen turned to politics, but he held that a clergyman "does not dare turn into an ordinary political or public figure" and kept to the altar, the sick, and the orphans [S8][S1]. In a Church of exiles that looked inward, he served the French and Dutch in their own languages and asked his Church to honor the old saints of the West [S1][S4].

## 2. Portrait

Metropolitan Anthony saw "this little, frail man, looking almost like a child" [S1]. A fellow student remembered a heavy youth in a fur coat and old boots who slept in lectures and woke to write again [S4, Zernov]. As a bishop he paid a teacher twenty dollars a lesson to speak clearly [S1]. He ate once a day, late at night, and after his vows never slept in a bed [S1]. He went barefoot or in sandals and gave his shoes away [S1][S10].

He was strict about church life, some found his services too long, and critics blamed him for mistakes in running a diocese [S1][S4]. He was also light: "How difficult it is to please you people. I celebrate too long, and he too short!" [S1]. He splashed holy water into children's faces [S1], and children felt "light and joyful" with him [S10].

Life with God: the Divine Liturgy every day, or Holy Communion when he could not serve; nights before the icons [S1]. "You cannot preach the gospel without showing love in deeds" [S8-ru]. At Christmas he asked his people to bring the Infant Christ "some kind of good deed" [S12, 1935].

## 3. The opening

Hook: the mistake. It raises the question (weakness against a great call) and gives away nothing of the end.

> In the spring of 1934 a young monk came up to Belgrade from a seminary in the south of Yugoslavia. On a streetcar he met a woman he knew, and he told her the bishops had made a mistake. They wanted another monk named John, he said. The next day he saw her again. "The mistake was bigger than I thought," he told her. "They did mean me." They meant to make him a bishop and send him to China, and when he said he could not even speak clearly, they reminded him that Moses could not either.

Sources: [S1 "Belgrade"][S4, Shatilova]. Ch. 4 starts after the naming and does not retell it.

Thesis (no repose, tomb, or glorification): He is remembered across the Orthodox world as the shepherd of the Russian exiles [S2][S3]. For thirty-two years he followed his scattered people to Shanghai, an island in the Philippines, the United States Senate, France, and California [S1][S4][S6]. He did it without politics, with the daily liturgy, the sick, and the orphans [S8][S1]. Ch. 5–10 answer it.

Ending: the climax is his repose in Seattle (ch. 11), with nothing in front of it. Then a short close: people prayed for him at the crypt, then to him [S21]; the 1987 book and the May 1993 vote, one sentence each [S3][S4]. Then one scene, October 11, 1993 [S5]. Closing image: a priest carries his sick two-year-old son down and touches him to Saint John's hands [S5]. At most one quiet sentence after: the boy was healed [S21], or the next summer his holy relics were carried up into the cathedral he had come to finish [S7]. Other glorification facts go to other sections.

## 5. The scenes

**TP** = turning point, told slowly from inside him. **R** = the period's resistance moment; compress the rest of that period.

### SC-01 — Parade day, 1909 (Poltava, before the cathedral) — R
- What happens: A cadet of thirteen turns to the cathedral and crosses himself, alone with his cap off. Boys mock him, officers punish him, and a higher officer turns it to praise.
- Why: the first time his faith costs him.
- Have: [S7 "Детство и отрочество"][S17], SC-01 card. Bridge: toy soldiers as monks, saints' lives told to siblings [S7].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-02 — Kharkov, 1914–1920 — TP1, R
- What happens: His parents insist on law instead of the academy, and he obeys. Archbishop Anthony takes him in; the monastery draws him more. The state falls, and he gives himself to God alone.
- Why: the desire, and why it was beautiful, at his first decision.
- Have: [S7][S4][S1 "Childhood"]; ¶1 and ¶4 with our English [S8-ru, SC-02 card]; court service [S4].

### SC-03 — Belgrade, early 1920s — R

### SC-11 — His room at Versailles, 1950s — R

### SC-12 — San Francisco, spring–August 1963 — TP5, R (the trial's concrete day)

- Why: the hardest resistance, from his own people.

### SC-13 — A hospital ward, about 1964; Oregon, November 12, 1965
- What happens: Tatiana's small daughter cries after an operation; the nurses fear to go in. At eleven at night he comes, and she smiles and sleeps. On his last visit he hears the family's confessions.
- Why: the orphan girl of the Christmas star is a mother now; the thread closes.

### SC-14 — Saint Nicholas Cathedral, Seattle, July 2, 1966 — climax
- What happens: In May he says where he will die. Four days before: "You will not kiss my hand again." With the Kursk Root Icon he serves the liturgy and prays three hours alone in the altar. A crash; his former altar servers find him dying. He gives his soul to God before the icon.
- Why: "no personal life" to the end.
- Have: [S1 "The Death of a Saint"][S7][S4][S21], SC-11 card.
- Need: nothing. No last words are recorded.

### SC-15 — The crypt, San Francisco, night of October 11, 1993
- What happens: The rusted lock opens as Archbishop Anthony sings "We have no other help." The face is uncovered: "there was an extraordinary silence." Father Yaroslav Belikow carries his sick son Vsevolod down and touches him to Saint John's hands.
- Why: the one named case after his repose; the closing image.
- Have: [S5]; the healing [S21]; SC-12 card.
- Need: nothing.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Trial: the complaint charged the former council with losses, and him with covering up that council's actions and calling an election to return its members [S20 pt. 3]. Never "covered the losses." "Generally unstable" is the petition's word. The outcome is attributed to his Church's accounts; no court record was found. Do not take the accusers' side.
- San Francisco arrival: December 4, 1962, the Entrance feast [S23], not November 21.
- The address was given "at his naming"; the order of naming and consecration is not stated.
- Tubabao: "days before Easter" [S4] against Pravenc's May 4 [S23]; Note on the Sources.
- Orphans: "about 3,500, by the Orthodox Encyclopedia" [S23]; no founding year.
- Poltava: Grand Duke [S7] or inspector general [S17]; write "an officer of higher rank."
- 1993: "the report calls the face incorrupt"; the mildew goes in the Note on the Sources [S5].
- Three Anthonys: Khrapovitsky (teacher), Sinkevich (against, 1993), Anthony of Western America (opened the tomb).
- Orthodox words: reposed, glorified, holy relics. "Saint" before every saint's name.
