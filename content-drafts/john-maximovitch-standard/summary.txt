One night in the slums of Shanghai, a Russian bishop who could not speak clearly traded a bottle of vodka for a baby crying in a garbage can. Saint John Maximovitch never slept in a bed, ate once a day, and gave his shoes to beggars. He believed that a bishop has no personal life. For thirty-two years he followed a people who had lost their country, to China, to a Philippine island, to Paris, and to San Francisco. He spoke for them before the United States Senate, and in San Francisco some of his own flock took him to court. Could a shepherd who kept nothing for himself hold together a flock that had lost everything?
