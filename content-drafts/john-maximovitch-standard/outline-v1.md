# St. John Maximovitch — scene outline (tier B)

Source keys follow `notes.md`. Most sources are facts-only: tell scenes in our own words. Quote only short lines from S5, S6, S8–S12.

## 1. The driving question

From his first memories he wanted "to serve justice and truth" and to give his life to the Church [S8]. Against him stood a frail body, unclear speech, his sense of being unworthy, and a flock of refugees scattered by revolution and war, later joined by opponents in his own Church [S1][S4][S8]. He lived his own rule for a bishop, "no personal life" [S8]: night prayer, the daily liturgy, love in action. So the man who thought his call to be a bishop was a mistake became the shepherd of exiles on four continents [S1][S4].

He is famous as the wonderworker of the Russian emigration, sought in sickness and danger [S2][S3].

In an age of political churchmen he refused politics and kept to prayer and the poor [S8][S4]. In a church of exiles that looked inward, he served in French, Dutch, Chinese, Greek, and English and brought the old saints of the West back into Orthodox prayer [S1][S4].

## 2. Portrait

Small and frail, almost a child to look at, yet "a miracle of ascetic firmness," his teacher wrote [S1][S8]. A fellow student remembered a heavy youth in a fur coat and old boots, asleep in lectures [S4, Zernov]. His speech was so unclear that as a bishop he paid for diction lessons [S1]. He ate once a day, late at night, and after his vows never slept in a bed [S1][S7]. He went barefoot or in sandals and gave his shoes away [S1][S4][S10].

He could be stern: he walked in silence through a Halloween ball [S1]. He could be light: he joked about his long services, splashed holy water in children's faces, and played with a child during a service [S1]. He put a boy's obedience to his parents above the boy's fasting [S1]. Critics called him a poor administrator [S4]. Children felt "light and joyful" with him [S10].

His life with God: the liturgy every day, or Communion when he could not serve [S1]; nights before the icons [S1]. "You cannot preach the gospel without showing love in your actions" [S8]. To the orphans: "whoever makes peace with others helps Christ" [S9].

## 3. The opening

Hook: the mistake.

> In the spring of 1934, a young monk who taught at a seminary in the south of Yugoslavia was called to Belgrade. He told a woman he knew that the bishops had made a mistake. They wanted another monk named John, he said, not him. The next day he saw her again. "The mistake was bigger than I thought," he told her. They meant to make him a bishop, and when he said he could not even speak clearly, they reminded him that Moses could not either.

Sources: [S1 "Belgrade"][S4, Shatilova]. Chapter 4 starts at the consecration and does not retell this.

Thesis: They sent him to Shanghai, a city of about 50,000 Russian refugees [S1][S4]. For 32 years he went where the exiles went. He gathered orphans, led some 5,000 people out of China, argued their case before the US Senate, served the Orthodox of France and the Netherlands, and finished a cathedral in San Francisco [S1][S4][S6]. The Church Abroad glorified him in 1994 and the Moscow Patriarchate in 2008 [S4][S7]. Chapters 11–12 answer it.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — Parade day, 1909 (Poltava, before the cathedral)
- What happens: A cadet crosses himself as his unit marches past the cathedral. Boys laugh and officers punish him; Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich turns it into praise.
- Why it is here: the first time he will not hide his faith, at a cost.
- Have: [S7 "Детство и отрочество"], one source. Bridge: toy soldiers dressed as monks, saints' lives told to siblings, governess baptized [S7].
- Need: a second source; his age (paragraph opens "when he turned 11"; notes say 13).
- Where to look: S7; Rose/Podmoshensky 1987.

### SC-02 — Kharkov, 1914–1918
- What happens: He gives up the theological academy because his parents insist on law. He reads saints' lives instead of attending lectures; Archbishop Anthony (Khrapovitsky) becomes his guide.
- Why it is here: desire and obedience together, in his own words.
- Have: [S7][S4] obedience; [S1 "Childhood"]; quotes 1–2 [S8].
- Need: the Russian text of the 1934 address, for our own translation.
- Testimony: his address, paragraphs 3–4 [S8].
- Where to look: S8.

### SC-03 — Bitol seminary dormitory at night, late 1920s–1934
- What happens: Students see their teacher never goes to bed. At night he walks the dormitories, covers sleeping boys, and blesses them.
- Why it is here: the hidden prayer life, and care for the young.
- Have: [S1 "Belgrade"][S7]; Velimirovich's "living saint" [S1][S6].
- Need: the student's name and words.
- Where to look: S7 "Эмиграция. В Югославии".

### SC-04 — Holy Trinity Church, Belgrade, June 10, 1934
- What happens: He is consecrated bishop. He says he dares not think himself worthy but is afraid to refuse. Metropolitan Anthony sends him off as "my soul, my heart."
- Why it is here: the driving question in his own words.
- Have: [S8][S4]; quotes 3, 4, 6; Anthony's letter [S1][S4][S8].
- Need: the Russian text of Anthony's letter.
- Testimony: the address [S8], short quotes.
- Where to look: S8.

### SC-05 — Shanghai back streets at night, 1930s
- What happens: Marina Shakhmatova follows him with two bottles of Chinese vodka. He trades one to a drunk guarding a garbage can where a baby cries, and comes back with two babies.
- Why it is here: love in action; the orphanage began with eight children.
- Have: [S6, quoting Podmoshensky]; eight children [S1][S4].
- Need: the original account and page in Rose/Podmoshensky 1987; the year.
- Where to look: S6.

### SC-06 — St. Tikhon of Zadonsk orphanage, Shanghai, 1938–1948
- What happens: The girls carry a Christmas star through the city, and he gives them sweets. The socks they knit him turn up on beggars. In rain and sandals he tastes their soup from the pot.
- Why it is here: the shepherd through a child's eyes.
- Have: Tatiana Kennedy [S10 "1938–1948. Shanghai"]; letters, quotes 7–12 [S9].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Kennedy [S10], short quotes.

### SC-07 — Tubabao refugee camp, days before Easter 1949
- What happens: He arrives by jeep and goes from church to church. Bells and choirs meet him at each.
- Why it is here: the shepherd reaches his people in exile.
- Have: Knyazev via [S4 "Бегство из Китая. Тубабао"]; about 5,000 people [S1][S2], 5,500 [S14]; the "four directions" remark [S1][S14].
- Need: Knyazev's memoir itself; the date.
- Where to look: S4; S14.

### SC-08 — US Senate, Washington, September 1949 – June 1950
- What happens: With N. N. Alexandrov he speaks to a Senate subcommittee. The bill stalls and he comes back. It passes on Holy Thursday 1950; Truman signs on June 16.
- Why it is here: love in action carried into politics without becoming politics.
- Have: [S6]; quotes 14–15; 4,000 places [S3].
- Need: the hearing record; the Truman statement.
- Where to look: S6; Congressional record.

### SC-09 — His room at Versailles, 1950s
- What happens: Mrs. Chertkova visits. There is no bed, only a tall stick with a cross-bar for prayer. He jokes, "How difficult it is to please you people."
- Why it is here: his life with God, and his humor.
- Have: [S1 "Paris"]; quote 20; "Saint John the Barefoot" [S1]; Western saints [S1][S4].
- Need: who the Catholic priest was.
- Where to look: S1 "Paris".

### SC-10 — San Francisco, summer 1963
- What happens: Opponents in his own Church sue him over cathedral money. On August 13 the Council of Bishops confirms him.
- Why it is here: the hard part; a man who refused politics is pulled into a church quarrel.
- Have: [S4]; all cleared [S1]; critics [S4]; quote 5 [S8].
- Need: a court record or second account; his conduct during the case; who sued.
- Where to look: Perekrestov, "Man of God"; archived trial page.

### SC-11 — St. Nicholas Cathedral, Seattle, July 2, 1966
- What happens: In May he says he will die "not in San Francisco, but in Seattle." Days before, he tells a man, "You will not kiss my hand again." After the liturgy and three hours in the altar, he dies in his room before the Kursk Root Icon.
- Why it is here: the death; his last acts are prayer.
- Have: [S1 "The Death of a Saint"][S4][S7]; quotes 23–24; six days in an open coffin [S4][S7].
- Need: why he was in Seattle; who found him.
- Where to look: S1; S7.

### SC-12 — Crypt under the San Francisco cathedral, night of October 11, 1993
- What happens: Three bishops open the tomb at 10:10 p.m. A priest's two-year-old son is touched to the saint's hands.
- Why it is here: the real case after death; the Church tests the memory before it glorifies him.
- Have: [S5]: key kept 27 years, face and beard preserved, white on hands was mildew, son of Priest Yaroslav Belikow, "extraordinary silence."
- Need: what became of the boy.
- Testimony: the report [S5], short quotes.

**What he changed**
- St. Tikhon orphanage, Shanghai, later San Francisco [S1][S4][S9] — ch. 5, 11.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Born June 4, 1896 [S1][S3][S4]; S4 also gives "4 (16)"; print no Gregorian day.
- Consecrated May 27 O.S. / June 10 N.S. 1934 [S8][S4], not May 28.
- Western Europe: named November 27, 1950; arrived Paris July 21, 1951 [S4].
- Moscow glorification: "June 2008" [S4], not July 2, 2008 [S2].
- Orphans: 1,500 [S1] vs 3,500 [S4]; attribute or give no total.
- Bitol start: 1927/1928/1929; write "late 1920s."
- Attribute in a few words: the Poltava parade, the Tubabao arrival, the lawsuit outcome (S4 favors John; no court record read).
- Velimirovich's words exist in three forms; use the S1 wording.
- Three Anthonys: Khrapovitsky (teacher), Sinkevich (opposed 1993), the 1993 commission archbishop [S5].
- The 1993 report calls the white on the hands mildew. Write "found preserved, the commission reported," not "incorrupt."
