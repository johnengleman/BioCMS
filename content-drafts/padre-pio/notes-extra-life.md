## 1. Childhood in Pietrelcina and the Forgione family
- 25 May 1887, 5 pm, Wednesday: born in the old Rione Castello. Birth certificate signed by acting mayor Gaetano Sagliocca lists the parents as "possidenti" (landowners). Midwife Grazia Formichelli, also godmother. [C-only, check; E1 and E13 agree on the date, parents, and baptism on 26 May in Sant'Anna church]
- Parents: Grazio Mario Forgione (1860-1946), called "Orazio" by neighbours, and Maria Giuseppa De Nunzio (1859-1929), "Mamma Peppa". They married 8 June 1881. Both could not read or write. Peasant owners, not poor; food every day but "little cash". [E1, E13; C-only for the wedding date]
- Eight children: Michele (older), a first Francesco who died at 19 days, Amalia (died at 20 months), the future Padre Pio (given the dead brother's name; E13 says his mother, devoted to St Francis, chose it), then Felicita, Pellegrina, Grazia (later a Bridgettine nun), and Mario, who died before age one. E1 counts "two infants who died" and names the sisters. [E1; C-only for Amalia and Mario]
- Home: a one-room house in Vico Storto Valle (about 145 sq ft, three outside steps, iron bed, donkey and tools in a cellar room under it); a separate two-room kitchen section on the same street; later "La Torretta", a one-room tower with steep outside stairs, at no. 1. No running water, stove, or electricity. [C-only, check; E1 and E17 agree that La Torretta/the home existed]
- Family religion: bell at dawn gathers the family to prayer; daily Mass; nightly Rosary; fasting from meat three days a week in honour of Our Lady of Mt Carmel; neighbours called them "the God-is-everything people". Grandparents and parents could not read but knew Scripture by memory and told Bible stories. [E1]
- Land at Piana Romana outside town: grapes, wheat, maize, olives, figs, plums; sheep, goats, hens, a cow or two. Cottage there for storing tools and summer sleeping. At about six he got four sheep and a goat to mind, and met other child shepherds (Scocca, Orlando, Vecchiarino, Fucci names). [C-only; E1/E13 confirm the Piana Romana land and that he did not attend school regularly in childhood]
- Little school: E13 says he did not attend regularly because he worked the land; at 12 he studied with the priest Domenico Tizzani, who covered the elementary programme in two years, then the gymnasium course. First Communion and Confirmation 27 Sept 1899 from Archbishop Donato Maria Dell'Olio of Benevento. E1: confirmed 27 Sept 1899 and tutored; three years of public school. (Conflict: E13 and E1 differ on the amount of schooling.) [E1, E13]
- NEW: "Padre Pio said at age 5 he decided to give himself to God". E1 says at five he "solemnly consecrated himself to Jesus", sang hymns, played at church. E13 says from 1892 he told of daily visions of Our Lady of the Libera and the Sacred Heart coming to the altar rail of Santa Maria degli Angeli, and thought all children saw them. Hagiographic; his own later statements. [E1, E13]
- Vocation: in 1897 (E1) a Capuchin quester, fra Camillo da Sant'Elia a Pianisi, from Morcone, came to Pietrelcina; Francesco told his parents he wanted to be "a friar... with a beard". E13 dates the entry papers to spring 1902, first request refused, accepted autumn 1902. Grazio went to America to earn the money for tutors; he and Michele returned 1903 (C-only on year) and again 27 Nov 1912 (letter to Fr Agostino, per C-only). [E1, E13, E17]
- Scenes from neighbours' memories (all [C-only, check]; the fan site names the witnesses and a Capuchin book): he locked himself in the church with the sacristan Michele Peruto to pray; gave a new garment to a poor boy and came home half dressed ("I gave it to a little boy who needed it more than I did"); cut the ribbon that the neighbour Andrianella was sewing on a Sunday; neighbours called him "lu santariello nuostro" (our little saint); at age 8 his mother found him beating himself with a chain; he slept on the floor with a stone for a pillow (the priest Giuseppe Orlando reported it). Light side, same source: a pair of hated trousers ruined on purpose so his parents bought a new pair; broccoli remark ("That's a sin!") and then eating figs. Use only with a second source or mark as local memory.
- Baby crying (C-only): his father Grazio told the story that the baby cried all night and he threw him on the bed; Padre Pio at his father's funeral (1946) said he never cried again from that day. Unchecked; the saint also told a friar he suffered "from the time I was in my mother's womb". Treat as shrine lore. [C-only]
- Fortune teller (C-only): when he was a few months old his mother took him to a village seer, Giuseppe Faiella, who said "the whole world" would honour him, "a lot of money" would pass through his hands but he would own nothing, and that he would reach 98. Padre Pio died at 81. [C-only, check; a good ironic detail if confirmed]

## 2. Novitiate, illness, and the Pietrelcina years (1903-1916)
- 6 Jan 1903 enters novitiate at Morcone; 22 Jan 1903 habit, name Fra Pio. E1: named for St Pius V, patron of Pietrelcina. Travelled by oxcart to the seventeenth-century friary. [E1, E2]
- First vows 22 Jan 1904; to Sant'Elia a Pianisi 26 Jan 1904; 1905 San Marco la Catola (philosophy); 1906 Sant'Elia (gymnasium); 1907-08 Serracapriola (theology) with Padre Agostino da San Marco in Lamis as lector, who later wrote that he found him "buono, obbediente, studioso, sebbene malaticcio" (good, obedient, studious, though sickly). Solemn vows 27 Jan 1907. [E13; E17 for Serracapriola Oct 1908 — dates conflict between E13 (1907-08) and E17 (Oct 1908); keep "1907-1908"]
- Illness and tears at Serracapriola (E13): meditating the Passion, he wept so much that his eyes grew sick; Good Friday 17 April 1908, chest illness plus migraine; letter to "carissimi genitori" about the summer heat ("qui si sta un po' male... non v'impensierite"). The friars, alarmed at his chronic loss of appetite, summoned his father by telegram through the parish priest Salvatore Pannullo; Grazio took him home for a doctor-advised holiday. [E13]
- 1909: deacon 18 July at Morcone (E13, E17). 10 Aug 1910: ordained at Benevento cathedral; E13 says the bishop made an exception for his age (he was 23; canon law then required 24). First Mass 14 Aug 1910 at Our Lady of the Angels in Pietrelcina. [E1, E13]
- 1910 daily resolutions (C-only): four hours of meditation, no fewer than five Rosaries, nine novenas. [C-only]
- 1910-1916: lives in Pietrelcina with the family on health leave; keeps the habit, says Mass, teaches school (E1). After Mass he walks to Piana Romana, where his brother Michele built a hut (E13); under the elm trees he prays and reads (a search summary says the elm was the "elm of the stigmata"; the trunk is preserved in a church there; the National Centre for Padre Pio holds a piece of that tree, E18). Per E13 and the Vatican-linked E2, he said the first marks appeared at Piana Romana on the afternoon of 7 Sept 1910; E2 gives 7 Sept 1910 as the stigmata date. (Conflict with the 1918 date remains.) [E2, E13, E18]
- 8 Sept 1911 (E13) letter to the director in San Marco in Lamis says the marks had come "for almost a year", and that he stayed silent because of "quella maledetta vergogna". Next morning he told Don Salvatore Pannullo ("Zi' Tore"): "Zi' Tore, fatemi la carità, chiediamo a Gesù che mi tolga questa confusione. Voglio soffrire, morire di sofferenza, ma tutto nel nascondimento." The priest answered that he would help him pray but he must bend to God's will. [E13; E2 for the 8 Sept 1911 letter]
- 7 Dec 1911 to 17 Feb 1916: returns to Pietrelcina, living in his brother Michele's house for most of it (E13); E17 says he lived mostly at a house in Via Santa Maria degli Angeli 44 (bought by Grazio and Michele in 1903) because the steps of La Torretta were too hard for him. [E13, E17: conflict on the house; list both.]
- Oct-Nov 1911: Naples doctor Antonio Cardarelli examined him; E17 says he judged the friar's days "numbered" and sent him to Venafro for convalescence. At Venafro (a month and a half) Padre Agostino says, in his diary (via E17), that from Nov 1911 he and the superior Fr Evangelista saw many ecstasies and attacks of the devil. E1 gives the same 28 Nov 1911 scene: Agostino rushes in, finds a man who seems dying, prays in the chapel, returns, and finds him alert and joyful. This was the first recorded ecstasy (E1). [E1; C-only for Cardarelli and details of the attacks]
- Padre Pio's test for visions (C-only, from the diary): he told the apparition "Say Long live Jesus"; if it was the devil it vanished. [C-only]
- 1912 21 March: letter to Agostino about the Mystical Body and his sense of future stigmata (E15). [E15]
- 1915: Fr Agostino sent Padre Pio three written questions "in the name of Jesus" (when did visions begin, whether he had stigmata, whether he felt the crown of thorns and the scourging); he replied: visions since the novitiate; the marks had been visible, especially in one hand, and had gone away at his prayer, but the pain stayed; he suffered the thorns and flagellation most weeks. (E5 and E13 agree; E5 cites Ep. I p.659.) [E5, E13]

## 3. Military service, Foggia, San Giovanni Rotondo arrival (1915-1918)
- Draft: E13 says 6 Nov 1915 at Benevento, then 10th Company of the Medical Corps, Naples, 6 Dec 1915 (E15 says drafted 15 Nov 1915; E7 gives 15 Nov 1915 too: conflict 6 vs 15 Nov). Recruit no. 12094 (C-only). 17 Dec 1915 exam: "pulmonary infiltration", one year's leave (C-only). He returned to hospital 18 Dec 1916, got a further six months' leave on 30 Dec 1916 (C-only), training 4 Sept 1917, leave 5 Nov 1917, back 6 March 1918, discharged 16 March 1918 for "double bronchoalveolitis" (E13 and E15 and E7 agree on 16 March 1918). [E13, E15, E17 for details]
- E7: he had served "182 days" in all, none at the front (the article's count; source unnamed). [E7, weak]
- Foggia: 17 Feb 1916 he arrives at the Sant'Anna friary in Foggia (E13). There he suffered sweats and fever; E17 says terrifying noises from his room ended in a crash that shook the walls (devil's rage, he told Fr Benedetto). [E13; C-only]
- San Giovanni Rotondo: E13 says he first came on the evening of 28 July 1916 with Fr Paolino da Casacalenda, felt better, went back to Foggia after a week because the provincial's permission was slow, and on 13 Aug wrote asking to spend time there, because Jesus had assured him he would be better. 4 Sept 1916: ordered to the friary of Santa Maria delle Grazie for good (E1, E2). Seven friars lived there; the friary stood about a mile from the town. Four friars soon left for military service. Padre Pio taught at the seminary, directed the students' souls, and when another friar was called up he took charge of the college. He spent his free time reading the Bible and handling letters. [E1, E2, E13, E15]
- Mary Pyle's pink villa, 1928: wealthy heiress and spiritual daughter; she hosted his mother at Christmas 1928; his mother died January 1929 of pneumonia (en.wikipedia; first pass says 3 Jan 1929 from S3). Grazio lived at the villa for years until his death in 1946 (E15 says "eight years"; check). [E15]

## 4. Stigmata 20 Sept 1918: what the sources say about witnesses
- E5 (reading Padre Pio's letter of 22 Oct 1918 to Padre Benedetto, Ep. I no. 510): after Mass he was in the choir for thanksgiving; the vision of a mysterious figure whose hands, feet, and side dripped blood; "I thought I would die"; then he saw that his hands, feet, and side were pierced and bleeding; the author notes that "there was nobody about" and that he lay on the floor afterwards. (paraphrase)
- CONFLICT, important: the L'Osservatore Romano obituary of 1968 (E3) says "a confrere found Father Pio unconscious behind the high altar of the church", his habit blood-stained, wounds in hands and feet; the Vatican newspaper also says the date fell "on the same day on which the Church celebrates the Stigmata of St. Francis". The writers differ on where he fell (choir v. behind the high altar) and on the discovery. The writer should state: first-person account says choir and nobody; 1968 Vatican obituary says a friar found him. [E3, E5]
- Hiding: he wore red mittens or black coverings on hands and feet, saying he was embarrassed (E15). The doctors of 1919: Romanelli (Barletta civil hospital) on 15-16 May 1919 wrote (Italian, from E13): "Le lesioni che presenta alle mani sono ricoperte da una membrana di colore rosso bruno, senza alcun punto sanguinante, niente edema e niente reazione infiammatoria nei tessuti circostanti. Ho la certezza che quelle ferite non sono superficiali perché, applicando il pollice nel palmo della mano e l'indice sul dorso e facendo pressione, si ha la percezione esatta del vuoto esistente." Our English: the hand lesions are covered by a reddish-brown membrane, no bleeding point, no swelling, no inflammation; pressing thumb on the palm and finger on the back gives "the exact sensation of the void" (paraphrase of the end). He was sent "by order of the provincial superior". [E13]
- Bignami (Rome professor of medical pathology), 26 July 1919 (E13): did not differ from Romanelli on the findings but said the lesions started as pathological (multiple neurotonic necrosis of skin) and were completed, perhaps unconsciously by suggestion or with a chemical such as iodine tincture. [E13; en.wikipedia E15 says iodine; date conflict with 12 July in first-pass notes]
- 1920: E13: Gemelli visited on his own initiative; Padre Pio, backed by his superiors, said he needed written permission through the Holy Office; Gemelli left offended and called it a "bluff" and the friar "isterico e psicopatico" in his letter. First pass has this; new here: E13 notes (via Luzzatto) that Gemelli went without being asked, while Castelli says Cardinal Merry del Val commissioned him (E15 repeats the contest). [E13, E15]
- 1920 Dr Giorgio Festa measured his temperature twice a day; E13 says some readings of the "mystical fevers" were so high that a clinical thermometer could not register them (readings quoted by Renzo Allegri; first-pass notes said 48 C; unconfirmed by me). [E13]
- Early press: Il Giornale d'Italia 9 May 1919, then Il Tempo 1 and 3 June 1919 (soldier's cure; "I miracoli di Padre Pio"), then a full page in Il Mattino of Naples on 20 June 1919 (search summary of an unread page; E19). By Nov 1920 the Southern Cross (Australia) wrote: "That there is a priest in Southern Italy marked with the stigmata is scarcely known at the moment." It added that his wounds "gave him more pain than usual on every Friday" and that doctors had examined them "without being able to come to any opinion on them, except that they cannot be explained from a natural standpoint". [E7 quoting the 1920 Southern Cross]

## 5. Vatican restrictions 1922-1934, local protest, press
- 31 May 1923 decree: "non constat de supernaturalitate". Published in L'Osservatore Romano on 5 July 1923 and taken up by papers worldwide (E13). [E13, E7]
- London Observer, Rome correspondent, summer 1923 (E7; quote exact): "Padre Pio himself lays no claim to miraculous gifts; he is the most modest and simple of men. He has a mild, ascetic countenance, with a fair beard, and hair turning grey on his high temples. His eyes are kind yet piercing; his voice has a peculiar charm." The same article says "more than one crowned head" had gone to San Giovanni Rotondo, and that when a move was rumoured the town rose and marched to the friary and refused to leave until he showed himself at a window. 40 local Fascists took turns guarding the building so he could not be taken in secret. (Observer wording for this part is the Irish Catholic's paraphrase; see E7.) NEW detail for English pages. [E7]
- Mgr Paschal Robinson (later nuncio to Ireland) took part in the inquiries (E7 says so; not verified). [E7]
- Autumn 1926 (E7, unverified): Holy Office decree forbidding Catholics to print, read, sell, lend, borrow, translate, or spread pamphlets about him; the booklets were placed on the Index. 1950: eight Italian books by enthusiasts put on the Index (E7); a warning against alleged "prophecies of doom" followed (E7). [E7 only; check]
- 15 Dec 1924 Dr Festa asked for a new exam and was refused (E13). 23 May 1931 fifth decree; Mass only in private, no confessions. E2 (L'Osservatore Romano 2009) gives 9 June 1931 as the date of the order to stop everything but private Mass (conflict with 23 May in E13 and first-pass S3; keep both). [E2, E13]
- Lifting: E2: early 1933 Pius XI ordered the ban on public Mass reversed; 25 March 1934 men's confession; E13: July 1933 revoked; in a report by Mgr Cornelio Cuccarollo OFM, Pius XI said Padre Pio had been "più che reintegrato" and added "è la prima volta che il Santo Uffizio si rimangia i suoi decreti" (our English: it is the first time the Holy Office eats its own decrees). E13 reports it; source of the quote is cited inside E13 as notes 39-41 (not read). Also the "badly informed" line is in E15. [E2, E13, E15]
- In 1939 Pius XII "encouraged people to visit Padre Pio" (E2). [E2]

## 6. Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza and the Prayer Groups
- Beginning: E6 says an old convent was turned into a small clinic of a few beds for extreme cases in 1925. At the end of 1939 he spoke again of a hospital with a group of men. 9 Jan 1940: first collaborators each give a small donation; E6: "I also want to give my offering", he said and handed over a ten-cent Swiss franc received that morning. He named it "Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza". E15 puts the quote "This evening my earthly work has begun" on the same day. [E6, E15]
- Committee (E14): chaired by Padre Pio, with Mario Sanvico, Guglielmo Sanguinetti, Carlo Kisvarday; architect Angelo Lupi. 1946 a company formed; construction begins 1947 (E14); foundation stone 16 May 1947 (E2); the first workers had no architectural plan and 4 million lire in the bank (E6). [E2, E6, E14]
- Funding (E2, Vatican newspaper): Barbara Ward, a British humanitarian worker, heard a Mass of Padre Pio, met one of his doctors, and asked that part of UNRRA's reconstruction fund go to the Home; UNRRA granted US $325,000. E16 (McCaffery) says Emanuele Brunatto laid the foundations of the fund, Ward obtained a substantial early gift as a memorial to Fiorello La Guardia, and the fund for the poor bore the name of a poor Italian emigrant worker, Mario Gambino; Padre Pio kept a 50-lira note offered by a poor widow in his habit pocket and showed it, sometimes in tears. E15 says it was first to be named "Fiorello LaGuardia" and cites Luzzatto on a contribution by Brunatto of 3.5 million francs (contested). [E2, E15, E16]
- 1954 outpatient clinic opens (first resident doctor Luigi Pancaro, who started the blood bank). 5 May 1956: Casa opens with 250 beds, surgery with two operating theatres, medicine and cardiology, orthopaedics, paediatrics, obstetrics, radiology, lab and transfusion; it has its own farm for milk, eggs, poultry, and oil. Consultants include Valdoni (surgery), Cassano, Bacialli, Marino Zuco, Salvioli, Toniolo. [E14; E6 for 250 beds and farm]
- McCaffery (E16, paraphrase): the opening day was bright and sunny. The recruiting was done by Gino Ghisleri, who searched Italy for doctors, nurses, and a body of nursing sisters; a Milan sister said she prayed with Padre Pio each morning at the time of his Mass. Professor Pietro Valdoni helped to bring the right men and to make the cardiology conference that went with the opening an international event. [E16 pp.43-45]
- Padre Pio's words at the opening (E6 quoting the Casa): "a seed has been sown..." (a place of prayer and science); 1957: patients, doctors, priests "shall be reserves of love". At the 10th anniversary in 1966 he called prayer the thing that "sustains the Casa". Of the work: "The Work is not mine... but Providence's." If he could, he said, he would build the hospital in gold. [E6]
- 1957: Pius XII dispensed him from his vow of poverty so he could oversee the project (E15). The Casa was later passed to the Holy See; E14 says Padre Pio was only "azionista" (shareholder) until death and named the Holy See legatee in his will. [E14, E15]
- 1964: Paul VI restored full freedom and moved Padre Pio's financial activities to the Holy See (E13). [E13]
- Prayer groups: after Pius XII asked for groups to pray (E6, E15 say 1942 wartime appeal; E15 says the 1950s), Padre Pio urged devotees to form them; a bulletin named after the hospital, from 1949, helped them grow (E15). E6 quotes him: "Without prayer, our House for the Relief of Suffering is somewhat like a plant without air and sun", and calls the groups the "frontline of this little City of charity". In 1968: about 700 groups, 68,000 members, 15 countries; 2013: 3,300 groups, about 3 million members, 60 countries (E15). The Friday before he died, delegates from Switzerland, Austria, England, America, India, Brazil and France gathered at San Giovanni Rotondo for the 50th anniversary of the stigmata (E3). [E3, E6, E15]

## 7. Famous visitors (with exact lines where possible)
- Wojtyla 1947 or 1948: conflict. E10 (Capuchins USA) and first-pass S5/S15 say the summer/spring of 1947 (priest less than a year, studying St John of the Cross in Rome); E9 says April 1948. E10: stayed nearly a week, attended Mass, confessed, "apparently the two spoke at length". E9: Wojtyla noticed the black scab covering the stigmata on the hands during Mass. E9 quotes his later handwritten Polish letter to the guardian of the friary: "I spoke with him in person and exchanged a few words, it was my first meeting with him and I consider it the most important" (translation, original not seen). E9's question about the wound: Wojtyla asked which hurt most; Padre Pio said the shoulder wound, not the heart, "the one on my back" (translation; E9 only). In 1987 John Paul II said the places "sono legati a ricordi personali, cioè alle visite da me fatte a padre Pio sia durante la sua vita terrena, sia, spiritualmente, dopo la morte" (links to personal memories: visits to Padre Pio in life and, in spirit, after death). [E9, E10, E12]
- 2002 canonization homily line as given by E9: "I too, in my youth, had the privilege of benefiting from his availability to penitents." (first pass has the Vatican text.) [E9]

## 8. Daily routine
- E4 (EWTN, unsourced): 3 am he rose and prayed for hours; Mass not before 5 am and lasting up to 2 hours; blessed and talked to people in the sacristy while vesting; thanksgiving in the choir loft; confessions the morning and several hours in the afternoon; noon lunch in the refectory; 30 minutes in the garden; short siesta, Rosary; afternoon Office; private prayer sometimes 2 hours; letters; more confessions; 4:30 pm Vespers and Benediction; at supper juice and a cracker ("By 1950 he was eating only an ounce of food a day"); at sundown he blessed the people under the choir window; compline; he visited Padre Agostino's room each night for a blessing; read the Bible until 12:45 am, then Matins with about 20 friars, bed at 1:30 am. That is the E4 claim; the schedule does not name a year (probably the 1950s). Summary: about 1.5 hours of sleep. [E4]
- McCaffery (E16 pp.56-57, paraphrase): he said his day revolved around the confessional; for fifty years he sat there hour after hour; he ate only a spoonful of soup or a forkful of spaghetti, rice, or vegetables once a day in the refectory under obedience; he rose well before four to prepare for Mass; his night was hardly sleep. McCaffery served his Mass seven times and describes a Latin Mass lasting about an hour and a half; Padre Pio gave Communion in the Mass only for a local child or someone brought specially. The Mass was on the St Francis side altar of the old church. [E16]
- Booking: by 1950 so many wanted to confess to him that a booking system was set up (E13). [E13]
- The 1964 Paul VI restoration and the Latin Mass indult (E13): Paul VI allowed him to keep saying the old Latin rite publicly even after the 1965 reform. [E13]

## 9. 1960s tensions
- E15 (secondary): under John XXIII, opponents put listening devices in the cell and the confessional; the Pope's private notes called him a "straw idol" (idolo di stoppa); Fr Carlo Maccari (secretary-general of the Rome diocese) made a visitation in 1960, with nine meetings, and recommended moving the friars gradually, an outside superior, limiting confession to once a month per person, and new statutes for the hospital. Maccari's diary: "Reticence, narrowness of mind, lies — these are the weapons he uses to evade my questions ... Overall impression: pitiful." [E15, from Luzzatto 2007 per E13]
- E13: John XXIII's note of 25 June 1960 (published by Luzzatto in 2007) says: "Mi dispiace di P.P. che ha pur un'anima da salvare, e per cui prego intensamente" (I am sorry for P.P., who has a soul to save, and for whom I pray intensely). He also wrote of the claimed discovery of improper relations with the women of his circle "if what is reported is true". Tapes were reported to the Pope in June 1960 by Mgr Pietro Parente. [E13]
- A rebuttal anecdote (E13, from Fr Carmelo Durante): Archbishop Andrea Cesarano of Manfredonia told John XXIII that the "kisses" on the tapes were his own sister kissing the friar's hand; John XXIII is said to have lifted his eyes and said "Sia lodato Dio!" Source: a Capuchin author; unverified. [E13]
- E13: local guardian in the 1960s was Fr Rosario da Aliminusa, who wrote that Padre Pio never failed in obedience; 30 July 1964 Paul VI, through Cardinal Ottaviani, restored all freedom of ministry. [E13]
- Letter on Humanae Vitae: E15 says that after the encyclical (25 July 1968) he wrote to Paul VI, dated 12 Sept 1968 (per a search summary), affirming obedience and promising his prayers and sufferings. Text not read (the EWTN letter page and Avvenire gave a 404); to check. [E15, E19]

## 10. Death and funeral (1968)
- E3 (Vatican newspaper, 3 Oct 1968): died 23 Sept at 2:30 a.m. at 81; gave the 20 Sept 1918 date and the stigmata story; named the hospital and the Prayer Groups. [E3]
- E2 (Vatican newspaper 2009): 20 Sept 1968 (E2 says Mass on the 50th anniversary of the stigmata) he celebrated Mass; "three days later, soon after midnight" he called the superior, confessed, and renewed his vows; died at 2:30 am on 23 Sept in his cell. Funeral 26 Sept; more than 100,000. Buried in the crypt of Our Lady of Grace. [E2] (First pass: last Mass 22 Sept per en.wikipedia and E13; E2's "20 Sept" is a conflict.)
- E15/E13: 22 Sept Mass was a Solemn Mass because of the crowds; he was so frail that he nearly fell on the altar steps and friars held him; TV crews present. [E13, E15]
- Funicelli's written account of 29 Sept 1968 (E8, from the Spanish; our paraphrase): After 9 pm on 22 Sept he called Fr Pellegrino by intercom and asked the time; his eyes were red from a "gentle, calm" weeping; at midnight "like a frightened child" he said "Stay with me, my son", then asked "Have you said Mass?" and said "then you will say it for me this morning". He asked to confess, then asked Funicelli to beg forgiveness from the brothers for all the trouble he had caused and to ask for prayers for his soul; he gave a blessing to the brothers, spiritual children, and sick; he sent love to his niece Pia, Ettoruccio (his nephew), and Sister Pia ("they know how much I loved them"); he renewed his religious profession. At one o'clock, wanting air, he asked to get up; he walked straight and fast "like a young man" to the terrace, sat five minutes, looked around as if seeking something, then could not rise; Funicelli wheeled him back in a wheelchair, and he said "take it out". Then he grew pale, sweating, lips livid, repeating "Jesus, Mary" ever more faintly, and said "do not wake anyone"; Funicelli ran for Fra Guglielmo and Dr Sala; Dr Gusso and Dr Scarale and his nephew Mario Pennelli were called; oxygen was given; Fr Paolo of San Giovanni Rotondo gave the anointing of the sick; at about 2:30 "he gently bowed his head on his chest" and was dead. (First pass has a summary from McCaffery; E8 is the primary witness.) [E8]
- Wounds after death: E13: friars at the preparation of the body saw no trace; photos confirm; the doctor at the bedside found the wounds "completely healed, without trace or scar" (E13; en.wikipedia agrees). The wounds had faded "a few days before" death (E13). [E13, E15]
- Funeral: body in a coffin in the church for pilgrims; funeral on 26 Sept in the church, more than 100,000 people from all over Italy (E13); procession through the town (E15). [E13, E15]
