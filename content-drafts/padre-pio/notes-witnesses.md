## 1. Identity
- Francesco Forgione, b. 25 May 1887 Pietrelcina (Benevento); d. 23 Sept 1968 San Giovanni Rotondo. Religious name Fra Pio, then Padre Pio; St Pio of Pietrelcina. [S1] [S3]
- Parents Grazio Forgione and Maria Giuseppa De Nunzio (Di Nunzio). Baptised the next day. [S1]
- Beatified 2 May 1999; canonized 16 June 2002 (both John Paul II, St Peter's Square). Feast 23 Sept (obligatory memorial, set 2002). [S1] [S3]
- Capuchin (OFM Cap). Catholic only (playbook latin-italy).

## 3. Timeline
- 1887 25 May: born Pietrelcina to Grazio Forgione and Maria Giuseppa De Nunzio; baptised 26 May as Francesco. [S1]
- Childhood (peasant family with land at Piana Romana; looked after the sheep until about 10; family prayed the rosary nightly; parents could not read but told Bible stories; ill with typhoid at 10 per Agostino's diary). Reported by Wikipedia from the diary of Agostino da San Marco in Lamis. NEW detail to verify in the diary. [S6]
- 1897-1903: Capuchin from Sant'Elia a Pianisi visited; Grazio went to the United States for work to pay tutoring. Confirmation: S1 says "at 12" (Cresima and first Communion); S6 gives 27 Sept 1899. [S1] [S6]
- 1903 6 Jan: enters novitiate at Morcone; 22 Jan takes habit, name Fra Pio. [S1] [S3] (S3 gives 2 Jan for arrival: conflict, see Other.)
- 1907 27 Jan: solemn vows. [S1] [S3]
- 1905-1909: illness (loss of appetite, fainting, vomiting, digested only milk and cheese per S6). Sent to mountain houses, then home to Pietrelcina in early 1909. One friar claimed to see him in ecstasy and levitating (S6, report only). [S3] [S6]
- 1909 18 July: deacon at Morcone. 1910 10 Aug: ordained priest in the chapel of Benevento Cathedral (S3; S6 says by Archbishop Paolo Schinosi). First solemn Mass 14 Aug at Pietrelcina. [S1] [S3] [S6]
- 1910 7 Sept (per S5): pastor Salvatore Pannullo of Pietrelcina said Padre Pio showed him wounds in his hands and asked him to pray for them to go away. In 1918 Pannullo reportedly said: "You see them now. I saw them in 1910." S5 (fan site; check). S3 says he first received the stigmata in 1910, citing Letters I pp.264-265. NEW for English pages. [S3] [S5]
- 1910-1916: lives mostly at Pietrelcina in the habit, because of illness; letters to his directors Agostino and Benedetto of San Marco in Lamis. 1911 Venafro: confined to bed; "extraordinary phenomena". [S1] [S3] [S6]
- 1915 6 Nov: drafted; 6 Dec Medical Corps, Naples; repeatedly discharged and recalled; 16 Mar 1918 declared unfit ("double bronchial pneumonia"). [S3] [S6]
- 1916 4 Sept (S1, S6) to San Giovanni Rotondo; he stayed to the end of his life, 52 years. S3 gives a first 8-day visit on 28 July 1916. Cell no. 5 (S5). [S1] [S3]
- 1918 5-7 Aug: transverberation. 1918 20 Sept: stigmata after Mass in the choir loft of Our Lady of Grace. He reported (S5 translation): sea of light, saw Jesus, rays from his wounds. S5 quotes Fr Joseph that nobody knew for nine days until a friar found blood on the sheets; check origin. [S3] [S5]
- 1918-19: Spanish flu. Padre Pio ran the boys' Seraphic College; S5 says no deaths reported there, and he secretly obtained disinfectant from a Foggia pharmacist's sister. Check. [S5]
- 1919 1 May: Dr Angelo Maria Merla examines (S5). 15-16 May: Dr Luigi Romanelli (head surgeon, Barletta) examines, five visits to July 1920. [S3] [S5]
- 1919 Bignami (Rome pathology professor): S3 says 26 July; S5 says 12 July. His view: lesions produced unconsciously by autosuggestion and kept with iodine (S5). Conflict on dates. [S3] [S5]
- 1919 Festa: S3 says 9 Oct; S5 says first visit 28 Oct. S5: Festa saw him again 9-15 July 1920 with Romanelli, and 28 Sept 1925. [S3] [S5]
- 1920 18 Apr: Fr Agostino Gemelli visits, asks to examine the wounds, Padre Pio asks for written authorization, Gemelli has none, is refused. Gemelli wrote a negative report (S5, no primary seen). 
- 1920: Archbishop Kenealy (24-27 March; S5 reports his remark "If he knows how to suffer, he also knows how to laugh"); Archbishop Cerretti; papal physician Giuseppe Bastianelli 20 July 1920 favourable. All via S5 only.
- 1921 14 June: Bishop Raffaello Carlo Rossi of Volterra, secret Holy Office visitor, eight days. S5, citing Castelli (2011): good religious; "serene, jovial, even humorous"; he smelled a violet-like fragrance and found only plain soap in the cell; judged the stigmata real. NEW for English pages. [S5]
- 1922 2 June: Holy Office first measures. 1923 31 May: finds no "supernatural nature". 17 June: Mass only in private chapel, no answering letters. 26 June: people's protest, Mass again in church. 30 July order to move to Ancona; people's agitation stops it (17 Aug). [S3]
- 1929 3 Jan: his mother dies at San Giovanni Rotondo (Mary Pyle's house hosted her at Christmas 1928 per S6). [S3] [S6]
- 1931: Holy See strips all priestly activity except private Mass (S3: 23 May; Search summary of S7 says 9 June). 1933 16 July: public Mass returns. 1934: men's confessions 25 Mar, women's 12 May. [S3]
- 1938-1940: clinic destroyed in an earthquake (S6). 1940 9 Jan: committee for the hospital founded. 1946 7 Oct: father dies. 1947 19 May: building starts. [S3] [S6]
- 1956 5 May: Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza dedicated; S1 cites "inaugurata il 5 maggio 1956". Conference of specialists and audience with Pius XII before the opening (S4 pp.45-46: Paul Dudley White at Padre Pio's side took photographs and said "congratulations on your wounds"; Pius XII showed deep respect). [S1] [S3] [S4]
- 1957: Pius XII gave a dispensation from his vow of poverty so he could oversee the hospital (S6). Opponents later alleged misuse of funds (S6).
- 1959 1 July: new church dedicated. 1965-66: permission to continue Latin Mass, then to celebrate seated. [S3]
- 1968 22 Sept: last Mass (solemn, 50th anniversary of stigmata), very frail. 23 Sept, about 2:30: died. S4: that night he asked to confess, renewed religious profession, asked Padre Pellegrino to pass on his blessing to his spiritual children, asked "Have you said Mass yet?"; stood and walked to the terrace without his wheelchair; then collapsed, repeating "Jesus, Mary"; Dr Gusso: "most serene passing he had ever witnessed" (paraphrase). Died in habit, rosary in hand. S4 says the stigmata had disappeared gradually before death; S6 says the doctor found them healed with no scar. Funeral 26 Sept, about 100,000. [S3] [S4 pp.125-126] [S6]
- 1971 20 Feb: Paul VI to Capuchin superiors on his fame (see quotations). [S1]
- Cause: nulla osta 29 Nov 1982; diocesan process 1983-1990; validity 7 Dec 1990; heroic virtues decree 18 Dec 1997; miracle (Consiglia De Martino) decree 21 Dec 1998; miracle (Matteo Pio Colella) decree 20 Dec 2001; canonization decree 26 Feb 2002. [S1]

## 4. Character (from witnesses)
- Humour: McCaffery says his memories of Padre Pio are "permeated by humour more than by anything else" (paraphrase). [S4 p.12]
- Rossi 1921: "serene, jovial, even humorous" with brethren. [S5]
- Humble by nature: when told a Jesuit superior wondered how his humility survived the crowds, he laughed hard and said he would be mad or a thief to keep for himself what belonged to God (watch image: a gold watch carried to Milan for repair). [S4 pp.62-63; paraphrase]
- Said of the perfume that it was "just sweets for the children" (paraphrase of S4 p.9).
- Hid the pain: told McCaffery the nights were worse than the days, because pain of the wounds multiplied if he slept. [S4 p.64]
- Wrote holy-picture notes for visitors, laughed at his "scrawls" (S4 p.65). The effort showed in the movement of his chin.
- Mock horror at "A Rector! God save us from all such!" (S4 p.79; paraphrase).
- Told a husband he would take fifty per cent of a talkative wife, and the husband the other fifty (S4 p.78; paraphrase).
- Severe confessor: S2 says he sometimes treated pilgrims with apparent harshness, and nearly all came back for forgiveness. [S2]
- Critics: Gemelli (negative report), Bignami (autosuggestion). Admirers: Romanelli, Bastianelli, Rossi. [S5]
- Wojtyla anecdote (S5): in 1947 he asked which wound hurt most; shoulder wound. Unchecked.

## 5. Quotations (exact copies from fetched text; all rights facts-only unless noted)
1. "Nei libri cerchiamo Dio, nella preghiera Lo troviamo. La preghiera è la chiave che apre il cuore di Dio" — our English: In books we look for God; in prayer we find him. Prayer is the key that opens God's heart. [S1 official bio] 
2. "Voglio essere soltanto un povero frate che prega" — I want to be only a poor friar who prays. [S1]
3. "Sono un povero frate che prega" — I am a poor friar who prays. [S1 homily 2002]
4. "Il Signore mi fece vedere, come in uno specchio, tutta la mia vita futura non essere altro che un martirio" — The Lord showed me, as in a mirror, that my whole future life would be only a martyrdom. [S1, epigraph]
5. "Sotto la Croce s'impara ad amare" — Under the Cross one learns to love (words Jesus gave him; Epist. I p.339). [S1]
6. "Il Calvario è il monte dei Santi" — Calvary is the mountain of the saints. [S1]
7. "Abbandonatevi pienamente sul cuore divino di Gesù, come un bimbo tra le braccia della madre" — Give yourselves fully to the divine heart of Jesus, like a child in its mother's arms. [S1 1999 homily]
8. Paul VI, 20 Feb 1971: "Era un uomo di preghiera e di sofferenza" — He was a man of prayer and suffering. Full passage in S1 asks why his fame: not a philosopher, not a scholar, no means; "diceva la Messa umilmente, confessava dal mattino alla sera". [S1]
9. John Paul II 1999: "Quando ero studente qui a Roma, ebbi io stesso occasione di conoscerlo personalmente" — When I was a student here in Rome I met him myself. [S1]
10. Padre Pio on the wounds (translation from S5, unsourced, check before use): "Do you think they are given for decoration?"
11. Festa's question and answer, via S5 translation: "Why should they have been in other parts of the body and not here?" Check.
12. To McCaffery (S4 p.64, paraphrase): "It is the nights."

## 7. Miracles (recorded; all unless stated are witness reports, not approved)
- 1995 Nov, Salerno: Consiglia De Martino, rupture of thoracic duct; approved (decree 21 Dec 1998; beatification miracle). [S1] [S3]
- 2000 Jan, San Giovanni Rotondo: Matteo Pio Colella, age 7, meningitis in septicaemic form; recovered; approved (decree 20 Dec 2001; canonization miracle). [S3] [S1]
- Post-war, Padua businessman cured of double lung embolism after a bearded monk appeared at his bedside; he later recognised the face in a photograph and Padre Pio asked how his lungs were. Reporter McCaffery (heard on a train); witness report only. [S4 pp.1-3]
- Fr Dominic (American Capuchin) cured of cancer; his father reportedly helped in bilocation at death in Milwaukee. Reported by McCaffery, shrine lore. [S4 ch.II]
- Perfume ("odour of sanctity") widely reported, incl. Rossi 1921 and McCaffery repeatedly. Popular. [S4 ch.III; S5]
- Stigmata themselves: 1910 (per S3), 1918 (visible); disappeared before death (S4, S6). Medical studies: Romanelli "not natural", Bignami autosuggestion, Bastianelli favourable (S5).
