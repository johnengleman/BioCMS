In 1222 a student in Bologna watched a preacher with a dirty habit and a plain face bring noble clans who had feuded for generations to make peace. Then the crowd pushed forward to tear scraps from his robe. The preacher was Francis of Assisi, once the young spender who paid for his friends' feasts. He gave his clothes back to his father in front of a bishop, kissed a leper's hand, and walked unarmed into a Sultan's camp. He founded an Order of thousands, and then he had to keep it poor. This is the story of what owning nothing cost him, and why the world still comes to his grave.
