## SC-11 — Christmas night, 1223 (Greccio)
- Status: full
- When and where: Christmas night, "in the third year before the day of his glorious decease" [HC 1C 84]; Bonaventure: "three years before his death" [HB X.7]. Greccio, in the open at the "place" (the manger was set up, not in a house).
- Who was there: Francis; John of Greccio, a noble of the town whom Francis loved, sent for about fifteen days before [HC 1C 84]; brothers called from many "places"; the men and women of the town with tapers and torches; a priest who said Mass [HC 1C 85]. Bonaventure calls John a knight who had left the secular army, and makes him the visionary [HB X.7].
- What happened, in order:
  1. About fifteen days before Christmas Francis tells John to prepare a manger, hay, ox and ass [HC 1C 84]. Bonaventure adds that Francis first "sought and obtained licence from the Supreme Pontiff", so it would not seem an innovation [HB X.7].
  2. Brothers gather; townspeople bring tapers and torches; "The night was lit up as the day" [HC 1C 85].
  3. Francis arrives, sees all ready and rejoices; "full of sighs, overcome with tenderness and filled with wondrous joy" [HC 1C 85]. Bonaventure: "bathed in tears, and overflowing with joy" [HB X.7].
  4. Mass over the manger; Francis, vested as a deacon, sings the Gospel "with sonorous voice", then preaches on "the birth of the poor King and the little town of Bethlehem" [HC 1C 86].
  5. A vision: "a certain man" sees a lifeless child in the manger whom Francis seems to rouse [HC 1C 86]. Bonaventure: the knight John saw a child asleep, who woke when Francis "embraced Him in both arms" [HB X.7].
  6. Afterwards the hay is kept; sick animals eat it and are healed; women in hard labour are delivered; an altar and church are raised over the manger [HC 1C 87].
- Details: ox and ass; hay; torches; woodland rings with voices and rocks answer [HC 1C 85]. Celano's aside: Francis said "Bethlehem" "in the manner of a sheep bleating", and licked his lips "relishing" the name "Jesus" [HC 1C 86].
- Words spoken: Francis to John: "I would make memorial of that Child who was born in Bethlehem, and in some sort behold with bodily eyes His infant hardships; how He lay in a manger on the hay, with the ox and the ass standing by." (Howell's English; speaker Francis; HC 1C 84). Francis at Christmas: "I would that the very walls should eat flesh on such a day, or, if they cannot, that they should at any rate be greased outside!" (to Brother Morico, a separate occasion, undated) [HC 2C 199]. Also 2C 200: oxen and asses to be given extra corn and hay on that day.
- Inner life: Celano says the Incarnation's humility and the Passion's charity filled his memory [HC 1C 84]; Christmas was "the feast of feasts", God "become a tiny infant, hung upon a human breast" [HC 2C 199].
- Testimony (Celano, public-domain, 1C 86, part): "The Saint of God was vested with Levitical ornaments, for he was a Levite, and with sonorous voice chanted the holy Gospel — an earnest, sweet, clear and loud-sounding voice, inviting all to the highest rewards. Then he preached to the people who stood around, and uttered mellifluous words concerning the birth of the poor King and the little town of Bethlehem. … There the gifts of the Almighty were multiplied, and a vision of wondrous efficacy was seen by a certain man; for in the manger he saw a little child lying lifeless, to whom the Saint of God seemed to draw near and (as it were) to rouse the child from the lethargy of sleep."
- What changed: a Mass over a manger of hay made the poor Child present; the place became a church.
- Not in the sources: the date "25 December" is not written in 1C 84 (it says "birthday of our Lord"); no Pope's name; no baby doll or real child; no cave; no winter weather; Celano names no priest. Celano does not name John as the visionary; Bonaventure does (writer: "a certain man", or "tradition names John"). Francis not called "deacon" by Celano except "Levite"; Bonaventure: "Levite of Christ".
- Sources: HC 1C 84-87, 2C 199-200; HB X.7.

## SC-12 — Seraph and parchment, September 1224 (La Verna)
- Status: full
- When and where: Lent from the Assumption (15 Aug) to St Michael (29 Sept), "two years before his death" [Leo's note, HQ p.199]. The vision "about the Feast of the Exaltation of Holy Cross" (14 Sept), "on a certain morning", on the mountain side [HB XIII.3]. Celano gives no date: "while he dwelt in the hermitage … called Alverna, two years before he gave back his soul" [HC 1C 94].
- Who was there: Francis alone at the vision. Later: Leo (the tempted brother; Howell's note names Leo [HC 2C 49 n.]); Brother Illuminato [HB XIII.4]; Rufino, who touched the side [HC 1C 95]; an unnamed brother and Pacifico at the later hiding stories [HC 2C 135, 137].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Francis begins a Lent in honour of St Michael; he opens the Gospel book three times and finds the Passion each time [HB XIII.1-2].
  2. A six-winged seraph descends, between the wings a crucified Man; Francis feels joy and grief at once and cannot understand it [HC 1C 94; HB XIII.3].
  3. Nail marks appear in hands and feet; the right side shows a scar that "often shed forth blood" [HC 1C 94-95].
  4. Francis wonders whether to tell; Brother Illuminato persuades him; he tells the vision but says that the Seraph spoke words he would never reveal [HB XIII.4].
  5. Leo, tempted "not of the flesh but of the spirit", longs for a writing from Francis's hand; Francis, unasked, says "Bring me paper and ink" and writes God's praises and a blessing; the temptation flees [HC 2C 49]. The parchment survives with Leo's note (see below).
  6. Francis descends at St Michael's feast carrying the marks [HB XIII.5]; he hides them for years [HC 1C 95-96; 2C 135-136].
- Details: nails with round black heads in the palms, points bent back "formed of the flesh itself" [HB XIII.3]. He washed only his fingers, held out his sleeve to be kissed, wore woollen socks with skin over the wounds [HC 2C 136]. Rufino's hand slipped to the side while scratching him; Francis was distressed and prayed the Lord to forgive Rufino [HC 1C 95].
- Words spoken: To a brother who saw his feet: "Mind your own business" [HC 2C 135]. When another saw the bloody tunic: "Ask what that is, if you don't know it to be an eye" [HC 2C 136]. To Leo: "Take this paper, and keep it diligently till the day of thy death" [HC 2C 49]. Leo's note on the parchment, Latin (Quaracchi's print, OCR-cleaned): "Beatus Franciscus duobus annis ante mortem suam fecit quadragesimam in loco montis Alvernae in honorem beatae Virginis Mariae, matris Domini, et beati Michaelis archangeli a festo Assumptionis sanctae Mariae Virginis usque ad festum sancti Michaelis septembris. Et facta est super eum manus Domini; post visionem et allocutionem seraphim et impressionem stigmatum Christi in corpore suo fecit has laudes ex alio latere chartae scriptas et manu sua scripsit gratias agens Domino de beneficio sibi collato." Own English: Blessed Francis, two years before his death, kept a Lent on Mount Alverna in honour of the Virgin and St Michael, from the Assumption to St Michael's feast in September. "The hand of the Lord was upon him." After the vision and the Seraph's words and the stigmata he made these praises, written on the other side, giving thanks to the Lord for the gift. Below, Leo adds: "Beatus Franciscus scripsit manu sua istam benedictionem mihi fratri Leoni", and last: "Simili modo fecit istud signum thau cum capite manu sua" (HQ p.199-200). Quaracchi says the side with the Praises is now mostly unreadable.
- Praises, Latin (HQ p.124-125; public-domain): "Tu es sanctus Dominus Deus solus, qui facis mirabilia … Tu es humilitas. Tu es patientia. … Tu es magna dulcedo nostra. Tu es vita aeterna nostra, magnus et admirabilis Dominus, Deus omnipotens, misericors Salvator." Blessing: "Benedicat tibi Dominus et custodiat te. Ostendat faciem suam tibi et misereatur tui. Convertat vultum suum ad te et det tibi pacem. Dominus benedicat, f. Leo, te." The text printed is from Assisi codex 344 (14th c.), copied from the autograph, because the autograph side is faded [HQ p.200].
- Inner life: Bonaventure: the vision taught him that he would be like Christ "not by martyrdom of body, but by enkindling of heart" [HB XIII.3]. Celano: he was "not vain in his heart" and kept the verse "I have hidden Thy words in my heart" [HC 1C 96].
- What changed: Francis carries Christ's wounds; his own writing, the Praises, now exists beside the parchment's blessing for Leo.
- Not in the sources: no date "14 Sept" in Celano or in Leo's note; Leo is not named as present at the vision; no seraph's spoken words; Celano does not call it "La Verna" or give a season; the later hiding stories are undated and have no place.
- Sources: HC 1C 94-96, 2C 49, 135-136; HB XIII.1-5; HQ p.124-125, 198-200.

## SC-13 — The promise, late 1225 (San Damiano)
- Status: full
- When and where: Mirror: "Two years before his death", in "a certain little cell builded of wattles" at San Damiano; eyes so sore that for "sixty days and more" he could not bear daylight or fire [HM p.175-176]. Celano II gives no place and no year, only "one night" [HC 2C 213]. (Outline: late 1225.)
- Who was there: Francis; "his companions" (unnamed) [HM p.176-178].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Mice swarm the cell night and day, run over him, climb on his table; he and his companions see "a temptation of the devil" [HM p.176].
  2. One night, "moved with pity of his own case", he prays for patience [HM p.177]. Celano's version: "he began in the depth of his heart to feel some pity for himself" and prayed to Christ [HC 2C 213].
  3. A voice in spirit asks if he would not rejoice for a treasure that made the whole earth nothing; he answers yes; "be glad and rejoice in thine infirmities … as if thou hadst already entered into My Kingdom" [HM p.177]. Celano's voice: "thy sickness is the earnest of My kingdom" [HC 2C 213].
  4. In the morning he tells his companions the parable of the Emperor and the realm and says he will make "a new song of praise" of the creatures [HM p.177-178].
  5. He sits, meditates, then says "Most high, almighty and most gracious Lord", "and made music thereupon", and teaches his companions to sing it [HM p.178]. Celano says only that "Then it was that he composed certain 'Praises concerning the Creatures'" [HC 2C 213].
  6. He wants Brother Pacifico, "King of Verse", to go with brothers through the world preaching and singing, calling them "minstrels of the Lord" [HM p.178-179].
- Details: Francis called his pains "sisters" [HC 2C 212]. Mice came at the table even while he ate [HM p.176].
- Words spoken: "Tell me, brother, if a certain man in return for these thine infirmities … would give thee a treasure so great and precious … wouldst thou not greatly rejoice thereat?" (the voice; HM p.177). "Great, verily, O Lord, would be that treasure" (Francis; HM p.177). Celano: "I should rejoice indeed … yea, and above measure I should rejoice" (Francis; HC 2C 213). Francis to companions: "We be the minstrels of the Lord, and this largesse do we crave of you, to wit, that ye shall be in the state of true repentance" (HM p.179).
- Inner life: "giving thanks unto God … that He hath condescended unto me His unworthy servant still living in the flesh, to certify me as concerning His Kingdom" [HM p.178].
- Testimony (Celano, public-domain, 2C 213, part): "And at length as he prayed amid the conflict, there was vouchsafed to him from the Lord the promise of eternal life under this similitude: 'If the whole bulk of the earth and machine of the world were gold beyond price, and if in exchange for the heavy sufferings thou art enduring all thy pain were removed, and there were given thee for a reward a treasure of glory in comparison whereof the aforesaid gold should be nothing … wouldst not thou rejoice and gladly bear what thou art bearing now?' 'I should rejoice indeed,' said the Saint; 'yea, and above measure I should rejoice.' 'Exult, then,' said the Lord to him, 'for thy sickness is the earnest of My kingdom.'"
- The Canticle in Umbrian (OW-I1) was not re-read this session; use the notes' text.
- What changed: pain becomes a pledge; the Canticle is begun.
- Not in the sources: mice and the cell are in the Mirror only (c.1318), not in Celano; Celano gives no place, season or sound; no moonlight, no tears named; the sung tune is not described.
- Sources: HC 2C 212-213; HM p.175-179 (Mirror ch. C); OW-I1.

## SC-14 — Death, evening of 3 October 1226 (Portiuncula)
- Status: full (the blessing of Assisi from the road: not found in any text I read; marked below)
- When and where: Saturday evening, the night he died [HM p.205]; the Portiuncula, to which he asked to be carried from the bishop's palace [HC 1C 108]. Celano I counts "two years" from the revelation at Foligno [HC 1C 109].
- Who was there: the brothers; his Vicar Elias at his left hand [HC 1C 108]; the warden [HC 2C 215]; a doctor [HC 2C 217]; the Lady Jacqueline of Settesoli, who arrived with her son [HM p.201-203; Mirror, c.1318].
- What happened, in order:
  1. At the bishop's palace he blesses the brothers, crossing his hands on Elias's head [HC 1C 108]. He asks to be carried to the Portiuncula, "where … he first knew the way of the truth perfectly" [HC 1C 108].
  2. At the Portiuncula he has two brothers sing praises of death; he recites Psalm 141 [HC 1C 109]. A brother says the children will be "fatherless"; Francis remits all faults and tells him to proclaim it [HC 1C 109].
  3. Jacqueline comes from Rome unsent, with ash-grey cloth, candles, almond sweets; Francis lets her in despite the rule against women [HM p.201-203].
  4. He has himself laid naked on the bare ground; he covers the side wound with his left hand: "I have done my duty, may Christ teach you yours" [HC 2C 214].
  5. The warden deprives him of the right to give away the tunic, drawers and cap, "lent" by obedience; Francis "rejoiced and exulted" [HC 2C 215].
  6. He blesses all, beginning with his Vicar, laying a hand on each head [HC 2C 216]; breaks bread and gives each a morsel, remembering the Last Supper [HC 2C 217].
  7. He has John 13 read, "Six days before the Passover …" [HC 1C 110]; larks wheel round the roof at the Saturday evening after vespers [HM p.205]. He dies [HC 1C 110].
- Details: sackcloth and ashes [HC 1C 110]; a sackcloth cap hiding the scars of his eye treatment [HC 2C 215]. Doctors amazed that "the skin alone … stuck to the bones" [HC 1C 107]. Francis said "Welcome, my sister Death" and to the doctor "boldly foretell that death is at hand" [HC 2C 217]. He asked to lie naked on the ground "as long as one might take to walk a mile gently" after death [HC 2C 217].
- Words spoken: "I have done my duty, may Christ teach you yours" (HC 2C 214). "Know that this tunic, these drawers, and this cap have been lent thee by me by order of holy obedience …" (the warden; HC 2C 215). "Welcome, my sister Death" (HC 2C 217). "See, my son, God is calling me, I remit all the offences and faults of my brethren" (HC 1C 109).
- Inner life: John 13 text above; Celano 2C 214: "he deemed it a disgrace to live for the world … he welcomed death with singing". Celano 2C 217: Death "will be the gate of life to me".
- Testimony (Celano, public-domain, 2C 214-215, part): "For, worn out as he was by that grievous sickness which was the end of all his sufferings, he bade that he should be laid naked on the bare ground, that in that last hour wherein the enemy could yet rage against him, he might wrestle naked with his naked foe. … Laid thus on the ground, his garment of sackcloth having been removed, he raised his face (as he was wont) to heaven, and, wholly intent on that [celestial] glory, he covered with his left hand the wound in his right side, that it might not be seen. And he said to the brethren, 'I have done my duty, may Christ teach you yours.'"
- What changed: he dies owning nothing, not even his habit.
- Not in the sources: the blessing of Assisi from the road (not read; not in HC 1C 105-110 or 2C 214-217, not found in the Mirror chapters I read); the hour of death (Celano gives none); no moon or weather; Celano I does not mention the larks; The Mirror says Jacqueline came "in that week" he died; Celano does not mention her. Elias's blessing differs in the two Lives (HC 2C 216 note: the later Life takes it back). Celano's 1C 108 ("two years") and 1C 109 ("eighteen years … two years") are one prophecy.
- Sources: HC 1C 105-110, 2C 212-217; HM p.201-205 (Mirror ch. CXII, CXIII, CXXIV). OW-S4 (Baring-Gould p.115-116) not read.

## SC-15 — Canonization, 16 July 1228 (Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: Assisi, "16th July, in the second year of the pontificate of Pope Gregory IX [A.D. 1228]" [HC 1C 126]. Celano says "the city of Assisi" and "the place prepared"; he does not name San Giorgio.
- Who was there: Pope Gregory IX; cardinals, bishops and abbots; a subdeacon, Ottaviano, who read the miracles; Cardinal-deacon Riniero (Capocci), who discoursed on them; royalty, counts and magnates; the brothers; a "well-nigh countless multitude" [HC 1C 124-126].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Pope Gregory leaves Rome (a quarrel with the Romans), goes via Rieti and Spoleto, and visits the "Handmaids of Christ" [HC 1C 122].
  2. At Assisi he greets Francis's sepulchre "with eager reverence": sighs, tears, bows his head. Conferences on canonization follow; miracles are heard and approved [HC 1C 123].
  3. A second meeting at Perugia: the cardinals read the miracles and say: "No need … for miracles to attest the most holy man's most holy life, which we have seen with our eyes, handled with our hands and proved under the teaching of truth" [HC 1C 123-124].
  4. On the day the Pope preaches on Ecclus. 50:6-7, "As the morning star amid vapour …"; recalling Francis's purity "he was altogether bathed in tears" [HC 1C 125].
  5. Ottaviano reads the miracles; Riniero speaks; the Pope, cardinals and people weep [HC 1C 125].
  6. The Pope decrees, hands raised: Francis "be numbered in the roll of saints, and that his festival be kept on the day of his death" [HC 1C 126].
  7. Te Deum; organs; olive-boughs green; the Pope descends and "kissed the tomb" and celebrates Mass [HC 1C 126].
- Details: the Pope's vestments, jewelled; cardinals in white [HC 1C 125]. The first healing in the miracle list is a girl with a bent neck, healed on the burial day when she put her head under the coffin; "a pit was seen" in her shoulder [HC 1C 127].
- Words spoken: decree: "To the praise and glory of Almighty God the Father, Son and Holy Ghost, and of the glorious Virgin Mary, and of the blessed Apostles Peter and Paul, and to the honour of the glorious Church of Rome, by the counsel of our brethren and the other prelates we decree, revering on earth the most blessed father Francis whom the Lord hath glorified in heaven, that he be numbered in the roll of saints, and that his festival be kept on the day of his death" (Gregory IX, Howell's English; HC 1C 126).
- Testimony (Celano, public-domain, 1C 127): "On the day that the hallowed and holy body of the most blessed father Francis was put away, like a most precious treasure … a girl was brought whose neck had for a year past been monstrously bent so that her head was joined to the shoulder, and she could only look up sideways. But after placing her head for some time under the coffin wherein the precious body of the Saint lay, forthwith she raised up her neck, through the most holy man's merits, and her head was restored to its proper position so that the girl was astounded at the sudden change in herself and began to run away, weeping excessively."
- What changed: the poor man is numbered among the saints by the Church; the Pope kisses his tomb.
- Not in the sources: Celano does not say that Gregory was Hugolino, nor that he was Francis's friend; the outline's identification is from the notes. No San Giorgio, no 16 July weather beyond "olive-boughs green" and "festive attire". Celano's sermon text is not a full sermon. CM-S5 (*Mira circa nos*) not re-read.
- Sources: HC 1C 122-127.

## FN-1 — The Franciscan family and the Poor Clares today
- Fact: Catholic Encyclopedia, "Franciscan Order": the First Order has three bodies: the Friars Minor ("parent stem", founded 1209), the Conventuals, and the Capuchins, "constituted independent orders in 1517 and 1619"; the Second Order is the "Poor ladies, now commonly called Poor Clares", cloistered nuns under the Rule of St Clare; the Third Order secular (c.1221) and regular. Dates and names are the page's. (Page read through WebFetch; summary only; numbers for today not given.)
- Source: newadvent.org/cathen/06217a.htm, 1909, public-domain. XW-X10 not read. Today's membership figures: not found.

## FN-2 — Pius XII, *Licet commissa* (1939)
- Fact: 18 June 1939, apostolic letter naming Francis and Catherine of Siena joint patrons of Italy. Published in *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* 31 (1939), pp. 256-257.
- Source: Wikipedia "Feast of Saints Francis and Catherine" (read via fetch_text; its footnote gives the AAS reference). The vatican.va Latin URL I tried returned 404. Primary text: not read. Rights: facts only.

## FN-3 — Official 1818-20 record of the remains
- Fact: the dig began with Pius VII's consent on 5 October 1818, at the request of Giuseppe De Bonis, Minister General of the Conventuals; the tomb was found on 12 December 1818 after a 52-night dig.
- Source: web search summary (keytoumbria.com/Assisi/S_Francesco_Crypt.html and others), not read page by page. *Assisiensem Basilicam* (1820) and Cooper 2005: not found, not read.
