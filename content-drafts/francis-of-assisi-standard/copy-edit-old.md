Only after his words does it say "then was the man of God seen to have a hair-shirt next his skin under his bright-coloured garments". The two sentences now agree. 2. The brother's mockery (biography). The Legend says the brother passed Saint Francis, who had risen early to pray in poor clothes, and said it "unto one of his fellow-citizens". Now: "said mockingly to a fellow citizen". The quoted words now follow the source: "Bid Francis sell thee at least one penny-worth of his sweat" (the added "now" is gone, and "at least" is back). Saint Francis's answer was in French and the source says he was filled with joy, so "Saint Francis smiled" and "Indeed" were replaced.
3. Jacques de Vitry (biography). His letter does not say "Lesser Sisters". It says "multi utriusque sexus" (many of both sexes) "qui fratres Minores vocabantur", and then "Mulieres vero" (the women) live in houses near the cities and live by the work of their hands. The text now says "people of both sexes", then "the men" and "the women" in his words. His "only" distress was too strong: the source says "valde dolent" (greatly distressed). The men "preached" became "to win souls" (his "ut aliquos lucri faciant").
4. Gospel book on La Verna. Bonaventure (Legenda maior XIII.2) says Saint Francis prayed, took the Gospel book from the altar, and "caused his companion to open it thrice in the name of the Holy Trinity". The book opened each time at the Passion. The biography said Saint Francis opened it himself. It now agrees with the miracle list, and the list gained the prayer and the Holy Trinity from the same passage. The words "as he had once done with Bernard and Peter" stay: Bonaventure says Saint Francis opened the book three times then.
5. Hail (miracles). Bonaventure XIII.7: before Saint Francis lived on La Verna, clouds rose from the mountain and a hailstorm destroyed the fruit around it. After the vision of the stigmata the hail ceased, to the amazement of the people. The entry now says this.
6. Famine (miracles). In Celano's Second Life §52 the "witnesses" are Celano and his contemporaries. The sentence now names Celano himself and others who lived in those years as those who saw the calm times and the famine.
7. Thirsty man (miracles). Celano (II §46) says Saint Francis was going up the mountain to a hermitage, was weak, and rode a borrowed donkey in summer. The man sank down before they reached the place. The entry now tells this. Bonaventure says only "a certain desert place" and a steep ascent. The line "The man found water in the rock" now says the water came by the power of Saint Francis's prayer, as Celano says.
8. Fonte Colombo (miracles). The Mirror itself gives "Fonte Palumbo (Colombo)". "Fonte Colombo" is the modern name and the one the other entries use. The last "Fonte Palumbo" was changed to "Fonte Colombo".
