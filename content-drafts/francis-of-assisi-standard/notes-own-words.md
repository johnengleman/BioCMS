## 1. Identity (own-words scope only)

- Name forms: Francesco d'Assisi; Francis of Assisi; Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone (from the task message, no source read by me for the birth name). Francis calls himself "frater Franciscus parvulus", "servus et subditus", "homo inutilis" (see quotes). [Q1 Test. p.81-82; Ep. II p.106; Ep. I p.89 text]
- Dates and canonization (1181/82, 1226, 1228): from the task message; I did not source them in this pass.
- Death: Saturday 4 October; buried in the cathedral of Assisi the next day. [S4 Gould vol.11 p.116 in print; PDF p.148]
- Tradition: Catholic only (born 1181/82, after 1054). Playbook: latin-italy.md.
- Feast: 4 October (Roman calendar, [S4]). Orthodox feast: not checked.

## 3. Timeline of the writings (own-words)

One line each. Dates for the writings come from R1 unless marked.

- late 1225: Canticle of the Sun/Creatures composed in a hut near San Damiano, where Francis lay ill. Heading in MS: "cum esset infirmus apud sanctum damianum". R1 says "appears to have been composed" then, with 2 strophes added later by tradition (Speculum Perfectionis): the one on forgiveness, in a feud between bishop and magistrates of Assisi, and the one on sister death at the approach of death. [I1; R1 p.150-151] Legendary or traditional detail: the story of the added strophes (Speculum).
- 1221 and 1223: the two Rules. Q1 has both: Regula I (1221, "Regula non bullata") and Regula II (1223). [Q1 p.37-72] Dates not re-checked in R1 in this pass (the 1223 papal confirmation date is not read).
- summer 1226: Testament written or dictated, probably at the Celle near Cortona (R1) or dictated to Angelo Tancredi in the infirmary hut by the Portiuncula (alternative). Published a few days before his death (Gregory IX bull Quo elongati, 28 Sept 1230, cites it). [R1 p.79] NEW to popular pages: the bull quotes it.
- 1224, Mount La Verna: after the stigmata, Francis writes the Praises of God and the Blessing for Leo on one parchment. Source: Leo's own marginal note on the parchment, which says "two years before his death", the Lent from the Assumption (15 Aug) to St Michael (29 Sept), "post visionem et allocutionem seraphim et impressionem stigmatum Christi in corpore suo". [Q1 p.199; R1 p.146-147] The parchment is kept at the Sacro Convento in Assisi, mentioned in the archive since 1348, and carried in procession each year at the Perdono feast. [R1 p.146]
- undated: Letter to Brother Leo (autograph at Spoleto cathedral). The autograph vanished, was found again in 1895 by a priest of Spoleto, Mgr Faloci gave it to Leo XIII, and about three years later it went back to Spoleto. Only one other autograph of Francis is known. [R1 p.130] NEW as detail.
- late in life: Letter to the whole Order, sent "in fine dierum suorum" to the general chapter and all the brothers. [Q1 p.? Epistola II heading, near line 3503 of OCR; page numbers 101-107]
- 4 Oct 1226: Francis dies. His last recorded words, according to Baring-Gould, are Psalm 141: "thou art my hope and my portion in the land of the living". Baring-Gould says "so far as any record informs us" and calls the Last Supper with Elias story "deserves no credence". [S4 p.116]
- 1912: Peace Prayer first printed anonymously in La Clochette (Paris). See section 8. [P1]

## 4. Character, as the writings show it

- Plain about his own faults: in the letter to the Order he confesses that he did not keep the Rule or say the office as the Rule requires, "sive negligentia sive infirmitatis meae occasione sive quia ignorans sum et idiota". [Q1 Ep. II p.105]
- Calls himself small and a servant in every closing: "frater Franciscus parvulus", "parvulus vester et servus". [Q1 Test. p.81-82]
- Wants to be controlled: "volo esse captus in manibus suis" about his minister. [Q1 Test. p.80]
- Works with his hands and wants all to work: "Et ego manibus meis laborabam et volo laborare". [Q1 Test. p.79]
- Hard on a few things: wants brothers who refuse the Rule seen "non teneo eos catholicos nec fratres meos; nolo etiam ipsos videre nec loqui". [Q1 Ep. II p.106]
- Tender to Leo: calls himself Leo's mother ("sicut mater"). [Q1 p.? Ep. to Leo]
- Anti-intellectual-but-not-hostile: tells brothers who do not know letters "non curent ... litteras discere" but "desiderare ... spiritum Domini". [Q1 Reg. II ch.10]
- Brief preaching: "verbum abbreviatum fecit Dominus super terram". [Q1 Reg. II ch.9]
- Humor: no jokes in the writings I read. Humor appears in the Fioretti ch.8 (the doorkeeper who beats them), a 14th-c. work. [F1]

## 5. Quotations (21 used of 25)

Format: Original (source and locator). Own English. Rights.
The Latin follows Q1 as in the edition; OCR fixes noted above.

Q-A. Canticle of the Creatures (Umbrian Italian, MS 338) [I1]
1. "Altissimu onnipotente bonsignore, tue so' le laude la gloria e l'honore et onne benedictione." (Cantico, l.1; I1, p.1 of the Wikisource edition). Own English: Most High, all-powerful, good Lord, yours are the praise, the glory, the honor and every blessing. Rights: public-domain.
2. "Laudato si' mi signore per sora nostra matre terra, la quale ne sustenta et governa, et produce diversi fructi con coloriti flori et herba." [I1] Own English: Praised be my Lord for our sister, mother earth, who sustains and governs us and brings forth many fruits with colored flowers and grass. Rights: public-domain. Note: "Laudato si'" is the title of the 2015 encyclical, which is copyrighted; the saint's words are not.
3. "Laudato si' mi signore per sora nostra morte corporale, da la quale nullu homo vivente po skappare." [I1] Own English: Praised be my Lord for our sister, bodily death, from whom no living person can escape. Rights: public-domain.
4. "Guai acquelli ke morrano ne le peccata mortali, beati quelli ke trovarà ne le tue santissime voluntati, ka la morte secunda nol farrà male." [I1] Own English: Woe to those who die in mortal sins; blessed are those whom death finds in your most holy will, for the second death will do them no harm. Rights: public-domain.
5. "Laudate et benedicete mi signore et rengratiate et serviate li cum grande humilitate." [I1, last line] Own English: Praise and bless my Lord, give him thanks, and serve him with great humility. Rights: public-domain.
Cross-check English: R1 p.152-153 ("Praise ye and bless ye my Lord, and give Him thanks, and be subject unto Him with great humility").

Q-B. Letter to Brother Leo [Q1 Epistola V; R1 p.132]
6. "F. Leo, F. Francisco tuo salutem et pacem. Ita dico tibi, fili mi, et sicut mater ..." (Ad Fratrem Leonem; Q1, OCR line 3969). Own English: Brother Leo, your brother Francis wishes you health and peace. I say it to you, my son, as a mother would. Rights: public-domain.
7. "In quocumque modo melius videtur tibi placere Domino Deo et sequi vestigia et paupertatem suam, faciatis cum benedictione Domini Dei et mea obedientia." [Q1 Ep. V] Own English: In whatever way seems best to you to please the Lord God and to follow his footsteps and his poverty, do it with the blessing of the Lord God and with my obedience. Rights: public-domain.
8. "Et, si tibi est necessarium propter animam tuam aut aliam consolationem tuam, et vis, Leo, venire ad me, veni." [Q1 Ep. V] Own English: And if you need it for your soul, or for some other comfort, and you want, Leo, to come to me, come. Rights: public-domain. Cross-check: R1 p.133 "come to me, come!".

Q-C. Praises of God and Blessing for Leo (parchment from La Verna) [Q1 p.124-125; R1 p.149-150]
9. "Tu es sanctus Dominus Deus solus, qui facis mirabilia." Own English: You alone are the holy Lord God, who work wonders. Rights: public-domain.
10. "Tu es humilitas. Tu es patientia. Tu es securitas. Tu es quietas. Tu es gaudium et laetitia." [Q1 p.124] Own English: You are humility. You are patience. You are security. You are rest. You are gladness and joy. Rights: public-domain.
11. "Tu es spes nostra. Tu es fides nostra. Tu es magna dulcedo nostra. Tu es vita aeterna nostra, magnus et admirabilis Dominus, Deus omnipotens, misericors Salvator." [Q1 p.125] Own English: You are our hope. You are our faith. You are our great sweetness. You are our eternal life, great and wonderful Lord, almighty God, merciful Savior. Rights: public-domain.
12. "Dominus benedicat, f. Leo, te." [Q1 p.125; with Num. 6:24-26] Own English: The Lord bless you, Brother Leo. Note: Francis wrote the name Leo around the Tau sign so the Tau divides the name, as in old missals (R1 p.149 note, citing Balfour). Rights: public-domain.

Q-D. Admonitions [Q1 p.5-19]
13. "Ubi caritas est et sapientia, ibi nec timor nec ignorantia. Ubi est patientia et humilitas, ibi nec ira nec perturbatio. Ubi est paupertas cum laetitia, ibi nec cupiditas nec avaritia." (Adm. XXVII; Q1 p.18) Own English: Where there is love and wisdom, there is neither fear nor ignorance. Where there is patience and humility, neither anger nor disturbance. Where there is poverty with joy, neither greed nor avarice. Rights: public-domain. Caution: Adm. XXVII is not the "Where there is hatred, let me sow love" of the Peace Prayer.
14. "quantum est homo coram Deo, tantum est et non plus." (Adm. XX; Q1 p.15) Own English: As much as a person is before God, so much he is, and no more. Rights: public-domain.
15. "Beatus homo, qui sustinet proximum suum secundum suam fragilitatem in eo, quod vellet sustineri ab ipso, si in consimili casu esset." (Adm. XVIII; Q1 p.14) Own English: Blessed is the person who bears with his neighbor in his weakness as he would want to be borne with, if he were in the same case. Rights: public-domain.
16. "Beati pacifici, quoniam filii Dei vocabuntur. Illi sunt vere pacifici, qui de omnibus, quae in hoc saeculo patiuntur, propter amorem Domini nostri Iesu Christi in animo et corpore pacem servant." (Adm. XV; Q1 p.13-14) Own English: Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God. Those are truly peacemakers who, whatever they suffer in this world, keep peace in mind and body for love of our Lord Jesus Christ. Rights: public-domain.
17. "Non potest cognosci servus Dei, quantam habeat patientiam et humilitatem in se, dum satisfactum est ei. Cum autem venerit tempus, quo illi qui deberent ei satisfacere, faciunt sibi contrarium, quantam habet ibi patientiam et humilitatem, tantam habet et non plus." (Adm. XIII; Q1 p.12-13) Own English: You cannot tell how much patience and humility a servant of God has while everything pleases him. But when the people who should satisfy him do the opposite, he has as much patience and humility as he shows then, and no more. Rights: public-domain.

Q-E. Rule of 1223 and 1221 [Q1]
18. "Fratres nihil sibi approprient nec domum nec locum nec aliquam rem ... quia Dominus pro nobis se fecit pauperem in hoc mundo." (Reg. II ch.6; Q1 p.68) Own English: The brothers shall make nothing their own, not house, nor place, nor anything. ... For the Lord made himself poor for us in this world. Rights: public-domain.
19. "quia, si mater nutrit et diligit filium suum carnalem, quanto diligentius debet quis diligere et nutrire fratrem suum spiritualem?" (Reg. II ch.6; Q1 p.69; Reg. I has the same idea at p.38) Own English: For if a mother feeds and loves her son in the flesh, how much more carefully should one love and feed his spiritual brother? Rights: public-domain.
20. "Et debent gaudere, quando conversantur inter viles et despectas personas, inter pauperes et debiles, infirmos et leprosos et iuxta viam mendicantes." (Reg. I ch.9; Q1 p.37) Own English: And they ought to rejoice when they live among people who are lowly and despised, among the poor and the weak, the sick, the lepers, and beggars by the roadside. Rights: public-domain.

Q-F. Testament [Q1 p.77-82; R1 p.79ff]
21. "Dominus ita dedit mihi fratri Francisco incipere facere poenitentiam, quia, cum essem in peccatis, nimis mihi videbatur amarum videre leprosos; et ipse Dominus conduxit me inter illos, et feci misericordiam cum illis. Et recedente me ab ipsis, id quod videbatur mihi amarum, conversum fuit mihi in dulcedinem animi et corporis." (Q1 p.77) Own English: The Lord gave me, Brother Francis, to begin to do penance in this way. When I was in sins, it seemed too bitter to me to see lepers. The Lord himself led me among them, and I showed them mercy. And when I left them, what had seemed bitter to me turned into sweetness of soul and body. Cross-check: R1 p.80 (and Robinson's note that some manuscripts differ on "feci misericordiam"). Rights: public-domain.
22. "Et ego manibus meis laborabam et volo laborare." (Q1 p.79) Own English: And I worked with my own hands, and I want to work. Rights: public-domain.
23. "Et eramus idiotae et subditi omnibus." (Q1 p.79) Own English: And we were uneducated and subject to everyone. Rights: public-domain.
24. "ipse Altissimus revelavit mihi, quod deberem vivere secundum formam sancti Evangelii. Et ego paucis verbis et simpliciter feci scribi; et dominus papa confirmavit mihi." (Q1 p.79) Own English: The Most High himself showed me that I should live according to the form of the holy Gospel. And I had it written in a few words and simply, and the lord pope confirmed it for me. Rights: public-domain.
25. "sicut dedit mihi Dominus simpliciter et pure dicere et scribere regulam et ista verba, ita simpliciter et pure intelligatis" (Q1 p.82) Own English: As the Lord gave me to say and write the Rule and these words simply and purely, so you should understand them simply and purely. Rights: public-domain.

## 6. Scenes (from the writings themselves)

1. San Damiano, late 1225: nearly blind and ill, Francis lies in a hut near the church of San Damiano and makes the Canticle in Umbrian Italian. [I1; R1 p.150] (Tradition on the added strophes: Speculum.)
2. La Verna, Lent of 1224: after the stigmata, Francis calls Leo, asks for ink and parchment, and writes the praises of God he "meditated in his heart". Leo is told to keep the page until death. A temptation leaves at once. [R1 p.146 quoting 2 Cel. 2,18, Celano about 1247]
3. The parchment today: reliquary in the sacristy of the Sacro Convento, brought out each year at the Perdono feast. [R1 p.146]
4. The autograph to Leo: lost, found, given to Pope Leo XIII, sent home. [R1 p.130]
5. The Tau cutting Leo's name: a sign of blessing drawn across the name. [R1 p.149 note; Q1 p.199]
6. Perfect Joy walk: Francis and Leo walk in winter from Perugia to Santa Maria degli Angeli. Francis lists wonders (healing the blind, raising the dead, knowing all languages) five times: "scrivi, che non è quivi perfetta letizia". Then the doorkeeper scene in the rain. Closing line: "Sopra tutte le grazie, e doni dello Spirito Santo, le quali Cristo concede agli amici suoi, si è di vincere se medesimo". Source: Fioretti ch.VIII, 14th c., not Francis's own writing. [F1 p.47-49] Echo in Admonition V: "in his nihil potes gloriari; sed in hoc possumus gloriari, in infirmitatibus nostris". [Q1 p.? Adm. V] Mark: reported speech, legend-level.
7. Last day: Francis has John 13 read ("Before the Feast of the Passover") and sings Psalm 141 with broken voice. [S4 p.115-116] Baring-Gould marks the Elias-and-bread story as invented.

## 8. Other

### Peace Prayer: NOT by Francis
- First printed in La Clochette, no.12, December 1912, p.285, Paris, anonymous, titled "Belle prière à faire pendant la messe". Opening: "Seigneur, faites de moi un instrument de votre paix." [P1]
- The Ligue de la Sainte-Messe published La Clochette; founder Fr Esther Bouquerel (1855-1923), possibly the author, but "the identity ... remains a mystery". [P1]
- 1915: sent in French to Pope Benedict XV by the Marquis Stanislas de La Rochethulon. 1916: Italian text in L'Osservatore Romano. About 1920: a French Franciscan priest printed it on an image of St Francis, titled "Prière pour la paix", without naming him as author. First attribution to Francis: 1927, by a French Protestant group, Les Chevaliers du Prince de la Paix (Étienne Bach). First English print known: Kirby Page, *Living Courageously*, 1936, which names Francis. Cardinal Spellman's books spread it during and after World War II. [P1] Rights: article is facts-only.
- Conclusion for the writer: say clearly that this prayer is not found in any manuscript or early source of Francis. Do not put it in his mouth. Wikipedia and the OFS Australia page both support this (search result seen, not read raw).

### Writings: authenticity and edition notes
- Canticle is not in Quaracchi because that series is Latin only. R1 says Boehmer and Goetz accept it as authentic. Tradition says Fra Pacifico helped with the embellishment ("had a hand"). [R1 p.150] Renan's claim that the Italian original is lost is called wrong by R1; the Assisi MS 338 and the Mazarin MS 1350 hold it. [R1 p.151]
- Letter to Leo: R1 says authenticity "cannot be challenged" since the autograph exists (Spoleto). Greeting "F. Leo F. Francisco tuo" is a grammar puzzle: dative, so the letter is TO Leo. [R1 p.130]
- Testament: Sabatier claimed several testaments; R1 rejects it. [R1 p.80]
- Rule 1221 versus 1223: Q1 prints both. The 1221 text is longer and more personal. Use it for rough speech ("inter viles et despectas personas").
- Letter to a Minister: Q1 Ep. VII (OCR line 3708-3770). The addressee is anonymous, "Fratri N. ministro".
- Letter to the whole Order: its confession is a rare self-portrait in his late voice.
- Admonition 27 ("Ubi caritas est et sapientia") is often misused with the Peace Prayer. It is real; the Peace Prayer is not.
- Original before edited text: for Canticle, I quote the MS 338 transcription (Wikisource) and not a 19th-c. edited Italian. For the Latin works, Q1 is the critical edition from manuscripts, which satisfies the rule. I did not read the manuscripts themselves.
