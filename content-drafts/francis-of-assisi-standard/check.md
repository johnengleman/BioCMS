## The copy editor's list

1. **Saint Nicholas church (n. 36).** The text is right. 3C §29: "the Blessed Francis, taking the shut Book, and bowing before the altar, did open the same." Bonaventure III (Temple Classics and Washbourne): "Francis ... thrice opened the book of the Gospels." Celano II §15 says only "aperiunt" (they opened), without a name. The La Verna scene is different and is also right: Bonaventure XIII.2 has his companion open the book (n. 78). No change.
2. **"Poor Ladies"** (ch. "Saint Francis Gives Up Leading His Order"). Glossed at first use: "the Poor Ladies, the order of sisters that had begun with Saint Clare" (Celano I §116 calls Clare "first plant of this holy order"; 3C §24). The sentence was 31 words, so it is now two sentences.
3. **Spoleto quotation (n. 11).** The words in quotation marks were an indirect question. 3C §6: "Which can do the better for thee, the lord, or the servant?" Now a direct question: "Which can do more for you, the lord or the servant?"
4. **Hen (n. 58).** Celano II §24: "gallinam parvam et nigram, columbae domesticae similem, crura tota pennata habentem cum pedibus." The hen is like a tame dove, and her legs and feet are feathered. Now "She was like a tame dove, with feathers down her legs and over her feet."
5. **"his captors" (n. 6).** 3C §4 says only "he was imprisoned along with the knights" (passive). Now "he was put with the captured knights."
6. **"at San Damiano" (n. 31).** Right. 3C §24: Francis cried in French that San Damiano "shall be the convent of Ladies." Kept.
7. **Titles.** No title added. Left plain (no source on file settles them): Bernard, Giles, Leo, Angelo, Peter Cathanii, Jacoba of Settesoli. Not saints: Elias, Pacifico, Leonard, James, John of Greccio, the popes and cardinals, Jacques de Vitry, Thomas of Spalato, Jordan of Giano. "Saint" is on every saint named (Francis, Clare, Michael, Catherine of Siena, John, Pope Saint John Paul II) and absent from every quotation.

## Fixes (biography.html unless stated)

1. **Ch. "Saint Francis Kisses a Leper", San Damiano work (n. 31).** "He rebuilt the church himself" is stronger than 3C §§21, 24, which name fellow-labourers. Now "He set to work rebuilding the church, gathering stones and carrying them on his own shoulders." In the Clare chapter (n. 45): "the church he had rebuilt with his own hands" became "the church he had worked so hard to repair" (Robinson p. 17).
2. **Scarlet cloth (n. 22).** "from his father's stock" is in no early Life. Bonaventure II.4 (Washbourne) says "he took out of his house a number of pieces of cloth." Now "from his father's house"; Bonaventure added to the note.
3. **Bishop's house (n. 25).** "He came out with the clothes in his arms" is a gesture the sources do not give (3C §20). Cut to "He came out and laid the clothes with the money before the bishop."
4. **Poor-ladies chapter, Clare (n. 45).** "Soon she went to San Damiano" skipped her stay at Sant'Angelo di Panzo (Robinson p. 17; Process W12). Now "Later".
5. **The hen (n. 58).** See copy-editor item 4.
6. **Keeping faith with Lady Poverty (n. 69).** "wore him down, and sometimes his anger broke out" added a cause and a feeling. Celano II §188 says only "in vehementia spiritus." Now "Keeping faith with that Lady cost him dearly. Once, lying ill, he raised himself on his bed with great force of spirit."
7. **The Sultan, Celano I §57 (n. 53).** The quotation "unlike almost all other men" follows Howell's "almost", which misreads *quasi virum omnibus dissimilem*. Now "unlike all other men" (our translation from the Latin; note changed).
8. **Jacques de Vitry block quote (n. 52).** Boehmer's text reads "Christum predicantem": "preached Christ to him and to his men" (was "the faith of Christ", from the Moschus print). The passage is the *Historia occidentalis* ch. 32; Boehmer prints it as "Historia orientalis II.32". Changed in the cite line, notes 51, 52, 131, and the Boehmer entry in the Sources list. Page numbers corrected (pp. 102–105; Letter 6 is pp. 101–102 and is dated March 1220).
9. **Greccio (n. 74).** "Every time he said 'Bethlehem'" is stronger than Celano I §86 "saepe." Now "Often when he said 'Bethlehem'".
10. **Leo (n. 85).** "loved Leo the way a mother loves her child. He said so" overstated the letter ("I speak to you, my son, as a mother does"). Now "cared for Leo like a son. He wrote him a short letter in his own hand. It is kept today in the cathedral of Spoleto."
11. **Hiding the wounds (n. 86).** Celano II §136: "pelle supra vulneribus posita" (a skin over the wounds, not sewn); and he held out his fingers, "nonnunquam" (sometimes) his sleeve. Now "a piece of hide laid over the wounds of his feet. When people asked to kiss his hand, he held out only his fingers, or sometimes his sleeve."
12. **Cell at San Damiano (n. 93).** Latin *storiis* = mats; Evans "wattles"; de la Warr "straw mats." Now "a little hut made of woven mats."
13. **First words of the Canticle (n. 95).** "most gracious Lord" differs from the block quote's "good Lord" (Umbrian *bon signore*). Now "Most High, almighty, good Lord."
14. **Fiddle (n. 97).** Celano II §127: "arculum filoflexum" (a little bow), not a second stick. Now "draw a little bow across it, as if he were playing a fiddle." Also "He had always been a singer" (absolute) became "He loved to sing."
15. **Jacoba (n. 110).** Mirror CXII: the letter had been written and a messenger was being sought when she knocked. "The brothers were still writing the letter" was wrong; now "had written the letter and were looking for a messenger."
16. **Last paragraph (n. 131).** "a bishop in Egypt" gave a place Jacques's text does not give (he wrote as bishop of Acre). Now "a bishop who knew the brothers."
17. **Jordan section numbers (notes 3, 48, 55, 57, 59, 60, 66, 125).** The notes used a numbering that does not match the Quaracchi edition in the Sources list (the crossing was "§8", the Pope's door "§11", the 1221 chapter "§13"). Checked against the raw text: Sultan §10, crossing and vicars §§11–12, prophetess §13, Pope's door §14, 1221 chapter §§16–17, Jordan's story §18. Notes changed.
18. **Note 47 and Note on the Sources.** The 1217 date rests on Howell ("Principal Dates", p. xxiii); Jordan's text reads 1219. Howell added to note 47; one sentence added to the Note on the Sources.
19. **Note 5, note 106.** Catholic Encyclopedia added for "1181 or 1182" (Howell says 1182); Howell's note naming Leo added to note 106 (as in note 82).
20. **Research paragraph.** "and checked against page scans" removed; this check read the Quaracchi OCR text, not the scans.
21. **summary.txt.** "paid for every feast" became "paid for his friends' feasts" (3C/Celano II §7 say the young men chose him as king because he paid; "every" was a claim stronger than the source).

## Checked and left as is
