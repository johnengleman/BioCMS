## SC-06 — Before Innocent III, c.1209–10 (Rome)
- Status: full, with one correction to the outline (see "Not in the sources").
- When and where: Rome, the papal court. HOW's date table: "1210. With eleven brethren he receives the tonsure at Rome. His Rule verbally approved by Pope Innocent III." [HOW p.xxiii]. No hour or room is given.
- Who was there: Francis and eleven brothers [3C §52]; Bishop Guido of Assisi, in Rome by chance [3C §47; 1C §32]; Cardinal John of S. Paul, bishop of Sabina, who spoke for them [3C §47-48; 1C §32]; Pope Innocent III.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Guido, fearing they would leave his diocese, was troubled until he learned why they came [1C §32; 3C §47].
  2. The cardinal asked Francis many questions and urged the life of a monk or hermit; Francis refused; the cardinal gave in and took the case to the Pope [1C §32].
  3. The cardinal told Innocent: "I have found a man of most perfect life", who would live by the Gospel [3C §48]. Next day Francis was presented to the Pope [3C §49].
  4. Innocent blessed them and told them to preach repentance; later he said the life seemed "too hard and rough" for those who would follow, and bade Francis pray to learn God's will [3C §49; 2C §16].
  5. Francis prayed and received a parable: a poor but beautiful woman in a wilderness, a king who loved her, their sons sent to his court [2C §16; 3C §50].
  6. He told it to the Pope: "I, my lord, am that poor woman" [3C §51].
  7. Innocent, amazed, told of his own vision: the Lateran falling, a small despised Religious propping it on his back [3C §51; 2C §17]. He embraced Francis and approved the Rule [3C §51].
  8. That night (or before, per 1C §32) Francis dreamed a tall tree he bent to the ground; Celano reads it as Innocent bowing to his petition [1C §32; 3C §53].
- Words spoken: Innocent, 3C §49: "Go with the Lord, brethren, and even as the Lord shall deign to inspire you, do ye preach repentance unto all." Innocent, 2C §17: "Surely ... this is he who by his acts and teaching shall prop the Church of Christ." Francis, 3C §51: "I, my lord, am that poor woman, whom God loved and of His mercy hath thus bedight."
- Testimony (Salter 3C §51, Innocent's vision; public-domain): "Upon hearing this, the lord Pope was amazed beyond measure, for that before the coming of the Blessed Francis he had seen in a vision the Church of S. John Lateran like to fall, and a certain Religious, small of stature, and lowly, holding it up by setting his own back thereunder." Parable in full: 3C §50 or 2C §16 (Howell, plainer English).
- What changed: Pope approved the Rule by word and gave leave to preach; the twelve received the tonsure [3C §52].
- Not in the sources: **The black hen is not in this scene.** Both 3C §63-64 and 2C §24-25 place the hen dream years later, before Francis went to Cardinal Ugolino and Pope Honorius, not Innocent (3C: "a few years" after the dream he came to Rome and asked for the Bishop of Ostia; 2C §25 names "Lord Pope Honorius"). Move it to ch. 8/13 or tell it as a later dream. Hen text: 2C §24 "a little black hen, like a tame dove, whose legs and feet were feathered all over ... I am this hen, small in stature and black by nature" [HOW]; 3C §63 matches. No weather, no room, no crowd, no words from Francis in the parable audience beyond 3C §51.
- Sources: 3C §47-53, §63-64 [SAL]; 1C §32-33; 2C §16-17, §23-25 [HOW]. OW-Q1 p.79 not re-read.

## SC-07 — Palm Sunday night, 18 March 1212 (Assisi to the Portiuncula)
- Status: full
- When and where: Palm Sunday and the night after; the cathedral at Assisi; then a walled-up door in her father's house; then St Mary of the Portiuncula. Robinson's note: the night after Palm Sunday 1212 was 18 March; the Legend gives no year [LEG-CL p.14 and note 101].
- Who was there: Clare (about eighteen per Bona, XW-X6 XVII.5, not re-read); "some trusty companions" [LEG-CL p.14; note 99: Pacifica "appears to have been one"]; the bishop (Guido, note 98); friars with torches; Francis.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Clare asked Francis's counsel as Palm Sunday drew near. He told her to come to the blessing of the palms dressed and adorned, and to leave "the camp" that night [LEG-CL p.13].
  2. At the church the others pressed forward for branches; Clare stayed in her place "through modesty"; the bishop came down from the steps and put a palm in her hands [LEG-CL p.14].
  3. That night she would not use the usual door and "broke open, with a strength that astonished herself", another door "walled up by a mass of beams and stones" [LEG-CL p.14].
  4. At the Portiuncula, friars keeping vigil "at the little altar with lighted torches" received her; she put off her ornaments and "her tresses" were cut by the friars [LEG-CL p.14].
  5. Francis led her to San Paolo; her kin came and tried force and promises; she grasped the altar cloths and bared her tonsured head [LEG-CL p.15-16].
- Details: Robinson's note 100 says the "death door" custom (doors walled for a year after a corpse left by them) is local tradition, from Goff 1908; the Legend does not say why the door was walled.
- Inner life: none given for Clare in this moment.
- Testimony (Legend of St Clare, Robinson tr. 1910, p.14; public-domain): "The following night Clare set about the accomplishment of the Saint's command, and, with some trusty companions, began her longed-for flight. But not wishing to leave by the usual door, she broke open, with a strength that astonished herself, another one which was walled up by a mass of beams and stones. Thus leaving behind her, home, city and kindred, Clare hastened to St. Mary of the Porziuncola, where the Friars who were keeping vigil at the little altar with lighted torches received the virgin Clare." (OCR spells "Clare" as "Cläre"; Robinson's text is the source.)
- Process witness 12, Beatrice (paraphrase from the notes; the PDF I fetched covered only part of the text and did not show this passage, so unverified this session): Francis cut her hair "before the altar" at the Portiuncula [XW-X6 XII.2-5, notes only].
- What changed: Clare left her father's house for the Order; Francis led her on to San Paolo.
- Not in the sources: the name of the walled door's location, who the bishop's words were (none given), her age in the Legend, weather, who cut her hair by name, whether Francis was present at the haircut in the Legend (it says "the Friars").
- Sources: LEG-CL p.10-17, notes 96-104. Timing conflict: the notes record a Process "about 42 years" count pointing near 1211; 18 March 1212 is Robinson's computation.

## SC-08 — The Sultan's camp, 1219 (Damietta)
- Status: full
- When and where: the Fifth Crusade siege of Damietta, Egypt, 1219. Celano I dates the visit "the thirteenth year of his conversion" [1C §57]; Howell's footnote gives AD 1219. Howell's date for the defeat is 29 August 1219 (a footnote, per XL-2C §30 notes).
- Who was there: Francis; a companion (unnamed in 2C §30 and 1C §57); Saracen guards; the Sultan of Egypt (Jacques calls him "Soldanus Egipti"; Boehmer's note names him Malek al Khamil); Christian soldiers.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Before a battle, Francis told his companion the Lord had shown him the Christians would not prosper; if he spoke he would be "deemed a fool", if silent he would not "elude my conscience" [2C §30].
  2. His companion said: "Make no account of being judged by men, for thou art not now first beginning to be deemed a fool" [2C §30].
  3. The soldiers ignored the warning, fought and fled; Celano gives "6,000 were killed and captured" and says Francis mourned [2C §30].
  4. Later Francis went toward the Sultan's camp; seized on the road by Saracens he said "ego Christianus sum, ducite me ad dominum vestrum" [BOE p.105].
  5. Celano I: he was "insulted and beaten" first, then "most honourably received" by the Sultan, who offered gifts, which Francis held "in contempt like dung" [1C §57]. Jordan: he cried "Soldan, soldan" amid blows [JOR §10].
  6. The Sultan heard him preach "per dies aliquot", then, fearing his soldiers might be converted, had him led back "cum omni reverentia et securitate" [BOE p.105].
- Words spoken: Francis (Jacques, Historia orientalis II.32): "ego Christianus sum, ducite me ad dominum vestrum" = "I am a Christian; take me to your lord." Sultan: "ora pro me, ut Deus legem illam et fidem, que magis illi placet, dignetur revelare" = "Pray for me, that God may reveal to me the law and faith that pleases him more." [BOE p.105]
- Testimony (Jacques de Vitry, eyewitness, BOE p.104-105, Latin, public-domain): "Vidimus primum huius ordinis fundatorem et magistrum ... virum simplicem et illiteratum, dilectum Deo et hominibus, fratrem Franciscum nominatum, ad tantum ebrietatis excessum et fervorem spiritus raptum fuisse, quod cum ad exercitum christianorum ante Damiatam in terra Egipti devenisset ad Soldani Egipti castra intrepidus et fidei clipeo communitus accessit." Own English: We saw the founder, a simple, unlettered man, loved by God and men, Brother Francis, so carried away by drunkenness of spirit that, coming to the Christian army before Damietta, he went fearlessly to the Sultan's camp, armed with the shield of faith. (Corrected OCR: "Testrum", "esercitu", "efficatia".)
- Also (letter 6, spring 1220, BOE p.101): he "preached the word of God for many days" and "achieved little" (modicum profecit); the Sultan asked that he pray to be inspired to follow the religion that pleased God more.
- What changed: Francis went back unharmed; Celano: God "did not fulfil S. Francis' desire" for martyrdom [1C §57].
- Not in the sources: fire ordeal, a secret conversion, the Sultan's name in Jacques, Francis's reasons for seeking him in Jacques, any text of his preaching, the companion's name, how many days, the Sultan's tent or gifts in Jacques.
- Sources: BOE p.101-105 (Jacques letter 6; Hist. orient. II.32); 1C §57; 2C §30; JOR §10 [JOR text, OCR]. Jacques's "Saracens beat the friars when they call Muhammad a liar" is at BOE p.105 if the writer needs it.

## SC-09 — The leper's dish, undated (Portiuncula)
- Status: full for the story and its date window; "earlier source" only partly met.
- When and where: Sabatier dates it between end of September 1220 and 10 March 1221, because Peter Cathanii is minister general, so after the resignation in SC-10 [SAB p.107 note 1]. Place: the church of Blessed Mary of the Portiuncula, then the brothers' table.
- Who was there: Francis; Brother James the Simple; a leper "valde ulcerato"; Brother Peter Cathanii (minister general); other brothers [SAB ch. 58].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Francis came back to the Portiuncula and found James had led the leper there from the hospital [SAB].
  2. He rebuked James: "Tu non deberes ducere christianos quia non est honestum pro te neque pro ipsis" [SAB].
  3. Then he blamed himself, thinking he had shamed the leper, and confessed the fault to Peter [SAB].
  4. He set his own penance: "Hec sit penitentia mea videlicet ut comedam simul in una scutella cum fratre christiano" [SAB]. Peter: "Frater, fac quid tibi placet" [SAB].
  5. At table, one dish stood between Francis and the leper, whose fingers were "contractos et sanguinolentos"; blood and matter flowed into the dish when he took his morsels [SAB].
  6. Peter and the brothers were "contristati" but said nothing, out of fear and reverence [SAB].
- Details: Francis called lepers "fratres christianos". James was so simple that he walked lepers from the hospital to the church as if they were friars [SAB].
- Testimony (Latin, SAB ch. 58 end): "Qui vidit hoc scripsit et testimonium perhibet de hiis." Own English: He who saw this wrote it and bears witness to these things. Rights: public-domain.
- What changed: Francis repaired his own rebuke by sharing the leper's dish.
- Not in the sources: James's reply, the leper's name or words, Francis's own prayer, any meal text beyond the dish.
- Earlier-source status: Sabatier lists manuscripts "Mz. 1743 21b-22a; Mz. 989 128a; Vat. 4354 98b" for this text and says "On retrouve litteralement toute cette page dans les Conform." I did not identify those manuscripts further and did not find Delorme 1922 online (one find_sources search returned nothing). So the text is still the Mirror tradition. Keep "his companions remembered".
- Sources: SAB ch. 58 (Latin), plus the notes' Evans English [XL-Mir. LVIII]; OW-Q1 p.37 (Rule I on lepers) from the notes only, not re-read.

## SC-10 — Resignation, Michaelmas 1220 (Portiuncula)
- Status: full for the resignation words; thin for Jordan's paragraph numbers.
- When and where: Michaelmas (29 Sept) 1220, a chapter of the brothers [HOW footnote to 2C §143]. Howell's table: "Michaelmas. S. Francis resigns his office of Minister-General" [HOW p.xxiii].
- Who was there: Francis; Peter Cathanii; the brothers in chapter; later Elias, Caesarius of Speyer, Pope Honorius, Cardinal Hugolino [JOR].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Jordan: while Francis was across the sea, his vicars (Matthew of Narni and Gregory of Naples, per the notes [XL-Jord. §8]) added new fasting rules; a brother brought the news; Brother Philip and John of Capella caused further trouble [JOR].
  2. A prophetess said: "Redite, redite, quia per absentiam fratris Francisci Ordo turbatur et scinditur et dissipatur" (Jordan: "Et hoc verum fuit") [JOR].
  3. Francis came back with Elias, Peter Cathanii and Caesarius, and went not to the troublemakers but to Pope Honorius [JOR].
  4. He lay at the Pope's door and "did not dare" to knock; he asked for one pope who would hear his cause: "Dominum Ostiensem" [JOR].
  5. In chapter he resigned: "Henceforth I am dead to you; but here is brother Peter Cathanii, and let you and me all obey him"; he bowed and promised obedience. The brothers wept [2C §143].
  6. He raised his clasped hands: "O Lord, I commend to Thee the family that Thou hast hitherto committed to me" [2C §143].
  7. Later, ill, he asked: "Who are they that have snatched the Religion of myself and the brethren out of my hands?" [2C §188].
  8. Distressed by evil examples, he got God's answer: "even though the Religion were reduced to the number of three, it shall ever, through My gift, remain unshaken" [2C §158].
- Words spoken: see steps 2, 4-8. Also 2C §144: "I have seen before now a blind man with a little dog only to show him the way." 2C §143 says the resignation was "a few years after his conversion" and was "to observe the virtue of holy humility".
- Testimony (Jordan, Latin; my cleaned reading of noisy OCR, check against a scan; public-domain): "Ad fores ergo domini Papae Pater humilis iacens, cubiculum tanti principis perstrependo pulsare non audebat ... Quo egresso, beatus Franciscus, facta ei reverentia, dixit: 'Pater Papa, Deus det tibi pacem.' At ille: 'Benedicat te Deus, fili.' Et beatus Franciscus: 'Domine, cum sis magnus et magnis saepe praeoccupatus negotiis, pauperes ad te accessum habere saepe non possunt nec tibi loqui, quoties necesse habent. Multos mihi papas dedisti, da unum, cui, cum necesse habeo, loqui possim, qui vice tua causas meas et Ordinis mei audiat et discutiat.' Ad quem Papa: 'Quem vis, ut dem tibi, fili?' Et ille: 'Dominum Ostiensem.' Et concessit." (One word after "exspectabat" is unreadable in the OCR; my own English: The humble father lay at the Lord Pope's door and dared not knock at so great a prince's chamber; when the Pope came out, Francis said "Father Pope, may God give you peace"; "May God bless you, son" ... "You have given me many popes; give me one to whom I can speak when I must, who will hear my cause and my Order's in your place"; "Whom do you want?" "The Lord of Ostia." And he granted it.)
- What changed: Francis gave up the Order's government to Peter Cathanii and obtained Hugolino as protector; he lived "in subjection until death" [2C §143; JOR].
- Not in the sources: how many were in the chapter, the room, what Peter said back, tears of Francis himself, the date of the Pope's door scene.
- Cautions: JOR's printed numbers differ from the notes (the Soldan story is Jordan's chapter 10, which Boehmer also cites; the pythonissa and the table scene sit after it). Cite by content. 2C §143's "Michaelmas 1220" is Howell's footnote, not Celano's text.
- Sources: 2C §143-144, §158, §188 [HOW]; JOR (Analecta Franciscana 1, OCR); notes XL-Jord. §8-11 (not fully re-verified).
