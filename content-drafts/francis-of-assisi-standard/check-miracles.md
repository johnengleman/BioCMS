# Check: miracles.html (Francis of Assisi, standard)

Checker: not the reviser. Copy of the reviser's file: `miracles.html.before-check` (105 accounts). Fixed file: `miracles.html` (92 accounts: 13 removed, none added).

Method: every account read against Howell 1908 (Celano First and Second Life, archive `livesofsfranciso00thom`, raw OCR text), the Legend of the Three Companions (Salter), the Mirror of Perfection (Evans 1899 and the 1902 translation), Bonaventure (Washbourne 1868 and the Everyman volume), the Little Flowers (Arnold), and Wadding vol. 2 (raw OCR). Also the notes files and the scene cards. Lint: no MUST FIX. One soft CHECK remains (average sentence 12.1 words; this is a list of short accounts).

## The reviser's five readings

1. **Villa is a boy's name.** Confirmed. Howell §149: "Another boy named Villa could neither speak nor walk." Kept; source line fixed to §149–150 (it said §147–150).
2. **The waxen image is not tied to Walter of Arezzo.** Confirmed. Howell §140 puts it on a fourth, unnamed person ("Another, nigh to death ... by making a waxen image"). The account now says so, and the title now says "Four healings" (it said three).
3. **Innocent's vision "before the coming of Francis".** Confirmed for the Legend of the Three Companions §51 only. Celano Second Life §17 says "a few days before"; Bonaventure says "about this time". The account said "reported only in the Legend of the Three Companions". That was wrong. Status now names all three and gives each wording. Source lines for Celano §17 and Bonaventure ch. 3 added.
4. **Bishop at Benevento / Monte Gargano.** Confirmed. Celano Second Life §220: lodging at Benevento on the way back from the church of S. Michael. Bonaventure ch. 14: on pilgrimage to S. Michael on Mount Gargano. Both are now given as two readings. Also fixed: the vision came "on the night of his death" (the account said "at the hour").
5. **Damietta date and 6,000.** Half right. Only the date (29 Aug 1219) is Howell's footnote. The 6,000 is in Celano's own text ("6,000 were killed and captured"). Text fixed. The title now says "1219" only.

## Restored private accounts

Rule change by the owner: private visions, voices, temptations, and inner experiences that the saint or a companion reported belong in the list. The 12 accounts removed above are back, with one new account (the snow balls at Sartiano). Copy of the file before this step: `miracles.html.before-restore`. They stand in a new group, "Private Visions, Voices, and Struggles" (13 accounts, between the Second Life and Mirror group and the group "After Francis's death"). Order is the order of the sources: Second Life §32 to §219, then the Mirror. No other account was changed. Lint: no MUST FIX; one soft CHECK (average sentence 12.7 words).

Method: each account was rewritten from the raw OCR text of Howell's Celano (archive `livesofsfranciso00thom`) and Evans's Mirror of Perfection (archive `mirrorofperfecti00leob`). Each is attributed to its reporter and carries a Status line and its own Source line. Where the earlier text went beyond the source, it was cut or fixed:

- Siena, returning brother (2C §32–33): Celano gives the cause of the flight in Francis's words to the brothers who asked. The brother knelt and owned his fault (added). The old text lacked the brother's wandering and temptations.
- Devil on a brother's back (2C §34): the brother obeyed after a messenger warned him; Francis told the messenger, his companion. Francis prayed and the devil left.
- Long temptation (2C §115; Mirror XCIX): Celano says "more than two years", not "two years". The title says "long". Celano does not say who told it, and the Status says so.
- Snow balls (2C §116–117; new): placed at the hermitage of Sartiano. Celano gives it as Francis's answer to a temptation of the flesh. He scourged himself with his cord first. Celano says "seven lumps like balls". A brother who was praying saw it by moonlight, and Francis told him to tell no one as long as Francis lived. No mention of loneliness. The title says "snow balls" and not "figures" for that reason.
- Cardinal's tower (2C §119–120; Mirror LXVII): Celano says Francis chose the tower (the Mirror has Brother Angelo suggest it). The old text said Francis "thanked the cardinal"; Celano says they told him everything and said farewell. The Mirror's hermitage is Fonte Palumbo near Rieti (Evans); the old text said "Fonte Colombo". Fixed. Added Francis's own reason (brothers in poor places might suspect luxury).
- Throne vision (2C §122–123; Mirror LIX–LX): the seat belonged to "one of the fallen" in Celano, not to "the fallen angel". The Mirror says "Lucifer". Celano does not name the companion; the Mirror names Pacifico and the church at Bovara near Trevi. Francis's answer was to the companion's question, as Celano tells it.
- Order will last (2C §158; Mirror LXXXI): Celano does not say that Francis told it himself. The old line "Celano says Francis told this himself" was cut. The quotation now keeps ellipses where words are left out. The Mirror's prayer ("resign the family") is given in modern words.
- Siena, when he serves God (2C §159): "Francis told his companions" is in Celano (he woke them). The old Status said they were "witnesses". Now it says he told them.
- Forbids preaching (2C §195): the vision is one companion's, whom Celano does not name. The sweetness lasted "several days".
- Crumbs (2C §209): "1223" is not in Celano's passage. Cut from the title and text; the Status says Celano gives no year. Id changed to `francis-sees-crumbs-of-bread-that-stand-for-the-rule`. Francis told his companions in the morning, and the voice explained the vision later, while he prayed.
- Kingdom promised (2C §213; Mirror C): Celano says "the Lord". The Mirror says "it was said to him in spirit". Celano says Francis told his companions a little of it. The Mirror's San Damiano, sixty days, and mice details are kept as the Mirror's.
- Purple robe (2C §219): the brother is unnamed and was praying. It was the night of Francis's death, "at that hour". Added that it seemed to the brother and the crowd that Christ and Francis were one. The palace and feast later in the vision are not told.
- Four promises (Mirror LXXVIII–LXXIX): the Mirror says Francis said he had obtained them from the Lord and that an angel announced them.

Titles: all 8 words or fewer. Ids of the 11 restored accounts are as before, except the crumbs account (above). The new snow account is `francis-meets-a-temptation-with-seven-snow-balls`.

Not checked: the Mirror (Evans 1899) is cited as the owner's notes cite it. Its date, "about 1318", comes from the file's own intro and was not rechecked.
