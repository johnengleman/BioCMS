Source keys follow outline.md. New in this file: 3C = Legend of the Three Companions, Salter 1902 (WT-S2), archive:legendofsaintfra00unse, read in full for §4-§32. Howell = Celano, Lives, 1908 (WT-S1), archive:livesofsfranciso00thom, read for I.3, I.7-17, I.22, II.4, II.10, II.12. All public-domain. OCR spelling kept in quotes where the page is clear.

## SC-01 — Prison, 1202-03 (Perugia)
- Status: full
- When and where: war between Perugia and Assisi; Francis is taken with many fellow-citizens and held at Perugia for a year, until peace between the cities (3C §4). Howell's date table gives 1202-1203 (WT-S1 p.xxiii "Principal Dates").
- Who was there: fellow-prisoners (noble knights, since he was "imprisoned along with the knights"); one companion who rebukes him; one hated knight.
- What happened:
  1. He is held with the knights because he was "a noble in his manners" (3C §4).
  2. The others are sad. He is joyous and seems to jest. A companion rebukes him as a madman (3C §4; Celano II.4: they "deemed him mad and demented", he "laughed at his chains").
  3. He answers loudly that he will be worshipped by the whole world (3C §4; Celano II.4).
  4. One knight has injured a fellow-prisoner. All the rest shun him. Francis alone will not, and urges the rest to do the same (3C §4). Celano II.4 calls the knight "very proud and unbearable" and says Francis "induced the others to make it up with him".
  5. At the year's end, peace; they go home to Assisi (3C §4).
- Words spoken, exact:
  - 3C §4 (Salter): "What think ye of me? I shall be worshipped by the whole world."
  - Celano II.4 (Howell): "Wherein think you that I exult? Another thought is with me: I shall yet be worshipped as a Saint all the world over." Two versions of one answer; Celano's is later (1244-47) and adds "Saint". The notes' stated Latin original was not read.
- Testimony (3C §4, Salter 1902, public-domain): "Now on a day his fellow-prisoners were sad, but he, being by nature joyous and merry, appeared not to be sad, but even in some sort to jest. Wherefore one of his companions rebuked him, saying it was like a madman to rejoice seeing that he were set in prison. Unto whom Francis made answer with a loud voice: "What think ye of me? I shall be worshipped by the whole world." And when one of the knights with whom he was conjoined had done an injury unto one of his fellow-prisoners, and on this account all the rest wished to hold aloof from him, Francis alone did not refuse to company with him, and furthermore exhorted the rest to do the same."
- What changed: nothing yet; he returns home, and the 3C text goes straight to the Apulia plan (§5). Celano I.3 has him freed and then ill, with fields and vineyards no longer pleasing him (end of chapter, outline).
- Not in the sources: what the knight did; his name; the cell, chains' weight, season, food. 3C does not mention chains (Celano II.4 does). Do not say how he knew he would be famous. Do not say the knight thanked him.
- Sources: 3C §4; Celano II.4; Howell p.xxiii.

## SC-02 — The leper, c.1205 (below Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: undated; riding "nigh unto Assisi" (3C §11). Howell dates his "conversion from the world" 1204-1206.
- Who was there: Francis and one leper. Later a leper house ("spital") with its residents.
- What happened:
  1. He is praying and an answer comes: what he loved after the flesh he must despise; the things he loathed will become great sweetness (3C §11). Celano II (Howell, the leper chapter) gives the same command.
  2. Near Assisi he meets a leper. He had greatly loathed lepers and "did violence unto himself" (3C §11).
  3. He dismounts, gives money, kisses his hand, receives the kiss of peace, remounts, rides on (3C §11).
  4. Celano's second version adds: the leper held out his hand for alms; Francis looked back across the open country and could not see him anywhere, "filled with wonder and joy" (Celano II, leper chapter, Howell p.153). 3C does not have this.
  5. A few days later he takes much money to the lepers' hospital, gives each an alms, kisses each hand. 3C: what had been bitter "was now changed into sweetness" (3C §11). Celano II adds that he kissed "on the hand and mouth".
- Details: before, he turned away his face and stopped his nostrils; Celano I.17: he did so at two miles' distance when he looked at their houses.
- Words spoken: the voice in prayer (paraphrase above; 3C §11 gives it in full in Salter's English: "Francis, all those things that thou hast loved after the flesh ... thou must needs despise and hate").
- Inner life / Testimony (Testament, Latin, Quaracchi 1904, Q1 p.77, public-domain; OCR read this session): "Dominus ita dedit mihi fratri Francisco incipere facere poenitentiam, quia, cum essem in peccatis, nimis mihi videbatur amarum videre leprosos; et ipse Dominus conduxit me inter illos, et feci misericordiam cum illis. Et recedente me ab ipsis, id quod videbatur mihi amarum, conversum fuit mihi in dulcedinem animi et corporis. Et postea parum steti et exivi de saeculo." Our English: The Lord gave me, Brother Francis, to begin to do penance in this way. When I was in sins, it seemed too bitter to me to see lepers. The Lord himself led me among them, and I showed them mercy. And when I left them, what had seemed bitter to me turned into sweetness of soul and body. And afterward I stayed a little while and left the world.
- What changed: the bitter turns sweet; soon after, he leaves the world.
- Not in the sources: the leper's face, name, age, disease stage, what he said; time of day; weather; the horse's colour; the amount of money. The Testament gives no horse, kiss or road; those come from Celano and 3C.
- Sources: 3C §11; Celano I.17, II (leper chapter); Q1 p.77 (Testament).

## SC-03 — The crucifix, c.1205-06 (San Damiano)
- Status: full
- When and where: undated; the almost ruined church of San Damiano near Assisi (Celano II.10). 
- Who was there: Francis alone before the image. Afterward a poor priest of the church; an "intimate friend" (Celano II.10).
- What happened:
  1. Walking by, "led by the Spirit" he goes in to pray (Celano II.10). 3C §13: "told him in the spirit that he should enter to pray".
  2. 3C §13: before "a certain Image of the Crucified" he prays; it speaks "in holy and gracious wise". Celano II.10: the painted image "moved its lips", calling him by name.
  3. He answers, trembling, "Gladly will I do it, O Lord" (3C §13). He understood the old church to be meant.
  4. He gives the priest money for oil for a lamp before the Crucifix (3C §13; Celano II.10).
  5. After that he cannot stop weeping; he tells one friend, who weeps too (Celano II.10).
  6. Later, he mounts, takes scarlet cloths, sells them and the horse at Foligno, brings the money to the poor priest of San Damiano, kisses his hands (Celano I.8-9; 3C §16). The priest thinks it a hoax and will not keep it for fear of Francis's parents. Francis throws it on a window-sill "valuing it no more than dust" (3C §16; Celano I.9 "heeding it as little as dust").
- Words spoken: 3C §13 (Salter): "Francis, seest thou not that My House is being destroyed? Go therefore, and repair Me it." Celano II.10 (Howell): "Francis," it said, "go repair My house, which as thou seest is wholly falling into ruin." Celano I.9 has no voice: he finds "a poor priest" (WT-S1 I.9).
- Testimony (3C §13, Salter, public-domain): "he began to pray right instantly before a certain Image of the Crucified, the which spake unto him in holy and gracious wise, saying: "Francis, seest thou not that My House is being destroyed? Go therefore, and repair Me it." And trembling and astonied he saith: "Gladly will I do it, O Lord." For he understood the saying to be of that Church, which through its exceeding great age seemed like soon to fall."
- What changed: first word from God, read as stones; he sells his goods and stays with the priest. Next: a month in a cave, then the father's anger (3C §16-18).
- Not in the sources: the image's appearance or which crucifix; how long he prayed; the hour. 3C and Celano do not say the voice was audible to others. Celano I.8 has no voice at all; say "the early Lives tell".
- Sources: 3C §13, §16; Celano I.8-9, II.10.

## SC-04 — Before Bishop Guido, c.1206 (Assisi)
- Status: full (Pietro's words: not found; see below)
- When and where: undated; Assisi, the bishop's chamber, with bystanders (3C §20). The bishop is called Guido in the outline; 3C and Celano here say only "the Bishop".
- Who was there: Francis; his father Pietro di Bernardone; the bishop; many bystanders ("many who had come together", Celano II.12).
- What happened:
  1. The father chains him in a dark place for days; the mother, alone at home, frees him while the father is away (3C §18; Celano I.12-13).
  2. The father complains to the consuls. They say: "From the time he entered the service of God, he hath gone out of our jurisdiction." Then to the bishop (3C §19).
  3. Francis: "Unto the lord Bishop will I come, for he is the father and lord of souls" (3C §19). The bishop receives him with joy and tells him to give back the money and trust in the Lord (3C §19).
  4. 3C §20: he goes into the bishop's chamber, takes off all his clothes, lays them with the money before the bishop and bystanders and comes out naked. Celano I.15: he strips "stark naked", even to his drawers, without waiting to be spoken to.
  5. Hair-shirt seen under his bright garments (3C §20); Celano II.12 says sackcloth.
  6. The father takes money and clothes home. Bystanders are indignant at him, are moved, weep (3C §20).
  7. The bishop puts his arms round him and covers him with his pall (3C §20); Celano I.15: his mantle.
- Words spoken: 3C §20 (Salter): "Hear all ye, and understand: until now have I called Peter Bernardone my father, but, for that I purpose to serve the Lord, I give back unto him the money, over which he was vexed, and all the clothes that I have had of him, desiring to say only, "Our Father, Which art in Heaven," not "my father, Peter Bernardone."" Celano II.12 (Howell): "Henceforth ... I may freely say 'our Father who art in heaven,' not any more 'father Pietro di Bernardone,' to whom lo! I not only restore the money but give up all my clothes. Naked therefore will I go to the Lord." Note: only Celano II has "Naked ... will I go". 3C's "my lord, I will give back ... not only the money ... but my clothes also" is his earlier line to the bishop.
- What changed: father, name and goods given up; the bishop becomes his helper (3C §20).
- Not in the sources: Pietro's own words before the bishop. 3C gives none; Celano I.14-15 gives none (only the father's wrath, 3C §20 "hot with grief and wrath"). Mother's name, the weather, the room, the clothes' colours beyond "bright-coloured", the pall's colour. Date. Do not say the bishop was named Guido without another source.
- Sources: 3C §18-20; Celano I.12-15, II.12.

## SC-05 — The Gospel book, c.1208-09 (San Nicolò, Assisi)
- Status: full
- When and where: Jordan places the Gospel call in 1209, "anno conversionis suae tertio" (XL-Jord. §1-2, from notes; Latin not re-read by me). Church of S. Nicholas "hard by the market-place of Assisi" (3C §28). Date uncertain.
- Who was there: Francis, Bernard (Messer Bernard of Assisi, rich), Peter (a would-be brother).
- What happened:
  1. After the San Damiano work, at Mass, Francis hears the Gospel of the Twelve sent out; he asks the priest to explain it. "This is what I wish, this is what I am seeking" (Celano I.22; 3C §25). He drops shoes, staff, belt for a cord and makes a rough tunic.
  2. Bernard spends the night at his house in the evening appointed. He asks what a man should do with what his lord gave him; Francis: return it. Bernard: he will give all away as Francis thinks best (3C §28).
  3. At dawn Francis, Bernard and Peter go to S. Nicholas, pray, for they were "unlettered" and did not know where to find the verse. Francis opens the shut book three times (3C §28-29).
  4. First opening: "If thou wouldst be perfect, go, and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven." Second: "Take nothing with you on your journey." Third: "He that will come after Me let him deny himself." (3C §29). Three times because he honoured the Trinity.
  5. He says to Bernard and Peter: "My brothers, this is our life and Rule, and that of all who shall wish to join our company. Go therefore, and fulfil that ye have heard." (3C §29)
  6. Bernard sells all and gives the money to the city's poor; Peter does as he can; both put on the habit (3C §29). Then Silvester asks more for stones (§30).
- Details: verses exactly as 3C prints them above (Salter's translation; 3C gives only opening lines for the 2nd and 3rd). Celano I.22 has no book scene.
- Testimony (3C §29, Salter, public-domain): "When their prayers were at an end, the Blessed Francis, taking the shut Book, and bowing before the altar, did open the same, and at the first opening thereof was discovered that counsel of the Lord: "If thou wouldst be perfect, go, and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven." At this opening, the Blessed Francis rejoiced greatly, and gave God thanks."
- What changed: the Gospel becomes the Rule and he has companions. 3C §29 quotes his Testament: the Lord "revealed unto me that I ought to live after the manner of the Holy Gospel".
- Not in the sources: Peter's surname; Bernard's age; the book's size or kind; what priest was present; the exact chapter references; weather; how much money Bernard gave. Celano I does not tell the three openings, so call it "the Three Companions tell". Verse references are not given in 3C.
- Sources: 3C §25, §28-30; Celano I.22; Jord. §1-2 (notes).
