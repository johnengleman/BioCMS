# Check: Francis of Assisi (standard, tier A)

## Fixes (biography.html unless noted)

1. **Opening paragraph of "What he wanted" (ch. 0):** "live the Gospel 'simply and purely,' in his own words" was a misattributed quotation. In the Testament (Quaracchi p.79) "simply and purely" is said of how the brothers must understand the Rule and the Testament (no glosses), not of living the Gospel. Now: "live by the Gospel and to own nothing, because, as he wrote in his Rule, 'the Lord made himself poor for us in this world.'" This is the only paraphrase found inside quotation marks that changed meaning (see "Paraphrase check" below).
2. **Ch. 3 (robbers):** "the first title anyone gave the new Francis" was an unsupported "first". Replaced with what Celano I.16 says: the robbers meant it as an insult; he shook off the snow, climbed out and went on praising the Creator.
3. **Ch. 7, Jacques de Vitry block quote:** the Latin (Boehmer p.104-105) has "cui tanquam summo priori suo omnes alii obediunt" between "master" and "a simple and unlettered man". It was dropped without a mark. Added an ellipsis.
4. **Ch. 7 (last paragraph on the Sultan):** "with no converts to report" is not in the sources (an absence claim). Cut. Kept "a Sultan's request for his prayers" (Jacques).
5. **Ch. 8 (Leonard):** Celano II.31 has Leonard "walking behind" driving the ass; the text said "beside". Fixed.
6. **Ch. 10 (Pacifico):** "The brothers called him that" (dearest mother) is not in Celano II.137. The source says "Francis made no answer". Replaced.
7. **Ch. 10 (Celano at prayer):** "Celano, who watched him at prayer" claims eyewitness where II.94 gives none (Celano claims "as we have seen" only for the stick-viol, II.127, which stays). Now "Celano says that when prayer came over him in public...".
8. **Ch. 9 (Greccio):** "That night, Celano says, a certain man saw a vision" -> "There". Celano I.86 gives no hour.
9. **Ch. 9 (Morico):** Celano II.199 is undated and not tied to Greccio 1223; scene card says "a separate occasion". Now "One year Christmas fell on a Friday... when the talk turned to fasting, Francis rebuked Morico". Also restored "I would," he said, "that..." (the quotation had silently dropped "said he").
10. **Ch. 11 (Elias):** Mirror ch. CXXI: Elias feared what the townspeople would say about singing so near death, not that he found it "unseemly". Fixed.
11. **Ch. 11 (Siena to Assisi):** the knights of Assisi who fetch him "for fear another city would keep his body" belong to the Nocera/Satriano journey (Celano II.77; Mirror ch. XXII), not to the Cortona-to-Assisi trip (Celano I.105: Elias brought him). Now two sentences.
12. **Ch. 13 (translation, 1230):** "the city's rulers seized the body" -> "the citizens" (Wadding's text of *Speravimus* blames the citizens).
13. **Ch. 13 (grave hidden):** "no one afterward knew exactly where it lay" -> "in time its exact place was forgotten". Key to Umbria: the exact place was lost by the end of the 14th century, not at once.
14. **Ch. 14 (Canticle verse in Laudato si'):** "the verse that Francis wrote to reconcile a bishop and a magistrate" stated a Mirror tradition (c.1318) as fact. Now "that, his companions said, Francis added...". (Laudato si' para. 91 does quote the verse; checked in vatican.va text.)
15. **Ch. 14 (2026 anniversaries):** the decree does not say the Church "was keeping" the Greccio, stigmata and Canticle anniversaries in 2026. It says they were "important Jubilees in recent years", with 2026 their culmination. Reworded and split into two sentences (lint long sentence).
16. **Research paragraph:** "more than thirty sources in seven languages" was inflated. Count: the Sources list has 30 entries; the notes tables hold about 33 distinct works (some facts-only pages, two listed "not read"). The "seven languages" came from counting Umbrian and Italian, and French and Old French, as separate. Now "about thirty sources in five languages: Latin, Italian (including the Umbrian of the Canticle), French (including one Old French chronicle), Spanish and English." Languages are shown by the notes: Latin (Quaracchi, Boehmer, Jordan, Wadding), Umbrian/Italian (Wikisource Canticle; Fioretti, Luzzi), Old French (Ernoul), French (Renoux 1912 text), Spanish (Process of Clare; Inter Sanctos), English.
17. **A Note on the Sources:** one-sided dating of the Mirror. Its own closing note (Evans, p.224) says 1228; Boehmer (completed 1318) and Howell ("possibly as late as 1318") say later. Added in a few words.
18. **summary.txt:** "end blood feuds that had divided the city for generations" -> "bring noble clans who had feuded for generations to make peace" (Thomas of Spalato: "multe tribus nobilium ... ad pacis consilium reducerentur").

## The blessing of Assisi (Mirror ch. CXXIV): confirmed in the source, not added

Evans's Mirror (1899), pp. 221-223, ch. CXXIV "How he gave his blessing to the city of Assisi when he was carried unto S. Mary's that he might die there": carried in his bed toward the Portiuncula, at the hospital halfway he had the bearers set the bed down and turn it so his face looked toward Assisi (he could hardly see), and, lifted a little, blessed the city: "Lord, whereas this city of old was, as I believe, the place and habitation of wicked men, so now do I see that by reason of Thine abundant mercy in Thine own good time Thou hast shown forth the multitude of Thy mercies therein above all other cities..." It is a Mirror (c.1318) scene, so it would need "his companions remembered". Not in the biography, as instructed.

### Fixes

1. **Ch. 7, Jacques de Vitry block (words added to a quotation).** The reviser had turned three Latin pronouns into names without a mark: "before him" (ante ipsum) became "before the Sultan", "as ... preached" (ipsum ... praedicantem) became "as Francis preached", and "his words" (verborum eius) became "Francis's words". Now "[the Sultan]", "[Francis]" and "[Francis's]", in square brackets. Reason: an addition inside a quotation must be marked.
2. **Ch. 7, Jacques block, the three new sentences.** Compared word by word with the Latin ("Tandem vero metuens ... cum omni reverentia et securitate ... reduci praecepit, dicens ei in fine, Ora pro me ..."). They match: nothing added, nothing dropped. Left as is. The earlier ellipsis for "cui tanquam summo priori suo omnes alii obediunt" (fix 3 above) is still there.
3. **Ch. 8, Jordan block (Pope's door).** The card's Latin is a cleaned reading of OCR, so I read both raw scans. Both give the same text, so the passage is confirmed from the source, and the block quote stays. Two corrections came from the scans. (a) The card read "praeoccupatus"; the scans read "praepeditus" ("hindered, tied down"). The block said "often busy with great affairs". Now "are often tied down by great affairs". (b) The OCR gap after "exspectabat" is readable in the second scan: "eius spontaneum egressum longanimiter exspectabat". The block had an ellipsis there. Now it says "but waited patiently for him to come out of his own accord", and the ellipsis is gone. Also "Poor men" became "Poor people" (pauperes), and the pronouns the Latin leaves out got brackets: "[The Pope] answered" (At ille) and "And [the Pope] granted it" (Et concessit). "Quo egresso" is now "When he came out" (no name). One connective, "ergo" ("so"), is not carried over at the start of the block; this is a natural English drop.
4. **Ch. 3, "peasant herald of God"** (flagged). Celano I.16 Latin: "Iace, rustice praeco Dei!" Rusticus means a countryman, a peasant. Confirmed; no change.
5. **Ch. 10, Bonaventure "a heart set on fire"** (flagged). Legenda maior XIII.3 Latin: "non per martyrium carnis, sed per incendium mentis". The word is *mentis* (mind, spirit, soul), not *cordis*. The old English "enkindling of heart" and the reviser's "heart" both drift from it. Now "not by martyrdom of the body, but by a fire in the soul." (Mens is the soul's highest part; "soul" is the usual plain English.)
6. **Ch. 11, "different fruits"** (flagged). Umbrian: "produce diversi fructi". Different is correct but reads oddly ("different from what?"). Now "various fruits", which is what *diversi* means here. Rest of the verse confirmed word for word on it.wikisource ("la quale ne sustenta et governa, et produce diversi fructi con coloriti flori et herba").
7. **Ch. 11, "the podestà" in the quotation** (flagged). Mirror ch. 101 Latin: "Magna verecundia est nobis servis Dei quod episcopus et potestas ita se invicem odiunt". Potestas is the podestà, and "High Bailiff" was Evans's word for it. Confirmed; no change. The sentence before the quote explains the word.
8. **Ch. 11, the Celano block on the mice (2C 213).** The Latin ends "nonne gauderes, libenter sustinens quae sustines ad momentum?" (gladly bearing for a moment what you bear). Howell and the revision both left out "ad momentum". Now "and gladly bear for a moment what you are bearing now?" The rest of the block matches the Latin (thesaurus gloriae tantae; arrha = pledge; machina mundi = frame of the world; the ellipsis stands for "vel etiam nominari non digna").
9. **Ch. 12, the Celano block on the bare ground (2C 214).** The reviser wrote "he asked to be laid naked". The Latin is "super nudam humum se [nudum] fecit deponi" (he had himself laid). "Asked" weakens the act. Now "he had himself laid naked on the bare ground. He did this so that in that last hour, while the enemy could still rage against him, he might wrestle naked with his naked enemy." (The purpose clause "ut ... luctaretur" is kept; split in two to stay under 30 words.) The closing words, "Ego quod meum est feci; quod vestrum est Christus edoceat", match.
10. **Ch. 12, the larks (attribution lost).** The earlier text said "his companions remembered" for the whole sentence. The revision stated the arrival of the larks as plain fact and kept the attribution only for the second sentence. Now "His companions remembered the larks. On the evening of Saturday, 3 October 1226, ... a great flock came down over the roof of the house where he lay." (The first try, one sentence with the attribution inside, was 32 words, so I made two sentences.)
