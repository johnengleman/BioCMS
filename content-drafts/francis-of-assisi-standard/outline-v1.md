# Francis of Assisi: scene outline (tier A)

**Keys.** The notes files number sources separately (S1–S7 differ by file), so every key has a file prefix: **OW-** notes-own-words; **WT-** notes-witnesses (S1 Celano I–II, S2 Three Companions, S5 Bonaventure); **CM-** notes-church-miracles (S1 Celano I, S2 Bonaventure 1868, S5 *Mira circa nos*); **XW-** notes-extra-writings (X1–X14); **XL-** notes-extra-lives (2C = Celano II, B1 = Boehmer texts, Jord. = Jordan of Giano, Mir. = Mirror).

## 1. The driving question

Francis wanted to follow the Gospel "simply and purely" and own nothing, because "the Lord made himself poor for us" [OW-Q1 p.68, p.82]. In his way stood first his hunger for glory and his horror of lepers, then his own success: thousands of brothers, ministers who wanted houses and books, and a body broken by penance and illness. Grace turned the bitter thing sweet at the start [OW-Q1 p.77]; at the end he gave back to God the Order, his body and his death, and met them singing [XL-2C §143, §158, §213; OW-I1].

He is famous as the poor man of Assisi: founder of the Friars Minor, the man of the Greccio crib, the Canticle and the stigmata. An eyewitness bishop found his brothers humbler "than all the religious of this time", with no monasteries, fields or houses [XL-B1d]. In a crusader camp he crossed unarmed to the Sultan, and in feuding cities he preached peace "like one addressing an assembly" [XL-B1d; XL-B1e].

## 2. Portrait

Joyous by nature, a spendthrift crowned king of the feasts he paid for [WT-S2 4, 7; XL-2C §7]. He sang in French and once fiddled a stick like a viol until he wept [XL-2C §127]. Quick humor: a small tonsure so his simple brothers "may have a share in my head" [XL-2C §193]. Honest to the point of comedy: he told a crowd he had eaten food "seasoned with lard" [XL-2C §131]. Faults: a hot temper over the Order [XL-2C §156, §188]; cruelty to his body, which he repented of at the end [XL-2C §129, §210-211]. Sharp with those who pried at his wounds: "Mind your own business" [XL-2C §135]. Tender "as a mother" to Leo [XW-X1 p.116].

Life with God: "not so much praying as … a living prayer", his face hidden in his sleeve [XL-2C §94-95]. "I know Christ, the poor man crucified" [XL-2C §105]. On La Verna: "Tu es humilitas … Tu es magna dulcedo nostra" [XW-X1 p.124-125].

## 3. The opening

Hook: an outsider's eyewitness, Bologna, 1222 [XL-B1e].

> On 15 August 1222 almost the whole city of Bologna crowded into the square before the public palace to hear a man preach. A student named Thomas, from Spalato, stood among them, and he did not flatter the preacher: the habit was dirty, the man looked contemptible, the face was plain. He did not preach like a preacher but talked like a man addressing a town meeting, and everything he said aimed at ending feuds. Noble clans who had spilled much blood in old hatreds made peace that day, and men and women pushed through the crowd to touch his hem or tear off a scrap of his tunic. He had nothing else to give them, and he had made sure of that.

Thesis: Francis founded the Friars Minor, who were to own "neither house nor place nor anything"; Honorius III confirmed their Rule on 29 Nov 1223 [OW-Q1 p.68; XW-X7]. By 1221 some 3,000 brothers met at the Portiuncula [XL-Jord. §13]. He set Clare on her way, made the Greccio crib, wrote the Canticle in his own Umbrian, and his vicar announced the wounds of Christ in his body [XW-X5; WT-S1 I.84; OW-I1; XW-X2 p.149]. Answered in chapters 6, 8–11, 13–14.

## 4. Chapters

1. **Revel king in a Perugian jail** — SC-01. Ends: freed and ill, the fields no longer please him [WT-S1 I.3].
2. **The bitter thing turns sweet** — SC-02, SC-03. Ends: he sells his father's cloth.
3. **Naked in the bishop's house** — SC-04. Ends: robbers throw "a herald of the great King" into snow [WT-S1 I.16].
4. **One book opened three times** — SC-05. Ends: a few brothers set out for Rome.
5. **A small black hen in Rome** — SC-06. Ends: a young noblewoman slips out to hear him [XW-X5 p.10].
6. **Clare leaves by the walled door** — SC-07; bridges birds, Morocco, Florence 1217 [WT-S1 I.56-59; XL-Mir. LXV]. Ends: he sails east [XL-Jord. §8].
7. **Unarmed into the Sultan's camp** — SC-08. Ends: "Go back, go back": the Order splits [XL-Jord. §10].
8. **"Henceforth I am dead to you"** — SC-09, SC-10; bridges the 1221 chapter [XL-Jord. §13-14]. Ends: the Rule sealed [XW-X7].
9. **A living child in the hay** — SC-11. Ends: he climbs La Verna [WT-S5 XIII.1].
10. **Wounds he would not show** — SC-12. Ends: eyes failing, doctors [WT-S1 I.97-101].
11. **Blind among the San Damiano mice** — SC-13; bridges the cautery and the Bishop–Podestà peace [XL-2C §166; XL-Mir. CI]. Ends: "Welcome, my Sister Death!" [XL-Mir. CXXII].
12. **Naked on the Portiuncula floor** — SC-14. Ends: Elias: "we are orphans without a father" [XW-X2 p.149].
13. **His friend becomes the Pope** — SC-15; bridges Clare at the window, San Giorgio, the 1230 translation [WT-S1 I.116-117; XL-Jord. §50; XW-X2]. Ends: the body hidden under the basilica.
14. **A hidden tomb, a world's patron** — Part 6 material. Ends: 2026 crowds file past his bones [XW-X9], closing the Bologna loop.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — Prison, 1202–03 (Perugia)
- What happens: captured, he jokes in chains; called mad, he says he will be "worshipped as a Saint all the world over"; he reconciles the hated knight.
- Why it is here: the glory-hungry boy grace will reverse.
- Have: [XL-2C §4; WT-S2 4; WT-S1 I.1, p.xxiii].
- Need: the Three Companions' wording of the knight episode.
- Where to look: WT-S2 §4.

### SC-02 — The leper, c.1205 (below Assisi)
- What happens: he dismounts, gives money, kisses the hand, receives the kiss of peace, then visits the leper house.
- Why it is here: the first turn, bitter to sweet, in his own words.
- Have: [WT-S2 11; WT-S1 I.17; XW-X1 p.77].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Testament opening [OW-Q1 p.77].

### SC-03 — The crucifix, c.1205–06 (San Damiano)
- What happens: in the ruined church he hears "Francis, go repair My house"; he weeps for days, sells cloth and horse at Foligno, throws the coins on the sill.
- Why it is here: first word from God, read literally as stones.
- Have: [WT-S1 II.10, I.8-9; WT-S2 13, 16; XL-2C §10].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-04 — Before Bishop Guido, c.1206 (Assisi)
- What happens: chained by his father, freed by his mother; before the bishop he strips: "Henceforth I may freely say 'our Father who art in heaven'"; a hair-shirt shows; the bishop covers him.
- Why it is here: he gives up father, name and goods.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.12-15, II.12; WT-S2 18-20].
- Need: Pietro's own words before the bishop, if 3C has them.
- Where to look: WT-S2 §19-20.

### SC-05 — The Gospel book, c.1208–09 (San Nicolò, Assisi)
- What happens: after the mission Gospel ("This is what I wish"), he, Bernard and Peter open the book three times: "this is our life and Rule"; Bernard gives all away.
- Why it is here: the Gospel becomes a rule; he is no longer alone.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.22; WT-S2 27-30; XL-Jord. §1-2].
- Need: the three verses exactly as 3C prints them.
- Where to look: WT-S2 §28-29.

### SC-06 — Before Innocent III, c.1209–10 (Rome)
- What happens: he dreams of a small black hen who cannot cover her chicks: "I am this hen"; tells the Pope the parable of the poor woman; Innocent recalls a little man holding up the Lateran.
- Why it is here: he entrusts his family to the Church.
- Have: [XL-2C §23-24; WT-S2 47-53; WT-S1 I.32-33; OW-Q1 p.79].
- Need: Innocent's words in full; the parable's text.
- Where to look: WT-S2 §49-51.

### SC-07 — Palm Sunday night, 18 March 1212 (to the Portiuncula)
- What happens: Clare, as Francis told her, breaks out by a walled door; friars with torches meet her; her hair is cut.
- Why it is here: his San Damiano prophecy comes true [WT-S2 24].
- Have: [XW-X5 p.10-17; XW-X6 I, XII, XVI, XVII].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Beatrice, Process witness 12, paraphrase [XW-X6 XII.2-5].

### SC-08 — The Sultan's camp, 1219 (Damietta)
- What happens: he foresees the 29 August defeat; later, seized on the road: "I am a Christian; take me to your lord"; the Sultan hears him for days, sends him back: "Pray for me …".
- Why it is here: unarmed where the crusade went armed.
- Have: [XL-B1d; XW-X3; XL-B1c; XL-2C §30].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Jacques de Vitry, *Historia orientalis* II.32 [XL-B1d].

### SC-09 — The leper's dish, undated (Portiuncula)
- What happens: he scolds Brother James for bringing a leper to the church, then condemns himself and eats from one dish with the leper's bleeding fingers.
- Why it is here: a real fault and its repair.
- Have: [XL-Mir. LVIII; OW-Q1 p.37].
- Need: an earlier source than the Mirror.
- Where to look: Latin Compilation of Assisi, Delorme 1922 [XL Part 7].

### SC-10 — Resignation, Michaelmas 1220 (Portiuncula)
- What happens: home to an Order in uproar, he resigns: "Henceforth I am dead to you"; later, ill: "Who are they that have snatched the Religion … out of my hands?"; God: it will stand if "reduced to … three".
- Why it is here: the hardest letting go, and its cost.
- Have: [XL-2C §143-144, §158, §188; XL-Jord. §8-11].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Jordan at the Pope's door [XL-Jord. §11].

### SC-11 — Christmas 1223 (Greccio)
- What happens: ox and ass, torches, Mass over the manger; he says "Bethlehem" like a bleating sheep; a man sees him wake a child in the hay.
- Why it is here: poverty seen in the Child.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.84-87; WT-S5 X.7]; the walls that should be "greased outside" [XL-2C §199].
- Need: nothing.

### SC-12 — Seraph and parchment, September 1224 (La Verna)
- What happens: the seraph and the marks; for tempted Leo he writes the Praises and a blessing; he hides the wounds.
- Why it is here: God's answer in his body; his prayer in his hand.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.94-95; WT-S5 XIII.1-5; XW-X1 p.124-125; XL-2C §49, §135-136].
- Need: Leo's marginal note on the Chartula, in full.
- Where to look: OW-R1 p.146-147; OW-Q1 p.199.

### SC-13 — The promise, late 1225 (San Damiano)
- What happens: nearly blind, mice on his table, he pities himself; a voice promises a treasure; at morning he begins "Most high, almighty, good Lord" and teaches the brothers to sing it.
- Why it is here: pain received as pledge of the Kingdom.
- Have: [XL-Mir. C; XL-2C §213; OW-R1 p.150-151].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: the Canticle in Umbrian [OW-I1].

### SC-14 — Death, evening of 3 October 1226 (Portiuncula)
- What happens: he blesses Assisi from the road; Jacoba arrives; naked on the ground, a habit lent him; "I have done my duty, may Christ teach you yours"; bread broken; John 13; larks.
- Why it is here: owning nothing, not even his habit.
- Have: [XL-2C §214-217; WT-S1 I.108-110; XL-Mir. CXII-CXIII, CXXIV; OW-S4 p.115-116].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Celano II §214-215 [XL-2C].

### SC-15 — Canonization, 16 July 1228 (San Giorgio, Assisi)
- What happens: Gregory IX, once his protector Hugolino, weeps; cardinals need no miracles for a life "we have seen with our eyes"; miracles read; decree; the Pope kisses the tomb.
- Why it is here: the "one pope" he asked for makes him a saint [XL-Jord. §11].
- Have: [CM-S1 §122-126; CM-S5 §7-8].
- Need: Celano's words for the first tomb healing.
- Where to look: CM-S1 §127.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** Rules 1221 and 1223 [OW-Q1 p.37-72; XW-X7] (ch. 4, 8); Chartula 1224 [OW-R1 p.146] (ch. 10); Canticle c.1225 [OW-I1] (ch. 11); Testament 1226, ruled on in *Quo elongati* 1230 [XW-X11 p.79-80] (ch. 12–13); Last Will for Clare, kept in her 1253 Rule [XW-X1 p.76] (ch. 13).
- **Changed:** Friars Minor, 1209–10, Rule 1223 [WT-S1 p.xxiii; XW-X7]; Poor Ladies, 1212 [XW-X5]; Germany mission 1221 [XL-Jord. §14]; Greccio crib, "first Nativity Scene" per the 2026 decree [CM-S9]; Portiuncula indulgence [XW-X10] (ch. 6–9, 14).
- **Famous:** Elias's letter 1226 [XW-X2]; Celano's Life 1228–29 [WT-S1]; translation 25 May 1230 [XW-X2]; relics in Germany c.1230 [XL-Jord. §59]; tomb found 1818 [XW-X8]; patron of Italy 1939, ecology 1979 [CM-S6, S7]; Pope Francis's name 2013 [CM-S10]; *Laudato si'* 2015, *Fratelli tutti* signed at the tomb 2020 [XW-X14]; Year of St Francis 2026 [XW-X10]. Needs people bring: peace, poverty, creation (ch. 13–14).
- **Path:** tomb healings from burial day; 1228 inquiry at Assisi and Perugia; canonized 16 July 1228; feast 4 Oct [CM-S1 §122-127; CM-S5 §9] (ch. 13).

**Fact needs:**
1. The Franciscan family and Poor Clares today. Look: Cath. Enc. "Franciscan Order"; XW-X10.
2. Pius XII, *Licet commissa* (1939), primary text. Look: vatican.va; AAS.
3. Official 1818–20 record of the remains. Look: *Assisiensem Basilicam* (1820); Cooper 2005 [XW-X8].

## 8. What we leave out

- Wolf of Gubbio, Perfect Joy: Fioretti, after 1322 [WT-S4; OW-F1].
- Fire ordeal, Sultan's secret conversion: not in the eyewitness [XL-B1d].
- Tomb miracle lists, stigmata-doubter stories: miracles section.
- Snowballs, devils in the tower: repeat SC-10's struggle [XL-2C §116-120].
- Mirror's mountain voice and 5,000 at the chapter: late; Jordan says 3,000.
- Nicholas V's 1449 vision of the upright body: legend [XW-X2].
- Clare's milk dream: belongs to her life [XW-X6].
- Elias's "heavenly market": obscure, unchecked [XW-X2].

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Born 1181/82; baptized John [WT-S1 II.3]. Hugolino = Gregory IX.
- Died evening of Saturday 3 Oct 1226; Elias counts it to Sunday 4 Oct; feast 4 Oct [XW-X2 p.150].
- Canonized 16 July 1228; Bonaventure 1868's "6th of July" is a misprint [CM-S2].
- Translation 25 May 1230 vs 21/22 May [XW-X2].
- Stigmata about 14 Sept 1224 (Bonaventure); Elias gives no place or date.
- Canticle: late 1225 vs 1224; strophe stories are the Mirror's (c.1318), attribute.
- San Damiano voice absent from Celano I: "the early Lives tell". Call him "the Sultan of Egypt" [XL-B1d].
- The Peace Prayer is not his: first printed 1912 [OW-P1].
- Mirror scenes (SC-09, SC-13): "his companions remembered".
- 2C §137-138 does not name Elias in the side-wound story [XL Part 6].
