## Changed sentences (biography.html)

### Opening

1. Before: A student named Thomas stood in the crowd, and he remembered the preacher without flattery.
   After: A student named Thomas stood in the crowd. He remembered the preacher without flattery.
   Why: two facts glued with ", and".

2. Before: Bologna had noble clans whose feuds had raged for years with much blood spilled.
   After: In Bologna, noble clans had carried on bloody feuds for years.
   Why: "with much blood spilled" was a loose, awkward tail.

3. Before: He founded the brothers called the Friars Minor, the Lesser Brothers, and their Rule told them to make nothing their own: …
   After: He founded the brothers called the Friars Minor, the Lesser Brothers. Their Rule told them to make nothing their own: …
   Why: an apposition plus a glued second fact in one sentence.

4. Before: He set Saint Clare on the way that became the Poor Clares.
   After: He set Saint Clare on the way of life that became the Poor Clares.
   Why: "the way that became" did not make literal sense.

5. Before: Then a love took hold of him that was so great he could not keep quiet about it.
   After: Then a love so great took hold of him that he could not keep quiet about it.
   Why: the "that" clause was separated from its noun.

### A Rich Young Man in Prison

6. Before: Because he was "a noble in his manners," they kept him with the captured knights.
   After: … his captors kept him with the captured knights.
   Why: "they" read as the men of Assisi, the last plural named. (The checker may confirm "captors"; it means only the men of Perugia who held him.)

### Saint Francis Kisses a Leper

7. Before: Outside the town there was a cave, and he went into it alone while a friend waited outside.
   After: … while a friend waited nearby.
   Why: "outside … outside" in one sentence.

8. Before: He gave something to each of them, …
   After: He gave something to each leper there, …
   Why: "them" followed "the lepers' hospital", not a plural noun.

9. Before: Not long afterward he was walking past San Damiano, a little church outside Assisi so old that it seemed about to fall.
   After: Not long afterward he was walking past San Damiano, a little church outside Assisi. It was so old that it seemed about to fall.
   Why: two modifiers stacked on one noun.

10. Before: He took the words at their plainest. He thought the house was the old church around him with its crumbling walls.
    After: He took the words at their plainest: the house, he thought, was the old church around him with its crumbling walls.
    Why: three sentences in a row began with "He".

11. Before: Saint Francis threw the coins onto a windowsill, "caring as little for it as for dust."
    After: Saint Francis threw the money onto a windowsill, …
    Why: "coins" did not agree with "it" in the quotation.

### Saint Francis Gives Everything Back to His Father

12. Before: It happened in the bishop's house, and many people had come to watch.
    After: They met in the bishop's house, and many people had come to watch.
    Why: "It" had no clear referent.

13. Before: He was so ashamed that he turned away. Then he went back in and told them aloud of his shame, and he begged in French. In French too he told the people around him that women given to God would one day live there.
    After: He was so ashamed that he turned away. Then he went back to the house, told the men aloud of his shame, and begged in French. In French too he told the people around him that women given to God would one day live at San Damiano.
    Why: he had not gone in, so he could not "go back in". "there" pointed to the gambling house, not to San Damiano. The text itself names San Damiano as the place of this saying (the paragraph about Saint Clare: "as he had once said in French they would").

### The First Brothers Join Saint Francis / Saint Clare

14. Before: … she held fast to the altar cloths and showed them her shorn head.
    After: … showed her family her shorn head.
    Why: "them" could be the nuns.

15. Before: He left two vicars to govern the brothers at home and took ship …
    After: He left two vicars to govern the brothers in his place and took ship …
    Why: "vicar" is a Church word; "in his place" gives its meaning with it.

### Saint Francis Goes to the Sultan

    After: Before one battle in August 1219, Saint Francis told his companion …
    Why: two phrases stacked before the subject.

17. Before: The Sultan was the Muslim ruler of Egypt, and Jacques calls his people Saracens:
    After: The Sultan was the Muslim ruler of Egypt. Jacques calls the Sultan's people Saracens:
    Why: glued facts; "his" could be Jacques.

18. Before: He did not know their language, so as the blows fell …
    After: He did not know the Saracens' language, so as the blows fell …

19. Before: He had hoped to die for the faith, but God did not grant it. He came back alive, carrying a Sultan's request for his prayers.
    After: … Instead he came back alive, carrying the Sultan's request for his prayers.
    Why: three "He" starts in a row; "a Sultan" for the Sultan already met.

### Saint Francis Gives Up Leading His Order

    After: He cancelled the letters the first brother had brought back and sent the other away in disgrace.
    Why: "the one brother's letters" was awkward.

22. Before: Saint Francis called lepers his "brother Christians," and he scolded James.
    After: Saint Francis, who called lepers his "brother Christians," scolded James.
    Why: two facts glued with ", and".

23. Before: … and the people of the country brought bread and wine in plenty.
    After: … and the people of the countryside brought bread and wine in plenty.

24. Before: Ministers were the brothers set over the others. One of them asked to keep his valuable books.
    After: One of the ministers, the brothers set over the others, asked to keep his valuable books.
    Why: a dictionary sentence before the story; now the meaning comes with the word.

25. Before: It also gave the reason he had lived by since the day he handed his clothes back to his father: …
    After: It also gave the reason Saint Francis had lived by since he gave his clothes back to his father: …
    Why: "he" read as Pope Honorius, the last man named; the sentence was over 30 words.

### Saint Francis Receives the Wounds of Christ

    After: The explanation now follows the first sentence: "Then marks like the wounds of nails began to appear in his hands and his feet. The Church calls them the stigmata." The paragraph ends on the quotation.
    Why: write-slim.md forbids a dictionary sentence tacked on afterward. The paragraph now ends on its strongest line. The note marker stays at the paragraph end.

27. Before: He said so in a short letter in his own hand, which is kept today in the cathedral of Spoleto.
    After: He said so in a short letter in his own hand. The letter is kept today in the cathedral of Spoleto.

### Saint Francis Makes Peace in Assisi

    Why: the doctor's words read as the narrator's statement; two sentences in a row began "Saint Francis". "Death would come" also leads into "Welcome, my Sister Death!"

31. Before: If only he could have that tunic when his father died, …
    After: If only he could have his father's tunic when his father died, …
    Why: "that tunic" had not been introduced.

## Resolved by the checker (2026-10-06)

1. Saint Nicholas church (n. 36): correct as written. 3C §29 and Bonaventure III both have Francis take the closed book and open it three times; Celano II §15 says only "they opened". No change. (La Verna, n. 78, also correct: his companion opens the book.)
2. "Poor Ladies": glossed at first use, "the Poor Ladies, the order of sisters that had begun with Saint Clare" (Celano I §116; 3C §24). The sentence was split to stay under 30 words.
3. Spoleto quotation: now a direct question, "Which can do more for you, the lord or the servant?" (3C §6).
4. Hen (n. 58): "She was like a tame dove, with feathers down her legs and over her feet" (Celano II §24).
5. "his captors" (n. 6): the source is passive ("he was imprisoned along with the knights"), so the text now reads "he was put with the captured knights." "at San Damiano" (n. 31): correct (3C §24).
6. Titles: no title added; Bernard, Giles, Leo, Angelo, Peter Cathanii and Jacoba stay plain because no source on file settles them (see facts.md).
7. Two long sentences in quotations (n. 109, n. 115) left alone; the quotations are exact.
8. Other fixes are listed in check.md.
