# Fact sheet: Saint Francis of Assisi (checked 2026-10-06, heart draft)

The teachings essay and the miracle list must use these names, dates, numbers, and wordings exactly. "n." means the note number in the current `biography.html` (135 notes; renumbered by the middle revision of 2026-10-07). Where a later writer finds a source that disagrees, list the conflict for the checker. This file replaces the older fact sheet.

## Names and titles

- **Saint Francis of Assisi.** Born John (named by his mother); his father, back from France, named him Francis (3C §2; n. 5). Father: Pietro di Bernardone, a rich cloth merchant. Birth: 1181 or 1182 (Catholic Encyclopedia; Howell gives 1182). Say "1181 or 1182".
- **Saint Clare** (Saint Clare of Assisi). The sisters: the Poor Ladies, later the Poor Clares. First use in the biography: "the Poor Ladies, the order of sisters that had begun with Saint Clare" (n. 55; 3C §24 and Celano I §116 call Clare the first plant of the order).
- **Pope Saint John Paul II** (title kept). Pope Francis, Pope Leo XIV, Pope Pius XII, Pope Pius VII, Pope Innocent III, Pope Honorius III, Pope Gregory IX (Cardinal Hugolino, bishop of Ostia, before 1227): no title.
- **Left plain, because no source read in this project settles the title** (the same decision as the Clare fact sheet): Brother Bernard (of Assisi, the first companion), Brother Giles (Butler's old text writes "B. Giles", but no decree is on file), Brother Leo, Brother Angelo, Brother Peter Cathanii, Lady Jacoba of Settesoli, Brother Elias, Brother Pacifico, Brother Leonard, Brother James, John of Greccio. Do not add "Blessed" to any of them without a source.
- Not saints, plain: Thomas of Celano, Thomas of Spalato, Jacques de Vitry (bishop of Acre), Jordan of Giano, Caesarius of Speyer.
- Saints named with a title in the text: Saint Francis, Saint Clare, Saint Michael, Saint Catherine of Siena, Pope Saint John Paul II, Saint John (the Gospel). Church and place names keep their usual form (Saint Nicholas church, San Damiano, San Giorgio, Saint John Lateran).
- Jacques de Vitry's passage on Francis is in his *Historia occidentalis* ch. 32 (book II of the Moschus edition). Boehmer prints it as "Historia orientalis II.32". Use "Historia occidentalis".

## Dates and numbers

- Perugia war, captivity: 1202 to 1203 (Howell, "Principal Dates"); "held in Perugia for a year" (3C §4).
- The Gospel call at the Portiuncula: "about the year 1209" (Jordan §2: 1209, third year of his conversion).
- The Rome journey of the twelve: eleven companions and Francis (3C §46; Celano I §32). Eight companions before the twelve (Celano I §29).
- Saint Clare's flight: the night after Palm Sunday, "1211 or 1212" (Robinson 1212; the Process about 1211). She stayed at San Damiano 42 years (Process; Robinson p. 18).
- 1217: the brothers sent abroad; Hugolino stops Francis at Florence (Howell, "Principal Dates"; Mirror LXV). Jordan's own text dates the first missions to 1219; the Note on the Sources now says so.
- Damietta: 29 August 1219 (Howell's note); 6,000 Christians killed or taken (Celano II §30); about two years (1219 to 1220) in the East.
- Jordan of Giano's section numbers are those of Analecta Franciscana I (Quaracchi 1885): Sultan §10; crossing and vicars §11; the prophetess §13; the Pope's door §14; the 1221 chapter §16; the call for Germany §17; Jordan's own story §18; the death §50; the relics §59.
- Michaelmas 1220 (end of September): Francis gives up the leadership; Peter Cathanii is vicar from September 1220 until his death on 10 March 1221 (Howell). Elias takes his place.
- Pentecost 1221: about 3,000 brothers, 23 tables, about 90 volunteers for Germany (Jordan §§16–17).
- 29 November 1223: Honorius III confirms the Rule (*Solet annuere*); the bull is kept at the Sacro Convento, Assisi (Robinson p. 64 note).
- Greccio: about fifteen days before Christmas, third year before his death (Celano I §84), so Christmas 1223.
- La Verna: Lent of Saint Michael, 15 August to 29 September 1224 (Leo's note; Howell); the seraph "about the feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross" (14 September).
- Spring 1226: Siena ("the sixth month before his death", Celano I §105), Cortona, then Assisi; July 1226 in the bishop's palace (Catholic Encyclopedia).
- Death: Saturday evening 3 October 1226, "in the first hour of the night"; Elias counted that hour as Sunday 4 October (Elias's letter, in Wadding vol. 2, p. 150). Jordan §50 gives the 4th of October.
- Canonization: 16 July 1228 at Assisi (Celano I §126: "the 17th day before the Kalends of August"). The Church keeps the feast on 4 October.
- Translation: 25 May 1230, the eve of Pentecost (Wadding vol. 2, pp. 234–235); the first stone was laid by Pope Gregory IX on the day after the canonization (Catholic Encyclopedia).
- Tomb found: 12 December 1818 after about 52 nights of digging (Catholic Encyclopedia; Key to Umbria). Examined in 1978 (Paul VI); the man about five feet two inches tall with deformed feet rests on the OFM Conv. page (not re-read in this check).
- Public display of the remains: 21 February 2026 (case moved to the lower church) to 22 March; first display since 1230 (Vatican News, 23 Feb 2026). Booking figure "nearly 400,000" rests on the OFM Conv. page (not re-read). The final count reported by the press was about 370,000.
- Patron of Italy with Saint Catherine of Siena: 1939 (Pius XII). Patron of those who care for the natural world: 1979 (John Paul II, *Inter Sanctos*).

## Places

- Assisi, Perugia, Spoleto, Foligno, San Damiano, the Portiuncula (the Little Portion), the church of Saint Nicholas near the marketplace, Bevagna, Florence, Damietta, Greccio, La Verna, Sartiano, Siena, Cortona, Arezzo (the doctor), San Giorgio (first burial), the Basilica of San Francesco, the Lateran.
- San Damiano was the church he repaired; Clare's house for 42 years. The cell there was "a little hut made of woven mats" (Latin *storiis*; Evans "wattles").

## Approved wording of quotations (plain modern English; our translation)

1. Thomas of Spalato, Bologna, 15 August 1222: "angels, men, demons" (n. 1). Robe dirty, "someone to despise", plain face; "God gave the plain man's words such power" (n. 2).
2. Rule of 1223, ch. 6: "neither house, nor place, nor anything" (n. 68); "the Lord made himself poor for us in this world" (n. 68).
3. Spoleto (n. 11): "Which can do more for you, the lord or the servant?" "The lord." "Why, then, are you seeking the servant instead of the lord?" "Lord, what do you want me to do?"
4. Prison (n. 7): "What do you think of me? I will be worshipped by the whole world." The proud knight: "very proud and unbearable" (n. 8).
5. The bride (n. 13): "I will marry a nobler and more beautiful bride than you have ever seen. She will surpass all others in beauty and in wisdom."
6. Testament (n. 18; Opuscula p. 77): "The Lord gave me, Brother Francis, to begin to do penance in this way. When I was in sins, it seemed too bitter to me to see lepers. The Lord himself led me among them, and I showed them mercy. And when I left them, what had seemed bitter to me turned into sweetness of soul and body. And afterward I stayed a little while and left the world." Also (n. 135; p. 79): "And we wanted to have no more." (n. 33; p. 80): "The Lord give you peace." (n. 38; p. 79): "in a few words and simply."
7. The San Damiano cross (n. 20): "Francis, do you not see that my house is being destroyed? Go, then, and repair it for me." "I will do it gladly, Lord."
8. Before the bishop (n. 26): "From now on I can freely say 'Our Father who art in heaven,' and no longer 'father Pietro di Bernardone.' See, I not only give him back the money, but I give up all my clothes as well. So I will go naked to the Lord."
9. Saint Nicholas church (n. 35–36): Francis took the closed book, bowed before the altar, and opened it three times himself (3C §§28–29; Bonaventure III: "thrice opened the book of the Gospels"; Celano II §15 says only "they opened"). "If you want to be perfect, go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven." "Take nothing with you on your journey." "Whoever wants to come after me must deny himself." "My brothers, this is our life and Rule, and that of all who will want to join our company. Go, then, and do what you have heard."
10. The Pope's vision (n. 40): "Surely this is the man who will hold up the Church of Christ by what he does and teaches." (Celano II §17.)
11. Saint Clare (n. 42): "His words seemed to her like fire, and his deeds seemed more than human." Window (n. 121): "to see the father who would not speak to them again, nor come back to them." Message to Clare (n. 111): "Go and tell Sister Clare not to grieve or be sad because she cannot see me now. Let her know that she will surely see me before her death, and that she and her sisters will be greatly comforted when they do."
12. Hugolino at Florence (n. 46): "God has chosen and sent the brothers for the good and the salvation of the souls of all the people of this world."
13. Damietta (n. 48): "If I tell them this, I will be thought a fool, and if I keep silent, I will not escape my conscience." "Take no notice of how men judge you, for this is not the first time you have been thought a fool."
14. The Sultan (n. 50–53; the block quote is now a scene in time order): Jacques de Vitry (Boehmer, Analekten, pp. 104–105) has Francis say to his captors, "I am a Christian. Take me to your lord." (The text names Jacques here. Jordan §10 gives the blows and the cry, so the two are told as two accounts.) The Sultan "armed with the shield of faith" is Jacques too. The Sultan's last words: "Pray for me, that God may reveal to me the law and faith that pleases him more." Celano I §57: the Sultan "looked on him as a man unlike all other men" (the Latin *quasi virum omnibus dissimilem*; Howell's "almost" is a misreading). "Soldan! Soldan!" (Jordan §10).
15. The prophetess (n. 54; Jordan §13): "Go back, go back! Because Brother Francis is away, the Order is troubled, split and scattered."
16. The hen (n. 56; Celano II §§23–24, Latin): a little black hen, "like a tame dove, with feathers down her legs and over her feet", with more chicks than anyone could count; "I am this hen, small in size and black by nature." The Church of Rome, "their mother", "would be zealous for the glory of their poverty" (*aemulabitur gloriam*; Howell: "jealous for"). Francis had brooded over enemies outside the Order and rebels inside it (§23).
17. The Pope's door (n. 57; Jordan §14): now a short scene, no block quote. "Father Pope, may God give you peace." "You have given me many popes. Give me one to whom I can speak when I need to, who will hear and settle my cases and my Order's in your place." He asks for the Lord of Ostia (Cardinal Hugolino), and the Pope grants it. Hugolino cancels Brother Philip's letters; the brother who gathered lepers (John of Compello) is driven from the Pope's court in disgrace (Jordan §14, passive).
18. Resignation (n. 58–59; Celano II §§143, 151): "From now on I am dead to you. But here is Brother Peter Cathanii. Let us all, you and I, obey him." "Lord, I entrust to you the family that you have entrusted to me until now." "a novice of one hour".
19. The leper's dish (n. 60): "You should not lead the Christians around, because it is not proper for you or for them." "Let this be my penance: that I eat from one dish together with my brother Christian." Mirror ch. LVIII (Evans: the leper's fingers "drawn together and bleeding"; de la Warr: "covered with running sores").
20. Poverty (n. 64; Celano II §84, Howell): "This man's need puts us to great shame, and sharply rebukes our poverty. … I chose Poverty for my riches and for my Lady. And see how she shines more brightly in that man." (Howell: "This man's want does us great shame, and severely rebukes our poverty … I chose Poverty for my riches, and for my Lady, and see! she shines more brightly in yonder man." Confirmed in Howell's English of 1908; the d'Alençon scan lacks the Latin page.)
21. Greccio (n. 76): "I want to call to mind that Child who was born in Bethlehem. I want in some way to see with my bodily eyes his hardships as a baby: how he lay in a manger on the hay, with the ox and the donkey standing by." Christmas on a Friday (n. 75): "I want even the walls to eat meat on such a day. If they cannot, they should at least be greased on the outside!"
22. The stigmata (n. 83): "not by martyrdom of the body, but by a fire in the soul." (Bonaventure, Latin *per incendium mentis*.) "My secret is mine." (n. 84)
23. Praises of God (n. 86; Opuscula pp. 124–125): "You alone are the holy Lord God, who work wonders … You are humility. You are patience. You are security. You are rest. You are gladness and joy … You are our hope. You are our faith. You are our great sweetness. You are our eternal life, great and wonderful Lord, almighty God, merciful Savior." Blessing: "The Lord bless you, Brother Leo." Leo's note: "the hand of the Lord was upon him." Letter to Leo (n. 88): "I speak to you, my son, as a mother does."
24. Hiding the wounds (n. 89): "Mind your own business." "Ask what that is, if you don't know that it is an eye." He held out only his fingers to kiss, or sometimes his sleeve; a piece of hide lay over the wounds of his feet (Celano II §136).
25. Sartiano (n. 92–93): "There, brother donkey. This is how you take the whip." The seven snow figures: wife, two sons, two daughters, servant, maid.
26. Mice (n. 97; Celano II §213): "Suppose the whole mass of the earth and the whole frame of the world were gold beyond price … Would you not rejoice, and gladly bear for a moment what you are bearing now?" "Then be glad, for your sickness is the pledge of my kingdom." Cell: a little hut made of woven mats.
27. The Canticle (n. 98–99): "Most High, almighty, good Lord"; "Altissimu onnipotente bonsignore, tue so' le laude la gloria e l'honore et onne benedictione." Forgiveness verse (n. 105): "Praised be my Lord for those who forgive for love of you, and bear sickness and trouble. Blessed are those who bear them in peace, for by you, Most High, they will be crowned." Death verse (n. 108): "Praised be my Lord for our sister, bodily death, from whom no living person can escape." "Welcome, my Sister Death!"
28. The minstrels (n. 100): "We are the minstrels of the Lord, and this is the reward we ask of you: that you be in a state of true repentance." The stick and the little bow: Celano II §127.
29. Peace in Assisi (n. 104–106): "It is a great shame for us servants of God that the bishop and the podestà hate each other so." The podestà: "Even if a man had killed my own brother or my son, I would forgive him!" The bishop: "Since I am quick to anger by nature, it is right that you should pardon me too."
30. The tunic (n. 109; Celano II §50): "I give you this tunic. Take it, so that from now on it is yours. Though I wear it while I live, at my death let it pass to you."
31. Elias (n. 110; Celano I §108): "On whom am I holding my right hand?" "On Brother Elias." "That is what I want."
32. Last will for Clare (n. 112; Opuscula p. 76): "I, little Brother Francis, want to follow the life and poverty of our most high Lord Jesus Christ and of his most holy mother, and to hold to it to the end."
33. Jacoba (n. 113; Mirror CXII): the brothers had written the letter and were looking for a messenger when she knocked. "This rule is not to be kept for that lady, whom such great faith and devotion have brought here from so far away."
34. Bernard (n. 114; Mirror CVII): "This is not the head of my brother Bernard." "The first brother that the Lord gave me was Brother Bernard."
35. The bare ground (n. 115; Celano II §214, block quote as in the biography), ending "I have done my duty. May Christ teach you yours."
36. Death (n. 117–120): "Brother doctor, boldly predict that death is near, for to me it will be the gate of life." The walk "for as long as it would take to walk a mile at an easy pace." Psalm: "With my voice I cried to the Lord." Elias (n. 120): "Before I begin to speak, I sigh." "We are orphans without a father, robbed of the light of our eyes." "I announce to you a great joy and a new miracle."
37. The cardinals at Perugia (n. 125; Celano I §124): "There is no need … for miracles to prove the most holy life of this most holy man. We have seen it with our eyes, touched it with our hands, and tested it under the teaching of truth."
38. The canonization (n. 126): "Like the morning star amid the mist"; "be numbered in the list of the saints, and that his feast be kept on the day of his death"; Gregory IX: "a patron whose friendship We enjoyed on earth."
39. Jordan's relics (n. 128): "Rejoice, brothers, for I know that you have praised not me as myself, but in me our Father, blessed Francis."
40. Pope Francis (n. 132): "Don't forget the poor!"; "the man of poverty, the man of peace, the man who loves and protects creation."

41. The new chapter, "Saint Francis Grieves for His Order" (n. 64–74):
    - Roof (n. 65–66; Mirror VII, Evans; Celano II §57, Latin): a small hut thatched with straw with walls of wattle and daub; the people of Assisi built a great house of stone and mortar "within a few days", "with the greatest devotion", without his consent and in his absence; Francis climbed up first (Celano II §57 *primus assurgit*), tore off tiles "with a strong hand" (*manu valida*), told the brothers to come up and pull down "that monster set against poverty" (*monstrum paupertati oppositum*). The knights: "Brother, this house belongs to the town of Assisi, and we are here for the town. Do not destroy what is ours." "If it is yours, I will not touch it."
    - Books and the Rule (n. 67; Celano II §62; Mirror III, Evans): "I am not going to lose the book of the Gospel, which I have promised to obey, for the sake of your books." The ministers had the clause "Take nothing with you on your journey" taken out of the Rule. "The ministers think they can deceive the Lord and me." He wanted the Rule to say at its beginning and its end that the brothers were bound to keep the Holy Gospel.
    - The novice and the psalter (n. 69–71; Mirror IV, Evans; Sabatier ch. 4, Latin): a novice who could read the psalms "though not very well"; the Minister General had allowed a psalter. "Once you have a psalter you will want a breviary too. And once you have a breviary, you will sit in a chair like a great lord of the Church (*magnus praelatus*) and say to your brother, 'Bring me my breviary!'" Ashes on his head: "I, a breviary! I, a breviary!" "Brother, I too have been tempted to have books." Later, at the Portiuncula: "Go and do as your minister tells you." "Wait for me, brother, wait!" "Come back with me, brother, and show me the place where I told you to do as your minister says about the psalter." "My fault, brother, my fault! Whoever wants to be a Lesser Brother should have nothing but his tunic, as the Rule allows him, and a cord, and drawers, and shoes for those who plainly need them." "The brother who now led the Order" is our inference (Francis had resigned).
    - The brother's question (n. 72; Celano II §188): "Son, I love the brothers as much as I can. But if they followed in my footsteps, I would love them more, and I would not make myself a stranger to them. Some of those set over them are drawing them toward other things. They hold up the examples of the men of old and think little of my warnings. But the end will show what they are doing."
    - The illness (n. 73; Celano II §188): "Who are they who have snatched my Order, mine and the brothers', out of my hands? If I get to the general chapter, I will show them what my will is!" "Let them live as they like. The ruin of a few is less harm than the ruin of many."
    - God's answer (n. 74; Celano II §158): "Why are you troubled, little man? Have I set you as shepherd over my Order in such a way that you do not know I am its chief protector? … I called the brothers, and I will keep them and feed them. … So do not be troubled, but work out your own salvation. Even if the Order comes down to three brothers, it will stand firm forever through my gift." Afterward he would say that one good man can overcome a great crowd of imperfect brothers, and that a single ray of light drives away countless shadows.
42. Florence (n. 46; Mirror LXV): his protest that it would shame him to stay behind while the brothers he had sent to far countries suffered for the Lord's sake.
43. Bevagna (n. 45; Celano I §58): "journeying through the valley of Spoleto", "near Bevagna".

## Wordings fixed by this check (2026-10-06)

- Spoleto: the Lord's first question is now a direct question ("Which can do more for you, the lord or the servant?"), as in 3C §6.
- "his captors kept him with the knights" became "he was put with the captured knights" (3C §4 is passive).
- San Damiano: Francis "set to work rebuilding the church", not "rebuilt it himself" (3C §24 names fellow-labourers). Clare chapter: "the church he had worked so hard to repair" (Robinson p. 17).
- Scarlet cloth "from his father's house" (Bonaventure II.4, Washbourne translation: "out of his house").
- The hen: "like a tame dove, with feathers down her legs and over her feet" (Celano II §24; 3C §63 says "feathered legs with feet like a tame dove").
- Greccio: "Often when he said 'Bethlehem'" (Celano I §86 "saepe", not every time).
- Jacoba: the letter was written, and the messenger was being sought, when she knocked (Mirror CXII).
- Leo's letter: "cared for Leo like a son" (the letter says "my son, as a mother does"); the Spoleto cathedral holds the autograph (Robinson p. 130).
- Stigmata hidden: "a piece of hide laid over the wounds" (Latin *pelle*), and "only his fingers, or sometimes his sleeve" (Celano II §136).
- Fiddle: a stick and "a little bow" (Celano II §127 *arculum*), not a second stick.
- "Most High, almighty, good Lord" (not "most gracious"): the same words as the block quote.
- The Sultan "looked on him as a man unlike all other men" (not "almost all"): the Latin has *quasi* meaning "as".
- Jacques: "preached Christ to him" (Boehmer's text *Christum predicantem*).
- Keeping faith with Lady Poverty "cost him dearly", and he spoke "with great force of spirit" (Celano II §188 *vehementia spiritus*): "his anger broke out" was an added feeling.
- Saint Clare went to San Damiano "Later", after the stay at Sant'Angelo di Panzo (Robinson p. 17).
- Summary: "paid for his friends' feasts" (not "every feast").
- "Sources: Notes": Jordan's section numbers corrected to Analecta Franciscana I; Jacques de Vitry's passage is *Historia occidentalis* ch. 32 (Boehmer pp. 102–105).

## Open points

- The OFM Conv. page ("His Body Made Visible") could not be re-read: the 21 February date, "nearly 400,000 booked", and the 1978 height and foot details rest on the researcher's earlier reading.
- Celano II §50 does not name Leo; Howell's note does (n. 85, n. 109).
- The leper's fingers ("bent and bleeding") follow Evans's Mirror; de la Warr's text says "covered with running sores". Both agree on the dish.

## Wordings fixed by the middle-revision check (2026-10-07)

- The Sultan scene: Jacques's "I am a Christian. Take me to your lord." and Jordan's beating and "Soldan! Soldan!" are told as two accounts, not one moment (Jacques has Francis speaking; Jordan says he did not know their language). The Sultan "said to him" the request for prayer (not "asked one thing").
- The hen: the Church of Rome "would be zealous for the glory of their poverty" (not "stand guard over").
- Pope's door: the brother who gathered lepers "was driven from the Pope's court" (Jordan §14 is passive).
- Novice: the explanation of "breviary" is outside the quotation marks. God's answer (Celano II §158): ellipses mark the two cuts.
- Note 3 now names the sources for "rich cloth merchant's son" (Three Companions §§3–4; Catholic Encyclopedia).
- Wording kept: "unlike all other men" (n. 52), "a novice of one hour" (n. 59), "Take nothing with you on your journey" (the Gospel words, in our modernized wording).
