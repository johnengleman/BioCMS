# Saint Francis of Assisi: scene outline (tier A, revision 2)

**Keys** as in outline-v1.md. The approved biography.html holds checked wording for every scene. No `sources/` folder exists.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Francis wanted to serve one Lord and own nothing, because "the Lord made himself poor for us in this world" [OW-Q1 p.68]. In his way stood his hunger for glory and his horror of lepers; then his success, thousands of brothers whose leaders wanted houses and books; then a blind, failing body. Grace turned the bitter into sweetness [OW-Q1 p.77]; he gave his brothers back to God [XL-2C §143], took his pain as "the pledge of my kingdom" [XL-2C §213], and died on the bare ground in lent clothes [XL-2C §214-215].

Why it was beautiful to him: it was a bride "nobler and more beautiful" than any [WT-S1 I.7]; later he named her: "I chose Poverty for my riches, and for my Lady" [XL-2C §84]. It freed him to say "Our Father who art in heaven" and go "naked to the Lord" [WT-S1 II.12]. The reader first hears it in ch. 2, his first great decision: at Spoleto, "Why are you seeking the servant instead of the lord?", and days later the bride [XL-2C §6-7]. Ch. 4 seals it.

Thread, used lightly: the would-be "great prince" [XL-2C §6] becomes "a herald of the great King" [WT-S1 I.16] and calls his brothers "minstrels of the Lord" [XL-Mir. C].

**Famous** as the poor man of Assisi who founded the Friars Minor and bore the wounds of Christ. **Apart:** an eyewitness bishop found his brothers humbler "than all the religious of this time", with nowhere "to lay their heads" [XL-B1d]. He crossed unarmed to the Sultan, and in feuding cities he preached peace [XL-B1d; XL-B1e].

## 2. Portrait

Joyous by nature; a spendthrift crowned king of the feasts he paid for [WT-S2 4, 7; XL-2C §7]. He sang in French and drew a stick across his arm like a fiddle bow until he wept [XL-2C §127]. Quick humor: a small tonsure so his simple brothers "may have a share in my head" [XL-2C §193]. Honest to comedy: he told a crowd his food had been "seasoned with lard" [XL-2C §131]. Courteous like a knight [XL-2C §31]. Faults: a hot temper over the Order [XL-2C §188]; cruelty to his body, which he asked to forgive him at the end [XL-2C §129, §210-211]. Sharp with people who pried at his wounds: "Mind your own business" [XL-2C §135]. Tender "as a mother" to Leo [XW-X1 p.116].

Life with God: he hid his face in his sleeve and seemed "a living prayer" [XL-2C §94-95]. "I know Christ, the poor man crucified" [XL-2C §105]. "You are our great sweetness" [XW-X1 p.125]. Christmas was "the feast of feasts" [XL-2C §199].

## 3. The opening

Hook: an outsider sees him, Bologna 1222 [XL-B1e]. Draft:

> On 15 August 1222, almost the whole city of Bologna crowded into the square before the town hall to hear a man preach. A student named Thomas stood in the crowd, and he did not flatter the preacher. The robe was dirty, the man looked like someone to despise, and his face was plain. He talked the way a man talks at a town meeting, and every word aimed at peace, until noble clans who had shed much blood in old feuds agreed to end them. Then men and women pushed through the crowd to touch his hem or tear off a scrap of his robe, because a scrap was nearly all anyone could take from him.

The opening and summary.txt must not tell the bishop's house, the leper, the wounds or the death, and must not preview the whole arc (v1 did).

**Thesis:** the man was Saint Francis, a rich cloth merchant's son. He founded the Friars Minor, who were to own "neither house, nor place, nor anything"; Honorius III confirmed their Rule on 29 Nov 1223 [OW-Q1 p.68; XW-X7]. By 1221 some 3,000 brothers met around him [XL-Jord. §13]. He set Saint Clare on her way, made the Greccio crib, and wrote the Canticle in Umbrian [XW-X5; WT-S1 I.84; OW-I1]. Answered in ch. 5–11 and 15.

**Ending:** short after-death part (ch. 14–15); canonization, feast, Orders and patrons come first and briefly. Closing image: February 2026, nearly 400,000 booked to file past his remains [XW-X9], a small man whose head, when the diggers found him in 1818, lay on a rough stone [XW-X8]; Jacques had written that his brothers had nowhere "to lay their heads" [XL-B1d]. One quiet line may follow: "And we wanted to have no more" [XW-X1 p.79]. What he offers a reader today, joy in having little, is felt in the story, not stated.

## 4. Chapters

1. **A Rich Young Man in Prison**: SC-01. Bridge: birth, illness, fields give no delight [WT-S1 I.3]. **Pull:** he plans to ride to war again.
2. **Saint Francis Turns Back From War**: SC-02. Ends on the bride.
3. **Saint Francis Kisses a Leper**: SC-03, SC-04. **Pull:** Pietro is about to learn where his cloth went.
4. **Saint Francis Gives Everything Back to His Father**: SC-05. Bridge: his brother's taunt; the beggar who blesses him in his father's place; the snow pit; San Damiano rebuilt stone by stone [WT-S2 21-24; WT-S1 I.16]. Ends on the bowl of scraps that tasted better than syrup [XL-2C §14].
5. **The First Brothers Join Saint Francis**: SC-06. Bridge: Innocent III approves by word; the Lateran vision [WT-S2 47-53]. **Pull:** a young noblewoman slips out to hear him [XW-X5 p.10].
6. **Saint Clare Leaves Home to Follow Saint Francis**: told short, from his side (her life tells it in full): his counsel, the torches, her hair cut before the altar, the half roll [XW-X5 p.10-17; XW-X6 I.8, XII.2-5]. Bridge: the birds; Hugolino stops him at Florence, 1217 [XL-Mir. LXV]. **Pull:** he sails for Damietta [XL-Jord. §8].
7. **Saint Francis Goes to the Sultan**: SC-07. **Pull:** "Go back, go back!" [XL-Jord. §10].
8. **Saint Francis Gives Up Leading His Order**: SC-08, SC-09. Bridge: the 1221 chapter and the tug on Elias's tunic; roof tiles and books [XL-Jord. §13-14; XL-2C §57, §62]. Ends on the 1223 Rule's "the Lord made himself poor for us".
9. **The First Christmas Manger at Greccio**: SC-10. **Pull:** the Passion was waiting on a mountain [WT-S1 I.84].
10. **Saint Francis Receives the Wounds of Christ**: SC-11. Bridge: hiding the wounds; the letter to Leo [XL-2C §135-137; XW-X1 p.116]. Ends on the man who had become a prayer.
11. **Saint Francis Writes the Canticle of the Sun**: SC-12. Bridge: "not cruel to yourself" [XL-Mir. XCI]; the snow figures [XL-2C §116-117]; "Let us begin, brothers," and his wish to serve lepers again [WT-S1 I.103]; the cautery [XL-2C §166]. Ends on "I know Christ, the poor man crucified."
12. **Saint Francis Makes Peace in Assisi**: SC-13. Before it: Siena, blood, the three-part will; home under guard [XL-Mir. LXXXVII, CXXI]. After it: "Welcome, my Sister Death!"; Leo and Angelo sing; he gives Leo his own tunic [XL-Mir. CXXII-CXXIII; XL-2C §50]. **Pull:** he asks to be carried down to the Portiuncula.
13. **The Death of Saint Francis**: SC-14. Ends on his death at nightfall; nothing after it.
14. **Saint Francis Is Declared a Saint**: SC-15. Bridge: Elias's letter; Gregory IX canonizes him; the basilica; the body hidden 1230 [XW-X2; CM-S1 §122-126]. Ends on the body passing out of sight.
15. **How Saint Francis Changed the Church**: Part 6 bridges. Ends on the closing image.

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points** (slow, from inside him): SC-03, SC-05, SC-08, SC-11, SC-12. **Climax:** SC-14, the answer to SC-05.

**Relationships and their ends:** Pietro: the curses and the beggar's blessing, ch. 4 (notes hold nothing later). Bernard: first brother, ch. 5; at the end, blind, Francis's hand falls on Giles: "This is not the head of my brother Bernard" (ch. 13) [XL-Mir. CVII]. Saint Clare: ch. 6; his message in the last week [XL-Mir. CVIII]; ends at the window, SC-15. Hugolino: Florence, protector, "not cruel to yourself"; ends weeping at the canonization, "a patron whose friendship We enjoyed on earth" [CM-S5 §8]. Leo: parchment, letter, song, tunic; laid near him in 1934 [XW-X8]. Elias: the tunic tug, the right hand on his head; ends with "we are orphans" and the basilica [XW-X2 p.149]. Jacoba: the last days; her casket joins him in 1934.

**Losses at their dates:** illness 1203; family c.1206; Damietta's dead 1219; eyes failing from 1220 [XL-Mir. XCI]; the Order 1220; Peter Cathanii dead March 1221 [WT-S1 p.xiv]; sixty days blind 1225 (SC-12, the concrete day); blood at Siena 1226.

### SC-01 — Prison, 1202–03 (Perugia)
- What happens: he jests in chains, says the whole world will honor him, and alone keeps company with the shunned knight.
- Why: the glory he wanted before he knew which glory.
- Have: [XL-2C §4; WT-S2 4]. Need: nothing.

### SC-02 — The road to Apulia, c.1205 (Spoleto, then Assisi)
- What happens: he gives his new clothes to a poor knight; at Spoleto the Lord asks "the servant or the lord?" and sends him home; crowned king of a feast, he lags behind in a sweetness that stops him; asked about a wife, he speaks of a nobler bride.
- Why: the first great decision; the desire in his words.
- Have: [XL-2C §6-7; WT-S2 6-7; WT-S1 I.7].
- Need: place and time of "my Lady". Where: Howell 2C §83-84.

### SC-03 — The leper, c.1205 (below Assisi) — TURNING POINT
- What happens: he dismounts, kisses the leper's hand, receives the kiss of peace; days later kisses each leper at the hospital.
- Why: grace turns bitter to sweet.
- Have: [WT-S2 11; WT-S1 I.17; XL-2C §9]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Testament [OW-Q1 p.77].

### SC-04 — The crucifix, c.1205–06 (San Damiano)
- What happens: "Francis, go and repair my house"; he weeps for days, sells cloth and horse, throws the coins on a sill.
- Why: God's first word, taken as stones.
- Have: [WT-S2 13, 16; XL-2C §10; WT-S1 I.8-9]. Need: nothing.

### SC-05 — Before the bishop, c.1206 (Assisi) — TURNING POINT
- What happens: chained, freed by his mother; before the bishop he strips: "From now on I can freely say 'Our Father'… I will go naked to the Lord"; the hair shirt; the bishop's cloak.
- Why: father, name and goods given up; the desire sealed.
- Have: [WT-S2 18-20; WT-S1 I.12-15, II.12].
- Need: any later word of Pietro or his mother. Where: 3C §23; Celano I.12-15.

### SC-06 — The Gospel and the book, 1208–09 (Portiuncula; San Nicolò)
- What happens: "This is what I want … from the very bottom of my heart"; with Bernard and Peter he opens the book three times: "this is our life and Rule".
- Why: the desire becomes a life, and he is no longer alone.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.22; WT-S2 27-30]. Need: nothing.

### SC-07 — The Sultan's camp, 1219 (Damietta)
- What happens: he foresees the defeat; seized on the road: "I am a Christian; take me to your lord"; the Sultan hears him for days and asks his prayers.
- Why: unarmed where the crusade went armed.
- Have: [XL-B1c-d; XW-X3; XL-2C §30; XL-Jord. §10]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Jacques de Vitry, Historia orientalis II.32.

### SC-08 — Giving up the Order, 1220 (Rome; Portiuncula) — TURNING POINT
- What happens: the black hen; he lies at the Pope's door and asks for "the Lord of Ostia"; at Michaelmas, "From now on I am dead to you", and he gives the family back to God. Later, ill: "Who are they who have snatched my Order?" God: it will stand even with three.
- Why: the hardest letting go; his temper shows the cost.
- Have: [XL-2C §24-25, §143, §158, §188; XL-Jord. §11]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Jordan at the Pope's door.

### SC-09 — The leper's dish, winter 1220–21 (Portiuncula)
- What happens: he scolds Brother James for bringing a leper out, blames himself, and eats from one dish with the leper's bleeding fingers.
- Why: a real fault, and the leper again.
- Have: [XL-Mir. LVIII]. Need: nothing.

### SC-10 — Christmas night, 1223 (Greccio)
- What happens: ox and donkey, torches, Mass over the manger; "Bethlehem" like a bleating sheep; a man sees him wake a child.
- Why: his life with God; the poor Child.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.84-87; WT-S5 X.7; XL-2C §199]. Need: nothing.

### SC-11 — The seraph, September 1224 (La Verna) — TURNING POINT
- What happens: the book opens three times at the Passion; the seraph; joy and grief together; the marks; for tempted Leo he writes the Praises and a blessing.
- Why: God's answer in his body; his prayer in his hand.
- Have: [WT-S5 XIII.1-5; WT-S1 I.94-95; XL-2C §49; OW-Q1 p.199]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: the Praises of God [XW-X1 p.124-125].

### SC-12 — The night of the mice, 1225 (San Damiano) — TURNING POINT
- What happens: blind sixty days, mice on his table, he pities himself and prays; "your sickness is the pledge of my kingdom"; at dawn he begins the Canticle and teaches it.
- Why: the long trial's concrete day; pain turns to praise.
- Have: [XL-Mir. C; XL-2C §212-213; OW-I1]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Celano II §213.

### SC-13 — The bishop and the podestà, summer 1226 (Assisi)
- What happens: "It is a great shame for us servants of God"; he adds the verse on forgiveness and sends two brothers to sing it; the two men embrace.
- Why: the peacemaker of Bologna makes peace from his deathbed.
- Have: [XL-Mir. CI]. Need: nothing.

### SC-14 — Death, evening of 3 October 1226 (Portiuncula) — CLIMAX
- What happens: blessings; Jacoba arrives; laid naked on the ground: "I have done my duty. May Christ teach you yours"; the guardian lends him clothes and he rejoices; bread; John 13; the psalm; larks; nightfall.
- Why: he goes naked to the Lord.
- Have: [XL-2C §214-217; WT-S1 I.108-110; XL-Mir. CVII, CXII-CXIII]. Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Celano II §214.

### SC-15 — The window and the burial, 4 October 1226 (San Damiano; San Giorgio)
- What happens: the procession with branches stops; the brothers hold his body at the sisters' window until Saint Clare and her sisters are comforted, as he promised; burial at San Giorgio; a girl bent for a year rises straight by the coffin.
- Why: Saint Clare's part ends; the first healing at his tomb.
- Have: [WT-S1 I.116-117; XL-Mir. CVIII; XL-Jord. §50; CM-S1 §127].
- Need: Celano's full words at the window, matched to the Clare biography (notes 42–43). Where: Howell 1C §116-117.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** Rules 1221, 1223 (ch. 8); Praises 1224 (ch. 10); Canticle c.1225 (ch. 11–12); Testament 1226; Last Will for the sisters, kept in Saint Clare's Rule 1253 (ch. 13, 15) [OW-Q1; XW-X1 p.76].
- **Changed:** Friars Minor; Poor Ladies 1212; Germany 1221; three branches and a Third Order; the "first Nativity scene"; the Portiuncula indulgence [XW-X7, X10; XL-Jord. §14] (ch. 6, 8, 9, 15).
- **Famous:** Elias's letter 1226; Celano's Life 1229; Jordan's relics c.1230 [XL-Jord. §59]; patron of Italy 1939, of ecology 1979; Pope Francis's name 2013; *Laudato si'*; Year of Saint Francis 2026 [CM-S6, S7, S10; XW-X14, X10] (ch. 15).
- **Path:** tomb healings from burial day; 1228 inquiry; canonized 16 July 1228; feast 4 Oct [CM-S1 §122-127] (ch. 14).

**Fact needs:**
1. Celano 1C §116-117 in full (the window), to keep both lives in step. Look: Howell 1908; Clare biography notes 42–43.
2. 2C §50 (Leo's tunic) and 2C §83-84 ("my Lady") in full. Look: Howell 1908.
3. Any later word of Pietro or Francis's mother. Look: 3C §23; Celano I.12-15. If none, close at the beggar's blessing.

## 8. What we leave out

- Wolf of Gubbio, Perfect Joy: Little Flowers, after 1322.
- Fire ordeal, Sultan's secret conversion: not in the eyewitness.
- Tomb miracle lists, stigmata doubters: miracles section.
- Devils in the tower: the snow figures show the temptation.

- Nicholas V's 1449 vision: legend.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Hugolino is Gregory IX (from the notes, not Celano).
- Died at nightfall, Saturday 3 Oct 1226; Elias counts it as Sunday 4 Oct.
- Translation 25 May 1230 (21/22 May in other sources).
- The bishop of 1206 and of 1226 may not be one man: write "the bishop of Assisi"; name Guido only in Rome [WT-S1 I.32].
- San Damiano voice is absent from Celano I: "the early Lives tell". "The Sultan of Egypt."
- Mirror scenes (SC-09, SC-12, SC-13, Bernard, Jacoba, larks, the message to Clare): "his companions remembered".
- The order of the cautery and the San Damiano night is unsure; do not join them with "after".
- The Clare biography calls the San Damiano cell "straw mats"; the Mirror says "wattles". Use one word in both lives.
- The Peace Prayer is not his (first printed 1912).
