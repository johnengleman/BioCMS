## Part A. Celano, Second Life (E1), read through 2C II §61 (Part 1 and early Part 2). Grades: A = Celano writes from first-hand or named-witness report; B = Celano reports, no named witness; C = moral tale with Celano's own message.

Note on source: Celano says the Second Life holds events "not put into the legends composed some time ago ... because they had not come to the author's notice", and that he writes from "constant association" of the brethren with Francis (2C Preface §1-2). It was commissioned by the Minister-General Crescentius (1244-47).

### Scenes and character (2C)
- 2C §4: Perugia prison. Fellow-captives "deemed him mad and demented". Francis: "Another thought is with me : I shall yet be worshipped as a Saint all the world over." He endures the proud knight and gets the others to make peace with him. (Already in pass-one notes; the exact words are here.)
- 2C §6: Second vision on the road to Apulia: "who could do better for him, the servant or the lord?" "The lord," said Francis. "Why, then, art thou seeking the servant instead of the lord?" Told his friends he was going to be "a great prince". Grade B. NEW detail: the "servant or lord" dialogue.
- 2C §7: Master of the revels. A band of Assisi young men made him chief of their feasts because they knew he would pay. He walked behind them with a staff, then fell behind; "as he himself related" he was filled with such sweetness that he "became speechless and was quite unable to move from the spot". Grade A (Francis himself is the source).
- 2C §8: In Rome he swaps clothes with a beggar and eats with the poor at St Peter's door, "ashamed of doing it before those who knew him". He throws a handful of coins at Peter's altar because offerings were so small. Grade B.
- 2C §9: First leper. He dismounts and kisses him, giving money. On remounting he looks round: no leper in an open field. Grade B (Celano's second version; the Testament is Francis's own).
- 2C §10: The crucifix spoke "moved its lips". After this he could not stop weeping: "He filled the ways with groanings". He told one close friend, who wept too. Grade B.
- 2C §12: Disowning scene: "Henceforth ... I may freely say 'our Father who art in heaven'". The brother of Francis taunts him in the cold: "Ask Francis to sell you now a pennyworth of sweat". Francis smiles: "Indeed I will sell my sweat very dear to my Lord". Sackcloth found beneath his clothes. (Both lines already in witnesses notes.)
- 2C §13: He is ashamed to beg oil at a house where men are gambling; he turns back, confesses the shame aloud, begs "in French", and prophesies in French that a monastery of virgins will be at San Damiano. Celano: "whenever he was filled with the ardour of the Holy Spirit he would utter ardent words in French". NEW/strong. Grade B.
- 2C §14: First begging. The priest of San Damiano feeds him; Francis argues with himself ("Thou wilt not find this priest everywhere") and begs scraps door to door; sees the bowl of mixed scraps, "filled with loathing", then eats "with delight of spirit". Grade B.
- 2C §15: Bernard asks what to do with another's goods "given back to his lord"; the Gospel opened three times at church. (Already in notes.)
- 2C §21-22 Discretion at night. A brother cries out "I am dying of hunger". Francis gets up, has the table laid, eats first so the brother is not shamed, then teaches discretion. Quote: "Know, dear brethren, that what I have done in eating now, I have done by dispensation, not by inclination, at the bidding of brotherly charity. Let the charity be your example, not the food; for food ministers to gluttony, but charity to the spirit." Early brothers wore iron hoops and mail until he told them to ease. Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §23-24 The black hen. Before going to Rome he dreams of a small black hen with feathered legs and countless chicks she cannot cover. His reading: "I am this hen, small in stature and black by nature". The chicks are the brothers; he will entrust them to the Roman Church. Grade B. NEW and vivid. Francis's own interpretation.
- 2C §25 (Honorius, c.1220): He preaches before Pope and cardinals; then asks for Hugolino: "Wherefore ... I beg of your Holiness' compassion that this Lord of Ostia may be granted us for a Pope". Grade B. Date: Howell refers to 1 Cel I.73; the pope in this scene is Honorius III (2C text).
- 2C §28: A brother thought thrice holy, who confesses only by signs. Francis: "Give it up, brethren, and don't praise the Devil's counterfeits in him to me!" Test: make him confess weekly; he refuses by finger to mouth; later leaves the Order. Grade B. NEW. Pairs with the Mirror (Spec. Perf. 102 per Howell).
- 2C §30 DAMIETTA, 29 Aug 1219 (Howell footnote): Francis in the camp tells his companion that if the Christians fight on a certain day they will lose. "If I tell them this I shall be deemed a fool, while if I keep silence I shall not elude my conscience." Companion: "thou art not now first beginning to be deemed a fool". Army ignores him, loses; Celano says "6,000 were killed and captured" (Howell's note), and Francis mourns "specially" the Spaniards. Grade B; matches Jacques de Vitry and Bonaventure on the battle (check). NEW as a scene.
- 2C §31 Brother Leonard of Assisi on the road back from over the sea. Francis rides an ass; Leonard on foot thinks "This man's parents and mine were not wont to play together as equals." Francis dismounts: "it is not meet that I should ride and thou be walking, because in the world thou wast nobler and mightier than I". Leonard weeps and confesses. Grade A/B (Leonard as witness). NEW. Great character scene.
- 2C §32-34: Devil-sight. Brother who left the brothers for solitude; at Siena Francis shuts himself in a cell to pray for him on seeing him come back: "I saw in my son that which gave [me] just displeasure". Another brother who obeys another superior: Francis "saw a devil on the disobedient brother's back". Grade B.
- 2C §35-36 GRECCIO wolves and hail. Francis offers a bargain to the people: confess and the wolves and hail stop; be ungrateful and the plague returns "doubled". The harm stops, hail passes round their borders; later they fall back, pestilence and fire destroy the fortress. LEGEND-grade for the later destruction; Celano moralizes. Grade B/C.
- 2C §37 PERUGIA. He says "The men of Perugia have done much evil to their neighbours"; goes and preaches; knights ride in with arms. "But hear what the Lord announces to you by me, the little poor man." Civil war follows days later. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §38 Lady of Volusiano (near Cella di Cortona): her husband resists her wish to serve Christ. Francis: "now is the time of salvation, and afterwards that of justice". Husband converts, "Lady, let us serve God and save our souls in our own house"; both died on the same day. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §39-40: He knows a lad of Lucca weeps falsely: "Unhappy and carnal boy, why dost thou trust in being able to lie to the Holy Spirit and to me?" Parents arrive and the boy goes home. And a brother lies "from false humility": "Beware, brother, of telling a lie from false humility". Grade B.
- 2C §41 RIETI (during his last illness, bishop's palace): canon Gideon, dissolute, begs the sign of the cross. Francis: "Since thou hast lived in the past according to the desires of the flesh ... how shall I sign thee with the Cross?" Then does; Gideon's loins "made a noise like that of dry pieces of wood". Healed; later killed when a roof falls on him. Miracle, grade B, legend-flavoured moral. NEW.
- 2C §42 Nail parings. A tempted brother of Marsica asks for Francis's nail parings. A companion says Francis orders them thrown away. Francis calls the brother and says "Find me scissors, my son, to cut my nails with at once!" and gives the parings; temptation ends. Grade B. NEW (and an early relic story).
- 2C §43 Cloth for six tunics: Francis asks his warden for cloth for a tunic, a man at the gate offers cloth for six. "Thanks be to Him who seems to take thought for us alone!" Grade B.
- 2C §44 Hermitage at Rieti: the eye doctor comes daily. Francis orders: "Invite the doctor, and give him a very good dinner". The brothers are ashamed: they have little bread, little wine. Doctor: "I shall take your penury for delicacies". A woman knocks with a basket of "fine bread, fish and pasties of crayfish ... honey and grapes". Doctor: "neither do ye know this man's holiness as ye ought, nor do we of the world know it". Miracle (provision), grade A/B (the doctor as witness). NEW.
- 2C §45 Greccio: two brothers come from afar for a blessing and leave sad. Francis, in his cell all day, cries after them from a stone's cast away, and blesses them. Grade B.
- 2C §46 Water from a rock. Francis borrows a poor man's ass; the peasant collapses with thirst on the mountain. Francis kneels; "yonder thou wilt find water which Christ in mercy has brought to thee at this hour out of the stone to drink." Celano says "Water had never flowed there before, nor could any be found afterwards, as diligent search has proved." Miracle, grade B. (Bonaventure tells it as Francis at Monte Casale? not verified here.)
- 2C §47 Redbreasts. A pair of birds feed at table and give their chicks to the brothers: Francis says "Look at what our brothers the redbreasts have done". One greedy chick bullies the others; Francis: "he will come to a bad end yet!" It falls in a water vessel and drowns. Grade B/C. NEW.
- 2C §49-50 LA VERNA / Leo. A tempted brother (Howell's note: Leo) wants a writing; Francis calls for "paper and ink, for I will write down words of God and His praises"; writes praises and a blessing; "keep it diligently till the day of thy death". Leo's tunic: when Francis lies sick at the bishop's palace in Assisi he gives Leo his tunic: "I make over to thee this tunic". Both: Francis answers a thought before it is spoken. Grade A (Leo is the source behind the book). Matches Chartula in own-words notes.
- 2C §51 Parsley at night, last illness: cook says he cannot find parsley in the dark. Francis: "bring the first herbs thou touchest". Among wild herbs they find a leaf of parsley. Francis: "do not allege that anything is impossible, for even were I to bid you do anything beyond your strength, obedience would not be wanting in strength." Grade B.
- 2C §52-53 FAMINE. Francis tells a brother: "To-day there is on the earth a servant of God for whose sake as long as he shall live God will not allow famine to rage against men." After his death he appears to the same brother: "Brother, now is coming the famine which, so long as I was alive, God did not suffer to come on the earth." Appeared a second time three nights later. Grade B, Celano says "all we who were witnesses" of the later famine. NEW.
- 2C §55-60 Poverty: "rich in a tunic only, a cord, and drawers". Houses of wood, small. Portiuncula (2C §57): the Assisi people build a house for the chapter while he is away; he climbs onto the roof "and tore down the slates and tiles"; stopped only because knights say the house belongs to the town. Bologna (2C §58): hearing "a house of the brethren" he turns aside and orders the brothers out, "even the sick"; Hugolino says the house is his. "He who was then turned out of the house sick bears witness to these things and writes them down." Grade A (named eyewitness). Sartiano (2C §59): hears "From brother Francis' cell" and leaves: "Since thou hast put Francis' name to the cell, appropriating it to me, look out for another inhabitant for it, for I will not stay in it any more." Strong NEW.

### 2C §61-104 (poverty, alms, prayer, Scripture)
- 2C §61 EASTER at Greccio: brothers lay the table with white linen and glass. Francis sees it, slips out, takes a poor man's hat and staff, and cries at the door "For the love of the Lord God give alms to this poor sick pilgrim". Brothers invite him in; he sits on the ground "and made the ashes his table, saying, 'Now I am sitting like a Lesser Brother'". Then: "I saw a table spread and adorned, and knew that it was not the table of poor men who go from door to door." Grade B (Celano marks it an "ensample"). NEW (popular pages do not have it). Compare the Perugia Legend/Mirror version (to check).
- 2C §62 Books. A Minister asks to keep valuable books: "I am not going to lose the book of the Gospel, which I have promised to obey, for the sake of thy books. Do however what thou wilt : my permission shall not be made a snare." Grade B.
- 2C §63 Hugolino at Portiuncula chapter sees the brothers' beds, "which you might take for the lairs of wild beasts", and weeps: "Look ... this is where the brethren sleep!" Grade B.
- 2C §64 Pillow. At Greccio, eyes bad, he was made to use a small pillow. Night: "my head trembles, my knees sink under me ... just as if I had eaten bread made of darnel. I believe the Devil is in this pillow". The companion who carries it out loses speech and is rooted until Francis calls him. Francis: "Very crafty and subtle is our foe, for, when he cannot hurt us in the soul within, at least he gives the body matter for murmuring." Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §65 Money. "Let dung and money be loved and valued alike". A brother had moved a layman's coin with his hand; Francis orders him to carry it in his mouth and lay it on asses' dung. Grade B.
- 2C §66 Two brothers find coins near a lepers' hospital; one takes them and loses speech. Grade B/C.
- 2C §67 Peter Cathanii asks to keep novices' property. Francis: "Strip the Virgin's altar ... she will be better pleased to have her Son's Gospel observed and her altar stript". Grade B.
- 2C §68 Near Bari, the purse of coins: Francis says it is a devil's trick; prays a stone's cast away; the purse then holds a snake. "To God's servants, brother, money is nothing else but a devil and a venomous snake". Grade B/C (wonder). NEW.
- 2C §69-70 Clothing and poverty. He allowed two tunics, patched; sewed sackcloth over his own tunic; asked that his burial tunic be covered with sackcloth (Celano). Prophecy: "Rigour shall yet be so relaxed, lukewarmness shall yet so prevail that the sons of the poor father will not be at all ashamed to wear even scarlet array, changing the colour only". "O that it might come to pass that the world, beholding the Lesser Brethren very rarely, should wonder at their fewness!" Grade B. NEW and strong.
- 2C §72 Dining with great lords, he first begs scraps, then sits to meat: he would not give up "an enduring inheritance for a fief granted for an hour". 2C §73 With Hugolino (then bishop of Ostia), dinner hour: he goes begging, lays scraps of black bread on the table. Hugolino embraces him: "My brother, why hast thou put me to shame in the house which is thine and thy brethren's, by going for alms?" Francis: "I have rather shown you honour ... For I have a royal dignity and a preeminent nobility". Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §75 "Go thy way, brother fly, for thou wouldst eat the sweat of thy brethren and be idle in God's work. Thou art like brother drone." Grade B. 2C §76: a brother returning with alms sings aloud; Francis runs out, kisses his shoulder, takes the wallet: "Blessed be my brother who goes readily, seeks humbly, and returns rejoicing". NEW.
- 2C §77 NOCERA, last illness: Assisi sends formal messengers (knights) "to demand delivery of his person" so that they do not "give their glory to another". On the way home at the poor village of Satriano the knights find nothing to buy; Francis: "You find not because you trust more in your flies than in God. (Now he called pieces of money 'flies'.)" They beg and get more than money would buy. Grade B. NEW, important for the death-chapter. (Satriano per OCR; check modern name.)
- 2C §78-79 Alexandria (Lombardy): host serves a capon; a false beggar keeps Francis's piece to shame him; next morning he waves it in the crowd and it appears to all to be a fish. Grade B/C legend. NEW.
- 2C §81 "Go thy way, brother fly, for thou hast not yet come out from thy house and kindred." Man in the March of Ancona gave goods to kin, not the poor.
- 2C §82 Vision of the Lady: head gold, arms silver, belly crystal, feet iron, "covered by a foul mantle". Pacifico's reading: Francis's soul, the mantle the body. Celano: Francis "refused to expound it". Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §83-84 He envies a naked poor man: "This man's want does us great shame, and severely rebukes our poverty ... I chose Poverty for my riches, and for my Lady, and see! she shines more brightly in yonder man." Grade B.
- 2C §85 Companion says a poor man may be rich in will. Francis makes him strip his tunic, kneel at the poor man's feet and ask his prayers: "Whenever thou seest a poor man, brother, a mirror of the Lord and of His poor Mother is set before thee."
- 2C §86 CELANO (town), winter: gives a borrowed cloth to an old woman; she cuts it up and returns because it is too short; Francis tells his companion, "Let us bear the cold for the love of God, and do thou give her cloth to finish her gown with." Both are left bare. 2C §87-92: more cloaks given away (Siena road; Cella di Cortona, where the brothers redeem the cloak; Colle, to make a man forgive his lord; hem of tunic, drawers; Rieti, to a woman with eye disease, with twelve loaves). Grade B. Mark the Colle one for dialogue: "Brother, forgive thy lord for the love of God".
- 2C §91 The first New Testament in the Order given away to a brothers' poor mother: "Give our mother the New Testament ... I believe that the gift of it will be more pleasing than the reading out of it." Grade B. NEW and striking.
- 2C §93 THREE POOR WOMEN, plain near Rocca di Campiglio, going from Rieti to Siena for his eyes with a physician: "Welcome, Lady Poverty!" They vanish. Celano: "a wondrous thing, of doubtful interpretation, but most certain truth". Grade A/B (physician and brothers saw). NEW.
- 2C §94-99 Prayer. Celano says "We relate, as we have seen with our eyes". Francis makes a cell of his cloak or hides his face in his sleeve; "the whole man, not so much praying, as having become a [living] prayer". Standing in the rain to say the Hours. Burns a bowl he was carving in Lent because it distracted his Tierce: "Alas for the fruitless work which has had the power to turn away my mind to it!" Grade A (Celano and brothers heard it). NEW.
- 2C §98 Borgo San Sepolcro: riding an ass to a lepers' house, crowds pull his habit and cut pieces from his tunic; he does not notice and later asks "when they would be getting near Borgo?" Grade A/B. NEW and vivid.
- 2C §99: noise at bedtime so that others know he is going to bed, while he rose to pray secretly. And on consolation: "I restore it to Thee that Thou mayest keep it for me, for that I am a robber of Thy treasure".
- 2C §100 The Bishop of Assisi (Guido) walks into Francis's cell at the Portiuncula unannounced: trembling, speechless, forced backward; speech comes back when he starts to confess his fault. 2C §101 Abbot of San Giustino (Perugia) asks his prayer; Francis says "Wait a little, brother, for I wish to pay the debt which I have promised to discharge"; the abbot feels warmth a distance away. Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §102-104 Scripture. "Memory supplied the place of books". "not to wander through a thousand treatises". Siena: a Dominican doctor asks about Ezekiel ("If thou proclaim not to the wicked man his wickedness"); Francis, saying he is unlearned: the servant of God "ought in life and holiness so to burn within himself as to reprove all the wicked by the light of his example". The doctor: "this man's theology, based on purity and contemplation, is a soaring eagle, while our learning crawls with its belly on the ground". A cardinal in Rome: "I do not question thee as being a lettered man, but as one who has the Spirit of God". Grade A/B. NEW.

### 2C §105-124 (Scripture, Pacifico, Sylvester, women, temptation)
- 2C §105 A companion urges him to have the Prophets read during illness. Francis: "It is good to read the testimonies of Scripture ... but for myself, I have already mastered so much of the Scriptures that I have an ample store for meditation and reflection. I need no more, my son : I know Christ, the poor man crucified." Grade A/B. NEW; ideal quotation.
- 2C §106 PACIFICO, the "King of Verses" (crowned by the Emperor), meets Francis at a convent of poor nuns in the March of Ancona; sees him "signed as with a cross with two glittering swords". Converts: "What need is there for more words? Let us come to deeds. Take me away from men, and restore me to the great Emperor." Next day Francis vests him and names him Pacifico, brought back to peace. Later Pacifico sees a Tau on his forehead "which with variegated circles displayed the beauty of the peacock". Grade A/B (Pacifico's own account). NEW.
- 2C §107 Preaching: "mingling ardent gestures and movements with his words". A natural philosopher: "Whereas I can remember every word of the sermons of others, the words uttered by holy Francis alone escape me". Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §108 AREZZO in civil war: Francis, lodged outside the city, sees demons over it, sends Sylvester to the gate: "On behalf of God, and by order of our father Francis, depart far hence, all ye devils!" Peace returns. Francis opens his sermon: "whom however now I know to have been set free by the prayers of a certain poor man". Grade B. NEW (not in pass-one notes if absent: check).
- 2C §109 Sylvester's conversion: the priest who sold stones to Francis, jealous of Bernard's giveaway, complains the price was unpaid; Francis "filled the priest's hands with uncounted money". Sylvester sees a golden cross issuing from Francis's mouth reaching heaven with arms around the world. Grade B. (Pass-one notes may hold it from Bonaventure.)
- 2C §111 SAN VEREcONDO near Gubbio: a sow kills a newborn lamb; Francis: "Alas, brother lamb, innocent creature, who representest the everlasting advantage of mankind! Cursed be the pitiless one that slew thee, and let no man nor beast eat of her!" The sow sickens and dies in three days and lies "dried up like a board" in the ditch. Grade B/C; NEW (the harsher animal story, not the wolf of Gubbio).
- 2C §112-114 Women. He spoke loudly so that all could hear; "there are two women only whose faces I should recognise". Parable of the king's two messengers (eyes in the head). At Bevagna, weak from fasting, a woman and her consecrated daughter bring bread and wine; he preaches but looks at neither. Companion asks why: "Who should not fear to look upon a bride of Christ?" Grade B. NEW, but see conflicts: Clare/Jacoba stories show a gentler side (flag for the writer).
- 2C §115 Two-year temptation. At the Portiuncula he hears "if thou wilt have faith as a grain of mustard-seed ..."; "The mountain is thy temptation". Gone at once. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §116-117 SARTIANO, night: the devil calls "Francis, Francis, Francis" and says those who kill themselves by hard penance find no mercy. Then a temptation of lust: he scourges himself with his cord: "There! brother ass, thus must thou stay, thus endure the whip! The tunic belongs to Religion! it is not lawful to steal it : if thou wilt go any whither, go!" Then he plunges naked in snow and makes seven snow balls: "this large one is thy wife, those four are thy two sons and two daughters, the two others are the manservant and maidservant ... make haste and clothe them all, for they are dying of cold!" A brother, praying, saw it by the moonlight; Francis, distressed, "bade him reveal the thing to no one as long as he should live". Grade A (a named witness; told after Francis's death). NEW; note: the Fioretti/Bonaventure version has roses (check Bonaventure).
- 2C §118 "no one should deem himself a servant of God until he has passed through temptations and tribulations. Temptation conquered ... is in some sort the ring whereby the Lord espouses to Himself the soul of His servant."
- 2C §119-120 BEATEN BY DEVILS. Cardinal Leo of Santa Croce lodges him in a secluded tower with nine vaulted chambers. At night the devils beat him "as if half dead"; he calls his companion: "Brother, I would have thee stay by me, for I fear to be alone". He trembles "like one who is suffering from a severe fever". Next morning he thanks the cardinal and leaves: "it better for him who is set as an example to fly from Courts". Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §122-123 Alone in a remote church, devils rush over the roof; he says "do to my body whatsoever you have been allowed to do ... having no greater enemy than the body". Next dawn his companion, praying, sees a throne of the fallen angel kept "for humble Francis". Francis: "I think myself the greatest of sinners, for if God had followed any other wicked man with such great mercy he would have been tenfold more spiritual than I." Grade A/B. NEW; direct line.
- 2C §124 A scrupulous brother is told: "say the Lord's Prayer seven times on my authority". Grade B.

### 2C §125-149 (joy, stigmata hiding, humility)
- 2C §125 Joy: "The Devil most exults when he can filch from a servant of God his joy of spirit"; "Devils cannot hurt Christ's servant when they see him filled with holy mirth". Grade B. NEW line.
- 2C §126 THE ANGELIC LUTE, Rieti. He asks a brother who had played the lute in the world to borrow one secretly to soothe "brother body"; the brother is ashamed of what people will say: "Let us give it up, then, brother ... It is good to give up many things to avoid shocking the opinion of others." That night a lute is heard moving about the room, no player seen. Next morning: "although I could not hear lutes of men, I have heard a lute sweeter than theirs." Grade B (brother as witness). NEW. (Not in popular English pages; check against Bonaventure Minor Legend / Mirror for dating.)
- 2C §127 Singing in French. Celano: "as we have seen with our eyes", he picks up a stick, lays it over his left arm and draws a bow "as if across a viol", singing in French about the Lord; ends in tears. Grade A. NEW and essential character image.
- 2C §128 A sad-faced companion is told: "Examine thine offences in thy chamber and weep and groan before thy God there. When thou returnest to the brethren put gloom aside and be like the rest." Chapter admonition written down: "Let the brethren beware of showing themselves outwardly sullen and gloomy hypocrites, but let them show themselves rejoicing in the Lord, merry, and joyful, and gracious, as is meet". Grade A.
- 2C §129 "Brother body" must be fed with discretion; Celano confesses "In this teaching alone did the most holy father's deeds not correspond with his words", because he scourged his innocent body.
- 2C §130 FOX SKIN: the warden wants a fox skin sewn under his tunic for the spleen and stomach. Francis: "have a piece of the same size sewn on outside, which may point out to men the skin hidden inside". Done: two patches. Grade A (warden). NEW.
- 2C §131 POGGIO (Bustone), about Christmas, to a crowd: "You believe me a holy man, and that is why you have come devoutly hither. But I confess to you that during all this fast I have been eating food seasoned with lard." Grade A/B. NEW; great Francis humour/honesty.
- 2C §132 Assisi, old woman, he gives his cloak and then confesses aloud the vainglory he felt. 2C §133: "I may have sons and daughters yet: praise not one who is [not] secure." To himself: "If the Most High had given as much to a robber, he would be more thankful, Francis, than thou art".
- 2C §135-136 HIDING THE STIGMATA. A companion sees the foot wounds: "What is this, good brother?" Francis: "Mind your own business." Companion shakes out the tunic, sees blood: "What manner of blood...?" Francis puts his finger to his eye: "Ask what that is, if you don't know it to be an eye". He rarely washed hands or feet, wore wool socks with skin over the wounds, gave only his sleeve to be kissed. Brothers turned their eyes away. Grade A (companions). NEW (the exact retorts).
- 2C §137 Brother from Brescia at Siena; Pacifico kisses Francis's hands and winks. Francis calls Pacifico back: "God pardon thee, brother, for thou givest me much distress sometimes". Pacifico: "What distress have I given thee, dearest mother?" (They called Francis "mother".) Francis says nothing. Grade A. NEW. 2C §138: only one brother saw the side wound alive, by asking "Shall we shake out thy tunic, father?"; Francis: "God reward thee, brother, for I want it to be done." (Different from 1 Cel I.95 and Bonaventure's Elias; Howell notes a "mean trick".)
- 2C §140 Humility: "among the 'lesser' he was the least." Preferred others' opinions. 2C §141 Terni: the bishop praises him as "poor and despised, simple and unlettered"; Francis falls at his feet: "thou alone hast kept them unharmed" (the things that are mine). 2C §142 A peasant: "Strive to be as good as all men say thou art; for many put their trust in thee"; Francis dismounts and kisses the peasant's feet. Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §143 Michaelmas 1220 (Howell's note): resigns as superior in chapter: "Henceforth I am dead to you; but here ... is brother Peter Cathanii, and let you and me all obey him." Prayer: "O Lord, I commend to Thee the family that Thou hast hitherto committed to me". 2C §144 Resigns his companions: "I have seen before now a blind man with a little dog only to show him the way". Grade A.
- 2C §145 His picture of a Lesser Brother: expelled by the chapter as "uneloquent, simple and ignorant", and hearing it "with unchanged looks, with unchanged gladness of mind". Grade B.
- 2C §147 IMOLA: bishop refuses licence ("it is enough for me to preach to my people"). Francis goes out, returns: "My lord, when a father has driven a son out of one door he must come in again by another". Bishop gives general licence. Grade B. NEW (famous in Franciscan lore, rare in popular pages).
- 2C §148 DOMINIC and Francis with Hugolino in Rome: the cardinal asks why not make bishops of the brothers. Each tries to make the other speak first. Dominic answers first. Francis: "My lord, my brethren have been styled Lesser that they presume not to become greater." Grade B. NEW.

### 2C §150-170 (Dominic, obedience, creatures)
- 2C §150 DOMINIC asks for Francis's cord; Francis is reluctant; Dominic girds it beneath his inner tunic. They clasp hands: "I would, brother Francis, that thy Religion and mine might be one". Dominic to bystanders: "the other Religious ought to follow this holy man Francis". Grade B. NEW (the cord exchange).
- 2C §151 Francis asks for a warden: "I would obey a novice of an hour's standing, were he assigned to me as warden". §152 "Take a corpse, and put it where you please, you will see that it does not resist being moved ... If it be placed on a throne it will look, not above, but beneath". Grade A/B. NEW; (the corpse image is also in Admonitions/Mirror; check in own-words notes).
- 2C §154 Hood in the fire: a brother who came alone without an "obedience" has his hood thrown in a large fire; no one dares rescue it; Francis tells them to pull it out, "uninjured". Grade B/C. NEW and odd (flag as wonder with a harsh edge).
- 2C §155 Brother Barbaro in Cyprus chews asses' dung after an angry word to a brother; a Cypriot knight is converted. Grade B (reported from distant brothers).
- 2C §156-157 FONDI: the bishop rebukes two brothers for overlong beards; Francis weeps and curses those whose bad example destroys the Order: "let them be cursed by Thee, most holy Lord, by all the Court of Heaven, and by me, thy little servant." "they are piercing me with a cruel sword and are plunging it into my bowels all day long." Prophecy: "The time will come when through evil examples the Religion beloved of God shall be so ill spoken of that it shall be ashamed to show itself in public." Grade B. NEW; adds shadow (Celano wrote after the Order's quarrels).
- 2C §158 God's reply: "Why, thou mannikin, art thou distressed? ... even though the Religion were reduced to the number of three, it shall ever, through My gift, remain unshaken." Grade B (Francis told it).
- 2C §159 Siena at night, he wakes his companions: "I asked the Lord that He would deign to show me when I am His servant and when not ... 'Know that thou art then truly My servant when thou thinkest, speakest, and doest holy things'." Grade A (companions woken). NEW.
- 2C §160 Idle words at the Portiuncula: say a Lord's Prayer for each. §161 "I desire that all my brethren work and be employed, and that those that know no craft, learn". 
- 2C §163 Preaching: "We ought to honour and revere all theologians"; letter to Anthony begins "To brother Antony, my Bishop". Grade A (the letter formula is documented). §164: "Why do ye boast of men converted, when it is my simple brethren that have converted them by their prayers?"
- 2C §165 CREATURES (Celano's long paragraph, quotation-ready): he refused to put out lanterns, lamps, candles; walked reverently over rocks and said "Beneath [my] feet hast Thou exalted me"; forbade cutting down a whole tree "so that it might have hope of sprouting again"; a plot of sweet flowers in the garden; "He picked up worms from the way that they might not be trodden on, and ordered honey and the best wine to be provided for bees". Called all animals brother. Grade A (Celano: general habit). NEW (several details not in popular pages).
- 2C §166 THE CAUTERY (Rieti or similar; date not given): "My brother fire ... Be kind to me at this hour, be courteous, for I have loved thee of old in the Lord." Surgeon cauterizes "from the ear to the eyebrow"; brothers flee; Francis smiles: "Faint-hearted and poor-spirited ones, wherefore did ye fly? ... I felt no heat of fire nor any pain in my flesh." "If the flesh is not well burnt ... apply the iron again". Doctor: "I have seen wondrous things to-day". Grade A (eyewitness doctor and brothers). Already known in outline from 1 Cel I.101 (pass one); here the exact words are new.
- 2C §167 Waterfowl on the Lake of Rieti: fisherman gives him a bird; Francis opens his hands; it stays "as in a little nest" while he prays long; then freed. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §168 FALCON at a hermitage wakes him for matins, but rings "his vocal bell about dawn" when he is ill. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §169 Bees fill his drinking vessel in a cell left after a forty-day fast. Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §170 PHEASANT sent by a nobleman of Siena: "Praised be our Creator, brother pheasant!" Brother carries it away to a vineyard; it comes straight back to his cell (story continues).

### 2C §170-191 (creatures, charity, Ministers, John the Simple)
- 2C §170 Pheasant: it keeps coming back to Francis's cell; a devoted physician takes it home and it will not eat until returned: "As soon as the bird was put down on the ground and saw its father, it put away its sadness and began to eat joyfully." Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §171 CICALA on a fig tree near the Portiuncula cell: "My sister cicala, come to me" ... "Sing, my sister cicala, and praise the Lord thy Creator with a joyful song". She sings until Francis joins; stays eight days. Then: "Let us give our sister cicala leave to depart ... that our flesh may not have occasion for vainglory by such things"; she never returns. Grade B. NEW; lovely.
- 2C §173 Answer to critics of his hard life: he was given to the Order "for a pattern, so that like an eagle he might incite his young ones to fly". Grade B.
- 2C §175 Sick care: eats on fast-days so sick brothers are not ashamed; begs meat in the streets for a sick brother; §176 takes a sick man to a vineyard and eats grapes first to encourage him. Quote from a Rule: "I beg all my sick brethren not to be angry or annoyed in their sicknesses". Grade A/B. NEW (vineyard scene; check whether 1221 Rule text is the source).
- 2C §177 "it is the proper function of a Superior who is a father and not a tyrant to forestall occasions of delinquency".
- 2C §178 A Spanish clerk reports a brother in ecstasy with candlesticks lit at head and feet; Francis, "as if his only glory were to hear good of the brethren", thanks the Lord. Grade C (second-hand report).
- 2C §180 "I will ... that my brethren show themselves sons of the same mother ... Let them share their books and all their pleasant things; nay, rather, let one compel another to take them." §181 Two French brothers ask for his tunic; he gives it, "remaining naked", takes the poorer of theirs. Grade B.
- 2C §182 Slander: Francis "turned away his ears" (Celano: "as we ourselves have seen"). To Peter Cathanii: "Hand him over to the Florentine bruiser if thou canst not punish him thyself." (John of Florence, tall and strong.) Strong humor. Grade A. NEW.
- 2C §184-186 THE IDEAL MINISTER-GENERAL, asked "near the end" by a brother: "My son, I see no one sufficient to be leader of an army so manifold ... But I will paint for you, and (as the saying is) fashion with my hand, one in whom it may be clearly seen what manner of man the father of this family should be." List: no private affections; early prayer; "ready to give answer to all"; execrate money; "his habit and a little book ... a box of pens and a seal"; "Let him not be a collector of books"; if he needs better food, eat it in public. Grade A (the questioner is a brother; known from Mirror too). Great for a "last year" chapter.
- 2C §188 Questioned why he gave the brothers to others: "if they followed my footsteps I should assuredly love them more". Then, ill, he raises himself on his couch: "Who are they that have snatched the Religion of myself and the brethren out of my hands? If I get to the chapter-general then I will show them what my will is!" Brother: "Wilt thou not also change those Provincial Ministers?" Francis, groaning: "Let them live as they like, for the perdition of a few is a lesser loss than that of many". Grade A (conflict with Elias/ministers). NEW. This is anger and weariness, a human note.
- 2C §189 Simplicity: "Hail, Queen Wisdom! The Lord save thee with thy sister pure holy Simplicity!" (Praise of the Virtues; own-words notes).
- 2C §190 BROTHER JOHN THE SIMPLE: a ploughman asks to join; Francis says give to the poor first; John unyokes the oxen and offers one: "Let us give this ox to the poor, for I am worthy to receive so much of my father's goods." The parents weep for the ox; Francis: "see, I give you back the ox though I take away the brother!" John copies every gesture: "if S. Francis spat, he spat; if S. Francis coughed, he coughed". Asked why: "I have promised ... to do all that thou doest; it is dangerous for me to leave anything out." Francis called him "Saint John". Grade A/B. NEW; funny and tender.

### 2C §191-211 (unity, learning, devotions, Poor Ladies, illness)
- 2C §191 Parable of the wise and the simple preacher at a chapter-general (wise man preaches in sackcloth and ashes; the simple one, then, preaches on a psalm: "God discourses with the simple ones"). Grade B.
- 2C §193 Barber: "Take care not to make me a large tonsure. For I wish that my simple brethren may have a share in my head." Grade A. NEW, funny and humble. He wanted to put "the Holy Ghost, the Minister-General of the Religion, rests equally on the poor and simple" in the Rule but the bull (29 Nov 1223, Howell's note) precluded it.
- 2C §194-195 On learning: "Learning ... makes many men indocile". "tribulation is indeed coming ... wherein books shall be thrown into cupboards and hiding-places as useless." A lay brother who wanted a psalter was offered ashes. After death Francis appears to a companion busy about preaching and forbids it. Grade B. Compare Anthony letter (own-words).
- 2C §196 "the love of God": hearing it, he was "aroused, stirred, inflamed, as though some inner chord of his heart were being touched by the plectrum of an outward voice". Asked by a poor man "for the love of God" with nothing to give, "he stealthily took scissors and hastened to divide his tunic with him" until caught by brothers. Grade A. NEW.
- 2C §197 Angels; fasted forty days from Assumption to St Michael (the Fast of St Michael). Grade B. 2C §198 Mary is "the Advocate of the Order".
- 2C §199-200 CHRISTMAS: "the feast of feasts". Brother Morico says there will be no meat on Friday: "Thou sinnest, brother, in calling the day on which a Child has been born to us Friday. I would that the very walls should eat flesh on such a day, or, if they cannot, that they should at any rate be greased outside!" Also "If I can have speech of the Emperor, I will beseech him that a general ordinance may be made that all who can are to scatter corn and grain on the ways ... especially our sisters the larks." Oxen and asses get extra hay. At dinner, when a brother speaks of Mary's poverty, he rises and eats the rest of his bread on the bare ground, sobbing. Grade A. NEW and essential for Greccio. (Pairs with 1 Cel I.84-87 for the crib.)
- 2C §201 Eucharist and priests: "If I chanced to meet at the same time any Saint coming from heaven, and any poor priest, I would do honour to the presbyter first ... 'Oh, wait, S. Lawrence! for this man's hands handle the Word of Life'". He loved France as friend of the Lord's Body "and longed to die there". Plan to send brothers with precious pyxes. Grade A/B. NEW.
- 2C §202 MONTE CASALE: he orders relics moved from an abandoned church; the brothers neglect it; later they find "fair and fragrant bones" on the altar. Francis: "Blessed be the Lord my God, who of Himself has fulfilled that which ye ought to have done". Grade B/C. NEW (wonder).
- 2C §204-207 POOR LADIES. "if it were a fault to cherish them in Christ, was it not a greater fault to have united them to Christ?" He sends only "unwilling and most reluctant men" to serve them. A brother with two daughters among the nuns is "rebuked ... very sharply". A brother who went in winter without knowing the rule is made to "walk several miles naked when the snow was deep". At San Damiano, urged by his Vicar to preach, he sits in a circle of ashes in silence, puts ashes on his head, then recites Psalm 50 ("Have mercy on me, O God") and leaves in haste; the sisters weep. Grade A. NEW (the "sermon" in ashes; Clare's sources only say Celano here; conflict with Clare-friendly stories in Mirror and Fioretti).
- 2C §208 Rule: "the book of life, the hope of salvation, the marrow of the Gospel, the way of perfection, the key of Paradise and the covenant of an eternal alliance". A boy-brother martyred by Saracens holds the Rule up: "Of all the things that I have done against this holy Rule, dearest brother ... I proclaim myself guilty." Grade B (martyr unnamed). 
- 2C §209 VISION OF THE CRUMBS during the Rule talks (1223): crumbs of bread to feed hungry brothers: "Francis, make one host out of all the crumbs, and give it to eat to all who will". Those who despise it get leprosy. Voice: "the crumbs ... are the words of the Gospel, the host is the Rule, the leprosy is wickedness". Grade B. NEW.
- 2C §210-211 SCRUPLE ABOUT CARING FOR HIS BODY (last illness). He asks a brother: "What thinkest thou ... of the frequent murmuring of my conscience touching the care of my body?" Brother asks if the body had obeyed him: "I bear it witness, my son ... that it has been obedient in all things and has spared itself in nothing". "Rejoice, brother body, and forgive me, for behold now I gladly fulfil thy desires". (Text continues.) Grade A (the brother is the source). NEW and moving; this is the scene behind Francis's late "forgive me, Brother Body" and the Mirror's "Sister Jacoba" parallel.

### 2C §212-224 (illness, promise, death)
- 2C §212 He called his pains not pains but "sisters". Celano: "Nor was it for nothing that his purgation was completed in the vale of tears". Grade B.
- 2C §213 THE PROMISE (night, pain; Howell does not give place or date): after pity for himself, a voice asks if he would rejoice if the whole earth were gold and all his pain were taken away and a greater treasure given instead. "I should rejoice indeed ... yea, and above measure." "Exult, then ... for thy sickness is the earnest of My kingdom". Celano: "Then it was that he composed certain 'Praises concerning the Creatures'". Grade A/B (Francis told it to companions). Compare the Compilation (to check) which says it happened at San Damiano.
- 2C §214-215 DEATH. "he welcomed death with singing". Laid naked on bare ground "that ... he might wrestle naked with his naked foe". He covers the right-side wound with his left hand. "I have done my duty, may Christ teach you yours". Warden takes the tunic, drawers, cap of sackcloth: "this tunic, these drawers, and this cap have been lent thee by me by order of holy obedience ... I deprive thee of all power of giving them to any one". Francis "rejoiced and exulted". The sackcloth cap covered "the scars which had been produced by the treatment he had undergone for the malady of his eyes". Grade A/B. NEW (the cap and scars).
- 2C §216 He blesses each brother with his right hand beginning with his Vicar; Howell's note: this is Elias, excommunicated by Gregory IX and Innocent IV, so Celano says the "special" blessing was not unconditional.
- 2C §217 Before dying: bread brought, blessed and broken, a morsel to each ("most sacred Supper", Celano). "Welcome, my sister Death." To the physician: "Brother physician, boldly foretell that death is at hand, for it will be the gate of life to me." To the brothers: "lay me naked on the ground, even as ye saw me three days ago, and let me lie there when dead for so long a time as one might take to walk a mile gently". Grade A/B. NEW: bread and mile line; physician line (name not given; Mirror and Compilation say Bongiovanni of Arezzo? check).
- 2C §218 Brother Augustin, Minister of Terra di Lavoro, speechless: "Wait for me, father, wait! See, I am now coming with thee!" Dies. 2C §219 another brother sees Francis in a purple dalmatic with a multitude; "Is not this Christ, brother?" 2C §220 Bishop of Assisi at Benevento on return from Monte Sant'Angelo: Francis appears: "See, father I am leaving the world and going to Christ"; the bishop has a notary write the day and hour. Grade A/B (bishop, notary).
- 2C §221-224: closing prayer of the "companions"; Celano names "Christ's servant our Minister, thy successor" (Crescentius) and "this thy son who now and before has devoutly written thy praises" (Celano himself). Dated 1244-47 (Howell intro).
- Treatise on the Miracles: NOT in Howell (Howell, intro p.vii-viii: composed after 1255, authorship uncertain; Goetz attributes to Julian of Speyer; printed in d'Alencon's edition). Need a Latin or other text.

## Part B. Latin texts in Boehmer, Analekten zur Geschichte des Franciscus von Assisi (Tübingen: Mohr, 1904), archive:MN5083ucmf_2 (public-domain 1904 edition of 13th-c. texts; OCR noisy; my English is "own English"). Key B1.

### B1a. Elias, circular letter on the death (Boehmer p.90-92). Latin: "Frater Helias ... Dilecto sibi in Christo fratri Gregorio ministro fratrum, qui sunt in Francia". Dated 4 Oct 1226 in Boehmer's heading. Grade A (written within hours or days by his Vicar).
- Opening: Elias "suspiro et merito" ("I sigh, and rightly"); "pupilli sumus absque patre" (we are orphans without a father).
- "non diu ante mortem frater et pater noster apparuit crucifixus quinque plagas, quae vere sunt Stigmata Christi, portans in corpore suo". The hands and feet "quasi puncturas clavorum habuerunt ... reservantes cicatrices et clavorum nigredinem ostendentes". The side "lanceatum apparuit et saepe sanguinem evaporavit". Elias calls it a sign never heard "A saeculo ... praeterquam in Filio Dei". Grade A. NEW: first document, mentions only sign, no La Verna.
- While alive his face was marked: "non erat in eo aspectus sed despectus vultus eius" (Isaiah echo) and "nullum membrum in eo remansit absque nimia passione". Limbs were stiff from shrunken sinews, "sicut solent esse hominis mortui". After death: "pulcherrimus aspectus est, miro candore rutilans", and the stiff limbs "facta sunt mollia nimis, sese vertentia huc atque illuc secundum positionem suam tamquam pueri delicati" (like delicate children). Grade A. NEW (the body softening, a detail from Elias himself).
- Blessing: "tamquam alter Jacob omnes filios suos benedixit et omnibus remisit culpas, quae in eum factae fuissent vel cogitatae ab aliquo nostrum" (he forgave all faults done or thought against him, Elias adds "by any of us"). Grade A. NEW; reads as a bearing on Elias's own guilt.
- Time: "Quarto Nonas Octobris die Dominica prima hora noctis praecedentis pater et frater noster Franciscus migravit ad Christum" (text as printed: 4 Oct, Sunday, in the first hour of the preceding night). Boehmer's note computes this as the evening of 3 Oct after sunset. Resolves the 3 vs 4 Oct conflict: Elias counts the day from the evening. Grade A.
- Elias's turns: "Licet enim pium sit congaudere Francisco, pium tamen est flere Franciscum". "ad caelestes nundinas abiit, saccum pecuniae secum ferens et in plenilunio reversurus" (he has gone to the heavenly market, carrying a bag of money, to return at the full moon). Also: "si laguncula testea confracta est in valle filiorum Adam, summus tamen ille figulus aliam honorificam restaurare dignetur" (if the earthen jug is broken, let the Potter make another worthy one) — Elias's prayer for a new leader (himself?). Grade A.
- Orders to the brothers: each priest says three masses, each cleric the Psalter, laymen five Our Fathers, clerics a solemn vigil. NEW.
- Elias signs "Frater Helias peccator". Boehmer p.90: wording from Elias's letter closely shadowed by 1 Cel I.94-95 (Celano reuses Elias).

### B1b. Jacques de Vitry, letter 1 (October 1216, Genoa) (Boehmer p.94-101) Latin. Grade A (eyewitness; first outside description of the movement).
- Dated by Boehmer's notes: Innocent III died 16 Jul 1216 at Perugia; Honorius III elected 18 July; consecrated 24 July; Jacques consecrated bishop 31 July 1216 (footnotes in Boehmer). Jacques found Innocent "mortuum, sed necdum sepultum" and the body "fere nudum et foetidum" in the church after thieves stripped him at night. He writes: "oculata fide cognovi, quam brevis sit et vana huius seculi fallax gloria". Grade A. NEW for Francis's biography as the setting.
- Honorius: "bonum senem et religiosum, simplicem valde et benignum, qui fere omnia, que habere poterat, pauperibus erogaverat".
- "Unum tamen in partibus illis inveni solacium": men and women "divites et seculares, omnibus pro Christo relictis seculum fugiebant, qui fratres Minores vocabantur. A domino papa et cardinalibus in magna reverentia habentur." They do not take care of temporal things; "ut animas, que pereunt, a seculi vanitatibus retrahant"; "veni et cortina cortinam trahat" (a curtain draws a curtain). "secundum formam primitive ecclesie vivunt ... multitudinis credentium erat cor unum et anima una". By day they enter cities and villages; by night they return to hermitages "vacantes contemplationi". Women live in hospices near cities, "de labore manuum vivunt", and are "valde dolent et turbantur, quia a clericis et laicis plus quam vellent honorantur" (distressed by too much honor). Once a year they meet at a fixed place to rejoice and eat together, make rules confirmed by the pope; then scatter through Lombardy, Tuscany, Apulia, Sicily. Brother "Nicholaus domini pape" (a papal penitentiary) has just fled the curia to join them but was recalled. Jacques: the Lord uses "simplices et pauperes homines" to save souls "in opprobrium prelatorum, qui quasi 'canes sunt muti non valentes latrare'". Francis is not named here. Date and place of the letter: Genoa, October 1216. NEW; no English popular page quotes more than a line.
- Other color from the same letter: his luggage nearly drowned in a flooded river in Lombardy; "libri mei aliquantulum obscurati sint, ubique tamen legere possum"; Genoese took his horses for their siege and he signed the wives with the cross.

### B1c. Jacques de Vitry, letter 6 (spring 1220, from Damietta; Boehmer p.101-102) Grade A.
- "Magister illorum, qui ordinem illum instituit, cum venisset in exercitum nostrum, zelo fidei accensus ad exercitum Sarracenorum pertransivit et, cum multis diebus Sarracenis verbum Dei predicasset, modicum profecit." Paraphrase: Francis, fired with zeal of faith, crossed to the Saracen army and preached many days; he achieved little. "Soldanus autem, rex Aegypti, ab eo petiit, ut Domino supplicaret, quatenus religioni, que magis Deo placeret, divinitus inspiratus adhereret." Paraphrase: the Sultan asked Francis to pray that God would inspire him to follow the religion that pleased God most. Jacques also notes the order "valde periculosa nobis videtur" because young and imperfect men go out two by two without a novitiate; and that men of his own entourage (Colin the Englishman, Master Michael, Lord Matthew, who had the church of the Holy Cross) joined the Friars, and he "vix retineo" (barely keeps) Cantor Henry and others. Grade A. NEW: the Jacques "reserve" plus the loss of his clerics to Francis.
- Boehmer footnote quotes Jordan 10: Francis went in the 13th year of conversion; "sed antequam perveniret ad ipsum, multis inimicitiis et contumeliis est affectus et linguam ipsorum ignorans inter verbera clamabat 'Soldan, soldan'!" (before he reached the Sultan he was beaten and insulted; not knowing their language he shouted "Soldan, soldan!" amid blows); "gloriose est ab ipso receptus et in infirmitate humane pertractatus" (received with honor and treated humanely). Boehmer's reading of the Chronicle; to check against the Latin of Jordan (below).

### B1d. Jacques de Vitry, Historia orientalis II c.32 (c.1220-25; Boehmer p.102-106) Grade A (eyewitness: "Vidimus").
- On the Order: "vere Minores et omnibus huius temporis regularibus in habitu et nuditate et mundi contemptu humiliores". "Habent autem unum summum priorem". "Non habent monasteria, non ecclesias, non agros vel vineas vel animalia, non domos, non possessiones neque ubi caput reclinent." "Si quis eos ad prandium vocaverit, que apud illos sunt, manducant et bibunt. Si quis eis aliquid misericorditer contulerit, non reservant in posterum." Chapter "semel aut bis in anno".
- THE SULTAN SCENE (eyewitness text). "Vidimus primum huius ordinis fundatorem et magistrum ... virum simplicem et illiteratum, dilectum Deo et hominibus, fratrem Franciscum nominatum, ad tantum ebrietatis excessum et fervorem spiritus raptum fuisse, quod cum ad exercitum christianorum ante Damiatam in terra Egipti devenisset ad Soldani Egipti castra intrepidus et fidei clipeo communitus accessit." Own English: we saw the founder, a simple, unlettered man, loved by God and men, Brother Francis, so carried away by drunkenness of spirit that, coming to the Christian army before Damietta, he went fearlessly to the Sultan's camp, armed with the shield of faith.
- "Quem cum in via captum Sarraceni tenuissent: 'ego', inquit, 'Christianus sum, duciteme ad dominum vestrum'." (Seized on the road by Saracens he said "I am a Christian; take me to your lord.") "videns eum bestia crudelis in aspectu viri Dei in mansuetudine conversa per dies aliquot ipsum sibi et suis Christum predicantem attentissime audivit." Own English: the cruel beast, seeing him, was turned to gentleness at the sight of the man of God, and for some days heard him preach Christ attentively to himself and his men. "Tandem vero metuens, ne aliqui de exercitu suo verborum eius efficatia ad Dominum conversi ad Christianorum exercitum pertransirent, cum omni reverentia et securitate ad nostrorum castra reduci precepit dicens ei in fine: 'ora pro me, ut Deus legem illam et fidem, que magis illi placet, dignetur revelare'." Own English: fearing his soldiers would be converted, he had Francis led back with all reverence and safety and said at the end: "Pray for me, that God may reveal which law and faith pleases him most." Grade A. This gives the only eyewitness account of the Sultan visit. It has no "fire ordeal" and no "Sultan secretly baptized". The fire trial and the Sultan's private conversion are in Bonaventure and the Fioretti only (to check in Bonaventure below).
- On Saracens in general: "Sarraceni autem omnes predictos fratres Minores tam diu de Christi fide et evangelica doctrina libenter predicantes audiunt, quousque Machometo tanquam mendaci et perfido in predicatione sua manifeste contradicunt. Ex tunc autem eos impie verberantes ... de civitatibus suis expellunt." They listen until the brothers say Muhammad is a liar; then they beat them and expel them. Jacques warns that the order's perfection "infirmis et imperfectis" is not for all.
- Jacques's view of the order: "religio pauperum Crucifixi et ordo predicatorum, quos fratres Minores appellamus"; a renewal "in vespere mundi" "contra Antichristi periculosa tempora".

### B1e. Thomas of Spalato (Historia pontificum Salonitanorum et Spalatensium), Bologna, 15 Aug 1222 (Boehmer p.106). Grade A (eyewitness: "vidi"; he was a student at Bologna).
- "cum essem Bononie in studio, vidi sanctum Franciscum predicantem in platea ante palacium publicum, ubi tota pene civitas convenerat." Sermon began: "Angeli, homines, demones". "multis literatis ... fieret admiracioni non modice sermo hominis ydiote" (many scholars wondered at the sermon of an unlettered man). "Nec tamen ipse modum predicantis tenuit, sed quasi concionantis" (not like a preacher but like one addressing an assembly). "Tota vero verborum eius discurebat materies ad extinguendas inimicicias et ad pacis federa reformanda."
- "Sordidus erat habitus, persona contemptibilis et facies indecora." Own English: dirty habit, contemptible person, plain face. "Sed tantam Deus verbis illius contulit eficaciam, ut multe tribus nobilium, inter quas antiquarum inimiciciarum furor immanis multa sanguinis effusione fuerat debacchatus, ad pacis consilium reducerentur." Crowds rushed to touch his hem or tear bits from his tunic: "viri et mulieres in eum catervatim ruerent, satagentes vel fimbriam eius tangere aut aliquid de paniculis eius auferre." Grade A. NEW and key: an outsider's description of his looks and sermon; no popular page quotes "facies indecora".
- Pairs with 2C §98 (Borgo San Sepolcro: crowds cut pieces from his tunic).

## Part C. Jordan of Giano, Chronica (written 1262 at dictation; Latin), read in Voigt/Quaracchi edition, Analecta Franciscana vol. 1 (Quaracchi 1885), archive:analectafrancisc01coll (public-domain; OCR very noisy; my English is "own English"). Key C1. Cited "Jord. §n" (chapter numbers of the edition).
Jordan was an eyewitness of the 1221 Chapter and was sent to Germany from it (see §15-16). Grade A for what he saw; B for what he was told.

- Jord. §1-2: "Anno Domini 1207 Franciscus, vir negotiator officio, compunctus corde afflatus Spiritu sancto, in habitu eremitico modum poenitentiae est aggressus" (1207, as a merchant). "Anno Domini 1209, anno conversionis suae tertio" he heard the Gospel of the mission, threw off staff, wallet and shoes, changed his habit. So Jordan counts the conversion from 1206-07 and the Gospel from 1209. Date note: Jordan counts "anno conversionis suae ... decimo" for 1219 and the 13th year for the Egypt voyage (Boehmer's footnote quoting Jord. §10 in the 1216 letter apparatus).
- Jord. §3-6 (1219 Chapter at the Portiuncula, 1219): Francis sends brothers to France, Germany, Hungary, Spain. French brothers asked "if they were Albigensians": they did not know what that meant, so they were counted heretics. Pope Honorius gave a letter calling them "filios Romanae Ecclesiae et fideles catholicos". Grade B.
- Jord. §8 (Francis crosses the sea with Peter Catanii): left two vicars, Matthew of Narni (Portiuncula, to receive the novices) and Gregory of Naples (to travel Italy and comfort the brothers). The vicars called a chapter and made new fasting rules: Monday fast added; no meat on meat-days unless offered. A lay brother ran to Francis overseas and "ad beatum Franciscum ... culpam suam" asked pardon for coming without licence and told him the vicars added to the Rule and the Order was in uproar. Grade A (Jordan names the messenger story). NEW: the cause of Francis's return from the East.
- Jord. §9 TABLE SCENE on return: Francis and Peter Catanii. Francis, with meat set before him, said: "Domine Petre, quid faciemus?" Peter: "Ha, domine Francisce, quod vobis placet, quia potestatem habetis." Jordan says: because Peter was learned and noble, Francis honored him by calling him "lord"; "Et haec mutua reverentia fuit inter ipsos tam ultra mare quam in Italia." Francis then: "Comedamus ergo secundum Evangelium quae nobis apponuntur." Own English: "Let us eat, then, according to the Gospel, what is set before us." Grade A/B. NEW; a scene about the quarrel over fasting and the "mutual courtesy" of Peter and Francis.
- Jord. §10 FORTUNE-TELLER: "Eodem tempore fuit ultra mare pythonissa quaedam, quae multa vera praedixit ... 'Redite, redite, quia per absentiam fratris Francisci Ordo turbatur et scinditur et dissipatur'." Own English: a woman with a spirit of prophecy beyond the sea said to the brothers "Go back, go back, because through the absence of Brother Francis the Order is troubled, split and scattered." Jordan: "Et hoc verum fuit." Also: Brother Philip, protector of the poor ladies, got letters from the Holy See against Francis's will; Brother John of Capella gathered lepers and began a new order. Grade B. NEW and sharp.
- Jord. §11 Return to Italy with Elias, Peter Catanii, Caesarius of Speyer: Francis goes not to the troublemakers but to the Pope. He lies at the Pope's door and "cubiculum tanti principis perstrependo pulsare non audebat" (did not dare knock on the great prince's room), waits for him to come out; "Pater Papa, Deus det tibi pacem." "Benedicat te Deus, fili." Francis then: "Domine, cum sis magnus et magnis saepe praeoccupatus negotiis, pauperes ad te accessum habere saepe non possunt ... Multos mihi papas dedisti, da unum, cui, cum necesse habeo, loqui possim, qui vice tua causas meas et Ordinis mei audiat et discutiat." "Quem vis, ut dem tibi, fili?" "Dominum Ostiensem." Granted. Hugolino then recalled Philip's letters and sent John of Capella away "cum verecundia". Grade A/B. NEW; this is the full scene behind 2C §25 (which has the preached sermon).
- Jord. §12 Francis commissions Caesarius of Speyer, a scholar, to adorn his Rule's words with Gospel verses. Brothers had heard he was dead, killed or drowned; when he returned "prae gaudio nova lux eis oriri visa est" (for joy it seemed a new light rose on them). Then Francis called the General Chapter at once.
- Jord. §13 THE 1221 CHAPTER at Pentecost (edition: "decimo kalendas Iunii" 1221, indiction 14, "in sancto die Pentecostes"; 10 Kal. June = 23 May by my count; check the date against Pentecost 1221, not verified). Jordan's own account, and he says he was there. "aestimati sunt Fratres, qui tunc convenerant, ad tria millia fratrum" (about 3,000 brothers). Present: Cardinal Reynerius (Rainerio Capocci), many bishops. "in campo spatioso et circumsepto sub umbraculis habitabant, comedebant et dormiebant, viginti tribus mensis ordinate et distincte et spatiose compositis" (in a wide, fenced field under shelters, with 23 tables laid out). Francis preached on "Benedictus Dominus Deus meus qui docet manus meas ad praelium" (Ps 143). The townspeople served bread and wine in abundance; after seven days the brothers had to close the gate and stop receiving food, and spend two more days giving away what they had been given. Jordan: "Tale capitulum tam in fratrum multitudine quam ministrantium solemnitate non vidi in Ordine." Grade A (eyewitness). NEW; matches the Fioretti "Chapter of Mats" (later legend); this is the earliest source: no mats mentioned, but shelters.
- Jord. §14 THE GERMANY REQUEST. At the end of the chapter Francis remembers the Order has not gone to Germany. "quia beatus Franciscus tunc debilis erat, quidquid ex parte sui capitulo dicendum erat, frater Helias loquebatur." Francis, sitting at Elias's feet, pulled him by the tunic; Elias bent, listened, straightened up and said: "Fratres, ita dicit Frater" (Brothers, thus says the Brother — Francis was called "the Brother" by the brothers). Francis asked for volunteers to go to Germany, where Christians run "cum longis baculis et largis ocreis" (long staves and wide boots) to praise God "in sudore et solis ardore". Those sent earlier to Germany (1217?) had been badly treated. "surrexerunt circiter 90 fratres" (about 90 rose, offering to die). Grade A. NEW (the tugging of the tunic is vivid and rarely used).
- Jord. §15-16 Jordan's own story. A brother in the chapter (Jordan, who writes of himself in the third person as "frater ille curiosus") was afraid to be sent among the Germans. A deacon Palmerius, the brother he met, said "Et tu ipse noster es et nobiscum ibis"; Jordan answered "Non sum de vestris, sed nosse volens vos ad vos veni, non animo vobiscum eundi" (I am not of yours; I came to know you, not to go with you), and was assigned to Germany anyway. Jordan himself: "Iste est frater Iordanus de Iano, qui hoc vobis scribit, qui tali eventu in Theutoniam venit". Elias by obedience: "Praecipio tibi, frater, per sanctam obedientiam, ut deliberes finaliter, an ire velis, an dimittere." Jordan: "nec ire volo nec manere, sed quidquid mihi praeceperis, hoc faciam" and Elias commands him to go. Grade A. Tone: humor of the Order.
- Jord. §17-30 (1221-1223): Caesarius with 12 clerics and 13 lay brothers (including John of Piano Carpini, Thomas of Celano) cross the Alps; Trent; at Mittenwald they walk a night on water and fruit; at Augsburg the bishop kisses each one; "Anno Domini 1222" first provincial chapter at Worms. Jord. §28: Thomas of Celano appears as "frater Thomas de Celano, qui legendam sancti Francisci primam et secundam postea conscripsit" (Jordan names Celano as author of the first and second Life, and as custos in Mainz, Worms, Cologne, Speyer in 1223). Grade A. NEW as a fix to Celano's biography: Celano was in Germany 1221-23.
- Jord. §30: "Anno Domini 1223 [OCR 1:27)], tertio kalendas Decembris confirmata est a domino Honorio III papa regula fratrum Minorum." Edition says 1223, 3 Kal Dec = 29 Nov 1223. 
- Jord. §31: Caesarius, now minister of Germany, visits Francis and Elias in the Spoleto valley and is relieved of office at the chapter at the Portiuncula, replaced by Albert of Pisa (1223 or 1224; year not clear in the OCR). Grade B.
- Jord. §50 DEATH: "Anno Domini 1226 [OCR 1:22(*)], quarto nonas Octobris institutor primarius Ordinis fratrum minorum, felix pater Franciscus, ad Dominum migravit ad Sanctam Mariam de Portiuncula." "Et licet felix Pater, beatus Franciscus, desideravit in ecclesia sepeliri, populus tamen terrae et cives de Assisio timentes, ne propter signa, quae per ipsum et in vita et post mortem Deus dignatus est operari, a Perusinis violenter tolleretur, ipsum prope muros Assisii in ecclesia Sancti Georgii, in qua primitus litteras didicit et postmodum primo praedicare coepit, transtulerunt et honorifice condiderunt." Own English: Francis wished to be buried in a church (the OCR reads "desideravit in ecclesia sepeliri"; the church is not named in my OCR, check the page scan), but the people of Assisi, afraid that the Perugians would seize him by force because of the signs God worked through him, carried him to San Giorgio and buried him with honor. Grade A. NEW: the fear of seizure by Perugia is told by Jordan; Celano and Bonaventure do not state it (to check in 1 Cel I.116).
- Jord. §50 continues: "Defuncto ergo beato Francisco, frater Helias, vicarius beati Francisci, fratribus turbatis super obitu tanti Patris per totum Ordinem litteras consolatorias destinavit, denuntians singulis et universis, quod omnibus, sicut ipse beatus Franciscus praeceperat, ex parte beati Francisci benediceret et ab omnibus absolveret culpis, insuper declarans de stigmatibus et aliis miraculis" (Elias's circular: bless all and absolve all faults "as Francis had commanded", and announces the stigmata and miracles; he told the ministers to gather to elect a general). Grade A. NEW: Jordan confirms Elias's letter and its content.
- Jord. §51 John Parenti (a Roman jurist, born in Città Castellana) elected the first minister-general after Francis (edition: chapter at Mainz "quarto nonas Februarii" 1227 per the German provincial). §53 Julian of Speyer (frater Julianus, "qui postmodum historiam beati Francisci et beati Antonii nobili stilo et pulchra melodia composuit") came to Germany with Simon of England. §54: "Anno Domini 1228 beatus Franciscus canonizatus est" and the canonization was announced to the brothers at the Worms chapter.
- Jord. §59 RELIC STORY (Jordan's own): returning from Thomas of Celano he had "de reliquiis beati Francisci" given by Celano. At Würzburg-Eisenach the brothers received him in procession with crosses, censers, palm branches and candles, singing "Hic est fratrum amator". He realized he had the relics in his bosom, said "Gaudete, fratres, quia scio, quod non me ut me, sed in me Patrem nostrum beatum Franciscum ... collaudastis", and placed them on the altar. Date c.1230-31. Grade A (author). NEW (Francis's first relic in Germany).
- Jord. §61 Elias elected minister-general in 1232 (edition: "Anno Domini 1232 in generali capitulo Romae celebrato absolutus est frater Johannes Parens ... ei est frater Helias substitutus"). Elias then "opus ad Sanctum Franciscum, quod in Assisio inceperat, perficere volens, fecit exactiones per totum Ordinem" (levies for the basilica). Grade A.

## Part D. Mirror of Perfection (Speculum perfectionis), Sebastian Evans tr., 1899, archive:mirrorofperfecti00leob (public-domain English). Key D1. Cited "Mir. ch." by Evans's chapter numbers. Grade for this text: C-late (compiled 1318 from Leo's rolls and the Assisi Compilation of 1244-60; each story says "we that were with him"), unless Part A or B matches. Quotations are Evans's archaic English; I give modern paraphrase or short exact quotes marked.
(Read so far: Mir. I-XI.)
- Mir. I RULE ON THE MOUNTAIN: Francis goes up a mountain with Leo and Bonizo of Bologna to remake the lost second Rule. Ministers gather round Elias: "we will not be bound unto that Rule. Let him make it for himself and not for us." Francis looks up: "Lord, said I not well when I told Thee they would not believe me?" A voice in the air: "Francis, nought is there of thine own in the Rule ... it shall be observed to the letter, to the letter, to the letter, without gloss, without gloss, without gloss!" Ministers leave confounded. LEGEND-flag (late; no sign in Celano; parallels Celano 2C §188 anger at ministers). Grade C. NEW.
- Mir. II Brother Richer of the March (nobles) asks Francis in the bishop's palace at Assisi whether clerics may keep books. Francis: his "first intention and my last will" is that "no brother ought to have anything save his habit ... with girdle and hosen." The compilers explain that the Rule did not say it because "he was sore afeared of scandal" and gave in to the ministers' wishes, then "fulfilled the word in himself". Grade C. NEW (Francis "gave in to avoid scandal" theme).
- Mir. III After the return from beyond the sea a minister with books worth "more than forty pound" asks. Francis: "I will not, nor I ought not, nor I cannot go against my conscience". Then to all ministers: "Ye would fain appear before men as brethren Minor ... but in your works would ye fain have treasure-chests!" The ministers remove from the Rule the clause "Take nothing for your journey"; Francis says (in front of some brothers) it is his will that the Gospel is named at the beginning and the end of the Rule. Grade C. Compare 2C §62 (books).
- Mir. IV THE NOVICE AND THE PSALTER: a novice with the General's leave asks Francis's approval. Francis: Charlemagne, Roland and Oliver "did achieve a victory ... and at the last did themselves die in battle, holy martyrs for the faith of Christ; yet now be there many that would fain receive honour and the praise of men for only telling the tale of the deeds they did". Weeks later at the fire: "After that thou shalt have had the psalter, thou wilt be covetous and desire to have a breviary also ... and wilt say unto thy brother, 'Fetch me the breviary!'" He takes ashes and puts them on his head, rubbing his hand over it like washing: "I a breviary! I a breviary!" He confesses that he himself was tempted to have books and opened the Gospel book for a sign: "Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the Kingdom of God". Months later at the Portiuncula, he tells the novice "Go and do herein as thy minister shall tell thee", then runs after him: "Wait for me, brother, wait!", takes him back to the place where he said it and kneels: "Mine is the fault, brother, mine is the fault!" Grade B/C. NEW and strong: Francis's repentance on the road, the ashes, and the Charlemagne remark (a medieval knight's reference). Pairs with 2C §104-105.
- Mir. V "he which had a tattered rag over his straw did hold the same for a feather-bed" (same as 2C §63).
- Mir. VI Bologna house: same as 2C §58. A sick brother "beareth witness to these things and did write this" (same sick witness; Mirror takes text from the Compilation).
- Mir. VII Portiuncula roof: "one small cell thatched with straw whereof the walls were of wattle and dab" replaced by a stone house; Francis climbs on the roof; soldiers of Assisi tell him it belongs to the Commune: "If so be, then, that it is yours, no will have I to touch it." Mirror adds: the people of Assisi then made a by-law that the high-bailiff must keep the house repaired. Grade B/C. NEW detail (the by-law, and Francis's cheerful words).
- Mir. VIII Vicar builds a small house at the Portiuncula so the brothers can say the Hours. Francis: "this place is the pattern and example of the whole Religion, and I would therefore that the brethren of this place should be the first to suffer tribulation and discomfort". Grade C.
- Mir. IX CELL: a spiritual brother builds a wooden cell; Francis: "Too fair is this cell!" and orders it covered with wattles and branches inside and out. Same Sartiano story as 2C §59. Mirror adds the will: "all the cells and houses of the brethren should be of stud and mud only". Grade B/C.
- Mir. X Siena, for his eyes: Doctor Bonaventura (a Sienese donor, not the saint) asks how the friary should be built. Francis gives a detailed plan: ask the bishop, dig a trench and plant a hedge "as a mark of holy poverty and humility", houses of "mud and stud", small cells, small churches: "the sorry houses, the little cells, and small churches will preach to them". Grade C. NEW (Francis as an architect of poverty).

- Mir. XII-XIII Francis on alms: "Never was I a thief in the matter of alms"; he took less than he might "lest the other needy ones should be cheated". Grade C.
- Mir. XVIII EARLY ALMS: when the first brothers were too ashamed to beg he went alone each day; when worn out he told them "My dearest brethren and little children, be ye not ashamed to go forth for alms" and sent them out one by one; on return each showed his alms and they said "I have gathered more alms than thou" and Francis rejoiced. Grade C. NEW and funny (Mirror only).
- Mir. XIX No cooking ahead: he forbade the cook to soak herbs the evening before so as to "take no thought for the morrow"; the cook began after matins. Grade C. NEW.
- Mir. XXII BAGNARA above NOCERA (not Satriano): "his feet began to swell grievously by reason of the ailment of a dropsy" and the Assisi soldiers came "fearing lest in case he should die there, others might have his most holy body". At a walled town they cannot buy food; Francis: "ye put your trust in your flies and your deniers" (he called money "flies"). They beg and "with much gladness and abundance" are fed. Mirror's wording matches 2C §77 (conflict only in the place name: Mirror says the stop was in a walled town in Assisi's lordship; 2C names Satriano). Grade B. Francis: "Verily, my Lord, I have shown thee the greatest honour"; then a longer speech: "I hold it for a greater consolation both of soul and body when I sit at the sorry table of the brethren". Grade B/C (same as 2C §73 with longer speech).
- Mir. XXVI REVELATION OF THE NAME "Minor": Francis said the brothers are "a certain little flock that the Son of God in this last hour did ask of His Heavenly Father". The Lord also revealed the greeting "The Lord give thee peace!" (in the Testament). In the earliest days a brother saluting people in the fields with "The Lord give ye peace" was mocked ("What meaneth this same salutation of yours?") and asked for leave to use another. Francis: "Let them talk, for they perceive not the things that are of God. Natheless, be not ashamed, forasmuch as nobles and princes of this world shall manifest their reverence toward thee ... by reason of this salutation." Grade C. NEW (the peace greeting story).
- Mir. XXVII RIVO TORTO: night, a brother cries "I am dying! I am dying!" of hunger (same as 2C §21-22, but the Mirror places it at Rivo Torto; "Arise, brethren, and kindle the light!"). Francis eats with him and then says he will not do it again as a rule and that each should "satisfy the needs of his body as may be necessary". - Mir. - Mir. LXV THE FRANCE JOURNEY (1217): after the chapter "wherein many brethren were sent to certain provinces beyond sea" Francis decides he must go too: "meet it is that I should be the pattern and ensample". He sends brothers to pray for the right province, then chooses France: "wherein is a Catholic folk ... they do manifest a special reverence toward the Body of Christ". Rule for the road: go two by two with strict silence from early morning until after tierce; "brother Body is our cell, and the soul is the hermit that abideth within the cell". At Florence Hugolino forbids him to cross the mountains: "many prelates there be that gladly would hinder the good work of thy Religion in the Court of Rome". Francis: "My lord, weenest thou that the Lord hath sent the brethren for the sake of these provinces only? ... God hath chosen and sent the brethren for the profit and salvation of the souls of all the men of this world; not alone in the lands of the faithful, but even in the lands of the infidel shall they be received". Hugolino marvels but forbids; Francis sends Pacifico to France and returns to the Spoleto valley. Also: he wanted brothers to carry "pyxes fair and clean" and iron "moulds ... for shaping of the Hosts" (matches 2C §201 pyxes). Grade B/C. ## 1. Timeline additions (dated, new to pass one)
- c.1206-07: Jordan counts Francis's conversion from 1207 ("vir negotiator officio ... in habitu eremitico modum poenitentiae est aggressus") and the Gospel-call from 1209, "anno conversionis suae tertio" [Jord. §1-2]. Dating conflict with Celano's table: keep as "sources differ".
- 16-31 Jul 1216, Perugia: Innocent III dies and is found stripped in the church; Honorius III elected 18 Jul; Jacques de Vitry consecrated 31 Jul (Boehmer's notes); his October letter from Genoa describes the "fratres Minores" as his one comfort at the curia [B1b]. NEW.
- 1217, Florence: Hugolino forbids Francis to go to France; Francis: "God hath chosen and sent the brethren for the profit and salvation of the souls of all the men of this world" [Mir. LXV]. NEW to popular pages.
- 1219 (chapter at the Portiuncula, then voyage): Francis leaves vicars Matthew of Narni and Gregory of Naples; goes to Damietta with Peter Catanii [Jord. §8]. ## 2. Character lines (one each, with source and grade)
- Rich boy: "a great prince" on the road to Apulia (2C §6, B).
- Funny and honest: at Poggio he confesses to the crowd that he has eaten food "seasoned with lard" [2C §131, A/B].
- Humble to the point of comedy: asks the barber not to cut a large tonsure: "I wish that my simple brethren may have a share in my head" [2C §193, A].
- Jealous of the poor: sees a naked man: "I chose Poverty for my riches, and for my Lady, and see! she shines more brightly in yonder man" [2C §84, B].
- Gives the clothes off his back; cuts his own tunic for a beggar when brothers stop him [2C §196, A].
- Burns a wooden bowl he carved in Lent because it distracted him at Tierce [2C §97, A].
- Strict with himself, tender to others: eats grapes with a sick brother; begs meat for a sick brother [2C §176; §175, B].
- Hot temper over the Order: "Who are they that have snatched the Religion of myself and the brethren out of my hands?" [2C §188, A]; and "let them be cursed by Thee ... and by me, thy little servant" [2C §156, B].
- Playful: a French song on a stick-viol [2C §127, A]; "I a breviary!" with ashes [Mir. IV, B/C]; "greased outside" walls at Christmas [2C §199, A].
- Fears the devil when alone: "I fear to be alone" after the beating in the tower [2C §119, A/B].
- Hides his wounds: "Mind your own business" [2C §135, A]; "Ask what that is, if you don't know it to be an eye" [2C §136, A].
- Admirers' view: Jacques de Vitry: "virum simplicem et illiteratum, dilectum Deo et hominibus" [B1d, A]; Thomas of Spalato: "Sordidus erat habitus, persona contemptibilis et facies indecora" [B1e, A]. Dominican doctor: his theology is "a soaring eagle" [2C §103, A/B].
- Critics' view: the ministers: "Let him make it for himself and not for us" [Mir. I, C]; Jacques: the order is "valde periculosa" because young men go out untested [B1c, A]; Elias: wary of Canticle joy [Mir. CXXI, C]; Celano himself complains of the later brothers [2C §162, §149, A].

## 6. Conflicts and cautions
- Death date: Elias: 4 Oct, "die Dominica prima hora noctis praecedentis" (Saturday night, from first hour of night). Jordan: "quarto nonas Octobris". Mirror: "Saturday evening", year 1227 in Evans's text. Use 3 Oct 1226 evening / 4 Oct for the Church day. [B1a; Jord. §50; Mir.]
- Sultan visit: Jacques (eyewitness): no fire, no baptism, Francis was sent back "with all reverence and security" and asked for prayer. Jordan (via Boehmer note): beaten and shouted "Soldan, soldan!"; both are A. Celano 2C §30 mentions only Damietta and no Sultan; Bonaventure and the Fioretti add the fire trial (not read here).
- Chapter at the Portiuncula: Jordan (eyewitness) ~3,000 brothers; Mirror: 5,000, "Chapter of the Wattles". Prefer Jordan.
- The stopping place of the Assisi knights carrying Francis: 2C §77 "Satriano" (Nocera region); Mir. XXII "Bagnara above Nocera ... a walled town".
- Sartiano: 2C §59 and Mir. IX use the same cell story but name Sartiano only in 2C.
- Francis and women: 2C §112-114 is severe (looks at no woman); Mirror and Fioretti show Clare, Jacoba, and the Poor Ladies kindly; Celano's own 2C §204-207 praises the sisters. Writer should balance.
- The side wound: 1 Cel I.95 (Rufino touched it; Elias saw it); 2C §138 (one brother saw by "pious fraud"); Bonaventure (cardinals saw the hands). Jordan §50: Elias's letter gives the stigmata.
- The Canticle's origin: 2C §213 (a night promise; the Praises) vs Mir. C (San Damiano mice, 60 days blind). Present as "the Compilation says".
- Mirror colophon says 1228; Evans's Latin edition vs critical date c.1318. Treat the Mirror as a late compilation: C.
- Pass one noted 2C §138 "mean trick" by Elias; in 2C §137-138 the trick is Pacifico's wink and a brother who "with prying curiosity" asked to shake the tunic, not Elias. Correct the pass-one line: Howell's intro says Elias; the Howell English of 2C §137-138 does not name Elias. [Howell II §137-138]
