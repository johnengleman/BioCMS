# Francis of Assisi — notes, focus: witnesses (life events and early Lives)

Researcher: witnesses agent. Every fact has a source key. Quotations are copied from the OCR text I fetched (archive.org). The Latin originals were NOT read (see "Not read"). All English texts are public-domain translations.

## 1. Identity (witnesses view only)

- Name forms in the Lives: Francis; called John at baptism by his mother, "Francis" came later [S1 II.3, Second Life]. Father: Pietro (Peter) di Bernardone [S1 II.12; S2 20]. Italian: Francesco d'Assisi.
- Birth 1182 and death 3-4 Oct 1226 in the translator's date table [S1 p.xxiii]. The task gives c.1181/82. No source I read gives a birth day.
- Bishop of Assisi in his time: Guido [S1 I.32].
- Tradition: Catholic (died after 1054).
- Death place: Portiuncula (S. Maria degli Angeli) [S1 I.105-110; S5 XIV.3].
- Canonized 16 July 1228 at Assisi by Pope Gregory IX [S1 I.126 end; table p.xxiii].
- Feast days, patronage: not in my sources. See the church-miracles notes.

## 2. Sources

Rights: all `public-domain` (pre-1931 translations of 13th-14th century texts).

- S1 Thomas of Celano, First Life (1228-29) and Second Life (1244-47), also the Treatise on Miracles. Translator A. G. Ferrers Howell, 1908. English only. Archive: `livesofsfranciso00thom` (fetched whole; also `livessfrancisas01howegoog`). Dates of composition: Howell Introduction p.vi-viii. Cited as "I.§" (First Life) and "II.§" (Second Life), Howell's paragraph numbers.
- S2 Legend of the Three Companions (letter dated Greccio, 11 Aug 1246). Translator E. G. Salter, Temple Classics, 1902. Archive: `legendofsaintfra00unse`. OCR is noisy; I quote only clean passages. Cited as "3C §".
- S3 Mirror of Perfection (Speculum Perfectionis), ascribed to Brother Leo. Archive: `TheMirrorOfPerfection` (1902 per catalog; translator not confirmed on the pages I read). Compilation finished about 1318 per Boehmer and Howell [S6 p.xix-xx; S1 p.xi]. Cited by section heading.
- S4 Little Flowers (Fioretti), translator T. W. Arnold (per archive id `littleflowersofs00arno` and S6 bibliography; title page not confirmed). Compiled after 1322; earliest dated manuscript 1390 [S6 bibliography]. LATE. Cited by chapter.
- S5 Bonaventure, Life of St Francis (Legenda maior). Written for the Chapter-General of Narbonne 1260, finished 1263 [S6 p.xx]. English text inside the Everyman volume, archive `thelittleflowers00unse` (Temple Classics translation, listed as "E.G.S. 1904" in S6 bibliography). Cited as "Bon ch.§".
- S6 Everyman volume Introduction and bibliography (same archive item, 1925). Secondary. Used only for dates of the texts.
- S7 Baring-Gould, Lives of the Saints vol 11 p.97-148 (Oct 4): found by `find.py`, NOT read. Says it borrows from Mrs Oliphant. Local: /Users/nicholas/Desktop/saints-website/source-library (baring-gould/vol11.pdf).

Order of the Lives (from S1 intro, S2, S5, S6): Celano First Life 1228-29 (commissioned by Gregory IX); 3C letter 1246; Celano Second Life 1244-47 (commissioned by Crescentius of Jesi, Minister-General); Bonaventure 1260-63; Mirror c.1318; Fioretti after 1322. Per S6 (secondary), the Chapter at Paris in 1266 ordered older Lives destroyed. Weight the sources in that order of closeness to Francis, but note that the Mirror and 3C contain old material from Leo, Rufino, Angelo [S1 p.x-xi].

Reliability notes:
- Celano joined the Order about 1213-16, was probably in Germany 1221-23+ and likely absent from Italy at the end of Francis's life; in I.88 he writes "as we have been able to learn rightly" [S1 p.vi-ix]. So his account of the last two years is second-hand.
- Howell argues the Second Life uses the Companions' notes; the "Elias" passages show Celano's later change of tone [S1 p.xii-xvi].

## 3. Timeline (date, event, vivid detail, source). NEW = likely missing from popular English pages. LEGEND = late or doubtful.

1. c.1182 Born in Assisi. His mother calls him John; "Francis" is the name later known [S1 table p.xxiii; II.3].
2. To about age 25. Rich cloth-merchant's son, "a shrewd trader but a most ostentatious spender", easy and kind, led friends into folly [S1 I.1]. 3C says he was "by nature joyous and merry" [S2 4]. Celano's moralizing preface blames the upbringing; do not take it as fact.
3. 1202-03 Captured in the war between Perugia and Assisi; held a year. Jests in chains; a fellow-prisoner calls him mad; he answers he will be worshipped by the whole world. He befriends the one hated knight of the group [S2 4; S1 II.4; date S1 p.xxiii].
4. After release, a long illness; he walks out on a stick and finds the fields no longer delight him [S1 I.3; S5 not checked on this].
5. Plan to ride to Apulia with a noble. Night vision of a house full of arms. 3C adds the count Gentile and a knighthood plan [S1 I.4-5; S2 5-6]. NEW detail: before Spoleto he gives his new clothes to a poor knight [S2 6; S5 I].
6. At Spoleto a voice asks which can do more, lord or servant; then "Return unto thine own country" [S2 6]. First Life does not have this second dream.
7. Master of the revels: his comrades choose him as king of the feast; he walks behind, silent; sweetness fills him; asked if he is thinking of a wife he says he is thinking of a bride "nobler and richer and fairer" [S2 7]. Celano has the bride saying but not this scene [S1 I.7]. NEW.
8. Rome pilgrimage: he flings a purse through the grating at St Peter's, then swaps clothes with a beggar and begs in French [S2 10]. NEW.
9. The leper. Celano: he kissed a leper [S1 I.17]. 3C: he got off his horse, gave money, kissed the hand; the leper gave the kiss of peace. He then visited the leper house and gave alms to each. Francis later wrote of it in his Testament [S2 11, 20; S1 I.17 quotes the Testament].
10. The cave and the "treasure": he prays in a cave outside Assisi while a friend waits. 3C NEW: a devil threatens him with the hump of a deformed Assisi woman [S1 I.6; S2 12].
11. c.1205-06 San Damiano crucifix. Celano First Life has no voice; he goes in, "Finding a poor priest" [S1 I.9]. 3C: the Image of the Crucified speaks: "seest thou not that My House is being destroyed" [S2 13]. Second Life: the painted image "moved its lips" and calls him by name [S1 II.10]. He first reads it as the ruined church; he asks the priest to burn a lamp [S2 13]. See Quotations 2, 3.
12. He sells cloth and horse at Foligno, offers the money to the priest, who refuses it for fear of the father; Francis throws it on a window sill "heeding it as little as dust" [S1 I.8-9; S2 16].
13. A month hiding in a pit or cavern; then comes out singing, is pelted with mud and stones [S1 I.10-11; S2 16-17].
14. His father drags him home, chains him in the dark. His mother frees him while the father is away [S1 I.12-13; S2 18].
15. Father goes to the consuls, who say Francis "hath gone out of our jurisdiction"; then to Bishop Guido. Francis answers the summons: "Unto the lord Bishop will I come, for he is the father and lord of souls" [S2 19]. Bishop tells him to give the money back.
16. Renunciation. Celano: he strips "stark naked", the bishop covers him with his mantle [S1 I.15]. 3C: he goes into the bishop's room and comes out naked carrying the clothes; a hair-shirt shows beneath; bystanders weep; bishop covers him with his pall [S2 20]. Second Life gives the "Our Father" line and sackcloth [S1 II.12]. See Quotation 4.
17. Robbers throw him into a snow pit ("I am a herald of the great King"); he is a scullion at a monastery for some days; the Prior later asks pardon; a friend in Gubbio gives him a tunic [S1 I.16]. NEW to many English readers.
18. Years at San Damiano: he carries stones, begs oil in French, confesses to a card-game group that he was ashamed to ask them [S2 21-24]. NEW detail: the priest feeds him syrups and sweets; he sees he should live as a beggar and eats a bowl of begged scraps "no rich syrup had he ever tasted aught so delightsome" [S2 22]. He hires a beggar to bless him when his father curses; the brother says "sell one penny-worth of his sweat" [S2 23; S1 II.12]. See Quotation 7.
19. Prophecy at San Damiano that it will become a convent of Ladies; Celano says the Order of Poor Ladies began there about six years after his conversion [S2 24; S1 I.18].
20. Third year of conversion: he repairs Portiuncula, a forsaken church of the Virgin; dresses as a hermit [S1 I.21].
21. The Gospel at Mass (Mark/Matthew mission to the Twelve): he asks the priest to explain it, then drops shoes, staff, leather belt, takes a cord, makes a tunic in the shape of a cross [S1 I.22; S2 25]. See Quotation 6.
22. 3C NEW: before him, a man used to walk through Assisi greeting "Peace and good, peace and good"; Francis's greeting "The Lord give thee peace" was revealed to him [S2 26; S1 I.23 has the greeting].
23. First followers. Celano: a man of Assisi, then Bernard, another man, Giles, Philip: seven [S1 I.24-25]. 3C: Bernard and Peter go with him; at the church of St Nicholas he opens the Gospel book three times, finding the rich-man verse, "Take nothing", and the "deny himself" verse; "this is our life and Rule" [S2 27-29]. Bernard sells all for the poor.
24. Priest Silvester asks more money for stones. Francis takes coins from Bernard's cloak and fills his hands. Later Silvester dreams of a huge cross growing from Francis's mouth and joins [S2 30-31]. NEW.
25. Giles joins; Francis and Giles walk the March of Ancona; the fishermen's-net parable; three more join (Sabbatino, Morico, John de Cappella) [S2 32-35].
26. Eight brethren sent in pairs: "Cast thy thought on the Lord and He will nourish thee"; they meet again by his prayer [S1 I.29-30]. Bernard and Giles go to Compostela [S1 I.30].
27. Insults on the road: dice put in their hands, hoods pulled from behind. Florence: two brothers sleep by an oven, the owner's wife sees them praying at matins and pities them [S2 39-40]. NEW.
28. About 1209-10 Rome and Innocent III. Bishop Guido is first annoyed, then helps. Cardinal John of St Paul (Sabina) urges monastic life and then becomes advocate [S1 I.32-33; S2 47-48]. Innocent says: "Go, my son, and pray God that He reveal unto thee..." [S2 49]. Francis returns with the parable of the poor woman and the king [S2 50-51]. Innocent had dreamed of the Lateran leaning and a small religious holding it with his back [S2 51]. Francis dreams of a tall tree that bends to him [S2 53]. They are tonsured as clerics [S2 52]. Date: translator's table gives 1210, "verbally approved" [S1 p.xxiii].
29. Birds. Near Bevagna he preaches to doves, rooks and others, touching them with his tunic; at Alviano he tells the swallows "My sisters, the swallows, it is now time for me to speak too" [S1 I.58-59].
30. Failed trip to Morocco: stopped in Spain by illness [S1 I.56; S5 IX.6].
31. 1219 Syria and the Sultan. Celano: in the thirteenth year of conversion, he and one companion go to Syria; beaten and insulted on the way; honorably received; the Sultan offers gifts; "gazed on him as a man unlike almost all men" [S1 I.57]. Footnote gives A.D. 1219. Bonaventure adds: companion Illuminato; two lambs on the road; fire ordeal offer; the Sultan's priest flees; Francis refuses gifts [S5 IX.7-8]. Fioretti LEGEND: twelve companions; the Sultan wishes to convert in secret [S4 XXIV]. Neither Celano nor Bonaventure names al-Kamil in the English I read. See Other.
32. 1220 (Michaelmas) he resigns as head of the Order [S1 table p.xxiii]. Peter Cathanii was his vicar from Sept 1220 until death on 10 March 1221; then Elias [S1 p.xiv].
33. Easter at Greccio (year unstated): he sees a tall decked table, goes out, takes a poor man's hat and staff and begs at the door as a pilgrim, then sits on the ashes: "Now I am sitting like a Lesser Brother" [S1 II.61]. NEW.
34. 1223 Greccio manger. Celano: "in the third year before the day of his glorious decease"; he sends for John of Greccio fifteen days before Christmas; ox and ass, torches, Mass over the manger, Francis in deacon's vestments sings the Gospel and preaches; he says "Bethlehem" like a sheep bleating and licks his lips at the name of Jesus; a man sees a lifeless child in the manger that Francis wakens [S1 I.84-86]. Bonaventure adds: papal licence first; knight John of Greccio sees the child sleeping and woken as Francis embraced him [S5 X.7]. Hay cures animals and women in labour [S1 I.87]. Celano's "Greccio as a new Bethlehem" is good for the writer.
35. Second Life Christmas sayings: walls should "eat flesh", and the Emperor should order corn scattered for the larks [S1 II.199-200]. NEW.
36. 29 Nov 1223 Rule confirmed by Honorius III [S1 table p.xxiii].
37. 1224 La Verna. Table: fast 15 Aug to 30 Sept [S1 p.xxiii]. Celano: two years before death, at Alverna, a six-winged seraph fixed to a cross; marks of nails appear; the side wound [S1 I.94-95]. Bonaventure: a Lent in honour of St Michael; he opens the Gospel book three times, each time at the Passion; the vision "about the Feast of the Exaltation of Holy Cross"; he descends at St Michael's feast; Brother Illuminato advises him to tell the vision [S5 XIII.1-5]. Bonaventure says words were spoken that he never told. Celano does not give day or month.
38. Hiding the stigmata. Rufino scratches his back, touches the side, Francis is distressed and prays pardon for him [S1 I.95]. Bonaventure: Elias is the "Vicar" who saw; a brother tending him saw the wound; cardinals saw the hands; Pope Alexander (IV) told a sermon audience, "myself among them" (Bonaventure), that he had seen them in Francis's life; more than fifty brothers, Clare and her sisters saw after death [S5 XIII.8]. Second Life II.138 reports a "mean trick" by which Elias saw the wound [S1 p.xiii, intro summary; text II.138 not read].
39. Last two years: eye disease, riding an ass; Elias persuades him to see doctors; Hugolino (Ostia) at Rieti; cautery of head, veins opened, plasters [S1 I.97-101]. Four brothers (Leo, Angelo, Rufino, Masseo per Howell's note) nursed him [S1 I.102 note].
40. Francis wants to start again: "Let us begin, brethren, to serve the Lord God, for hitherto we have profited little or nothing"; wishes to serve lepers again [S1 I.103; S5 XIV.1].
41. Spring-summer 1226 Siena: sixth month before death; he vomits blood; Elias comes; Cella di Cortona; swollen belly, legs; asks to go to Assisi [S1 I.105]. The city rejoices, hoping to keep his body [S1 I.105 note].
42. Mirror: carried to the Bishop's palace in Assisi. A quarrel between the Bishop and the Governor; Francis adds a verse about pardon to the Canticle and sends two brothers to sing it before both; they reconcile [S3 ch.X]. Source late; the Canticle is also claimed in Second Life only as "Praises concerning the Creatures" [S1 II.213].
43. Sister Death. He tells Angelo and Leo to sing the Canticle and adds the verse on Sister Death [S3, section "Sister Death"]. Celano First Life has no Canticle or Sister Death passage in the sections I read.
44. The litter stops at the hospital halfway to the Portiuncula; he has it turned toward Assisi and blesses the city [S3]. LATE source (c.1318).
45. Lady Jacoba dei Sette-Soli arrives with cloth and almond cakes just as the brothers write to ask for them. Francis waives the rule against women in the enclosure [S3 "Of the food and cloth he desired before his death"]. NEW. LATE source.
46. Foligno vision: a white-haired priest tells Elias that eighteen years since his conversion are fulfilled and he will live two more [S1 I.108].
47. Last blessing. He crosses his arms to put his right hand on Elias: "On whom am I holding my right hand?" [S1 I.108]. Second Life says he blessed all, beginning with his Vicar, and Celano adds a note that the special blessing of Elias is not unconditional [S1 II.216 and note].
48. He is laid naked on the ground. Warden lends him habit, cord and breeches by obedience so that he owns nothing. He covers the side wound with his left hand [S1 II.214-215; S5 XIV.3-4].
49. Gospel of John 13 (Before the Passover) is read; he recites the psalm that begins "I cried unto the Lord with my voice" [S1 I.109; S5 XIV.5]. Celano: ashes and sackcloth. Sunset 3 Oct: died. Table gives 3-4 Oct 1226 [S1 table p.xxiii]. No source I read says "Saturday" or gives the hour.
50. A brother sees his soul as a star, as large as the moon, on a white cloud [S1 I.110; S5 XIV.6]. Bonaventure only: Brother Augustine, speechless, cries "Tarry for me, Father" [S5 XIV.6]; the Bishop of Assisi, at Monte Gargano, sees him that night; larks flock to the roof at his death [S5 XIV.6].
51. Next day. The body is carried in procession to San Damiano; the window is opened for Clare and the sisters: "Father, father, what shall we do?" [S1 I.116-117]. Bonaventure: more than fifty brothers and Clare saw the stigmata [S5 XIII.8].
52. 16 July 1228 canonization at Assisi by Gregory IX; the Pope kisses the tomb [S1 I.126 end]. Miracle accounts read before the Pope [S1 I.127].
53. May 1230 body moved from St George's to the new church built by Elias; its resting place was not found again until 1818 [S1 p.vii and note].

## 4. Character (one line each)

- Spendthrift charm. Rich, generous, flowing dress, songs; "master of the revels" [S1 I.1; S2 7].
- Quick joker. Laughs at his chains; sits in the ashes at the Easter table at Greccio and says he sits "like a Lesser Brother" [S2 4; S1 II.61].
- Loved French. Sings praises and begs in French [S1 I.16; S2 10, 21, 24, 33].
- Self-mocking. Says he sinned "much against Brother Body"; calls ash "Brother Ash is pure" [S2 14-15]. Calls himself the son of Peter di Bernardone and has brothers revile him [S1 I.53].
- Public penitent. Drags himself by a rope through Assisi for eating chicken [S1 I.52].
- Fear of lepers early; he stopped his nose from two miles off [S1 I.17].
- Secret keeper. "My secret to me" [S5 XIII.4]. Gave a signal verse to dismiss visitors [S1 I.96].
- Quarrelsome father, a brother who mocks, a mother who frees him [S1 I.12-13; II.12].
- Tender to animals: birds, swallows, larks [S1 I.58-59; II.200].
- Critics and doubters. Townsmen call him mad [S1 I.11]. Bishop says his life is "hard and harsh" [S2 35]. Elias is warm in the First Life and cold in the Second, because of what Elias later did as head of the Order [S1 p.xiii-xv].
- Late-life fear of laxity: "a great trial is coming upon you" [S1 I.108].
- Spoken to the cardinal Hugolino as "bishop of the whole world" in letters [S1 I.100].

## 7. Miracles and wonders (reporter, status = popular unless noted; none is a Church-approved miracle in what I read)

1. Voice from the San Damiano crucifix, c.1205-06. Reported by 3C (1246) and Second Life (1244-47); First Life (1229) is silent. Source status: early Lives, recorded story. [S2 13; S1 II.10]
2. Pope Innocent's dream of the Lateran held up by a small friar, before 1210. 3C only. [S2 51]
3. Priest Silvester's dream of a cross from Francis's mouth. 3C; Bonaventure also alludes [S2 31; S5 XIII.10].
4. Greccio: hay cures sick animals and women in labour; altar built over manger. Celano I.87 (1229). Recorded.
5. Greccio vision of the child woken (unnamed man in Celano; John of Greccio in Bonaventure). [S1 I.86; S5 X.7]
6. The preached-to birds; swallows quiet [S1 I.58-59]. Recorded; no healing.
7. Stigmata, 1224. Celano 1229 (hearsay for seeing; saw them after death); Bonaventure lists witnesses incl. Pope Alexander. [S1 I.94-95, 112-113; S5 XIII.8] Recorded, later church-recognized (see church notes).
8. Rieti plague of sheep and oxen ended by water that washed Francis's hands and feet. Bonaventure only. [S5 XIII.6]
9. Alverna hail stopped after the vision. Bonaventure only. [S5 XIII.7]
10. Poor man's ass: Francis's touch removes the cold of a winter night under a rock. Bonaventure only. [S5 XIII.7]
11. Ascent of his soul seen as a star by an unnamed brother. [S1 I.110; S5 XIV.6]
12. Brother Augustine's cry "Tarry for me, Father", and the Bishop of Assisi's vision at Monte Gargano, at the hour of death. Bonaventure only. [S5 XIV.6]
13. Larks at the death-house roof. Bonaventure only (not in the Celano sections I read). [S5 XIV.6]
14. Body after death: flesh white, limbs soft, nail-like marks black "formed out of his flesh", side red. Celano [S1 I.112-113]; Bonaventure compares the side to "a rose most fair" [S5 XV.2].
15. Lady Jacoba's arrival, prompted in spirit with exactly the things in the letter. Mirror (c.1318). [S3] Late.
16. Wolf of Gubbio. Fioretti (after 1322, earliest MS 1390). LEGEND. [S4 XXI]
17. Post-death miracles read before Gregory IX: first listed is the healing of the crooked on the day of burial. Heading only read; texts in S1 I.127ff not read.

## 8. Other

- Conflicts between sources:
  - San Damiano voice: First Life silent; Second Life and 3C tell it. A writer should say "the early Lives tell" and not state it as certain.
  - Second dream at Spoleto: 3C only. First Life has one vision (arms).
  - Sultan: Celano names no companion; Bonaventure names Illuminato; Fioretti has twelve companions and a secret wish to convert (legend). Sultan's name (al-Kamil) is NOT in any source I read.
  - Greccio vision: unnamed man (Celano) vs the knight John (Bonaventure). Papal licence only in Bonaventure.
  - The wound in the side: Celano I.95 says Elias saw it "somehow" and Rufino touched it; Howell says II.138 tells how Elias contrived to see it by a "mean trick" [S1 p.xiii]. Bonaventure mentions a vicar seeing it.
  - Elias blessing: Celano I.108 gives Elias a special blessing; II.216 and its note say it did not stand [S1 p.xiv-xvi].
  - Date of stigmata: Celano "two years before"; Bonaventure "about the Feast of the Exaltation of Holy Cross" and descent at St Michael's (29 Sept); Howell table 15 Aug-30 Sept.
