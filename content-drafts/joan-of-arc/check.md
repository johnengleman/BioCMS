# Check: Joan of Arc (joan-of-arc)

Checker run 2026-10-01. The earlier checker run left no `check.md` and no visible edits, so all fixes below are new. Originals are kept as `biography.html.before-check`, `summary.txt.before-check`, and `entry.json.before-check`. Every fix to `biography.html` was applied to `entry.json` (biography, summary, miracles, teachings, quotes, share kit, gaps) so the files stay in sync.

Raw sources used: Murray 1902 (OCR text of the Internet Archive scan), Quicherat vols. 1 to 5 (OCR), the Latin bull *Divina disponente*, Guérin vol. 15 (local copy), Catholic Encyclopedia, Wikipedia, Nature, EWTN.

## Fixes

1. **Opening scene, day wrong** (biography para 1 to 4; entry.json miracle m24, teachings). Murray p. 16 to 17: at the 24 Feb hearing she said the voice woke her "yesterday morning", so the waking was 23 Feb, and she told the judges the next day. The draft said 24 Feb and "that day she would stand before the court". The new text says 23 Feb, and that she asked the voice how she should answer (her words). Also changed "Two years earlier" to "Only a few years earlier" (she had left Domrémy by early 1429), and "sat in chains" to "a prisoner in irons".
2. **Bull and martyrdom** (last section; miracle m26). The draft said the bull states "at the end she asked for martyrdom". The bull says only that in her last hour she understood what her voices had foretold ("ne sollicita sis et turberis erga martyrium..."). Rewritten.
3. **Same soldier, not two** (death scene; miracles m29, m30). Isambart (Murray p. 191) and the bull make the soldier who swore to bring wood the same man who saw the white dove. The draft had "another English soldier". Fixed.
4. **One-sided claim about her last morning** (last section; Note on the Sources; gaps). The draft ended on "until the last ... her voices ... had not deceived her" (Manchon, via the Catholic Encyclopedia only). Pierre Maurice and Toutmouillé (Murray pp. 149 to 151) report she said "they have deceived me". Both sides are now stated in a few words, and the Manchon quote (not confirmed in Murray's text of his deposition) is replaced with Ladvenu's words from Murray p. 195.
5. **Bull on the king's secret** (Chinon; miracle m08). The bull says the secrets were "Deo tantum nota" (known to God alone), not "God and the king alone". Fixed.
6. **Sign quotation** (Chinon; miracle m09). Murray: "You will not have them from me this year", in answer to what revelations the king had. The draft's "You will not get that from me this year" was not Murray's wording.
7. **Poitiers quotations misattributed** (Poitiers). "I am not come to Poitiers to shew signs" is from Seguin; "I know neither A nor B" and "I am come from the King of Heaven..." are from a different witness (Murray p. 265) describing a visit by two doctors to her lodging. The draft ran them together as one answer to "the men who questioned her". Split and attributed.
8. **Sword at Fierbois** (Tours; miracle m10). Murray p. 28: she sent an armorer of Tours, whom she had never seen, and wrote to the priests; the rust fell off when they rubbed it. The draft said she had never seen the sword and "never saw the man who dug it up". Fixed.
9. **Banner description** (Tours). Used her own description at the trial (white, sprinkled with lilies, the world, an angel on each side; Murray p. 31), not Dunois's.
10. **Letter to the English** (Poitiers). Removed "it did not ask permission" (invention). "never in her letter at all" is now "she said ... had not been in her letter". "Jhesus Maria on her letters" is now "on some", as she said.
11. **Dunois and the archbishop** (Reims chapter). Murray p. 239 to 241: Joan rode between the archbishop and Dunois, the people cried "Noël" at La Ferté and Crépy, and the archbishop asked where she hoped to die ("Where it shall please God..."). The draft had her "speaking to the archbishop" and "something Dunois never forgot" (invented). The sheep quotation was cut short; the Latin ends "with my sister and my brothers, who would be so glad to see me". Completed, here and in `entry.json` quotes.
12. **Vineyard prayer** (Orléans; teachings; share kit). Dunois's Latin ("quasi per spatium dimidii quarti horae") does not give "a quarter of an hour"; Murray says "half an hour". Changed to "a short while" everywhere.
13. **Fall at Jargeau** (Jargeau; miracle m17). D'Alençon says she was thrown to the ground, not into the ditch.
14. **Who said what in the camp** (Jargeau section; Paris section). Swearing: d'Alençon says she scolded him (Murray p. 280, not Louis). Saint-Denis sword: d'Alençon, not Louis. Chastity: the two witnesses are Jean de Metz (Murray p. 225, "carnal desire") and Dunois ("an evil thought"), not d'Aulon and Louis; "for months" removed. A woman slept near her "whenever one could be found" (Coutes).
15. **Reims banner quotation** (Reims). The Catholic Encyclopedia's "as it had shared in the toil ... victory" is a paraphrase. Replaced with our translation of the Latin (Quicherat I, 17 Mar. 1431: "fuerat in poena ... rationabile erat quod esset in honore").
16. **Lagny miracle** (Paris to Melun chapter; miracle m21). Murray p. 52: the child "yawned" (not "gasped"), was "as black as my coat" (not tunic), and she answered "I did not enquire about it". Moved after the Sully letters (Murray's chronological table puts Lagny in April 1430), with "that spring".
17. **Beaurevoir** (Compiègne chapter). Joan said she had heard the people of Compiègne were to be put to fire and sword (not just "suffering"). Her wording "the greatest [sin] I have ever committed" is kept ("worst wrong"; teachings softened to "greatest wrong").
18. **Trial details** (trial chapter). Chains: she complained of being fastened with chains and fetters; the draft had her "in chains" in the chapel. The brother's remark: Joan said her brother told her what people in the neighborhood said, which she denied (the draft said the judges had heard it). "Be quiet" changed to Murray's "Hold your tongue in the devil's name!". "The friars said later" changed to "one of the friars" (Ladvenu).
19. **Abjuration paragraph** (For Fear of the Fire). Fixed a broken sentence. Bull: she said she abjured out of force and fear, without understanding it; eight lines per Massieu.
20. **"An English lord had forced her"** (relapse). Murray and the bull give "insulted" and "violence attempted". Changed to "assaulted her", unquoted.
21. **28 May order** (relapse). Her voices' words and the "damned myself" quotation now come before "All I said and revoked"; the margin note "Responsio mortifera" is attached to the answer it belongs to ("did not intend to deny my apparitions"; Murray p. 137 to 138).
22. **Domrémy details** (Teased for Going to Church). Cut the godparents (Joan and her neighbors name different people). The church: Joan says the principal church is at Greux; now only "dedicated to Saint Rémi". "None of them remembered any vision" narrowed to "None of these neighbors". The fairy sentence now says Joan never saw one "that she knew of". Guillemette's goodbye was said to his father.
23. **Bed for the poor** (Domrémy). Guérin: "often lent her own bed"; attributed to the old Lives.
24. **Mother of the Maid / rehabilitation** (Her Mother at Notre-Dame). "Six years after the fire the English lost Paris" (Catholic Encyclopedia: 12 Nov. 1437; Murray's note: 1436) changed to "Within the seven years she had named". "Early the next year" for Isambart and Ladvenu removed (only the researcher's inference). Dupanloup was the bishop of Orléans, not "a preacher" (Catholic Encyclopedia, Wikipedia).
25. **Orléans procession** (8 May). "Every year ... until 1920" contradicted the 1793 suspension later in the text. Now "kept 8 May ... through the centuries".
26. **Capture and prophecy** (Compiègne). "As she had told the king she would" was the writer's link; now attributed to the old Lives. 23 May kept (Guérin, Bourgeois, Cagny, Murray's table; the bull and Catholic Encyclopedia say 24 May; the Note on the Sources says so).
27. **Thérèse of Lisieux** (canonization chapter). The draft said she wrote "prayers for her beatification". The library volume (de Teil 1913, p. 90) says a great part of her verses were about Joan and she prayed ardently for the beatification. Reworded; source added to the list (title taken from the running head; author unconfirmed).
28. **Relics paragraph.** "Had never been burned" to "had not been burned" (Nature). Diocese of Tours statement kept; the Nature disagreement is now in the Note on the Sources.
29. **Louis de Coutes on meals** (Orléans). He says twice a day, and "many times" only a morsel of bread.
30. **Small softenings.** "a sentence that still stops readers" (invented reader reaction) cut. "Charles refused to receive her at all" to "would not receive her". "the only sign they needed" to "what sign they needed". "Orléans never forgot her" to "kept her memory". "nothing is left to venerate" to "no relic is known". "often wrong" (Cagny dates) to "sometimes wrong". "Ridden at the head of an army" to "ridden with an army".
31. **Note on the Sources and How This Was Researched.** Source count now matches the list (twelve). "All five volumes" of Quicherat softened (volume 1 was only searched). "Seldom or never found in popular English accounts" (an untested judgment in the notes) now "did not expect most popular accounts to include". More than twenty is a count of the NEW tags (26). Added the source disputes found above.
32. **Summary.** "Led the army" to "rode with the army"; "carrying her banner instead of a sword so that she would never kill anyone" to "so that, as she said, she would not kill anyone". Also in the share kit.
33. **entry.json only.** Miracles: m14 note on 27 May reworded; m25 gives both dates for Paris; m31 inquiry date ("1449 to 1450"). Teachings: "never claimed" and "would not kill" softened to what she said; "not ashamed of it" (unsupported) cut. Quote sources: page and date corrected for "Never have I cursed" (3 Mar., p. 54, was p. 74), "They love what God loves" (17 Mar., p. 88, was "about p. 86"), "forty times better" (27 Feb., p. 32, was p. 31). Gaps list rewritten.

## Checked and confirmed (no change)

- Birth: 6 Jan. 1412 (Boulainvilliers) vs 6 Feb. 1412 (bull); the text says only "about nineteen"; `birth_year` null.
- Place: Domrémy (with Greux); Old Market, Rouen.
- Coronation at Reims 17 July 1429 (Catholic Encyclopedia; also the date of her letter to Burgundy, Quicherat V).
- Relief of Orléans: Les Tourelles 7 May, English leave 8 May 1429. Wound date: Dunois's printed Latin reads "27 May"; Murray corrects to 7 May in a note; the Note on the Sources explains it.
- Capture 23 May 1430 (see 26). Trial: 21, 22, 24, 27 Feb.; 1, 3, 17 Mar.; abjuration 24 May; 28 May; relapse 29 May; death 30 May 1431.
- Rehabilitation: 11 June 1455 judges named; 7 Nov. 1455 opening; 7 July 1456 sentence (bull). Beatification 18 Apr. 1909, canonization 16 May 1920, feast 30 May, patron 2 Mar. 1922 (bull, Wikipedia).
- The five cures (names, illnesses, 1908 and 1918 decrees) match the bull word for word: Thérèse of Saint Augustine (stomach ulceration), Julie Gauthier (ulcer of the left breast), Jeanne Marie Sagnier (tubercular bone disease), Maria Antonia Mirandelle (perforating disease of the sole), Thérèse Belin (peritoneal and pulmonary tuberculosis, mitral valve lesion). The bull gives no dates or places; the text does not.
- Chinon jar: rib, wood, linen, cat femur; 1867 Paris pharmacy; 2007 Charlier; Egyptian mummy; Tours statement. The carbon-14 date (3rd to 6th century BC, in Nature) is not used.
- Every block quotation compared with Murray's OCR text (trial answers, first letter) or Quicherat (witness words; our translations of the Latin or French): 11 blocks, all confirmed. The lint flags remaining are only two long sentences.
- Absolute claims searched ("never", "no one", "only", "first", "always", "all"): each is her own words, a named witness's words, or now softened.
