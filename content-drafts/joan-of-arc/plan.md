# Plan: St. Joan of Arc

## The driving question

What did the girl from Domrémy hear in her father's garden, and would she keep faith with it once it stopped bringing victories? At thirteen a Voice told a farm girl who "knew nothing of riding or fighting" to raise the siege of Orléans. She asked it for one reward only, "the salvation of my soul," and in the summer of her triumph she told the archbishop that she wished God would let her put down her arms and go home to keep her parents' sheep. Obedience took her to Chinon, to Orléans, to Reims, and then to capture, a prison guarded by English soldiers, and a court that wanted her to say the Voices were false. Once, at Saint-Ouen, the fear of the fire broke her and she signed. Four days later she took it back: "I have damned myself to save my life." She died at the Old Market with a cross held before her eyes, calling the name of Jesus.

The hook raises the question (the Voice wakes her in her cell and tells her to answer boldly; that day her judges ask whether she is in the grace of God). The Saint-Ouen and relapse chapter is the crisis. The death chapter resolves it, and the last chapter closes the loop: the Church that condemned her in 1431 annulled the verdict in 1456 and canonized her in 1920, and the notary of her trial remembered that to the end she said her Voices had not deceived her.

## Why famous, and what set her apart

Famous: the peasant girl of about seventeen who led the army that broke the siege of Orléans, brought her king to be crowned at Reims, and was burned at Rouen at about nineteen.
Set apart: she could not read, yet theologians at Poitiers and at Rouen could not shake her, and her captains said she handled an army as if she had twenty or thirty years of war behind her. She carried her banner so as not to kill anyone, wept over French blood, and held a dying English prisoner's head while he confessed.

## The hook

Type: scene at the edge plus her own words. Rouen, 24 February 1431, before dawn: the Voice wakes her; she sits on her bed, joins her hands, and hears "Answer boldly; God will help thee." That day the judges ask whether she is in the grace of God. "If I am not, may God place me there; if I am, may God so keep me." Thesis: an illiterate girl of about nineteen who two years earlier had ridden at the head of an army; three months to live. Answered in chapters 14–15; closed in chapter 17.

## Open loops

1. The grace question (hook): answered by the relapse and death, closed in chapter 17.
2. The sheep and the burial wish (chapter 10): she was never buried; ashes thrown into the Seine (chapter 15).
3. "A year and not much more" (chapter 8): fulfilled at Compiègne (chapter 12).
4. The sign at Chinon (chapter 5): she refuses to tell it, then tells it as the allegory of the crown (chapter 13).
