# Joan of Arc — notes, focus: own words

Researcher: own-words agent. Tier A. Slug: joan-of-arc. Date of work: 2026-10-01.

Method: I read the full text of Murray (S1) with Python-free grep and sed, and read Quicherat vol. 5 (S3) for the letters. I did not verify the trial answers against Quicherat's Latin/French (S2). Every trial quotation below is Murray's English, copied exactly. Murray's page numbers come from the running heads in the OCR text, so treat them as approximate.

## 1. Identity (own-words agent: short)

- Names: Jeanne d'Arc; "Jeannette" at home (she says so, S1 p.6); "la Pucelle" (the Maid); Joan of Arc; Saint Joan of Arc. Surname: she says "Of my surname I know nothing" (S1 p.6).
- Born Domremy (with Greux), baptised there. She gives her age as "about nineteen" on 21 Feb 1431 (S1 p.6). Murray's note gives birth as 6 Jan 1412, citing Boulainvilliers (S1 p.6 n.1). Treat as the tradition; the witness agent should check.
- Tradition type: Catholic. Died 30 May 1431, Old Market, Rouen (S1 p.142).
- Feast, canonization dates: not in my sources. List for church-miracles agent.
- Tier A, focus own-words.

## 3. Timeline (events as she tells or writes them; one line each)

- 1424/25? (age 13), summer, midday, in father's garden: first Voice, "very much frightened". She had not fasted the day before. Voice from the right, toward the church, with light. [S1 p.10] NEW? (popular pages quote "light" but not "had not fasted")
- Her saints: Saint Michael first, "surrounded by the Angels of Heaven"; later Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret, recognised "by the greeting they give me" [S1 pp.23-25].
- Feb 1429 (Murray's note: 12 Feb): Vaucouleurs. Baudricourt refused her twice, received her the third time. She "recognized" him though she had never seen him [S1 p.11].
- Feb-Mar 1429: Poitiers examination. Witness: "I am not come to Poitiers to shew signs" [S1 p.307, witness Seguin de Seguin, 1455-6]. NEW in this form.
- 8 Mar to 20 Apr 1429: lodged in the Coudray tower at Chinon, per Murray's note [S1 p.13 n.1]. She says she knew the King among the crowd "by the counsel of my Voice" [S1 p.13].
- 22 Mar 1429 (Quicherat's date; Murray prints "March 22nd, 1428", old-style year): letter to the English [S1 pp.36-38; S3 pp.95-97].
- 25 Jun 1429: letter from Gien to the loyal French of Tournai [S3 pp.124-125].
- 17 Jul 1429: letter from Reims to the Duke of Burgundy, on coronation day [S3 pp.126-127].
- 8 Sep 1429: wounded at Paris; she says it healed in five days [S1 p.14].
- Mar 1430: letters from Sully: to the Hussites (3 Mar) and to Reims (16 and 28 Mar) [S3 pp.156-161].
- Autumn 1430: leap from Beaurevoir tower. Reasons in her own words (Compiegne; sold to the English) [S1 pp.74-75].
- 21 Feb 1431: first public hearing in Rouen castle chapel. She refuses to say the Pater except in confession [S1 p.7].
- 22 Feb to 3 Mar 1431: public hearings; sign of the angel and crown told as allegory in March private hearings [S1 pp.60-73].
- 24 May 1431: abjuration on the Saint-Ouen scaffold (S1 pp.121-135, not read in detail).
- 28 May 1431: she says she resumed man's dress. Her answers about Voices, "damned myself to save my life" [S1 pp.135-138].
- 30 May 1431: death at the stake. Deponents: Ladvenu, Isambart de la Pierre, Toutmouillé, Massieu, Leparmentier, Guesdon, Ricquier, Marguerie [S1 pp.158-163, 175-176, 208, 300-302, 305]. Note: these are rehabilitation depositions of 1449-56, not the 1431 record.

## 4. Character (as shown by her own words)

- Quick, dry wit under pressure. "You burden me over-much!" when pressed to swear a third time [S1 p.9].
- Proud of a plain skill: "in sewing and spinning I fear no woman in Rouen" [S1 p.9].
- Stubborn on one point: she will not swear to tell all, and says so with an oath-lawyer's care: she will tell "as much as I would to the Pope of Rome" about the case [S1 p.33].
- Sharp memory. Witness (Manchon, Rouen 1455-6): when a notary said she had not answered a point of eight days before, the records were read and she was right; she said she would pull his ears if he erred again [S1 p.305].
- Honest about a wrong: she names the leap from Beaurevoir as her gravest fault and asked pardon of God [S1 pp.74-75; also S1 line ~5356 of text, "the leap from the Tower of Beaurevoir ... besought their pardon"].
- Fear and courage. She weeps at the news of the fire (Toutmouillé, 1449) and later is steady at the stake (many deponents). Both are in the sources. [S1 pp.158, 301]
- Does not swear. "Never have I cursed any of the Saints; and it is not my habit to swear" [S1 p.74].
- Dislike of killing: she carried the banner "to save killing any one, for I have never killed any one" [S1 p.31].
- Critics: Bishop Cauchon says "diabolical obstinacy" (sentence, S1 p.143). The rehabilitation witnesses speak of pity.
- Fairness note: Murray's footnotes are 1902 opinion (for example that the bishop's claim of jurisdiction "is distinctly untrue", S1 p.4 n.1). Treat as Murray's view.

## 5. Quotations (25 max). Exact Murray text unless marked. Rights: public-domain unless marked.

Voices

1. "I was thirteen when I had a Voice from God for my help and guidance. The first time that I heard this Voice, I was very much frightened; it was mid-day, in the summer, in my father's garden." Trial, 22 Feb 1431. [S1 p.10]
2. "I heard this Voice to my right, towards the Church; rarely do I hear it without its being accompanied also by a light." [S1 p.10]
3. "It said to me: 'Go, raise the siege which is being made before the City of Orleans.'" [S1 pp.10-11]
4. "I replied that I was but a poor girl, who knew nothing of riding or fighting." [S1 p.11]
5. "There is not a day when I do not hear this Voice; and I have much need of it. But never have I asked of it any recompense but the salvation of my soul." [S1 p.13]
6. "I was asleep: the Voice awoke me." And: "I was sitting on the bed; I joined my hands; I implored its help. The Voice said to me: 'Answer boldly; God will help thee.'" 24 Feb. [S1 pp.16-17]
7. "There is a saying among children, that 'Sometimes one is hanged for speaking the truth.'" 24 Feb. [S1 p.18]
8. "If I am not, may God place me there; if I am, may God so keep me." Asked if she was in the grace of God. [S1 p.18] (the full answer is longer; use the whole)
9. "I saw them with my bodily eyes as well as I see you; when they went from me, I wept. I should have liked to be taken away with them." On Saint Michael and the angels, 27 Feb. [S1 p.25]
10. "It is well to know, they smelled good." (Saints Catherine and Margaret, embraced.) And: "It is more proper and respectful to kiss their feet." [S1 p.92]
11. "I would rather have been torn asunder by four horses than have come into France without God's leave." [S1 p.26]
12. "They love what God loves: they hate what God hates." On whether the saints hate the English. [S1 p.(near text line 5513, p.~86)]. Exact page to check.

The sign (the crown)

13. "It was beautiful, honourable, and most credible; the best and richest in the world." [S1 p.59-60]
14. "No man in the world could devise so rich a thing as this sign; but the sign that you need is that God may deliver me from your hands; that is the most sure sign He could send you." Allegory later explained by Joan as her own mission (Murray's note, S1 p.61 n.1; not verified against her last-day words). [S1 p.60]
15. "Yes; I made a salutation, knelt down, and took off my cap." On the angel. [S1 p.61]
16. "He left me in that little Chapel. I was much vexed at his going; I wept; willingly would I have gone with him — that is to say, my soul." [S1 p.72]
17. "Yes, it smelt good, and will smell good always, if it be well guarded, as it should be." (the crown) [S1 p.72]

Letters (originals)

18. English letter, 22 Mar 1429, Quicherat's text of the copy: "Roy d'Angleterre, ... rendés à la Pucelle cy envoiée de par Dieu le roy du ciel, les clefs de toutes les bonnes villes que vous avés prises et violées en France." Murray: "give up to the Maid, who is sent hither by God, the King of Heaven, the keys of all the good towns in France." [S3 p.96; S1 p.37]. She says at trial that "rendez à la Pucelle" should be "rendez au Roy", and that "Chieftain of war" and "body for body" were not in her letter [S1 p.38; and S1 p.12]. S3 notes the words are in three early French chronicles.
19. Tournai, 25 Jun 1429: "Maintenés vous bien loiaux Franchois, je vous en pry, et vous pry et vous requiers que vous soiës tous prestz de venir au sacre du gentil roy Charles à Rains." [S3 p.125]
20. To the Duke of Burgundy, Reims, 17 Jul 1429: "et s'il vous plaist à guerroier, si alez sur les Sarrazins." And: "tous ceulx qui guerroient oudit saint royaume de France, guerroient contre le roy Jhesus, roy du ciel et de tout le monde, mon droicturier et souverain seigneur." [S3 pp.126-127]
21. To Reims, 16 Mar 1430, from Sully: "je les feray chausser leurs espérons si à aste qu'il ne sauront por ho les prendre" ("I will make them put on their spurs so fast they will not know how to catch them"), and "mès que soyez toutjours bons et loyals." Also: "Je vous mandesse anquores auguunes nouvelles de quoy vous sériés bien joyeux; mais je double que les letres ne fussent prises en chemin." [S3 p.160]. OCR of the spelling: check against a clean scan.
22. To the Hussites, "3 mars" 1430 (header OCR reads 1480; context 1430), from Sully: "Êtes-vous donc tout à fait enragés! Quelle fureur insensée vous possède?" This is Quicherat's French rendering of a German version. No Latin or French original survives. Quicherat says it was "composée par un clerc" and cannot be compared with her other letters. [S3 pp.156-159]. Use as a minor note. Its Joan-voice is doubtful.

Rouen, last days

23. "I would rather die than be in irons! but if I am allowed to go to Mass, and am taken out of irons and put into a gracious prison ... I will be good, and do as the Church wills." 28 May. [S1 p.136]
24. "All I said and revoked, I said for fear of the fire." And: "I have damned myself to save my life!" (what the Voices said). The registrar wrote in the margin "Responsio mortifera." [S1 p.137]
25. "Bishop, I die through you." Toutmouillé, 1449 deposition on the morning of 30 May; Isambart gives "Alas! I die through you, for had you given me over to be kept in the prisons of the Church, I should not have been here!" [S1 pp.158-159, 163]

Other lines I kept in reserve (not counted above; swap in if wanted):
- "Better, forty times better, my banner than my sword!" and "to save killing any one, for I have never killed any one." [S1 p.31]
- "I know neither A nor B." Rehabilitation witness at Poitiers [S1 p.265ff (deposition of Jean Erault or Seguin), text line 13390].
- "I am come from the King of Heaven to raise the siege of Orleans and to conduct the King to Rheims for his crowning and anointing." (same witness) 
- "You may well ask me some things on which I shall tell you the truth and some on which I shall not tell it you. If you were well informed about me, you would wish to have me out of your hands. I have done nothing except by revelation." [S1 p.9]
- "Is not this lawful for all prisoners?" when told she wished to escape: "It is true I wished to escape; and so I wish still" [S1 p.7].
- "What concerns this dress is a small thing — less than nothing." [S1 p.26]
- "I had rather die than be in the hands of my enemies, the English." and "I would rather have died than live after such a destruction of good people." (Compiegne) [S1 p.74]
- "Maitre Pierre, where shall I be this evening?" Her question to Pierre Maurice on the morning of death; he asked if she had good hope; she said God willing she would be in Paradise (Ricquier's report of Maurice, not direct) [S1 p.302].

## 6. Scenes (10 max)

1. Rouen, 21 Feb 1431, chapel. Forty-two assessors, chains on her. She will not say the Pater unless the bishop hears her confession. [S1 p.7]
2. 22 Feb. Told to swear a third time: "I made oath to you yesterday ... you burden me over-much!" [S1 p.9]
3. 24 Feb. Dawn in her cell: the Voice wakes her, no touch. "Answer boldly." She tells the bishop that he puts himself in great danger. [S1 pp.14-17]
4. The Fairies' Tree: the old women say the fairies haunt it; she says she sang there more than she danced, and has "given myself up as little as possible to these games" since her mission [S1 pp.20-21]. NEW to many English pages: her brother's remark "Jeannette received her mission at the Fairies' Tree" and her denial.
5. The Chinon night: the angel goes first into the King's chamber, then Joan: "Sire, there is your sign; take it." (allegorical, her own account of 1431) [S1 p.71]
6. Beaurevoir: the saints say do not leap; she leaps; after, she cannot eat or drink for days; Catherine tells her to confess [S1 pp.74-75].
7. Morning of 30 May: Ladvenu tells her of the fire. She tears her hair and cries out she would rather have her head cut off seven times than be burned (Toutmouillé, 1449) [S1 p.158].
8. The cross: an Englishman breaks a stick into a small cross; she kisses it and puts it in her bosom; then she begs for the church cross to hold before her eyes (Ladvenu, Isambart) [S1 pp.161, 175-176].
9. At the pyre: "Do thy duty" said to the executioner; she cries "Jesus!" more than six times and again with her last breath (Leparmentier) [S1 pp.161, 301].
10. An English soldier who had sworn to bring a faggot is stunned at her cry of "Jesus" and later confesses; the executioner says he fears he is damned (Isambart) [S1 pp.162, 191]. Mark: report of a witness years later.

## 7. Miracles

This is not my focus. Items found in the text of S1, for the church-miracles agent:
- Heart not burned. Isambart (1449): the executioner said the heart and entrails would not burn despite oil, sulphur, and charcoal, "as a most evident miracle" [S1 pp.161-162]. Status: recorded testimony at the rehabilitation, popular.
- White dove. English soldier saw "the spirit departing from her, as it were a white dove, going away from France" [S1 p.191]. Status: popular, reported by Isambart.
- Her horse stood still at the town cross (Laval letter, 1429) [S1 p.30 n.1]. Status: popular, letter of the Laval brothers, June 1429.

## 8. Other

- Calendar conflicts: Murray prints the English letter date as "March 22nd, 1428" (year starts at Easter, old style); Quicherat gives 22 mars 1429 (S3 p.95). Use 1429.
- Hussite letter: survives only in German; dated 3 March (1430 in context); flag as doubtful in voice. Quicherat vol. 4 (p.503) holds Johannes Nider's report of it (not read).
- A "Book of Poitiers" with her examination in March-April 1429 is lost (S1 p.24 n.).
- Murray's trial translation follows the French "Minute" and the Latin "Text" and marks differences in notes. Quote from Murray and mark that Quicherat's French original has not been compared.
- Seals and crosses: she says she put "Jhesus Maria" and sometimes a cross on her letters; the cross was a sign for her side not to do what the letter said [S1 p.36]. Image candidate: this sign on the letter to the Burgundian duke ("+ Jhesus Maria", S3 p.126).
- Image candidates in S1: the Fouquet miniature "Court of Justice" (S1, near p.142); photographs of Chinon Coudray tower room and the Cauchon palace gate (in S1 plates, titles seen in OCR).
- Orthodox note: none. Catholic only.
- Not read: Joan's signature letter to Troyes, the Riom letter, and the surviving autograph "Jhesus Maria" letters other than the Reims copy; Perceval de Cagny and Journal du siège (S3 refers); the abjuration formula; the Barrett and Pernoud translations (rights unknown, treat as facts-only).
