## The driving question

Augustine wanted rest: happiness, truth, a love that would not die. He looked for it everywhere outside himself (in friends, love affairs, ambition, eloquence, the Manichees) and found that the thing in his way was his own divided will: he asked God to make him chaste, "only not yet", and feared that God would hear him too soon. Grace broke the deadlock in a garden in Milan, and the rest of his life turned that restless searcher into a man who gave his own rest away: seized for the priesthood in tears, made a bishop against his will, and dying in a besieged city with the penitential psalms on his wall.

The hook raises the question (how does a man who is afraid of his own prayer being answered become the teacher of the whole Western Church?). The garden chapter answers the first half. The ending answers the whole: the heart he called restless "until it repose in Thee" goes to its rest in the third month of the siege, with nothing to leave but his books.

## Why famous, and what set him apart

Famous: he wrote the Confessions and the City of God and is one of the four great Latin Doctors of the Church.

Set apart: he told the story of his own sins and his own conversion as a prayer to God, without disguise, at a time when no one wrote that way. And he kept his restless mind to the end: as a bishop he judged lawsuits, fed his clergy at a plain table, argued with heretics in public, and still wrote that two loves build two cities.

## The hook

His own words: the young man's prayer "Give me chastity and continency, only not yet", and his fear that God would hear him soon. Then the contrast: the same man is now honored by the Church as one of its four great Latin Doctors, and his book of confessions is still read. How did that happen? Answered in "Take Up and Read" (the will is healed) and in "The Third Month of the Siege" (the rest is reached).

## Chapters

1. **The Prayer He Feared Answered** (hook, thesis). Quote: "only not yet". Pull: it began in a small town in Numidia.
2. **A Small Town in Numidia.** Birth, parents, boyhood illness and delayed baptism; the pears at sixteen. Quote: the pears (Pusey). Pull: Carthage.
3. **A Cauldron at Carthage.** Hortensius at nineteen, the concubine and Adeodatus, nine years a Manichee hearer, the death of the friend: "half of my soul". Pull: Monica will not give up.
4. **The Son of These Tears.** Monica's dream and her quick answer; the bishop's line; Faustus disappoints; the lie at the harbor and the night sailing. Quote: the bishop. Pull: Rome fails him; Milan.
5. **A Bishop Reading in Silence.** Ambrose; the dismissal of the mother of his son; "not yet" in full. Quote: Ambrose reading. Pull: Ponticianus arrives.
6. **Take Up and Read.** The garden scene. Quote: "tolle lege". Pull: they go in to tell Monica.
7. **A Window at Ostia.** Toothache, baptism at Easter 387, hymns, the window, Monica's death. Quotes: hymns; "Lay this body any where". Pull: home to Africa.
8. **Seized in the Church at Hippo.** Three years at Tagaste, Hippo 391, the tears misread, Letter 21, the monastery, Fortunatus. Quote: Letter 21. Pull: Valerius hides him.
9. **A Bishop at a Plain Table.** Consecration under compulsion; daily life of the bishop: lawsuits, table verse, money, the returned deed. Quote: the table verse. Pull: the roads are not safe.
10. **Enemies on the Road.** Donatists, the wrong road, Crispinus; Firmus at his feet; the Pelagians. Pull: relics arrive at Hippo.
11. **Seventy Miracles in Hippo.** Stephen's relics; Innocentius at Carthage remembered; Paulus and Palladia. Quote: "What am I to do?" Pull: Rome falls.
12. **Two Loves, Two Cities.** 410, City of God, sing and walk. Quotes: two cities; canta et ambula. Pull: the Vandals cross from Spain.
13. **The Third Month of the Siege.** The prayer at table, fever, the healing, the psalms on the wall, death, no will. Quote: the siege prayer. Pull: what became of him.
14. **A Box Under the Altar at Pavia.** Hippo burned; relics to Sardinia (contested), Pavia, 1695, 1728, Annaba; Doctor 1298; ending on "Too late loved I Thee" and the restless heart.

## Open loops

1. "Not yet": planted in the hook, paid off in the garden (chapter 6).
2. "The son of these tears should perish": planted in chapter 4, answered at Monica's death (chapter 7).
3. Tears: Augustine weeps at every turn (ordination, the siege, the psalms); the meaning is answered at the end.
4. The restless heart: first line of the Confessions, raised in chapter 1, resolved in chapters 13-14.
