## Fixes in biography.html

1. "Possidius ... 'intent, hanging and fixed'" (ch. "A Bishop Reading in Silence"): not in Weiskotten (his English is "in eager suspense"; the Latin is "intendebat suspensus atque affixus"). Quote replaced with "in eager suspense". "Ambrose answered them one by one" replaced with Possidius's wording (the questions of the Law were solved).
2. "I entered into my inmost self, with you as my guide": own translation presented as if in the Milan silence scene. Now Pusey's "I entered even into my inward self, Thou being my Guide". Cut the invented "something of that silence was beginning in Augustine too".
3. Garden scene: "Alypius followed him" was wrong. In Pusey, Augustine left Alypius sitting and went off alone to weep. Fixed.
4. Monica and the bishop: "stung by her tears" changed to Pusey's "a little displeased at her importunity". "Professional debater" changed to "made his living with words".
5. Monica "Nothing is far to God": Pusey says Augustine heard of it afterward, when he was away. Added.
6. Patricius (conflict, in text): "He was a pagan" stated flatly. Now "As Augustine tells it, he did not yet share his wife's faith, and he was quick to anger" (Conf. I and IX.9), with Butler's phrase attributed. Late baptism now attributed to Butler and Weiskotten.
7. Consecration conflict, Source note: it said Weiskotten dates the consecration "to 391 and to 395-396". Weiskotten gives 391 (Megalius, coadjutor) and 396-397 for the succession; "395-396" was the writer's. Rewritten to say exactly that, with the Catholic Encyclopedia's 396.
8. "He never defended his own case": unsupported. Possidius ch. 8: he later said and wrote it was wrong, and worked to have councils require that ordaining bishops make the rules known. Fixed.
9. Judge saying (ch. 19): the remark about strangers and friends was "a certain one's", not Augustine's. Fixed.
10. Ambrose's three rules (ch. 27): Possidius says Augustine said these ought to be observed, not that he "kept" them; the third is "a feast in his own community", not "a banquet in one's own city". Fixed.
11. "Never spoke with a woman except with clergy present": attributed to Possidius.
12. Ambushed bishop (ch. 12): "beaten and robbed by them" and "fellow bishop". Now: a bishop Augustine had sent out from his monastery; baggage taken; badly hurt. "He never reached" the ambush changed to "they failed to catch him".
13. Crispinus (ch. 12): wrong on two points. The fine was incurred as a heretic, and he was judged one in debate. The later effort was to lift the condemnation of all Donatists through the emperor, not "to ease the penalty for Crispinus himself". Fixed here and in teachings (entry.json).
14. "He added a sentence that sums up how the friends understood it": that was Possidius's own sentence. Fixed.
15. Table verse "cut into" the table: Possidius says only "inscription". Changed to "inscribed" in biography, summary, entry.json.
16. Sermo 256: removed "advice of a man who had done all three" (invented moralizing).
17. Absolutes: "He never stopped asking questions" softened; "first biographer" cut; "largest of his books" now "one of the greatest"; "the most famous of the Hippo cures" now Augustine's own claim (no inhabitant of Hippo failed to see or hear of it); "best-known story about him today" now "a well-known story".
18. Heading "Seventy Miracles" changed to "Almost Seventy Miracles in Two Years" (City of God XXII.8: "almost seventy").
19. Florentius: he prayed "to the Twenty Martyrs, whose shrine stands in Hippo", not "at the shrine" (also in the miracle list).
20. Death date: "the date given in the chronicle of Prosper" changed to "by the traditional date". Source: Weiskotten's note (Intro n. 1) says Prosper gives 28 Aug 430. Prosper was not read. The Source note already names Weiskotten.
21. Relics: Pavia, custody. "Friars given their care" was wrong. Sciberras: in 1327 the Hermits joined the canons who already served the church. Sardinia now attributed (Butler with Bede and Oldrad; Sciberras says no reliable document). Pavia move "in the 720s" (Butler about 720; Sciberras 721-725). John Paul II 2004: relics were brought to Rome. Annaba statue attributed to Wikipedia.
22. Orthodox feast 15 June: now attributed to OrthodoxWiki (it says his Orthodox calendar status is uncertain). OrthodoxWiki added to Sources. Source note says the feast and patronages rest on OrthodoxWiki and Wikipedia only. Siege miracle marked contested in the Source note.
23. How This Life Was Researched: "thirteen details they did not find on popular English pages" removed (the notes mark 14 "NEW" and never surveyed popular pages). "nineteen sources" now "twenty" (OrthodoxWiki added).
24. Lint: removed "journey" (AI-tell list) and shortened three long sentences. No MUST FIX lines; 10 CHECK lines (long sentences, narrative rhythm). 8,318 words.
