# Thérèse of Lisieux — notes: church-miracles
Focus: Church documents, cause, relics, patronage, cult after death, every miracle. Tier A. Tradition: Catholic only (Western; also honored in Eastern Catholic rites, S1 §2; Episcopal/Anglican feast since 2022, S4).

## Identity (short; the identity agent owns the full list)
- Marie-Françoise-Thérèse Martin; b. Alençon 2 Jan 1873; d. Lisieux 30 Sep 1897 [S1 §5].
- Religious name: Thérèse of the Child Jesus and the Holy Face (OCD) [S1].
- Feast: 1 Oct (General Roman Calendar since 1969). Earlier 3 Oct (added 1927). Canonization decree set 30 Sep as her dies natalis [S3 p.7; S4].
- Titles: Virgin; Doctor of the Church (1997); "Little Flower".

## Timeline (cause, relics, cult)
- 1883, Pentecost (13 May): Thérèse, age 10, sick for weeks, sees the statue of Mary smile; she is healed. She calls it a miracle of Our Lady of Victories. Her father had paid for a novena of Masses at Notre-Dame des Victoires, Paris. JPII lists it [S1 §5]; Taylor quotes her [S3]. Detail: she failed to recognize her sister Marie at first. Not a cause miracle.
- 17 Jul 1897: she says "I will spend my Heaven in doing good upon earth." Taylor dates it [S3 p.231 n.]. Variants: "I will let fall a shower of roses", said also to novices [S3; S4].
- 30 Sep 1897: she dies. Same night, as the community tells it: a lay sister kisses her feet and is cured of "cerebral anaemia"; one nun smells violets; a child of ten smells lilies, though only paper lilies stand there. NEW (popular English pages skip these). Status: recorded by the Carmel, not investigated [S3 pp.240–41].
- 4 Oct 1897: burial in the municipal cemetery of Lisieux, in the new Carmelite plot. She is the first Carmelite buried there [S3; S4]. A palm branch is put in her hand.
- 1898–1899: *Story of a Soul* printed. Over a million French copies by 1926. English version by Dziewicki 1901; Taylor's abridgment of 1911 reaches 175,000 copies by 1926 [S3 Part III; S1 §3]. First English edition of *Soeur Thérèse*, Christmas 1912, 9,000 copies.
- c.1898–1910: pilgrims to the grave, about 80,000 a year (Taylor's figure). Some go barefoot. A saying: "like praying at Lourdes". The Carmel keeps "a selection" of favours that fills seven volumes. [S3 p.247–48]. Status: popular and Carmel records.
- Early 1909: Fr Rodrigo of St Francis of Paula OCD becomes postulator. Mgr Roger de Teil is vice-postulator in Paris [S3].
- Mar 1910: Bishop Lemonnier of Bayeux and Lisieux orders a search for her writings [S3 p.244].
- Jul 1910: he sets up the diocesan tribunal. Sessions start 12 Aug 1910 [S3]. Witnesses: 11 from the Carmel (9 lived with her) and 26 others, 16 of them eyewitnesses. Taylor says he testified right after Mother Agnes (Pauline).
- 6 Sep 1910: first exhumation, before the tribunal. Only bones remain; the palm branch is fresh and whole. The remains go to a cemented vault in the middle of the plot [S3 pp.241, 245]. Legend/popular: the night before, Thérèse tells Mother Carmela, prioress at Gallipoli (Italy): "They will find only my bones" [S3 p.245]. NEW.
- 1911: the "non-cult" process (proof that no public cult came before the Church's ruling). Documents reach the Congregation of Rites in Feb 1912 [S3 p.246].
- 10 Jun 1914: Pius X signs the decree introducing the cause [S1 §2; S3; S4]. Taylor and S3 papal text give 9 Jun in one place: conflict, see Other.
- 1915–1917: apostolic process at Bayeux. 10 Aug 1917: second exhumation (canon law). Two medical experts identify the bones. Private by plan, but thousands come. Bones go into an oak chest inside a lead-lined rosewood chest. 30 Oct 1917: 91st session. Record: 2,500 pages, 2,000 on heroic virtue [S3 pp.248–49].
- 10 Dec 1918: Rome confirms the documents are valid. Benedict XV then waives the usual 50-year wait [S3; S4].
- WWI (1914–18): soldiers on the front petition for her beatification; she is the "little sister of the trenches" [S3 p.247]. Petitions from England and Canada stand out. Benedict XV says English-speaking devotion shows "the finger of God" (as Mgr de Teil reports) [S3 p.245].
- 14 Aug 1921: Benedict XV reads the decree of heroic virtue (Venerable). He gives a speech on the way of spiritual childhood [S1 §2; S3 p.249].
- 30 Jan 1923: the general congregation on the miracles meets with the Pope present [S3]. 11 Feb 1923 (Lourdes feast): Pius XI promulgates the approval of the two miracles. 19 Mar 1923: *de tuto* decree [S3 pp.251–52].
- 25 Mar 1923: relics move from the cemetery to the Carmel. Over 200 priests, 50,000 pilgrims. When the vault opens, a scent of roses fills the air (Taylor, popular report) [S3 p.252].
- 29 Apr 1923: beatification by Pius XI, the first he beatifies [S1 §2; S3].
- 4–6 Oct 1923: triduum at Notre-Dame des Victoires, Paris [S2 foreword].
- 17 May 1925 (feast of St Paschal Baylon, per S3): canonization by Pius XI in St Peter's, 28 years after death; first canonization of a daughter of St Teresa of Avila (S3 says first Carmelite daughter to be canonized; check wording). Feast of St Paschal. About 60,000 inside, 500,000 in the square by evening [S4]. Taylor: 33 cardinals, about 200 prelates, about 4,000 in the procession; a wireless relay of the Pope's voice, a first at a canonization; the first time applause broke out after the formula [S3 pp.6–9]. Decree enters her in the catalogue on 30 Sep. 5 years and a day after Joan of Arc [S4]. Pius XI revives the old torches and oil lamps on the dome. Vivid: the night facade is "a giant rose of fire" (Taylor).
- 17 May 1925, during the homily: five roses from a cluster of lights in the apse fall and land at the Pope's feet (Taylor) [S3 p.10]. Status: eyewitness report by an attendee, popular.
- 18 May 1925: French pilgrims meet Pius XI under Mgr Lemonnier. The Pope speaks of a "deluge of glory" on Lisieux and of his "dear little Saint". He talks over an hour with Cardinal Dougherty about his "guiding star" [S3 pp.11–12].
- Jul 1925: Carmel holds its thanksgiving triduum. 30 Sep 1925: first feast in Lisieux. Papal legate Cardinal Vico puts a golden rose (blessed by the Pope) in the hand of the effigy. Reliquary is carried through rose-hung streets. An aviator showers roses over the procession. Cardinal Bourne and Cardinal Dougherty attend. [S3 pp.12–14].
- 4 Oct 1925: feast at Carfin, Scotland, 30,000 pilgrims [S3 p.14–15].
- 1927: feast added to the General Roman Calendar for 3 Oct [S4]. 14 Dec 1927: Pius XI names her patron of the missions with St Francis Xavier, at the request of missionary bishops [S1 §2].
- Pius XI also makes her co-patron of Sant'Antonio Abate all'Esquilino (Rome), the Russian Catholic church, with a Slavic-rite feast on 17 May. Hence "patron of Russian Catholics" [S4]. Check in Carmel source.
- 1929: Lisieux basilica begins (Cordonnier family). Crypt done 1932. 11 Jul 1937: Cardinal Pacelli blesses the basilica as papal legate. 1944: Pius XII names her co-patron of France with Joan of Arc [S4]. Bombing in 1944 destroys two-thirds of Lisieux; the basilica has minor damage [S5]. Consecrated 11 Jul 1951; completed 1954 (S4 says consecrated 1954, S5 says 1951: conflict).
- 2 Jun 1980: John Paul II visits the basilica [S5].
- 1994–: relics tour the world. Ireland and Canada 2001. England and Wales Sep–Oct 2009: a night at York Minster (Anglican) on 1 Oct 2009; a quarter of a million venerate. South Africa Jun–Oct 2010 during the World Cup [S4]. 2008: astronaut Ron Garan carries a relic on Discovery, 31 May–14 Jun [S4].
- 8 Mar 1997: Bishop of Bayeux and Lisieux, the Carmelite Superior General, and the postulator file the petition (Supplex Libellus) for the Doctorate [S1 §4]. 5 May 1997: CDF consultors judge "eminent doctrine". 29 May: Causes consultors examine the Positio. 17 Jun 1997: joint cardinals/bishops session, unanimous. Ratzinger and Bovone inform the Pope. 24 Aug 1997: the Pope announces it at the Angelus at World Youth Day, Paris. 19 Oct 1997 (World Mission Sunday): proclaimed Doctor of the Universal Church in St Peter's Square; the Pope declares it in his own words in the Mass. Fourth woman Doctor (S4 lists Teresa of Avila, Catherine of Siena, Hildegard of Bingen as the others; Hildegard was named after Thérèse, in 2012, so say 'third' at the time: check). Title of Doctor chosen for centenary of death [S1 §§4–5, end; S4].
- 2008–2015: parents Louis and Zélie Martin. Cure of Pietro Schiliro (b. 2002, lung disorder, near Milan), accepted by the Archbishop of Milan in 2004; beatified in Lisieux 19 Oct 2008. Second miracle: Carmen Pérez Pons, premature girl in Valencia, brain hemorrhage, born four days after the beatification; diocesan process 7 Jan–21 May 2013. Canonized 18 Oct 2015: first married couple canonized together [S4]. Sister Léonie: servant of God, diocesan inquiry opened 2 Jul 2015 [S4].

## Miracles (all events)
Beatification (approved 11 Feb 1923, both with three doctors each):
1. 1906, Bayeux seminary. Charles Anne, 23, from Lisieux, seminarian, tuberculosis (cavitary, with hemorrhage). A novena to Our Lady of Lourdes through Thérèse fails. A second novena to Thérèse alone, so her role is clear. After a severe hemorrhage he cries: "I did not come here to die!" He falls asleep holding a relic that "came mysteriously" into his hands, and wakes cured. X-ray 15 years later shows the cure holds (S2). The seminary superior, Fr Duboscq, later is Promoter of the Faith in the cause. Anne attends the 1923 beatification. Status: approved miracle (S2 pp.199, 201–03; S3 pp.249–50).
2. Sep 1916, Ustarritz (Basses-Pyrénées). Sister Aloysia of St Germain (S2; Taylor and the Latin-text decree in S3 say "Louise") of the Daughters of the Cross. Stomach ulcer with hemorrhage from 1913; last sacraments 1915. Novena; a scent comes and goes. Night of 10 Sep: Thérèse appears: "Be courageous, you will be cured, I promise you" (S2; S3 words differ). Morning: rose petals of many colors on the floor by the bed. Cured 22 Sep (S2) or woke cured 25 Sep (S3). X-rays. Report by Dr Le Bec, Paris surgeon. Status: approved miracle. The roses in this case are in a painting in St Peter's (S3 p.9).
Canonization (approved by decree before 17 May 1925):
3. 1923, Parma. Sister Gabriel Trimusi, Sacred Hearts congregation. Since 1913, age 23: left knee swells (she broke firewood on it), then spine. Treated at Mediano and in a hospital with light therapy. Tuberculosis of the knee (periostitis of the tibia on X-ray) and of the dorsal spine. Novena starts 13 Jun 1923 before a small picture. On its last day, at a triduum at the Carmel, she walks there with great pain. That evening she kneels without pain; later she runs from her room crying "I am cured!" Doctors: chronic synovitis and spondylitis, no natural cure. Status: approved. Detail: the Promoter of the cause argued this counts as two miracles (two diseases). The Congregation declined; it accepted the cure as one, but a stronger one (S2 pp.213–17).
4. Mar 1923, Lisieux. Marie Pellemans (Belgian), tuberculosis of lungs and intestines since Oct 1909. Sanatorium La Hulpe; Lourdes in 1920 with no result. Pilgrimage to the Carmel; she kneels at the tomb, invokes Thérèse, is cured at once. Three doctors. Status: approved (S2 p.215). S2 says Mary Pellemans; I give "Marie" as the likely French form: unchecked.
Not in the cause (recorded, popular, or contested):
5. 1883 smile of Mary and healing of Thérèse herself (see timeline). Status: reported by Thérèse; cited in S1 §5 as healing "through the intercession of Our Lady of Victories".
6. Sep 1897: lay sister healed on kissing her feet; scents of violets and lilies (see timeline). Status: Carmel record [S3].
7. Six cures were examined in the cause; two were kept for beatification. The other four are unnamed in S2/S3 [S2 p.199]. Not found.
8. 1906–1916: Taylor reports a torrent of "roses" from 1912 after her word "I am about to let fall a torrent of roses": scents of roses as a sign of her presence. Generic, popular [S3 p.246].
9. 1922, Édith Piaf, age 7: some biographies say she regained sight after a pilgrimage to the grave. Popular legend; only S4 (secondary). Do not state as fact.
10. 1923 conversion letter printed by Fr. Martin (S2 foreword): a man who planned a murder and suicide hears her words "I will spend my Heaven..." and enters Notre-Dame des Victoires (Paris), sees them on the choir wall, repents. Anonymous, printed. Status: popular/recorded testimony [S2 foreword, 4th edition].

## Church recognition and patronage (quick list)
- Venerable 14 Aug 1921; Blessed 29 Apr 1923; Saint 17 May 1925; Patron of missions 14 Dec 1927; Co-patron of France 1944; Doctor 19 Oct 1997.
- Patronage lines (S4): missions, France, Russian Catholics, aviators, florists and gardeners, tuberculosis and illness, orphans, the homeless.
- Pius XI called her "the star of my pontificate" and, in S3, "a miracle of virtues and a prodigy of miracles". Pius X called her "the greatest saint of modern times" (S4, unverified in a primary source).
- First daughter of St Teresa to be canonized: Taylor's claim that she is the first of Teresa's "daughters" (Carmelite nuns) to be canonized must be checked against Teresa's own order; other Carmelite women (Maria Magdalena de' Pazzi, canonized 1669) were earlier. ## Other
- Conflicts: (a) Nun at Ustarritz: "Aloysia" (S2) vs "Louise" (S3; S3 decree text, too) of St Germain. (b) Her cure date: 22 Sep (S2) vs 25 Sep (S3). (c) Introduction-of-cause date: 10 Jun 1914 (S1, S3 narrative, S4) vs 9 Jun (S3 decree text). Use 10 Jun. (d) Basilica consecration 1951 (S5 body) vs 1954 (S4 and S5 infobox "completed"). (e) Feast day: 3 Oct (1927) vs 1 Oct (1969). (f) S4 says relics stay in the Carmel; shrine effigy is a statue (gisant) containing ribs and other remains.
- Relics: body in the Carmel of Lisieux since 25 Mar 1923; reliquary was a gift of Brazil; effigy is based on drawings and photographs; the figure by Fr Marie-Bernard (Louis Richomme) [S4]. Relic tour since 1994. Largest relic holding outside Lisieux: National Shrine of St Thérèse, Darien, Illinois [S4]. Third-class relics and her writing desk toured the US in 2013 [S4].
- Sanctuary: Carmel, Les Buissonnets, the cathedral of St Pierre, the cemetery, the basilica (capacity about 4,000; more than two million visitors a year per S5 versus about one million for the city per S4: conflict).
- Connections: parents Louis and Zélie (saints 2015); sister Léonie (servant of God); Mother Agnes (Pauline), postulation; Mgr Lemonnier; Fr Rodrigo; Mgr de Teil; Maximilian Kolbe; Daniel Brottier; Alphonsa; Mother Teresa chose the name Teresa after Thérèse [S4].
- Surprise: Pope Pius XI made her his first canonization; Benedict XV waived the 50-year rule.
- Surprise: the Promoter of the Faith, Fr Duboscq, had been Charles Anne's seminary superior.
- Image candidates: Céline's "Thérèse aux roses" (1912); 41 photographs by Céline taken 1894–97 (Carmel of Lisieux archives; rights of the Carmel); 1925 canonization pictures in S3 (photographic plates); crypt mosaics; unedited photos released after 1959 [S4].
