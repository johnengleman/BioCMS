## Witness roster (who testified and how)

- Mother Agnes of Jesus (Pauline, eldest sister after Marie; prioress 1893-96 and later for decades). Took notes of Thérèse's last words in pencil on loose sheets. Editor of *Story of a Soul*. [S1, S4]
- Marie of the Sacred Heart (Marie, eldest sister, Thérèse's godmother). Called the most sober and least calculated deponent by the historian. [S4]
- Sister Geneviève of the Holy Face (Céline, closest sister, also Thérèse's novice). Testimony "essential, too abundant, too sure", close to Agnes's aims. [S4]
- Marie of the Trinity (novice), Marie-Madeleine of the Blessed Sacrament (novice), Sister Marthe (novice), two lay sisters. Heard Feb-Mar 1911. The Trinity novice claims the young sisters' daily view counts most. Open conflict with Geneviève. [S4]
- Marie Guérin (Sister Marie of the Eucharist, cousin) and prioress Marie de Gonzague died before the trials. Two sick nuns left the community in 1909. Six others, old or uninvolved, were not called. [S4]
- Outsiders: Jesuit Auriault (on doctrine), Pastor Grant (a Protestant converted by Thérèse), the bishop of Nardò (reported a word heard in an apparition). Fr Godefroy Madelaine, Premonstratensian, "grandfather of the Story of a Soul", could not testify in 1916 because of the German occupation of Belgium. [S4, S5]
- Dr de Cornière, monastery physician (said the suffering was dreadful; astonished at her patience and smile). [S2]
- Only one apostolic-process witness newly knew her: the priest who heard her last confession. [S5]

## Timeline (process and witness history, date order)

- 1897 May 27: Thérèse says she welcomes the "circular" (obituary letter) and does not understand sisters who refuse one. Agnes then recalls their mother's letters about the two youngest girls. [S1]
- 1897 May 30: Thérèse, with prioress's leave, tells Agnes the secret of her first hemoptysis (Holy Thursday 1896). Agnes sees the TB is old. This starts the plan for a circular. NEW (for English popular pages: the link between the circular and the book) [S8]
- 1897 June 2-4: Prioress Marie de Gonzague asks Thérèse to write on her religious life. Thérèse stops all else from June 4 and writes Manuscript C in a black notebook, knowing it will form part of her circular. [S8]
- 1897 (date unknown, before death): one nun asks what the prioress can write in an obituary for a sister who "entered, lived and died". Two phrasings exist in popular sources. Unverified in primary text. [secondary: web search, https://angelusnews.com/faith/st-therese-lisieux-cameron/ not read in full]
- 1897 July 6: Hemorrhages start again, two or three a day for the month. July 8: moved to the infirmary. [S2]
- 1897 July 9-10: Fr Superior (Canon Maupas, parish priest of Saint-Jacques) weighs Extreme Unction. She stays so cheerful that the sacrament is postponed. She jokes she "does not know that trade" of winning one's point. July 10 she receives Extreme Unction and Viaticum. [S1]
- 1897 Aug 19: her last Communion, per Agnes's handwritten notes (Agnes says the autobiography wrongly gives Aug 16). [S1]
- 1897 Sept 14: she strips a rose over her crucifix and asks sisters to keep the petals. A petal is said to cure Ferdinand Aubry of tongue cancer, Sept 1910, at the Little Sisters of the Poor hospital, Lisieux. [S1]
- 1897 Sept 29: eve of death. She asks Mother how to die. She waves a childish "threat" at the devils when Agnes reads the prayers for the dying. [S1]
- 1897 Sept 30, about 3 pm: arms in a cross; Agnes puts a picture of Our Lady of Mount Carmel on her knees. [S1, S2]
- 1897 Sept 30, about 5 pm: agony starts; community gathers. Celine wets her lips with ice. About 7:20 pm she dies after a last look at the crucifix. Agnes had dismissed the community shortly after 7 pm, thinking the end far off, then rang the bell again. [S1, S2]
- 1897 Oct 4: burial. Limbs stayed supple until then, says Agnes. [S1]
- 1898: *Story of a Soul* appears, made from three manuscripts with editing by Agnes and others (additions and deletions visible in a side-by-side in S8). [S8]
- 1907: Bishop Lemonnier asks the Carmelites to write their memories and approves a prayer for her beatification. [S7]
- 1909 Jan: postulator Fr Rodrigue of St Francis of Paula, OCD, and vice-postulator Mgr de Teil named. [S7]
- 1910 Feb 10 to Jun 12: process of the writings (10 witnesses holding her papers). [S7]
- 1910 Aug 3 (court formed), Aug 12 (opened): informative (Ordinary) process. 93 sessions, 48 witnesses, 30 questions. Three sisters heard Aug-Sept 1910. Others Feb-Mar 1911. [S7, S4]
- 1910 Sept 6: first exhumation. [S7]
- 1911 Aug 30 to Sept 7: non-cult process, 16 witnesses. 1911 Dec 12: diocesan process closes at Bayeux. [S7]
- 1914 Jan onward: Mgr Verde as Promoter of the Faith. His objections end April 1914. [S6]
- 1914 June 10: Pius X signs the introduction of the Cause. [S7]
- 1915 Mar 17: apostolic process opens at Bayeux cathedral. 58 sessions, about 20 witnesses, closes Oct 30, 1917. Reputation for holiness not examined because of soldiers' petitions. [S5, S7]
- 1917 Aug 9-10: second exhumation. [S7]
- 1921 Aug 14: Benedict XV decrees heroic virtue. He also speaks on the "Little Way". [S7]
- 1923 Apr 29 beatification; 1925 May 17 canonization (dates from Carmel pages titles; 1923 Mar 26 remains return to Carmel). Not read in detail: see Not read. [S7 headings]

## Character (as the witnesses show her)

- Cheerful in pain on purpose: she says she acts cheerful when able, to give pleasure. [S1 Sept 6]
- Corrects those who praise her: she denies patience and denies being an angel. [S1 Aug 18, Sept 27, Sept 20]
- Teases and deflates: tells sisters "I only said that to frighten you" after telling them not to grieve. [S1 Sept 17]
- Stays a novice-mistress to the end: corrects a novice for sitting sideways on a chair. [S1 Aug 19]
- Afraid of false pride: fears an infidelity of a proud thought ("I have acquired this virtue"). [S1 Aug 7]
- Shuns news of the world: not dazzled by the Czar's reception in France, wants talk of God. [S1 Sept 3]
- Doubts and dryness are real: she says she has no consolation, and that her joys are only in the depths of her soul. She asks that this be told so that souls are not discouraged. [S1 Aug 15, June 5]
- Fears for her mind in the last weeks. [S1 Aug 19]
- Faults of childhood: stubborn will and pride at three (Articles). [S3]
- Critics: the Promoter of the Faith says she is admired mostly through her own book, her humility is not heroic, her deathbed words about relics and roses do not fit humility, and Lisieux ran a publicity campaign. [S6]
- Some sisters did not see holiness in her; the popular "obituary" anecdote sums this up. [secondary]

## Quotations (our paraphrase; locator; rights facts-only)

1. "If you find me dead one morning, do not worry: Papa le bon Dieu came to fetch me." (Paraphrase of the 5 June remark.) [S1 June 5]
2. Everything is a grace, including missing the sacraments. [S1 June 5]
3. "I shall send down a shower of roses" after her death, told to Marie. [S1 June 9]
4. She says she must be resigned "to live", not to die, told to the chaplain. [S1 June 5]
5. Good God is coming as a thief to steal her; she wants to help Him. [S1 June 9]
6. Her candle image: one little lamp lights all the candles, like hidden souls behind saints and Doctors. [S1 July 15]
7. She would not want to die right after Communion: little souls could not copy that. [S1 July 15]
8. To Fr Superior: she has not earned her crown, God earned it for her. [S1 July 9]
9. She heard a doctor say she would have no agony, then said she would accept one. [S1 Sept 14]
10. "I suffer only from moment to moment." Discouragement comes from the past or future. [S1 Aug 19]
11. "No, I am not an angel. Angels cannot suffer." [S1 Sept 27]
12. To Celine on the last day: all is said, all is finished, only love matters. [S1 Sept 29]
13. "I do not repent of giving myself up to Love." [S1 Sept 30]
14. To Agnes: how should I set about dying? [S1 Sept 29]
15. She will send petals: "gather them carefully, they will give pleasure later." [S1 Sept 14]
16. Crucifix: she prefers to see Christ dead, because then He suffers no more. [S1 Aug 19]
17. Last words: "My God, I love You." [S1 Sept 30]
18. "I have never sought anything but the truth." [S1 Sept 30]
19. Do not lament by a sickbed. [S1 Sept 17]
20. She asks not to cough so Celine can sleep, and tells the Virgin she will love her anyway if refused. [S1 Aug 15]

## Scenes (max 10)

1. July 15: a dead ember of a lamp lights Marie Guérin's candle; Thérèse turns it into a picture of the Communion of Saints.
2. July 9: the priest says she has not earned a crown. She smiles and agrees. She is too cheerful to be anointed that day.
3. Aug 6-7 (see S1): her long talk on falling like a child and loving parents anyway.
4. Sept 8: a robin comes into the infirmary and sits on her bed on the anniversary of her profession; a nun brings wildflowers.
5. Sept 14: she drops rose petals on the crucifix; petals fall to the floor and the sisters collect them.
6. Sept 29: Agnes reads the prayers for the dying. At the mention of devils Thérèse makes a mock-threat gesture.
7. Sept 30 about 5 pm: the community in the infirmary; Celine gives her a piece of ice; Thérèse looks at her.
8. Sept 30, 7 pm: the prioress dismisses the sisters; Thérèse asks if she is not dying; the bell is rung again and the doors opened.
9. Sept 30 about 7:20: a last ecstasy "for the length of a Credo", her face "like a lily", she breathes out; she seems 12 or 13 years old when washed. Birds sang in the tree outside all through the agony; the sky cleared at dusk. Agnes reports both; sisters confirm the birds.
10. Aug-Sept 1910 (S4, S7): depositions by three sisters, prepared with Bishop de Teil's Articles, with Agnes asking how to cite Thérèse's words. This shows how "testimony" was built.

## Miracles (from the witness side; church-miracles agent owns the full list)

- 1910 Sept, Lisieux hospital of the Little Sisters of the Poor: Ferdinand Aubry, cancer of the tongue, cured by a rose petal of 1897. Reported by Agnes in footnote to S1. Status: reported by the Carmel; not one of the two beatification miracles that I confirmed. [S1]
- Beatification miracles: two chosen from three; one is Sister Louise Saint-Germain, Daughters of the Cross of Ustaritz. The second is named in S5 as the seminarian Charles Anne (TB). Approved. [S5, S7; details not read]
- 1897-1911 ex-votos: 50 identified in 1911, 460 by 1917. [S5]
- The "bishop of Nardò" witness reported a word of Thérèse in an apparition ("my way is sure"). Testimony accepted at trial. [S4]
- Protestant Pastor Grant: conversion attributed to her. [S4]
- Sept 30: birds singing and clearing sky (recorded by Agnes; popular, not a miracle claim by the Church). [S1]

## Other

- Conflicts between sources: (a) Aug 16 or Aug 19 for her last Communion. Agnes's pencil notes say Aug 19. [S1 fn]. (b) Agnes's 1917 document "In what milieu Sister Thérèse sanctified herself" accuses the former prioress Marie de Gonzague. Langlois says it fed a "dark legend" counter to official hagiography. [S5] (c) Sisters competed in their depositions: Geneviève vs Marie of the Trinity. [S4] (d) S8 shows the 1898 book differs from her manuscript (additions added and sentences removed). The scholarly background (Fr François de Sainte-Marie's 1956 facsimile, later criticism that *Last Conversations* was also edited) is known but I did not verify it from the sources I read.
- Connections: Bl. Théophane Vénard (she received his relic Sept 6 and told his story); St John of the Cross (quoted to her by Agnes); Fr Roulland, missionary brother (letter referenced). [S1]
- Family after 1897: Marie died 1940, Agnes 1951, Léonie (Visitation Caen) 1944; Céline alive in Aug 1952. [S1 note]
- Image candidates: Carmel archives have photo albums, "iconographie théresienne" and 1917 exhumation photos. Not checked for licence. https://archives.carmeldelisieux.fr/en/naissance-dune-sainte/iconographie-theresienne/
- NEW for English pages: Langlois's account of the trial (only half the ordinary-process witnesses knew her; Verde's critique; the apostolic process skipped reputation for holiness because of war petitions).
