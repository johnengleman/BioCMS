# Plan v3: St. Thérèse of Lisieux

## The driving question

How did the nun who "never did anything worth speaking about" become one of the most loved saints and a Doctor of the Church? Thérèse wanted to be a great saint ("I choose everything"). Everything in her stood in the way: a furious, then oversensitive child; a body and a family broken by illness; a convent where she was small, young, and often misjudged; at the end, eighteen months in which Heaven itself went dark. She found that she was a grain of sand, and instead of climbing she let herself be carried: the little way, the lift of Christ's arms. Grace answers her twice in the story: the smile of 1883 and the Christmas of 1886. Her own choices answer the rest: the walk with Sister Saint-Pierre, the moss cross, the smile for the sister she disliked, the last "My God, I love you" said in the dark.

The hook (Example 2) raises the question in the kitchen and the meat-juice scenes. The ending answers it: the book she wrote in cheap notebooks went out in place of the obituary, and the grain of sand turned out to be a seed ("I am a little seed; no one knows yet what will come out of it").

## Why famous, and what set her apart

Famous: her autobiography taught millions a "little way" of trust and small acts of love, and the Church made her a saint in 1925 and a Doctor in 1997.
Set apart: she turned the ambition to be a great saint into the acceptance of being small, and she wrote honestly about the darkness of faith at the end of her life while still loving.
