# Thérèse of Lisieux: notes, focus own-words (also Identity)

Tag [M] = from memory, not yet checked in a source. Check before publishing.

## 1. Identity

- Birth name: Marie Françoise Thérèse Martin. Called "Thérèse" at home; "Little Flower" (1898 book); "Soeur Thérèse".
- Religious name: Sister Thérèse of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face (Thérèse de l'Enfant-Jésus et de la Sainte-Face).
- Other forms: St. Thérèse of Lisieux; "the Little Flower of Jesus"; Spanish Teresita; Italian Teresa di Lisieux. Not Teresa of Ávila (her patron model).
- Born 2 Jan 1873, Alençon, Normandy. Baptized 4 Jan 1873 [S1 "Days of Grace"].
- Parents: Louis and Zélie Martin (canonized together 18 Oct 2015 [M]). Youngest of 9 children; 5 daughters lived, all became nuns [S1 prologue].
- Lived at Lisieux from late 1877 [S1]; home = Les Buissonnets.
- Entered Lisieux Carmel 9 Apr 1888, age 15. Clothed 10 Jan 1889. Professed 8 Sept 1890 [S1 "Days of Grace"].
- Died 30 Sept 1897, Lisieux Carmel, age 24, of tuberculosis [M for cause].
- Tradition type: Catholic only. (Orthodox do not list her; no Orthodox feast found.)
- Feast: 1 Oct, General Roman Calendar since 1969 [M]. Older calendar: 3 Oct [M].
- Beatified 29 Apr 1923; canonized 17 May 1925, Pius XI [M]. Co-patron of missions with Francis Xavier, 14 Dec 1927 [M]. Co-patron of France with Joan of Arc, 1944 [M]. Doctor of the Church, 19 Oct 1997, John Paul II, "Divini Amoris Scientia" [M; S3 fetch failed].
- Tier: A.

## 3. Timeline (own-words view)

- 2 Jan 1873: born, Alençon. [S1]
- 1877: mother dies (Aug 1877) [M]; family moves to Lisieux near Uncle Guérin. She was four and a half when she lost her "strength of mind" [S1 ch. IV]. Seen in her list: "strength of mind which she had lost at the age of four and a half".
- Age 8.5: starts at the Benedictine Abbey school in Lisieux; Léonie leaves, she replaces her [S2 ch. III].
- 2 Oct 1882 [M]: Pauline enters Carmel; Thérèse's second "mother" lost; the strange illness follows (1883). [S1 ch. III]
- 13 May 1883 [M] (her own list says 10 May 1883 [S1 Days of Grace]): the statue of Our Lady smiles and cures her strange illness. Marie prays at the bedside. Keep "Smile of Our Lady" as key scene. CONFLICT: date.
- 8 May 1884: First Communion; confirmation 14 Jun 1884 [S1].
- 25 Dec 1886: "Christmas conversion", age 13. Father says the shoes are "too babyish"; she hears it, swallows tears, and gives a happy welcome to the presents. She says she lost her oversensitive tears in one night [S1 ch. V]. NEW detail for popular pages: Celine whispers "wait, you will cry".
- 1887: Pranzini case, her "first sinner". She prays for a murderer; in the paper La Croix she reads he kissed the crucifix three times at the scaffold [S1 ch. V; S2 ch. V]. (Execution 31 Aug 1887 [M].)
- 31 Oct 1887: Bayeux audience with Bishop Hugonin; Father takes her side [S1].
- 20 Nov 1887: Papal audience, Leo XIII. She kneels, rests hands on his knee: "if only you say yes". He says "You will enter if it is God's will." A guard lifts her away [S1 ch. VI]. She cries on the way out.
- 9 Apr 1888: enters Carmel, Feast of the Annunciation (postponed). [S1]
- 1889-1894: father paralysed (Bon Sauveur asylum, Caen, 1889-1892 [M]); dies 29 Jul 1894. His last word to her: "In Heaven!" [S1]
- 8 Sept 1890: profession. She carries a letter on her heart asking Jesus to take her from this world rather than let her stain her baptismal robe [S1].
- Novitiate years (after 1888): Sister St. Peter and laundry stories (see scenes) [S1 ch. X].
- 1893: named assistant to novice mistress (Mother Agnes prioress from Feb 1893 [M]). She is never officially novice mistress. [M]
- 9 Jun 1895: Act of Oblation to Merciful Love. [S1]
- Early 1895 (Jan): Mother Agnes asks her to write childhood memories; Ms A. [S1 ch. I; M for detail].
- 8 Sept 1896: Ms B, letter to Sister Marie of the Sacred Heart: "my vocation is love". [M for date; text S1 ch. XI]
- 3 Apr 1896 (night of Holy Thursday-Good Friday): first haemoptysis; she is glad. [S1 ch. IX; date of night S1 "Good Friday"; 3 Apr date M]
- Easter 1896 onward: nights of doubt, "the thick fog" (Easter 1896) [S1 ch. IX].
- Jun 1897: Ms C for Mother Marie de Gonzague, left unfinished. [M]
- 25 Dec 1896: gift of a top and a "letter from Jesus" to a novice who plays "ninepins" [S1 Counsels].
- 30 Sept 1897: dies about 7:20 pm [M]. Last words: "Oh! I love Him! My God, I love You!" [S1 epilogue].
- 1898: Histoire d'une âme published. [M]

## 4. Character (one line each)

- Sensitive and cried easily as a child; her own words, "cry-baby" sense. [S1 ch. IV-V]
- Quick temper as a small girl: stamped her foot at the maid Victoire who laughed during prayers, then wept with shame. [S1 ch. II; S2 ch. II]
- Stubborn and bold: at Rome she broke the rule of silence to ask the Pope for Carmel. [S1 ch. VI]
- Fell asleep often at prayer. She says she is not upset: a child is dear to a father asleep or awake. [S1 ch. VIII]
- Hated confrontation; ran to sit on the stairs after a quarrel about noise; "an odd kind of courage." [S1 ch. X]
- Humor: she laughs at her own novice days; she called the novices "rabbits" (hunted in the "great forest"). [S1 ch. X, letters]
- Playful games with God: "little ball" for the Child Jesus; "ninepins" game for souls; a top at Christmas. [S1]
- Used the gift of comparison: lift for stairs, sunlight and fog, bird and eagle, grain of sand, flowers, toys.
- Practical about small things: pick up a bit of thread for Jesus when you feel lazy. [S1 Counsels]
- Sharp with novices at times: "Is that how people hurry when they have children?" (said to a novice walking slowly to laundry). [S1 Counsels]
- Stubborn refusal of excuses: she says she will not justify herself when accused of making noise. [S1 ch. X]
- Anxious about dryness and the dark: "I am no saint," she says, of feeling dry. [S1 ch. VIII]

## 6. Scenes (max 10)

1. Christmas 1886: shoes by the chimney, Father's "too babyish" remark, Celine's whisper, she goes down and pulls out the presents "looking as happy as a queen." [S1 ch. V]
2. Smile of Our Lady (1883): she lies ill; her sisters kneel and pray to the statue; the statue seems to come alive and smile; she sees Marie and understands. [S1 ch. III]
3. Pranzini: she offers a murderer to God as "my first sinner", asks for one sign, then reads La Croix the next day and runs out, in tears. [S1 ch. V]
4. Rome, 20 Nov 1887: kneeling before Leo XIII, hands on his knee, the Noble Guards lift her away, the Pope's hand on her lips. She cries outside; "on the surface all was bitterness." [S1 ch. VI]
5. Carmel 9 Apr 1888: Father blesses her kneeling, weeping; doors close; she falls into Pauline's arms. [S2 ch. VII]
6. Sister St. Peter: each evening ten minutes before six she leads an old, ill nun to the refectory; wins her over, finally brings a smile. [S1 ch. X]
7. Laundry splash: the Sister opposite splashes her with dirty water; she decides she likes it and plans to come back. [S1 ch. X]
8. The horse at the garden gate (childhood): she slips between its legs while her friends argue. She uses it to teach "pass beneath." [S1 Counsels]
9. Good Friday night 1896: blood on the handkerchief; she goes to sleep in peace and checks it at dawn. [S1 ch. IX]
10. Christmas 1896: she leaves a top and a letter "To my beloved little spouse, player of ninepins on the Mountain of Carmel" for a novice. [S1 Counsels]

Extra: last hours, Angelus bell, looking at the statue of Our Lady; eyes fixed above it as she dies. [S1 epilogue]
