=== 236 | Roger Lefèvre lifted up on the battlefield, 1915
On 15 July 1915 Céline heard Private Roger Lefèvre, of the 224th Infantry, aged 29, tell how Thérèse appeared and lifted him up as he lay bathed in his blood. He wished all unbelievers such an apparition. Asked if she was beautiful, he said "much more beautiful than on her pictures." Céline swore to this second-hand.

=== 237 | Henri Lamielle, a stretcher-bearer, 1915
Sergeant Henri Lamielle, a stretcher-bearer, wrote that he saw Thérèse leading him to the wounded and saying "Come on, my friend, forward, there are souls to save." The letter is in the Carmel's collection of letters from the front.

=== 238 | Auguste Cousinard's apparition on the battlefield, 1915
In the Carmel parlour Céline heard Auguste Cousinard describe an apparition of Thérèse on the battlefield that converted him. Laveille calls him a lukewarm soldier, and Céline found him truthful. He sent an account that was printed in a wartime booklet.
@ {PA} witness 8 (Céline); {LAV} scan p. 424, note
=== 239 | Corporal Marcel Guyot, 1915
Corporal Marcel Guyot of the 134th Regiment wrote that Thérèse led him by the hand and that his squad had no losses. The Carmel's collection of letters from the front lists other soldiers' reports of the same kind, including Pierre Aubert, Charles Gérard, Pierre Vanlaethem, Captain Tropet, and the naval captain de la Vaissière.
@ Carmel of Lisieux, "Courrier du front," First World War letters
=== 240 | Marcelline's varicose inflammation, 1916
Marcelline had varicose ulcers on the leg at various times. In December 1916, after unusual fatigue, a violent inflammation began that from experience would become an ulcer, and no one could replace her in her work. She put a relic of Thérèse on the leg, and without remedy or rest the inflammation vanished.

=== 241 | The legless Bavarian prisoner, about 1916
A German prisoner from Bavaria, who had lost both legs, was dying. A French chaplain told him to pray to the saint of Lisieux. After a night of prayer he reported that he saw her come down toward him, and his wounds healed. He said he would make her known in his country.

=== 248 | Corporal Henri Bellois and the medal, 1918

=== 249 | Laura Fitchett, nephritis, Dublin, 1921
Laura Fitchett, aged 13, had acute nephritis and dropsy, and Dr Garland saw no hope. She was anointed, and she reported seeing Thérèse as a child in a First Communion dress, with the Virgin. She was told to say three Hail Marys, was given a white rose to smell, and heard "Good-bye now, Laura, you are cured." Professor Patrick Beecher of Maynooth looked into it privately on 16 April 1925, and no Church tribunal did. Two priests' signatures and a medical attestation are printed in Shower of Roses.

=== 250 | A child from Angers walks from the open grave, 26 March 1923
At the translation of the relics a poor woman of Angers carried her god-daughter to the graveside by special leave. The child, aged twelve with Pott's disease according to the Carmel, could not move a limb and had been unable to walk for months. The woman laid her on the earth of the open grave above the coffin and prayed. After a few moments the child sat up and walked unaided from the tomb.

=== 258 | Bernardino Augusto de Lima and his brother, Brazil, 1924
Bernardino Augusto de Lima had cancer and offered his agony for his sceptic brother, a deputy and academician. The brother received Communion on 13 May 1924. Rose petals appeared on three mornings in the invalid's water, the sign the brother had secretly asked for, and his symptoms eased. Bernardino died on 18 May 1924, having foretold that "Theresinha" would fetch him at eight. The account is by the deputy's wife, and O Globo printed it on 14 October 1925.
