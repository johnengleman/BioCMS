=== 029 | Madagascar, a woman who walked and a mother's "beautiful lady," 1909
At Ambatolampy a woman who had been unable to move for ten years walked after novenas and was baptised Mary Teresa. A Malagasy mother said that "the beautiful lady," meaning Thérèse's picture, came in the night in a white robe and cured her dying baby. Sister Berchmans reported both accounts on 19 December 1909.

=== 030 | Brother Paul, a knee wound, Trappist monastery of Rogersville, Canada, 1910
Brother Paul, a Trappist of Rogersville, was reported cured suddenly of a serious knee wound after an apparition of the Servant of God. Dr Bourret of Rogersville attested on 22 April 1910 that the healing of such a wound was so quick that he felt bound to ascribe it to a cause "quite supernatural." Mère Agnès lists it as item 6 of her summary.

=== 031 | Abbé Claude-Marcel Weber, a cataract, Saint-Jean-de-Luz, 1910
Abbé Weber, a priest aged about 75, had a beginning cataract confirmed in Paris in 1900, and by 1909 an optician called the right eye "completely extinguished." The exiled Carmel of Bordeaux at Zarautz made novenas to Thérèse without telling him. After the second novena, with a picture-relic laid on his eyes each evening, he reported that he could read the small print of his breviary and recognise people at over a hundred paces. During the third he saw bee eggs in a queen cell with the naked eye. He gave sworn testimony at the Ordinary Process on 22 and 23 March 1911, and Dr Baradat wrote on 7 September 1910.
@ {DET} art. 144, pp. 200–205; {PO} witness 19, ff. 1130r–1138v; {T24} scan pp. 405–407
=== 032 | Ferdinand Aubry, an ulcer of the tongue, Lisieux, 1910
Ferdinand Aubry, aged 60, lived at the home of the Little Sisters of the Poor in Lisieux and had a gangrenous ulcer of the tongue that was feared to be cancer. On 28 September 1910 he swallowed a rose petal from Thérèse's deathbed. On 2 October he said he was cured, and the missing part of the tongue is said to have grown back in three weeks. A photograph is attached to the doctor's certificate. He died peacefully on 18 December 1910. The sources differ on whether it was cancer or a gangrenous ulcer.
@ {DET} art. 147, pp. 213–218; {LAV} scan p. 392; {PA} witness 23 (Jeanne La Néele), [1360]
=== 033 | Sister Mary-Dominic, a fibroid tumour, Vertou hospital, 1910
Sister Mary-Dominic of St-Gildas-des-Bois, aged 52, had a fibroid in the left side of the abdomen "the size of a newborn's head." Three doctors advised an operation. After nine novenas she woke on 3 October 1910 lying on her left side with no swelling. Dr H. found no tumour and no scar.
@ {DET} art. 146, pp. 208–213
=== 034 | Abbé Jules Rossignol, an apparition before death, Beaupréau, 1911
Abbé Jules Rossignol, about 76, had taught theology at the Luçon seminary and lived at a retired priests' house at Beaupréau. For over forty years he had been secretly afraid of God's judgment. On the morning of 16 January 1911 he reported that he saw "little Sister Thérèse" at his bedside, smiling, and she made him understand that she was taking care of his affair. "It is not a dream, I was fully awake," he said. His fear gave way to joy, and he died almost suddenly on 18 January, with no sign of fear. He had a chronic intestinal disorder, but nothing foretold his death.
@ {DET} art. 145, pp. 205–208; {PO} witnesses 21–22, ff. 1162r–1167v; {T24} scan pp. 434–436
=== 035 | Clémentine Derenne, albuminuria and meningitis, Laval, 1911
Clémentine Derenne, aged 17, had albuminuria threatening consumption, then cerebral uraemia, and on 1 February 1911 the doctor said meningitis had set in. On 2 February she was given wool from Thérèse's pillow and could read its tiny print. She reported a young nun who said "You will be cured" and a perfume, and by 6 p.m. Dr Pivert found no trace of lung trouble or meningitis. A third visit on 4 February spoke of personal matters. Monsignor de Teil made an inquiry and confirmed the facts.
@ {T24} scan pp. 409–411; {PA} witness 6 (Mère Agnès), [536], item 12
=== 036 | The Rev. Alexander Grant, a minister who became a Catholic, Edinburgh, 1911
Alexander Grant, a United Free Church minister, read the English Story of a Soul and, ill with influenza late in 1909, felt a sense that Thérèse was in the room. He reported hearing "choose my little way" and later "Why do you ask me to pray for you and ignore the Blessed Virgin?" At the end of March 1911, during a novena in a convent chapel, he felt Christ present in the tabernacle. He was received into the Church on 20 April 1911. This is a conversion, not a healing, and he testified at both processes.
@ {DET} art. 148, pp. 218–222; {DOL} ch. XII; {PO} witness 27, ff. 1251r–1255v

=== 047 | Aline Poirson, liver and spleen disease, Anrosey, 1912
Aline Poirson of Anrosey had been ill since she was twenty and could not eat meat or bread for nineteen years. She was bedridden for five and a half years with a swollen liver and spleen, kept alive by injections. A relic arrived on 15 June 1912 and a novena began on 19 June. At 2 a.m. on 1 July she felt well and reported hearing "Get up; you are cured." She walked to Mass at 5.30 and ate meat that day. Dr Vauthrin said the cure came without any action of medical art, and Dr Malingre called it absolutely miraculous.
