=== 130 | Céline's three inner signs from Thérèse, 1897 to 1908
In October 1897 Céline saw a flame while making the Way of the Cross in the cloister and cried "It is Thérèse." Later she heard the words "Bien humblement!" In June 1908, at 4 a.m., she heard "One gives oneself to Love only as far as one gives oneself to suffering." Céline alone swore to these at the process, and none is an approved miracle.

=== 131 | A lay sister cured of "cerebral anaemia" by kissing her feet, October 1897
A lay sister of the Lisieux Carmel had once spoken unkindly to Thérèse over the wreaths round Mère Geneviève's coffin. For many years she had suffered from "cerebral anaemia," which kept her from reading or mental prayer. Repentant, she kissed the dead Thérèse's feet, leaned her forehead on them, asked pardon, and felt herself suddenly cured. Céline heard from the sister herself that she owed Thérèse the cure.

=== 132 | Perfumes, a light, a crown and a kiss around her death, October 1897
In the days around the death one nun smelled violets, and one felt a kiss from an invisible being. One saw a light in the sky, and another saw a luminous crown rise from the earth and vanish. Mère Agnès swore that after Thérèse's death her limbs stayed supple until burial on 4 October. She kept a gentle smile, looked twelve or thirteen, and had great beauty. === 135 | A ring of light around the moon, October 1897
Fifteen days after the death Céline saw in the sky a luminous circle traced by a flame that seemed alive. It left the right side of the full moon, circled beneath it, and re-entered on the left. It gave her a strong inner grace and sudden understanding of many things Thérèse had told her, and she was sure it was her sister's soul. A halo round a full moon is a natural possibility that the testimony does not discuss.

=== 137 | The broken sandal, winter 1897 to 1898
Marie Guérin, who became Sister Marie de l'Eucharistie, had frozen feet and chose to mortify herself and not put on her warm rope-soled sandals, which she had permission to wear because of nosebleeds. As she passed the room where they dried, one of her cold sandals broke beyond repair, so she had to put on the warm ones. She wrote that it was one of her little Thérèse's tricks. The account is her letter to her father of 3 July 1898, filed at the Ordinary Process by her sister Jeanne.

=== 143 | Pierre-Marie Derrien, illness, an inner voice and a cure, Brittany, 1904 to 1913
Pierre Derrien, a Breton postal courier born in 1880, was abandoned by the doctors. Dolan says he invoked Thérèse in 1904 and was immediately cured. His own sworn account of 1916 describes three years of bronchitis, typhoid and enteritis and an inner voice saying "Pierre, I am looking for souls for Jesus." He says a full cure came only gradually and without remedies, after he accepted suffering. On 29 September 1913 a telegram from the prioress asked him to become "little Thérèse's servant," and he became sacristan of the Carmel chapel. The sources differ on whether the cure was immediate in 1904 or gradual in 1911.

=== 144 | The bandits' powder blows up, Sichuan, about 1904
During a persecution about 200 Christian women and girls took refuge at the residence of the missionary Roulland, who was away. Bandits prepared to attack, and before setting out they set off firecrackers to their gods. One lit their powder, blew up the pagoda, and killed or burned many bandits, and the rest fled. Roulland reported that the women were safe before they knew the danger.

=== 150 | Sister Marie-Madeleine, thirty-five boils, Lisieux Carmel, 1908
From September 1907 the lay sister Marie-Madeleine had a leg illness that kept her in the infirmary eight months, with an unbroken series of abscesses, 35 by her count, and wasting of the limb. In May 1908 the prioress proposed that she call on Sister Thérèse. During the first novena the illness grew worse, and a second novena brought complete cure. On the first Sunday of June she took up her kitchen work and did all the cooking alone. Céline links the cure to a veil believed, but not surely, to have been worn by Thérèse.

=== 151 | The Eure family, a boy with a fractured skull, 1908

A novena began on 24 August. He was unconscious and delirious for eight days, with four crises, and on the ninth day he recognised everyone and was calm. He returned to school with no trace.

=== 159 | Abbé Pichon's broncho-pneumonia, Paris, 1909
On 28 August 1909, during a retreat for Augustinian nuns, Abbé Pichon collapsed with infectious broncho-pneumonia after an intestinal blockage. Three doctors said he would die within hours, and he received Extreme Unction. He asked Thérèse to be obedient in heaven as on earth and to obtain a simple, quick cure without too striking a miracle.
