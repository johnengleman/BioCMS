=== 082 | The veiled, bent man like her father, Les Buissonnets, about 1879
When Thérèse was about six or seven she stood alone at a window of Les Buissonnets and saw in the garden a man with her father's height and walk, but much more stooped, his head covered with a veil. === 083 | The smile of the Virgin, Les Buissonnets, 13 May 1883
Thérèse, aged ten, was ill from March 1883 with terrors, hallucinations, falls, loss of voice and states in which she did not know her sisters. Dr Notta is reported to have said that science was powerless. Her father had paid for a novena of Masses at Notre-Dame des Victoires. On Pentecost Sunday, 13 May, Marie, Léonie and Céline prayed before the statue of the Virgin, and Thérèse stared at it for four or five minutes with two large tears falling. She wrote that the Virgin "seemed to me beautiful" and that her pains vanished. Her sisters and Laveille say the statue moved, and she herself says the cure came gently.
