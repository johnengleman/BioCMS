# Saint Anthony of Padua: scene outline, v2 (tier A)

**Keys** as in `outline-v1.md` (W- witnesses, O- own words, C- church-miracles notes). **SC-xx / FN-x** = the checked cards in `scenes-1/2/3.md`; card numbers kept. Saved texts: `sources/acta-raw.txt` (W-S1), `sources/rigauld-1904.txt` (W-S2), `sources/coleridge-1883.txt` (C-S2). Facts in `biography.html` are approved; `check.md` and `check-reverence.md` list what was removed.

## 1. The driving question

Fernando wanted to give his whole life to Christ at once, as the five friars killed in Morocco had done [W-S1 p.198; W-S1 p.196]. A long illness, a contrary wind, and his own weakness stood in the way, and he reached Italy sick and wanted by no one [SC-03; SC-04]. Grace answered through obedience: ordered to speak at Forlì, he found that his fight would be fought with words, and he gave his life slowly, in pulpits and confessionals, until he could say at Arcella, "I see my Lord" [SC-06; SC-13].

**Why it was beautiful to him.** The news of the martyrs turned in him to compassion for "the wrong done to Christ". He felt he did nothing at all unless he too faced the tyrant and won the same palm [SC-02, Julian]. His resolve, in Rigauld's words: "I will take their habit, and so will I, too, go to the fight" [W-S2 p.36]. The reader first hears it in **chapter 2**, Coimbra 1220. Chapter 1 plants its first form: he left home for "peace of soul" [SC-01].

**Famous** as the wonder-worker of Padua, the saint people ask for lost things, and a Doctor of the Church, canonized in under a year [C-S1; W-S1 p.204; FN-1].

**Set apart.** In an age of famous preachers, he was a learned canon who hid his learning in a kitchen until an order brought it out [SC-05; SC-06]. He turned his preaching on usurers, the rich, and bad clergy, and Padua passed a law for debtors at his request [O-S2 pp.81-83; C-S1].

## 2. Portrait

Serious and loved: his Lisbon superior let him go only with difficulty, for "the seriousness of his character" (Julian) or because "he was beloved by all" (Rigauld) [SC-01]. He wanted to be hidden: a new name against visitors, a crypt cell, the kitchen pots and the friars' feet [SC-02; SC-05; W-S2 p.44]. His memory served him "in the place of books" [W-S2 p.33]. In the pulpit he was gracious and severe at once, so love and fear rose together [W-S1 p.200]; he called an archbishop "mitre-bearer" to his face [W-S2 pp.67-68]. Heavy and ill, he never refused a sermon or a confession [SC-10]. He called his body his "little ass", after Saint Francis [O-S2 p.~66 n.1]. Dry wit: "When you hear that I am a saint, surely you will give thanks to God" [W-S2 p.38]. He preached from vines, spiders, cranes, and doves [O-S2 pp.77-80]. No clear fault in the notes; the nearest are the move the early Life defends against the charge of a restless heart, and his severity.

**Life with God.** He kept night watches alone at Monte Paolo, with "the anchor of a most firm hope" (Julian, about him) [SC-05]. His own words of trust: "I trust in Him who never suffers us to trust in vain" [SC-09]. The Virgin runs through his life. He grew up in her church, called her name when the devil attacked him, and died singing her hymn [W-S1 p.198; SC-11; SC-13]. He saw the wound in Christ's side as the cleft in the rock where the soul nests like a dove [O-S2 pp.79-80].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** hook, a scene at the edge: the friar no one asked for, Assisi, Pentecost 1221 (SC-04). The opening tells SC-04 in full. Chapter 4 does not tell it again: one line back to it, then Gratian.

**Draft:**
At Pentecost in 1221 the friars of Saint Francis held their general meeting at Assisi. When it ended, the brothers were sent back to their places in every direction. One was left over. He was a friar from Portugal, weak and ill, and he had never meant to come to Italy. He had sailed to Morocco to die for Christ, but a long illness stopped him there, and on the way home the wind drove his ship to Sicily. An early Life says it in one line: only Saint Anthony was asked for by no one. [SC-04; SC-03]

**Do not spend:** the thirty thousand in the fields, the children's cry, the death, "I see my Lord", the tongue, or the line "a day at a time". The current `summary.txt` spends the crowd, the children, and the death. The new summary must stop at the question.

**Thesis (after the opening).** Pope Pius XII named him a Doctor of the Church in 1946, the Evangelical Doctor, because his sermons draw so constantly on the Gospels [FN-1]. One English biographer calls him perhaps the first friar to teach theology to the brothers [O-S2 pp.30-31]. At his request Padua freed ruined debtors from prison for life [C-S1; SC-10]. Pope Gregory IX, who knew him, named him a saint in under a year [C-S1; W-S1 p.204]. Answered in chapters 5, 6, 7, 10, and 11.

**Ending.** The after-death part is short: chapters 10 to 12, about 1,200 words in total. The canonization, the first feast, the Doctor's title, Tuesdays, and "Si quaeris" all come before the image. **Closing image:** in 1263 Saint Bonaventure opens the tomb, finds the tongue whole and red, and kisses it (SC-15). After it, at most one quiet sentence: the vocal organs are on view in Padua today [C-S5]. What the reader is offered, felt and not stated: a plan given to God and refused, and the same life given back a day at a time by a man who first had to be ordered to speak.

## 4. Chapters

A "Pull" is planned only where the story truly turns. The other chapters end on their own image.

1. **Saint Anthony Leaves Home for Coimbra.** SC-01. Ends on its image: eight quiet years, a canon who knows his books by heart.
2. **Saint Anthony Becomes a Friar.** SC-02 (turning point 1). **Pull:** The robe was his, and now he asked for the fight: leave to go among the Saracens.
3. **Saint Anthony Seeks Martyrdom in Morocco.** SC-03 (turning point 2). **Pull:** Friars in Sicily told him of the meeting at Assisi. He was weak and sick, and he went.
4. **Saint Anthony Hides at Monte Paolo.** One line back to the opening; Gratian takes him "kindly". SC-05. Ends on its image: the friar who cannot hold himself up at the evening hour.
5. **Saint Anthony Is Ordered to Preach.** SC-06 (turning point 3). **Pull:** The fight he had wanted came to him at last, and it would be fought with words.
6. **Saint Anthony Preaches in Italy and France.** Bridge: Rimini and the heretic leader (SC-07 card, two sentences); Bologna and the letter of Saint Francis (attributed); Guardian at Limoges, Custodian, Brive; Bourges. SC-08, SC-09. Ends on its image: the soaked ground and the dry crowd.
7. **All of Padua Comes to Hear Saint Anthony.** Bridge: the 1230 chapter at Saint Francis's new tomb; freed to preach; "Ark of the Covenant"; the cardinal's request; the winter of writing. SC-11, SC-10 (turning point 4, the fullest scene before the death). Ends on its image: the priests cannot hear all the confessions; the law of 15 March.
8. **Saint Anthony Prays in a Walnut Tree.** SC-12. **Pull:** The friars wept as they put him in the carriage for Padua.
9. **Saint Anthony Dies at Arcella.** SC-13 (turning point 5, climax). **Pull:** The friars meant to keep his death quiet.
10. **Padua Fights Over Saint Anthony's Body.** SC-14. Bridge: envoys, the students' letter, the commission; Spoleto, Pentecost 1232; first feast 13 June 1232; the relationship ends (Holy Cross canon, Tiso, mother). Ends on its image: the pope raises his hands and names him a saint.
11. **Why the Church Remembers Saint Anthony.** Bridge only: the sermons; Doctor, 1946, on the feast of the five martyrs; the basilica; Tuesdays; lost things; "Si quaeris". Ends on its image: the friars sing "Si quaeris" every Tuesday.
12. **Saint Bonaventure Opens Saint Anthony's Tomb.** SC-15. The closing image.

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points** (slow, told from inside him): SC-02, SC-03, SC-06, SC-10, SC-13.

**Relationships, followed to the end**
- **Parents and home.** He leaves them about 1210 and gives up his inheritance [SC-01]. He takes a new name to stop the visits [SC-02]. End, ch 10: Lisbon tradition that his mother lived to see him a saint, and that people asked his aunt to ask him [C-S2 p.195], attributed.
- **The canons of Holy Cross.** The grieving canon: "Go… you will perhaps become a saint." His answer: "When you hear that I am a saint…" [W-S2 p.38]. End, ch 10: the canonization at Pentecost 1232. State it once, plainly.
- **Brother Gratian.** He takes him in and sends him to Monte Paolo [SC-04; SC-05]. End: not in the notes (Fact need 3).
- **Saint Francis.** The Lives record no meeting. Then the letter on theology (attributed), Arles 1226 (SC-08), the death of Saint Francis in 1226 (one beat), and the 1230 chapter at his new tomb (ch 7) closes the thread. His response to the death: Fact need 2.
- **The Virgin.** His childhood church [W-S1 p.198], the Lent night (SC-11), "O gloriosa Domina" (SC-13). Rigauld's "as in his childhood": Fact need 1.
- **Tiso.** He builds the walnut cell (SC-12). End, ch 10: he joined the Third Order and died 31 January 1234 [W-S1 note g p.201, late].
- **Gregory IX.** He hears him and calls him the "Ark" (ch 7), then names him a saint and writes that he knew him (ch 10) [C-S1].

**Losses at their dates.** About 1210, home (SC-01). 1220, Holy Cross and his name (SC-02). 1220-21, the illness and the lost plan (SC-03). 1221, unwanted (opening). The long weakness on one concrete day: the evening his step wavered (SC-05). 1226, Saint Francis dies (ch 6, one beat). Lent 1231, ill and nearly crushed (SC-10). June 1231, the friars' tears at the carriage (SC-12).

### SC-01 — Leaving home, about 1210-1212 (St Vincent's, Lisbon; Holy Cross, Coimbra)
- What happens: The visits of family and friends break his peace. After nearly two years he asks to go far away, and his superior lets him go only with difficulty.
- Why it is here: the first form of the desire; a hard part (the early Life answers the charge of a restless heart).
- Have: card SC-01 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Rigauld, W-S2 p.34.

### SC-02 — Coimbra, 1220 (Holy Cross; the hermitage of St Antony) — TURNING POINT 1
- What happens: The relics pass through Coimbra. His inner turn (compassion, the war-horse, the resolve). Some poor friars beg at the door. He takes the robe. The canon's farewell and his answer. The new name.
- Why it is here: the first great decision; the desire named, in his resolve.
- Have: card SC-02 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Rigauld, W-S2 pp.36-37.

### SC-03 — Morocco and Sicily, 1220-1221 — TURNING POINT 2
- What happens: A long, heavy illness. "Nothing he wished for was going well." He sails for Spain, the winds drive him to Sicily, and he sees "his plan taken from him completely".
- Why it is here: the central obstacle, told through Julian's "he saw".
- Have: card SC-03 (thin). Need: nothing more exists in the Lives; keep it short.

### SC-04 — Pentecost chapter, Assisi, 1221 (opening only)
- Have: card SC-04 (full). Need: nothing.

### SC-05 — Monte Paolo, 1221-1222
- What happens: The crypt cell, night watches, bread and water. The concrete day: at the evening hour his step wavers and he cannot hold himself up.
- Why it is here: hiddenness; life with God; the long weakness.
- Have: card SC-05 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Julian, W-S1 p.199.

### SC-06 — Forlì, about 1222 — TURNING POINT 3
- What happens: Everyone refuses to speak. The minister orders the kitchen friar. He protests, obeys, begins with simple words, and the room is astonished.
- Why it is here: obedience opens the hidden gift; his fear of "an ambitious act" is the inside view.
- Have: card SC-06 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Rigauld, W-S2 pp.45-46.

### SC-08 — Arles, about 1226
- What happens: He preaches on the title of the cross. Saint Francis appears at the door, arms out like a cross, and blesses the friars.
- Why it is here: the bond with Saint Francis; Rigauld links it to his long wish for the cross.
- Have: card SC-08 (full). Need: nothing.

### SC-09 — Rain held back, Creux-des-Arènes
- What happens: Thunder; the crowd starts to break up. He speaks quietly, and no rain falls on them.
- Why it is here: his own words of trust in God.
- Have: card SC-09 (full). Need: nothing. No town name.

### SC-11 — A night near the start of Lent, 1231 (his cell, Padua)
- What happens: The devil grips his throat. He calls on the Virgin's name, and light fills the cell.
- Why it is here: life with God, told by himself "among friends"; the Virgin thread.
- Have: card SC-11 (full). Need: Fact need 1.

### SC-10 — Lent 1231, Padua — TURNING POINT 4
- What happens: His plan for the whole of Lent. The churches overflow into the fields. People come by night with lights; thirty thousand fall silent; the shops shut. He is ill and heavy, and nearly crushed. Enemies make peace, debts are returned, and the law of 15 March passes. One sample of his words: the "purple of the poor".
- Why it is here: the refused life, given daily; the payoff the opening sets up.
- Have: card SC-10 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Julian (thirty thousand); the robber, W-S2 p.74 (not placed in Padua).

### SC-12 — Camposampiero, spring to June 1231
- What happens: From a hill he praises Padua. Tiso builds the walnut cell. He prays there. One day at the meal he collapses, asks not to burden the poor friars, and is carried off in tears.
- Why it is here: the last retreat; a parting.
- Have: card SC-12 (full). Need: nothing. Hill words are indirect only.

### SC-13 — Arcella, Friday 13 June 1231 — TURNING POINT 5, CLIMAX
- What happens: He turns aside to Arcella, confesses, begins "O gloriosa Domina", looks up: "I see my Lord." The anointing words, the psalms, and half an hour later he dies "like one gently falling asleep".
- Why it is here: the desire answered. Nothing stands in front of it: no Church-word paragraph before the quotation, no hint in the summary or the opening. Rigauld's Friday meaning follows in two sentences at most [W-S2 p.80].
- Have: card SC-13 (full). Need: nothing. Testimony: Julian n.31.

### SC-14 — The fight for the body, 13-17 June 1231
- What happens: The children's cry; armed men at the cell; the bridge of boats; the procession; and Capo di Ponte barefoot at the tomb.
- Why it is here: the after-death case: a named community, a year.
- Have: card SC-14 (full). Need: nothing. Compress the four days to one paragraph each for the cry, the fight, and the burial.

### SC-15 — The tongue, 1263 — CLOSING IMAGE
- What happens: Saint Bonaventure opens the shrine, finds the tongue whole and red, kisses it, and speaks.
- Why it is here: the fight with words, sealed; the strongest image.
- Have: card SC-15 (full). Need: nothing. Use Coleridge's words only.

## 6. The saint in the Church

**Writings:** Sunday sermons; feast-day sermons written at Padua, winter 1230-31, at the cardinal of Ostia's request, half done at his death [W-S1 p.202; O-S2 p.69]; outlines, not finished talks [O-S2 p.76]; Lent sermons printed at Paris, 1521 [O-S3]. Ch 7, 11.
**What he changed:** taught theology to the friars at Bologna, about 1222 [O-S2 pp.30-31]. Ch 6. The Padua law of 15 March 1231 [C-S1]. Ch 7. Doctor of the Church, 16 January 1946, "Exulta, Lusitania felix", dated on the feast of the five martyrs of Morocco [FN-1]. Ch 11.
**Why he is known:** cures at the tomb from the day of burial [W-S1 p.203] (ch 10); Tuesdays [C-S2 p.191]; the stolen book and lost things (Liber miraculorum, about 1367) [O-S2 p.35]; "Si quaeris", Julian of Speyer, 1233 [FN-3]; the basilica, begun 1232, body moved 1263 [FN-2] (ch 11); 650,000 visitors in 1981 [C-S5] (ch 12, one sentence).
**Path to sainthood:** envoys within a month and a commission of three [C-S2 pp.191-192]; canonized at Spoleto, Pentecost, 30 May 1232; feast 13 June [W-S1 p.204]. Ch 10.

## 8. What we leave out

- Fish, horse, poisoned dish: one sentence in ch 6; miracles section.
- Bilocation, St Junien, Brive cabbages, the Queen of Portugal: miracles section.
- Ezzelino (Verona; the voice from the tomb): apocryphal or late [C-S1; C-S2 p.197].
- Quarrel with Elias: late (Wadding).
- Leonardo's foot, the miser's heart, the speaking infant: later legends.
- Child Jesus: place disputed; one attributed sentence in ch 11 at most.
- Cardinal's dream, Lisbon bells: legendary.
- Translations of 1310, 1350, 1745; fire and storm of 1749 and 1756: they bury the ending.
- Portrait debate; long runs of sermon images: teachings section. Keep only the dove (ch 8) and the purple line (ch 7).
- Parents' names; the "Tony, Tony" rhyme: late, not his.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- "Some poor friars" begged at the door, not five. "Go to the fight" is Rigauld's statement of his resolve, not a spoken line [SC-02].
- The anointing words ARE in the early Latin Life (n.31). v1 was wrong. Do not use "Brother Roger" [SC-13].
- The collapse is on "one day"; only the death is dated 13 June [SC-12]. "Forgot to eat" is Woodcock's alone: leave it out [SC-10].
- The rain scene has no town. The robber has no town or year [SC-09; SC-10].
- The meeting with Saint Francis at Assisi is Pius XII's tradition only: attributed, or put in the Note [scenes-3, "Found but not assigned"].
- Pentecost 1221 and Arles 1226 are Bollandist estimates. The "eight years" at Coimbra rests on Coleridge [check.md].
- The basilica: begun 1232, body moved 1263, main part done by about 1300; no "1301". For the tongue, write only "1263" [FN-2].
- "Si quaeris" is by Julian of Speyer, 1233; mention no Bonaventure [FN-3].
- Benedict XVI is under copyright: one short line at most. Call W-S1 "an early Life".
- Use "Saint" before every saint's name, and "robe", not "habit", in the narrative.
