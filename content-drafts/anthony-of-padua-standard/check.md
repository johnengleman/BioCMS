## Fixes

Quotations
1. Block quote, Monte Paolo (note 25): "made himself strong against the temptations of the spirits" misread "sacris meditationibus contra tentationes spiritum roborans" (strengthening his spirit against temptations). Now "he strengthened his spirit against temptations through holy meditation".
2. Rain quote (note 46): "…never lets us trust him in vain, and the rain will not touch you" made the rain a separate claim. Rigauld (and Woodcock, who quotes him) have "I trust in Him who never suffers us to trust in vain, that the rain will not touch you". Now "…never lets us trust in vain, that the rain will not touch you."
3. Dove quote (note 68): the writer joined "The world despises them as useless" to the list of virtues and dropped "They are the virtues of our Saviour". Now "… They are the virtues of our Saviour: humility, gentleness, poverty, patience, and self-denial. The world despises them as useless." (Woodcock pp. 79–80.)
4. Tiso's tomb words (note 88): the Latin is "sepultura Domini Tisi de Campo sancti Petri". Now "The tomb of Lord Tiso of the Field of Saint Peter."

Wrong or overstated facts
5. Tiso (ch. "Why the Church Remembers"): "under Saint Anthony's direction" stood after the death with no time. Note g says Saint Anthony's sermons set him on fire and he gave himself into the saint's direction as a Third Order disciple. Now told that way, with his death in 1234 last.
6. "And he was never well" (Padua Lent): the Latin has "aegrotatione continua" (continual illness) in that Lent only. Now "ill through all of it".
7. "Inside the house the friars and the Poor Ladies were afraid": Latin n. 37 has the friars afraid and the Poor Ladies just as afraid; no shared house. Also "shut the ringleaders up" was too strong: the Podesta "separated" them into another part of the city for the day. Fixed both.
8. Arles: "Only one of the brothers saw him" kept (n. 12 "cujusdam assidentium oculis"; Rigauld names Monaldo; Coleridge p. 81). The notes said n. 11, p. 201; it is n. 12, pp. 200–201. Also note 44 now has Woodcock p. 28 for the death of Saint Francis in 1226.
9. Ark and chest (Padua): Pius XII says Gregory IX had heard him preach and called him "Arca Testamenti" and "Sacrarum Scripturarum scrinium"; Coleridge p. 142 "storehouse of the Sacred Scriptures". The second title is now "a storehouse of the Holy Scriptures". Added Coleridge p. 142 and Dal-Gal to note 49.
10. "Then he became Custodian" (France): the sources disagree on the order (Rigauld names him Guardian in Limousin, and Custodian; Coleridge has Guardian at Le Puy first, then Custodian of Limoges). Now both offices, no order.
11. "The friars' Chronicle" (Tuesday custom; research section): the 1883 Chronicle is a Jesuit's edition (Coleridge), not a friars' work. Now "The English Chronicle of 1883 says it is well attested that no sufferer…" and "the Chronicle of Saint Anthony of Padua in its English edition of 1883".
12. The stolen book: "From that story people began to ask him for whatever they had lost" is stronger than Woodcock p. 35 ("generally supposed to be the origin"). Now "Many believe that this story is how people began…".
13. "Most likely he preached from memory": Woodcock p. 76 says "most likely he wrote from memory" and that he could not have preached from the notes as they stand. Sentence taken out of the story; the doubt, as Woodcock has it, is in "A Note on the Sources".
14. Acta Sanctorum volume: the volume is "Junii tomus tertius" (title page of the scan). The Sources list said tomus II and eleven notes said "vol. 2". Now tomus III, vol. 3 throughout.
15. "Italian" among the research languages: no Italian source was read. Now "Latin and English".
16. Note on the Sources: the Surius and Wadding insert comes after paragraph 12 (the Arles paragraph), not 11. The year line now reads "1195, 1210, and 1221 are Bollandist estimates, and so is about 1226 for the Arles chapter" (Saint Francis's death in 1226 is not an estimate).
17. Summary: "turned down" (God turning down a life) changed to "when he has other plans for it". Reverence.

Reverence and the story voice
18. "By God's will he had already been cheated of his great desire" now "denied" (Latin "divino nutu fraudatus").
19. The "God had decided otherwise" lines (Sicily): the book talk is gone ("Both early biographers believed…", "The early Life says…", "Rigauld says…"); God's act is told plainly, with the same Latin sense ("aliud a sensu humano decrevit": something other than human sense would choose).
20. Other places where a book spoke in the story and a plain telling does the same work: compassion for the martyrs, the name Fernando, "the friar who wanted the fullness of grace… Gratian", treasure on the road, "In his heart he still burned with zeal", "the Lord governed his tongue", "fastened to the cross of penance… a martyrdom", Saint Francis "as if to approve the sermon", "God had let the storm come first", the Field of Saint Peter, "his true home was already in heaven", "it was right to rejoice… to weep". Kept where the book gives its own view or a quoted phrase (Rigauld on the height of the cell, the pearl of great price, "a martyr of desire", the two writers on the hill, the two reasons for his leaving Lisbon). No scene was flattened and no chapter-ending line was touched.
