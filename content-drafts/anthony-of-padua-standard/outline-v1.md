# Anthony of Padua: scene outline (tier A)

**Key prefixes.** The three notes files number their sources separately. Every key here has a file prefix:
- **W-** = `notes-witnesses.md` (W-S1 Bollandist Life, probably Julian of Speyer; W-S2 Rigauld, CTS 1904; W-S3 Baring-Gould).
- **O-** = `notes-own-words.md` (O-S1 Benedict XVI 2010; O-S2 Woodcock 1911; O-S3 Lent sermons, Paris 1521; O-S4 Kerval 1904).
- **C-** = `notes-church-miracles.md` (C-S1 Catholic Encyclopedia; C-S2 Coleridge 1883; C-S5 Basilica site; C-S9 Messenger 2025).

## 1. The driving question

Fernando wanted to give his life for Christ all at once, as a martyr among the Saracens [W-S2 p.41; W-S1 p.196]. Illness and a storm stopped him, and he arrived in Italy sick, unknown, and wanted by no one [W-S1 pp.198-199]. Grace answered through obedience: ordered to speak, he found his gift, and he gave his life slowly instead, preaching and hearing confessions until his body gave out at 36 [W-S1 pp.200, 202].

He is famous as the wonder-worker of Padua and the saint people ask to find lost things, canonized in under a year [C-S1; W-S1 p.204]. In an age of famous preachers he was the learned canon who hid his learning in a kitchen [W-S1 p.199]. He also turned his preaching on usurers, the rich, and bad clergy, and Padua passed a law to protect debtors in his last Lent [O-S2 pp.81-83; C-S1].

## 2. Portrait

Humble to the point of hiding: at the hermitage he washed pots and the friars' feet, and no one knew he was learned [W-S2 p.44; W-S1 p.199]. He took the name Antony so that his family could not find him easily [W-S1 p.198]. His memory served him "in the place of books" [W-S2 p.33]. In the pulpit he was "gratiosus pariter et severus"; his hearers felt love and fear together, and famous preachers hid their faces in their sleeves [W-S1 p.200]. He was corpulent and often sick, yet he never refused to preach or hear confession [W-S1 p.202]. He called his body his "little ass", after Francis [O-S2 p.~66 n.1]. He had a dry wit: told he might become a saint, he answered that then they should thank God [W-S2 p.38]. He drew sermons from vines, spiders, cranes, and doves [O-S2 pp.77-80]. The notes show no clear fault; the nearest are his restlessness at St Vincent and his severity (the "mitre-bearer" rebuke) [W-S2 pp.33, 67-68].

Life with God: under attack at night he called on the Virgin [W-S1 p.202]. He saw the wound in Christ's side as the cleft of the rock where the soul nests like a dove [O-S2 pp.79-80]. He died singing "O gloriosa Domina" and saying "I see my Lord" [W-S2 pp.79-80].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** hook, a scene at the edge: the unchosen friar at Assisi, 1221.

**Draft:** At Pentecost in 1221 the brothers of Francis met at Assisi, and when the meeting ended the ministers chose the brothers they wanted for their regions. One brother was left over. He was a Portuguese priest, thin and sick, and he had come to Italy by accident. He had sailed to Morocco to die for Christ, fallen ill there, and a storm had blown his ship home onto Sicily. An early Life says it plainly: no one asked for Anthony. Ten years later, thirty thousand people stood in the fields of Padua to hear him, and not a sound came from them. [W-S1 pp.198-199; W-S2 pp.41, 75]

**Thesis (after the opening):** The Church calls him its Evangelical Doctor, because his written Sunday and feast-day sermons teach the whole Gospel through Scripture [C-S8; O-S1]. He was the first friar set to teach theology to the brothers [O-S2 pp.30-31]. His last Lent in Padua emptied the shops, reconciled enemies, and made usurers repay [W-S1 p.202]. Pope Gregory IX, who knew him, canonized him less than a year after his death [C-S1; W-S1 p.204]. Chapters 5, 8, 12, and 13 answer it.

## 4. Chapters

1. **Fernando Leaves Lisbon to Pray in Peace.** SC-01. Ends: eight quiet years of study at Coimbra, then the relics arrive.
2. **Fernando Becomes a Friar Named Anthony.** SC-02. Ends: he asks leave to go to the Saracens.
3. **Anthony Sails to Morocco to Die for Christ.** SC-03. Ends: the storm lands him in Sicily, and he hears of a meeting at Assisi.
4. **No One at Assisi Asks for Anthony.** SC-04, SC-05. Ends: the hermitage friars go down to an ordination.
5. **Anthony Is Ordered to Preach Without Warning.** SC-06. Ends: the order to preach everywhere.
6. **Anthony Preaches Against Heresy in Italy.** SC-07. Bridge: teaching theology at Bologna; the pope's "Ark of the Covenant". Ends: sent to France.
7. **Anthony Preaches and Leads the Friars in France.** SC-08, SC-09. Bridge: Guardian at Limoges, Custodian, Brive, Bourges. Ends: Francis is dead; the 1230 chapter frees Anthony to preach and asks him to write.
8. **All of Padua Comes to Hear Anthony.** SC-10, SC-11. Bridge: writing the sermons in winter 1230-31. Ends: on a hill after Easter he says Padua will soon be honoured.
9. **Anthony Prays in a Cell in a Walnut Tree.** SC-12. Ends: he falls ill at dinner and asks to go back to Padua.
10. **The Death of Anthony.** SC-13. Ends: the friars try to keep the death secret.
11. **Padua Fights Over Anthony's Body.** SC-14. Bridge: envoys, commission, canonization at Spoleto, 1232. Ends: the first feast in Padua, 13 June 1232.
12. **How Anthony Changed the Church.** SC-15. Bridge: the sermons, the basilica, Doctor of the Church 1946. Ends: the relics shown to 650,000 people in 1981.
13. **Why People Ask Anthony to Find Lost Things.** Bridge: Tuesdays, the stolen book, "Si quaeris". Ends: closes the loop: the man no one asked for, whom everyone now asks.

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — Leaving home, about 1210-1212 (St Vincent's outside Lisbon; Holy Cross, Coimbra)
- What happens: The cathedral-school boy joins the Augustinian canons, but friends and family visit so often that he loses his peace. After about two years he asks to move far away to Coimbra.
- Why it is here: first hard choice of God over home; a hard part.
- Have: cathedral and St Vincent's body [W-S1 p.198; W-S2 pp.31-32]; visits, two years, leave given "with difficulty" [W-S1 p.198; W-S2 p.33]; "like another Abraham" [W-S2 p.34]; memory for books [W-S2 p.33].
- Need: the Assidua's reason for leaving, in English; his age on entering.
- Where to look: W-S2 pp.31-34; W-S1 p.198.

### SC-02 — Coimbra, 1220 (Holy Cross; the friars' hermitage of St Antony)
- What happens: Prince Pedro brings the relics of five friars killed in Morocco through Coimbra. Poor friars beg at the door; Fernando asks them for their habit. A canon's farewell, his answer, and the new name.
- Why it is here: the wish for martyrdom is born.
- Have: relics [W-S1 p.198, note g; W-S2 pp.35-36]; war-horse image [W-S1 p.198; W-S2 p.36]; "I will take their habit... go to the fight" [W-S2 p.36]; canon's words and reply [W-S2 p.38]; name chosen to hide [W-S1 p.198; W-S2 p.39].
- Need: whom he spoke the "habit" words to.
- Testimony: Rigauld, W-S2 pp.36-38.
- Where to look: W-S2 pp.35-39; W-S1 p.198.

### SC-03 — Morocco and the storm, 1220-1221
- What happens: He sails to Morocco, falls gravely ill, cannot work, and sails home. Contrary winds drive him to Sicily.
- Why it is here: the plan fails; the central obstacle.
- Have: W-S1 pp.198-199; Rigauld's "efforts... were vain" [W-S2 p.41]; Bartholomew of Trent on the martyr's crown [W-S1 p.196].
- Need: how long the illness lasted; where in Sicily he landed and who took him in.
- Where to look: W-S1 pp.198-199 and notes.

### SC-04 — Pentecost chapter, 1221 (Assisi)
- What happens: Sick and weak, he goes to the chapter. All are sent off; no one asks for him. He asks Brother Gratian, minister of Romagna, to take him and teach him the Rule.
- Why it is here: the lowest point; the hook.
- Have: "Solus Antonius a nullo petebatur" [W-S1 pp.198-199]; Gratian [W-S1 p.199; W-S2 p.42]; Francis in the Order, no record of a meeting [W-S1 note n p.200].
- Need: Gratian's answer; a clean English rendering of the Latin line.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.199; W-S2 p.42.

### SC-05 — The hermitage of Monte Paolo, 1221-1222
- What happens: Crypt cell, bread and water; too weak to walk to the meal; he scrubs pots and hides his learning.
- Why it is here: chosen hiddenness; his life with God in prayer.
- Have: "simplicem inter simplices", "sub indocti specie" [W-S1 p.199]; weakness [W-S2 p.42]; pots and feet [W-S2 p.44].
- Need: how he spent his days in the cell, per the Assidua.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.199; W-S2 pp.42-44.

### SC-06 — The ordination meal, about 1222 (Forlì)
- What happens: The minister asks the Dominicans, then the friars, to speak; all refuse. He orders Anthony. Anthony begins simply, and the room is astonished.
- Why it is here: obedience opens the hidden gift.
- Have: W-S1 p.200; W-S2 pp.45-47; "not of man, but of God" [W-S2 pp.45-46]; "Never man spoke like this man" [W-S2 pp.46-47]; text Phil 2:8 by tradition [O-S2 pp.21-22].
- Need: his words of refusal in the Assidua.
- Testimony: W-S2 pp.45-46.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.200.

### SC-07 — Rimini, among the heretics (1220s)
- What happens: He preaches against the Patarines. Bononillo, a heretic leader for thirty years, is converted.
- Why it is here: the "fight" he wanted, won with words.
- Have: W-S1 p.200; W-S2 p.65 ("blinded by the pestilential mists"); Bonvillo's challenge [C-S2 pp.63-64] belongs to the mule legend: keep apart.
- Need: how the early Lives tell the conversion; the Patarines in one line.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.200; W-S2 p.65.

### SC-08 — The chapter at Arles, about 1226 (Provence)
- What happens: Anthony preaches on the title of the cross. Brother Monaldo sees Francis at the door, raised in the air, arms out like a cross, blessing the friars.
- Why it is here: the bond with Francis, whom he never met; reverence.
- Have: W-S1 p.200; W-S2 pp.65-66; Bartholomew [W-S1 p.196]; Celano [C-S7].
- Need: Celano's wording of the event.
- Testimony: W-S2 pp.65-66.
- Where to look: W-S2 pp.65-66.

### SC-09 — Rain held back, Creux-des-Arènes (Limoges)
- What happens: Storm clouds gather over an open-air crowd. He tells them not to move; the rain falls all around and not on them.
- Why it is here: France years; his trust in God in words.
- Have: Anthony's words [W-S2 p.71]; eyewitness friars [W-S2 pp.71-72].
- Need: crowd and setting details; names of Rigauld's witnesses.
- Testimony: W-S2 pp.71-72.
- Where to look: W-S2 pp.71-72.

### SC-10 — Lent 1231 (Padua)
- What happens: Daily sermons move to the fields; shops shut; thirty thousand silent. Enemies reconcile, usury is repaid, priests cannot hear all confessions, and he forgets to eat.
- Why it is here: the answer to the opening; his life spent.
- Have: W-S1 p.202; W-S2 pp.73-75; "thirty thousand" [W-S2 p.75]; fasting till evening [O-S2 p.81]; debtors' law 15 March 1231 [C-S1]; the old robber [W-S2 p.74]; a sample: "purple of the poor" [O-S2 p.82; O-S3 fol. LXXXV].
- Need: the Assidua's account of the crowds; the debtors' law in brief.
- Testimony: the old robber, W-S2 p.74.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.202; W-S2 pp.73-75.

### SC-11 — A night in Lent 1231 (Padua)
- What happens: The devil seizes his throat. He calls on the Virgin; light fills the room.
- Why it is here: his life with God; a trial.
- Have: W-S1 p.202 ("as he himself later told it among friends").
- Need: an English translation of the full passage.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.202.

### SC-12 — Camposampiero, spring to 13 June 1231
- What happens: From a hill he blesses the plain of Padua. Count Tiso builds him a cell in a walnut tree. At dinner on 13 June he collapses.
- Why it is here: the last retreat; prayer.
- Have: hill [W-S1 p.202; W-S2 p.77]; walnut tree [W-S1 p.202; W-S2 p.78; C-S9]; Tiso [W-S1 note g p.201]; collapse [W-S1 p.202].
- Need: his exact words on the hill.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.202; W-S2 pp.77-78.

### SC-13 — Arcella, 13 June 1231
- What happens: Carried on a cart toward Padua, he stops at Arcella. He confesses, sings "O gloriosa Domina", says "I see my Lord", sings the psalms, and dies.
- Why it is here: the death; the driving question resolved.
- Have: W-S1 p.202; W-S2 pp.78-80; ox cart [C-S9]; Brother Roger [W-S3 pp.187-188]; unction words [O-S2 p.91].
- Need: is the unction line in the early Life?
- Testimony: W-S2 pp.79-80.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.202.

### SC-14 — The fight for the body, June 1231 (Padua)
- What happens: Children cry the news. Two districts arm for the body; the bridge of boats is smashed; the bishop rules; the procession reaches St Mary's.
- Why it is here: the first proof of his fame.
- Have: W-S1 p.203; W-S2 pp.81-83; cry of the children [W-S1 p.203]; burial 17 June [C-S5]; the first pilgrims come barefoot [W-S1 p.203]; giant candles [C-S2 pp.189-190].
- Need: nothing.
- Where to look: W-S1 p.203.

### SC-15 — The tongue, 1263 (Padua)
- What happens: Bonaventure opens the coffin. The body is dust, but the tongue is red and whole. He kisses it and speaks.
- Why it is here: the post-death case; the preacher's mark.
- Have: C-S5; C-S2 p.198; C-S1; W-S2 pp.75-76.
- Need: nothing. Use one version of the words.
- Where to look: C-S2 p.198.

## 6. The saint in the Church

**Writings, sermons, and works**
- Sunday Sermons and Feast-day Sermons, written at Padua, winter 1230-31, at the request of the Cardinal of Ostia [W-S1 p.202; O-S2 p.69]. Chs 8, 12.
- Written as outlines, not finished talks [O-S2 p.76; O-S5]. Ch 12.
- Lent sermons printed at Paris, 1521 [O-S3]. Ch 12.

**What he changed in the Church**
- First teacher of theology to the friars, Bologna, 1222-23; Francis's letter disputed [O-S2 pp.30-31; O-S4 p.259]. Ch 6.
- Padua law to protect debtors, 15 March 1231 [C-S1]. Ch 8.
- Doctor of the Church, "Doctor Evangelicus", Pius XII, 1946 [C-S8; C-S11]. Ch 12.
- The basilica, about 1232-1301 [C-S12, weak]. Ch 12.

**Why he is famous**
- Assidua, 1232, written for the canonization liturgy [W-S4]. Ch 11.
- University students wrote to the pope as eyewitnesses, 1231 [C-S2 p.192]. Ch 11.
- Tuesday devotion from the Tuesday the body reached Padua [C-S2 p.191]. Ch 13.
- Lost things: the stolen book story, Liber miraculorum about 1367 [O-S2 p.35; O-S4 pp.259-260]; "Si quaeris" responsory [C-S1]. Ch 13.
- 1981 exhumation, 650,000 visitors [C-S5]. Ch 12.

**The path to sainthood**
- Cures at the tomb from the day of burial [W-S1 p.203]. Ch 11.
- Padua envoys within a month; a commission of the bishop and two priors [C-S2 pp.191-192]. Ch 11.
- Canonized by Gregory IX at Spoleto, Pentecost, 30 May 1232; feast 13 June [W-S1 p.204; C-S2 p.194]. Ch 11.

**Fact needs**
1. Exulta Lusitania felix: date and one line. Look: C-S11, C-S8.
2. Basilica start and finish dates. Look: C-S5; C-S4 after p.182.
3. Date and authorship of "Si quaeris". Look: C-S1; C-S2 footnote.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Name: Fernando at baptism; write "Anthony", though old translations say "Antony".
- Birth about 1195, estimate; no day [W-S1 note C].
- Death: Friday 13 June 1231, Arcella; joined the friars 1220 or 1221.
- No source says he met Francis. Francis's letter is disputed [O-S4 p.259].
- "Ark of the Covenant": 1227 [C-S3; C-S4] or 1230 [O-S2].
- Forlì sermon text (Phil 2:8) is tradition [O-S2 pp.21-22].
- Unction words are Rigauld's, not in the early Life [O-S2 p.91].
- Tongue: say only "1263".
- Benedict XVI is under copyright: one short line at most [O-S1].
- W-S1 is probably Julian's Life, not the Assidua: write "an early Life".
