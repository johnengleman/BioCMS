Saint Anthony Preaches in Italy and France

He exchanged the quiet of the hermitage for a kind of work he had never known. At Monte Paolo he had watched over his own soul. Now he went far and wide through the cities and fortified towns for the good of others. In northern Italy the heretics called Patarines taught that the world was ruled by two powers, one good and one evil, and Rimini was one of their strongholds. There he answered them with fitting arguments and brought very many back to the full Christian faith. One of their leaders had lived in the darkness of that error for thirty years. Saint Anthony led him into the light of the truth, and the man kept the Church's commands until he died.32

About this time he began to teach theology to the brothers at Bologna, in the friars' own house rather than at the city's famous university. Saint Francis had not at first meant his brothers to study theology, but he had changed his mind. The friars have handed down a short letter that Saint Francis sent about this work. “To my dearest Brother Anthony, Brother Francis sends greeting in Christ,” it begins. “It pleases me that you teach sacred theology to the brothers, provided that by such study you do not put out the spirit, as the Rule says. Farewell.”35

About 1224 the Order sent him over the mountains into France. He led the friars at Limoges and in the whole Limousin district, founded their first house at Brive, and for a time taught theology at Montpellier, where he lectured on the Psalms. Though he was their superior, he wanted to be taken for the lowest of the brothers. On the road he gave way in everything to the friar who walked with him.36

On those roads he carried nothing, yet God saw to it that he and his companions never went without what they needed. Bread and water were his food when he fasted, and he wore an iron chain next to his skin. Often his feet trembled so much that they could hardly hold him up. Fastened in this way to the cross of penance with Christ, he lived a kind of martyrdom that lasted his whole life.37

Prayer was the heart of it all. One night in the Limousin, while he prayed in the chapel after Compline, the last prayer of the day, some friars ran to him in tears. By moonlight they had seen a band of men tearing up the ripe grain of a good friend of the friars. He calmly told them to leave it alone and go on with their prayers, since the enemy only wanted to disturb their rest and break off their prayer. At daylight they went over the field and found that not a stalk had been touched.39

In the pulpit he was “gracious and severe” at once, so that his hearers felt love and fear rise in them together. He gave each hearer what that person could take in, and no one went away without some good. But he rebuked powerful men so sternly that famous preachers who heard him trembled at his courage. Red with shame at their own timid hearts, they wished themselves far away, and they hid their faces behind a hand or a sleeve.33

Once he preached at a meeting of the clergy at Bourges, with the archbishop among his hearers. Suddenly he turned to him and called him by the tall headdress of his rank: “I speak to you, mitre-bearer!” Then he rebuked the archbishop for faults hidden in his conscience. He quoted the Scriptures against them so clearly that the archbishop was moved to tears. When the meeting was over, the archbishop took him aside and humbly opened to him the wounds of his conscience. From that day the archbishop was more faithful to God, and he became a friend to the friars.41

This was the fight he had been given. He had once thirsted so eagerly for “the cup of suffering” that he sailed to Africa to meet a tyrant's sword. Now, for the sake of the truth, he gave way to no one, however great, and the fear of death did not move him. With wonderful firmness he stood up even to the tyranny of powerful men.32b

Another time in France an immense crowd gathered to hear him on a wide plain called the Creux-des-Arènes. The temples of the old pagan gods had once stood there. No church could hold so many. He had begun to preach, and the people were listening closely. Then thunder rolled and lightning flashed across the clouds, and the rain began to fall. The people were afraid, and they started to break up and leave. He calmed them and spoke quietly:45

“Do not be afraid. Do not move, and do not stop listening to the word of God. I trust in the One who never lets us trust in vain, that the rain will not touch you.”

— Saint Anthony, in Jean Rigauld's Life of Blessed Anthony46

The people obeyed and stayed where they were. God held the rain back from them, and all around the crowd it poured down in torrents. Saint Anthony went on preaching as long as he wished. When he had finished, the people looked at the drenched ground around them. Then they looked at the dry earth under their own feet. They praised the power of God, who is wonderful in his saints.47

About 1226 the friars of Provence gathered at Arles for their chapter meeting, and he preached to them. His subject was the title that Pilate had set over the cross: “Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews”. He spoke of it gently and warmly. Saint Francis was still alive then, but far away in Italy. As Saint Anthony preached, Saint Francis appeared at the door of the chapter-room. He was raised in the air with his arms stretched out in the form of a cross, and he blessed the friars. Only one of the brothers saw him with his own eyes, a friar of proven virtue named Monaldo.42

Saint Francis came, as if to approve the sermon and to show the hearers what they should imitate. He marked his sons with the sign of the cross. It was fitting that he came to that sermon. Saint Francis had carried the cross of Christ before all the friars. And from the day he entered the Order, Saint Anthony had longed for the sufferings of the cross. He “still continued to desire” them, even while he preached about them to others. The martyr he had meant to be lived on in him.43

In 1226 Saint Francis died, and the friars lost the father of their Order. He had been Saint Anthony's father too, the father who had blessed the friars at Arles while Saint Anthony preached the cross. After the news reached him, Saint Anthony left France and set out again for Italy.44
