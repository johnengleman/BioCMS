# Anthony of Padua: notes-witnesses (focus: life events and early Lives)

Researcher: witnesses agent. Date: 2026-10-01.
Method: read in full the Bollandist Life (Julian of Speyer text) and the Rigaldina in English. Read Baring-Gould for the late legends. The Latin Assidua itself was NOT found online in readable form (see "Not read").
Tag key: [S1 p.198] etc. Latin quotes come from OCR of the 1867 reprint. They carry scan errors. I quote only passages I checked as clean.

## 1. Identity (witnesses agent only: details of dates from the sources I read)

- Names: baptized Fernando (Fernandus, Ferdinand) [S1 p.198; S2 p.31]. Took the name Antony (Antonius) at the Franciscan hermitage called St Antony, near Coimbra [S1 p.198; S2 p.39]. Called "de Padua", "Patavinus", "Lusitanus" (the Portuguese) in the Latin sources [S1 p.199, 202].
- Born at Lisbon, near the cathedral (church of the Virgin, where the body of St Vincent lies) [S1 p.198; S2 p.31]. The Bollandists compute 1195 because Antony was in his 36th year at death (miracle no. 37 says he was 36) [S1 note C p.199]. No source I read gives a birthday.
- Death: Friday 13 June 1231, at the cell of the Poor Clares' friars at Arcella, outside Padua [S1 p.202 ("sexta feria... die Junii tertio decimo"); S2 p.80; S3 p.188].
- Canonization: Pope Gregory IX, at Spoleto, Pentecost 1232 (the Bollandists state Pentecost was 30 May 1232; their Easter figure is garbled in the OCR) [S1 p.204 and note h]. Less than one year after death [S1 p.204].
- Tradition: Catholic only (a post-1054 Western saint). Feast: 13 June (Roman calendar) [S1].
- Not in my focus: patronage, relics, doctor of the Church. See the church-miracles notes.

Early Lives in order, with S4 and S2 as authority (not checked against the Latin):
1. Assidua (Vita prima), anonymous, 1232, for the canonization office. About 14 pp. on his life before Padua, 24 pp. on Padua and death, 40 pp. of miracles [S4]. Informants: Soeiro, Bishop of Lisbon, then at the papal court, and other friars [S4; S2 intro p.14].
2. Julian of Speyer: Officium rhythmicum (rhymed office) and a Life, 1230s, built on the Assidua. Adds the Celano incident [S4].
3. Dialogus (about 1245, under Minister General Crescentius): adds nothing new [S4].
4. John Peckham, about 1280: new research in Portugal and Italy [S4].
5. Raymundina (Peter Raymond of St Romain, after 1293) [S2 intro p.18] and Rigaldina (Rigauld, about 1293-1300) [S2; S4]. Rigauld has "four splendid miracles" from Limousin [S2 intro p.19].
6. Later: Liber miraculorum (1367 per S2 intro p.19), Fioretti, Bartholomew of Pisa's Liber conformitatum, Surius and Wadding (16th-17th c.). S2 says the Liber miraculorum is the only source for his time in France in older biographies and mixes truth with later embellishment.
- Gap: Julian and Assidua say almost nothing about the years from Forlì to the 1230 Chapter [S2 intro p.16; S4]. S2 suggests the cause: the quarrel with Brother Elias, the minister general, which could not be told under his generalate (1232-1239). That is the 1904 editor's guess. Mark as opinion.

## 3. Timeline

Dates marked "(BL)" are Bollandist computations in S1 notes. Treat as estimates.

- 1195 (BL): Fernando born in Lisbon. Parents "just before God"; his family house stood near the cathedral [S1 p.198; S2 p.31]. NEW detail: the Bollandists record, from the 17th-century Spanish Epitome of Pacheco, that the house was later turned into the church of St Antony, and that his mother's tomb lies in St Vincent. This is late [S1 note b p.199].
- Boyhood: given to the church of the Virgin (cathedral) for letters and morals. He did not follow the pleasures of youth [S1 p.198; S2 p.32].
- About 1210 (BL, doubtful): enters the house of the Augustinian Canons Regular of St Vincent outside Lisbon. Friends and family visit so often that peace is lost. After about two years he asks for transfer to Holy Cross (Santa Cruz) at Coimbra. The superior gave leave with difficulty [S1 p.198 ("ferme duobus annis"); S2 p.33]. The Bollandist note on the date: "an. 1210 Sanctus appulit" [S1 note e p.199].
- Coimbra, about 8 years: reads the Fathers. "His memory served him in the place of books" [S2 p.33; S1 p.198 "memoria pro codicibus uteretur" in ch. 2]. Santa Cruz was the house of St Theotonius [S1 note f p.199].
- 1220: five Franciscan friars (Berard, Otto, Peter, Adjutus, Accursius) killed at Marrakesh. The Infante Pedro of Portugal brings their relics through Coimbra and tells of the miracles [S1 p.198 and note g p.199 (Bollandists give year 1220, feast 10 Jan); S2 p.35]. Rigauld has the friars as "valiant athletes" and Pedro as saved "from great dangers" by their merits [S2 p.35-36]. The news strikes Fernando "like a war-horse ready for the day of battle, or an elephant excited by the sight of blood" (both Julian and Rigauld use the image) [S1 p.198; S2 p.36].
- 1220 (BL, after the relics): five poor friars come to beg alms at the Augustinian house. Fernando takes them aside and asks for the habit. They return on the day set, and clothe him [S1 p.198; S2 p.36-37]. A canon says to him, "Go, you may become a saint." He answers that if so, they should praise God [S1 p.198; S2 p.38]. See Q1.
- Name change: the friars' hermitage was called St Antony (Olivais; "de Olivares" in popular speech, per Bollandists, with a chapel and image placed there in 1650 by a Jesuit) [S1 p.198 and note l p.200]. He takes the name "Antonius" so that those who looked for him would not find him easily [S1 p.198; S2 p.39].
- 1220-1221: leaves for Morocco with leave of his superior. Falls gravely ill, stays long, cannot work for the faith. Sails home. Contrary winds land him in Sicily (Messina or Taormina per the Bollandist notes) [S1 p.198-199; S2 p.41]. Rigauld: "He arrived, indeed, among the Saracens, but his efforts to obtain the much coveted palm of martyrdom were vain" [S2 p.41].
- 1221 (BL, Pentecost, 30 May): General Chapter at Assisi, about to meet. Antony, weak and sick, goes. After it, all are sent away. "Solus Antonius a nullo petebatur", because he was unknown and looked useless [S1 p.198-199]. He goes to Brother Gratian, minister of Romagna, and asks to be taken and taught the discipline of the Order [S1 p.199; S2 p.42]. NEW for English pages: the Bollandists say the Chapter had Elias as vicar of the General and Francis present in the Order; they do not say Antony met Francis [S1 note n p.200]. I found NO source that says Antony met Francis in person. Rigauld says Francis lived "in a distant part of Italy" at the Arles event [S2 p.65].
- Hermitage of Monte Paolo near Forlì: a cell in a crypt, bread and water, so weak he could not carry himself to the meal [S1 p.199; S2 p.42]. NEW detail: "Simplicem inter simplices" is the phrase for his life there. He hides the gift of wisdom "sub indocti specie" [S1 p.199].
- Forlì ordination (age 26 per the Bollandists, perhaps 1222 per Wadding via the notes; Bishop Albert per the Bollandists' reading of Robert of Lisle): friars come to be ordained. At the meal or collation the local minister asks the Dominicans present, then the Franciscans, to speak. All refuse. He turns to Antony, who knows only kitchen work. Antony says he is not fit. He obeys the order. He begins "simply", then "so great was the splendour of his words" that all marvel [S1 p.200; S2 p.45-46]. See Q3, Q4. The Minister General hears of it and orders him to preach [S1 p.200].
- Preaching years (dates uncertain): Romagna, Rimini and the Patarines (Bononillo the heresiarch, thirty years in error, converted at Rimini) [S1 p.200; S2 p.65]. Papal court calls him "Arca testamenti" (ark of the covenant) [S1 p.200].
- Arles (Provence), a Provincial Chapter: Antony preaches on the title of the cross. Francis, "alive at that time but in a distant part of Italy", appears in the air at the door, arms stretched in the form of a cross, and blesses the friars [S1 p.200; S2 p.65-66]. Rigauld names an eyewitness: Friar Monaldo [S2 p.66]. Bartholomew of Trent (c.1240) also tells it [S1 p.196]. The Bollandists guess the year 1226 [S1 note e p.201]. First attested early: Celano's Vita prima (mentioned by S4) [S4].
- France, years uncertain (Rigauld): Antony is Guardian at Limoges, then Custodian of Limousin [S2 p.29, 47]. Founds the friary at Brive and a hermitage near it (cave outside Brive per the Wadding extract) [S2 p.53; S1 p.203]. At Limoges on Holy Thursday night he preaches at St Pierre-du-Queyroix while he is also present in choir to read his lesson at Matins in the friary [S2 p.47-48] (bilocation; recorded 60-70 years later). At St Junien the platform collapses; no one is hurt [S2 p.66-67]. At the Synod of Bourges he addresses the archbishop [S2 p.67-68] (see Q14). Rigauld writes "Certain Friars, worthy of belief, related to me the following". At Creux-des-Arenes (Limoges) he stops the rain over the crowd [S2 p.71] (Q10).
- Italy, 1227-1230: the Bollandist embolism (from Surius and Wadding) has him sent from Sicily to Rome in 1227, heard by Pope Gregory IX, then Guardian at Le Puy, then at Brive; later quarrel with Elias at Rome [S1 p.203, p.201-202, Embolismus]. LATE. Surius and Wadding wrote in the 1500s and 1600s. I mark this section "late narrative" below.
- 1230, Chapter of Assisi, Francis's relics moved: Antony freed from office (Minister Provincial) and given freedom to preach everywhere. The Cardinal of Ostia asks him to write the festival sermons as he had written the Sunday sermons [S1 p.202; S2 p.73]. Both sources agree: "one year before his death".
- Winter 1230-31: at Padua, writing the Sermons. Lent 1231 (Ash Wednesday 5 Feb, Easter 23 March 1231 per the Bollandists, who computed) [S1 note c p.202]: daily preaching, then in fields because no church holds the crowds [S1 p.202; S2 p.73]. Shops shut. "In trigintα vel amplius hominum millibus" (thirty thousand or more) no sound [S1 p.202; S2 p.75] (Q6). Enemies reconciled, prisoners freed, usury repaid, prostitutes repent, priests cannot hear all confessions [S1 p.202; S2 p.74].
- Early Lent 1231: at night the devil seizes his throat and would have strangled him. He calls on the Virgin. A light fills the room and the devil flees. He told this "familiariter" later [S1 p.202] (no Rigauld parallel). NEW for English pages.
- After Easter: stands on a hill and looks over the plain of Padua and says it will be honoured "in proximo" [S1 p.202; S2 p.77].
- Harvest, June 1231: he goes to Campo San Pietro (Camposampiero) north of Padua. The lord, Tiso, builds him a cell in a walnut tree (Rigauld: "cell... on a height"; Julian: cell on a walnut tree with branches spreading from one trunk) [S1 p.202 and note h; S2 p.78]. Tiso later became a tertiary disciple of Antony and died 31 Jan 1234 (Bollandists quote Scardeone) [S1 note g p.201].
- 13 June 1231: at the dinner hour he is struck by sudden illness, loses strength. To avoid burdening the poor friars at Campo San Pietro he asks to be taken back to Padua. A carriage carries him. Near Padua a friar meets him and advises the Poor Clares' friars' house at Arcella rather than the noisy city friary [S1 p.202; S2 p.78-79]. Baring-Gould names the companion: Brother Roger [S3 p.187-188; S1 note i p.202]. After confession and communion he sings "O gloriosa Domina". He looks up. Asked what he sees he says "I see my Lord". He receives unction, sings the penitential psalms and "like one quietly falling asleep" dies [S1 p.202; S2 p.79-80] (Q8). Friday 13 June [S1 p.202].
- The friars keep the death quiet. Children run through Padua crying "Mortuus est Pater Sanctus : mortuus est, inquiunt, Sanctus Antonius" [S1 p.203; S2 p.81] (Q9). Quarrel for the body: the Capo di Ponte district armed to keep it; the friars wanted burial at St Mary's. Night attempts to break into the cell fail: attackers are "stupefied and struck with blindness" [S1 p.203; S2 p.81-82]. Wood coffin laid in a shallow grave in the summer heat; rumor of theft sends a mob with swords and clubs [S1 p.203]. Minister arrives at vespers. The Podesta fines 100 pounds for violence. After four days the bishop rules for the friars [S1 p.203; S2 p.82]. Podesta builds a bridge of boats; the Capo di Ponte men smash it; both sides arm; the friars and Poor Clares pray; the Podesta confines the ringleaders; the body goes in procession to St Mary's [S1 p.203; S2 p.82-83].
- After burial: cures start the same day. Paduans of Capo di Ponte come barefoot to the tomb [S1 p.203].
- Canonization process: Padua sends envoys (bishop, clergy, friars, students, the Podesta), the pope orders an inquiry. Two cardinal legates then in the March of Treviso are in Padua and write to the Curia. Bishop of Sabina (BL: John of Abbeville) is told to examine the miracles. Some object that not a year has passed. The pope, in a dream, finds relics for an altar and recognizes the body in the shroud; all agree [S1 p.204].
- 30 May 1232 (Pentecost): Gregory IX, at Spoleto, with a solemn ceremony, reads the approved miracles, raises his hands, names Antony a saint, and sets the feast on 13 June [S1 p.204 and note h]. Miracles at the process: 19 lame cured, paralytics, 6 blind, 3 deaf, 3 mute, 2 epileptics, 2 with fevers, 2 raised from the dead [S1 p.204] (church-miracles agent to list).
- 1263: St Bonaventure, Minister General, moves the body; the tongue is found fresh and red; he holds it up and says it proves how truly Antony preached [S2 p.75-76; S1 p.196 ("S. Bonaventura inde levavit")]. Rigauld puts this in the middle of his preaching chapter. Legend: a later translation in 1350 [S1 p.196-197].

## 4. Character (one line each)

- Humble to the point of hiding his learning: washes pots and the friars' feet and kisses them [S2 p.44].
- Weak and thin when he first arrives in Italy; unknown, useless to others [S1 p.199].
- Severe in the pulpit: famous preachers trembled and hid their faces behind a hand or sleeve [S1 p.200].
- Sweet and strong together: "gratiosus pariter et severus", so listeners felt "amorem simul... ac timorem" (love and fear) [S1 p.200].
- Corpulent by nature and chronically sick, yet never refused preaching or confession [S1 p.202 "ex corpulentia premeretur"] NEW; unusual against the popular thin saint. The Bollandists add that the Lateran mosaic shows him lean while Cardoso describes a thin-faced man; they prefer the Paduan portrait of a gravely young face [S1 note e p.202].
- Wanted martyrdom; went to the Saracens and was refused by illness. Both Lives state it as his driving wish [S1; S2 p.41].
- Hidden sins: people said he appeared at night to sleepers and told them to confess sins that only God knew [S1 p.201 chap. 15 of the Surius-based text]. Late.
- Obedient even when afraid: accepts the order to speak, then the order to preach [S1 p.200].
- Went fully by memory, not books [S1 p.198; S2 p.33].
- Admirers: the pope called him "Arca testamenti" [S1 p.200]. Bartholomew of Trent: "Antonius, quem ipse vidi et cognovi" [S1 p.196].
- Critics and enemies: heretics at Rimini and Padua (Rigauld), some who laughed at his preaching [S2 p.68]. Augustinians of Lisbon and Coimbra were sore about the loss of him; Pope Gregory IX sent a brief in 1232 ordering their prior to stop harassing the friars (Pacheco, via Bollandist note k; late, 17th c.) [S1 note k p.199].
- Friars' opposition to Elias (Wadding, Surius) [S1 p.201-202]. LATE; see Embolismus.

## 5. Quotations (max 25; all rights public-domain)

Latin: from OCR in S1 (spelling as printed). English: the 1904 CTS translation (S2), copied exactly. Where I give "our English", it is mine.

Q1. S2 p.38. A canon at Coimbra: "Go, I tell you, go. You will perhaps become a saint." Antony: "When you hear that I am a saint, surely you will give thanks to God." (S1 p.198 Latin: "Vade, inquit. vade, quia forsitan Sanctus eris." OCR of the answer is poor; use English.)
Q2. S2 p.36, on hearing of the Moroccan martyrs. "I will take their habit," he said, "and so will I, too, go to the fight."
Q3. S2 p.45-46, Forlì: "it was marvellous that an unknown Religious, of whose learning the Friars themselves were unaware, should have been so persistently compelled to preach! But this was not of man, but of God."
Q4. S2 p.33: "his memory served him in the place of books, and he was presently filled with the Spirit of Wisdom."
Q5. S1 p.200 (Julian), after the first words at Forlì: paraphrase, no marks. The hearers marvelled at the sudden turn and admitted they had hardly ever heard such things. (Latin has "vix unquam audisse se talia faterentur", OCR, not quoted.)
Q6. S2 p.75, Padua: "amongst thirty thousand men, and even more, not the slightest sound could be heard when the servant of God was speaking."
Q7. S2 p.65-66, Francis at Arles: "appeared at the door of the Chapter-room, raised in the air, with his arms stretched out in the form of a cross blessing the Friars".
Q8. S2 p.79-80, last words. Asked what he saw: He answered: "I see my Lord."
Q9. S1 p.203 (Julian, clean OCR): "Mortuus est Pater Sanctus : mortuus est, inquiunt, Sanctus Antonius." S2 p.81, English: "The holy father is dead; St Antony is dead!"
Q10. S2 p.71, Creux des Arenes: "Fear not ; do not move nor cease listening to the Divine Word, for I trust in Him who never suffers us to trust in vain that the rain will not touch you."
Q11. S2 p.68 (the fish, see section 8): "Since you show yourselves unworthy to hear the Word of God, behold, I turn to the fishes, that your unbelief may be put to shame."
Q12. S2 p.56, the poisoned dish: "I do this not to tempt God, but to show my constant and earnest zeal for your salvation, and the triumph of the Faith."
Q13. S2 p.69, the horse, Antony to the heretic: "If the horse you so often ride should adore the Body of Christ really present under the species of bread, would you believe in the truth of the Sacrament of our Lord as taught by the Church?"
Q14. S2 p.67-68, Bourges synod: "I speak to thee, mitre-bearer!" (Rigauld: "Certain Friars, worthy of belief, related to me the following".)
Q15. S2 p.74, the old robber, c.1292: "I was a robber by profession," ... "I belonged to a gang of a dozen brigands; we lived in the forest and plundered all the passers-by."
Q16. S1 p.196, Bartholomew of Trent, c.1240: "Antonius, quem ipse vidi et cognovi, Hispanus fuit genere". Our English: "Anthony, whom I myself saw and knew, was a Spaniard by birth." (Latin clean through "genere". The word "Hispanus" is the writer's own for Iberian.)

Q18. S2 p.38 (Rigauld, on the bed at Coimbra): "the bed on which Antony slept in the dormitory has become an object of the greatest veneration". Rigauld heard it "by the Custodian of St. Jacques".
Q19. S2 p.34 (Rigauld, on the move to Coimbra): "he wished, like another Abraham, to go forth out of his country and from his kindred."
Q20. S2 p.46-47 (Rigauld on Forlì): "Never man spoke like this man."
Q21. S2 p.65, Rigauld on the heretic at Rimini: "a noted heresiarch, named Bononillo, who had been for thirty years blinded by the pestilential mists of error."
Q22. S2 p.82, the Capo di Ponte rioters, then the pope's inquiry: "not to a coffin, but to a pearl of great price" (Rigauld on the crowd's cult).
Q23. S2 p.76, Bonaventure on the tongue: "the tongue so miraculously preserved was a proof of how faithfully the Man of God had preached the immortal truth."
Q24. S1 p.202 (Julian), the devil at Lent: "sicut ipse postmodum familiariter retulit". Our English: "as he himself later told it among friends". (paraphrase)
Q25. S4 (Messenger on Celano's Vita prima): an early mention in 1228 of "one whose mind the Lord had opened to understand the Scriptures" etc. I do not quote it: facts only.

## 6. Scenes (max 10)

1. Lisbon, the cathedral school: a boy raised in the church of the Virgin, with St Vincent's body in the same church, who "never gave way to his passions" [S2 p.32; S1 p.198].
2. Coimbra, 1220: relics of five martyred friars carried through the city by the Infante Pedro. A young canon stands in the crowd and the news hits him like a trumpet [S1 p.198; S2 p.35-36].

4. After the Assisi chapter, 1221: crowds leave; one thin, ill, unknown friar waits for someone to take him [S1 p.198-199].
5. Monte Paolo near Forlì: a crypt cell, bread and water; he nearly falls on the way to the meal [S1 p.199; S2 p.42].
6. Forlì, ordination day: three groups of friars refuse to speak. The minister turns to the kitchen friar [S1 p.200; S2 p.45].
7. Arles chapter: Antony preaches on the cross; Francis, seen by one friar, floats at the door with outstretched arms [S1 p.200; S2 p.65-66].
8. Padua Lent 1231: the fields at night with lights, thirty thousand silent, shops shut [S1 p.202; S2 p.73-75].
9. Campo San Pietro: Tiso builds a cell in a walnut tree. A sudden pain at dinner and the carriage back to Padua [S1 p.202; S2 p.78].
10. Arcella, 13 June 1231: "I see my Lord". Children running through Padua. The fight for the body [S1 p.202-203; S2 p.79-83].

## 7. Miracles (one line each; the church-miracles agent will complete)

Status for all: recorded in early Lives; not an approved list unless stated.
- Fernando, Coimbra: the future saint's bed in the dormitory venerated; also, after his canonization, he appears to a canon to announce the death of the Queen of Portugal almost at the time (Rigauld, told by the Custodian of St Jacques) [S2 p.38] (later, popular).
- Limoges, Holy Thursday night, bilocation: preaching at St Pierre-du-Queyroix while reading the lesson in the friars' choir [S2 p.47-48] (Rigauld; compared to Ambrose at Martin's burial).
- Provence, village woman: broken glass mended and spilt wine restored [S2 p.49-50] (Rigauld).
- Limoges: novice Peter freed from temptation by Antony breathing on him [S2 p.51] (Rigauld).
- Brive: cabbages carried in the rain with no wetness; told by Pierre de Brive, canon of Noblac, who heard it from his mother [S2 p.53-54] (Rigauld, with named witness).
- Italy: eats poisoned dish served by heretics unharmed; they convert [S2 p.55-56] (Rigauld).
- Arles chapter: Francis appears [S1 p.200; S2 p.65-66] (Julian; Rigauld, with eyewitness Monaldo; Bartholomew of Trent).
- Rimini: heresiarch Bononillo converted [S1 p.200; S2 p.65] (Julian; Rigauld).
- St Junien: platform collapses, no one hurt, as he foretold [S2 p.66-67] (Rigauld).
- Bourges: archbishop moved to tears by the rebuke [S2 p.67-68] (Rigauld).
- The fish: see section 8 [S2 p.68-69; S3 p.188] LATE LEGEND.
- The horse (mule in later tellings): see section 8 [S2 p.69-70; S3 p.188] LATE LEGEND.
- Creux des Arenes: rain stopped over the crowd [S2 p.71-72] (Rigauld, with eyewitness friars).
- Padua death and burial: children cry the news; blinded attackers cannot enter the cell; the mob and the bridge [S1 p.203; S2 p.81-83] (Julian; Rigauld).
- Cures at the tomb the same day; nineteen lame, six blind, two dead raised, etc. [S1 p.204] (process; approved by canonization).
- The woman who fell in water stays dry; the woman guarded by sparrows; shipwrecked men saved [S1 p.204] (process).
- 1263 translation: the tongue found fresh and red [S2 p.75-76; S1 p.196] (Rigauld names Bonaventure).
- Late: Lisbon boy resurrected to clear Antony's father; Antony appears in court to clear his father's debt; capon turned to fish bones; owl served as capon; Pope hears sermon [S1 Embolismus pp.200-203] (Surius and Wadding, late; mark LATE).

## 8. Other

### Legends and late stories (mark for the writer)

- Fish sermon. Rigauld (c.1293-1300) has it: heretics near Padua "openly ridiculed his words", Antony goes to the river, says the words in Q11, and the fish crowd, raise their heads and open their mouths [S2 p.68-69]. So the story is NOT only a Fioretti story, as S2 intro p.19 says. It is 60-70 years after the death and told as a "God made irrational creatures honour" story. Baring-Gould puts it at Rimini, at the mouth of the Marecchia, and calls the fish part "no doubt an addition" by those who missed the "biting satire" [S3 p.188]. Place: Padua area in Rigauld; Rimini in later writers. Do not state either as fact.
- Mule/horse. Rigauld: an "obstinate and crafty heretic", a horse starved for three days, oats on one side and the Host on the other; the horse kneels [S2 p.69-70]. Rigauld took it from "a small treatise on his miracles". He gives no town. Baring-Gould: a heretic at Toulouse, "my mule", the mule breaks halter and kneels as Antony carries the Host to a dying man [S3 p.188]. Baring-Gould notes the mule became his emblem in art [S3 p.188]. So the mule is a later form; horse is older. LATE LEGEND.
- Ezzelino da Romano and Antony. Baring-Gould tells it at Verona [S3 p.185-186]. The Bollandists print it in the Liber miraculorum text (no. 33 and no. 68, in the AS "Miracula") [AS lines 47,989 and 49,670, same S1 volume]. I did not check the date of Ezzelino's rule against 1231. Under "Other: to check".
- Antony and the Child Jesus in a window (a nobleman in Limousin watches through a chink) [S3 p.188]. No early source read. LATE.
- Preaching in Italian with perfect ease though born far away (Surius embolismus n. 16) [S1 p.201]. LATE (Surius).
- Preaching to Elias before the pope (embolism n. 21-25): Antony and Adam of Marisco resist Elias, the pope deposes Elias [S1 p.201-202; S3 p.186-187 gives a simplified account]. Source is Wadding; neither Julian nor Rigauld has it [S2 intro p.16 on the silence]. Treat as disputed.
- Antony sets up the first flagellants (embolism n. 17): late, Wadding [S1 p.201].

### Conflicts between sources

- Birth: no source gives a day. Bollandists estimate 1195 [S1 note C]. The 36-year figure is in the process miracles [S1 p.199 note C].
- Name of the Lisbon church of his youth: cathedral (S1) versus St Vincent (Canons) [S1 p.198].
- The Augustinian house: Julian says "S. Augustini... praefatae civitati vicinum" (near Lisbon) then Holy Cross at Coimbra [S1 p.198; S2 p.33]. Same in both.
- Forlì ordination year: S1 note says age 26 (c.1221-22). Rigauld has no year.
- Who ordered him to preach: Julian says the Minister General; Rigauld says "the General" [S1 p.200; S2 p.46].
- Arles: Julian puts it in a Provincial Chapter; Bollandists say year guess 1226 [S1 note e]. Rigauld says "at Arles, in Provence".
- Lateran mosaic shows a bearded Antony; Bollandists argue that the order did not allow priests to wear beards in the 13th century and that the portrait is later than the Padua likeness [S1 note e p.202].
- Date of Assidua authorship: S2's 1904 editor guessed Celano; S4 says anonymous friar in the Padua community. I follow S4.
- Name of the Poor Clares' site: "Cellas" (Bartholomew; Julian) = Arcella [S1 note k p.202; S1 p.196]. Later writers (Pacheco, Cardoso) wrote "Ara coeli" mistakenly [S1 note k].

### Surprises for the writer

- Both early Lives and Bartholomew name the Francis vision at Arles, and Bartholomew says he saw Antony himself. No source I read has Antony meeting Francis alone.
- The Padua crowds: a procession of friars and citizens carried candles so large that they had to be cut to fit into the church; processions came barefoot [S1 p.204]. NEW vivid detail.
- After the miracle in Padua the people of Capo di Ponte (who fought for the body) were the first to come barefoot to the tomb [S1 p.203].
- The bishop of Lisbon, Soeiro, was at the papal court when the Assidua was written and supplied the Lisbon part [S4; S2 p.14].
- Bonaventure's 1263 tongue scene is told in Rigauld's chapter on preaching, as proof of the truth of his preaching [S2 p.75-76].
