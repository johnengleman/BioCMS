# Check: miracles.html (Anthony of Padua, standard)

Original kept as miracles.html.before-check (47 accounts). The file now has 65 accounts. Checked against the raw OCR of the Acta Sanctorum (Julian's Life, the Embolismus, the Liber miraculorum, the appendix), Rigauld 1904, Coleridge 1883, Kerval 1904, C. M. Antony 1911, the Catholic Encyclopedia, Baring-Gould, Butler, and the Basilica pages. Lint: no MUST FIX; one soft note on average sentence length (12.1, many short Status lines).

## Errors fixed

1. Page numbers for the Latin Life were wrong throughout. The printed page headers in the OCR give: Rimini p.200, Arles p.201, devil p.204, hill p.205, children and break-in p.207, cures p.208, dream and canonization miracles p.209 (not 200, 202, 203, 204). Embolismus pp.201-203. Butler's rain is p.179 (not 178); Ezzelino pp.179-180. Baring-Gould mule pp.188-189.
2. Canonization list (c01): "some people freed from paralysis" now five paralyzed and five hunchbacked (Rigauld p.90 gives the counts; the Latin OCR is garbled). Added Rigauld as a second source.
3. "Our sources do not give the forty-five cases one by one" was wrong. The Acta Sanctorum appendix (from p.210, Ancona manuscript) prints many by name. Sentence replaced.
4. Tunic (monk's temptation): the old note said it was not in Rigauld. It is: Rigauld pp.60-61. Account rewritten from Rigauld.
5. Leonardo's foot: Rigauld pp.61-62 tells it (in Spain, with the mother's words), and the Liber miraculorum n.34 (p.223) tells it at Padua. The old text gave a Catholic Encyclopedia quotation as the chronicle's. Fixed and attributed.
6. Glass (Provence): the glass was borrowed from a neighbor, not the woman's own. Fixed (Rigauld p.49).
7. Cardinal's dream: the Pope was consecrating a church, not "blessing" it. Fixed.
8. Cures at the tomb: both Julian and Rigauld say those who could not get near were healed outside. The text had given this to Rigauld only. Fixed.
9. Fish sermon: Coleridge gives the sermon, which begins "My brothers, the fishes"; it is not Anthony's "first words". Fixed. The Liber (n.1-4, p.216) also sets it at the river mouth at Rimini.
10. Miser's heart: Coleridge pp.118-120 sets it at Florence (Bonaventure as authority). Added. Tuscany stays with the Basilica.
11. "Soldier's wife" and the Basilica's "violent husband" are one story told in two places. Merged. The old text said the Basilica lists "a soldier of Padua"; it does not.
12. Stolen book: the 1911 book tells the devil offering to carry the novice over a flooded river, and calls the book "notes"; Coleridge says "sermons on the Psalms". The old text said both call it "notes". Fixed.
13. Child Jesus: Kerval p.261 (the Liber, window, host kept secret until after the saint's death). Coleridge says keyhole. Both given.
14. Arcella: the Latin says "cella of the friars"; Arcella is Coleridge's naming. Now attributed to the chronicle.
15. Sparrows: the woman was set to guard millet (the Bollandist note); text now says so.
16. Mule: added the Toulouse form (Liber n.5, Coleridge p.73 and note with the name Guialdo, Basilica, Baring-Gould). Merged into one account.
17. Baring-Gould's Montpellier story is a different tale (the gradual, no Easter or Holy Thursday). Moved to the new Montpellier Easter account.
18. Ezzelino: Coleridge sets the scene at Bassano; Butler and Baring-Gould at Verona; the Liber prints another telling (n.33). Added.
19. Lisbon dead man: Coleridge adds the request for release from excommunication and the line "I am come to clear the innocent". The Basilica's version differs (the dead man names the murderer). Both stated.
20. Tongue (d11): removed "no Church document declares it a miracle" (argues with the story). Basilica and CNA facts checked.
21. Voice from the tomb (d10): the Basilica gives 1256 and Saint Anthony's appearance to two friars. The text no longer says "we calculate 1256".

## Added accounts (all read in their sources)

From the brief: Poor Clare cleansed, boy's neck swelling (relapse), knight's glass (merged with Aleardino's glass, c04), cleric who mocked the inquiry, murder plot stopped, arm healed and lost again (Latin Life n.45-46, p.209); dead youth raised at Gemona (Coleridge pp.91-92; Bollandist note p.203); notary at Le Puy (Embolismus p.202; Coleridge pp.78-80); frogs at Montpellier; Easter Alleluias at Montpellier; Toulouse mule (merged).

Also added, because the same sources hold them: the baby who names his father at Ferrara (Coleridge pp.116-118); Eurilia (full Basilica page); Thomazino/Tommy (Rigauld pp.99-100 and Basilica, one story); the friars' field in Limousin (Rigauld pp.57-58); six accounts from Brother Peter Raymund's 1293 collection in Rigauld ch. 10 (Cambius, the lady of Forlì, Bernardin, the deaf and mute servant, the magic circle, the Portuguese woman's parchment).

Note on the knight, nun, boy, and cleric: Julian n.44-45 puts them inside the approved list. n.46 (plot, arm) says they were true but "not so solemnly approved"; the text says so.

## Owner's rules applied

- "Pater Sanctus": the children's cry is now told as "crying out that Saint Anthony was dead" (no quotation marks), so no word inside a quotation was changed. No "Holy Father" remains.
- "Saint" before every saint's name in the body and headings (Anthony, Francis, Clare, Bonaventure). Blessed Luke Belludi keeps "Blessed". Quotations and Source lines unchanged.
- Tradition: group headings now "Stories told of Saint Anthony in later centuries"; "legend", "late", "contested", "disputed", "doubt" removed from headings and Status lines. Each Status line names the teller and the century. Scholars' views (Catholic Encyclopedia on Ezzelino, Baring-Gould on the fish) moved to the closing "A Note on the Sources".
