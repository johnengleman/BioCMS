# Check: Anthony of Padua (standard, tier A)

Checked against the raw text of Julian's Life (Acta Sanctorum OCR), Rigauld 1904, Coleridge 1883, Woodcock 1911, the 1521 Lent sermons, Pius XII (Latin, vatican.va), Benedict XVI (vatican.va), and the Catholic Encyclopedia. Originals kept as biography.html.before-check and summary.txt.before-check. Lint shows no MUST FIX and no CHECK lines.

Settled points confirmed: "some poor friars"; the robe resolve not spoken; Gratian silent; pots and feet as a general account; "collation" as the evening gathering; no Limoges at the rain scene; robber unplaced; anointing words from the early Latin Life; "began to say" O gloriosa Domina; the collapse on "one day"; hill words indirect; Francis meeting attributed to Pius XII; Doctor on 16 January 1946; body moved 1263 with no "1301"; "Si quaeris" credited to Julian of Speyer, 1233. Law of 15 March 1231 is eight days before Easter (23 March 1231): correct. Lent began 5 February 1231: correct. The block-quote translations match the Latin and the old English in meaning.

## Fixes

Wrong or overstated facts
1. Ch 12, Ezzelino: "ruled 1237 to 1259" and "20 June 1259" were wrong. 20 June 1259 was a Friday, and Coleridge has the battle in the reign of Alexander IV with 20 June on a Tuesday. Now "took the city in 1237" and "20 June 1256" (the only year in that reign with a Tuesday). "Eighth day after the feast" is now "a week after". The year 1256 is computed, not printed in Coleridge.
2. Ch 4, Monte Paolo: Rigauld says Anthony wanted silence, prayer, and study, and that God ordered the seed-sowing. The text had made this Gratian's hope. Fixed; "on a hill" cut (not in the sources).
3. Ch 4 and opening: "that is how the Church remembers it" (Pius XII) cut. Pius says Anthony went to Assisi and recognised Francis; he does not say "chapter". Now "gave no source".
4. Ch 11, canonization: the sparrows story was wrong. Julian says a woman who guarded a crop got the sparrows to leave it alone. Fixed.
5. Ch 11: the minister arrived the evening before the third day, and the Podesta's fine came the next morning (Julian nn.35). Fixed.
6. Ch 11: candles "cut down to get them through the church door" was wrong. Julian says they were cut to stand inside the church; sixteen men is Coleridge. Fixed and split by source.
7. Ch 12: tongue "as if buried a week" was wrong. Rigauld: as if just buried. Fixed.
8. Ch 7: Brive cabbages. A maid carried them to the friars, and the lady's son, a canon of Noblac, told the story. The text had a canon "of that town" telling Rigauld. Fixed.
9. Ch 1: the bed and the Queen of Portugal story. Rigauld names "the Custodian of St Jacques", not a Coimbra custodian, and only for the bed. Fixed. "That is how the canons remembered him" cut (invented).
10. Ch 1: "parents good people before God" was credited to the early Life. Julian says "venerable"; "just before God" is Rigauld. Fixed. "Seventy years" now "more than sixty". "A friar named Bartholomew" now "a writer".
11. Ch 2: Pedro was "the king's son". Rigauld says only "a great prince"; he was the king's brother. Now "a prince of Portugal".
12. Ch 3: Bartholomew as "a friend's account": he says only that he saw and knew him. Fixed.
13. Ch 8 and ch 11: "Chronicle says only time he asked a favour" is a remark by a writer in Coleridge, not the Chronicle. Fixed. "Mother alive" now "old Lisbon tradition"; the aunt saying is "people said".
14. Ch 6: Bourges "some years later" (no date) cut. Fish story now "heretics near Padua mocked his words" (Rigauld), not "a town would not listen".
15. Ch 8: "the early Life agrees he was ill through all of it" applied to the winter; it covers Lent. Cut. "Bishop sat among them" now "was there".
16. Ch 8: "He gave Padua hard words about money" implied the purple line was said in Padua. Card says that is not known. Now "His Lenten sermons have hard words". "Usurer worse than Judas" now "in another place".
17. Ch 8: "Some of the sermons were given at night by lamplight" cut. Julian says people came at night with lights to take places early.
18. Ch 8 and ch 9: hill. "One day after Easter" read as the next day. Now "one day that spring"; the words are indirect.
19. Ch 10: friar on the road "begged" and "no rest": Julian says "persuaded", because of the crowds and noise. Now "urged", "because of the crowds". "Dark cell" cut.
20. Ch 11 opening and summary: "within the hour" and "before the friars could tell anyone" were invented. Cut.
21. Ch 12: Pius XII on "O doctor optime" is "tradunt" (they say). Now attributed to tradition, and "more than seven hundred years".
22. Ch 12: "Bonaventure's wooden chest" is not in the notes' main source. Now "a wooden chest". "What remains of the voice" now "the vocal organs".
23. Ch 12: Benedict XVI. "One line that holds the rest" was Benedict's own claim in the text. Now "quoted one of his lines". The Jesus line is now Jesus who melts hard hearts, as Benedict wrote.
24. Ch 12: "first mark of his preaching" and "made it possible for a Franciscan to study" were absolutes. Cut. "First to teach theology" is now "one English biographer calls him the first" (Woodcock: "perhaps the first").
25. Ch 13: the "old" rhyme: no source dates it. Now "not Anthony's, and its origin is unknown". The "Si quaeris" lines no longer say what Padua "had seen"; they now state what the text says.
26. Ch 13 and ch 11: "Padua gave him a law" reversed the facts (the law was passed at his request). Fixed.
27. Opening: "from every province" now "many provinces". "Whole Gospel out of Scripture" now "draw constantly on the Gospels" (Pius XII).
28. Summary: "Millions" cut; no source gives a number.
29. Ch 8: the law is kept "in the museum at Padua" (Catholic Encyclopedia, 1913); "to this day" cut. Ch 4: "Nobody at Monte Paolo knew he was learned" and "he had arranged it that way" replaced by the Life's own words ("hid the light of grace") and a note that Rigauld's pots and feet are general.
30. Ch 7: Francis "died that same year" now "in 1226". The Arles quotation: Anthony's witness as "Rigauld's witnesses remembered it" changed to "in Rigauld's Life".

Quotations (modern wording against the original)
31. Monte Paolo: Julian has "aliquando" (sometimes) at the evening hour. Added "sometimes".
32. Forlì (Rigauld): two gaps without an ellipsis. Added "…" after "no choice" and after "astonished"; restored "and spoke so powerfully".
33. Arles (Rigauld): added "…" before Friar Monaldo (Rigauld has a gap there).
34. Robber (Rigauld): added "…" at two gaps.
35. Dove (Woodcock): added "…" at the two gaps; "best-loved" now "one of his favourite" (Woodcock).
36. Sources: Woodcock's name is "Antony, C. M.". The note on the editor: it was the 1904 editor of Rigauld, not of the Acta; the Messenger says Julian built on the first Life.
37. "How This Life Was Researched": "twenty-two sources" is now "more than twenty" (the notes show a few not read in full). "Friars' Chronicle of 1883" is the English edition. The Padua statute cut from the list (not read directly).

## Left as is (judgment calls)
- Pentecost 1221 is a Bollandist estimate; stated plainly, as the card does.
- The "eight years" at Coimbra rests on Coleridge; it is not in Julian or Rigauld.
- The Basilica pages (tongue feast in February, Tuesday singing of Si quaeris, 1232 start, 1981 findings, 1310 and 1350 moves) were taken from the notes and cards; not re-read.
- The ending of the death chapter ("ten years instead of an hour") is the writer's gloss on Rigauld's Friday remark. It is brief and kept.

Ready. The lint script shows no MUST FIX and no CHECK lines.
