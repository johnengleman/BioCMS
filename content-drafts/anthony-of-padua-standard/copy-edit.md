## Resolved by the checker (2026-10-06)

1. Tiso "under Saint Anthony's direction": the Bollandist note g (Scardeone) says he was set on fire by Saint Anthony's sermons, left soldiering and the honours of the world, put himself and all he had in the man of God's direction ("viri Dei arbitrio"), and became his disciple from the Third Order. The text now tells it in that order, with no time after the death. The tomb words now follow the Latin ("de Campo sancti Petri"): "The tomb of Lord Tiso of the Field of Saint Peter."
2. "Most likely he preached from memory": Woodcock p. 76 says he could not have preached from the written notes as they stand and that "most likely he wrote from memory". The sentence is out of the story and the doubt is in "A Note on the Sources", worded as Woodcock has it.
3. "never went without": kept. Rigauld p. 53: "nothing was ever wanting to him or to those who were with him".
4. "Only one of the brothers saw him": kept. Early Life n. 12 says it appeared "cujusdam assidentium oculis" (to the eyes of one of those seated), Rigauld names Monaldo as eyewitness, and Coleridge p. 81 says only Monaldo, besides Saint Anthony, saw it. The note now cites n. 12 (it was n. 11) and these.
5. Chest and Ark: the sources give two titles, "Arca testamenti" (Early Life n. 10; Rigauld p. 64) and "scrinium/armarium Scripturarum" (Pius XII; Coleridge p. 142: "storehouse of the Sacred Scriptures"). The second is now "a storehouse of the Holy Scriptures".
6. "aliud a sensu humano decrevit": now "had decided something different from what human sense would choose". The "book says" lines around it were cut, so God's act is told plainly.
7. "Inside the house": the Latin (n. 37) says the friars were afraid and the Poor Ladies were just as afraid ("non minus perterritae"). It does not put them in one house. Now "Both the friars and the Poor Ladies were afraid."
8. 1226: the Bollandists estimate the Arles year as 1226 or a little earlier (note e). The death of Saint Francis in 1226 is not an estimate (Woodcock p. 28 now in the note). The Note on the Sources now says "about 1226 for the Arles chapter".
9. "two early Lives": kept.
10. The 34-word sentence on poverty: kept (the quotation is most of it).
11. The canons' three sentences: kept (Woodcock p. 7 gives the rule and the study).
12. "no smell of death": kept (Rigauld p. 83).
13. "cheated of his great desire" (item 18): the Latin is "divino nutu fraudatus". It now reads "by God's will he had already been denied his great desire once", so God does not "cheat". The quotation "cheated of his plan completely" in Sicily is the Latin "penitus a proposito se fraudatum" and stays.
