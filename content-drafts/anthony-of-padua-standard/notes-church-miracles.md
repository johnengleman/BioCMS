# Anthony of Padua: notes, focus church-miracles

Researcher: church-miracles agent. Date: 2026-10-01. Tradition: Catholic (Franciscan, Doctor of the Church). Tier A.
Limits: ~40 tool calls used. The Assidua itself, the bull Cum dicat Dominus, and Exulta Lusitania felix were NOT read (see Other).

## 1. Identity (church-miracles part only)
- Born Fernando, Lisbon, c.1195 [S1, S8]. Name Anthony taken on entering the Friars Minor, 1220/1221 [S1, S3].
- Died 13 June 1231 at Arcella, outside Padua [S3 p.188, S9]. Age 36 [S1, S3].
- Canonized by Gregory IX at Spoleto, Pentecost, 30 May 1232 [S1, S2 p.194]. Doctor of the Church 1946 (Pius XII, title Doctor Evangelicus) [S8; date 16 Jan 1946 only in S12, weak: to check].
- Feast: 13 June, ordered by Gregory IX at the canonization [S2 p.194].

## 3. Timeline (cult and miracles)
- 1195, Lisbon: birth as Fernando [S1, S8].
- 1220: relics of the five Franciscan martyrs of Morocco reach Coimbra; he joins the friars [S1, S4 p.175, S8].
- c.1222, Forli ordination: unplanned sermon, Dominicans decline, Anthony speaks [S1, S3 p.182, S4 p.176-7]. Status: early tradition, not a miracle.
- 1224-1227, France: bilocation at Limoges/Montpellier (Holy Thursday sermon, appears in choir to sing a lesson) [S1; S3 p.184 says Montpellier]. Legendary-leaning; recorded in the later Lives (S1 calls it among "most authentic miracles of that period").
- 1227: Gregory IX hears him preach at Rome, calls him "Ark of the Covenant" [S3 p.184, S4 p.178; S1 gives no year; S7 says Gregory is documented in Assidua per S11].
- 1230, Verona: Ezzelino scene. CE: "An apocryphal legend" of the tyrant humbling himself; Anthony risked his life but may have failed [S1]. B-G and Butler tell it as fact [S3 p.185, S4 p.179]. Status: contested/legendary.
- 15 Mar 1231, Padua: city law to protect debtors, copy in Padua museum [S1]. Status: documented civic fact, not miracle.
- Lent 1231: sermons to crowds of "30,000 and more" [S1].
- Spring 1231: retires to Camposampiero (Count Tiso's hermitage) [S1, S2 p.173, S9]. Walnut tree retreat [S9].
- 13 June 1231 (a Friday): collapses at midday meal; sets off for Padua on an ox cart; stops at Arcella; dies that evening [S9]. B-G: murmurs hymn "O gloriosa Domina", eyes light up, arms raised as if seeing the Saviour [S3 p.188]. Assidua is the source of the death account and S9 warns it "appears to embellish" [S9].
- Day of death, Vercelli: appears to Abbot Thomas Gallo [S1]. Padua: children cry "The holy Father is dead; St. Anthony is dead!" [S1]. Status: reported in the early Lives.
- Tuesday 17 June 1231: burial in Santa Maria Mater Domini, probably in a raised marble tomb so pilgrims could touch it [S5]. Coleridge: the body entered Padua on a Tuesday; "in no single instance" did a sufferer who called on him that day fail to be cured; origin of the nine Tuesdays [S2 p.191]. Status: tradition of the Chronicles.
- Summer 1231: processions from Capodiponte (barefoot), the University, the bishop; giant candles, some needing sixteen men [S2 pp.189-190]. NEW for popular pages.
- c. July 1231: Padua sends a deputation to Rome "a month had not passed" after death [S2 p.191]. Gregory IX names a commission: Bishop of Padua, Prior of St Benedict, and a Dominican prior (Giovanni da Vicenza) [S2 p.192]. Students of the University write a separate letter as eyewitnesses [S2 p.192]. Cardinals Odo of Montferrat and Jacopo of Palestrina recommend [S2 p.192]. The papal court was at Spoleto [S2 p.194].
- Cardinals' dream: a cardinal who opposed canonization before one year had passed dreams of a body that proves incorrupt and fragrant; he then meets the Padua envoys and says "This is our dream and its interpretation" [S2 pp.193-4]. Status: legendary (Chronicle tradition).
- 30 May 1232, Spoleto cathedral, Pentecost: canonization, 11 months and 17 days after death (count: 13 Jun 1231 to 30 May 1232 = under a year) [S1, S2 p.194, S5/S12 agree]. Gregory IX says in the bull that he knew Anthony personally [S1]. Bull Cum dicat Dominus; original in Padua archive (Veneranda Arca) [S12, to check].
- 30 May 1232, Lisbon: church bells ring by themselves at the moment of the sentence [S2 p.194]. Status: legendary/early tradition.
- Mother alive and aunt (canoness of Coimbra) invoked for favours: "to obtain a favour of St. Antony it was a sure way to get his aunt to ask for it" (paraphrase of [S2 p.195]). NEW.
- 13 June 1232: first feast kept in Padua [S2 p.195].
- Basilica: begun c.1232, finished 1301 [S12 only: to check]. Shops in Padua shut on the feast, decreed four years later [S2 p.195 fn].
- 1237-1259: Ezzelino rules Padua. Fra Bartolomeo Corradini hears a voice from the tomb: the city will be freed "on the octave of my feast". Army of the Pope and Venice beats Ezzelino's nephew Ansidisio on 20 June 1259, a Tuesday [S2 p.197; year 1259 inferred from S4 p.180 which has Ezzelino captured in 1259; S2 text names Alexander IV, so 1256-1259: check]. Status: reported in the later Chronicles.
- 8 Apr 1263, Padua: first translation to the new church, Bonaventure (Minister General) presiding [S5]. Body: flesh dust, bones disjointed, hair, teeth and skin of the head perfect, tongue incorrupt and of natural colour [S2 p.198, S1 says flesh "reduced to dust but the tongue uninjured, fresh, and of a lively red colour"]. Bonaventure kisses the tongue [S1, S2, S5].
- Date conflict: CNA gives Feb 18 for the day the tongue was found, and "Feast of the Tongue" [S6]; S5 gives 8 April 1263; Coleridge says a Lyons chapter in 1351 fixed 15 February for the feast of the translation [S2 p.201-2]. Writer: say only "1263".
- 1310 (14 June per S5; "octave of the Saint" per S2 p.200): second translation to the chapel on the left of the nave [S2, S5].
- Feb 1350 (14 Feb per S5; 15 Feb per S2 p.201): third translation; Cardinal Guy de Boulogne/Guy of Montfort (name differs by source), cured through Anthony's intercession (S5 says of the Black Plague; S2 says his "life had been preserved"), gives the silver urn and a reliquary for the jaw [S2, S5].
- 20 Jun 1745: smaller relics moved to the chapel where they are now, by Cardinal Rezzonico (later Clement XIII) [S2 p.201].
- 1749 fire; none of the people on the burning roof was hurt, the saint's altar unharmed [S2 p.202]. 1756 storm: lead roof of the City Hall falls with no injury [S2 p.203]. Status: popular tradition (Chronicle of Azevedo, 18th century).
- 1946: Pius XII, Apostolic Letter Exulta Lusitania felix, Doctor of the Universal Church, "Doctor Evangelicus" [S8, S11]. Benedict XVI: the sermons were so rich that Pius XII proclaimed him Doctor in 1946 [S8].
- 6 Jan 1981: scientific examination of the tomb begins for the 750th anniversary, under a religious commission and a technical-scientific committee, both named by the Holy See [S5]. Found: fir-wood chest in a second chest, three compartments: skeleton (without jawbone, left forearm, other minor parts kept elsewhere), the habit, and "mass corporis", the ashes [S5]. Relics put in a crystal urn; displayed 31 Jan to 1 Mar 1981 (S5 says 29 days: the dates give 29-30 days), over 650,000 visitors [S5]. Vocal apparatus and habit permanently displayed in the Chapel of Relics [S5]. S6: the vocal apparatus was "still wet and soft" in 1263 per Fr Mario Conte; 1981 found Bonaventure's chest [S6]. Pope: John Paul II authorized the exam (S12 and a search summary; not in a source I read: to check).
- 2015: Pope Francis raises Padua shrine to minor basilica [S6]. (Check; not a primary source.)

## 4. Character (miracle-relevant)
- Humble about wonders: Chronicle says public miracles were to confirm Catholic truth, private ones were hidden and credited to the faith of the person [S2 p.62].
- Wanted a miracle once for himself: the stolen book of notes [S2 p.72].
- Unwell, thin, put in the kitchen at first; hidden talent [S3 p.182, S4 p.176].
- Wore a modest look as a sermon: "We have done it, by our modest looks, and by the gravity of our behaviour" [S4 p.179, paraphrase of the OCR text, no quotation marks used].

## 6. Scenes
1. Rimini, fish: Anthony fasts and prays, then goes to the Marecchia mouth; crowd mocks; fish line up by size, smallest nearest the shore [S2 pp.57-8]. Place conflict, see Other.
2. Rimini/Toulouse, the mule: Bonvillo starves the mule three days; Anthony comes with the Host after Mass, the man pours oats, the mule kneels to the Host [S2 pp.63-4]. B-G has Toulouse and the mule breaking its halter [S3 p.188].
3. Padua, Leonardo: kicks his mother, confesses, hatchets off his own foot taking Anthony's words literally; mother upbraids Anthony; he rejoins foot, "without wound or scar" [S2 pp.100-1].
4. Padua, the blank paper: a young man too weeping to confess writes his sins; Anthony reads, each is wiped out [S2 p.100].
5. Camposampiero, Infant Jesus: Count Tiso sees the Infant with the saint (B-G: "through a chink in the wall", Limousin house) [S3 p.188; S2 p.173 "holy secret of the visit of the Infant Jesus"]. Place disputed [S1].
6. The stolen book (Coleridge says manuscript of notes): novice steals it, meets a "man of savage countenance" with a drawn sword on a bridge, returns it [S2 p.72]. Status: legendary (later tradition). "The only time when he ... asked a grace of the kind for himself" [S2 p.72, paraphrase].
7. 30 May 1232, Lisbon bells ring unprompted [S2 p.194].
8. 1263 opening: kiss on the tongue by Bonaventure [S1, S2].
9. Arcella, 13 June 1231: ox cart arrives at dusk; "signs of intense anguish"; psalms read [S9].
10. Padua, children running through the streets crying that the saint is dead [S1].

## 7. Miracles (status key: A = Church-approved/recognized by the canonization or a Holy See act; E = reported in the early Lives (13th c.); L = legendary; C = contested)
- Note on "approved": the Church recognized the cult by canonization after a commission examined miracles (1231-32) [S2 pp.192-4]. The Chronicle says 45 miracles were "judicially proved" before canonization, two of them raisings of the dead [S2 p.207]. No list of the 45 was read. Individual later stories are not approved one by one.
- Fish sermon: Rimini (Marecchia mouth) per S2, S3; Brenta river near Padua per S1; "not at Padua, as is generally supposed" [S1]. Status E/C. B-G calls the fish coming "no doubt an addition" to a satire on deaf men [S3 p.188].
- Mule and the Eucharist: Rimini, Bonvillo (S1, S2); Toulouse (S3, S10); other places in 14th-c. legends (Bruges, "Wadding") [S1]. Status E/C by place. In S1 it is among "the three most noted" and a "horse" in S1, a mule in S2/S3; CE says horse.
- Poisoned food rendered harmless by the sign of the cross: Italian heretics [S1]. E.
- Bilocation at Limoges/Montpellier choir during sermon [S1, S3 p.184]. E/L.
- Rain and storm held back from his audience: Limoges square, Brive maid-servant (S1); France field with thunder (S4 p.178). E.
- Pulpit at St Junien breaks, none hurt [S1]. E.
- Tunic frees a monk from temptation; breath on a novice's face confirms his vocation [S1]. E.
- Infant Jesus vision: Camposampiero (Italian view) or Chateauneuf-la-Forêt, Limousin (French view); "existing documents ... do not decide the question" [S1]. B16: "a miraculous apparition mentioned in several literary sources" [S8]. Status C (place). It is not in the Assidua per my reading? NOT checked.
- Apparition of Francis at the Chapter of Arles during sermon [S1; Celano via S7]. E (S7: Celano, issued while Anthony lived, mentions "one whose mind the Lord had opened to understand the Scriptures" [S7]).
- Provence wine: cask refills, glass mended at a poor woman's house [S1]. L/E.
- Leonardo's foot: Padua [S1, S2 pp.100-1]. CE: "which Franciscan writers attribute" [S1]. Status L/C (later Lives).
- Miser's heart: funeral in Tuscany (S10) / "invited to preach" [S1]. L.
- Speaking infant (nobleman's jealous husband), violent husband Tuscany, the dead man who speaks (Lisbon), soldier who stabbed his wife (Padua, wife healed) [S10 titles; S2 p.101]. L (later Lives; not checked against Assidua).
- Aleardino's glass: knight, Padua [S10 title]. L.
- Posthumous: Padua tomb cures on Tuesday; "forty-five miracles judicially proved" before canonization [S2 p.207]; drowned nephew Parrisio/Apparizio of Lisbon restored to life after three days [S2 p.207, S10]; Eurilia raised [S10 title]. Status: first 45 are the Assidua tradition (not read); the rest E/L.
- Lost things: manuscript of Ambrosius Catharinus, thurible of the sacristan, King Charles II's money [S2 pp.213-4]. These are 16th-17th century reports. Status popular. Responsory "Si quaeris miracula" by Julian of Speyer (CE) / attributed to Bonaventure (Coleridge fn) [S1, S2 p.? line 7865]. Conflict.
- Liberation of Padua from Ezzelino, voice from the tomb [S2 p.197, S10 title]. E/L.
- Tongue and vocal organs incorrupt: 1263 [S1, S2 p.198, S5, S6]; vocal apparatus displayed since 1981 [S5]. Status: relic reported by the Basilica and Order; not a defined Church miracle. I found no document where the Church declares it a miracle: to check.
- Padua 1749 fire and 1756 storm [S2 pp.202-3]. Popular.
- Ezzelino kneeling in Verona: C [S1, S3 p.185, S4 p.179].
- Bells of Lisbon 1232 [S2 p.194]. L.
