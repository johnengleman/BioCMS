# Mining inventory: Saint Anthony of Padua, miracle list (2026-10-05)

Sources read as raw OCR text: Acta Sanctorum, June vol. 2 (archive.org `actasanctorum23unse`), Rigauld 1904 (`LifeOfStAntonyOfPadua`), Coleridge 1883 (`MN5128ucmf_2`). Page and paragraph numbers are the printed ones; the Acta Sanctorum OCR garbles some numbers and some columns, and the notes below say where.

## Totals

- Source accounts listed: 168 (a row can hold several people, for example one numbered paragraph of the Ancona appendix).
- New: 83 source accounts. They are told in 59 new list entries (c06–c21, l21–l33, d16–d29, x04–x19). Six of the entries group thin cases of the same kind (c15, c16, c17, c18, x09, x12), 30 source accounts in all, so that each person keeps a name, a place, and what happened.
- Already in the list and merged with new detail: 21 existing accounts (25 rows).
- Already in the list, nothing new: 40 rows.
- Same account as another row: 5 rows.
- Skipped: 15 rows, each with its reason.
- Not added (limit): none. I read the limit of 60 as 60 list entries, and 59 were added. If the limit counts source accounts instead (83), the grouped entries c15–c18, x09, and x12 would be the first to cut.

Marks: **new** = added as a new entry; **merged** = already in the list, and a new detail from these sources was merged into that account; **already** = already in the list, nothing new to add; **same** = the same account as a row above; **skip** = not added, with the reason.

## Corrections and uncertain readings

- The existing account l20 (Ezzelino) cited the Liber as "n. 33, p. 223". In the OCR the Ezzelino paragraphs are printed 35 and 36 (the OCR reads 35 as "33"). The citation is now "nn. 35–36, p. 223". n. 33 is the night apparitions, now cited in x01.
- The existing account d13 (Father Ignatius Martini) follows Coleridge, who says the seventeenth century. The Acta Sanctorum gives 1581. Both are now in the status line of d13.
- The intro paragraph said stories first written down "in the 1500s and 1600s" have their own group. Azevedo (Coleridge's source) wrote in the 1700s, so the sentence now says "from the 1500s to the 1700s".
- Place and personal names are spelled as the OCR gives them where I could not check them against another source: Poceili, Capite-Gauri, Corniclano, Pulcigno, Nocrite, Elbrun, Salsaterra, Anguillara. The Basilica's "Salvaterra" for the knight in c04 is kept in the existing status line.
- n. 55 (d23): the OCR does not allow a safe reading of the husband's trade ("smith" or "lewd man"), so the text says only "a wicked man". The smith who kills his wife in the devil's sign in n. 52 (d22) is a different man.
- n. 57 (d24): the OCR reads "quorum nomina non tacebat", so the text says she named the living people she saw. The names are not given.
- Analecta n. 25 (x12, the Antwerp bond): the middle of the account is cut by a page break in the OCR. The text tells only what is clear: the insult, the advice to have a Mass said, the Mass, and the servant's message that his mistress would pay on her word.
- Dates read from OCR digits and checked against the text around them: Tortamano 1675 (the Florentine print of 1678 tells events of three years earlier), Father Dammey's letter 1680, the Venice fire 2 June 1686, the Duke of Poland 1350 (the Bollandists call it the Jubilee year), the surgeon 1367 (the Bollandists would date it 1366; this is in the closing note).
- The Bollandists' doubts about which queen is meant in n. 43 and n. 50 and about the year of the surgeon's story are in the closing "A Note on the Sources" only.
