# Check of the mined miracle accounts (Saint Anthony of Padua), 2026-10-06

Checked: every new account (c06–c21, l21–l33, d16–d29, x04–x19) and every merged account against the raw OCR of the Acta Sanctorum (Ancona appendix nn. 48–74, Liber miraculorum nn. 1–71, Analecta nn. 13–35, the Cortusi note p. 232, the relics appendix nn. 77–78), Coleridge 1883, and Rigauld 1904. Original saved as `miracles.html.before-check-mining`. Fixed in `miracles.html`. The check script shows no MUST FIX line (one CHECK line about average sentence length, 12.6).

## Fixes

- c15, Prosdocima: the Latin says one hand ("manu") and both feet. Text said "both her hands and both her feet". Now "Her hand and both her feet".
- c15, Massaguerra: the editor's note h glosses "li Saccili" as Sacile. Now "Massaguerra of Sacile".
- Monselice woman (woman-dry-after-falling-into-water): the husband wanted to "temper her joy" ("volens temperare"). Text said "to tease her". Now "To cool her joy".
- Shipwrecked men: the Latin says "ferme viginti sex" (about 26). Text said "Twenty-six". Now "About twenty-six". Narrator "Blessed Anthony" now "Saint Anthony".
- l09: status line now says the 1367 collection adds the fall, the vision, and what Peter said (it said only the last).
- l27: "bitter enemies" is not in the source ("inimicitias speciales"). Now "enemies".
- d10: the voice sounded "multum clare" (very clearly), not "loud". Now "A clear voice".
- d26: the Pope's last words ("possemus cum eo potius perire quam lucrari") read as if the Pope would perish beside the saint. Now "we could sooner perish than gain anything in dealing with him".
- d27: "Peter joined his hands" is a guess from a garbled word ("adjrans"). Removed.
- d29: heading said "withered arm"; the source says the hand and arm were weakened ("debilitatur"). Heading now "weakened hand and arm". The Duke's words were "Ille cum precibus imploretur, qui percussit" ("let him be asked with prayers who struck him"). Text said "God, who struck him"; now "the one who struck him". Narrator "Blessed Anthony" now "Saint Anthony" (two places). The date 1350 and 10 April are confirmed: the Analecta calls that year the Jubilee.
- d18: heading said "Mockers ... lose their sight". Only one man lost his eyes, and he was healed. New heading: "A faked blindness becomes real, then is healed".
- d04b, d20, d21: narrator wrote "Blessed Anthony" (five places). Now "Saint Anthony". Quotations keep "Blessed Anthony" because they are quotations.
- d13: first paragraph said "in the 1600s" as fact. Now the paragraph gives no date; the status line gives 1581 (Acta) and the 1600s (Coleridge).
- x06: Saint Beatrice de Silva was canonized in 1976. Heading and body now say "Saint Beatrice". Also made clear that the Virgin, not Beatrice, kept the promise of freedom.
- x12: heading said the bond was "recovered". It was not found; the other woman agreed to pay on her word. New heading: "A lost bucket and a lost bond, Antwerp". The "better adviser" is the writer himself ("in manum meam incidit"). Added.
- x13: "Only the devil could have carried him there" stood as the narrator's claim. Now "The account says no one but the devil could have carried him there".
- x15: the first man struck "decies octies" (eighteen times), not ten. Fixed. "Monte Muro" now "Montemurro" (same region as Picerno and Baragiano).

## Confirmed without change

- Corrections to old text. l20 now cites nn. 35–36, p. 223: the OCR prints "33" for the Ezzelino paragraph, but the sequence 33 (apparitions), 34 (Leonardo), "33" (Ezzelino), "30" (gift), 37 (Camposampiero) makes it nn. 35–36. d13: the Acta gives 1581; the same passage says he preached sixteen years and died in 1598, which fits 1581. Coleridge's "seventeenth century" is wrong, and the status line gives both. Intro "from the 1500s to the 1700s": Coleridge says Azevedo "wrote late in the last century", so the 1700s is right.
- Dates read from garbled digits: Tortamano April 1675 (printed at Florence 1678, "three years" later), the Dammey letter 1680, the Venice fire 2 June 1686, the Duke of Poland 1350, the surgeon 1367. All agree with the Latin and with the surrounding text.
- Place names kept as the OCR spells them, because the editors say they are not found or give no gloss: Capite-Gauri (note e: not on the maps), Poceili, Corniclano (note b: not on the maps), Anguillara (note k: not found), Salsaterra, Pulcigno, Nocrite, Trimegone, Campo Rotondo, Elbrun, Anicium (note k: Le Puy or Annecy). Names the Latin plainly means are modern: Monselice, Pieve di Sacco, Montagnana, Loreto, Treviso, Comacchio, Monopoli, Setúbal, Alcácer do Sal, Linhares, Guarda, Torres Novas.
- The six grouped entries (c15, c16, c17, c18, x09, x12): all 8 + 6 + 10 + 2 + 2 + 2 short cases match their numbered paragraphs. Counts in the headings are right (8, 6, 10).
- Every other new and merged account (c06–c14, c19–c21, l21–l26, l28–l33, d16, d17, d19–d25, d28, x04, x05, x07–x11, x14, x16–x19; c02–c05, l09, d02, d04a, d04b, d06, d08, d10, d12, d14, r02, r06, x01, the Monselice, sparrow, and shipwreck merges) matches the Latin or the English source in people, places, numbers, order of events, and quotations. Page and paragraph numbers in the "Source:" lines match the printed pages.
- Reverence: God is named as actor only where the source names him (d29 "Dei judicio", c09 "divinum subsidium", l30 "potentia Dei", l32 "illius virtute", l31 prayer to God and the saint). Status lines name the teller with no doubt words.
- Titles: no bare saint name outside quotations. Not saints, left plain: Pope Gregory IX, Pope Boniface VIII, Pope Benedict XVI, Thomas of Celano, Thomas Gallo, Father Ignatius Martini, Ambrosius Catharinus, Brother Philip, Peter Peters. "Blessed Amadeus" and "Blessed Luke Belludi" keep "Blessed".
- Dark or hard accounts (d24 vision of hell, d26 deaths of the clerics, d22 Lupa) are told as the source tells them, with the teller named, and without doubt words.

## Left as is, for the owner to know

- c-series status "approved by the Church": the Ancona heading says the cases were read before Gregory IX, and the Liber (n. 39) says the Pope canonized after the miracles were read and approved by all. Kept to match c01.
- d25 heading year 1367 is the source's year. The Bollandists would say 1366; that is in the closing note.
- l30 joins two tellings: the Liber says she walked firmly at once, and the Ancona manuscript says she grew strong soon after with a little cart. Both are told, each with its teller.
