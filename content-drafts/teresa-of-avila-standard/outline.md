**Keys.** **W-** witnesses, **O-** own-words, **C-** church-miracles, **F-** extra-foundations, **L-** extra-letters notes (sub-keys as in v1). **Card SC-nn** = `scenes-1/2/3.md`, with the card's own keys. **FN-n** = fact cards in `scenes-3.md`.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Teresa wanted to enjoy God forever. For twenty years her own divided heart stood in the way: the parlour, its praise, and the prayer she gave up [W-S3 p.7; card SC-04]. Then the Church's own men stood in the way: confessors, a city, a princess, the Inquisition, a nuncio, friars of her Order. Grace turned her before the wounded Christ; she answered with prayer as friendship and obedience above her own visions, and died saying the hour she had longed for had come [F-E2 Life 8.5; F-E1 ch.24 n.9; L-XP1 p.83].

**Why it was beautiful to her.** At seven she thought the martyrs "bought very cheaply the going to enjoy God," and the word that held her was "forever, forever, forever": through it, she wrote, the way of truth was printed on her as a child [card SC-01: VIDA-Sp ch.1 pp.6-7]. She admits it was not yet love [ibid.]. The reader hears it in the opening, at her first decision. It returns as loss ("I did not enjoy God, and I was not content in the world," VIDA-Sp ch.8) and is answered at Alba ("It is time now for us to be together," L-XP1 p.83).

**Famous** as the reformer of Carmel, a teacher of prayer, and the first woman Doctor of the Church [C-S2; L-XV1].

**Set apart.** A woman with no learned training wrote on mystical prayer while the Inquisition held her book, and popes later called her a teacher [F-E1 ch.23 n.1; L-XV1]. She founded in strict poverty, yet laughed, forbade harsh penances, and trusted her superiors' orders above her own revelations [F-E1 ch.15 §14, ch.18 n.4, ch.24 n.9; L-E4 vol.6 p.12].

## 2. Portrait

Saint Teresa was charming, and she knew it. People came to the Incarnation parlour for her "buena gracia y donaire," and she wept over that all her life [W-S3 p.7]. She was funny: "a bargainer and business woman" [L-XL1 p.63]; "Now let me sleep" to a frightened companion [F-E1 ch.19 §5]; her portrait "blear-eyed and ugly" [F-E1 ch.17 n.5]. She was practical: straw for the healthy, mattresses for the sick [L-E4 vol.6 p.7, 11-12]. She hated long roads and never refused one [F-E1 ch.18 §4]. She named her faults: vanity, twenty divided years, impatience with God in a leaking chapel [W-S2 ch.2; card SC-04; card SC-09]. She feared being fooled and begged her confessor to "look again" [W-S3 p.7]. People were surprised that she obeyed a young superior against her own revelation [F-E1 ch.24 n.9].

Her life with God was friendship: prayer is "nothing other than a friendship," time alone "with the One who we know loves us," and no one took Him for a friend without being repaid [F-E2 Life 8.5]. She found Him "among the pots and pans" [O-S4 ch.5], and taught "not to think much but to love much" [O-S3 Fourth Mansions].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** a plain beginning (Example 3). The first true story is the strongest start, and it names the desire. The opening tells SC-01 in full; chapter 1 does not retell it.

> One day in Ávila, when Saint Teresa was about seven, she and her brother Rodrigo took a little food and left their father's house. They went out by the Adaja gate and over the bridge, on the road to the land of the Moors. They meant to beg their way there for the love of God, so that the Moors would cut off their heads. An uncle met them and brought them home, and Rodrigo had his excuse ready: his sister had made him take that road. Years later she explained it herself. The martyrs, she thought, had bought the joy of God very cheaply, and that joy would last forever.

Keys: [card SC-01: Ribera p.96; VIDA-Sp ch.1 p.6-7].

**Thesis** (after the opening): She never lost that wish, but for twenty years she could not live it. When she did, she reformed the Carmelites: Saint Joseph's in Ávila in 1562, sixteen more houses of nuns, and with Saint John of the Cross the Discalced friars from 1568 [C-S2; F-E1 ch.14 n.4]. Her books teach prayer as friendship with God, and in 1970 the Church named her its first woman Doctor [F-E2 Life 8.5; L-XV1]. Answered in ch.6-8, 10-11, 14-15.

**Do not spend:** the opening, thesis, and summary must not use the deathbed, "the hour I have longed for," "daughter of the Church," or the 1970 Mass. The nuncio's "restless, roving woman" moves to ch.10.

**Ending.** Climax: the death (SC-14), alone in ch.13. Then a short after-death part (ch.14: calendar, burial, body, books, people, one healing, canonization). Closing image: in 1970, in Saint Peter's, before pilgrims from Spain, the Pope says her last words in her own language, in the Spanish part of his homily: "En fin, soy hija de la Iglesia" [card SC-15: V-2]. At most one quiet sentence after it. What she offers today is felt, not stated: a woman who prayed badly for twenty years and began again near forty, as a friend.

## 4. Chapters

"Pull" marks a true turn; other chapters end on their own image.

1. **Saint Teresa Leaves Her Father's House** — SC-02. **Pull:** her body did not agree with her soul.
2. **Saint Teresa Nearly Dies and Cannot Walk** — SC-03. **Pull:** "I did what I do: I misused the favour."
3. **Saint Teresa Is Torn Between God and the World** — no full scene (Fact need 1). Ends on: "I did not enjoy God, and I was not content in the world."
4. **Saint Teresa Weeps Before the Wounded Christ** — SC-04. **Pull:** the Friend begins to speak back.
5. **Saint Teresa's Visions Frighten Her Confessors** — SC-05. **Pull:** she wants to leave the large house for a small poor one.
6. **Saint Teresa Founds a Poor Convent in Ávila** — SC-06. **Pull:** Rubeo: as many houses as hairs on her head [W-S3 p.8].
7. **Saint John of the Cross Joins the Reform** — SC-07. Ends on: on to Toledo with almost nothing.
8. **Saint Teresa Founds Convents with Almost Nothing** — SC-08, SC-09. **Pull:** one house brings her real grief.
9. **A Princess Turns Against Saint Teresa's Nuns** — SC-10. **Pull:** Gracián has authority over her and will use it.
10. **Saint Teresa Obeys and Travels to Seville** — SC-11. **Pull:** the older branch moves against the reform.
11. **Saint John of the Cross Is Seized at Night** — SC-12 (Fact needs 2, 3). Ends on: Saint John leaves without her.
12. **Saint Teresa's Last Journey to Alba** — SC-13. Ends on: "Now I have."

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points** (tell slowly, from inside her): SC-02, SC-04, SC-06, SC-11, SC-13. **Climax:** SC-14.

### SC-01 — The road to the Moors, about 1522 (Ávila, the Adaja bridge) — OPENING
- What happens: two children set out to be martyrs; an uncle brings them home; Rodrigo blames her.
- Why it is here: her first decision; names the desire.
- Have: card SC-01; W-S1 p.96-97.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: VIDA-Sp ch.1 pp.6-7 (card's English).
- Where to look: card SC-01.

### SC-02 — Leaving her father's house, 2 November 1535 (Ávila) — TURNING POINT 1
- What happens: her father refuses; after three months' struggle she leaves at dawn with Antonio, telling no one; "every bone"; joy at the habit.
- Why it is here: first adult choice for the desire, against her own heart.
- Have: card SC-02 (VIDA-Sp ch.3-4 pp.17-20; Ribera p.103).
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: VIDA-Sp ch.4 pp.19-20.
- Where to look: card SC-02.

### SC-03 — "This daughter is not for burying," 15 August, about 1537 (her father's house)
- What happens: four days senseless; wax on her eyes; her father refuses burial; Lorenzo and the candle; one finger, a sheet; crawling, she praises God.
- Why it is here: the concrete day of a three-year trial; opens Lorenzo.
- Have: card SC-03; W-S1 p.110-112; W-S3 p.22-23.

- Testimony: VIDA-Sp ch.5 p.32.

### SC-04 — Before the wounded Christ, Lent, about 1554-55 (Incarnation oratory) — TURNING POINT 2

### SC-14 — "The hour I have longed for," 3-4 October 1582 (Alba de Tormes) — CLIMAX
- What happens: she asks the nuns' pardon; sits up alone as the Sacrament enters: "the hour I have longed for has come"; "daughter of the Church"; "a little earth here"; dies about 9 pm in Blessed Ana's arms.

### Relationships, to their end
- **Her father:** refuses (1535); "not for burying" (1537); his death (Fact need 1).
- **Rodrigo:** the bridge; later died a soldier in the Río de la Plata [card SC-01] (one line, ch.1).
- **Lorenzo:** the candle (1537); letter from Peru (1570); home (1575); her letter of 19 June 1580, "Trust in God, who is a faithful Friend"; he dies days later [L-XL4 CCCXXXII] (Fact need 2).
- **Teresita:** the reason for the last road home; professed 5 Nov 1582, a month after her death [L-XL4 p.365 n.].
- **Saint John:** Medina; Duruelo; seized; parting Dec 1581 (Fact need 3); her greeting to him, 1 Sept 1582 [L-XL4 p.365].
- **Antonio of Jesus:** the broom; the order to Alba; the deathbed question.
- **Gracián:** Veas; Seville; absent at her death; opens her tomb; expelled 1592, Tunis, *Defensa* 1597, died 1614 [F-E1 ch.23 n.2; L-XP1 p.XXVI].
- **Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé and Blessed Anne of Jesus:** figs, the rain, the death; both reach Paris, 1604 [FN-3].

**Losses, at their dates:** mother (about 1528); her father's house (1535); illness, about 1537-40, one concrete day (one finger, a sheet); her father (Fact need 1); the city's anger (1562); the book seized (1575); Seville's "trials inside" [F-E1 ch.26 §2]; Saint John taken (Dec 1577); Lorenzo (June 1580); "Go away," and Gracián absent (Sept 1582).

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** *Life* 1562-68, with the Inquisition about twelve years, cleared by the Grand Inquisitor in 1580 [W-S1 intro] (ch.3-5, 9); Constitutions by 1567 and *Way of Perfection* about 1565 [L-E4 vol.6 p.XV; FN-2] (ch.6); *Foundations* from 1573 (ch.8); *Interior Castle* 1577 [O-S3 intro] (ch.11); letters (ch.8-12); "Nada te turbe" in her breviary [O-S2 vol.6] and Luis de León's edition, 1588 [C-S5 p.610] (ch.14).
- **What she changed:** Discalced nuns, 1562 and sixteen more houses [C-S2] (ch.6-12); friars, Duruelo, 28 Nov 1568 [F-E1 ch.14 n.4] (ch.7); separate province 1580-81 [FN-1] (ch.11); Genoa 1584, Paris 1604 [FN-3] (ch.14); Pius XI, 1922, "highest teacher of contemplation" [L-XV1] (ch.14).
- **Why she is known:** body found whole 4 July 1583, to Ávila Nov 1585, back to Alba 23 Aug 1586 [L-XP3 art.97, 101]; Ribera's *Life*, 1590; Isabel de Montoya healed at Salamanca, Feb 1603 [L-XP3 art.108]; patron of Catholic writers [C-S2] (all ch.14).
- **Path to sainthood:** over 400 witnesses, 1591-1610; beatified 24 Apr 1614; canonized 12 Mar 1622 [L-XP1 p.XVIII, XXVII] (ch.14). Doctor, 27 Sept 1970 [L-XV1] (ch.15).

**Fact needs**

2. Her own words on Lorenzo's death, June 1580 — Letters CCCXXXIII (4 July 1580) and CCCXLVIII [L-XL4 p.47-48, 89-91].
3. Saint John: her words on his escape (1578) and their last meeting, Dec 1581 — Stanbrook *Letters* vol.3; Ribera p.320 (notes say "Veas"; she was in Ávila then). If not found, ch.11 ends on the Castro letter.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Entry 2 Nov 1535 (Ribera); Silverio 1536; annals 1533 [card SC-02]. Mother's death: she says about twelve; the will makes her nearly fourteen [W-S2 ch.1 n.].
- Coma, wounded Christ, angel: "about" years only. The hell vision goes before the angel by the editors' date.
- Angel: manuscript "cherubim"; "seraph" is Báñez's margin [card SC-05].
- Saint Joseph's: tell Teresa's lone priest; Báñez's claim goes to the Note [check.md].
- Duruelo: "honour," not "dignity" [card SC-07].
- The Ana and Juana exchange rests on one witness, Mariana de la Encarnación [L-XP1 p.90].
- Died about 9 pm, 4 Oct 1582; next day 15 Oct; feast 15 Oct. Canonized 1622, not 1621.
- "Blessed" for Ana de San Bartolomé and Anne of Jesus. Do not quote Peers [O-S3].
