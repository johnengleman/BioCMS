This list gathers the visions, healings, and other wonders attributed to St. Teresa of Ávila, with their sources and their status. Many come from her own account in her Life and her Foundations. Others come from the sworn testimony of nuns and friars in the processes for her beatification, from the articles drawn up in 1609–10 for the hearings toward her canonization, from her early biographer, Francisco de Ribera, and from later Lives. Each entry says who reported it. The list makes no claim of scientific proof, and it does not dismiss any account. Where a story is late, legendary, or contested, the entry says so.

How the Church examined her miracles

More than four hundred witnesses gave sworn statements in the processes for Teresa’s cause. In 1609–10 the hearings at Salamanca examined witnesses on a list of articles that put forward eighteen accounts of her body, her relics and healings after her death; they are given below, under “Put forward for her canonization”. The auditors of the Roman Rota reported to the pope on her miracles in a decree dated 2 January 1614, and Pope Paul V beatified her on 24 April 1614. New processes followed, and Pope Gregory XV canonized her on 12 March 1622. Our sources do not say which miracles were finally approved for the beatification or the canonization, or how many, so none is listed here as approved.

Source: Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, “Teresa d’Avila”, https://www.causesanti.va/it/santi-e-beati/teresa-d-avila.html; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), introduction, pp. XVIII–XXVII, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo, arts. 97–114, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Decretum Rotae (Rome, 1614), https://archive.org/details/A11012710.

During Teresa’s life

Four days as if dead, Ávila, August 1537

After her profession Teresa’s health broke, a harsh cure made her worse, and her father brought her to his house in Ávila, where the doctors gave her up. On the night of 15 August 1537 she fell into a coma that lasted about four days. She found afterward that wax had dripped on her eyelids, and a grave stood open at the Incarnation for a day and a half. Her father would not let her be buried: “Esta hija no es para enterrar” (This daughter is not for burying). She revived. Ribera mentions a claim that she saw her future foundations during the coma, and he doubts it.

Status: Her own account (Life, ch. 5) of the four days, the wax and the open grave; her father’s words come from Ribera (1590) and, at second hand, from Ana de la Encarnación in the beatification process. Natural causes are not excluded, and no Church finding was made.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 110–111, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1, pp. 22–23, https://archive.org/details/BMC18ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoI; Butler, Lives of the Saints, vol. 10, p. 316.

Healed of paralysis through St. Joseph, about 1537–1540

After the coma Teresa was carried back to the Incarnation at Easter and lay crippled for eight months. Ana de la Encarnación, repeating what she had heard, said that she could move only one finger of one hand. The illness lasted about three years, and when she began to move again it was by crawling. Teresa credited her recovery to St. Joseph, and her first foundation, in 1562, bore his name.

Status: Her own testimony. A modern Italian devotional profile calls it a true miracle; that is not an official finding.

Source: Butler, Lives of the Saints, vol. 10, p. 317; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 111–112, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Santi, beati e testimoni, https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/24850.

Christ stern and grave, about 1542

Teresa wrote that once, while she was with a person whose friendship was doing her no good, Christ stood before her, stern and grave, and she understood that he was displeased with her. The annals printed with David Lewis’s translation of her Life place this in the parlour of the monastery in 1542; her own text gives neither the place nor the year.

Status: Her own account (Life, ch. 7); the place and year come from the annals.

Source: Teresa, The Life of Saint Teresa of Jesus, ch. 7 and annals, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/the-life-of-saint-teresa-of-jesus-by-herself.

Christ beside her, 29 June 1557

On the feast of St. Peter, Teresa felt Christ close beside her. She saw nothing, and yet she was certain that he was there.

Status: Her own account (Life, ch. 27).

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 27, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

First rapture and the vision of hell, 1558

The annals of her life date her first rapture, and a vision of hell, to 1558. Our sources give the year only.

Status: Her own account, dated by the annals.

Source: Teresa, The Life of Saint Teresa of Jesus, tr. David Lewis, annals, https://archive.org/details/the-life-of-saint-teresa-of-jesus-by-herself.

Lifted from the ground in choir and at a sermon, before 1562

Teresa wrote that in rapture her soul was carried away, “and now and then the whole body as well, so that it was lifted up from the ground.” Once in choir, as she was about to receive Communion, she felt herself rising from her knees and told the nuns to say nothing. Another time, during a sermon on the feast of the house with great ladies present, she threw herself on the ground and the nuns held her, and it was seen all the same. She begged God to give her no more favours that others could see, and she wrote that in public “I have never been enraptured since.”

Status: Her own account (Life, ch. 20).

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 20, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Clutching the floor mats, as Ana de los Ángeles saw it

In the beatification process María de Jesús testified that Ana de los Ángeles had seen Teresa lifted up while she prayed, and that Teresa held on to the floor mats so that no one would see.

Status: Second-hand sworn testimony.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de Jesús, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Rapt in the kitchen with a pan in her hand

María de San José testified that María Bautista had seen Teresa lifted from the floor several times, and once rapt in the kitchen with her pan in her hand. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912, citing Ribera, tells it as a sister finding her with her feet above the floor, still holding the pan of fish she was frying.

Status: Second-hand sworn testimony. The frying-fish version is popular tradition.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), pp. 247–248, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

The angel with the golden spear, about 1559

Teresa wrote that she saw a small angel beside her, his face burning, holding a long golden spear with a little fire at its point. He seemed to thrust it into her heart and to leave her “all on fire with a great love of God.” In her manuscript she called the angel one of the cherubim; her confessor Báñez wrote in the margin that he seemed rather a seraph, and later editions followed him. She says that it happened more than once, and the Incarnation kept a tradition of a second time while she was prioress there (1571–1574). Ribera’s editor dates the first to 1559, when she was forty-four.

Status: Her own account (Life, ch. 29), known as the Transverberation. The Carmelite feast of the Transverberation was not checked for this entry.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 29, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 139–140 and note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

The cross of four stones

Teresa said that Christ handed her a cross that seemed to her a cross of four great stones, carved with the five wounds. No one else saw the stones. She gave the cross to her sister Joan (Juana), who died at Alba, and Diego de Yepes listed miracles worked through it.

Status: Her own account; the later miracles are reported by Yepes and were not read for this entry.

Source: Butler, Lives of the Saints, vol. 10, pp. 337–338.

A nephew restored to life, 1561

The annals printed with David Lewis’s translation of her Life record that in 1561 Teresa restored a nephew to life. Her own account of it was not read for this entry.

Status: Reported in the annals; details not checked.

Source: Teresa, The Life of Saint Teresa of Jesus, tr. David Lewis, annals, https://archive.org/details/the-life-of-saint-teresa-of-jesus-by-herself.

Candles that did not burn down

In the beatification process Juana del Espíritu Santo described candles that weighed the same after burning as before, and said that the sacristan attributed this to Teresa. The full circumstances are not clear from the passage read.

Status: One sworn testimony.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Juana del Espíritu Santo, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A nobleman freed at the first Mass, Valladolid, August 1568

Don Bernardino de Mendoza had offered Teresa a house by the river at Valladolid and then died suddenly, without confession. In prayer, she wrote, Christ told her that his salvation had been in serious danger, and that he would be freed from purgatory at the first Mass said in that house. At that Mass, on 15 August 1568, she saw him beside the priest, “his face was bright and shining, his hands were joined,” and he thanked her.

Status: Her own account (Foundations, ch. 10). Her editor notes that she was one day out in the date she gave.

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 10 and notes, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

Saints Catherine and Dominic at a child’s sickbed, Salamanca, 1571

While Teresa was lodging with the Count and Countess of Monterrey at Salamanca, their small daughter was dying. Teresa prayed for her, and, the story goes, St. Catherine of Siena and St. Dominic appeared to her and said that the child would live and must wear the Dominican habit for a year. Teresa told Báñez, who told the parents. The girl grew up and married the Count of Olivares.

Status: Told in the seventeenth-century chronicle of the Discalced Carmelites, as quoted by Zimmerman; not in Teresa’s own writings as read.

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 19, note 14, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

“Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana”, 1574

When the nuns of Pastrana left the princess of Eboli’s town at midnight in Holy Week 1574 and travelled to Segovia, Teresa, at Segovia, is said to have told her nuns, at the very hour of their danger on the road, “Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana.”

Status: Tradition from the chronicle of the order, quoted by Zimmerman.

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 17, notes 19–20, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

Silk that did not burn, Seville, 3 June 1576

On the day the Blessed Sacrament was solemnly carried into the new convent at Seville, gunpowder set off for the celebration exploded, and flames ran up the cloister. Teresa wrote that the silk hangings on the walls were left unsinged.

Status: Her own account (Foundations, ch. 25).

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 25, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

Her brother Lorenzo’s soul, 1580

Teresa’s brother Lorenzo died at La Serna in June 1580. A note to the English edition of her letters tells that when the news came she turned pale at recreation, went to the chapel, and said afterwards that his soul had passed through purgatory; a few days later, at Communion, she saw St. Joseph and Lorenzo. Her own letters say only that he died of a haemorrhage in less than six hours, two days after the feast of St. John.

Status: Tradition, reported in a translator’s note; the timing in the note does not match her own letters.

Source: Teresa, The Letters of Saint Teresa, vol. 4, tr. Benedictines of Stanbrook (1924), letters CCCXXXII–CCCXXXIII and CCCXLVIII and notes, https://archive.org/details/lettersofsaintte0004card.

The crystal castle, Trinity eve 1577

Diego de Yepes said that on the eve of Trinity Sunday 1577 Teresa saw in a vision a castle of crystal with seven rooms, the image behind The Interior Castle, and that he promised to say nothing about it until after her death.

Status: Reported by Yepes, as quoted in the introduction to the 1921 English translation.

Source: The Interior Castle, tr. Benedictines of Stanbrook, rev. B. Zimmerman (1921), introduction, https://archive.org/details/stteresaofavilat0000stte.

A page written in a trance, Toledo, 1577

Mary of the Nativity, a nun of the Toledo house, said she often saw Teresa writing The Interior Castle, generally after Communion, her face “resplendent.” Mother Mariana of the Angels reported hearing from her that once, when she brought a message to the cell, Teresa was seized by a trance that lasted for hours; when it ended, the paper, which had been blank, was covered with writing, and Teresa put it away in her box.

Status: Witness report, at second hand (Mary of the Nativity, as reported by Mariana of the Angels).

Source: The Interior Castle, tr. Benedictines of Stanbrook (1921), introduction, https://archive.org/details/stteresaofavilat0000stte.

Lightning at Granada, 1581–1582

Diego de Yepes wrote that the archbishop of Granada, who had not yet allowed the new house, was struck by lightning in his own house and then gave his permission.

Status: A report by Yepes, told as providence, quoted in a footnote to Ribera.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 321 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Angel water at Alba, September 1582

María de San Francisco testified that when some medicine was spilled on Teresa’s bed at Alba, it smelled of agua de ángeles, angel water.

Status: Sworn testimony, quoted in a footnote to Ribera.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 334 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Sitting up alone to receive the Host, 3 October 1582

Mariana de Jesús, then a girl living in the convent at Alba, swore in 1592 that when the Host was carried into Teresa’s cell on 3 October 1582, Teresa, who until then had needed two people to turn her, sat up alone and with great ease. Her face, the witness said, looked more like that of a very venerable man than of a woman.

Status: Sworn testimony of an eyewitness. Juana del Espíritu Santo also says she repeated that she was a daughter of the Church.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 83, 101, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Isabel de la Cruz healed of a headache, 4 October 1582

On the day Teresa died, Isabel de la Cruz, who had a headache and pain in her eyes, took Teresa’s hands, laid them on her own head, and was freed from the pain at once, according to María de San Francisco, who was present.

Status: Sworn testimony in the beatification process.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 336 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

A face like the full moon, 4 October 1582

María de San Francisco testified that as Teresa lay dying, her face, turned toward the nuns, was very beautiful and glowing, “que no parecía sino una luna llena,” like nothing so much as a full moon. She lay so from seven in the morning until her death between nine and ten at night.

Status: Sworn testimony.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 336 and note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), pp. 58–59, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

After Teresa’s death

The Lord at the foot of the bed, 4 October 1582

Ana de San Bartolomé, in whose arms Teresa died, testified in 1595 that at the moment of death she saw the Lord at the foot of the bed with saints and angels, waiting for Teresa’s soul. She asked God to take her too, or not to leave her alone, and she said that after that she never again felt grief at Teresa’s death.

Status: The sworn testimony of one witness about her own vision; legendary in character.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 170, testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, 19 Oct. 1595, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Catalina Baptista’s sense of smell, October 1582

Catalina Baptista, who had lost her sense of smell, recovered it when she kissed Teresa’s feet after her death.

Status: Testimony quoted by Ribera’s editor.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 336 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Fragrance in the cell, and shining hands

Ana de San Bartolomé testified that when Teresa died a great fragrance filled the cell, so that even the little dishes in which she had been given some broth gave off a scent, and that in the church her hands seemed to shine. Mariana de Jesús, who watched by the body that night, said she could not say what the fragrance was like, because it smelled of many things. Lovat adds that the flesh stayed soft and the limbs flexible.

Status: Sworn testimony of eyewitnesses; Lovat’s details are retold from witness reports.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 83, 170, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

A dove, a star, and visitors in white

Ana de San Bartolomé and the infirmarian Catalina de la Concepción reported a white dove coming from Teresa’s mouth, a star over the convent, and visitors in white robes in her cell. In 1596, at Medina, a witness who had heard it from the nuns added a crystal-coloured ray of light by the cell window.

Status: Witness reports given years later, much of it hearsay, with the marks of legend.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 337 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), p. 59, testimony of Polanco, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

A crystal globe, music, and a procession of virgins

Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912, citing depositions printed by the Bollandists, says that two nuns saw Teresa’s soul rise, one as a white dove and one as a crystal globe, and that a third heard music and saw a procession of virgins.

Status: Witness depositions retold in a later devotional Life; legendary in character.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Died of love: a revelation at Veas

At Veas, Catalina de Jesús said that it was revealed to her that Teresa had died of an ímpetu de amor, an impulse of love.

Status: A reported revelation.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 337–338, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

The dead tree that flowered at Alba

Lovat says that a tree beside the chapel at Alba, dead for years, put out leaves and flowers after Teresa’s death, and that the bull of canonization mentions it. We have not read the bull.

Status: Reported; its place in the bull is not verified.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

A relative of the duchess cured of fever

Ana de San Bartolomé testified that a nun at Alba, a relative of the Duchess of Alba, who suffered from a continual fever, was healed when Teresa’s clothes were put on her.

Status: Sworn testimony of one witness.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

The cloth with fresh blood

A woollen cloth had been placed under Teresa for the flow of blood in her last illness. Ana de San Bartolomé testified that when she took it off, the blood was fresh. The articles of 1609–10 say that after three years it was found soaked in blood “as if in that very hour it had come from the body,” and that doctors judged it a miracle. They add that drops the colour of blood had been seen on the girdle she was buried in, kept by the Discalced nuns of Zaragoza.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 100, p. LXXI, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

The body found whole, 4 July 1583

Teresa was buried between the two grilles of the lower choir at Alba, and a wall of stones and lime was built over the coffin. The nuns noticed a strong fragrance from the tomb and asked leave to open it. On 4 July 1583, nine months after her death, the provincial, Jerome Gracián, dug it open with his companions. The earth and stones smelled sweet; the coffin was broken, rotten and mouldy, and her clothes had rotted, but the body, the witnesses said, was whole and flexible, “so fresh and whole that it seemed the blood was running.” Only the tip of the nose was damaged, where a board had broken under the weight of the stones and lime. Gracián cut off the left hand, which went to the Carmelites of Lisbon, before the body was buried again. The next day a painter working on the grille asked what the smell was. The articles of 1609–10 say that learned doctors judged it a miracle.

Status: Sworn testimony of the nuns of Alba, among them Mariana de Jesús, who helped to dig; put forward in the articles of 1609–10. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 83–84, 101–102, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 97, p. LXIX–LXX, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 606, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Oil from the body and its relics

The articles of 1609–10 say that an oil or balm flowed from Teresa’s body and from every fragment of her flesh, and soaked into many cloths. Ana de San Bartolomé, who cleaned the body at Ávila in 1585, described a sweat “like a kind of oil, or water mixed with blood.”

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 98, p. LXX, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

The body carried to Ávila, and back, 1585–1586

Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza had built the main chapel of St. Joseph’s in Ávila on condition that his body and Teresa’s lie there, and the friars’ first chapter after her death agreed. In November 1585 the provincial came to Alba with Gracián; the nuns were sent to Matins, and the body was taken from the tomb. Juana del Espíritu Santo said she held it in her arms a long while, and that it was as light as if it were not flesh and bone; an arm was cut off to stay at Alba “as easily as the breast of a bird.” In choir the nuns smelled the fragrance and left Matins. At Ávila, Ana de San Bartolomé found that the clothing touching the flesh was as on the first day. The Duke of Alba and his uncle, the prior of the Order of St. John, won letters from Pope Sixtus V, and on 23 August 1586 the body was returned to Alba.

Status: Sworn testimony of Juana del Espíritu Santo and Ana de San Bartolomé; put forward in the articles of 1609–10.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 101–102, 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 101, p. LXXI–LXXII, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Teresa’s appearances to Ana about her body

Ana de San Bartolomé testified that after the body was taken to Ávila, Teresa appeared to her and told her that it would be taken back. About eighteen months before Ana testified in 1595, in Madrid at the dawn hour of prayer, Teresa told her that letters had gone to Rome for a papal brief to return the body to Alba, and Our Lord told her it would return “through a woman,” whom Ana does not name.

Status: The sworn testimony of one witness.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 171–172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Later openings, 1592 to 2024

In March 1592 the bishop of Salamanca, Jerónimo Manrique, moved the body to a new chest given by María de Toledo, Duchess of Alba, after almost three years unopened, and found it as before. About 1594 Ana de San Bartolomé saw much flesh cut from a shoulder and almost half the belly, taken for relics, and the fragrance stronger. Later openings up to 1750, when the body was clothed again and given a new silver shrine, reported it in the same state, and so did the opening of 1914. After the tomb was opened again for study in 2024, two radiographers reported in an interview in October 2024 that the body is better described as mummified than as literally incorrupt. They found a severe curvature of the spine.

Status: Sworn testimony for 1592–94; contested in modern terms. “Incorrupt” is the popular word; the 2024 radiographers preferred “mummified.”

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 90–91, 172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 608, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich; Aleteia, 15 Oct. 2024, https://aleteia.org/2024/10/15/1st-results-from-exams-of-teresa-of-avilas-relics/.

The heart and its thorns

Lovat says that Teresa’s heart was taken out by a lay sister and is kept in crystal at Alba, that it shows the wound of the angel’s spear, and that three “thorns” formed from the dust at the bottom of the reliquary. The date when the heart was removed was not found.

Status: Legend; the thorns are disputed.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 607 note, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Appearances after death

Lovat reports that Teresa appeared the day after her death to the prioress of Veas, later to a young nun at Veas, and also to Father Gracián and to Teresita.

Status: Witness reports retold by Lovat.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), pp. 608–609, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

A relic in Rome, 1602

Gracián wrote that when a piece of Teresa’s flesh was carried into the church of the new Discalced convent of San Giuseppe a Capo le Case in Rome, it gave off a sweet fragrance, and a nun there, a niece of Cardinal Baronio, was healed. The pope then allowed Teresa’s portrait to be placed on the altar, which Gracián called “a kind of beatification.”

Status: Gracián’s account in his Peregrinación de Anastasio, quoted by Silverio.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), introduction, p. XXVI, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Put forward for her canonization, 1609–1610

The articles drawn up for the hearings at Salamanca in 1609–10 set out these healings through her relics, and witnesses were examined on them. They are told here as the articles tell them.

A nun who could not smell, Lisbon

Sister Inés de la Madre de Dios had had no sense of smell since birth. When the provincial brought Teresa’s hand to the convent at Lisbon and her sisters spoke of its fragrance, she knelt and said she would not get up until she could smell what her sisters smelled. She was healed.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 99, p. LXX, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

“They say it smells; it stinks”, Malagón

At Malagón a lay sister who had once been corrected by Teresa thought, when the nuns spoke of the fragrance of a finger relic, “they say it smells; rather, it stinks.” A strong fragrance rose from the relic, she fell to the floor, and she confessed her fault.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

A judge’s son near death, Alba

Licentiate Vallejo, a judge of the Duke of Alba’s council, had an only son, two years old, whose life had been despaired of. The chaplain, Pedro de Zamora, laid on the child’s head a cloth stained with Teresa’s blood. The child revived, clutched the cloth and would not let anyone take it. His father, in the church, heard the child’s voice and did not dare to look.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 102, p. LXXII, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Shot at close range, Seville

A priest, Pedro Fernández de Barragán of Valverde, carried in his shirt a paper with words of Teresa copied from Ribera’s Life. A loaded arquebus in the hands of the vicar general, Bernardino Rodríguez, fired ten or twelve lead balls at his chest, two fingers from the paper. The balls rebounded five or six cubits as if from a stone, and he was unhurt. An inquiry was drawn up at the time.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Isabel de San Jerónimo, paralysed on one side, Seville

Sister Isabel de San Jerónimo, paralysed on one side, was healed when the provincial laid the finger relic on her arm. The articles say she lived twelve years afterwards without pain.

A prioress with consumption, Segovia

Isabel de Santo Domingo, prioress at Segovia, was ill with consumption. She improved after taking a little of the earth from Teresa’s tomb, and on Christmas Day she led the choir.

An earache, and an oath, Zaragoza

Sister Leonor de los Ángeles, in severe pain in her ear, was healed by a cloth with oil from Teresa’s body. When she refused to swear to the miracle, having never taken an oath, the prioress said, “Be quiet, sister, the holy Mother will take revenge for this.” She fell ill with a double tertian fever and recovered after she promised to swear.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

Isabel de Montoya’s cataracts, Salamanca, February 1603

Isabel de Montoya, of the convent of Santa María de las Dueñas, was blind from cataracts. On 10 February 1603 a relic of Teresa’s flesh was touched to her eyes. By the following Saturday she could see the Host, and on 21 February she walked without a guide, and the nuns sang the Te Deum.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

A dying priest who could not confess, Lisbon

Licentiate Tomás de Baez de Polanco, once vicar general of Córdoba, was dying with his mind so clouded that he could not confess. Teresa’s hand was laid on his head; his mind cleared, he made a general confession, and he recovered.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

A boy with lethargy, Medina del Campo

Antonio de Villarroel, a boy sick with modorra, a deep lethargy, was healed within a quarter of an hour when a cloth with Teresa’s oil was laid on his head.

Fifty fainting fits a day, Medina del Campo

Luisa de Ordas, aged sixteen, fainted fifty times a day. She was healed by Teresa’s small scapular. When the fits returned five years later, it healed her a second time.

A crippled novice walks, Medina del Campo, 1 January 1586

Juana del Espíritu Santo, a novice, had suffered eighteen months of fever and sciatica and could not walk. On the feast of the Circumcision, 1 January 1586, she was given a piece of Teresa’s girdle. She felt great pain, then stood up and walked down the stairs alone.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.

A prioress in time of plague, Granada

Ana de Jesús, prioress at Granada, fell ill with blisters and fever during a plague. She slept with a piece of Teresa’s habit and woke healthy.

Miracles in many kingdoms

The last of the articles says that miracles through Teresa were being worked not only in Spain but in many other provinces and kingdoms. Lovat likewise writes that many miracles were worked through her relics in quick succession while her cause went forward.

Status: Put forward in the articles of the canonization hearings at Salamanca, 1609–10, on which witnesses were examined. Whether the Church finally approved it was not found.
