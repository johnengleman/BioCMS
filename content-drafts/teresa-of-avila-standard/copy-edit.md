## Biography: sentences changed

### Opening

1. Before: She wrote that through it the way of truth was printed on her as a child.
   After: She wrote that through that word the way of truth was printed on her as a child.
   Why: "it" could mean "love" from the sentence before.

2. Before: They piled little stones into cells in the garden, and the stones kept falling down.
   After: They piled up little stones to make cells in the garden, and the stones kept falling down.
   Why: Stones are not piled "into" cells; they make the cells.

3. Before: When at last she gave herself wholly, she reformed the Carmelite Order.
   After: When at last she gave herself wholly to God, she reformed the Carmelite Order.
   Why: "gave herself wholly" needs an object.

### Saint Teresa Leaves Her Father's House

4. Before: The hardships of a nun could not be worse than purgatory, she argued, and afterward she would go straight to heaven, "which was my desire."
   After: She argued that the hardships of a nun could not be worse than purgatory, and that afterward she would go straight to heaven, "which was my desire."
   Why: Speaker tag in the middle of the sentence; the reported speech now reads in one line. Quotation unchanged.

5. Before: For her that was almost like taking the habit, because she never went back on her word.
   After: She felt that telling him was almost like taking the nun's habit, because she never went back on her word.
   Why: "that" was unclear (the courage? the telling?). "habit" needed "nun's" at its first use.

6. Before: On All Souls' Day, 2 November 1535, Antonio walked with her from their father's house …
   After: On All Souls' Day, 2 November 1535, her brother walked with her from their father's house …
   Why: The story later follows a second Antonio (Antonio de Heredia, Antonio of Jesus). Using "her brother" here keeps the two men apart. The name stays in the sentence before.

### Saint Teresa Nearly Dies and Cannot Walk

7. Before: The doctors gave her up.
   After: The doctors gave up hope for her.
   Why: "gave her up" can be read as "handed her over".

8. Before: … lay without any sense for almost four days.
   After: … lay without her senses for almost four days.
   Why: "without any sense" means "foolish". Now matches "He lay three days without his senses" later.

9. Before: She could move one finger of her right hand and nothing else, so two people moved her in a sheet, one at each end.
   After: … so two people shifted her in a sheet, one at each end.
   Why: "move … moved" in one sentence.

10. Before: As soon as she could, she hurried to be carried back to her convent just as she was.
    After: As soon as she could, she had herself carried back to her convent just as she was.
    Why: "hurried to be carried" is awkward; the haste stays in "as soon as she could".

11. Before: A nun of the Incarnation remembered that she brought joy to everyone who nursed her.
    After: A nun of the Incarnation remembered that Saint Teresa brought joy to everyone who nursed her.
    Why: "she" could be the nun.

12. Before: Saint Teresa left one line about the day she could first crawl: …
    After: She left one line about the day she could first crawl: …
    Why: Avoids the name twice in a row after fix 11.

### Heading

13. Before: Saint Teresa Torn Between God and the World
    After: Saint Teresa Wavers Between God and the World
    Why: The heading had no verb. "Is Torn" made 9 words (limit 8). Anchor id unchanged.

### Saint Teresa Wavers Between God and the World

14. Before: He believed her, because he never told a lie himself, and he felt sorry for her.
    After: He never told a lie himself, so he believed her, and he felt sorry for her.
    Why: Two clauses stacked after the verb; cause now comes first.

15. Before: She told him that perhaps His Majesty wanted him to feel a little of that pain.
    After: She told him that perhaps God wanted him to feel a little of that pain.
    Why: Outside a quotation, "His Majesty" reads as the King.

16. Before: On the day he died the Lord gave him back his mind so fully that they were amazed.
    After: … so fully that those around him were amazed.
    Why: "they" had no clear antecedent.

17. Before: She called those years a stormy sea, and she sailed it for nearly twenty years.
    After: She called her life in those days a stormy sea, and she sailed it for nearly twenty years.
    Why: "those years … twenty years", and "it" did not match "years".

### Saint Teresa Weeps Before the Wounded Christ

18. Before: Her soul was tired, but her old habits would not let it rest.
    After: Her soul was tired, but her old ways would not let it rest.
    Why: In a nun's story "habits" reads as her robes.

19. Before: One day she went into the oratory of the convent and saw an image …
    After: One day she went into the small chapel of the convent and saw an image …
    Why: Plain word for a Church word.

### Saint Teresa's Visions Frighten Her Confessors

20. Before: The graces she now received in prayer frightened her, for she was afraid of being deceived.
    After: The graces she now received in prayer troubled her, for she was afraid of being deceived.
    Why: "frightened … afraid … great fear … such fear" in four lines.

21. Before: She was in an oratory in great distress when she read some words of Saint Paul in a book.
    After: She was in great distress in a small chapel when she read some words of Saint Paul in a book.
    Why: Plain word; same as fix 19.

22. Before: It seemed to her that the Lord had put it into her hands.
    After: It seemed to her that the Lord had put the book into her hands.
    Why: "it" could be the words or the book.

23. Before: God is faithful, the words said, and He never lets those who love Him be deceived by the devil.
    After: The words said that God is faithful, and that He never lets those who love Him be deceived by the devil.
    Why: Source tag in the middle of the sentence.

24. Before: He told her the visions were the work of God, and she was greatly comforted.
    After: … and she was greatly consoled.
    Why: "comforted" ends the paragraph just before.

25. Before: About 1559 she saw an angel at her left side in bodily form.
    After: About 1559 she saw an angel in bodily form at her left side.
    Why: Word order; "in bodily form" belongs to the angel.

26. Before: She herself went out most of all, because great people asked for her company.
    After: … because important people asked for her company.
    Why: Plain word.

### Saint Teresa Founds a Poor Convent in Ávila

27. Before: Her sister Juana and Juan de Ovalle, Juana's husband, bought a small house …
    After: Her sister Juana and Juana's husband Juan de Ovalle bought a small house …
    Why: Interruption between subject and verb.

28. Before: Had she disobeyed her Provincial?
    After: Had she disobeyed her superior, the Provincial?
    Why: First use of the Church word; the meaning now comes with it.

29. Before: When the Provincial came, she went to her judgement glad to be suffering something for the Lord.
    After: When the Provincial came, she went to be judged, glad to suffer something for the Lord.
    Why: "went to her judgement glad" is awkward.

30. Before: He rebuked her severely.
    After: The Provincial rebuked her severely.
    Why: After "how Christ had been judged" and a quotation, "He" read as Christ.

31. Before: Then she was told to explain herself before the nuns, and the Provincial was satisfied.
    After: … and he was satisfied.
    Why: Avoids the title twice after fix 30.

32. Before: One Dominican said that the matter belonged to the bishop and should be studied, not dissolved.
    After: One Dominican said that the matter belonged to the bishop, and that the house should be looked into, not dissolved.
    Why: A matter is not "dissolved"; the house is.

33. Before: The day before the Provincial came, the prioress ordered her …
    After: The day before a visit from the Provincial, the prioress ordered her …
    Why: "the Provincial came" already happened two paragraphs earlier; this is a later visit.

34. Before: Her nuns asked for a book of their own, so very probably at the end of 1565 she wrote them The Way of Perfection.
    After: Her nuns asked for a book of their own, so she wrote them The Way of Perfection, very probably at the end of 1565.
    Why: Word order. The doubt word stays; see "Listed" below.

### Saint John of the Cross Joins the Reform

35. Before: She was very glad, for now she had "two living stones" for the house she wanted to build for her Spouse, whom she loved so much.
    After: She was very glad. Now she had "two living stones" for the house she wanted to build for Christ, the Spouse she loved so much.
    Why: Readers may not know that "her Spouse" means Christ; the sentence had two tails.

36. Before: Saint Teresa saw the house in Lent 1569 on her way to Toledo. Two merchant friends from Medina came with her.
    After: Saint Teresa saw the house in Lent 1569, on her way to Toledo.
    Why: The block quote that follows tells the same fact in her words ("two merchants, friends of mine, who had come with me from Medina"). The slim prompt asks not to retell a block quote just before it. No fact lost.

### Saint Teresa Founds Convents with Almost Nothing

37. Before: She says she spoke with a certain firmness.
    After: She wrote that she spoke with a certain firmness.
    Why: Tense; the rest of the text uses "she wrote".

38. Before: At Salamanca in 1570 she came with only one companion.
    After: She arrived at Salamanca in 1570 with only one companion.
    Why: "At Salamanca … came" does not fit.

39. Before: She almost complained to the Lord. Either He should stop ordering her to take on these works, she told Him, or He should fix this need.
    After: Almost complaining, she told the Lord that He should either stop ordering her to take on these works or help her in this need.
    Why: "almost complained" then "she told Him" contradicted itself; mid-sentence tag; "fix this need" is odd.

40. Before: She saw that the good man had done far better to trust than she had done with her worry.
    After: She saw that the good man's trust had done far better than all her worry.
    Why: Awkward comparison.

### A Princess Turns Against Saint Teresa's Nuns

41. Before: Her husband was Ruy Gómez de Silva, one of the most powerful men at the court of King Philip II.
    After: The Princess's husband was Ruy Gómez de Silva, …
    Why: "Her" could be Saint Teresa.

42. Before: Saint Teresa begged her superiors by every means to move the convent, and they agreed.
    After: Saint Teresa pleaded with her superiors in every way she could to move the convent, and they agreed.
    Why: "begged … by every means" is not natural English.

43. Before: She handed back every gift the Princess had ever made against a written receipt, and the last Mass was said.
    After: She handed back every gift the Princess had ever made and took a written receipt. Then the last Mass was said.
    Why: "against a written receipt" is trade jargon; two facts glued with ", and".

44. Before: Six nuns went with her, with the priest Julián de Ávila and a layman named Antonio Gaytán.
    After: Six nuns went with her, and so did the priest Julián de Ávila and a layman named Antonio Gaytán.
    Why: "with her, with".

### Saint Teresa Obeys and Travels to Seville

45. Before: Seville tried her more than any house except Ávila, and the trials were inside her.
    After: At Seville she suffered more than at any other house except Ávila, and the trials were inside her.
    Why: A place did what only a person does.

46. Before: But she felt that the Lord was holding back His hand a little. In that way she would see that any courage she had ever had was not her own.
    After: But she felt that the Lord was holding back His hand a little, so that she would see that any courage she had ever had was not her own.
    Why: "In that way she would see" read as a result, not the purpose the sentence means.

47. Before: And Gracián ordered her to sit for her portrait to Fray Juan de la Miseria, as one more act of self-denial.
    After: Gracián also ordered her to let Fray Juan de la Miseria paint her portrait, as one more act of self-denial.
    Why: "sit for her portrait to" is awkward.

48. Before: The Pope's envoy in Spain took its side. Felipe Sega called her "a restless, roving woman," and under him the leaders of the reform were jailed or sent away.
    After: The Pope's envoy in Spain, Felipe Sega, took its side. He called her "a restless, roving woman." Under him the leaders of the reform were jailed or sent away.
    Why: The reader could not tell that Sega was the envoy; facts glued with ", and". See "Listed" for the date.

## Listed for the checker (not fixed)

1. Opening, paragraph 2 and block quote: the text says Rodrigo "was four years older than his sister". In the quote that follows she writes "I had one brother almost my age". A reader will see a conflict. Check note 2 against the quote, or add a word that reconciles them.
2. "Saint Teresa Leaves Her Father's House", paragraph 4: "and the old question came back." No question was set up earlier. Which question does the source give (being a nun? eternal things?)? A fix needs the source's words.
3. Block quote, note 12: "I remember clearly, and it is true, that when I left my father's house, I do not think the pain will be greater when I die." The English sentence is broken (the "that" clause never finishes). Translate again from Vida ch.4.
4. Block quotes, notes 46 and 101: "It was made for you … you do it" and "O God, you will not despise" use a lowercase "you" for God. The other quotes and the narrative use "You", "He", "Him". Decide one style. Not changed, because it is inside quotation marks.
5. "very probably at the end of 1565" (note 49): a doubt word in the story. The slim prompt asks to say less (drop the date) or move the doubt to the note. That removes a fact, so the checker decides.
6. Toledo paragraph (note 56): "Early the next morning Andrada came back." The text never says that she sent him off to find a house, so "came back" has nothing before it. The missing step needs the source.
7. Toledo paragraph (note 58): the nuns "answered" with "What is the matter, Mother?" In English this reads as the nuns asking her the same question. The Spanish (Fund. ch.15) seems to mean "What should be the matter with us, Mother, when we no longer seem poor?" Translate again.
8. Two women with near-identical names: Blessed Anne of Jesus (notes 62, 113) and Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé, who is "Blessed Ana" throughout. A reader can confuse them. Consider giving Anne of Jesus her full name every time, or a role ("one of the Salamanca nuns").
9. Princess paragraph (note 64): four new names in one paragraph (Ana de Mendoza, Ruy Gómez de Silva, Philip II, Juan de la Miseria). The rules allow two. Cutting a name is a content choice.
10. Paragraph of 19 July 1575 (note 79): it says the Pope's envoy "took its side" and names Felipe Sega. The paragraph is dated 1575. Check the date Sega became envoy. If he came later, the paragraph needs a time word ("Later the Pope's new envoy…").
11. Arm (note 84): "For the rest of her life Saint Teresa could not use that arm". Her own letter says "I can hardly use it". At Alba she "lifted her arm". Check whether "could not use" is too strong, and which arm she lifted.
12. Valladolid farewell (note 92): "The prioress of that house plucked her sleeve" — whose sleeve, Saint Teresa's or Blessed Ana's?
13. Fix 55: "She obeyed to be like Christ…" is Ribera's reading (note 93). The checker should confirm that Ribera states it as fact, not as a guess. If he guesses, use "Her first biographer believed…".
14. Alba paragraph (note 107): "The convent's foundress asked a mason…" A reader may think this is Saint Teresa, who founded convents. Give the foundress a role or a name if the source has one.
15. Pedro de Castro letter (note 89): "to-night" is an old spelling inside a quotation. Modernize to "tonight" if the checker agrees.
16. Homily quote (note 117): "We have conferred, or rather we have recognized, the title of Doctor of the Church on Saint Teresa" — "recognized … on" does not fit. Translate again from the Italian.
17. Doctor chapter (note 116): "the Pope approved the vote in her favour" — no vote was mentioned before. Name who voted (the Congregation?) from the source.
18. "Doctor of the Church" is never explained. The opening says "its first woman Doctor". A short gloss with the first use would help readers, but it needs a source (Paul VI's letter).
19. Saint John's chapter (note 50): "He was then called John of Saint Matthias" has no "Saint" before the name. This is his name at the time, not a missing title, so it was left. Confirm.
20. Death chapter (note 97): "Afterward some of the nuns heard him ask her…" is near a proof sentence, but it gives "them" in "not to leave them" a meaning. Left.
21. Death chapter (note 103): the text calls it a "psalm verse" (fix 60) because paragraph 101 says "a verse from a psalm". If the source gives several verses, use "the psalm verses".
