## SC-01 — The road to the Moors, about 1522
- Status: full
- When and where: about 1522, Ávila. Teresa was about seven [Silverio note, VIDA-Sp p.7 n.1: "cuando Teresa tenía siete años"]. They left by the "puerta de Adaja" and went "por la puente adelante" [Ribera p.96].
- Who was there: Teresa; her brother Rodrigo; an uncle who met them; her mother, who had the house searched. Teresa herself does not name her brother. Ribera names him Rodrigo de Cepeda and says he later died in the Río de la Plata as a captain [Ribera p.96].
- What happened, in order:
  1. The two read lives of saints together; martyrs seemed to buy heaven "muy barato" [VIDA-Sp ch.1 p.6].
  2. They agreed to go "a tierra de moros, pidiendo por amor de Dios", to be beheaded there [VIDA-Sp p.6].
  3. Ribera adds that they took "alguna cosilla para comer" and left their father's house [Ribera p.96].
  4. They crossed the bridge over the Adaja. An uncle met them and brought them home [Ribera p.96].
  5. Their mother had been searching everywhere, afraid they had fallen into the house well [Ribera p.96].
  6. Rodrigo made his excuse: "El niño se excusaba con decir que su hermana le había hecho tomar aquel camino" [Ribera p.96]. Plain: the boy excused himself by saying his sister had made him take that road.
  7. At home they were frightened by what they read: glory and pain last "para siempre". They kept repeating it [VIDA-Sp p.7; Ribera p.97].
  8. Then they tried to be hermits, piling little stones in the garden. The stones fell down [VIDA-Sp p.7].
- Details: they thought "el tener padres" was the biggest obstacle [VIDA-Sp p.7]. Silverio (facts-only) puts the stop near the "Cuatro Postes", where a wayside shrine stands today [VIDA-Sp p.7 n.1].
- Words spoken: Rodrigo's excuse above [Ribera p.96]. Silverio quotes Yepes with almost the same line: "riñóles la madre de la ausencia que habían hecho, y el hermano se excusaba diciendo, que la niña le había incitado y hecho tomar aquel camino" [VIDA-Sp p.7 n.1, citing Yepes, *Vida* I, c.2]. This is a later report, not an eyewitness. The uncle's words are not recorded.
- Inner life: she says she did not do it from love of God, "no por amor que yo entendiese tenerle", but to enjoy heaven soon [VIDA-Sp p.6]. Later she writes that it moves her to devotion to see how soon God gave her what she lost by her own fault [VIDA-Sp p.7].
- Testimony (Teresa, VIDA-Sp ch.1 pp.6-7, public-domain; "..." marks my cuts):
  > "Tenía uno casi de mi edad ... Como vía los martirios que por Dios las Santas pasaban, parecíame compraban muy barato el ir a gozar de Dios ... Concertábamos irnos a tierra de moros, pidiendo por amor de Dios, para que allá nos descabezasen ... sino que el tener padres nos parecía el mayor embarazo. Espantábanos mucho el decir que pena y gloria era para siempre, en lo que leíamos. ... gustábamos de decir muchas veces: ¡para siempre, siempre, siempre!"
  
  Plain: "I had one brother almost my age. When I read how the saints suffered martyrdom for God, it seemed to me they bought the joy of heaven very cheaply. We planned to go to the land of the Moors, begging for God's sake, so that they would cut off our heads. Only our parents seemed to stand in the way. It frightened us to read that pain and glory last forever. We liked to say it again and again: 'Forever, forever, forever!'"
- What changed: the wish to give God everything took root; she writes that this repeated word left "el camino de la verdad" printed on her childhood [VIDA-Sp p.7].
- Not in the sources: the uncle's name in Teresa or Ribera (Silverio's note names him Francisco de Cepeda, citing Yepes; treat as a late claim); his words; the time of day; the season; how far they walked; any tears or scolding by the uncle. Teresa does not describe the arrest at all. Ribera says Rodrigo was four years older; Silverio says born 1511. Ribera p.96 n. says Gregory XV had the episode put into the canonization process.
- Sources: VIDA-Sp pp.6-7 and notes; Ribera pp.96-97; Lewis ch.1 §4-5.

## SC-02 — Leaving her father's house, 2 November 1535 (date disputed)
- Status: full
- When and where: early morning of All Souls' Day, 2 Nov 1535, Ávila: from her father's house to the Incarnation. Ribera: "un día muy de mañana, que fué á 2 de Noviembre, día de la Conmemoración de las Animas, año de 1535, siendo de edad de veinte años y siete meses" [Ribera p.103].
- Who was there: Teresa; her brother Antonio de Ahumada, who went with her; her father, who did not know [Ribera p.103].
- What happened, in order:
  1. For three months she fought herself over becoming a nun, and read St Jerome's letters [VIDA-Sp ch.3 pp.17-18].
  2. She told her father. He loved her too much and refused: "en ninguna manera lo pude acabar con él". The most he would grant was that she could do as she liked after his death [VIDA-Sp p.18].
  3. She feared her own weakness and chose another way [VIDA-Sp p.18].
  4. She had persuaded a brother to become a friar. They agreed to set out early one morning [VIDA-Sp ch.4 p.19].
  5. Ribera: she told nobody except Antonio, who went with her, and walked straight to the Incarnation [Ribera p.103].
  6. On taking the habit she felt at once a great joy [VIDA-Sp p.20].
- Details: Ribera adds that she went "sin dar cuenta á nadie" [Ribera p.103]. Her own text does not name the brother; Silverio's note says Antonio [VIDA-Sp p.19 n.1, facts-only].
- Words spoken: none quoted.
- Inner life: "me parece cada hueso se me apartaba por sí" [VIDA-Sp p.19]. After the habit: "A la hora me dió un tan gran contento de tener aquel estado, que nunca jamás me faltó hasta hoy" [VIDA-Sp p.20].
- Testimony (Teresa, VIDA-Sp ch.4 pp.19-20, public-domain):
  > "Acuérdaseme, a todo mi parecer, y con verdad, que cuando salí de casa de mi padre, no creo será más el sentimiento cuando me muera; porque me parece cada hueso se me apartaba por sí, que, como no había amor de Dios que quitase el amor del padre y parientes, era todo haciéndome una fuerza tan grande, que si el Señor no me ayudara, no bastaran mis consideraciones para ir adelante. Aquí me dió ánimo contra mí, de manera que lo puse por obra."
  
  Plain: "I remember clearly, and it is true, that when I left my father's house, I do not think the pain will be greater when I die. It felt as if every bone in me was pulling away from the others. No love of God had yet taken away my love for my father and family, and the pull was so strong that, without the Lord's help, my own reasons would not have carried me on. There He gave me courage against myself, and I did it."
- What changed: she is a nun. Soon after, her health fails (the first year of "harto mala salud", fainting and a bad heart) [VIDA-Sp ch.4 p.21].
- Not in the sources: any goodbye, any note left for her father, her clothes, the weather, the walk's length. The date is disputed: Ribera says 2 Nov 1535 on documents; Silverio's own note in the 1915 *Obras* says habit 2 Nov 1536 and profession 3 Nov 1537 [VIDA-Sp p.20 n.1]; the Lewis annals say 1533 and profession 1534. Use one date and say "about" if unsure. The father's consent: refused (Teresa and Ribera agree).
- Sources: VIDA-Sp pp.17-21; Ribera pp.103-104; Lewis ch.3 §9, ch.4 §1-2 (annals).

## SC-03 — "This daughter is not for burying", August of the year of her great illness
- Status: full
- When and where: the night of the August feast of Our Lady (15 Aug), at her father's house in Ávila [VIDA-Sp ch.5 p.32; Ribera p.110]. Year not in her own text. Lewis annals: 1537 (listed under July). Treat as 1537 with care; the entry-date dispute (SC-02) affects it.
- Who was there: Teresa; her father Alonso; her brother Lorenzo; nuns and friars who came to watch. Doctors had given her up as "ética" [Ribera p.110].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Teresa wanted to confess before the feast; her father, thinking it was fear of death, refused [VIDA-Sp p.32].
  2. That night she fell into a fit and was senseless for almost four days. She was anointed; they said the Creed over her [VIDA-Sp p.32].
  3. The grave was open at the Incarnation for a day and a half; funeral honours were held by the friars elsewhere [VIDA-Sp p.32].
  4. Ribera: her father felt her pulse, would not believe she was dead, and said "Esta hija no es para enterrar" [Ribera p.111]. Ana de la Encarnación (sworn testimony, hearsay: "Esto oyó decir") has him kneeling at the foot of the bed, saying she was "no era hija para enterrar" [Proc-I pp.22-23].
  5. Ribera: Lorenzo fell asleep watching; a candle burned down and set the pillows and covers alight; the smoke woke him [Ribera p.111].
  6. She came to herself and asked at once to confess. She received Communion with many tears [VIDA-Sp pp.32-33].
  7. She could not move, "sólo un dedo" of her right hand; they moved her in a sheet until Easter; then she had herself carried to the Incarnation [VIDA-Sp p.35-36].
  8. She was crippled eight months and a bad paralysis for almost three years. She writes: "Cuando comencé a andar a gatas, alababa a Dios" (plain: when I began to crawl on my hands and knees, I praised God) [VIDA-Sp p.36].
- Details: wax on her eyelids, found later: "hasta la cera me hallé después en los ojos" [VIDA-Sp p.32]. Tongue bitten to pieces; throat so dry she could not swallow even water [VIDA-Sp p.35]. Ana says she gave joy to those who nursed her [Proc-I p.23, paraphrase].
- Inner life on Joseph: she turned to "los del cielo" after "los médicos de la tierra" failed; she took "por abogado y señor a el glorioso San Josef" [VIDA-Sp p.38]. Later: "Pues él hizo, como quien es, en hacer de manera que pudiese levantarme, y andar, y no estar tullida; y yo, como quien soy, en usar mal de esta merced" [VIDA-Sp p.39 n.]. Plain: "He did what he does: he made it possible for me to rise and walk and not be crippled. And I did what I do: I misused the favour."
- Testimony (Teresa, VIDA-Sp ch.5 p.32, public-domain):
  > "Dióme aquella noche un parajismo, que me duró estar sin ningún sentido cuatro días poco menos. En esto me dieron el Sacramento de la Unción, y cada hora u memento pensaban expiraba, y no hacían sino decirme el credo, como si alguna cosa entendiera. Teníanme a veces por tan muerta, que hasta la cera me hallé después en los ojos. La pena de mi padre era grande de no me haber dejado confesar; ... tiniendo día y medio abierta la sepoltura en mi monesterio ... quiso el Señor tornase en mí."
  
  Plain: "That night I had a fit that left me without any sense for almost four days. They gave me the sacrament of the sick, and every hour they thought I was dying. They only said the Creed to me, as if I could understand anything. At times they thought me so dead that I found wax on my eyes afterward. My father was in great distress that he had not let me confess ... The grave had stood open a day and a half in my convent ... and the Lord was pleased that I came back to myself."
- What changed: she walks again but, within the same chapter, she says she soon fell back: "¡Quién dijera que había tan presto de caer!" [VIDA-Sp p.39].
- Not in the sources: when exactly Joseph healed her (she gives no date); how she was healed; her father's words at the bedside other than the single line. Ribera himself doubts the report that she saw heaven, hell, and her future convents in the coma, and says she called it nonsense [Ribera p.111]. Her father's kneeling at the bed foot is Ana's hearsay only.
- Sources: VIDA-Sp pp.31-39 and n.4; Ribera pp.110-112; Proc-I pp.22-23 (Ana de la Encarnación; facts-only, quotation 7 words); Lewis annals.

## SC-04 — Before the wounded Christ, in Lent (year unsettled)
- Status: full
- When and where: the Incarnation, Ávila, in the oratory. Year not in her text. Lewis annals place a related change in 1555; the outline says about 1554. Mark "about 1554-55".
- Who was there: Teresa alone.
- What happened, in order:
  1. She was worn out, tied to old habits [VIDA-Sp ch.9 p.62].
  2. Entering the oratory one day, she saw an image of Christ "muy llagado" that had been brought there for a feast of the house [p.62].
  3. She was upset that she had paid back those wounds so badly. Her heart seemed to break. She threw herself down beside it, weeping, and begged to be strengthened "para no ofenderle" [p.62].
  4. She told Him she would not rise until He did what she asked [p.63].
  5. After that she improved. About this time the *Confessions* of St Augustine were given to her, "porque yo no las procuré" [p.65]. At his conversion in the garden she wept a long time [p.66].
- Details: the image is called "imagen"; Silverio's note (facts-only) says it was a pitiful, tender *Ecce Homo* still venerated at the Incarnation [p.62 n.1]. She says she could not picture Christ in her mind, which is why she loved images [p.65].
- Words spoken: "no me había de levantar de allí hasta que hiciese lo que le suplicaba" (reported by her, p.63).
- Inner life, on the lost years: "estuve un año, y más, sin tener oración, pareciéndome más humildad" and called it "la mayor tentación que tuve" [ch.7, page not checked]. "Pasé este mar tempestuoso casi veinte años" and "ni yo gozaba de Dios, ni traía contento en el mundo" [ch.8 pp.55-56]. Of twenty-eight years since she began prayer, she says "más de los deciocho pasé esta batalla" [p.56].
- Testimony (Teresa, VIDA-Sp ch.9 pp.62-63, public-domain):
  > "Acaecióme que entrando un día en el oratorio, vi una imagen que habían traído allí a guardar ... Era de Cristo muy llagado, y tan devota, que en mirándola, toda me turbó de verle tal, porque representaba bien lo que pasó por nosotros. Fué tanto lo que sentí de lo mal que había agradecido aquellas llagas, que el corazón me parece se me partía, y arrojéme cabe El con grandísimo derramamiento de lágrimas, suplicándole me fortaleciese ya de una vez para no ofenderle. ... Paréceme le dije entonces que no me había de levantar de allí hasta que hiciese lo que le suplicaba."
  
  Plain: "One day I went into the oratory and saw an image that had been brought there to be kept ... It showed Christ covered with wounds, so moving that when I looked at it, it shook me to see Him so, because it showed well what He suffered for us. I felt so strongly how badly I had thanked Him for those wounds that my heart seemed to break. I threw myself down beside Him, weeping hard, begging Him to give me strength once and for all not to offend Him ... I think I told Him then that I would not get up from there until He did what I asked."
- What changed: she improved from then on and gave herself more to prayer, "aunque aun no las dejaba del todo" [p.67]. (The outline's next chapter, God beginning to speak to her, is SC-05.)
- Not in the sources: any answer in words; how long she lay there; the hour; the year; whether others saw. She says "creo cierto me aprovechó" (she believes it helped), not that anything happened on the spot.
- Sources: VIDA-Sp ch.7, ch.8 pp.55-56, ch.9 pp.62-67 and n.1; Lewis ch.9 §1-3, §8 and annals (1555).

## SC-05 — The angel with the golden spear, and the doubt that followed
- Status: full
- When and where: the Incarnation. Year not in her text. Lewis annals and the Ribera editor say 1559; Silverio's note says it was before she wrote the passage, "probablemente ... hacia el año 1562" [VIDA-Sp p.234 n.2]. Use "about 1559".
- Who was there: Teresa; she sees an angel "cabe mí hacia el lado izquierdo en forma corporal" [VIDA-Sp ch.29 p.234].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She records that she sees angels in bodily form "por maravilla", not often [p.234].
  2. The angel is small, "hermoso mucho", face aflame. She thinks he is of the highest order and writes "Querubines" [p.234].
  3. He holds a long golden spear with a little fire at the point. He thrusts it into her heart, to her entrails; withdrawing it, he seems to take them with him, leaving her "toda abrasada en amor grande de Dios" [p.234].
  4. The pain made her moan, yet the sweetness was so great she did not want it taken away [p.234].
  5. For days she walked "como embobada" and wanted to see or speak to no one, "sino abrazarme con mi pena" [p.235].
  6. Her raptures grew so strong that she could not resist them among other people, "con harta pena mía, se comenzaron a publicar" [p.235].
  7. She begged God for no more graces with outward signs: "Supliqué mucho a el Señor que no quisiese ya darme más mercedes que tuviesen muestras exteriores" [ch.20 p.147]. She adds that He seems to have heard her, "que nunca más hasta hora lo he tenido; verdad es que ha poco" [ch.20 p.147].
  8. Her confessors' fear: two men she trusted told her the visions were "demonio". She writes: "díjome que a todo su parecer de entramos era demonio" [ch.23, page not checked; Lewis ch.23 §16]. She cried, then read St Paul: God is faithful and never lets those who love Him be deceived by the devil [Lewis ch.23 §17].
  9. Later, Francisco de Borja told her the visions were God's work; she was greatly comforted [Lewis ch.24 §4; paraphrase].
- Details: she says it is "no corporal sino espiritual, aunque no deja de participar el cuerpo" [p.234]. Silverio's note (facts-only): the manuscript says "Cherubines"; Báñez wrote in the margin that it looked more like a seraph; Luis de León printed "Serafines" [p.234 n.1].
- Words spoken: Báñez, sworn 16 Oct 1591, says she begged him: "Por amor de Dios, Padre, que no esté tan sin miedo, que me le hace tomar a mí; de nuevo mire, que no querría engañarle" [Proc-I p.7]. Plain: "For God's love, Father, do not be so free of fear, you make me afraid. Look again; I would not want to deceive you."
- Inner life: "suplico yo a su bondad lo dé a gustar a quien pensare que miento" [p.234]. Plain: I ask His goodness to let anyone who thinks I am lying taste it.
- Testimony (Teresa, VIDA-Sp ch.29 p.234, public-domain; "..." marks my cuts):
  > "vía un ángel cabe mí hacia el lado izquierdo en forma corporal ... no era grande, sino pequeño, hermoso mucho, el rostro tan ecendido que parecía de los ángeles muy subidos, que parecen todos se abrasan. ... Víale en las manos un dardo de oro largo, y al fin de el hierro me parecía tener un poco de fuego. Este me parecía meter por el corazón algunas veces, y que me llegaba a las entrañas. Al sacarle, me parecía las llevaba consigo y me dejaba toda abrasada en amor grande de Dios. Era tan grande el dolor, que me hacía dar aquellos quejidos; y tan ecesiva la suavidad que me pone este grandísimo dolor, que no hay desear que se quite"
  
  Plain: "I saw an angel beside me on my left, in bodily form ... He was not large but small, very beautiful, his face so aflame that he seemed one of the highest angels, who seem all on fire. ... I saw in his hands a long golden spear, and at the iron's tip there seemed to be a little fire. He seemed to thrust it several times through my heart, down to my entrails. When he drew it out, it seemed he took them with him and left me all on fire with great love of God. The pain was so great that it made me moan, and the sweetness of this great pain so excessive that one cannot wish it to end."
- What changed: she is carried into raptures that cannot be hidden, and the suspicion of confessors begins; the order to keep the old strict rule belongs to the next chapter.
- Not in the sources: the room, the date, the hour, who heard her moan (the 17th-century nun María Pinel, quoted by Silverio, says a nun heard groans from the prior's cell; that is a late tradition and her account puts it when Teresa was prioress, 1571-74); any pains in the heart after. Her choir raptures (kneeling before Communion, and a sermon on the feast of her house) happened "después que tengo oficio de Priora" [ch.20 p.147], so after the angel; do not place them in 1559. The outline's "order to scorn a vision" (Butler pp.337-338) was not read in this session.
- Sources: VIDA-Sp ch.20 p.147, ch.23 (confessors), ch.29 pp.234-235 and notes; Proc-I p.7 (Báñez; facts-only, one short quotation); Lewis ch.20 §5-6, ch.23 §16-17, ch.24 §4, ch.29 §16-19, annals.
