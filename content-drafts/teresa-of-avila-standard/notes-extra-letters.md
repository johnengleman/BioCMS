## Timeline additions (letters and last years)
- 1546, 10 Jan, Incarnation, Avila: earliest letter found (discovered 1914, Fita): to Alonso Venigrilla: feed the doves of her dove-cote in the cold months; signed "Teresa de Ahumada" [XL4 Letter CCCCLXIV, Appendix]. NEW. Note: Stanbrook marks date "1546?".
- 1570, 17 Jan, Toledo: to brother Lorenzo (Lima): "I have become such a bargainer and business woman..." [XL1 Letter XXIII p.61-65]. Also: she says she has not felt so well for forty years.
- 1580, 15-19 June, Segovia: to Lorenzo at La Serna, anxious; 19 June tells him not to expect death. Lorenzo died "seven days after" (Stanbrook note, B.Z.): Teresa at recreation turned pale, went to chapel; said his soul passed Purgatory; a few days later at Communion saw St Joseph and Lorenzo [XL4 Letter CCCXXXII p.46 n.]. Note: Stanbrook note is a tradition; Teresa's own letter says death by hemorrhage in under six hours, Communion two days before [XL4 CCCXXXIII, 4 July 1580, p.47-48]. Conflict: letter to nephew says "two days after the feast of St John" [XL4 CCCXLVIII, 27 Dec 1580].
- 1580, 27 Dec, Valladolid: to nephew Lorenzo in Peru: news of father's death; Teresita (Lorenzo's daughter) "bears it like an angel" [XL4 CCCXLVIII p.89-91].
- 1581, 15 Dec, Avila: to nephew Lorenzo in Peru: "It is Sister Teresa de Jesus who cheers my spirits... Though I laughed when I read her letter to you..." [XL4 CCCCXVIII p.268-269].
- 1582, 1 Sept, Valladolid: TWO letters to Gracián at Seville: CCCCLVIII [XL4 p.356-359] (grief at his absence; Lorenzo's will; "Teresa [Teresita] ... how bitterly disappointed she was at your Reverence's not returning!") and CCCCLIX [XL4 p.359-365] (Salamanca nuns' house, caution, Teresita's profession). Signed "To-day is September 1." CORRECTS the earlier line.
- 1582, 2 Sept, Valladolid: to Ana de los Angeles (Toledo): note "not in my handwriting" [XL4 CCCCLX p.365-367].
- 1582, 15 Sept, Valladolid: leaves; Ana de San Bartolomé (autobiography, via B.Z. note) says prioress Mary Baptist plucked her sleeve "Go away, both of you, and never come back" (quote is Stanbrook's translation of Anne of St Bartholomew) [XL4 Letter CCCCLXII p.368, prefatory note]. At Medina Fr Antonio de Jesus waits with the Duchess of Alba's coach; Teresa obeys; leaves Medina next morning fasting; reaches Alba "in a dying condition" (B.Z.) [XL4 p.369].
- 1582, 15-16 Sept, Valladolid and Medina: LAST dated letter (to Catalina de Cristo, Soria): "we are on the point of starting, with so much to do that I feel beside myself" and postscript "We have reached Medina." [XL4 CCCCLXIII p.370-372]. NEW detail: last letter still thinks of going to Avila, Salamanca, Madrid.

Q2. "I do not think I have felt so well for the last forty years" - same letter [XL1 p.62].

## Part 2 (written 2026-10-01, continued)
Sources new: XV1 = Paul VI, Apostolic Letter *Multiformis sapientia Dei*, 27 Sept 1970, vatican.va English https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_letters/documents/hf_p-vi_apl_19700927_multiformis-sapientia.html (also /la/ and /it/ at same filename). Holy See document, quote short.

### Doctor decree (27 Sept 1970) [XV1]
- Intention first stated publicly 15 Oct 1967 [XV1]. 20 Dec 1967: Congregation of Rites ordinary assembly asks if title can go to women. 21 Mar 1968: Paul VI confirms that it can. General of the Discalced Carmelites then was Michelangelo of St Joseph, who asked for the title [XV1]. 15 July 1969: Congregation for the Causes of Saints votes yes (promoter: Cardinal Arcadio Larraona). 21 July 1969: Pope approves. 
- 27 Sept 1970, St Peter's, during Mass, Paul VI says: "WITH TRUE KNOWLEDGE AND THOUGHTFUL DECISION AND FOR THE FULLNESS OF OUR APOSTOLIC AUTHORITY WE DECLARE SAINT TERESA OF JESUS, VIRGIN OF AVILA, DOCTOR OF THE UNIVERSAL CHURCH" [XV1, Vatican English tr.]. Letter is dated 27 Sept 1970, eighth year of pontificate. NEW detail: Vatican text names the process dates.
- Letter says (Vatican English): "a woman of simple customs and ignorant of literary culture, she excelled so much in words and writings that these words can be referred to her: 'in the full assembly she opened her mouth' (Sir 15:5)". It calls her "a Doctor of the Church, first among women, especially for her knowledge and doctrine of divine things." It names Christ "the only source of her doctrine and almost a living book". It says of the Letters that they are "full of humanity". It cites Life 8:5 on prayer as friendship (confirms the earlier fix) and quotes her dying words: "I thank You, Lord, because I die a daughter of the Church".
- Earlier papal testimonies cited in the letter (facts): Gregory XV bull ("Omnipotens sermo"); Benedict XIII in bull for John of the Cross; Pius X; Benedict XV 24 Dec 1921; Pius XI 25 July 1922 "sapientissimam matrem", "altissimam contemplationis magistra"; Pius XII 23 Nov 1951; John XXIII 16 July 1962. Salamanca theologians 1657: "halo of a doctor". Teresa's saintly admirers named: Peter of Alcantara, Francis Borgia, John of the Cross, Juan de Ribera, John of Avila, Francis de Sales, Alphonsus Liguori.
- Letter dates her death 4 Oct 1582 and says she was beatified by Paul V and canonized by Gregory XV (no dates in the letter).

### Letters to Philip II [XL1-XL3]
- 1573, 4 June, Incarnation, Avila: Letter XLVII [XL1 p.119-120]: she writes to the King she prays for him, the Queen and Prince Don Fernando (b. 1571, oath of allegiance end of May 1573; he died 18 Oct 1578 [XL1 note p.119]); asks for support through Licentiate Juan de Padilla. Quote: "I do you some good by inciting the Discalced nuns to pray for you". Signed "Teresa de Jesus, Carmelite". Also: "In these days of trials and persecutions it is a great consolation that God should have provided for the Church such a valiant defender".
- 1575, 19 July, Seville: Letter LXXX [XL1 p.212-213]. She asks him to make the Reform a separate province under Gracián. Quote: "I have lived in the Order for forty years and, considering all things, I am convinced that unless the Discalced are constituted as a separate province without delay, serious injury will result to them". Also: "since God Himself listens to the poor, and your Majesty stands in His place, I do not think that you will be annoyed with me." Also thanks for the Caravaca licence (royal patent 9 June 1575, note). Ends "To-day is July 19."
- 1577, 18 Sept, Avila: Letter CXCV [XL2 p.311-313]: defends Gracian and the nuns against false charges: "I am astounded at the artifices of the devil and the Calced Fathers". Says she would "laugh at their inventions, did I not fear that the devil might harm us by them". Stanbrook note: original not found; Fuente 169. Same note: Juan Gutierrez, new provincial, excommunicated the 55 nuns of the Incarnation who voted for her, and seized John of the Cross on the night of 3 Dec [1577] (Stanbrook note p.311, Acta Cap. Gen.).
- 1577, 4 Dec, Avila: Letter CCI [XL3 p.4-6]: protests the seizure of John of the Cross and Fray Germán. Quote: "I would rather have seen our fathers in the power of the Moors who might be more merciful." Also "he is so weak from all he has suffered that I fear for his life" and "we have no other earthly aid." Also "All Avila is so astonished at the improvement wrought by him that they consider him a saint, as in my opinion he is". NEW as direct evidence (earlier notes have the story, not the letter).
- 1577, between 11 and 18 Dec (Stanbrook's date reasoning, not certain): Teresa has an interview with Philip II at the Escorial. Source: letter to Doña Inés Nieto, "Avila, after December 17, 1577", Letter CCIV [XL3 p.16-17], first published 1919 in the Boletin de la Real Academia de la Historia (Marques de San Juan de Piedras Albas) from the original; first page missing. Quotes (Stanbrook tr.): "I was too confused to speak, for his penetrating gaze ... was fixed on me"; "making this wretched nun, his unworthy servant, the most courteous bow I ever saw, he gave me his hand to kiss"; the King: "you may be at peace, for all shall be done as you wish". CAUTION: authenticity not checked beyond Stanbrook's note; I recall doubts about this letter but have no source read. Mark "to check" before use. Stanbrook also reports an Escorial tradition that she began "Sire, you are thinking: 'I see before me this gad-about woman...'" (quoting Nuncio Sega) [XL3 p.17 n.2], tradition only.
- Dec 24, 1577 (Christmas eve): she breaks her arm (Stanbrook note, same page) [XL3 p.16 n.]. Check against other notes.

### Letters to Gracián and others
- Late 1579 (Malagón), Letter CCCV, to Gracián, fragments [XL3 p.294-295]: "houses founded in poverty should not have an income, for I know, and have learnt by experience, that in all such cases, if the nuns are faithful to God, they will always possess the greatest liberty of spirit. But if they are unfaithful, let them die out, for there are enough relaxed convents already." Also on melancholy: "It would be far better not to make foundations than to receive people suffering from melancholia who ruin the house they enter." Stanbrook footnote (p.296 n.2): "Dios me libre de Santos encapotados" - "God deliver me from sour-faced saints". The footnote is Stanbrook's claim that she often exclaimed this; NOT in a letter text. Classic misquote check: it is a note, tradition.
- Before 28 Nov 1581, Avila, to Canon Pedro de Castro, Letter CCCCVIII [XL4 p.249-250], about a sermon: "they would evidently rather give the partridges than lose the sermon". Humor: nuns paid the preacher in partridges.

### Last journey and deathbed: sworn testimony (Spanish originals, 1591-1596) [XP1 = Silverio ed., Procesos vol. I, archive:procesosdebeatif01silv; XP2 = vol. II, archive:procesosdebeatif02silv]
Rights: testimonies are 1590s public-domain text; the 1930s edition notes are facts only. Page numbers are printed pages found by the OCR; accents were rechecked by eye in the Spanish.
- 1582, 5 Sept, Valladolid: Teresa to Fr Pedro Sancho (confessor at Alba): "God willing, I shall visit Alba soon" [XL4 Letter CCCCLXI p.367-368]. She expects to go to Avila.
- 1582, mid-Sept: At Medina the prelate (Fr Antonio de Jesus, vicar provincial) orders her to Alba for the Duchess. Ana de San Bartolomé, 19 Oct 1595, Avila process: "ninguna cosa en toda su vida se le había mandado más grave que el mandarla fuese a Alba, hallándose como se hallaba, y siendo su camino para Avila, pero que ella había de obedecer como siempre lo había hecho" [XP1 p.169-170, dicho of Ana de San Bartolomé, 44 years old, 24-25 years a nun, five years Teresa's travelling companion]. Own English: nothing in all her life had been ordered of her more heavy than to be sent to Alba, in her state and when her road led to Avila; but she must obey as always. NEW (the "hardest order of her life" is not in popular English).
- Ana at the death: "adonde murió teniéndola en sus brazos esta declarante" [XP1 p.170]. She says Teresa told her two days before she would die of this illness and had not said so earlier to spare her pain [XP1 p.170].
- 1582, 3 Oct 1582, Alba (vigil of St Francis), 5 pm: asks for the Sacrament. Mariana de Jesus (Mariana Gaytan, daughter of Antonio Gaytan, then a young pupil at Alba, deposed 1 Apr 1592): "al entrar por la celda se sentó en la cama con gran ligereza ella sola, habiendo menester antes ayuda de dos personas para rodearla ... con un rostro que parecía más de un hombre muy venerable que de mujer" [XP1 p.83]. Own English: when the host came in she sat up alone with great ease, though before she needed two people to turn her; her face looked more like that of a very venerable man than a woman. Quote: "¡Oh, Señor y Esposo mío; ya es llegada la hora de mí tan deseada; hora es ya, Dios mío, que nos juntemos" [XP1 p.83]. "dando muchas gracias a Dios que le había hecho hija de la Iglesia, y repetíalo muchas veces" [XP1 p.83]. Same words in Juana del Espiritu Santo [XP1 p.101] and in Polanco at Medina [XP2 p.58].
- That night: she repeats the verses "Cor contritum et humiliatum", "Ne projicias me a facie tua", "Cor mundum crea in me, Deus", and asks the nuns to keep the Rule and Constitutions and "no mirasen a su mal ejemplo" [XP1 p.83]. Own English: not to look at her bad example. Polanco (heard from the nuns): she asked pardon of the sisters with great humility [XP2 p.58].
- NEW KEY SCENE, 3 Oct 1582 night (Mariana de Jesus, Mariana de la Encarnacion, Juana del Espiritu Santo agree): Fr Antonio de Jesus asks if she wants her body taken to Avila or buried at Alba. Teresa: "¿y aquí no me darán un poco de tierra?" (Own English: Will they not give me a little earth here?) [XP1 p.83, p.90, p.101]. Ana de San Bartolomé says "Ande, Madre, vámonos a Avila, que es priora de aquella casa"; Teresa lifts her arm, "con una como manera de enfado": "Déjense de eso." Juana del Espiritu Santo says: "bien hace, Madre, que ésta y todas son sus casas, y Cristo en esta vida no tuvo casa propia." Teresa answers: "¡Oh!, ¡qué bien me dice, hija!" [XP1 p.90, dicho of Mariana de la Encarnacion]. Strong, human scene. Conflict: the first part is in three depositions; the Ana/Juana exchange is in one.
- 4 Oct 1582: the next morning at seven she turns on her side "de la manera que pintan a la Magdalena" with a crucifix in her hand [XP2 p.58, Polanco, hearsay]. She dies at 9 pm, "jueves, día de San Francisco, a cuatro de octubre de 1582" [XP2 p.59 Polanco; XP1 p.83 "a las nueve de la noche, el día de San Francisco"]. "entre nueve y diez" [XP2, M. de S. Francisco, eyewitness].
- Ana's vision at the moment of death: she sees Our Lord at the foot of the bed with saints and angels waiting for the soul; she tells God to take her too if He wants but not to leave her alone; "nunca más sintió pena" at Teresa's death [XP1 p.170]. Tradition/vision; mark as witness report.
- Perfume: Ana: a great fragrance fills the cell, "hasta las salserillas donde la habían dado un poco de pisto daban de sí un olor tan extraordinario" (even the small dishes where they gave her a little broth) [XP1 p.170]. Mariana de Jesus: they stayed that night with the body, "no sabe a qué poderlo comparar, porque olía de muchas maneras" [XP1 p.83]. Her hands seemed transparent in church "relucían las manos" [XP1 p.170].
- The odd marvels (Polanco, Medina 1596, hearsay, XP2 p.59): a great star over the church; a crystal-coloured ray by the cell window; a sick nun sees many shining people in white enter the cell; another sees a white dove leave her mouth. Treat as popular reports only. A nun of Alba was healed of continual fever when Teresa's clothes were put on her (Ana; "una parienta de la Duquesa") [XP1 p.172].
- 5 Oct = counted as 15 Oct (see below). Burial next day [Mariana de Jesus: the town council (cabildo), Franciscans and many people came "como a cuerpo santo" and kissed her feet and habit; she is buried between the two grilles of the lower choir, in a coffin without lime, with a wall of stones and lime built over it by a mason and a carpenter at the request of Teresa de Laiz, the foundress [XP1 p.83-84].
- Ana de San Bartolomé's role: before burial she "la amortajó en Alba" [XP1 p.170] (dressed the body). A cloth held to the blood flow from the illness was found with the blood fresh when she took it off [XP1 p.171].

### Exhumations and moves (primary: three nuns of Alba and Ana, 1591-92) [XP1]
- Order of events from Juana del Espiritu Santo (Alba) [XP1 p.101-102]: (1) After about nine months, Gracián (provincial) comes to Alba; the nuns tell him of the perfume from the tomb; he and companions dig up the body, helped by the witness [Mariana de Jesus says she helped; p.84]. Coffin and clothes mouldy and rotten; body "tan fresco y entero, que parecía estaba corriendo la sangre"; only the tip of the nose damaged because a board broke under the weight of stone and lime; breast and belly white and of good colour [XP1 p.84]. Gracián cuts off the left hand (now at the Lisbon convent) before reburying, in a clean sheet, habit and cloak, with boards and a thin partition [XP1 p.84]. A painter painting the grille asks next day what the smell is [XP1 p.84, 102].
- (2) After 2 years 3 months more (Juana): the provincial Gregorio Nacianceno comes to Alba with Gracián for an election; sworn silence under censures; they take out the body, "tan tratable y ligero como si no fuera de carne y hueso"; Juana says she held it "gran rato" in her arms [XP1 p.102]. While the nuns are sent to Matins, they carry the body away; the nuns in choir smell the perfume and leave Matins; the body goes to Avila after the missing arm is cut off to stay at Alba: "le cortó con tanta facilidad como si cortara una pechuga de ave" (cut as easily as the breast of a bird) [XP1 p.102]. The date by counting 9 months + 2 y 3 m from Oct 1582 is about Oct 1585; the usual date is 24-25 Nov 1585 (Lovat, notes-church-miracles). Ana de San Bartolomé says the body arrived in Avila on "víspera y día de santa Catalina" (24-25 Nov) [XP1 p.171], and that Teresa appeared to her to say it would return; the day of the Presentation (21 Nov) it left Alba [XP1 p.171].
- (3) After about 8 months (Juana): by order of the Pope, at the request of the Duke (of Alba) and the Prior of San Juan, the body returns to Alba [XP1 p.102]. Ana de San Bartolomé, about 18 months before 1595, in Madrid at dawn prayer, is told by Teresa in an apparition that letters had gone to Rome for a Breve to return the body to Alba, and Our Lord says it will return "por medio de una mujer" [XP1 p.172]. The "woman" is not named by Ana.
- (4) 1592, March (Mariana de la Encarnacion): Bishop Jerónimo Manrique of Salamanca, the provincial Juan de San Pablo, and Rector Francisco de la Madre de Dios of San Lazaro enter the convent to move the body to a new chest given by María de Toledo, Duchess of Alba, after "casi tres años" without opening; the body is as before [XP1 p.90-91].
- Ana de San Bartolomé on the state of the body in Avila (1585): she touched it, cleaned the earth, took off the clothes: clothing that did not touch the flesh crumbled; what touched the flesh was as on the first day; the belly with the bowels, the head with all the hair; the left arm missing and a finger of the other; sweat "como a modo de aceite o de una agua envuelta en sangre" [XP1 p.171]. It was not opened or embalmed [XP1 p.172]. About a year before 1595 she saw the body again at Alba, "le faltaba mucha carne cortada de una espalda y casi el medio vientre" - relic cutting, and the perfume stronger [XP1 p.172].
- Teresa appeared to Ana in Avila after the move and said: "no puede ser menos sino que me he de ir ahora de con vosotras, pero presto se tornará a traer aquí mi cuerpo" [XP1 p.171-172].
- Conflicts: Ana's own account says the body came to Avila after nine months; the other nuns say a first dig after nine months and a second after more than two years. Use the nuns' order (three events plus 1592). The 1583 and 1585 dates are from Lovat (earlier notes), not from these depositions (relative times only).

### Calendar change and the date of the feast [Butler vol.10 pp.374-377 (S6 local library: butler/vol10.txt, lines ~23280-23700); CE; bull text]
- Gregory XIII's bull *Inter gravissimas*, 24 Feb 1582 (Wikipedia gives date; bull text in English tr. read at bsswebsite.me.uk/A%20Short%20History%20of/InterGravissimas.htm): paraphrase: ten days are removed from October 1582, from the 5th to the 14th inclusive; the day after 4 October (St Francis, "when one traditionally celebrates Saint Francis") shall be the 15th, the feast of Denis, Rusticus and Eleutherius [bull, English tr.]. Rights: PD text, modern tr.: paraphrase only. The bull gives distant lands until October 1583 or later.
- Teresa died at about 9 pm on the night of St Francis, 4 Oct. By the bull the next day was 15 Oct [Butler vol.10 p.374: "(by the reformation of the calendar made that year by cutting off those ten days) being reckoned the fifteenth, the day which was afterward fixed for her festival"]. Also Butler p.374 (footnote on the calendar): the surplus of ten days "happened the very night St. Teresa died"; Butler p.375: she lived "sixty-seven years, six months, and seven days", 47 in religion and 20 under her reformed rule. Catholic Encyclopedia (newadvent.org/cathen/14515b.htm): "the following day, owing to the reform of the calendar, being reckoned as 15 October" ... "the feast being fixed on 15 October". So: she died before midnight on 4 Oct; the next calendar day was the 15th; the date of the feast is not the death date but the day after. The Church places a feast usually on the day of death; she is the exception. Reason in the bull: Butler says (p.374 note) Gregory chose October "on the day after the feast of St. Francis, finding there were fewer feasts from this day to the fifteenth". Do not claim Gregory did it because of Teresa; the dates came first.
- Spain: the Alba nun Leonor de San Gabriel (Sanlucar process, 1596, not an eyewitness) says Teresa died "a las nueve de la noche, que fué en el año que se ganaron los diez días" (in the year they gained the ten days) [XP2, Proceso de Sanlucar, dicho of Leonor de San Gabriel, OCR line ~10178]. Locator by page not found. Checks 4 Oct 1582 as the old-style date; Spain used the new calendar from that October (not separately sourced).

Beatification brief 24 Apr 1614 (Dicastery, earlier notes). So the miracles were examined before 1614. XR does not list them; it shows examination before the Bishops of Salamanca and Avila and Cardinal Archbishop of Toledo "delegati". NOTE: not read in full.
- Not found: Gregory XV bull text with the miracles; the Rota decree for canonization 1621-22; official count of miracles approved for beatification/canonization. The Rótulo gives the miracles argued (below), all from Teresa's relics and body, not from living-time wonders.

#### Post-mortem miracles in the Rótulo [XP3, Rótulo arts. 97-114, printed pp. LXX-LXXVII; own English summaries; status: proposed in the cause, witnesses examined 1609-10]
- Art. 97 (body): buried without embalming; lime and stones laid on the tomb; strong perfume "like lily, violet and other flowers"; the nuns ask leave to open the tomb. Exhumation by the provincial "a los cuatro de Julio del año de mil y quinientos y ochenta y tres, habiendo pasado nueve meses"; earth and stones from the tomb smell; the coffin "rota ... podrida y mohosa", the clothes rotten, the body whole and flexible "como si estuviera vivo", with oil flowing; "lo cual visto por muy doctos médicos y por otras personas de grande autoridad, fué tenido y aprobado como milagro" [XP3 p.LXIX-LXX]. This gives the date: 4 JULY 1583 (the first exhumation). NEW and sourced.
- Art. 98: oil or balm flows from the body and from any fragment of her flesh, soaked into many cloths [p.LXX].
- Art. 99: perfume from relics. Lisbon: Sister Inés de la Madre de Dios, who had no sense of smell from birth, kneels and says "verdaderamente que no me tengo de levantar de aquí si no huelo aquello que mis hermanas huelen" when the hand relic reaches her; she is healed (the hand brought by the provincial). Malagón: a lay sister, who had been corrected by Teresa and thought "dicen que huele, antes hiede" ("they say it smells; it stinks"), falls to the floor when a strong perfume rises from the finger relic; confesses her fault [p.LXX]. Good human detail.
- Art. 100: the woollen cloth (placed for the blood flow before death) found after three years soaked in fresh blood, "como si en aquella misma hora hubiera salido del cuerpo"; doctors judged it a miracle; the girdle (correa) in which she was buried is kept at the Discalced nuns of Zaragoza and drops of blood colour have been seen on it [p.LXXI].
- Art. 101: transfer to Avila: Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza (as Bishop of Avila) had built the main chapel of St Joseph's on the condition that his body and Teresa's lie on each side; the order's first chapter after her death agrees; two prelates take the body, leaving one arm at Alba; the nuns at Matins smell the perfume and leave choir; the body reaches Avila "en el mes de Noviembre de mil y quinientos y ochenta y cinco". The Duke of Alba, Antonio de Toledo, and his uncle Fernando de Toledo, Prior of the Order of St John, sue; they win letters of Sixtus V; the body returns to Alba "a los veintitrés días de Agosto del año de mil y quinientos y ochenta y seis"; it could not be taken out again despite pleas from Avila [p.LXXI-LXXII]. This gives the date: 23 AUG 1586 (return). Also conflict: Butler (p.375-376) says the body was brought back in 1586 (matches).
- Art. 102 (Alba): Licentiate Vallejo, judge of the Duke of Alba's council, has a two-year-old only son given up for dead; the chaplain Pedro de Zamora puts on his head a cloth stained with Teresa's blood; the child revives, clutches the cloth, and will not let anyone take it; the father, in church, hears the child's voice and does not dare look [p.LXXII].
- Art. 103 (Seville): priest Pedro Fernández de Barragán of Valverde carries in his shirt a paper with her words from Ribera's Life ("bendito Dios, que en esta ciudad verdaderamente me conocen, porque en otras no me conocen"); the vicar general Bernardino Rodríguez hands him a loaded arquebus; it fires ten or twelve lead balls at his chest, two fingers from the paper; the balls rebound as if from a stone five or six cubits; he is unhurt; the process drew up an information [p.LXXII-LXXIII]. NOTE: the quoted words are an attribution to Teresa's Seville letter, check.
- Art. 104 (Seville): nun Isabel de San Jerónimo, paralysed on one side, healed when the provincial puts the finger relic on her arm; twelve years without pain [p.LXXIII].
- Art. 105 (Segovia): prioress Isabel de Santo Domingo, consumptive, improved by a little of Teresa's earth; led choir on Christmas Day [p.LXXIII]. (She also testified; her own dicho is in XP2 line 25434.)
- Art. 106 (Zaragoza): nun Leonor de los Angeles, severe ear pain, healed by a cloth with oil from the body. When she refused to swear the miracle, having never sworn an oath, the prioress said "calla, hermana, que la santa Madre tomará venganza de esto"; she gets double tertian fever and, after she promises to swear, recovers [p.LXXIII-LXXIV]. Humor in the record.
- Art. 107 (Salamanca): nun Juana de Jesús, abscess in the throat, cured by a cloth with oil [p.LXXIV].
- Art. 108 (Salamanca): Isabel de Montoya, of Santa María de las Dueñas, blind from cataracts; relic of flesh touched to her eyes on Tuesday 10 Feb 1603 ("a diez de Febrero del año de mil y seiscientos y tres"); she sees the host by Saturday and walks without a guide on 21 Feb; the nuns sing Te Deum [p.LXXIV-LXXV]. NOTE: date 1603 is in the printed text (Rótulo 1609-10, so about six years earlier).
- Art. 109 (Lisbon): licentiate Tomás de Baez de Polanco, ex vicar general of Cordoba, dying, mind clouded and unable to confess; the hand relic on his head clears it; he makes a general confession, then recovers [p.LXXV].
- Art. 110 (Medina del Campo): Antonio de Villarroel, boy with "modorra" (lethargy), healed in a quarter hour by a cloth with the oil laid on his head [p.LXXV].
- Art. 111 (Medina): Luisa de Ordas, 16, fainting fits fifty times a day; healed by Teresa's small scapular; fits return after five years, and a second use cures her [p.LXXV-LXXVI].
- Art. 112 (Medina, 1 Jan 1586, "el día de la Circuncisión"): Juana del Espíritu Santo, novice, 18 months of fever, sciatica and crippled; given a piece of Teresa's girdle (faja) at 21h she feels great pain, then stands and walks downstairs alone [p.LXXVI].
- Art. 113 (Granada): prioress Ana de Jesús, in a plague, with blisters and fever, sleeps with a piece of the habit and wakes healthy [p.LXXVI].
- Art. 114: general: miracles are worked "no solamente en España, pero en otras muchas provincias y reinos" [p.LXXVI].
- Status line for all: proposed in the cause in 1609-10; witnesses examined; the Rota examined miracles by 1614 (XR). Which of these were finally approved by the Congregation is NOT FOUND.
- Earlier-notes check: the Alba tree that bloomed (Lovat via the canonization bull) and the 24 Nov 1585 date: the Rótulo only says "Noviembre 1585".
- Other: Mariana de la Encarnación (Alba, 1592): 'casi tres años' since the chest was last opened; bishop of Salamanca Jerónimo Manrique, with provincial Juan de San Pablo, moved the body to a new chest given by María de Toledo, Duchess of Alba, March 1592 [XP1 p.90-91].

### More letters (Stanbrook) [XL4 unless marked]
- 1580, 7 Aug, Medina del Campo, Letter CCCXXXVI [XL4 p.60-61], to her niece Sister Teresa de Jesus (Teresita, Lorenzo's daughter): "perfection does not consist in feelings but in virtue" (Stanbrook tr.; exact: "Do not be unhappy about it: perfection does not consist in feelings but in virtue. Your devotion will return when you least expect it.") On dryness: "The dryness from which you suffer seems to prove that our Lord considers you a strong soul". For bad thoughts: "sign yourself with the cross, recite a Pater noster, or strike your breast, and try to think of something else". Ends "You know that your Charity must always be the one I love best." "Don Francisco is like an angel" (her nephew Francisco, travelling with her). Dated "To-day is the feast of St. Albert."
- 1581, 6 Jan, Palencia, Letter CCCLXI, to Mary of St Joseph, Seville [XL4 p.114-116]: "We were much amused at what the old ladies say of our Father" (the old women of Seville adored Gracián's sermons); "my head is too tired to do anything more except scold you, for I was amused at the remark you made to Father Nicolas." Also: "I am afraid Seville disagrees with you". Ends "To-day is the feast of the Kings." Money point: she asks Mary to send 200 ducats for her brother's (Lorenzo's) wishes, "(this is between ourselves)" not via Doria; Stanbrook note (p.115 n.2): Mary disobeyed and gave the sum to Fr Nicolas Doria. Humor/character: practical, relaxed, loves details.
- 1581, 19 Nov, Avila, Letter CCCCVII, to Canon Pedro de Castro [XL4 p.247-249]: "the kindness done to me by your letter so overpowered me that before thanking you I said a Te Deum laudamus to our Lord" ... "my ill-doings have benefited your Reverence, and well they may, since you see that I am not in hell as I have so often deserved to be. That is why I named the book 'Of the mercies of God.'" Also: "Let your Reverence avenge His Majesty on me, since He only punishes me by showering on me fresh favours, which is no light penalty for the soul that knows itself." And "I have never been so happy as I am to-night." Stanbrook note p.247: Castro said he had little faith in her revelations ("Speak to me of the humility and other virtues of Mother Teresa de Jesus, but say little of her revelations for I have small faith in them"), she asked him to be her confessor, and in his canonization deposition he said "no book has roused my devotion more, and very few as much". Note is Stanbrook's translation of a deposition; check it in the Procesos. NEW: the doubter who became her confessor.
- 1582, 5 Sept, Valladolid, Letter CCCCLXI, to Fr Pedro Sancho (Alba): see above.
- 1573-1577 letters to Philip II: see above. The three autograph letters to the King that survive: noted in a Spanish search result only (not read); skip.

### The Constitutions [E4 = Obras de Santa Teresa, ed. Silverio, vol. 6 (BMC 1915), archive:obrasdesantatere06tere. Spanish text (Teresa's words PD); intro facts only]
- Primitive Constitutions for St Joseph's: Silverio (vol.6 intro p.XV-XVII): the date is unknown, but they existed in April 1567 when Rubeo, the General, visited Avila; she had shown them to the General and asked him to approve; Fr Angel de Salazar, then Provincial of the Calced Carmelites of Castile, testified in Valladolid in 1595 that he saw and approved "los capítulos y Regla" she presented to the General. She consulted Báñez, Daza, Gonzalo de Aranda, Julián de Ávila, Francisco de Salcedo, Baltasar Álvarez and the bishop Álvaro de Mendoza, who approved (Jerónimo de San José via Silverio, vol.6 p.XVI n.1). Medina's prioress Jerónima de la Encarnación, in the process: "Les dió constituciones que guardasen, y ha oído decir que Nuestro Señor se las comunicó en la oración" (heard from Alberta Bautista) [E4 vol.6 intro p.XV, citing the process article 51].
- Contents (Silverio summary, facts only): two hours of mental prayer a day besides private prayer; perpetual abstinence from meat; fasts from 14 Sept (Exaltation of the Cross) to Easter; poor beds, no curiosity in cells; convents live by alms and manual labour; enclosure; rules on visitors and on entering novices; care of the sick; recreation; discipline [vol.6 intro p.XVI-XVII].
- Text (Teresa's words), printed pp.6-13 of vol.6 (Constituciones, text):
  - Poverty (p.6): "En ninguna manera posean las hermanas cosa en particular ... ni tengan arca, ni arquilla, ni cajón, ni alacena" and the reason: "porque en pocas cosas, puede ir el demonio relajando la perfeción de la pobreza." Own English: the devil may loosen poverty in small things.
  - Beds (p.7): "Las camas sin ningún colchón, sino con jergones de paja: que probado está por personas flacas y no sanas, que se puede pasar." Own English: beds with no mattress, only straw sacks, since weak and unhealthy people have tried it and it can be done. Also "En vestido y en cama jamás haya cosa de color, aunque sea cosa tan poca cómo una faja." (no coloured thing, not even a sash).
  - The sick (pp.11-12): "Las enfermas sean curadas con todo amor y regalo y piedad conforme a nuestra pobreza"; "antes falte lo necesario a las sanas, que algunas piedades a las enfermas" (let the healthy go without necessities before the sick lack some kindnesses); and "tengan lienzo y buenas camas, digo colchones" (sick nuns get linen and good beds, "I mean mattresses"). She breaks her own mattress rule for the sick. Good human touch.
  - Work (p.12): "Tarea no se dé jamás a las hermanas: cada una procure trabajar para que coman las demás." (Never a set task; each works so the others may eat; quotes the Rule: who will not work shall not eat; and St Paul.)
  - Recreation (p.12): "Juego en ninguna manera se permita, que el Señor dará gracia a unas para que den recreación a otras: fundadas en esto, todo es tiempo bien gastado. Procuren no ser enojosas unas a otras, sino que las burlas y palabras sean con discreción." Own English: No games at all; God will give some sisters the grace to entertain others, and on that ground all is time well spent; let teasing and words be discreet. Locator: Constituciones, Obras vol.6, p.12 (printed). NEW for English; shows she allowed fun at recreation.
  - Friendship (p.13): "Ninguna hermana abrace a otra, ni la toque en el rostro, ni en las manos, ni tengan amistades en particular, sino todas se amen en general". (No hugging, no touching faces or hands, no particular friendships; all love in general.) Contrasts with her letters (warm), a fact to use with care.
- Alcalá Constitutions of the Discalced friars: chapter at Alcalá de Henares from 3 March 1581, under Gracián; the nuns' Constitutions revised there (Silverio, vol.6 p.~XXXV-XXXVII, OCR lines ~899-1035; facts only; not read in full). Teresa wrote to Gracián on 21 Feb 1581 about the Alcalá chapter (Silverio cites; not checked).

### Beatification and canonization process [XP1 = Silverio's introduction to Procesos I, p.XVIII-XXVII; facts only]
- Process timeline from Silverio: more than 400 witnesses gave sworn statements, many who knew Teresa; Clement VIII issued the remissorial letters; the Salamanca process ended 22 Sept 1610 and was handed over 3 Dec 1610 [XP1 p.XVIII]; two processes (compulsorial and remissorial "in forma longa") reached the Congregation of Rites in Rome, receipt signed 16 June 1611 by the secretary Giovanni Paolo Mucanzio [XP1 p.XIX]. The Rota auditors' attestation is dated Rome, 2 Jan 1614 [XP1 p.XIX]; the same MS gives the beatification Breve [XP1 p.XIX]. Beatification Breve of Paul V: 24 April 1614 [XP1 p.XXVII]. Then new processes for canonization were made by Cardinal Rojas (Archbishop of Toledo) and the bishops of Avila and Salamanca; examined by the three Rota auditors (Francisco Sacrato, Alonso Manzanedo de Quiñones, J. B. Coccino) and then approved by the Sacred Congregation of Rites; Paul V died 28 Feb 1621 before canonizing; Gregory XV canonized her 12 Mar 1622 with Ignatius of Loyola, Francis Xavier, Isidore the Farmer and Philip Neri [XP1 p.XXVII].
- Rome, 1602: a miracle by a relic of flesh in the new Discalced convent of San Giuseppe a Capo le Case: a niece of Cardinal Baronio, a nun there, was healed. Gracián (Peregrinación de Anastasio, Dialogue XIII): a piece of Teresa's flesh carried into the church "dió tanta suavidad y fragancia de olor" and the Pope "concedió la licencia ... de poner en el altar el retrato de la Santa Madre, que es un género de beatificación" [XP1 p.XXVI, quoting Gracián; B.M.C. 17 p.206].
- Gracián, Valladolid, 26 Apr 1603, to the nuns of Consuegra: he went to Avila to find a relative or friend to press the canonization, and says that without his 200 reales to Rome "no estuvieran presentadas en la Rota las Informaciones sumarias" [XP1 p.XXVI-XXVII]. Gracián, Rome, 1 June 1597, to his sister: finished "Defensa de la doctrina de la madre Teresa de Jesús, contra los que la quieren hacer herética" [XP1 p.XXVI]. Antwerp, 14 March 1610: he wrote a 12-sheet deposition for the cause; Ana de San Bartolomé and Ana de Jesús were to testify there [XP1 p.XXVII]. NEW: Gracián, the friar who had been expelled, is working for her cause.
- Cult before beatification: the Rota decree is 2 Jan 1614; the Rótulo of the Salamanca process 1609-10 (see above).
- Not found: the text of the 24 April 1614 Breve; the Rota's judgment on specific miracles; the canonization bull; the names of approved miracles; Bollandist Acta Sanctorum Oct. VII (not read).

## Scenes (new, dated, for the writer; at most 10)
1. 4 June 1573, Avila: Teresa, prioress of the Incarnation, writes to Philip II that she prays for him and that his oath-taking day for Prince Fernando had special prayers; signs "Teresa de Jesus, Carmelite" [XL1 Letter XLVII].
2. 19 July 1575, Seville: in deep grief about the Order, she writes the King for a separate province under Gracián and ends "To-day is July 19" [XL1 LXXX].
3. 3 Dec to 4 Dec 1577, Avila: John of the Cross seized at night by Calced friars; next day Teresa writes the King "I would rather have seen our fathers in the power of the Moors who might be more merciful" [XL3 CCI]. (Interview with the King at the Escorial in Dec 1577 from the Nieto letter: authenticity unchecked; do not use as fact.)
4. 7 Aug 1580, Medina del Campo: she tells her niece that dryness means a strong soul, and "perfection does not consist in feelings but in virtue" [XL4 CCCXXXVI].
5. 19 Nov 1581, Avila: to Pedro de Castro, the doubter who read her Life: "I said a Te Deum laudamus ... I have never been so happy as I am to-night." [XL4 CCCCVII].
6. 1 Sept 1582, Valladolid: last letters to Gracián, who is in Seville; she signs "To-day is September 1", asks to greet "Fray John of the Cross" and says she will not drag Teresita about from place to place any longer [XL4 CCCCLIX p.365].
7. 15-16 Sept 1582, Valladolid to Medina: farewell to the nuns ("Desire great things") and the last letter with "we are on the point of starting" (see above) [XL4 CCCCLXII-CCCCLXIII].
8. Mid-Sept 1582, Medina: Fr Antonio de Jesus gives the order to go to Alba; Teresa, near the end, "ninguna cosa en toda su vida se le había mandado más grave" [XP1 p.169-170, Ana de San Bartolomé 1595].
9. 3 Oct 1582, Alba, 5 pm to night: she sits up alone when the Host enters; "¿y aquí no me darán un poco de tierra?"; "Déjense de eso"; Juana del Espíritu Santo: "Cristo en esta vida no tuvo casa propia" [XP1 p.83, 90, 101].
10. 4 July 1583, Alba: Gracián opens the sealed tomb after nine months; the earth and stones smell; the body is whole; he takes a hand to Lisbon [XP3 Rótulo art. 97; XP1 p.84, 101]. Then Nov 1585 the body is taken to Avila at night while the nuns are at Matins; 23 Aug 1586 returns to Alba [XP3 Rótulo art. 101].

## Conflicts and doubts
- 15 Oct: Butler says the new calendar caused the date to be the day "afterward fixed for her festival"; CE says "the feast being fixed on 15 October" (not 4 Oct). The Bull says the day after 4 Oct was 15 Oct. No source read says why the Church did not keep 4 Oct (St Francis).
- Canonization year: Butler vol.10 p.378 gives 1621 ("canonized by Gregory XV. in 1621"); Silverio, the Dicastery and CE give 12 March 1622. Use 1622 (ceremony) and note the 1621 date is Butler's error or the pope's decree year; do not use 1621.
- Exhumation order: Ana says the body came to Avila after nine months; the Rótulo and the nuns put 4 July 1583 (first opening, body reburied) and Nov 1585 (move). Use Rótulo dates.
- Lorenzo's death (June-July 1580): Stanbrook note says Teresa saw his soul in a vision "seven days after" the news; the 27 Dec 1580 letter says death was "two days after the feast of St John". Writer: use the Dec letter, and keep the vision as tradition.
- Letter to Doña Inés Nieto about the Escorial interview with Philip II: published 1919 from the original; Stanbrook date reasoning only; I could not find later scholarship either way. Do not state the interview as fact without a second source.
- Silverio gives the Rota attestation date "2 de enero de 1614"; the Rota decree itself prints "M.DC.XIII" in the OCR (Roman year, the new year started on 25 March in some Roman styles). Use 2 Jan 1614 (Silverio).
