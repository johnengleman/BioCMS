This list gathers the Church’s memory of the wonders God worked through Saint Teresa of Ávila, and her own accounts of the graces He gave her in prayer. Most come from her books and from the sworn testimony taken for her beatification and canonization. Many of the witnesses knew her. Others tell what they saw or heard after her death. Still others come from the first Life of Saint Teresa by Francisco de Ribera, from the articles for her canonization hearings, and from later Lives. Each account names the person who told it.

How the Church examined her miracles

More than 400 witnesses gave sworn statements in the processes for Saint Teresa’s cause. A process is an official Church inquiry. In 1609 and 1610 hearings at Salamanca, Alba, Ávila, Madrid, and other cities examined witnesses on a list of articles. Eighteen of these articles are about her body, her relics, and healings after her death. They are numbers 97 to 114. Four of them are told below under “After Saint Teresa’s death”. The other fourteen are told under “Put forward for her canonization”.

The Roman Rota is a Church court. Its auditors reported to the pope on her miracles in a decree dated 2 January 1614. Pope Paul V beatified Saint Teresa on 24 April 1614. Beatification is the Church’s declaration that a person is blessed. Pope Gregory XV canonized her on 12 March 1622. Canonization is the declaration that a person is a saint.

During Saint Teresa’s life

Four days as if dead, Ávila

Ribera wrote that harsh cures made Saint Teresa worse, so her father took her home to Ávila, and the doctors gave her up. On the night of the August feast of Our Lady she fell into a fit and lay unconscious for almost four days. They gave her the Anointing of the Sick and said the Creed over her. Saint Teresa wrote that wax was found on her eyes and that a grave stood open at her convent for a day and a half. Ribera wrote that her father felt her pulse and said, “This daughter is not for burying.” Then the Lord was pleased to bring her back to herself, she wrote. She asked at once to confess, and she received Holy Communion with many tears.

David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Paralysis ends through Saint Joseph, Ávila, about 1542

Ribera wrote that Saint Teresa was crippled for eight months and that the illness lasted three years. Saint Teresa wrote that she could move only one finger of her right hand and that she praised God when she began to crawl. The doctors of the earth had failed her, so she turned to the doctors of heaven. She took Saint Joseph as her advocate and lord and asked him for her health. He gave it back, and she could rise and walk. She wrote that in heaven Jesus grants Saint Joseph whatever he asks. Her first convent bore his name.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 111–112, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Butler, Lives of the Saints, vol. 10, p. 317; Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, chs. 5–6 and annals, tr. Saint Teresa is lifted in choir

Saint Teresa wrote that in a rapture her soul was carried away, and now and then her whole body was lifted up from the ground. After she was made prioress it once happened in choir as she knelt to receive Communion, and she ordered the nuns to tell no one. Another time she threw herself on the ground at a sermon on the feast of her house, and the nuns held her, but the rapture was seen. She begged God to give her no more favours that others could see. She wrote: “I have not had one since, though it is true that it has been only a short while.”

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 20, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Saint Teresa clutches the floor mats

María de Jesús swore in 1595 that she had heard this from Ana de los Ángeles. Ana de los Ángeles had seen Saint Teresa lifted from the floor in prayer at Ávila. Saint Teresa held on to the floor mats to keep herself down, so that no one would see her raised.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de Jesús, 18 July 1595, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Saint Teresa enraptured in the kitchen

María de San José swore in 1595 that María Bautista had told her about Saint Teresa at the convent in Valladolid. María Bautista had seen Saint Teresa lifted from the floor in prayer several times. Other nuns of the house had seen her so deep in prayer that she was sometimes caught up in rapture in the kitchen with her pan in her hand. Saint Teresa worked to hide such things, and she told the nuns to hold her robe firmly so that she would not rise from the floor. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 tells of the day a sister found Saint Teresa with her feet above the floor, still gripping a pan of frying fish.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, 27 May 1595, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), pp. 247–248, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Saint Teresa restores her nephew, Ávila, 1561

The annals in Lewis’s translation of her Life record that Saint Teresa restored a nephew to life in 1561. A note there says the boy was the child of her sister and Juan de Ovalle, and that falling stones killed him while workmen built her first convent. Saint Teresa laid the body in her lap and covered it with her veil. She prayed, and the boy came back to life. María del Nacimiento swore on 27 May 1595 to what she had heard from a priest. In the priest’s account the boy fell from a gallery. Saint Teresa walked with him in her arms for half an hour and prayed. María de San José swore the same day to what Saint Teresa’s sister Juana de Ahumada had written in a memorial. In that memorial the boy was three years old. He fell from a height and lay senseless. Saint Teresa held him against her face under her veil, and he came to himself. She gave him back to his mother with the words “Take your son.” The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Teresa’s friend Guiomar de Ulloa said, “This child is dead, but with God nothing is impossible. Pray for him.” Saint Teresa took the boy in her arms and prayed. In a short time she gave him back to his mother alive and well with the words “Here is your son.” Within half an hour he was playing with other children. When Guiomar asked what had happened, Saint Teresa kept silent. She let Guiomar’s words stand, which she did not usually do. Years later the boy told Saint Teresa that she was bound to win him salvation, because she had called him back when he was sure to have it. Dr. Diego de Polanco swore at Medina del Campo in 1596 that the boy was Don Gonzalo de Ovalle. He said Saint Teresa held him on her knees and breathed on him. Then she gave him back alive and well.

A dying child is spared, Salamanca, 1571

In February or March 1571 Saint Teresa lodged at Salamanca with the Count and Countess of Monterrey. Their little daughter was dying, and they begged Saint Teresa to pray. She went to her room and prayed for the child. The chronicle of the Discalced Carmelites says that Saint Catherine of Siena and Saint Dominic appeared to her. They told her the child’s life was granted to her prayers, and that the child must wear the Dominican habit for a year. Saint Teresa told Báñez, and he told the parents. The girl wore the habit for a year. Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that Saint Teresa asked Báñez to arrange this in secret, so that nothing would be known of her part. But Báñez wanted to comfort the child’s mother and grandmother, the two countesses, and he told it in a way that let everyone learn of it.

The chronicle also names a second cure in that house, the cure of María de Arteaga. Blessed Anne swore about her too. María de Arteaga was the wife of the tutor of the Count’s children, and she was near death with typhus. The two countesses asked Saint Teresa to see her. Saint Teresa laid a hand on her face. The sick woman cried out, “Who touched me? I feel well!” Saint Teresa begged her to be quiet and not to show so soon that she was relieved. Everyone present had heard, and they began to thank Saint Teresa. Saint Teresa went back to the convent distressed. The woman told Anne that she had never felt so well in body and soul as when Saint Teresa laid a hand on her face. Ana de la Encarnación swore at the Salamanca inquiry of 1591–92 that Saint Teresa touched or embraced María out of pity. María said at once that she was better.

The rain stops at Salamanca, September 1573

The Salamanca nuns moved into their new house on 28 September 1573, the eve of Saint Michael, and heavy rain came through the chapel roof. Saint Teresa wrote that she almost complained to the Lord and said that He must either stop sending her to found houses or meet this need. She wrote that on Saint Michael’s day the sun began to shine as the people came. Blessed Anne of Jesus swore that the sky cleared that same night, after Saint Teresa told her to ask God herself. María del Nacimiento swore in 1595 that she had heard Blessed Anne of Jesus tell of a night like this. In that account Saint Teresa smiled when the nun asked her to pray. María said the rain soon stopped and that all present thought it a miracle. She did not remember which house it was.

Saint Teresa prays for nuns in danger, 1574

The chronicle of the Order tells of the nuns who left Pastrana at midnight and reached Segovia in Holy Week 1574. On the road they were once in danger of death. At that moment Saint Teresa was in Segovia, and she said to her nuns: “Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana.” The travellers arrived in Segovia.

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 17, notes 19–20, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

Silk hangings escape fire, Seville, 1576

On 3 June 1576 the archbishop of Seville carried the Most Holy Sacrament in procession into the new convent. Saint Teresa wrote what those who saw it told her. After many salvoes of artillery and rockets that evening, some powder caught fire. A huge flame rushed to the top of the cloisters, which were hung with silk of purple and gold. She wrote that the silk was not damaged at all, though the stonework under it was black with smoke. She added that the man who held the powder was not killed. Leonor de San Gabriel and Isabel de San Francisco were in the house and swore in 1596 to what happened. A man who had fired many rockets carried powder, and it all caught fire. He and those near him were in great danger. They told Saint Teresa, and she prayed. The people ran and found the man unhurt and the silk hangings unharmed. A mark of fire and powder stayed on an arch beneath them for more than ten years. The nuns believed it was a great miracle done through Saint Teresa’s prayer.

A blank page fills with writing, Toledo, 1577

Saint Teresa began The Interior Castle at Toledo in 1577. María del Nacimiento was a nun of the Toledo house. She swore in 1595 that she often saw Saint Teresa write it right after Holy Communion. Saint Teresa’s face was beautiful, and she wrote so fast that noise did not disturb her. Mother Mariana of the Angels reported that María once brought a message to the cell as Saint Teresa began a new sheet. Saint Teresa fell into a trance that lasted several hours, and the nun kept still and watched. The blank page was covered with writing when the trance ended, and Saint Teresa quietly put it in her box.

Lightning strikes an archbishop’s house, Granada, 1581–82

Diego de Yepes wrote that the archbishop of Granada had long refused to let the Discalced nuns found a house in his city. On the first night after the nuns left Veas, they stopped at a place called Daifuentes. Blessed Anne of Jesus and Saint John of the Cross were talking there about how to win the archbishop. Yepes wrote that the nuns heard a terrible thunder that night. They learned later that lightning had struck the archbishop’s house near where he slept, burned part of his library, and killed some animals in his stable. The fright made the archbishop ill, and he soon gave the permission. Yepes told it as the Lord’s doing.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 321 and note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Spilled medicine smells sweet, Alba, 1582

María de San Francisco was a nun at Alba, and she swore that Saint Teresa lay ill in bed there when the doctors ordered a medicine made of oils. The oils all had a very bad smell, and the medicine spilled over the bed. The Duchess of Alba came to visit at that moment, and Saint Teresa feared the smell would offend her. María said the bed smelled as if it had been sprinkled with angel water, and the Duchess said the same. María thought it was a miracle that bad-smelling oils gave off such a fragrance.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 334 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Saint Teresa sits up alone, 3 October 1582

Mariana de Jesús lived in the convent at Alba, and she swore in 1592 about what happened on 3 October 1582. Before that day Saint Teresa needed two people to turn her in bed. When the priest brought the Blessed Sacrament into her cell, she sat up in bed alone and with great ease. She said, “Oh my Lord and my Spouse, the hour I have longed for has come. It is time now for us to be together, my God.” Mariana said that Saint Teresa’s face looked more like the face of a very venerable man than of a woman. Ribera wrote that her face became very beautiful and glowing, and much younger than her years.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 83, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 335, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

A headache ends at Saint Teresa’s bedside, 1582

María de San Francisco was a nun of Alba. She testified that Isabel de la Cruz came to Saint Teresa on 4 October 1582, the day Saint Teresa died. Isabel had a bad headache and pain in her eyes. She took Saint Teresa’s hands and put them on her own head. At once she was free of all pain. María ended her account with the words “I saw all this.” Isabel de la Cruz swore at Alba in 1592 that her head always hurt badly. When Saint Teresa was near death, Isabel took her hands with great faith and laid them on her own head. She never felt that pain again. Her eyes could not bear the light. She put fingers to them with great faith, and they were at once well. The articles of 1609–10 tell the same case and say she laid Saint Teresa’s hands on her head and her eyes.

Saint Teresa’s face glows like a full moon

María de San Francisco testified about the day Saint Teresa died, 4 October 1582. From seven in the morning Saint Teresa lay on her side with her face turned toward the nuns. The witness said that her face was very beautiful and glowing, that it looked like a full moon, and that she did not know where the wrinkles had gone. She said Saint Teresa at times gave off a very strong fragrance. Saint Teresa gave three soft sighs and died, and the witness said her face then shone like a burning sun.

A sick prioress rises well, Medina del Campo

Ana de la Trinidad was a nun of Salamanca. She swore on 3 January 1592 that she had heard of a prioress at Medina del Campo. The prioress had a high fever and a pain in her side, and her life was in danger. Saint Teresa came to the house and went at once to visit her. She embraced her, and the pain left. The next day the prioress rose well. Dr. Diego de Polanco knew Saint Teresa. He told the same story about Alberta Bautista at the hearing at Medina on 9 April 1596. Polanco said that Saint Teresa touched her side and told her she hoped in God that it would be nothing. By his account the nun rose the next day as if she had never been ill.

A toothache goes at Saint Teresa’s blessing

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 that she travelled with Saint Teresa and was badly troubled by toothache. She said that Saint Teresa laid a hand on her face and blessed her, and the pain went away. The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Teresa often freed Ana de San Bartolomé and others from toothache with the sign of the cross.

A fever ends at midnight, Valladolid

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 about an evening at Valladolid, when a trip to Salamanca had been planned. A great fever with much vomiting sent her to bed. She thought she would not be able to serve and accompany Saint Teresa on the trip. At midnight Saint Teresa came to her cell and asked, “Daughter, are you sleeping?” Blessed Ana answered, “I was sleeping, Mother.” Saint Teresa said, “Get up, just as you feel.” She got up and felt well and free of fever. Saint Teresa said, “Blessed be the Lord, for I have been asking Him to give you health.” They set out in the morning, and Blessed Ana had no more of that illness. Diego de Yepes swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 that Blessed Ana told him the same. She had been a month in bed with a continual fever. Saint Teresa told her they must set out the next day, and Blessed Ana excused herself because of her illness. Saint Teresa answered that the road does not excuse itself. At midnight Blessed Ana found herself without fever and with strength to walk.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, 19 Oct. 1595, p. 172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Diego de Yepes, Madrid, p. 286, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A wounded man stops crying out, Burgos

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 about the hard foundation at Burgos. Saint Teresa and her nuns lodged in the Hospital of the Conception, in an upper room above the infirmary. One of the sick below had many sores, and his cries from the pain of the dressings were heard upstairs. One day Saint Teresa went down to visit the sick, as she did when she had strength. She told him, “Brother, commend yourself to the Lord, bear your illness with patience, and do not cry out so loud.” He answered that the pain was too great, and she gave him her blessing. He said afterward that his sores no longer hurt as before, and no one heard his cries again when they dressed them. In time he was healed of his illness. Juan Carrillo had been secretary to Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza of Ávila. He swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 that Saint Teresa’s niece, Teresa de Jesús, told him the same. While the archbishop refused leave to found, the nuns heard Mass through a grille from an empty room of the hospital. The poor man’s cries moved everyone who passed. Saint Teresa went down and asked what he felt, and his cries eased. She comforted him, and from then on no one heard him complain. He said that Saint Teresa had taken away his pains.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, 19 Oct. 1595, pp. 172–173, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Juan Carrillo, Madrid, p. 390, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A brawl at an inn stops, 1575

Saint Teresa’s companions told this at three hearings. María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that the nuns on the road to Seville stopped at a crowded inn. They camped in the open field and said their usual prayers. More than a hundred common people at the inn began a brawl with lances, crossbows, and swords. The frightened nuns ran to Saint Teresa, who was at prayer. She told them it would be nothing, only the devil’s riot. The fight stopped at once. No one was killed or wounded.

Leonor de San Gabriel and Isabel de San Francisco swore in 1596 that this happened near the inn of Andino. Soldiers and mule drivers were slashing at each other, and no one could separate them. Saint Teresa looked out of her cart and spoke one word. The men calmed down and went off across the field, and the nuns believed it was a miracle.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, Lisbon, p. 502, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Leonor de San Gabriel, Sanlúcar, p. 181, and of Isabel de San Francisco, p. 185, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

The ferry comes to Saint Teresa’s bank, Guadalquivir, 1575

Leonor de San Gabriel and Isabel de San Francisco swore in 1596 that they crossed the Guadalquivir with Saint Teresa on the way to found the house at Seville. Saint Teresa and the nuns crossed first by boat. The carts and baggage followed on a ferry. Its rope broke, and the ferry drifted down the river as if lost. Saint Teresa stood on the bank and prayed. Isabel de San Francisco saw a boy of six on the ferry. He worked like a giant, and the ferry came to land at the bank where Saint Teresa stood. Those on board believed it was a miracle. They said they were born that day, as people say who have escaped death.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Leonor de San Gabriel, p. 181, and of Isabel de San Francisco, p. 185, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

A voice warns the nuns, Sierra Morena

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that she was one of eight nuns who travelled with Saint Teresa to found the house at Beas. On the last day in the Sierra Morena the carters lost the road among high crags. Saint Teresa told the nuns to ask God and Saint Joseph to guide them. At that moment a voice like an old man’s called from a deep ravine, “Stop, stop, you are lost, and you will fall if you go further.” The voice told the party to turn to one side, and they all saw that the carts had been able to cross there by a miracle. Some of the party went to look for the man. Saint Teresa said with tears that it was her father Saint Joseph, and that they would not find him. They came back without finding any trace of him. From then on the mules went so lightly that the carters swore the mules flew, and the party crossed the Guadalimar without leaving the carts. The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Joseph appeared and showed the right road, and that the nuns reached Beas on the feast of Saint Matthias.

A light shows the way on a dark night

Quiteria Dávila swore at Ávila in 1595 that she travelled with Saint Teresa from Ávila to Salamanca. The night was harsh and dark, and the road came to a stream they had to cross. Quiteria was afraid, and Saint Teresa told her not to be. Then Quiteria saw a bright light ahead of them, as if someone were carrying a torch behind them. It lit the way until they came into a village. Quiteria asked what had become of the light. Saint Teresa answered, “Ask God.”

In 1610 four nuns of the Encarnación at Ávila swore that Quiteria had told them this many times in the convent. In their telling the light shone on the far side of the river and lit the crossing, and then it vanished. Two of them gave Saint Teresa’s answer as “Go on, daughter, give thanks to God and ask no more.” The other two said she told Quiteria to ask the Lord. Lázaro Suárez was a priest of the parish of San Vicente in Ávila. He swore that Quiteria also told him of a coach on a dark night with a ravine ahead. A light like lightning showed the danger, and the travellers found another way.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Quiteria Dávila, p. 237, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimonies of Mencía Roberto, p. 368, María Coronel, p. 381, Antonia de Guzmán, p. 393, and María del Castillo, p. 408; Lázaro Suárez, p. 321, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

Saint Teresa learns of a canon’s death

Quiteria Dávila swore in 1595 that she was helping Saint Teresa to say Matins one night at Salamanca. Matins is the night prayer of the nuns. Saint Teresa stopped with her hand over her eyes and stayed still for a while. When she came back to herself, she told Quiteria that Don Francisco de Guzmán had died at Ávila. He was a canon of great virtue, and she was glad that his soul was at rest. She was sorry for his sister, a nun at the Encarnación. Three or four days later Father Ripalda came from Ávila and said that Don Francisco had died on that day and at that hour. Quiteria saw that it was the hour when Saint Teresa had spoken.

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that she was with Saint Teresa at Salamanca at the hour of the afternoon rest. Saint Teresa’s face was flushed. She sent Anne to call Father Baltasar Álvarez, then her confessor. She told him, “Our good friend Don Francisco de Guzmán is in heaven. He has just died, and he appeared to me in a great light.” Father Álvarez noted the day and hour, and he found that Don Francisco had died at that time.

A dying nun is visited from Segovia

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that Isabel de los Ángeles lay ill for eight months at Salamanca. She suffered great pain and deep fear for her soul. This was more than twenty years earlier, while Saint Teresa was founding the house at Segovia. On the feast of Saint Barnabas the nuns left her tired and went to Mass. They came back and found her full of joy. Anne had been her teacher, and Isabel told Anne that Saint Teresa had come to her during Mass. Saint Teresa blessed Isabel and laid her hands on Isabel’s face. Saint Teresa told her not to be afraid, because great glory waited for her that day. That night the nuns put a crucifix and a candle in her hand and said the Creed with her. She died as she said the last word.

Anne swore that Isabel’s body then took on a beauty and a brightness that every nun saw. Many outsiders came to the burial, and the Count of Fuentes and the commander Páez had to guard the bier. The nuns wrote to Segovia the same day. The prioress and subprioress there told Saint Teresa. They said that after Holy Communion that morning she had lain as if dead, at the same hour, and had not answered them when they came twice with a message. Saint Teresa laughed and said, “Go away, what strange things they invent.” Saint Teresa had also written to the Salamanca prioress about two things she could not have known without seeing the house. Beatriz de Jesús swore that the nun’s companion found her joyful the same way. María de San José swore that Saint Teresa told the sick nun, “Do not be sad, my daughter, for tonight you will see God.”

Toothache and earache end at Saint Teresa’s touch, Toledo

Ana de la Trinidad swore at Salamanca on 3 January 1592 about a woman of the household of Doña Luisa de la Cerda at Toledo. For a long time the woman had suffered a toothache and an earache that nothing helped. She knew Saint Teresa’s holiness and asked her to make the sign of the cross over the pain. Saint Teresa pushed her away in a playful, scornful way. She said, “Get away with you, make it yourself. The holy cross has its power from itself and not from my hand.” As she pushed her away she touched the aching place. The woman was healed at once, and those who knew her never saw her with that pain again. Dr. Diego de Polanco swore the same story at Medina del Campo in 1596.

A skin infection heals, Medina del Campo

The articles of 1609–10 say that a nun of the Medina del Campo house had a fever and an inflamed erysipelas. Erysipelas is an infection that makes the skin red, hot, and swollen. The doctors feared it would turn to cancer. Saint Teresa passed her hand over the nun’s face and said, “Trust, daughter, God will cure you.” From that hour the nun was well of the erysipelas and of all her illness, and nothing like it came back in the twenty years she lived afterward. The article names the nun Juana del Espíritu Santo. The witnesses name her Ana de la Trinidad.

Mariana de Jesús swore at Alba in 1592 that Ana de la Trinidad had told her the story. Ana was very ill with a dangerous erysipelas. Saint Teresa was moved to pity and passed her hands over Ana’s face and head. She said, “Hush, my daughter, this will be nothing,” and added other words of comfort. From that moment Ana was much better, and the illness never came back. Dr. Diego de Polanco swore at Medina del Campo in 1596 that the nun had a bad swelling of the nose from before she entered the convent. Three doctors thought two cauteries in her arms were needed to stop a cancer. A cautery is a burn made on purpose with a hot iron to treat a sore. Saint Teresa passed her hand over Ana’s face and said she hoped in God to heal her. The fever and the sore left at once and had not come back in fifteen or sixteen years. María de San Francisco told it the same way.

An abscess opens at Saint Teresa’s touch, Seville

María de San José was prioress at Seville in 1575, and she swore at Lisbon in 1595 to this account. A novice there had an abscess in her eye. The doctor had decided to open it the next day, and the novice was afraid that night. Saint Teresa went to her and said, “Do not worry, my daughter. I trust in Our Lord that your abscess will not need to be opened.” She touched it with her hand, and it opened. Nothing was needed the next day, and the novice soon recovered. The nuns, the novice, and the doctor all believed it was a miracle, and the doctor said that the abscess was not ready to open without an instrument.

The articles of 1609–10 tell the same case. They say the doctors came on the day set for the cautery and found the swelling already open. They add that the same nun’s pain in the stomach and the heart ended at once at Saint Teresa’s touch.

Saint Teresa reads a friar’s hidden temptation, Pastrana

The articles of 1609–10 tell of Fray Agustín de los Reyes. He was a novice at the Discalced friars’ house at Pastrana, and he later became provincial, the head of the friars of a province. He had a great temptation that slowly wore him down, and he dared tell no one, not even his master of novices. When Saint Teresa came to the house she saw him and knew what he suffered. She called him aside and asked what was wrong. He answered three times that nothing was. Then she said, “Son, why do you hide your temptation from me? Is this not your temptation and your affliction?” She told him all that he had hidden. She said he had no reason to fear, and she gave him a way to overcome it and other words of comfort. From that hour he was free of it.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 82, p. LXI, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé learns to write

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé swore at Ávila in 1595 about a day at Salamanca. Saint Teresa had many letters to answer and was tired. She said to Ana, “If you knew how to write, you would help me with these letters.” Ana asked for something to learn from. Saint Teresa gave her a letter in good handwriting from a Discalced nun. Ana said she thought she would learn better from Saint Teresa’s own writing, so Saint Teresa wrote two lines and gave them to her. That afternoon Ana wrote a letter to the sisters of Saint Joseph at Ávila, in imitation of those lines. From that day she wrote and helped to answer the letters Saint Teresa received. She had never had a teacher, and before this she could read only a little Spanish. She believed that Our Lord did this to help Saint Teresa in her work. Catalina de la Concepción swore at Zaragoza in 1595 that she heard the story from Ana at Segovia. She told it a little differently. Saint Teresa gave Ana a letter in her own hand to learn from. Ana copied the writing so well that many nuns thought it was Saint Teresa’s.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, Ávila, p. 173, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Catalina de la Concepción, p. 126, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

Christ meets Blessed Ana on a stair

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé swore at Ávila in 1595 that Saint Teresa had left her at Seville with high fevers. She stayed ill for many months. When Saint Teresa came back to Ávila, she found Ana weak after a long illness. She made Ana infirmarian, the nun in charge of the sick. Five nuns lay in bed with fevers, and Ana was to serve them. One of them had such a loathing for food that she took nothing but drink for days. Ana thought she could not move. Trusting that Saint Teresa had commanded it, she obeyed. As she began to climb the stair she grieved over her weakness. She saw Christ Our Lord at the top of the stair, as painters show Him in His life on earth. He said to her, “Go up.” She found herself at His feet without feeling that she had climbed. She went with Him to the cell of the sick nun. He stood at the head of the bed and said, “Put down what you bring. I will feed this sick woman, and you go and feed the others.” Ana went with a great lightness and was fully well from then on. The sick nun lost her loathing for food from that day. Ana believed it was a miracle through Saint Teresa’s prayer, because Saint Teresa had given her this work.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, Ávila, pp. 173–174, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Saint Teresa’s face shines, in life

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé swore at Ávila in 1595 that on two occasions she saw something wonderful in Saint Teresa. At Valladolid Ana found Saint Teresa seemingly asleep. Ana sat beside her without waking her and noticed a fragrance as of many relics. When Saint Teresa woke she said gladly, “Were you here, daughter?” At Ávila Ana saw Saint Teresa’s face shine with a great radiance in the chapter room. It lit the whole room and made it hard for Ana to see. She did not know whether the other sisters saw it, but they came out full of fervor and comfort.

Diego de Yepes swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 that Ana told him more. Saint Teresa sometimes worked at her letters until two in the morning and asked to be woken two hours later. Ana found her face aglow and so beautiful that she was amazed. It slowly turned back to its tired color as Saint Teresa woke. Yepes also swore that he heard her confession at Osma. When she came to receive Holy Communion, her face was earth-colored from age and illness. As she received Our Lord in her mouth her face took on a most beautiful color and seemed transparent. She stayed with such gravity and majesty that he felt great reverence. The articles of 1609–10 say that her face shone at Communion, in rapture, and sometimes as she wrote. At Matins in Valladolid she held a candle, and the light from her face was brighter than the candle’s.

A dying confessor lives after her prayer

Beatriz de Jesús swore at Ávila on 22 October 1595 about Father Baltasar Álvarez. He was a Jesuit and had once been Saint Teresa’s confessor. He fell so ill that the doctors gave him no more than two hours to live. Saint Teresa loved him greatly and felt his loss for herself and for the many souls he served. She went to the choir to pray for him. When she came out she met Beatriz, and she saw Beatriz glance at her calm face. Saint Teresa said, “I trust Our Lord to do what a great friend does for another, and I hope for the sick man’s health.” Father Álvarez did not die.

Saint Teresa’s tongue is freed at Communion

Teresa de Jesús was Saint Teresa’s niece. She swore at Ávila in 1595 about a trouble she saw in her aunt. Saint Teresa suffered from a palsy that sometimes locked her tongue. Teresa de Jesús saw that as soon as Saint Teresa received Holy Communion, her tongue was freed, and she spoke.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Teresa de Jesús, Ávila, p. 198, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A novice stays after Saint Teresa’s blessing

María del Castillo swore at Ávila in 1610 that she was a novice at the Encarnación when Saint Teresa became prioress there. She was set on leaving the habit, and she went to ask the provincial to take it from her. A nun named María de Muñó y Erro stopped her and led her by the hand to Saint Teresa for a blessing. Saint Teresa blessed her, and at that moment María felt a great comfort and peace. Her wish to leave was gone. A few months later she made her profession into Saint Teresa’s hands. In her profession a nun makes her vows. She never had that temptation again. Inés de Quesada swore that she was present. She said that neither Saint Teresa nor anyone else had warned or urged the novice, and that Saint Teresa had given only her blessing. The community believed the change was a miracle through Saint Teresa’s intercession.

A nun given up by doctors recovers, Ávila

María del Castillo and Inés de Quesada swore at Ávila in 1610 that Saint Teresa was prioress of the Encarnación when a nun lay near death. The best doctors in the city had given her up, and the order was given to anoint her. Saint Teresa came into the cell and knelt at the foot of the bed with a hand on the sick woman’s forehead for a while. The sick woman’s sister was weeping because the confessors were slow to bring the anointing. Saint Teresa lifted her head and said, “Do not cry, daughter. Your sister will not die of this illness, and so she does not need the anointing.” In a few days the nun was well. María del Castillo shared the cell and saw it.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of María del Castillo, p. 406, and of Inés de Quesada, p. 420, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

A fallen nun lives in Saint Teresa’s arms

María Coronel, Catalina de Velasco, and Ana María de Jesús swore at Ávila in 1610 to what happened to Elvira de Guzmán. Elvira was a young nun of the Encarnación and was very unhappy. While Saint Teresa was prioress, she came out of her cell one day. Elvira fell down in the cloister as if dead. Saint Teresa took her in her arms with great love and prayed to God for her. After a little while Elvira came back to herself, but she could not speak. She lived about a year in bed. She made her confession in writing and died with signs of salvation. Ana María de Jesús said she was present. She believed that without Saint Teresa’s prayer Elvira’s soul would have been lost. The nuns believed it was a miracle.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimonies of María Coronel, p. 382, of Catalina de Velasco, p. 438, and of Ana María de Jesús, p. 453, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

The nuns’ houses are free of lice

The articles of 1609–10 say that the nuns of the first houses were troubled by many lice, above all when they prayed. They asked Saint Teresa to ask the Lord to free them of the trouble. She did, and from then on there were no lice in her houses. This was so even though the nuns wore wool shirts. The article adds that nuns who had lice before they entered had none once they became nuns.

Isabel de Santo Domingo swore in 1595 that she was in the house when the nuns made the request. She said that from then on they were never troubled. Isabel Bautista swore that visiting nuns from other houses suffered from lice while the sisters of the house of Saint Joseph at Ávila did not. Both believed it was a lasting miracle.

A glass jar survives a fall, Segovia

Catalina de la Concepción was sacristan at Segovia in the year the house was founded. She swore at Zaragoza in 1595 that Saint Teresa had a fever for six months that year. Juan de Orozco Covarrubias was an archdeacon of Segovia, a senior priest of the cathedral. He sent her a large glass jar to cheer her. Saint Teresa gave it to Catalina and said it would be good for flowers on the altar, but she must not let it break. Catalina filled it with water and set it on a small altar with other vases. She heard all the vases fall and found them broken on the stone floor. Only the jar was unbroken. It lay turned over, and yet the flowers had not fallen out of it. Two days later she told Saint Teresa, who said it was a miracle. Isabel de Santo Domingo swore that Saint Teresa made the sign of the cross and praised Our Lord, and told the sacristan, “That is a miracle. Keep it.”

The flour does not run out, 1580

The articles of 1609–10 say that nine nuns without dowries entered the new house at Villanueva de la Jara. Saint Teresa promised them in God’s name that they would never lack what they needed. The first year was very poor, and six measures of wheat fed sixteen people for six months. By natural means even sixty measures would not have been enough. María de los Mártires was then the prioress. She swore at Valencia that the house had only a basket with about six fanegas of flour. A fanega is an old Spanish measure of grain, about 55 liters. She had bread baked from it for sixteen or seventeen people, and the flour lasted until the new wheat came, more than six months. María de la Asunción swore at Villanueva in 1596 that the prioress asked at the end of Lent how much flour was left. The nun in charge said she had not dared to say that the flour in the basket never went down. They all went to see, and they thanked God.

In September of that year a catarrh spread through the whole town, and the house had no alms. María de los Mártires wrote to a rich churchman and had no answer. The Lord then gave one small pear tree in the house so much fruit that the nuns ate pears cooked and roasted. They gathered loads of pears to sell in the town square. The articles say this fed the sick nuns and many sick poor of the town for two months.

Money at the feet of the Child Jesus

The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Teresa gave Ana de San Agustín an image of the Child Jesus. She told her that whenever the house lacked something she should turn to the Child. Ana did this with trust and sometimes found money beside the image. Once she found thirty escudos there, and the article says only God could have put them there. Ana de San Agustín swore at Villanueva in 1596 that she often found money at his feet and in other places where she was moved to look. Once she found three hundred reales, and no one could tell who had put them there. This went on in Saint Teresa’s life and after her death. Elvira de San Ángelo swore at Huete about the portress, the nun who kept the door. The portress once came with money in her hand. She said she had found it for the nuns at the feet of the Child Jesus. At that time thirteen of the fourteen nuns were sick and in need.

Saint Teresa takes a priest’s torment on herself

The articles of 1609–10 say that a priest had lived in a grave sin for more than two years and still said Mass. He heard of Saint Teresa’s fame and spoke with her, and she promised to pray for him. When he was away he asked her to write, and she wrote. In his first answer he confessed the sin. He said he felt better and had kept from the sin for some days. But he said the demons tormented him with temptations that seemed to him the torments of hell, and he asked her to pray. Saint Teresa asked God to ease his torments. She asked that they fall on her, as long as God was not offended. It happened as she asked. For a month demons tormented her body with grave pains, and the priest was wholly free. He gave great thanks to God and to her. He told her later that whenever temptation came, it left him as he read her letters.

Visions Saint Teresa described

These are the graces God gave Saint Teresa in prayer, told as she tells them in her own books. Two come from other tellers: her friend and confessor Diego de Yepes, and the Carmelite chronicle quoted in the English edition of her letters. Purgatory is the state of cleansing that Catholics believe some souls pass through before heaven.

Saint Teresa sees Christ stern and grave

Saint Teresa wrote that she was once with a person whose friendship was not good for her. Christ stood before her, stern and grave, and she understood that something in her conduct offended Him. Saint Teresa saw Him with the eyes of the soul, more clearly than she could have seen Him with the eyes of the body. Astonished and disturbed, she resolved not to see that person again. She wrote that this had happened more than twenty-six years earlier.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 7, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Saint Teresa feels Christ close by, 1557

Saint Teresa wrote that she was praying on the feast of Saint Peter when she felt Christ close beside her. She saw nothing with the eyes of the body or with the eyes of the soul. She had not known that such a vision was possible, so she was afraid and wept. Christ spoke one word to reassure her, and she became calm. It was 29 June 1557.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 27, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Saint Teresa’s first rapture, in a hymn, 1558

Saint Teresa wrote that her confessor told her to pray the hymn Veni Creator so that God would show her what to do about her friendships. While she was saying it, she fell into a trance so suddenly that she was carried out of herself. She heard these words in the depth of her soul: “I do not want you to talk with men, but with angels.” She wrote that this was the first time God gave her the grace of ecstasy.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 24 and annals, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

Saint Teresa is shown hell, 1558 or 1559

Saint Teresa wrote that she was praying one day when she found herself, in a moment, plunged into hell. She understood that the Lord wanted her to see the place the devils kept ready for her, which she had deserved by her sins. It lasted only a moment, and she said she could never forget it. She described a long narrow passage like a furnace, muddy ground full of vermin, and a hollow in the wall where she was shut in. She called the vision one of the greatest mercies of the Lord. It took away her fear of trouble, and she gave thanks to the Lord who had delivered her from such pains.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 32 and annals, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft.

An angel pierces Saint Teresa’s heart, about 1559

Saint Teresa wrote that she saw an angel at her left side in bodily form. He was small and very beautiful, and his face was so aflame that he seemed one of the highest angels. In his hands she saw a long golden spear with a little fire at its tip, and he seemed to thrust it into her heart several times. When he drew it out, he left her “all on fire with great love of God”, and the pain made her moan. She wrote that the soul is then content with nothing less than God. It was a caress of love between the soul and God, and she prayed that anyone who thought she was lying might taste it.

Source: Teresa, The Life of St. Teresa of Jesus, ch. 29, tr. David Lewis, https://archive.org/details/lifeofstteresaof00tereuoft; Teresa, Obras, ed. Silverio de Santa Teresa, vol. 1, p. 234 and note, https://archive.org/details/obrasdesantatere01tere; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 139–140 and note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Christ gives Saint Teresa a jeweled cross

Saint Teresa wrote that she held the cross at the end of her beads, and Christ took it in His hand. When He gave it back, it looked like four great stones, more precious than diamonds and carved with the five wounds. She saw it that way from then on, but no one else saw the stones. Bishop Diego de Yepes wrote that the cross passed to her sister Joan and that miracles were worked through it.

Source: Butler, Lives of the Saints, vol. 10, pp. 336–338.

A dead nobleman is freed, Valladolid, 1568

A nobleman named Don Bernardino de Mendoza offered to give Saint Teresa a house and garden at Valladolid. About two months later he fell ill and died, and he could not make his confession clearly. Saint Teresa wrote that Christ told her the man’s salvation had been in serious danger, and that he would stay in purgatory until the first Mass said in that house. That first Mass was said on Sunday 15 August 1568, the feast of the Assumption. At the Mass she saw him beside the priest with a bright and shining face and joined hands. He thanked her for what she had done to free him from purgatory.

Source: Teresa, The Book of the Foundations, ch. 10 and notes, tr. David Lewis, ed. B. Zimmerman (1913), https://archive.org/details/bookoffoundation00tereuoft.

Saint Teresa sees a crystal castle, 1577

Diego de Yepes was her friend and confessor, and he wrote that the idea for The Interior Castle came from a vision on the eve of Trinity Sunday 1577. God showed her a globe of crystal shaped like a castle with seven rooms, and the King of glory shone in the seventh room at the centre. Then the light disappeared, the crystal turned dark, and venomous animals came in. Yepes wrote that she told him the same day. He promised to say nothing until after her death.

Source: The Interior Castle, tr. Benedictines of Stanbrook, rev. B. Zimmerman (1921), introduction, https://archive.org/details/stteresaofavilat0000stte.

Saint Teresa sees her brother after death, 1580

Saint Teresa’s letters say that her brother Lorenzo died of a hemorrhage in less than six hours, two days after the feast of Saint John in June 1580. The Carmelite chronicle tells more, and the English edition of her letters quotes it. Saint Teresa turned pale at the nuns’ recreation hour, went to the chapel, and said that his soul had passed through purgatory. A few days later at Holy Communion she saw Saint Joseph and Lorenzo.

Source: Teresa, The Letters of Saint Teresa, vol. 4, tr. Benedictines of Stanbrook (1924), letters CCCXXXII–CCCXXXIII and CCCXLVIII and notes, https://archive.org/details/lettersofsaintte0004card.

Saint Teresa sees Saint Dominic on the Segovia road

The articles of 1609–10 tell of a day when Saint Teresa came back from Segovia toward Ávila, where she was prioress. On the way she went in to pray in a chapel of the convent of Santa Cruz, where Saint Dominic had done great penance in his life. The prior went with her. So did her confessor, Fray Diego de Yanguas. As soon as she knelt, Saint Dominic appeared to her in great splendor and glory. He promised her his favor and protection in the work of the new Reform. She made her confession in the same place and saw him standing at her left side. When she received Holy Communion she saw Christ beside him. Christ said, “Rejoice with my friend,” as He vanished. Saint Dominic stayed with her and told her of his struggles with the devil in that place and of the favors God had given him there.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 40, p. XXXI, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Catalina de Cardona appears to Saint Teresa

The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Teresa was unsure about founding the house at Villanueva de la Jara because it was very poor. She prayed, and the Lord told her to go on. He reproved her and told her to remember the treasures with which she had carried out all that she had begun in the new Reform. He told her that in time this house would give Him great service. She set out from Malagón. On the road she came to a house of the Discalced friars, Our Lady of Socorro at La Roda. A hermit named Catalina de Cardona had built it before she died. Saint Teresa went into the church to pray, and Catalina de Cardona appeared to her with many angels in great glory. Catalina encouraged Saint Teresa to go on with the foundations and promised her help. Elvira de San Ángelo swore at Huete in 1595 that she saw this written in Saint Teresa’s own hand. Elvira read that Catalina de Cardona told Saint Teresa that Our Lord would be much served through the foundation. Elvira said she had seen this come true.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 46, p. XXXIV, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Elvira de San Ángelo, Huete, p. 531, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Christ’s blood fills her mouth, Palm Sunday

The articles of 1609–10 say that Saint Teresa went to Holy Communion nearly every day for more than twenty-three years. On a Palm Sunday she received the Host in her mouth and fell into a great rapture before she swallowed it. When she came back to herself she felt that her mouth was really full of the blood of Christ our Redeemer. She felt that her face and her whole body were soaked with the same blood, as hot as if it had just been shed. Her soul was filled with wonderful sweetness. She heard the Lord say, “Daughter, I want my blood to profit you. Do not fear that my mercy will ever fail you. I shed it with many pains. You enjoy it with great pleasure, as you see.”

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 73, pp. L–LI, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Saint Teresa is comforted about her book, Beas

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that a messenger came to Saint Teresa at Beas about twenty-two years earlier. He brought letters from Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza and from the nuns. They said the Inquisition had asked for the book of her life and was collecting all her papers. Saint Teresa told Anne that she had written it without fear. She said she did not mind whether the Inquisition found mistakes in it, because God knew the truth of what she wrote. She feared only for the houses of the Order. Anne said that if Saint Teresa asked God, she trusted that He would answer soon. After Holy Communion the next morning, Saint Teresa told Anne to give thanks to God. She had received Our Lord and been comforted by the words, “Do not be troubled, your cause is mine.” She understood that no harm would come to these houses, but much good. Anne said that Saint Teresa never heard anything more about the book for the rest of her life.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de Jesús (Blessed Anne of Jesus), Salamanca, pp. 479–480, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

After Saint Teresa’s death

Blessed Ana sees Christ at the deathbed, 1582

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé held Saint Teresa in her arms when she died, and she testified in 1595 about that night. She said that before Saint Teresa breathed her last, she herself saw the Lord at the foot of the bed in a great light, with many saints and angels. They were waiting to take Saint Teresa’s soul to glory, and she saw this with the eyes of the soul. She asked the Lord to take Saint Teresa at once, and she felt as if she were dead. Afterward she was greatly comforted, and she never again grieved over Saint Teresa’s death.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 170, testimony of Ana de San Bartolomé, 19 Oct. 1595, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Catalina Baptista regains her sense of smell, 1582

Catalina Baptista had lost her sense of smell. After Saint Teresa died, Catalina Baptista kissed her feet. She got her sense of smell back and noticed a great fragrance at the feet. María de San Francisco told this and ended with the words “I saw all this.” Catalina de San Ángelo was a nun of Alba. She swore in 1610 that Catalina Baptista had been without the sense of smell for some months. She said the fragrance was so strong that the nuns opened the door and the window of the cell. Catalina Baptista kissed the feet and said she smelled what the others smelled, and she kept her sense of smell afterward. Diego Núñez de Godoy swore in 1595 that he lived at Alba for two and a half years and heard the confessions of many of the Discalced nuns there. He told of a lay sister whom he did not name, and he heard her confession all the time he lived there. She had no sense of smell at all. On the day Saint Teresa died everyone praised the fragrance in the house, and the lay sister could not share it. She asked God in prayer to give her the sense so that she could take part, and she smelled what the others smelled. Later she smelled the same fragrance strongly in the kitchen. She found that it came from a little salt dish that Saint Teresa had used.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 336 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Proceso de Alba, testimony of Catalina de San Ángelo, 1610, pp. 205–206, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Diego Núñez de Godoy, Segovia process, p. 457, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Fragrance in the cell and shining hands, Alba

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified that when Saint Teresa died a very good smell filled the cell. She said even the little dishes that held Saint Teresa’s broth gave off an unusually good scent, and the nuns in the cell all noticed it. She said Saint Teresa’s body became so clear that the hands seemed to be see-through. While the body lay in the church, the hands could be seen shining from the choir. Mariana de Jesús watched by the body that night and said she could not say what the fragrance was like, because it smelled of many things. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 adds that the flesh stayed soft and the limbs flexible.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 83, 170, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Funeral candles keep their weight, Toledo

Juana del Espíritu Santo was a nun at Toledo, and she swore on 14 July 1595 that her convent held memorial services for Saint Teresa after her death. Four candles hired from a candle-maker burned during the services. The sacristan was Juan Bautista de Vargas, and he weighed them when they came and when they went back. She said he was amazed that no weight was lost, and he credited Saint Teresa. The candle-maker received no payment, and the sacristan was dead by the time she spoke.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Juana del Espíritu Santo, 14 July 1595, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A lay sister regains her sight, Toledo

Juana del Espíritu Santo swore on 14 July 1595 that a lay sister of the Toledo convent had lain ten days unconscious and without food. The doctor marveled that she was alive. He said she had only her breath left, and her pulse failed at times. She improved after ten days but stayed blind for about two months. One day Mass was offered before her, and she said she saw the Sacred Host raised. From then on her sight never failed. The sister always said firmly that Saint Teresa had given her back her sight, but she would not tell how.

María de Jesús swore at Toledo in 1595–96 about a nun of the house named Isabel de Jesús. Isabel was blind and saw nothing. The doctor had given up on her and said he did not know what more to do. She asked for a cloth in which Saint Teresa’s dead body had lain, and she said that Saint Teresa would heal her. She put it on her eyes a few times, and her sight came back as before. Isabel always said that Saint Teresa healed her, but she would not tell how.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Juana del Espíritu Santo, 14 July 1595, p. 253, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de Jesús, Toledo, p. 259, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

White-robed visitors, a star, and a dove, Alba

Catalina de la Concepción was the nun who cared for the sick at Alba. On the night Saint Teresa died, she sat by a low window in the cell. She heard a great noise like a joyful crowd, and she saw many shining people in white come into the cell and reach the bed as Saint Teresa died. One nun saw a white dove leave Saint Teresa’s mouth and another saw a star over the church tower. Dr. Diego de Polanco knew Saint Teresa. At the hearing at Medina on 9 April 1596 he told what he had heard from those who were there. Polanco spoke of the same white-robed visitors and the dove. He added that before her death some nuns saw a very large bright star over the church. One nun saw a crystal-colored ray pass the window of her cell.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 337 note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Diego de Polanco, 9 April 1596, p. 59, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

Two nuns see Saint Teresa’s soul rise, 1582

Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 tells of two nuns who saw Saint Teresa’s soul rise to heaven. One saw it as a white dove and the other as a shining crystal globe. Lovat says that Catherine of the Conception heard sweet music and saw a procession of virgins in white lead Saint Teresa to paradise. Lovat took this from the papal decree of canonization.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Saint Teresa appears at Veas the same day

Ribera’s Life says that Catalina de Jesús was gravely ill at Veas when Saint Teresa died. The nuns tried to hide Saint Teresa’s death from her to spare her pain, but she found out. She told Fray Jerónimo de la Madre de Dios that Saint Teresa had appeared to her in great glory. She said Saint Teresa told her she was going to enjoy God. Saint Teresa said that in her death she had a great impulse of love, and that by it her soul left her body. Ribera adds that Saint Teresa revealed the same to a senior superior of her order. The articles of 1609–10 say Saint Teresa appeared to Catalina de Jesús that night and cured her illness.

Isabel de Santo Domingo swore at Zaragoza in 1595 that Father Gracián was at Veas when letters brought word of the death. He stepped aside to read the letters, and when he came back to the sick nun he tried to hide the news. She said to him, “Why do you hide the news from me? I already know that Mother Teresa is dead. She came to see me and told me I will not die of this illness. As a sign she took away a skin sore I had.” Ribera also writes that Saint Teresa touched a sore under the nun’s breast that no one could cure. She also touched a large round black mark on the nun’s hand, and both were gone. María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that Saint Teresa told the nun not to blame the superior who had sent Saint Teresa to Alba. Saint Teresa said that the cause of her death was a flow of blood that came on her in a rush of prayer.

Source: Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 337–338 and note, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 95, pp. LXVI–LXVII, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Isabel de Santo Domingo, pp. 96–97, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, Lisbon, p. 503, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, pp. 540–541, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

A dead tree flowers at Alba, 1582

Catalina de San Ángelo was a nun of Alba. She swore in 1610 that she and other nuns saw a tree in a little field in front of Saint Teresa’s cell on the morning after she died. It was a small dry tree that had never borne fruit or flower, and now it was covered with flowers as white as snow. She said it was 5 October, in the cold of winter. The tree did not flower again. She and the other nuns thought it a miracle. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 tells the same story. Lovat says the tree stood beside the convent chapel, was half buried under a rubbish heap, and had been dead for years.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Proceso de Alba, testimony of Catalina de San Ángelo, 1610, p. 205, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 605, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

A nun’s fever ends at Alba, 1582

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 that Saint Teresa’s clothes were asked for after she died. They were wanted for a nun in a convent at Alba. The nun was a relative of the Duchess of Alba, and she had had a continual fever for more than a year. When the clothes were put on her, the fever left her.

Francisca de Fonseca swore at Alba in 1592 about Doña Bernardina de Toledo y Enríquez, sister of the Duchess. On the day of the burial Doña Bernardina sent to ask for a relic. Francisca sent a linen jacket that Saint Teresa had worn. Doña Bernardina had been ill with a great fever for more than two months. She kissed the jacket with great devotion and put it on. A terrible sweat came over her, and she was free of the fever. Ribera tells the same of Doña Bernardina de Toledo, a nun of the inner convent at Alba. He says the doctors and the nuns were amazed, because they had not expected it.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Francisca de Fonseca, Alba, p. 137, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Francisco de Ribera, Vida de Santa Teresa de Jesús, p. 553, https://archive.org/details/RIBERAVidaDeSantaTeresaDeJess.

Saint Teresa’s cloth is found soaked in blood

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 about a cloth put on Saint Teresa for the flow of blood. She said the blood was fresh when she took the cloth away. The articles of 1609–10 say that after three years the cloth was soaked in blood “as if it had come from the body at that very hour.” They say doctors judged this a miracle. They add that drops the color of blood have been seen on the girdle she was buried in. That girdle is kept at Zaragoza.

Diego de Yepes swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 about Jerónima del Espíritu Santo. On her way from Genoa to be prioress at Madrid, she came to Zaragoza. Jerónima had been told that a leather belt of Saint Teresa sometimes sweated blood, and she had little faith in this. As she held the belt to put it down, she suddenly saw two drops of blood. She gathered them with a scrap of paper and a bit of serge. Yepes kept them. He showed them to the notary Dr. Mármol. The notary smelled the same fragrance as on Saint Teresa’s other relics.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 100, p. LXXI, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Diego de Yepes, Madrid, p. 288, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Saint Teresa’s body is found whole, 1583

The nuns of Alba buried Saint Teresa between the two grilles of the lower choir and built a wall of stones and lime over the coffin. They noticed a strong fragrance from the tomb and asked leave to open it. On 4 July 1583 the provincial Jerónimo Gracián did so. Mariana de Jesús helped to dig and swore that the body was whole and flexible, “so fresh and whole that it seemed the blood was running.” Both the coffin and the clothes had rotted, but a broken board had damaged only the tip of the nose. The articles of 1609–10 say that learned doctors judged this a miracle.

María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that the nuns of Alba smelled a great fragrance from the tomb many times. They also heard knocks inside it. They woke one another and asked who was sleeping, and they took the sounds as a call from Saint Teresa to be ready for prayer. These were the reasons they pressed their superior to open the tomb.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 83–84, 101–102, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 97, pp. LXIX–LXX, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 606, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, Lisbon, p. 507, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Oil flows from Saint Teresa’s body and relics

The articles of 1609–10 say that an oil or balm flowed from Saint Teresa’s body and from every piece of her flesh, and soaked into many cloths. Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé cleaned the body at Ávila in 1585. She described the arm sweating “like a kind of oil, or water mixed with blood.” She said in 1595 that any cloth laid against the body was soaked through as if it had been wet with oil.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 98, p. LXX, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), p. 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Saint Teresa’s body goes to Ávila and returns

Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza built the main chapel of Saint Joseph’s in Ávila on condition that Saint Teresa’s body lie there, and the provincial of the Order signed the deed. In November 1585 two superiors took the body and left one arm at Alba. They ordered the nuns of Alba to keep silence. Juana del Espíritu Santo lived at Alba. She swore that the body was as light as if it were not flesh and bone. At Ávila, Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé found that the clothing touching the flesh was as it had been on the first day. The articles of 1609–10 say the body returned to Alba on 23 August 1586.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 101–102, 171, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Rótulo of 1609–10, art. 101, pp. LXXI–LXXII, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII.

Saint Teresa appears to Blessed Ana, 1585–94

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé swore in 1595 that at Alba she heard Saint Teresa’s voice say: “Do what your superior commands and go to Ávila.” A few days later at Ávila, Saint Teresa appeared at dawn and showed Blessed Ana her own body in a vision. She told Blessed Ana to look and see that it was not corrupted. Saint Teresa said the body would soon come to Saint Joseph’s on the day of the Presentation, and Blessed Ana said it left Alba that same day. About eighteen months before 1595, Saint Teresa appeared to her in Madrid. She told her that letters had gone to Rome for a brief to bring the body back from Alba to Ávila. Saint Teresa called it worldly intrigue. Our Lord then told Blessed Ana that the body would go to Ávila “through a woman” whom she did not know.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), pp. 171–172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

Later openings of the tomb, 1592 to 2024

In March 1592 Bishop Jerónimo Manrique of Salamanca moved the body to a new chest given by the Duchess of Alba and found it as before. Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 that she had seen the body a year earlier, with much flesh cut away and a stronger fragrance. Lady Lovat writes that openings up to 1750 gave the same report. The tomb was last opened in 1914. In 2024 the largest part of her remains was taken out again for a new study. Aleteia reports that the body was still in the state it was in at the opening of 1914.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Mariana de Jesús, 1592, p. 85, and of Ana de San Bartolomé, 1595, p. 172, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 608, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich; Aleteia, 15 Oct. 2024, https://aleteia.org/2024/10/15/1st-results-from-exams-of-teresa-of-avilas-relics/.

The heart is kept at Alba

Catalina de San Ángelo was a nun of Alba. She swore in 1610 that Bishop Manrique held an inquiry at the convent. His doctors opened the body at the side to see whether it had been embalmed, and they found it whole. The heart was taken out then. She was prioress at the time, and she kept it for a while. She said it was burst on one side. Inés de Jesús was prioress of Alba in 1610. She swore that she once held the heart in her right hand and wondered if it was the heart of Saint Teresa. She felt it beat against her palm. When she put it down and closed her hand again, the beating stopped, and she took it as a miracle. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 says that a lay sister took out the heart and that it shows the wound made by the seraph’s spear. It also says that three “thorns” formed from dust at the bottom of the crystal case.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 3 (BMC 20), Proceso de Alba, testimony of Inés de Jesús, May 1610, pp. 185–186, and of Catalina de San Ángelo, 1610, pp. 207–208, https://archive.org/details/BMC20ProcesosDeBeatificacinYCanonizaciinDeSantaTeresaTomoIII; Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), p. 607 note, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

Saint Teresa appears to nuns and friends

Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 reports that Saint Teresa appeared to the prioress of Veas the day after she died. Saint Teresa told her that she enjoyed the vision of God because she had practiced virtue, and not because of revelations and ecstasies. A short time later she appeared to a young nun at Veas, who was suffering great spiritual trials. She also appeared several times to Father Gracián and more than once to her niece Teresita.

Source: Alice Lady Lovat, The Life of Saint Teresa (1912), pp. 608–609, https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintteres00bensrich.

A relic eases a difficult birth, Lisbon, 1591

María de San José swore on 27 May 1595 that she held a letter from her brother, Father Gracián, written at Lisbon on 29 May 1591. He wrote about Doña Inés de Ayala. She was the wife of the chief steward of the Cardinal Archduke, and on the May feast of the Cross she was in great danger in childbirth. People brought her a hand of Saint Teresa that he had carried to Lisbon. Gracián happened to pass by, and they had him come in. He laid the hand on her, and she soon came out of danger. They counted it a miracle because of how grave her case was. Francisca de las Llagas swore on 7 June 1595 that she had read the same letter.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, 27 May 1595, p. 327, and of Francisca de las Llagas, 7 June 1595, p. 345, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A fever and side pain end, Salamanca

Ana de la Trinidad was a nun of Salamanca. She swore on 3 January 1592 that she once had a sharp pain in her side. She seemed to improve, but then she grew so ill that Dr. Tierra ordered more bloodletting to begin the cure again. They went to look for the barber and could not find him. In the meantime she put on her head half a shirt that Saint Teresa had worn when she received the last anointing, and she asked Saint Teresa’s help. A great sweat came over her, and her pain lessened as the sweat grew. After about an hour and a half the sweat ended, and the pain and the fever ended with it. She said the doctor and everyone were amazed.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de la Trinidad, 3 Jan. 1592, p. 49, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

An outside helper’s arm is healed, Cuerva

María del Nacimiento swore on 27 May 1595 that she knew of a cure at the Carmelite convent of Cuerva, near Toledo. She learned it from letters of Ana de los Ángeles, the prioress, and later from the woman herself. The convent had an outside helper who ran its errands. Her arm was so bad that she might lose it. Doctors had met to plan a major treatment. Sister Isabel de San Jerónimo gave her a little earth from Saint Teresa’s tomb to put on the arm. The pain grew much worse, so the helper took the earth off. The nuns went to rest that night, and the helper stayed outside alone. She said someone called her to the turn, which is the revolving hatch in the wall. The voice said, “Sister, do not be foolish, put that earth on.” The woman put it on, fell asleep, and woke well. She spoke to me and told me to put on the earth, and now I am well.” The doctors saw the arm and found it healthy, and all counted it a miracle.

Ana de la Madre de Dios was a nun of Cuerva. She swore that the helper was Catalina Gómez. The doctor and the surgeons had told Catalina there was no remedy. She was made well in a short time through a relic of Saint Teresa.

María del Nacimiento swore on 27 May 1595 about Count Tiburcio, a gentleman of the Empress. He was very sick with a stone. He was to be cut open, or had already been cut. The Count sent to the Discalced convent in Madrid to ask for the nuns’ prayers. In great pain he fell asleep and dreamed that he saw some Discalced nuns around his bed. A nun’s madness ends, Madrid

Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé testified in 1595 about a nun of the Discalced convent at Madrid who suddenly went mad. The nun was kept tied up. The prioress was María de San Jerónimo, and she told Blessed Ana to care for her. Blessed Ana asked Saint Teresa again and again in prayer to obtain healing for the nun. One morning she thought she saw Saint Teresa calling her, and she dressed and followed her to the nun’s closed cell. There Saint Teresa vanished, and Blessed Ana called the nun by name. The nun answered, “Come in, I am praying,” and Blessed Ana found her calm and kneeling. The nun rose and went with Blessed Ana to the chapel where the nun was usually taken to pray. Blessed Ana asked the nun’s pardon and put the chains on her, as obedience required. The nun said she had never known the worth of chains until then. They passed before the Blessed Sacrament. The nun asked to pray, and she was well. Blessed Ana said the nun was still well in 1595 and lived as the other nuns did. She credited the cure to Saint Teresa’s prayers.

A shining nun visits Blessed Anne, 1582

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that she lay at Granada in October 1582 with such a severe illness that the doctors gave her up. Saint Teresa’s last illness was short, and none of her houses knew of it. The prior of the Discalced friars at Granada was Anne’s confessor. He waited all night for her fits to ease so that she could receive Holy Communion. After she received it she asked to be left alone. At once she saw beside her bed a nun in their habit, so glorious and covered with light that she could not make out her face. She thought, “I know this nun.” The nun smiled and came closer. The nearer she came, the less Anne could see her because of the light. Anne felt great esteem for their way of life. She wished to tell everyone how little it was to give one’s life to keep their way of life, and what glory waited for them.

Anne thought it was a sign of her death, and she called two of the oldest nuns to tell them. Then she began to recover so quickly that the doctors marveled. As she began to rise, news came that God had taken Saint Teresa. She understood at once that the nun had been Saint Teresa. She could not finish reading the line that told it. Then she remembered that the Church did not end when Saint Peter and Saint Paul died on one day. She told herself that the Order would not end either, because Saint Teresa could help it better from heaven. She took comfort and comforted the others.

Saint John of the Cross survives the plague

Blessed Anne of Jesus swore at Salamanca in 1597 that plague reached Granada from Seville in 1582 or 1583. Two friars of the Discalced house there fell dead within one week. In the same week the prior was saying Mass in the nuns’ church when a violent pain and fever struck him. He could not leave the church. They sent a relic of Saint Teresa to be laid on his wound. He improved at once, so that he could be taken to his own house. He recovered and lived six or seven years more. Breast tumors end at Saint Teresa’s hand

Doña Luisa de Porres swore on 16 July 1596 that Saint Teresa had accepted her as a nun of the Order. She had given her word to Father Baltasar Álvarez. She did not enter, because the aunt she lived with fell seriously ill. Then she fell and struck her chest, and she hid the swelling for ten months from all but her confessor. Then the pain and hardness in both breasts grew so grave that she had to be treated. The treatment lasted about nine years with the best doctors and surgeons. They said she had many tumors, and they never dared to open her breasts. At the end they gave her up. One night she was in great anguish and thought she was dying. She believed she saw two or three women in white and at once felt much relief. She sensed that one of them was Saint Teresa, and she asked for her help as if Saint Teresa were there in person.

Soon she improved, and in eight or nine days she had strength to go to the Discalced convent at Lisbon. There she laid Saint Teresa’s hand on her breasts with great trust. The nuns keep this relic. At once she was so well that a wound in her arm closed that same day. It had been kept open for about five years, and the doctors had forbidden it to close. The next day she could dress as when she was healthy, after nine or ten years without a bodice. A month later a little hardness and pain came back in one breast. She went again and touched the hand to the place, and the pain never returned in the nearly three years before she testified. After all those years of strict diet she ate everything, fish included, and she fasted the whole Advent that followed. She was certain that Our Lord did this through Saint Teresa’s prayer.

A fever patient sees two figures, 1595

Antonio Tamayo swore on 15 June 1596 that in April 1595 he had a severe typhus. The doctors gave him up. On the fourteenth day he sent for his cousin Canon Tamayo of Palencia. Antonio began to sleep and eat. He told no one at the time. He was certain that Saint Teresa had obtained his health by a miracle.

A carpenter’s arm is saved from amputation, Salamanca

Pedro Hernández was a carpenter of Salamanca. He swore in 1610 that he was hanging a bell on the roof of a convent on the day the nuns moved in. An auger slipped and bored through his left palm to the bone. He finished his work and went home. He wrapped the hand in a cloth and did not treat it. At dawn he was called to build the catafalque for Bishop Jerónimo Manrique, who had died. A catafalque is the raised stand that holds the coffin. The timbers were heavy, and his hand and arm swelled. The doctors thought they had grown fistulous. A fistula is a deep sore like a tube that does not close. He also had a high fever. Three doctors decided to cut off the arm below the elbow. He remembered Saint Teresa and asked the convents to pray for him. He sent to the Discalced nuns of Saint Joseph at Salamanca. Sister Encarnación was a nun there and a relative of Saint Teresa. She said, “Is it possible that he is so ill, and we in this house did not know?” She sent a jar of water of Saint Albert and a cloth touched to the oil of Saint Teresa’s body. Water of Saint Albert is water blessed in honor of a Carmelite saint. Pedro drank the whole jar because of his great thirst, and the fever left him at once. He laid the cloth over the dressings on his hand and slept for more than two and a half hours. After that he felt throbbing in his hand for about an hour, and the abscess burst through the palm. The surgeon Linares came and thought he was joking when Pedro said he had no fever. Then Linares unwrapped the hand and found it burst. The pus ran for more than two hours. The doctors and Pedro believed it was a miracle through Saint Teresa’s prayers and the prayers of her daughters. Within six days he was up and well.

A blind woman sees, Alba, 1582

A boy recovers in Saint Teresa’s sheet, Alba, 1591

The licentiate Pedro de Vallejo swore on 3 April 1592 that his son Alonso lay ill in bed for four months and almost died. On 19 December 1591 Vallejo sent word to the prioress of the Discalced nuns at Alba. He asked for their prayers and for a sheet in which Saint Teresa’s body had been wrapped. Vallejo took the sheet off so as not to soil it. The boy looked sad, and his pains and distress came back. María González and the others in the room begged Vallejo to put it back. He put it back folded long and narrow around the boy’s neck and shoulders, and the pains stopped again. Vallejo said that God gave the boy the health he had then, though he was still weak.

A prioress’s locked jaws ease, Salamanca

Ana de la Encarnación was prioress of the Discalced convent at Salamanca. She swore at the inquiry of 1591–92 that after a grave illness she could hardly eat, because her jaws and throat were so tight. In her weakness her jaws had slipped out of place. After they were set she was so afraid at every meal that she felt she was choking, even on tiny bites. Saint Teresa came to the Salamanca house, saw her, and was moved to great pity. After Saint Teresa’s death Ana put on her throat a leather belt that Saint Teresa had worn at night. Ana now ate without her old trouble, and the nuns had seen it. Ana believed that Our Lord gave her this mercy through Saint Teresa’s prayers in life and through the belt after her death. Isabel de la Cruz and Isabel de San Jerónimo swore that the improvement began at Saint Teresa’s visit and that the sisters understood it came from her prayers. Isabel de San Jerónimo added that Ana’s health was complete after Saint Teresa’s death.

A stuck fishbone comes out, Medina del Campo

Ana de la Encarnación swore at the Salamanca inquiry of 1591–92 that she was eating fish at Medina del Campo when a bone stuck in her throat. She could not bring it up although she tried every remedy she could. Upset, she remembered the leather belt that Saint Teresa had worn. She commended herself to Our Lord. She asked Him to free her by the merits of His Passion and through Saint Teresa’s prayer. Then she pressed the belt to her throat and brought the bone up at once. A sister who was with her saw the bone. Isabel de la Cruz, María de los Santos, and Isabel de San Jerónimo swore that they heard her tell it.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Ana de la Encarnación, Salamanca, p. 24; of Isabel de la Cruz, p. 33; of María de los Santos, p. 40; of Isabel de San Jerónimo, p. 67, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A heart pain ends in half an hour

Ana de la Trinidad swore at Salamanca on 3 January 1592 that four years earlier she had a great pain at the heart, which she had never had before. It pressed her so hard that she fainted, and her fever rose. The remedies did not help. Isabel de Jesús was then prioress, and she laid on Ana a cloth with Saint Teresa’s blood. Ana asked Saint Teresa to beg Our Lord to take away the pain and fill her heart with Himself. Soon after the cloth was laid on, she felt great anguish, and a sweat broke out on that place. Within half an hour the pain was gone, and it had not come back in the four years since. Fray Alonso de los Ángeles swore at Zaragoza in 1595 that Ana told him of another time. Seven years after her death, Saint Teresa appeared to Ana. Ana said that Saint Teresa healed her of an illness of the heart and eased a need of her soul.

A quartan fever ends, 1586

Francisco de Morales swore on 18 August 1595 that from September 1585 he had a severe quartan fever, a fever that returns every fourth day. It brought chills, vomiting, and a deep loathing of food, and it lasted nearly six months. He tried many remedies and asked religious and devout people to pray for him. A Discalced nun sent him a cloth with Saint Teresa’s blood and told him to put it on with devotion and see the mercy God would do through her. On 11 March 1586 he put it on. On the day the fever was due he got up and went about his business for most of the day. That night he felt well and ate his supper. By God’s mercy he never felt it again, not even the weakness such fevers leave behind. From then on he always carried a relic of Saint Teresa.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Francisco de Morales, Madrid, p. 407, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A dying woman swallows again, Toledo

Doña Gabriela Hurtado swore at Madrid on 20 September 1595 that more than two years earlier her sister, Doña Ana María, lay gravely ill at Toledo. The doctors had urged the family one night to give her the anointing, and a Franciscan habit was ready. They did not expect her to live two hours. For eleven days she had hardly been able to swallow, and on some days not even a drop of water. Early that morning a devout woman named María Bautista came to the house. She said, “Mother Teresa has pressed me all night to put a relic of hers on Doña Ana María. If you do not have one I will go to the Discalced for it.” Father Salazar of the Society of Jesus had given the family a piece of the serge sheet in which Saint Teresa died. They laid it on the sick woman’s throat. They gave her an egg at once, and she swallowed it. A swelling came out on her throat and blotches covered her body. The doctors said it was the melancholy coming out. She grew better and recovered, and she was alive and well when Gabriela testified.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of Gabriela Hurtado, Madrid, pp. 411–412, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A woman given up recovers, Madrid

Doña Beatriz de Mendoza and Doña Orofrisia de Mendoza swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 about Doña Estefanía, wife of Valderrábano, secretary to the Prior Don Fernando. The doctors had given Doña Estefanía up. She had received the anointing and had lost her senses. Orofrisia was the wife of Saint Teresa’s nephew Francisco de Cepeda. She owned the headcloth in which Saint Teresa died. They put it on Doña Estefanía. At that moment she came back to herself and began to improve, and then she was well. Orofrisia said that when they laid the cloth on the sick, those who were to die grew worse at once. Those who were to live improved at once.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimonies of Beatriz de Mendoza, pp. 397–398, and of Orofrisia de Mendoza, p. 402, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A nun’s crippled arm is healed, Cuerva

Diego de Yepes swore at the Madrid inquiry of 1595–96 that he gave a picture of the Child Jesus to Ana de los Ángeles, prioress at Cuerva. Saint Teresa had carried it in her breviary, the book of the Church’s daily prayers. A nun of the house had an arm half crippled after a bloodletting and was sad that she could not serve the others. Doña Aldonza Niño was the foundress and a nun there. She said to the sick nun, “Sister, I want to heal you.” With great faith Doña Aldonza took off the plasters and cloths and laid the picture on the abscess. For half an hour a great fire came out through the palm of the hand, as if flames were held in the arm. When it calmed, the nun was healed. María del Nacimiento swore at Madrid that she heard of this from letters sent from Cuerva. She named the nun Ana de la Madre de Dios. She said that the nun put the picture on her bad arm in great pain and was healed.

Demons flee from a picture of Saint Teresa, Seville

María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that soon after Saint Teresa’s death a man of Seville named Fernando de Treixo was tormented by demons. They sometimes appeared to him in plain sight. One night he held two paper pictures, one of Our Lady and one of Saint Teresa. In the dark he took the picture of Saint Teresa to use against the demons, but he thought it was Our Lady’s. When he held it toward them he saw them flee, howling and roaring, as if driven out by a great force. He was freed from the torment and gave thanks to Our Lady. When he went to the light he found that it was Saint Teresa’s picture, and he was astonished and became very devoted to her. Treixo himself told it to María de San José. His confessor was there too, the Jesuit Rodrigo Álvarez.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, Lisbon, p. 508, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A nursing mother’s milk returns, Seville

María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that when she was prioress at Seville a poor woman came to the convent. She was nursing her baby, and her milk had dried up. She was very sad and asked for a relic of Saint Teresa. The nuns gave her a piece of Saint Teresa’s habit. As soon as she laid it on her breasts she had milk. The woman came back to the convent and thanked the nuns. She and the nuns believed it was a miracle.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 1 (BMC 18), testimony of María de San José, Lisbon, p. 508, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif01silv.

A knight gives up killing his wife, Lisbon

Isabel de la Cruz swore at the Salamanca inquiry of 1591–92 that Father Jerónimo Gracián told her that Saint Teresa’s hand at Lisbon worked more miracles than her body. A knight of Lisbon had decided to kill his wife because of suspicions that the devil had put into him. The prioress of the Discalced nuns spoke to him and begged him not to do it. When she saw that nothing worked, she took out Saint Teresa’s hand. Isabel did not remember whether the prioress showed it to him or touched him with it. His evil purpose left him at once.

Ribera writes that the knight had come to the convent the day before and told the prioress his anguish and his evil thought. She asked him not to go home that night but to stay with the Discalced fathers, who would console and advise him. When he would not agree and his anger did not calm, she laid the hand on his heart. The wish left him, and he was quiet and consoled. María de San Francisco swore at Medina del Campo that he was a good husband from then on.

Put forward for her canonization, 1609–1610

The articles drawn up for the hearings at Salamanca in 1609 and 1610 set out these healings through her relics. Witnesses were examined on them. Each account is told as the article tells it.

Witnesses at Ávila in 1610 told more healings that they had seen or heard of. Those accounts are told as the witnesses told them.

A nun born without smell is healed, Lisbon

Sister Inés de la Madre de Dios had had no sense of smell since birth. The provincial brought Saint Teresa’s hand to the convent at Lisbon, and her sisters spoke of its fragrance. Inés knelt with the hand at her nose and said she would not get up until she could smell what her sisters smelled. She felt a warm moisture rise into her nose, and her sense of smell opened little by little. Afterward her sense of smell was perfect. The sisters held different scents before her, and she said which smelled good and which smelled bad.

María de San José was then prioress at Lisbon. She swore in 1595 that the hand in its closed casket gave off a fragrance so strong that her own color changed and her hair stood on end. Inés had never had any sense of smell. She took the hand and put it to her nose. She said, “My Mother, make me feel what my sisters feel.” At once she said a warm vapor rose up her nose, and she smelled. María tested her with good and bad scents, and Inés named each one. Ribera adds that Inés was later a professed nun. One night a violent attack came on her. It felt as if her bones were breaking and her heart was being torn out, and three or four sisters could not hold her. When they laid Saint Teresa’s hand on her, she was free at once.

A doubting lay sister smells a relic, Malagón

A provincial brought a finger of Saint Teresa to the convent at Malagón and told the nuns to notice how it smelled. Saint Teresa had corrected a lay sister there during her life, and this sister thought, “They say it smells; it stinks.” She had hardly finished the thought when a strong fragrance came from the finger. She fell to the floor almost senseless. When she came to herself, she admitted her fault and said in a louder voice than usual, “Now it smells very strong.”

Catalina de San Cirilo swore at Malagón in 1596 that the nuns told her the story this way. Father Gracián showed them a finger of Saint Teresa, and one nun doubted whether Saint Teresa had been so holy. A great fragrance came from the finger to her nose, and she fell senseless to the ground. Afterward she confessed her fault and said the fragrance was given to cure her mistake.

A judge’s dying son revives, Alba

Licentiate Vallejo was a judge on the Duke of Alba’s council. His only son was two years old, and everyone had lost hope for the child’s life. Pedro de Zamora was chaplain to the Discalced nuns, and the father sent for him to read the Gospel over the child and pray. The chaplain laid on the child’s head a small cloth stained with Saint Teresa’s blood, and the child revived. He held the cloth tight and cried if anyone tried to take it, and his father heard his voice in the church but did not dare to look.

Pedro de Vallejo swore at Alba on 3 April 1592 that his son Alonso was twenty-two months old and had a cold. On 12 March 1587 the boy grew so ill that his father and all who saw him despaired. Vallejo asked the priest to say the Gospels over him, and the priest brought a little cloth stained with Saint Teresa’s blood. The priest laid it on the boy’s forehead in the cradle and said the four Gospels. Then they went to the church of Saint Stephen. Vallejo knelt in prayer by the altar steps. He heard a voice behind him say, “Father.” He knew it was his son’s voice, but he did not turn because he thought the boy was dead. The boy called again, and Vallejo turned and saw him coming on his own feet. The nurse was Elena Rodríguez. She called out, “What do you think, sir, of the great mercies Our Lord has done us?” The boy would not give up the cloth in his fist, and for hours they could not take it from him. In Vallejo’s sworn account the priest is Antonio de Zamora.

A priest survives a gunshot, Seville

A priest named Pedro Fernández de Barragán of Valverde read Saint Teresa’s books every day. He carried in his shirt a paper with words of hers copied from Ribera’s Life. One day the vicar general Bernardino Rodríguez tried to fire a loaded arquebus and failed, so he handed it to the priest. It went off and fired ten or twelve lead balls at the priest’s chest, two fingers from the paper. The balls bounced back five or six cubits as if they had hit a stone, and he was unhurt. An inquiry was drawn up at the time. Pedro de Tolosa swore at Seville that the priest himself had told the story. The shot of twelve pellets struck his chest, and the pellets bounced back without harming him. The priest said that Our Lord did it through Saint Teresa.

A paralysed nun is healed, Seville

Sister Isabel de San Jerónimo lay paralysed on one side with an arm stuck to her body. She could not move in bed without the help of two nuns. The provincial laid a finger relic of Saint Teresa on her arm and hand, and she was healed at once and cried out that her arm was cured. He then laid it on a swelling in her throat and on her sick left side, and the swelling went away. The articles say she lived twelve years afterward without the pains that had often troubled her.

Ribera writes that this illness often struck Isabel de San Jerónimo and sometimes left her crippled on one side. On Saint Michael’s day it struck so hard in her arm that for more than twenty-four hours she could not stop groaning. Father Jerónimo Gracián was there with a finger of Saint Teresa. He had it laid on her arm without telling her or the other nuns whose it was. As soon as it touched her hand, she moved it and was amazed at the lightness that rose up her arm. The finger was passed over her whole crippled side, and she was free from then on. María de San José swore at Lisbon in 1595 that this happened in 1583, when she was prioress at Seville. Isabel de San Francisco swore that the illness never came back to Isabel de San Jerónimo in the four or five years they lived together.

A prioress with consumption improves, Segovia

Isabel de Santo Domingo was prioress at Segovia and had consumption, and her doctors had lost hope. She also had strong heart palpitations and tremors. A little earth from Saint Teresa’s tomb was applied to her, and she felt a great improvement. After two or three days she got up from bed, and on Christmas Day she did the prioress’s duties in choir. All the nuns of the convent counted it a miracle.

Many nuns of the house swore to the same at Segovia in 1595. Inés de Jesús said that Isabel had been nearly three months in bed, spitting blood and unable to eat. Dr. Velázquez said she would die if this went on. Letters came six days before Christmas. Isabel said at once, “Do you not notice the fragrance these letters bring?” Inside was a little earth that had been taken from Saint Teresa’s body when it was dug up at Alba. The papers around it were soaked with oil from the earth. Isabel was greatly comforted and improved noticeably. Two days later she got up, and she attended the Calenda early on Christmas Eve, the solemn announcement of Christ’s birth, and all the prayers and services of Christmas.

A nun healed of earache refuses to swear, Zaragoza

Sister Leonor de los Ángeles had a very severe earache that made her beside herself for two hours. The prioress laid on her ear a small cloth wet with oil from Saint Teresa’s body, and the pain stopped at once and never returned. Leonor refused to swear to the miracle at the hearings because she had never taken an oath in her life. The prioress said, “Be quiet, sister, the Holy Mother will take revenge for this.” Leonor then caught a dangerous fever (the articles call it a double tertian, a fever that comes in regular attacks), and she got better after she promised to swear.

María de la Visitación swore at Zaragoza in 1595 that this happened in 1588, as she remembered it. Leonor had a strong toothache and earache and did not know Saint Teresa as well as the other nuns. They laid on her ear and teeth a cloth that had touched the body, and she was free of all pain at once. From then on she was very devoted to Saint Teresa. Isabel de Santo Domingo was the prioress who laid on the cloth. She swore that Leonor never complained of that ear again in all the time she lived with her at Zaragoza. Before she became a nun the ear had troubled her for many years, and it sometimes ran.

An abscess in the throat bursts, Salamanca

Sister Juana de Jesús had an abscess in her throat so large that she could not swallow her saliva. The doctor and the surgeon said a cautery was needed and that she would die if it touched the vein. She asked Saint Teresa for help, and a small cloth wet with Saint Teresa’s oil was laid on the swelling. The abscess opened, and she was well at once. The surgeon was amazed because it had been as hard as stone.

Juana de Jesús told it herself under oath at Salamanca in 1591–92. For a long time she could not eat or swallow her saliva. The doctor and the surgeon planned to burn the abscess with a hot iron, and the surgeon feared that if he struck a vein she would die. She was afraid. She commended herself to Our Lord and to Saint Teresa and put the cloth on. The abscess opened, and the surgeon was amazed to find it open. Ana de la Encarnación swore the same. She added that the abscess had lasted almost a year and that the pain was great after the cloth was put on. The infirmarian Ana de la Trinidad said the same.

A blind nun sees again, Salamanca, 1603

Isabel de Montoya was a nun at Santa María de las Dueñas in Salamanca. She was blind from cataracts, and the operation had made the sickness worse. A nun advised her to ask Saint Teresa’s help and to touch a piece of Saint Teresa’s flesh, wrapped in linen, to her eyes. On 10 February 1603 the relic was touched to her eyes, and she began to see a little brightness. By the next Saturday she saw the Host clearly at Communion, and on 21 February she walked to Communion without a guide while the nuns sang the Te Deum.

A dying priest confesses and recovers, Lisbon

Licentiate Tomás de Baez de Polanco had once been vicar general of Córdoba. He was dying in Lisbon, and his mind was so clouded that he could not remember his sins. His confessor left without hearing him. Saint Teresa’s hand relic was brought and laid on his head, and at once his mind cleared. He made a general confession with more peace of conscience than he had ever had, and then he recovered.

A boy with lethargy wakes, Medina del Campo

Antonio de Villarroel was the son of a town councillor of Medina del Campo. He had a grave sickness called modorra, a deep lethargy that took away his senses. The doctors lost hope, and his mother asked the Discalced nuns for a linen cloth with oil from Saint Teresa’s body. She laid it on the boy’s head for a quarter of an hour. Then he called his mother and sisters with joy and was fully well, to the amazement of the doctor and the nobles present.

His aunt Jerónima de la Encarnación swore at Toledo in 1595–96 that he lay ill at Medina del Campo. He did not eat or speak and seemed to have no senses. They laid on his head a sheet of Saint Teresa that the Medina nuns keep. At once he sat up in bed, asked for food, and recovered. Those who saw it were amazed, because they had no hope for his life.

Daily fainting fits end, Medina del Campo

Luisa de Ordas was sixteen and had trembling and fainting fits up to fifty times a day. No doctor knew the illness, and she was given the sacrament of the sick. The Discalced nuns of Medina told her mother to bring her to touch Saint Teresa’s small scapular, a cloth garment worn on the shoulders. The scapular was put on her, and the fits came back with full force for three hours. Then she was relieved. She had no fits for five years, and when they returned the scapular healed her again for good.

A crippled novice walks, Medina del Campo, 1586

Juana del Espíritu Santo was a novice at Medina, and she had had a fever for almost eighteen months. She also had sciatica and stiff nerves, and she could not hold a dish or move without the help of two nuns. On 1 January 1586 she was given a piece of Saint Teresa’s girdle. She felt such great pain that she thought she would die, and then another nun took her hand and lifted her. She stood up and walked down the hard stairs alone, and her fever was gone.

Juana de Torres Dantisco was once the novice Juana del Espíritu Santo, and she later became a nun at Madrid. She swore in 1610 that she was fourteen when she took the habit at Medina del Campo. The climate did not suit her, and she became crippled in her feet and hands. She had heart trouble, fainting, and a constant fever. For almost two years she could not leave her bed, and two nuns had to carry her. She had had more than forty purges and twice as many bleedings. The doctors said she would not recover unless she went back to the air of her native place. Three nights before the New Year she saw three nuns come into her cell, and she knew that one was Saint Teresa. Saint Teresa held a cross in one hand. She laid her hands on Juana’s face and said, “Take courage, your troubles will soon end.” Then a great fragrance remained, like that of Saint Teresa’s relics. On New Year’s Day they carried her to Holy Communion, and she fainted during the Mass. That afternoon the infirmarian laid a piece of the girdle of Saint Teresa’s mantle on her neck. She trembled, and the pains grew so strong that she wanted to take it off. A nun named Juana de Jesús told her, “Have faith and stay still. Who knows what limits God’s works have?” By four o’clock she felt no pain. Juana de Jesús urged her to try to stand, and she stood as lightly as if she had never been ill. They wept in each other’s arms. She walked down more than twenty stairs alone to ask a blessing of the prioress Ana de la Encarnación. The prioress asked, “What is this?” She answered in tears, “The mercies God has done me through our holy Mother.” The nuns took her to the choir and sang a Te Deum in thanks. That night Saint Teresa came again and took her by the left hand. Saint Teresa said she held her as a daughter and would help her when she needed it. María de San José swore at Madrid in 1595–96 that the nuns told her of that New Year’s Day of 1586. She said the novice gave thanks to God with many tears.

A prioress in a plague is healed, Granada

During a plague at Granada the prioress Blessed Anne of Jesus fell ill with blisters and fever. She slept with a piece of Saint Teresa’s habit. She woke well, as if she had never been ill.

Blessed Anne swore at Salamanca in 1597 that she felt such a pain under one arm, with fever, that she called two of the oldest nuns. She told them to let no one in, because she felt that the plague had struck her. They insisted on seeing the arm. It had a large dry scab, and fiery red streaks ran down the arm from it like welts. They laid a relic of Saint Teresa on the sore. She fell asleep and woke well, as if nothing had been wrong. Doña Catalina Ronquillo was a lady of Granada who knew of it. She had the same pain under her arm and was healed the same way.

Miracles in many kingdoms

The last of the articles says that miracles through Saint Teresa were being worked in Spain and in many other provinces and kingdoms. It says the witnesses believed these miracles were true and came from God, and not from any trick or evil pact. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 likewise says that many miracles were worked through her relics in quick succession while her cause went forward.

A palsied nun walks, Ávila, 1606

Inés de Jesús swore at Ávila in 1610 about Ana de San José, a nun of Saint Joseph’s. Ana had a violent palsy that shook her head or feet about eighteen hours a day. The bed made such a noise that it was heard on the floor below, and her groans carried far. She had a weak heart. Any small blow made her very ill, and a harder one made her bark like a dog. For nearly two years she could not feed herself. She had a constant fever that the doctors called hectic. On the feast of Saint John the Baptist in 1606 she begged to be carried to the hermitage of the Christ at the Column. A hermitage here is a small chapel in the convent garden, and Saint Teresa had had the Christ painted there. The prioress gave leave, and some nuns carried her in a chair. At the door of the hermitage she threw herself to the ground to crawl to the feet of the Christ. After she had crawled a little she felt great strength and breath. She stood and walked alone to the image. Then she walked without help to the other hermitages and climbed a steep stair of eight or nine steps. She ate and drank with her own hands. Inés de Jesús was then prioress. She came and found Ana standing at the door of the hermitage. The whole convent believed it was a miracle, and Inés said that Ana had better health afterward than in all her twenty-two years as a nun.

A nun’s growth dissolves, Ávila, 1609

Five witnesses swore at Ávila in 1610 about Magdalena de la Madre de Dios, a nun of Saint Joseph’s. Inés de Jesús and Ana de los Ángeles were nuns of the house. Miguel González Vaquero was the chaplain, and Luis Amador was the physician. Francisco Mena was a priest who served the nuns as chaplain and confessor. Magdalena had epileptic fits and a hard growth in the stomach that the doctors said was as big as a brick. Dr. Montemayor said it lay close to the heart and that a touch would kill her. She had been three months in bed and had been anointed. A raging frenzy followed, and for ten or eleven days she took no food or drink. She fainted for hours, and three or four nuns could not hold her. On 2 August 1609 she asked to be taken to the hermitage of the Christ at the Column. The nuns carried her in their arms, and they set the cloth with Saint Teresa’s blood in a niche facing the door. At the door Magdalena looked at the relic and said, “Let me go.” They let go, and she stood on her own. She walked quickly to the feet of the Christ and knelt through one or two litanies. She rose and said she felt well, only very weak. Between one and two o’clock she ate. At two she went to Vespers, and that night to Matins. After that she followed the community’s life in full health.

The growth stayed at first. A few days later Inés de Jesús and another nun took her to the hermitage again. Inés laid on the growth a cloth with the oil of Saint Teresa’s body. Before they left the hermitage Magdalena said she felt something dissolve within her. She wore the cloth for nine days. Then Dr. Amador examined her and said that no growth or hardness was left. He said that no natural cause could explain it, and that it was a miracle. Francisco Mena swore that he saw her well that same afternoon, though she had been given up.

A crippled boy walks, Ávila

Mencía Roberto was a nun of the Encarnación at Ávila. She swore in 1610 that about four years earlier a poor woman brought her little boy Francisco to the convent every day. He was about four, and his body was twisted like the letter S. He could not stand or crawl, and his mother had to carry him. The nuns said he had been born so, and a fall from a bench when he was tiny had made it worse. He felt no pain, and no one thought a doctor could help. Mencía made a novena of nine days. Each day she took him once to the cell where Saint Teresa had lived, where her picture hangs and a lamp burns. The boy began to crawl a little, but he stayed as he was. Some days later she made a second novena. On the last day he suddenly stood up well and walked through the whole convent with no one’s help. When they asked who had healed him he answered, “Mother Teresa of Jesus,” and said the name clearly though he stammered other words. He ran and played in the church with other children afterward. His mother was too poor to buy any remedy, and the nuns gave him none. María Coronel, Antonia de Guzmán, María del Castillo, Catalina de Velasco, and Ana María de Jesús swore to the same story.

An abbess’s sore eyes are healed, Ávila

Antonia Mejía was abbess of the Poor Clares of Santa María de Jesús at Ávila. She swore in 1610 that for years her eyes were bloodshot and watery. Every morning until eight o’clock tears ran down her cheeks and hindered her sight. Prime is the early morning prayer of the Church. She had to be called an hour before Prime and sit in bed wiping the matter from her eyes before she could read. At Matins the lights of the choir hurt her, and she could only pray with glasses. The remedies did nothing for her. She sent to the Discalced house of Saint Joseph for a relic. They sent a piece of a veil that Saint Teresa had worn and a little cloth that had touched her body, stained with the oil and sweet with its scent. She laid the cloth on her eyes with great faith. At once she was healed. That night she prayed Matins without glasses for the first time in many years, and the candlelight did not hurt her. She believed it was a miracle, because the doctors’ remedies had done nothing. The trouble never came back.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Antonia Mejía, Ávila, pp. 354–356, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

A possessed friar goes free, Ávila

Inés de Jesús swore at Ávila in 1610 to what happened about eighteen or twenty months earlier. A friar of the Order of Saint Paul came to the turn of the Discalced convent. He said that his brother had been possessed for more than six years and had suffered much. The brother was also a religious. The exorcists told the demon to leave. It said it would only go if a scapular of one of the daughters of “that Teresa” were brought. The friar asked for one at the turn. The prioress was Isabel de Santo Domingo, and she ordered that one be given. Later the friar came back to give thanks and said his brother was well. Inés heard this from the nun at the turn who gave the scapular and spoke with the friar.

Source: Silverio de Santa Teresa, ed., Procesos, vol. 2 (BMC 19), testimony of Inés de Jesús, Ávila, p. 550, https://archive.org/details/procesosdebeatif02silv.

A note on the sources

Historians read some of these accounts with care, and we record their questions here with respect. Several accounts come from later tellings. The notes in the editions of 1913 and 1924 give the Segovia night, the Monterrey child, and the vision of Lorenzo, and they take them from older Carmelite chronicles. Lady Lovat’s Life of 1912 gives the dove and the crystal globe, the sweet music, and the visits to Veas. Many healings are reported by the person healed or by a nun of the same house, and some witnesses told what they had heard from others. Editors give different years for the four days of coma. The annals of Lewis’s edition say 1539, and a note in the same edition can be read as 1538. Saint Teresa’s manuscript calls the angel with the spear one of the cherubim. Her confessor Báñez wrote “seraph” in the margin, and later editors followed him. Ribera heard that Saint Teresa spoke of her future convents during the four days, and he wrote that she herself called such talk nonsense.

The editor of Ribera’s Life identifies an unnamed nun of Alba as María de San Francisco. She told the accounts of the headache, the sense of smell, and the face like a full moon. Our sources do not say which miracles the Church approved for the beatification or the canonization, so none is marked here as approved. In 2024 two radiographers studied the relics. They described the body as mummified rather than literally incorrupt, and they found a severe curve of the spine. The Church’s own judgment stands above these questions: Pope Paul V beatified Saint Teresa in 1614, and Pope Gregory XV canonized her in 1622.
