Raw texts read for this check: Silverio's *Obras* vols 1, 5, 6 (Spanish), Ribera 1590, Silverio's *Procesos* I and III, Lewis's *Foundations* 1913, Stanbrook *Letters* vols 1–4, and the Vatican text of Paul VI's letter and homily. ## Block quotations (compared with the Spanish, or the Stanbrook English for letters)

1. Life ch.1 (road to the Moors): cuts had no ellipsis. Added `[...]` four times. "Only our parents seemed to stand in the way" overstated "el tener padres nos parecía el mayor embarazo". Now "Our parents seemed the greatest obstacle."
2. Life ch.9 (wounded Christ): a cut after "not to offend Him" had no ellipsis. Added `[...]`.
3. Life ch.29 (angel): a cut (the cherubim sentence) had no ellipsis, and the end dropped "ni se contenta el alma con menos que Dios". Added `[...]` and the closing clause.
4. Foundations ch.19 (Salamanca rain): the quote stopped before Teresa's own conclusion. Added "It moved me deeply, and I saw how much better that blessed man had done to trust in Our Lord than I had with my worry."
5. Letter CCI to Philip II: a clause was dropped without an ellipsis ("the confessors are subjects of the Apostolic Commissary…"). Added `[...]`. The quote began "He holds…" with no antecedent. The prose now names the friar the letter blames, Hernando Maldonado, prior at Ávila (Stanbrook heading).
6. Checked and left unchanged: Life ch.4 (leaving home), Life ch.36 (the house is not mine), Foundations ch.14 (Duruelo), Foundations ch.24 (fever and ferry), Ribera p.335 (daughter of the Church), the "Nada te turbe" lines.

## Order and fact errors fixed

- **Ferry and fever (ch.10).** Teresa says the ferry came "a little before, I do not know if it was two days" before the fever day. The draft put the river after the fever. Reordered, and the narration now says so.
- **Córdoba.** "with the crowd staring" was not in the text. Teresa says the sight of the nuns in their white mantles and veils startled everyone, and that the surprise took away her fever.
- **Nuns' joke at Toledo (ch.8).** The draft had the nuns ask "with a straight face". In Lewis and the Spanish, the nuns were sad, Teresa asked what the matter was, and they answered "What is the matter, Mother? We no longer seem to be poor." The card's "What should we have" was a mistranslation. Fixed, and the invented gesture is gone. The gold-jewels line now says "robbed of them", as in the source.
- **Salamanca (ch.8).** "The companion lay awake" was invented. Teresa says she kept looking about the room.
- **Andrada (ch.8).** "with the keys in his hand" and "from their beds" were embellishments. Removed.
- **Deathbed exchange (ch.13).** The draft said Juana del Espíritu Santo "remembered" her own line. The exchange is in the sworn dicho of Mariana de la Encarnación (Procesos I, p.89–90), and Juana's own dicho does not repeat it. Now attributed to Mariana. The Note on the Sources names her.
- **Verse and anointing order (ch.13).** Ribera has the psalm verse repeated before and after the anointing at nine. The draft said "through the night" and then "at nine that evening". Fixed.
- **Breviary poem "in her own hand" (ch.13).** Silverio says the poem was kept in her breviary, and that the hand in the breviary is Gracián's inscription. Now "She had kept a short poem of her own inside it."
- **"A young girl named Mariana was a pupil".** Mariana's deposition does not say pupil, and she calls herself one of the sisters. Now "lived in the house".
- **Excommunication (ch.11).** Letter CCII says the nuns "refused obedience" to the confirmed nun, and the friar excommunicated them. The draft said they were excommunicated for voting for Teresa. Fixed.
- **Letter CXCV (ch.11).** The devil might harm "us", not "them". Now "the Discalced".
- **John of the Cross's cottage (ch.11).** "The nuns had asked for Teresa years before" had no support. Letter CCI says only that the nuns wished her to come to them. Rewritten from the letter. John went first to the Ávila priory (the letter says "his priory") and later to Toledo. The draft sent him to Toledo before the letter.
- **Castro letter (ch.11).** Teresa "said" a Te Deum, not "sang" it. "She ended" the letter with the line was wrong (the letter ends after it). "He became her confessor" is not in the sources, only "she asked him". Fixed. "Teresita … young nun" changed to novice (professed 5 Nov 1582).
- **Grand Inquisitor (ch.9).** The raw text (Ribera's editor, quoting Gracián) shows the Inquisitor spoke to Teresa in March 1580, in Gracián's presence. Now attributed to Gracián's account and dated. "The Inquisition did not have to search for long" was invented. Removed.
- **Princess and the Inquisition (ch.9).** The servants-and-Inquisition link rests on Zimmerman quoting the *Reforma* (1644). Now attributed ("Carmelite histories say").
- **Gracián's age.** Born June 1545, so twenty-nine in spring 1575, not thirty.
- **Run-away walk (ch.1).** "On the far side" an uncle met them: not in the sources. Mother "searching the whole house": Ribera says "por todas partes". Fixed.
- **Coma year.** "In the summer of 1537" now "about 1537" (the plan and the card both hedge it).
- **Coma details (ch.3).** "into her ear" (Teresa says only that they said the Creed to her), "wax on her eyelids" (Spanish "en los ojos"), and "the nuns dug her grave" (Teresa says only that the grave stood open). Fixed. "A nun who nursed her" became "A nun of the Incarnation later testified": the notes do not say Ana nursed her.
- **Wounded Christ chapter (ch.4).** "Still she went back to the parlour" had no source. Removed. "the plainest thing she ever said about prayer" softened.
- **Confessors (ch.5).** The draft said the visions frightened the confessors "more than" Teresa, then said she feared being fooled "more than they did". Both now say she feared it as much. "Talked it over" and "did not resent the doubters" were invented. "something she kept all her life" about Báñez's advice was invented. The unsourced motive "A nun who is talked about … has a choice" is gone (the card says the order to keep the old rule belongs to the next chapter).
- **Choir raptures (ch.6).** Teresa's "nunca más hasta ahora lo he tenido; verdad es que ha poco" had its qualifier dropped. Restored.
- **Constitutions.** "Teasing was allowed if it was kind" is now "done with good sense" ("con discreción"). Rubeo "read" the Constitutions became "she showed him".
- **Way of Perfection.** Now "very probably at the end of 1565".
- **Duruelo (ch.7).** "In the morning" and John saying Mass dropped (the notes give the night in prayer and Zimmerman's first Mass on 28 Nov; the two did not fit). "until dawn" became "until the morning office" (Prime). "She did not insist" was invented. "Of the day at Duruelo" became "Of Duruelo". I added one sentence explaining Discalced and Calced at first use, with no new facts beyond the notes' "sandals".
- **Boiled greens (ch.12).** "and there was nothing else" was not in Ribera. Now "but she ate it".
- **Calendar (ch.13).** The bull was of February 1582 and took effect in October. "That same autumn Pope Gregory XIII corrected the calendar" now says "That October the calendar changed".
- **Montoya cure (ch.14).** The process text says "martes a diez de febrero 1603", but 10 Feb 1603 was a Monday in the Gregorian calendar. Weekdays removed.
- **Paul VI homily (ch.15).** The Italian text only "invites" everyone to say "siamo figli della Chiesa". The draft said he asked the whole basilica to say it with him. Corrected. Placement at the end of the Italian part also checked.
- **Closing paragraph.** "obeyed every superior she was given" was absolute; now "even against her own visions". "sitting up in bed" while saying the line is not in Ribera. "founded seventeen houses" is now "of nuns". "One of those houses gave her no laughter at all" is now "brought her real grief".
- **Other.** "Luis de León printed" is now "published". "Her reform crossed the sea with her relics" is now "spread beyond Spain" (Genoa, Rome, Naples, Paris were not tied to relics in the sources).

## Sources note and counts

- Teresa's books "give almost no dates" became "few dates". "Most editions" became "later editors". "Neither is in any of her writings" became "We found neither in her writings."
- **Báñez at the council.** The card and plan say the claim was "not found" in Báñez's deposition because the scan has a gap. The raw text of Procesos I p.8 does say it: "entonces solo este testigo estuvo de su parte … y así lo dijo este testigo en público consistorio de Ávila". The Note on the Sources said it was not found, which is false. It now says Báñez swore he alone took her side, that Teresa's account gives the part to a priest the bishop sent, and that the two are hard to fit together. The story text still keeps Teresa's account and does not name Báñez as lone defender. The "hairs on her head" line is on the same page of the same deposition, so it stands.
- The Note now names Mariana de la Encarnación (deathbed exchange), María de San Francisco (full moon), and Lovat (1912 "never could").
- "Quotations … from the original text" was untrue for the letters and for several Foundations passages put into English from Lewis (plan.md). Rewritten to say Spanish where reached, Lewis and Stanbrook otherwise, and letters from Stanbrook only.
- "Twenty-six sources" matches the list below it.
- `summary.txt`: "Four centuries later" now "Nearly four centuries later" (about 390 years from the late 1570s).
