Raw texts read: Procesos vols 1, 2, 3 (Spanish OCR from archive.org), Ribera 1908 (Spanish), Lewis's Life and Foundations, the Stanbrook Interior Castle and Letters vol. 4, Lovat 1912, Silverio's Obras vol. 5, Butler vol. 10, the Decretum Rotae, Aleteia 2024. ## The reviser's corrections

- **Coma "1537–1539": partly wrong.** Lewis's annals put the four days in 1539 (July). A note in the same edition puts her in her father's house from July 1538. The Life gives no year. The title now has no year, and the text says the annals place it in 1539 and the Life gives none. The biography still says "about 1537". That is a mismatch for the owner (biography.html not touched). "A grave stood open" is right (Life ch. 5 §19: a day and a half).
- **Dove, star, visitors split among nuns: right.** But the reviser wrote "Ribera wrote". The passage is the editor's note (p. 337) in the 1908 edition, citing Yepes and the depositions. Fixed in m20 and m22.
- **Ana asked God to take Teresa at once: right** (Procesos I p. 170).
- **Rome brief: right.** The brief was to bring the body back from Alba to Ávila (p. 171). Worded so.
- **Candles at Toledo, July 1595: right** (Juana del Espíritu Santo, 14 July 1595, p. 253). The sacristan is named, and "one witness" is dropped.
- **Mendoza: right.** Fixed "one day out". The Lewis note says only that a Sunday did not fall between her arrival and the feast. The Mass was Sunday 15 August 1568.
- **"Probably María de San Francisco": right, and it is the editor's inference** ("Sería sin duda", p. 336). Now said so in m17, m18, m19.

## Errors found and fixed

- **Wrong witness.** On Procesos I pp. 305–316 the deposition is María del Nacimiento, not María de San José (María de San José starts at p. 317). Fixed in the rain at Salamanca (she did not remember the house) and in the nephew (the priest's version). María de San José's own nephew version, from Juana de Ahumada's memorial (pp. 323–324), is added to m11. The Cuerva cures also belong to María del Nacimiento, not María de San José (see additions).
- **m03 (Christ stern and grave).** "Annals date it 1542" is false: the annals do not date it, and Teresa says "more than twenty-six years" before she wrote. The notes' "in the parlour" is not in the text. Title and text fixed.
- **m42 (Rome 1602).** Gracián writes only of fragrance and the portrait on the altar. The healing of Baronio's niece is Silverio's statement. Reattributed.
- **m35 (Lorenzo).** "Timing does not match her letters" was wrong. The note's dates fit the letters (died two days after Saint John's day). Sentence removed.
- **m39.** The cloth was "put on" her for the flow of blood, not "under" her.
- **m26.** The superiors "ordered silence" (censures). They did not "swear the nuns".
- **m43.** The nun told good smells from bad, not "different scents".
- **m40.** The arm "sweated" like oil. It was not the whole body.
- **m34.** Saint Teresa wrote what those who saw it told her.
- **m27 sources.** The March 1592 opening is on p. 85, not pp. 90–91.
- **m14.** Added María del Nacimiento's own sworn words (p. 315); the blank page is Mariana of the Angels's report of her.
- **m23 and m28 upgraded** from Lovat alone to sworn Alba testimony of 1610. Catalina de San Ángelo saw the dry tree in flower on 5 October, p. 205. She and Inés de Jesús tell of the heart: removed during Bishop Manrique's inquiry, burst on one side, and felt to beat in the prioress's hand (pp. 185–186, 207–208). "We did not find when it was removed" is answered. m19 gets Catalina de San Ángelo as a second witness.
- **m22.** The articles (art. 95) add that Saint Teresa appeared to Catalina de Jesús that night and cured her.

## Rule 2 (trust the tradition)

Removed from bodies: "legend", "marks of legend", "mostly hearsay", "we found it only there", "we did not read his list", "we did not find whether the Church approved", "not a Church finding", "one witness". The intro no longer says that doubtful stories are marked. A short closing note, "A note on the sources", holds the historians' points: later tellings, second-hand reports, the coma year, and the 2024 "mummified" remark (moved from m27). The intro still says that no account is marked as approved, because no source names one.

## Rule 3 (Saint)

"Saint" is now before every "Teresa", "Joseph", "Michael", "Peter", "John", "Catherine of Siena", and "Dominic". "Blessed" is before Ana de San Bartolomé and Anne of Jesus (as in the biography). Source lines and the titles of works are unchanged. Titles are 8 words or fewer. Left plain: Pope Paul V, Pope Gregory XV, Gracián, Báñez, Yepes, Ribera, Cardinal Baronio, Silverio, all other nuns (Ana de los Ángeles, Ana de la Trinidad, Mariana de Jesús, and so on). No name was left plain out of doubt.
