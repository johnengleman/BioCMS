# Fact sheet: Saint Teresa of Ávila (checked 2026-10-06, after the middle revision of the biography)

The teachings essay and the miracle list must use these names, dates, numbers, and wordings exactly. "n." means the note number in the current `biography.html` (130 notes). Where a later writer finds a source that disagrees, list the conflict for the checker. Page numbers of Ribera and Procesos are plus or minus one (OCR order). "Obras" is Silverio's 1915 edition.

## Names and titles

- **Saint Teresa of Jesús (Teresa de Jesús, of Ávila).** Born Teresa de Cepeda y Ahumada, Ávila, **28 March 1515**. Father Alonso Sánchez de Cepeda; mother Beatriz de Ahumada, his second wife (Butler vol.10 p.309; n.6). Three children by his first marriage and nine by his second (Butler; Silverio). She took the name "of Jesus" when she went to live at Saint Joseph's (Lewis, 1563; Ribera says from the founding; both in the Note on the Sources).
- Brothers: **Rodrigo** (the one of the road to the Moors; died a captain in the Río de la Plata, Ribera p.96). No age is given for him: Teresa says "almost my age", Ribera and Silverio say four years older. **Antonio** (younger; went with her to the Incarnation, Ribera p.103). **Lorenzo** (born 1519; went to the Indies in 1540; returned August 1575; died **26 June 1580**; n.82, 96). Half-sister **María de Cepeda**, married 1531. Sister **Juana** (and her husband **Juan de Ovalle**). Niece **Teresita** (Lorenzo's daughter).
- **The Incarnation** (convent in Ávila): entered **2 November 1535**, aged twenty years and seven months (Ribera p.103). **Saint Joseph's** (San José, Ávila): founded **24 August 1562** (feast of Saint Bartholomew); habit given by **Gaspar Daza** (a priest sent by the bishop), with her cousins **Inés and Ana de Tapia**, nuns of the Incarnation, present (Silverio n. to Vida ch.36 p.305). Four poor women took the habit.
- **Saint John of the Cross**: then **John of Saint Matthias**, a young Carmelite friar from Salamanca; first friar of the reform; reached Duruelo at the end of September 1568. **Antonio de Heredia**, later **Antonio of Jesus**: prior at Medina, about fifty-eight, reached Duruelo **27 November 1568**. First Mass at Duruelo: **Sunday 28 November 1568** (the editor's date; Teresa could not remember which Sunday of Advent). The porch "of Bethlehem".
- **Jerónimo Gracián** (Fray Jerónimo Gracián of the Mother of God): born 5 or 6 June 1545 (so twenty-nine in spring 1575); first Provincial of the Discalced (elected at Alcalá, **3 March 1581**); expelled **17 February 1592**; died Brussels **21 September 1614**.
- **Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé** (Teresa's young companion, lay sister; in whose arms she died; beatified 1917). **Blessed Anne of Jesus** (Ana de Jesús: a different nun; in Salamanca in 1573; first prioress at Paris, arrived 15 October 1604). Never shorten either to "Blessed Ana" without the full name at first use in a new text.
- Popes and others: **Pope Saint Paul VI** (Doctor, 27 September 1970); **Gregory XIII** (calendar, 1582; brief of 22 June 1580); **Gregory XV** (canonized 12 March 1622); **Paul V** (beatified 24 April 1614); **Pius XI** (1922, "supreme teacher of contemplation"). **Felipe Sega**, the Pope's envoy who came in **1577** (after Ormaneto died in June 1577). **Father Rubeo** (head of the whole Order; in Ávila **April 1567**). **Domingo Báñez** (her Dominican confessor). **Francisco de Ribera** (first biographer, 1590). **Luis de León** (first edition of her works, **1588**).
- **Princess of Éboli** (Ana de Mendoza); husband **Ruy Gómez de Silva**, died **29 July 1573**. Convent at **Pastrana**, founded **1569**, moved to **Segovia** (arrived Holy Week **1574**).
- **Teresa de Laíz**, founder of the convent at Alba (asked for the wall over the coffin, Procesos I p.84).

## Dates and numbers

- Age seven: road to the Moors (Ribera "six or seven"; Silverio 7). Mother died about 1528 (Teresa "about twelve, or a little less").
- Boarding school with the Augustinian nuns: **1531**, a year and a half. Coma and grave: about **1537** (marked "about"); more than eight months in bed; crippled almost three years.
- Father died **24 December 1543** (Silverio; Ribera's editor 1 February 1544). Wounded Christ: Lent about **1554 or 1555**. Saint Peter's feast, Christ at her side: **29 June 1557**. Angel: about **1559**. Vision of hell: after the angel in her book; editors 1558 or 1559.
- First draft of the *Life*: finished **June 1562** (note at the end of the manuscript). Rubeo's words: "as many monasteries as she had hairs on her head" (Báñez, Procesos I p.8).
- Medina del Campo **1567** (second convent). Toledo **1569** (licence delayed more than two months; first night 14 May 1569). Salamanca **1570** (arrived 31 October, eve of All Saints). Salamanca move: eve of Saint Michael **1573**. Seville: left Veas **18 May 1575**, reached Seville **26 May 1575**; opening **3 June 1576**.
- *Interior Castle*: begun **2 June 1577** (Trinity Sunday), about four weeks of work in two parts, a break of five months, finished **29 November 1577**.
- Arrest of Saint John of the Cross: night of **3 December 1577**; her letter to the King **4 December 1577**; his escape on the feast of the Assumption, **August 1578**; nine months in the cell. Arm broken at Christmas **1577**.
- Quiroga's remark about the book: March **1580**. Brief for the Discalced province: **22 June 1580**. Alcalá chapter: **3 March 1581**. Book kept by the Inquisition about twelve years.
- Pedro de Castro's letter and her answer: **19 November 1581**. Saint John's visit to Ávila: late November **1581** (eve of Saint Andrew).
- Burgos left summer **1582**; Valladolid farewell **15 September 1582**; Medina; reached Alba on the eve of Saint Matthew (**20 September**). Last Communion 29 September (Saint Michael). Last anointing at nine, **3 October**; died about **nine in the evening, Thursday 4 October 1582**, aged sixty-seven. The next day was counted as **15 October** (calendar change); feast **15 October**.
- Alba: body opened **4 July 1583** (Gracián); taken to Ávila **November 1585**; back at Alba **23 August 1586**. Isabel de Montoya: relic touched **10 February 1603**, sight returned, walked to Communion **21 February**.
- Procesos: more than **four hundred** witnesses, **1591 to 1610**. Doctor of the Church: Paul VI announced **15 October 1967**; Congregation of Rites **20 December 1967**; Pope approved **21 March 1968**; cardinals' vote **15 July 1969**; Pope approved **21 July 1969**; proclaimed **Sunday 27 September 1970**, Saint Peter's. First woman Doctor.
- **Seventeen** houses of nuns by 1582, Saint Joseph's included: the witnesses say seventeen monasteries of nuns, fifteen founded by her in person and two by sending nuns (Procesos I p.23, Ana de la Encarnación); Santi e Beati says the first foundation was followed by sixteen others. The text says "By then there were seventeen houses of her nuns" when she left Burgos (n.100). The opening no longer gives a count.

## Added in the middle revision (note numbers are the current ones)

- **Parlour (n.20 to 23).** Christ appears "with great severity" while she talks with a person she had only begun to know; the place, the parlour, is Silverio's tradition and Lewis's annals (1542). The friend's sex is not given in the Spanish. The toad is seen by the two of them and by others. The old nun who warns her is a relative. She saw Christ more than twenty-six years before she wrote. Her words: "But as it was not to my liking, I made myself deny it"; "Oh greatness of God, with what care and pity You were warning me in every way, and how little good it did me!"
- ***Veni Creator* (n.38).** Her first rapture (Silverio dates it 1558; the text gives no year). The confessor is a Jesuit (Baltasar Álvarez, per Silverio; not named in the text). Words: "I no longer want you to talk with men, but with angels."
- **Duruelo licence (n.55 to 56).** The General's licence allowed two houses of friars, with the consent of two Calced superiors (the present and the former Provincial; Fray Alonso González and Fray Ángel de Salazar). Antonio answered that he would live there "in a pigsty too" (no quotation marks in the text). Saint John went with her to Valladolid. "He was so good that I, at least, could have learned far more from him than he from me." The bishop of Ávila and his sister Doña María de Mendoza helped win the former Provincial.
- **The Incarnation, 1571 to 1574 (n.67 to 68, 73).** Sent as prioress in 1571 by the apostolic visitor Fray Pedro Fernández, a Dominican; the nuns had not chosen her; Te Deum; Our Lady's image in the prioress's seat; Saint John of the Cross as confessor of the house. Her three years ended 6 October 1574. Many of the nuns, though not all, wished to elect her again. Both scenes come from her English editors (Lewis and Zimmerman, introduction pp.XIX to XXIII, XXXIX).
- **Seville, 1575 (n.79, 83 to 85).** The Calced prior of Seville (Miguel de Ulloa) asks by what authority the convent was opened and for a copy of the General's letter; she refuses the copy. The decree of the general chapter at Piacenza (1575): found no more houses, do not leave the house she chooses; she calls it "a kind of prison"; Father Rubeo is displeased; two other false charges. Her joy, and King David before the ark. Gracián's memory: she rubbed her palms together for joy. Gracián kept her in Seville until the house was settled.
- **Saint John's escape (n.92 to 93).** On the feast of the Assumption, August 1578, from the priory at Toledo; Our Lady shows him the window; a rope too short and weak; he hides in the nuns' infirmary at Toledo (Stanbrook vol.3 Letter CCXLI n.3). "I do not know how God can allow such things" (Letter CCXLVI, end of August 1578).
- **Gracián confined (n.94).** In the Calced priory at Madrid, then at Alcalá; her letter to his mother, Juana Dantisco, 28 December 1578.
- **The body taken from Alba (n.118 to 119).** November 1585: the Provincial (Fray Gregorio Nacianceno) comes with Gracián to hold an election; secrecy under Church penalties; Juana del Espíritu Santo holds the body; the nuns are sent to Matins at an unusual hour; the prioress, Juana, and María de San Francisco stay (Mariana de Jesús; Juana herself says three stayed with her, and the text follows Mariana); the order is read; the nuns reach the Gospel, smell the fragrance, hurry down; the arm that lacked its hand is cut off and left at Alba.

## Places and spellings

Ávila, Alba de Tormes, Medina del Campo, Duruelo, Toledo, Salamanca, Pastrana, Segovia, Veas, Seville, Córdoba, Burgos, Valladolid, Palencia, Peñaranda, Guadalquivir (the ferry). Incarnation, Saint Joseph's, Santa María de Gracia (Augustinian nuns). Calced and Discalced (unshod; they wore sandals).

## Approved wordings of quotations (use these exactly)

- **Leaving home (n.12, Vida ch.4):** "I remember clearly, and it is true, that the grief I felt when I left my father's house will not, I think, be greater when I die. It felt as if every bone in me was pulling away from the others."
- **The coma (n.16, Ribera p.111):** her father said, "This daughter is not for burying."
- **Saint Joseph (n.19, Vida ch.6):** "He did what he does. He made it possible for me to get up and walk and not be crippled. And I did what I do. I misused the favour."
- **The long struggle (n.29 to 30):** "On one side God was calling me. On the other, I was following the world." "I did not enjoy God, and I was not content in the world."
- **The wounded Christ (n.32, Vida ch.9):** "I threw myself down beside Him, weeping hard, begging Him to give me strength once and for all not to offend Him. [...] I think I told Him then that I would not get up from there until He did what I asked."
- **Fear of being deceived (n.36, n.39):** "It gave me such fear and pain that I did not know what to do. All I could do was weep." To Báñez: "For the love of God, Father, do not be so free of fear. You make me afraid. Look again. I would not want to deceive you."
- **The angel (n.40, Vida ch.29):** "He seemed to thrust it several times through my heart, down to my entrails. When he drew it out, it seemed he took them with him and left me all on fire with great love of God." She called it "a caress of love between the soul and God". "I ask His goodness to let anyone who thinks I am lying taste it."
- **The call to Saint Joseph's (n.43 to 44):** "I was so very happy in the house where I was, because it was much to my liking, and so was my cell." Christ's promise: Saint Joseph at one door, Our Lady at the other; "a star that gave great light".
- **The lawsuit (n.48, n.50):** Christ: "Do you not know that I am almighty? What are you afraid of?" Teresa: "Lord, this house is not mine. It was made for You. Now that there is no one left to plead for it, You do it."
- **Poverty at Toledo (n.62):** "I left very glad, for it seemed I had everything while I had nothing." The nuns: "What should be wrong, Mother? We no longer seem to be poor."
- **The kitchen (n.69, Fund. ch.5):** "The Lord walks among the pots and pans, helping you inside and out."
- **Obedience (n.77):** "I gave in at once. Our Lord gives me this grace, that my superiors always seem to me to be right."
- **Seville (n.81):** "I never saw myself so faint-hearted and cowardly in my life. Truly, I did not know myself."
- **Saint John (n.93, n.90):** "Fray John saw no one during his captivity. I envy him intensely. Thank God for giving him courage for such martyrdom!" Of Saint John: "as in my opinion he is" a saint.
- **Letter to Philip II (n.89):** "It grieves me greatly. I would rather have seen our fathers held by the Moors, who might show more mercy."
- **Castro (n.98):** "My ill-doings have benefited your Reverence, and well they may, since you see that I am not in hell as I have so often deserved to be." "I have never been so happy as I am tonight."
- **Alba, the figs (n.103 to 104, Ribera pp.333 to 334):** "Do not worry about me, child; these figs are very good. Many poor people will not have such a treat." "God help me, how tired I feel. It is more than twenty years since I went to bed early. Now I have."
- **To her nuns (n.107):** "My daughters and my ladies, for the love of God I ask you to take great care to keep the Rule and the Constitutions. Do not look at the bad example this bad nun has given you, and forgive me for it."
- **At the Sacrament (n.109 to 110):** "Oh my Lord and my Spouse, the hour I have longed for has come. It is time now for us to be together, my God." "In the end, Lord, I am a daughter of the Church." Psalm verse, above all: "A broken and humbled heart, O God, You will not despise."
- **Her grave (n.111):** "Am I to have anything of my own? Will they not give me a little earth here?" Juana del Espíritu Santo: "You do well, Mother. This house and all of them are yours, and Christ in this life had no house of His own." Teresa: "Oh, how well you put it, daughter!" (Mariana de la Encarnación, Procesos I pp.89 to 90).
- **The poem (n.122, Poesías IX):** "Let nothing trouble you, let nothing frighten you. All things pass. God does not change. Patience gains everything. Whoever has God lacks nothing. God alone is enough." She kept it in her breviary.
- **Paul VI (n.128, n.130):** "To Saint Teresa of Jesus we have conferred, or rather we have recognized, the title of Doctor of the Church." Spanish: "En fin, soy hija de la Iglesia."

## Things the text does not say (so no later text may)

- No age for Rodrigo; no date for the *Way of Perfection*; no weekday for the Montoya cure (10 February 1603 was a Monday in the new calendar, the process says Tuesday).
- The devil throwing her down the stairs, and Lorenzo seen in glory, rest on later footnotes and are left out.
- The popular sayings "Christ has no body now but yours" and "More tears are shed over answered prayers than unanswered ones" are not in her writings.
- Saint Teresa does not say that she, not the sisters, thought of hell in the carriages (Fund. ch.24 p.208).
- No year for the *Veni Creator* rapture; no name for the confessor of that rapture; no sex for the friend of the parlour scene.
- The 1571 scene at the Incarnation and Saint John's escape come from her English editors; no text may add detail to them.
