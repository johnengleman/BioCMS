## SC-06 — St Joseph's opens, 24 August 1562
- Status: full
- When and where: St Bartholomew's Day, 24 Aug 1562, Ávila. The house was her brother-in-law's (Juan de Ovalle), bought to hide the plan. Teresa belonged to the Incarnation convent and was there with leave of her superiors [Life-L ch.36 §2, §4; Ribera end of Libro I, with note].
- Who was there: Teresa; four novices (Antonia de Henao, María de la Paz, Úrsula de los Santos, María de Ávila, sister of Julián de Ávila); two Incarnation nuns, her cousins Inés and Ana de Tapia; priest Gaspar Daza, who gave the habit and reserved the Sacrament; witnesses Gonzalo de Aranda, Francisco de Salcedo, Julián de Ávila, Juan de Ovalle and Juana de Ahumada [Obras1 ch.36 n.2, citing the foundation record; Ribera note].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Four poor women took the habit and the Sacrament was placed in the house; the four were taken without dowry [Life-L ch.36 §4-5].
  2. Teresa felt "as if in bliss". About three or four hours later a fit of doubt struck her: had she disobeyed the Provincial, would the nuns bear the strict life, had she done a silly thing? She went before the Sacrament, could not pray, and said she was like one in the agony of death. She promised to seek leave to enter the house; the doubt left at once [Life-L ch.36 §5-9].
  3. She rested a little after dinner. News had spread; the prioress sent for her at once. She went, leaving the nuns in distress, expecting prison [Life-L ch.36 §11].
  4. The Provincial came, rebuked her, and she asked pardon without defending herself. She was then told to explain before the nuns; the Provincial was satisfied and promised leave to live in the house once the city was quiet [Life-L ch.36 §12-14].
  5. Two or three days later the governor, council members and Chapter met and resolved the monastery must not exist. All the orders sent two learned men each; most were silent or against. One Dominican said it should not be dissolved but studied, since the matter was the bishop's [Life-L ch.36 §14-15].
  6. Teresa was in distress two days. Christ told her: "Do you not know I am Almighty? What are you afraid of?" [Life-L ch.36 §16].
  7. A priest sent by the Bishop "stood alone against all" at a great meeting and won a short respite; Teresa says this priest gave the habit and reserved the Sacrament. The attack lasted about six months [Life-L ch.36 §18].
- Details: house tiny and poor; the Bishop's servant Juan Carrillo says the women stood behind board screens and mats because there was no enclosure yet [Proc1, Carrillo; facts only]. Carrillo says the city council, all the orders and many private people rose against the house that same day, and that the Carmelites of the mitigated rule took Teresa back to the Incarnation that night [Proc1, Carrillo; hearsay of one witness, and he is unsure of the year: "one of the said two years or the year sixty-four"]. Teresa herself gives 1562 and says nothing of a night recall.
- Words spoken: Christ: "¿No sabes que soy poderoso? ¿de qué temes?" (Do you not know that I am almighty? What are you afraid of?) [Obras1 Vida ch.36; Life-L ch.36 §16]. Teresa to Christ, the day before the Provincial came: "Señor, esta casa no es mía, por Vos se ha hecho; ahora que no hay nadie que negocie, hágalo Vuestra Majestad." (Lord, this house is not mine. It was made for you. Now that no one is left to argue for it, you do it.) [Obras1 Vida ch.36].
- Inner life: "Hice mi culpa, como muy culpada" — plain: "I admitted my fault as if I were very guilty." She says she thought of Christ's trial and saw her own was nothing [Obras1 ch.36; Life-L §12].
- Testimony (Teresa, Spanish, Obras1 *Vida* ch.36; public-domain): "Yo me fui a Dios y díjele: Señor, esta casa no es mía, por Vos se ha hecho; ahora que no hay nadie que negocie, hágalo Vuestra Majestad. Quedaba tan descansada y tan sin pena, como si tuviera a todo el mundo que negociara por mí, y luego tenía por siguro el negocio."
  plain: "I turned to God and said: Lord, this house is not mine. It was made for you. Now that there is no one left to plead for it, you do it. I was left as much at rest and free of worry as if the whole world were pleading for me, and at once I took the matter as safe."
- What changed: the house survived the first storm, but it stayed under suit and city anger for months.
- Not in the sources: any speech by Báñez here. The outline's claim that Báñez "alone defended it before the city council" (W-S3 p.8) is NOT found: in the Proc1 scan Báñez's deposition has a gap between its printed p.7 and p.17, and what is there says only that she met opposition from scholars and laymen and that her works "passed through the crucible of the Holy Office" [Proc1, Báñez, 16 Oct 1591]. Teresa names a priest (Daza) who stood alone; do not name Báñez. Nothing on weather, crowds, or a ceremony of bells. The "head of the Order tells her to found more" is not in my sources.
- Sources: Life-L, Obras1, Ribera, Proc1 (Báñez; Carrillo). Ribera's page number was not confirmed; the passage is the end of Libro I, just before Libro II.

## SC-07 — A friar sweeping the door, Lent 1569 (Duruelo)
- Status: full
- When and where: Lent 1569, on her way to Toledo; she arrived one morning at Duruelo. The first Mass in the "porch of Bethlehem" was on the first Sunday of Advent 1568 (28 Nov, Zimmerman from the monastery's own book; she herself says "first or second Sunday, I do not remember") [Found-L ch.14 §5 and n.; Obras5 ch.14 and n.2].
- Who was there: Teresa; Fray Antonio of Jesus (Heredia); two merchant friends who had come with her from Medina; John of the Cross lived there (Zimmerman note); a friar of the mitigated rule, too sick to change habit; a young friar not yet ordained [Found-L ch.14 §5-6 and n.].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She arrived one morning; Antonio was sweeping the church door with a joyful face [§5].
  2. She asked what had become of his dignity; he answered he cursed the time he had any [§5].
  3. Inside, she was amazed at the spirit there; the two merchants did nothing but cry at the crosses and skulls [§5].
  4. She noticed a small wooden cross by the holy water with a paper Christ pasted on it, which moved her more than fine work [§6].
  5. The choir was the garret, so low one had to stoop to hear Mass; two hay-filled hermitages in the corners; stones as pillows; crosses and skulls [§6].
  6. She learned that after Matins they stayed in prayer until Prime and once went to Prime with snow on their habits without noticing [§6].
- Details: cold place; hay; stone pillows; the roof almost touched their heads [§6]. Teresa says she once asked them not to be so hard on themselves and they made light of it (§11, not read in full; see notes F-E1).
- Words spoken: Teresa: "¿Qué es esto, mi padre? ¿qué se ha hecho la honra?" Antonio: "Yo maldigo el tiempo que la tuve." (What is this, Father? What has become of your honor? / I curse the time when I had it.) [Obras5 ch.14]. Note: Lewis renders honra as "dignity"; "honor" is closer.
- Inner life: she says she was glad the house stood there, and that the friars gained a good name quickly [§7].
- Testimony (Teresa, Spanish, Obras5 *Fundaciones* ch.14; public-domain): "Llegué una mañana; estaba el Padre Fray Antonio de Jesús barriendo la puerta de la iglesia, con un rostro de alegría que tiene él siempre. Yo le dije: «¿qué es esto, mi padre? ¿qué se ha hecho la honra?» Dijome estas palabras, diciéndome el gran contento que tenía: «Yo maldigo el tiempo que la tuve». Como entré en la ilesita, quedéme espantada de ver el espíritu que el Señor había puesto allí. Y no era yo sola, que dos mercaderes que habían venido de Medina hasta allí conmigo, que eran mis amigos, no hacían otra cosa si no llorar. ¡Tenía tantas cruces! ¡tantas calaveras!"
  plain: "I arrived one morning. Father Antonio of Jesus was sweeping the church door, with the joyful face he always has. I said to him: 'What is this, Father? What has become of your honor?' He answered, telling me how glad he was: 'I curse the time I had any.' When I went into the little church I was amazed at the spirit the Lord had put there. And I was not alone: two merchants, friends of mine, who had come with me from Medina, did nothing but cry. There were so many crosses, so many skulls!"
  (Orthography as printed: "ilesita" is the OCR/edition spelling for "iglesita".)
- What changed: she saw the men's reform alive and working; she went on to Toledo.
- Not in the sources: Teresa's feelings in her own words beyond "amazed"; John of the Cross speaking in this scene (the notes place him there but not in this passage); the date of the day within Lent.
- Sources: Found-L ch.14, Obras5 ch.14.

## SC-08 — Three ducats and a student's key, May 1569 (Toledo)
- Status: full
- When and where: Toledo, 1569. She met the Council's administrator on 8 May in a church near his house; Mass in the new house, 14 May (feast of St Boniface); Zimmerman, from the *Reforma* [Found-L ch.15 nn.9, 11].
- Who was there: Teresa and two nuns (Isabel of St Dominic, Isabel of St Paul, per Zimmerman); the administrator (Don Gómez Tello Girón); a poor student, Andrada, sent by the Franciscan Martín de la Cruz; Doña Luisa de la Cerda, in whose house they lodged; later Don Pedro Manrique [Found-L ch.15 §5-12 and nn.].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Permission had been blocked more than two months (a deal with a wealthy heir had failed). She went to a church near the administrator's house, asked to speak to him, told him it was hard that women wanting to live austerely were hindered by people who lived at ease; "with a certain firmness". He gave the licence before she left [§5-6].
  2. She had perhaps three or four ducats and bought two pictures on canvas (she had no image for the altar), two straw mattresses and a blanket [§6].
  3. Her friend and promised guarantor Alonso de Ávila fell ill. She remembered the poor student; her companions laughed and told her not to [§6-7].
  4. Early next morning he told her he had found a house and had the keys; they went and saw it [§8].
  5. She had possession taken at once. When he asked what furniture to send, she said two straw mattresses and a blanket; her companions were vexed [§9].
  6. At nightfall they entered with a hand-bell borrowed (the one rung at the elevation) and cleaned all night in great fear. At dawn they opened a door in the wall to a neighbouring house of women, who rose in terror [§9-10].
  7. For some days they had only the mattresses and blanket. On the first day "not a withered leaf to fry a sardine"; someone laid a faggot of wood in the church [§13].
- Details: cold night; they covered themselves with the blanket and their serge cloaks; Zimmerman adds that Mother Isabel of St Dominic, asked for a covering, answered with humor that she was already wearing all there was, the two companions' cloaks (Lanuza, via Silverio's note) [Found-L ch.15 §13; Obras5 ch.15 n.2].
- Words spoken: the nuns, when the poverty ended: "¿Qué hemos de haber, madre? que ya no parece somos pobres." (What should we have, Mother? We no longer seem to be poor.) [Obras5 ch.15; Found-L §14].
- Inner life: "como si tuviera muchas joyas de oro, y me las llevaran y dejaran pobre: ansí sentía pena de que se nos iba acabando la pobreza" — plain: "it was as if I had owned many gold jewels and they were taken and I was left poor: so I grieved that our poverty was ending." [Obras5 ch.15].
- Testimony (Teresa, Spanish, Obras5 *Fundaciones* ch.15; public-domain): "Yo me fui muy contenta, que me parecía ya lo tenía todo, sin tener nada; porque debían ser hasta tres u cuatro ducados lo que tenía, con que compré dos lienzos (porque nenguna cosa tenía de imagen para poner en el altar) y dos jergones y una manta." And: "aun aquel día ni una seroja de leña no teníamos para asar una sardina, y no sé a quién movió el Señor, que nos pusieron en la ilesia un hacecito de leña, con que nos remediamos."
  plain: "I left very glad, for it seemed I had everything while I had nothing. I had perhaps three or four ducats. With them I bought two canvases (I had no image at all for the altar), two straw mattresses and a blanket." And: "That day we did not even have a dry leaf of firewood to roast a sardine, and I do not know whom the Lord moved, but someone put a small bundle of wood in the church, and that helped us."
- What changed: the Toledo house stood; poverty gave way to gifts; she wished it had lasted.
- Not in the sources: the student's name beyond "Andrada"; what the neighbour women said. The street-shoe-blow anecdote (Zimmerman n.10, from *Oeuvres*) is a footnote tradition.
- Sources: Found-L ch.15 and nn.; Obras5 ch.15 and nn.

## SC-09 — Rain in the chapel, Salamanca, 28-29 September 1573
- Status: full
- When and where: Salamanca, 28-29 Sept 1573 (eve and feast of St Michael). They moved before dawn on the eve to a house bought from Pedro de la Banda, with a newly built chapel whose roof leaked [Found-L ch.19 §7-8 and nn.; Obras5 ch.19].
- Who was there: Teresa; Nicolás Gutiérrez, "the good man"; Anne of Jesus and two nuns; Fr Julián de Ávila and the chaplain Licentiate Nieto (Anne's account); workmen [Found-L n.; Obras5 ch.19 n.1].
- What happened, in order:
  1. The Mass of the Sacrament and a sermon were announced for St Michael's day. On the eve a heavy rain fell and made it hard to bring in what they needed; the chapel roof let in rain [Obras5 ch.19].
  2. Teresa, with the event already public, "did nothing but melt away", and said to the Lord, almost complaining, that He must stop making her take up these foundations or fix this need [Obras5 ch.19].
  3. Nicolás Gutiérrez, calm, told her gently not to worry; God would provide [ibid.].
  4. On St Michael's day, when the people came, the sun began to shine; she saw that the good man had done better by trusting than she by worry [ibid.].
  5. Anne of Jesus's sworn account: it was after eight in the evening; she went to Teresa with two nuns and said firmly, could she not ask God to stop the rain; Teresa answered, ask Him yourself; Anne left, looked up, saw a clear starry sky, came back and said the line below; Teresa went to her cell laughing [Obras5 ch.19 n.1, Anne of Jesus's deposition].
- Details: music, many people, the Sacrament brought in "with great solemnity" [Obras5 ch.19]. The two accounts differ: Teresa says the sun shone on the feast day; Anne says the sky cleared the night before and the feast dawned very clear. Do not merge them; tell Teresa's, and give Anne as another witness.
- Words spoken: Anne of Jesus: "Antes pudiera V. R. haber pedido esto a Dios" (Your Reverence could have asked God this sooner); Teresa to Anne: "Pídaselo ella, si tan presto le parece lo ha de hacer porque yo se lo diga" (You ask Him, if you think He will do it so quickly because I tell Him) [Obras5 ch.19 n.1, Anne's deposition].
- Inner life: "Yo os digo, hijas, que me vi harto imperfeta aquel día." [Obras5 ch.19]. Plain: "I tell you, daughters, I saw myself to be very imperfect that day."
- Testimony (Teresa, Spanish, Obras5 *Fundaciones* ch.19; public-domain): "Yo os digo, hijas, que me vi harto imperfeta aquel día. Por estar ya divulgado, yo no sabía qué hacer, sino que me estaba deshaciendo, y dije a Nuestro Señor, casi quejándome, que u no me mandase entender en estas obras, u remediase aquella necesidad. El buen hombre de Niculás Gutiérrez, con su igualdad, como si no hubiera nada, me decía muy mansamente, que no tuviese pena, que Dios lo remediaría. Y ansí fué, que el día de San Miguel, al tiempo de venir la gente, comenzó a hacer sol."
  plain: "I tell you, daughters, I found myself very imperfect that day. Because it was already announced, I did not know what to do. I just fell apart, and I said to Our Lord, almost complaining, that either He should not order me to take on these works or He should fix this need. The good man Nicolás Gutiérrez, with his even temper, as if nothing were wrong, told me very gently not to worry, that God would fix it. And so it was: on St Michael's day, at the time the people came, the sun began to shine."
- What changed: the Sacrament was placed with a full church; she wrote that Nicolás trusted better than she did.
- Not in the sources: the exact hour of Teresa's complaint (Anne says after 8 pm; Teresa gives no hour). Anne's phrase in Zimmerman's English is "a little sooner"; the Spanish is closer to "you could have asked this earlier".
- Sources: Found-L ch.19 §7-9 and n.; Obras5 ch.19 and n.1.

## SC-10 — The Pastrana nuns leave at midnight, Holy Week 1574
- Status: full (with one weak line: "Let us pray" is only in Zimmerman's footnote)
- When and where: Pastrana, then the road to Segovia, Holy Week 1574 (Segovia was founded 19 Mar 1574 [Found-L ch.21]). Teresa had founded Pastrana in 1569 and lived there three months [Found-L ch.17 §12].
- Who was there: Princess of Éboli (Ana de Mendoza), widowed 29 July 1573; prioress Isabel of St Dominic; the nuns; the corregidor and a notary; escorts Julián de Ávila, Antonio Gaytán, Fray Gabriel of the Assumption; the bishop of Segorbe, who followed to Segovia [Found-L ch.17 nn.17, 19, 20].
- What happened, in order:
  1. In 1569 Teresa spent three months at Pastrana. The Princess "insisted on certain things unbecoming our order"; Teresa planned to leave rather than agree; Ruy Gómez calmed his wife [Found-L §12].
  2. The Princess had borrowed the *Life*, ridiculed it, and left it for her servants, who spread its contents; this led the Inquisition to demand it (Zimmerman, from the *Reforma*) [Found-L n.14].
  3. After Ruy Gómez died, the Princess became a nun at once, came with her mother at eight in the morning, took the lowest seat, asked for her own maids and visitors, and retired to a garden hermitage. The prioress is said to have cried: "The Princess a nun? The house is lost." [Found-L n.17; Obras5 n.4: "es fama"].
  4. Teresa begged superiors to move the nuns; they agreed (Salazar, Pedro Fernández, Báñez, Hernando del Castillo). The prioress called the corregidor and a notary, handed over every gift against a written receipt, and the last Mass was said [Found-L n.20; Obras5 n.1].
  5. The nuns left at midnight (Yepes, via Zimmerman), bringing only their own beds and small things, plus one nun the Princess had forced on them [Found-L §15 and n.20].
  6. In Segovia, when they were in danger on the road, Teresa said to her nuns: "Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana" (Reforma, via Zimmerman) [Found-L n.20].
  7. The bishop of Segorbe threatened a lawsuit for the Princess's gifts; the receipt was shown and he said no more [Found-L n.20].
- Details: before leaving for Toledo in 1569, Teresa had ordered every gift recorded with date and the prioress's signature [Obras5 ch.17 n.1]. Zimmerman's footnote has the Princess crying to the prioress "What have the friars to do with my monastery?" [Found-L n.17].
- Words spoken: Teresa, in her book: "Yo con el mayor contento del mundo, de verlas en quietud" (I had the greatest joy in the world to see them in peace) [Obras5 ch.17; Found-L §15]. Teresa (Zimmerman's report): "Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana." Spanish not found; Reforma not reachable.
- Inner life: she says the nuns were blameless and treated the Princess with respect; she blames the Princess's grief and a servant; "the Lord who permitted it must have seen that the monastery was not rightly placed there"; she was guided by "saintly and learned men" [Found-L §15].
- Testimony (Teresa, Spanish, Obras5 *Fundaciones* ch.17; public-domain): "Yo procuré con cuantas vías pude, suplicándolo a los perlados que quitasen de allí el monesterio, fundándose uno en Segovia, como adelante se dirá, adonde se pasaron, dejando cuanto les había dado la Princesa, y llevando consigo algunas monjas que ella había mandado tomar sin nenguna cosa. Las camas y rosillas que las mesmas monjas habían traído, llevaron consigo, dejando bien lastimados a los del lugar. Yo con el mayor contento del mundo, de verlas en quietud."
  plain: "I tried by every means to ask the superiors to take the convent away from there. A house was being founded in Segovia, as I will tell later, and they moved there, leaving everything the Princess had given them. They brought some nuns the Princess had ordered them to take without any dowry. They took the beds and small mats the nuns had themselves brought, and left the townspeople very sorry. And I felt the greatest joy in the world to see them at peace."
  (Spelling as printed in the 1915 edition; "rosillas" is the printed word.)
- What changed: the convent split from the Princess; her anger toward Teresa and the *Life* went on.
- Inquisition and the book (the Need): Ribera's editor, citing Anne of Jesus's sworn account, says Teresa learned at Veas in 1575 that the Inquisition was searching for the *Life*; a messenger came from Bishop Álvaro de Mendoza and the nuns [Ribera p. XXI-XXII; Found-L ch.23 n.1 says the same, with letters from the bishop]. Báñez, quoted by the editor: "Yo llevé el libro ... al Santo Oficio de la Inquisición en Madrid" (I took the book to the Holy Office of the Inquisition in Madrid) and the inquisitor Soto y Salazar returned it to him to review; his written opinion is dated 7 July 1575 [Ribera p. XXII and n.3]. The book stayed with the Inquisition about twelve years [Ribera p. XXII]. These sources do not date the Princess's complaint; Zimmerman and Ribera's editor tie it to Pastrana and the Princess. The exact day the Inquisition took the book: not found.
- Not in the sources: the Princess's speech at midnight; any scene in Pastrana on the night they left; the nuns' feelings; weather; how many nuns left (Zimmerman names only Anne of the Incarnation, whom the Princess pushed on them, and Isabel of St Dominic).
- Sources: Found-L ch.17 and nn.14, 17, 19, 20; Obras5 ch.17 and notes; Ribera intro pp. XXI-XXII.
