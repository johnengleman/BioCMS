## 1. The driving question

Teresa wanted to belong to God completely. For years she could not: she loved the parlour and its praise, gave up prayer, and nearly died of illness [W-S3 p.7; W-S2 ch.2, ch.5; W-S1 p.110-112]. When grace turned her, her visions and poor convents made churchmen, a princess, and the Inquisition suspect her [W-S1 p.304, intro; W-S3 p.8, 10-11]. She answered with prayer as friendship with Christ and with obedience above her own visions, and died thanking God that she was "a daughter of the Church" [F-E2 Life 8.5; F-E1 ch.24 n.9; L-XP1 p.83].

She is famous as the reformer of Carmel, a teacher of prayer, and the first woman Doctor of the Church [C-S2; L-XV1].

While the Inquisition searched for her book, a woman without learned training wrote on mystical prayer with an authority popes later recognized [F-E1 ch.23 n.1; L-XV1]. She founded houses in strict poverty, yet she laughed, forbade harsh penances, and wanted cheerful nuns [F-E1 ch.15 §14, ch.18 n.4; L-E4 vol.6 p.12].

## 2. Portrait

Teresa was charming, and knew it. People visited the Incarnation parlour for her "buena gracia y donaire", and she wept over that all her life [W-S3 p.7]. Humor runs through her life: she called herself "a bargainer and business woman" [L-XL1 p.63], told a frightened companion to let her sleep [F-E1 ch.19 §5], and called her only portrait "blear-eyed and ugly" [F-E1 ch.17 n.5]. She was practical: straw beds for the healthy, mattresses for the sick [L-E4 vol.6 p.7, 11-12]. She hated long journeys but never refused one [F-E1 ch.18 §4]. She named her own faults: vanity and years of a divided heart [W-S2 ch.2, ch.5], impatience with God in a leaking chapel [F-E1 ch.19 §8]. She distrusted her visions and preferred learned confessors to mystics [W-S3 p.7]. People were surprised that a sick woman past sixty outworked the friars, and obeyed a young superior against her own revelation [F-E1 ch.24 n.9].

Her life with God was friendship. Mental prayer is "being on terms of friendship with God, frequently conversing in secret with Him Who, we know, loves us" [F-E3 ch.8 §7]. She found Him "amidst the pots and pans" [O-S4 ch.5]. "It is not so essential to think much as to love much" [O-S3 Fourth Mansions].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** hook, broken picture.

> In the late 1570s the pope's ambassador in Spain had a name for Teresa of Ávila: "a restless, roving woman." A preacher at Medina had compared her to a famous fraud, and the book she had written about her own soul lay with the Inquisition. She was past sixty, often sick, and still on the roads of Castile in a covered cart, founding convents with almost no money. In 1970 a pope stood in St Peter's and declared her a Doctor of the Church, the first woman ever given the title. Her road between those two verdicts began in a convent parlour in Ávila, where for years she tried to belong both to God and to the world.

Keys: [W-S1 p.304 (Yepes); W-S3 p.10-11; W-S1 intro; F-E1 ch.24 §3; L-XV1].

**Thesis:** Teresa reformed the Carmelites. She founded St Joseph's, Ávila, in 1562 and sixteen more houses of nuns [C-S2], and with John of the Cross began the Discalced friars at Duruelo in 1568 [F-E1 ch.14 n.4]. Her *Life*, *Foundations*, *Interior Castle*, and letters teach prayer as friendship with God; Paul VI declared her the first woman Doctor of the Church in 1970 [F-E2 Life 8.5; L-XV1; C-S4]. Answered in chapters 6–7, 11, and 15.

8. **Teresa Founds Convents with Almost Nothing** — SC-08, SC-09. Ends: a powerful princess wants a convent of her own.
9. **A Princess Turns Against Teresa's Nuns** — SC-10. Ends: the Inquisition holds her book; a new superior sends her south.
10. **Teresa Obeys and Travels to Seville** — SC-11. Ends: the older branch of the Order moves against the reform.
11. **John of the Cross Is Seized and Teresa Writes to the King** — SC-12. Ends: the reform survives; Teresa is old and tired.
12. **Teresa's Last Journey** — SC-13. Ends: she reaches Alba and goes to bed.
13. **The Death of Teresa at Alba** — SC-14. Ends: the nuns smell a fragrance at her grave.
14. **Two Towns Fight Over Teresa's Body** — bridge, no scene: exhumations 1583–86, processes, beatification 1614, canonization 1622. Ends: popes keep naming her a teacher.
15. **Teresa Becomes the First Woman Doctor of the Church** — SC-15. Closes the loop with the "restless woman".

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — The road to the Moors, about 1522 (Ávila, the Adaja bridge)
- What happens: Teresa, about seven, and Rodrigo set out to beg their way to martyrdom; an uncle meets them at the bridge; Rodrigo blames her.
- Why it is here: her first wish to give God everything.
- Have: W-S1 p.96-97; O-S1 ch.1 §4-5 (Q1, Q2, "For ever, ever, ever"); W-S4 p.309.
- Need: the uncle's name and words; Rodrigo's excuse, in Spanish.
- Testimony: Life ch.1 §4-5 (Lewis).
- Where to look: W-S1 p.96-97.

### SC-02 — Leaving her father's house, 2 November 1535 (Ávila, the Incarnation)
- What happens: she leaves home for the convent; it feels "as if every bone in my body were wrenched asunder".
- Why it is here: her first choice for God against her own feelings.
- Have: O-S1 ch.4 §1 (Q4); W-S1 p.106, p.110 n.
- Need: whether her father consented; who went with her; Q4's chapter.
- Testimony: Life ch.4 §1.
- Where to look: O-S1 ch.3-4.

### SC-03 — "This daughter is not for burying", August 1537 (Ávila)
- What happens: four days in a coma; wax on her eyes; a grave dug; her father refuses to bury her. Later she crawls, praising God.
- Why it is here: the cost of her early years; her trust in St Joseph.
- Have: W-S1 p.110-112; W-S3 p.22-23; O-S2 vol.1 p.36 (Q5); C-S6 p.316-317.
- Need: her own words on the cure through St Joseph.
- Testimony: Ana de la Encarnación, W-S3 p.22-23 (short quote).
- Where to look: O-S1 ch.5-6.

### SC-04 — Before the wounded Christ, about 1554 (the Incarnation oratory)
- What happens: she throws herself down before an image of Christ "most grievously wounded" and will not rise until He grants her petition; then Augustine's *Confessions* reach her.
- Why it is here: the end of the divided years.
- Have: O-S1 ch.9 §1-3, §8 (Q6, Q7); W-S2 ch.9; W-S1 p.121-122.
- Need: her words on the years she gave up prayer, and how many; the year.
- Testimony: Life ch.9 §1-3, in full.
- Where to look: O-S1 ch.7-9.

### SC-05 — The angel with the golden spear, about 1559 (the Incarnation)
- What happens: a small angel thrusts a golden spear into her heart and leaves her "all on fire with a great love of God". Raptures lift her; she begs God to stop public ones. Confessors fear the devil.
- Why it is here: the centre of her life with God, and the start of suspicion.
- Have: C-S1 ch.29 §16-17, ch.20 §4-7; Báñez [W-S3 p.7]; order to scorn a vision [C-S6 p.337-338].
- Need: her account of confessors who called her visions the devil's work (Life ch.23-25).
- Testimony: Life ch.29 §16-17.
- Where to look: C-S1 ch.20, 29.

### SC-06 — St Joseph's opens, 24 August 1562 (Ávila)
- What happens: four novices are clothed before a few friends; the city and the orders oppose the house; Báñez alone defends it before the council.
- Why it is here: the first reform, the first open opposition.
- Have: W-S1 n. (Ribera text line ~11117); W-S3 p.8; O-S4 intro; L-E4 vol.6 p.6-13.
- Need: her account of the day and of her recall to the provincial; the Ribera page.
- Testimony: Báñez, sworn 1591, W-S3 p.8 (short).
- Where to look: O-S1 ch.36.

### SC-07 — A friar sweeping the door, Lent 1569 (Duruelo)
- What happens: Fray Antonio sweeps the church door ("what has become of your dignity?"); merchants weep at the skulls; friars pray with snow on their habits.
- Why it is here: the reform reaches the friars.
- Have: F-E1 ch.14 §2, 5-6, 11, n.1-4; Rubeo [W-S3 p.8]; John [W-S1 p.222].
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Foundations ch.14 §5-6, in full.
- Where to look: F-E1 ch.14.

### SC-08 — Three ducats and a student's key, May 1569 (Toledo)
- What happens: she wins the licence; buys two pictures, two straw mattresses, a blanket; a poor student finds a house; no wood "to fry a sardine".
- Why it is here: poverty and trust, with humor.
- Have: F-E1 ch.15 §5-14, n.5, n.9.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Foundations ch.15 §6-8, 13-14.
- Where to look: F-E1 ch.15.

### SC-09 — Rain in the chapel, 28–29 September 1573 (Salamanca)
- What happens: the chapel leaks; she complains to Christ; the sun shines; "I found I was very imperfect that day"; Anne of Jesus: "a little sooner."
- Why it is here: a fault she owns.
- Have: F-E1 ch.19 §8 and n.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Foundations ch.19 §8.
- Where to look: F-E1 ch.19.

### SC-10 — The Pastrana nuns leave at midnight, Holy Week 1574
- What happens: the widowed princess tries to rule the convent; Teresa withdraws the nuns by night with a receipt for every gift; "Let us pray for those who are coming from Pastrana."
- Why it is here: the hardest opposition; it brought the Inquisition to her book.
- Have: F-E1 ch.17 §12-15, n.14, 17, 19, 20; W-S1 intro.
- Need: when the Inquisition took the book.
- Testimony: Báñez, "Yo llevé el libro ... al Santo Oficio" [W-S1 intro].
- Where to look: F-E1 ch.17, ch.23 n.1.

### SC-11 — The ferry and the saw, May 1575 (road to Seville)
- What happens: Gracián orders Seville against her revelation; she obeys; fever in carriages "like Purgatory"; a ferry drifts; at Córdoba the carriages are sawn.
- Why it is here: obedience above her own visions.
- Have: F-E1 ch.24 §3-8, n.9, n.10.
- Need: the Spanish of the obedience reply (Yepes).
- Testimony: Foundations ch.24 §4-8.
- Where to look: F-E1 ch.24.

### SC-12 — John of the Cross is seized, 3–4 December 1577 (Ávila)
- What happens: 55 nuns who voted for her are excommunicated; John is seized at night; she writes to Philip II: "I would rather have seen our fathers in the power of the Moors."
- Why it is here: the storm against the reform, in the year of *Interior Castle*.
- Have: L-XL3 CCI p.4-6; L-XL2 CXCV p.311 n.; O-S3 intro.
- Need: the King's answer; where John was held.
- Testimony: Letter CCI, in full.
- Where to look: L-XL3.

### SC-13 — The hardest order, September 1582 (Medina to Alba)
- What happens: on her way home to Ávila she is ordered to Alba and obeys; she faints near Peñaranda; figs: "many poor people will not have such a feast."
- Why it is here: obedience to the end.
- Have: L-XP1 p.169-170; W-S1 p.333-334; L-XL4 p.368-372.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Ana de San Bartolomé, L-XP1 p.169-170.
- Where to look: L-XP1.

### SC-14 — "A daughter of the Church", 3–4 October 1582 (Alba de Tormes)
- What happens: she sits up alone when the Sacrament enters; asks pardon for her bad example; "Will they not give me a little earth here?"; dies about 9 pm in Ana's arms.
- Why it is here: resolves the driving question.
- Have: L-XP1 p.83, 90, 101, 170; W-S1 p.335-337; C-S5 p.602-604.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: Mariana de la Encarnación, L-XP1 p.90 (short); Ribera W-S1 p.335 for longer text.
- Where to look: L-XP1; W-S1.

### SC-15 — The declaration, 27 September 1970 (St Peter's, Rome)
- What happens: Paul VI declares her "Doctor of the Universal Church", the first woman, and quotes her dying words.
- Why it is here: the real case for the ending.
- Have: L-XV1; C-S4.
- Need: nothing.
- Testimony: none.
- Where to look: L-XV1.

## 6. The saint in the Church

**Writings**
- *Life*: drafted 1562, final 1568 [O-S2 vol.1; F-E1 ch.10 n.4]; with the Inquisition about 12 years; Cardinal Quiroga, 1580: nothing not "very good" [W-S1 intro]. Ch.5, 9.
- Constitutions of St Joseph's, by 1567 [L-E4 vol.6 p.XV]. Ch.6.
- *Foundations*, begun 1573 [O-S4 intro]. Ch.8.
- *Interior Castle*, June–Nov 1577 [O-S3 intro]. Ch.11.
- Letters, "full of humanity" [L-XV1]. Ch.10-12.
- "Nada te turbe", in her breviary at Alba [O-S2 vol.6]. Ch.13.
- First edition, Luis de León, 1588 [C-S5 p.610]. Ch.14.

**What she changed in the Church**
- Discalced nuns: 1562 and sixteen more houses [C-S2]. Ch.6-12.
- Discalced friars: Duruelo, 28 Nov 1568 [F-E1 ch.14 n.4]. Ch.7.
- People she formed: John of the Cross, Gracián, Ana de San Bartolomé [F-E1; L-XP1 p.XXVII]. Ch.14.
- Teacher of prayer: Pius XI, 1922, "altissimam contemplationis magistra"; statue "Mater Spiritualium" in St Peter's [L-XV1; C-S4]. Ch.15.
- First woman Doctor, 27 Sept 1970 [L-XV1]. Ch.15.

**Why she is famous**
- Body found whole, 4 July 1583; Ávila and Alba fight; it returns to Alba 23 Aug 1586 [L-XP3 art.97, 101]. Ch.14.
- Ribera's *Life*, 1590 [W-S1]. Ch.14.
- Relic healings, e.g. Isabel de Montoya, blind, Salamanca, 10 Feb 1603 [L-XP3 art.108]. Ch.14.
- Patron of Catholic writers; invoked against heart disease [C-S2]. Ch.15.

**Path to sainthood**
- Processes 1591–1610, over 400 witnesses [L-XP1 p.XVIII]; Gracián's *Defensa*, 1597 [L-XP1 p.XXVI]. Ch.14.
- Beatified 24 Apr 1614, Paul V; canonized 12 Mar 1622, Gregory XV, with Ignatius, Francis Xavier, Isidore, Philip Neri [L-XP1 p.XXVII; C-S3]. Ch.14.

**Fact needs**
1. When the Discalced became a separate province — L-E4 vol.6 intro p.XXXV.
2. Date and purpose of *The Way of Perfection* — O-S2 vol.3.
3. How the Discalced spread beyond Spain — L-XP1 p.XXVII (Antwerp).

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Entry: 2 Nov 1535 [W-S1 p.110 n.]; Lewis annals say 1533.
- Mother's death: she says "about twelve"; the will makes her nearly fourteen [W-S2 ch.1 n.].
- The angel: her manuscript says "cherubim"; "seraph" is Báñez's margin [C-S1 ch.29 n.].
- Wounded Christ: write "image" (statue or picture in the sources).
- Prayer as friendship is *Life* 8.5, not *Way of Perfection* [F-E1].
- Died about 9 pm, 4 Oct 1582; the next day was 15 Oct; feast 15 Oct [L-Butler p.374].
- Canonized 1622, not 1621 [L-XP1 p.XXVII].
- "Santos encapotados" is a witness report: attribute it [O-S2 vol.2].
- Names: Jerónimo Gracián; Ana de San Bartolomé; Princess of Eboli (Ana de Mendoza).
- Do not quote the Peers translation [O-S3].
