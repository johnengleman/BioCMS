## Errors of fact fixed in `biography.html`

- **Rodrigo (opening, note 2).** The text said he was four years older (Ribera, Silverio), and the block quote says "almost my age" (Teresa). Now no age is given in the story. The Note on the Sources states both.
- **Relative, not cousin (chapter 1, note 8).** The friend who shared her vanities was "una parienta", and Silverio separates her from the male cousins. "Cousin" is now "relative".
- **"The old question came back" (note 9).** No question was set up. The source: the nun's talk "began to root out" her habits and bring her thoughts back to eternal things, and her dislike of being a nun grew weaker. Rewritten.
- **Quote, Vida ch.4 (note 12).** The English sentence was broken. Spanish: "no creo será más el sentimiento cuando me muera." Now "the grief I felt when I left my father's house will not, I think, be greater when I die." Nothing added.
- **Ana de la Encarnación (note 18).** She "ha oído decir" what she tells (she did not nurse Teresa herself). Now "later told the Church what she had heard". Pages 22 to 23.
- **"Then she gave up mental prayer" (note 21).** Teresa tells the loss of prayer before the vision, not after. Now "She also gave up".
- **"She never heard him complain again" (note 25).** Source: "no creo le oí quejar". Softened.
- **Quote Vida ch.8 (note 28).** The cut after "no one to give me life" dropped "y no la podía yo tomar". Restored.
- **"No one ever took Him for a friend without being repaid" (note 32).** Silverio's note: the sense was left open in the original and Fray Luis de León completed it. Cut.
- **Provincial (note 43).** The nuns heard her case first, then she spoke to him alone and he was satisfied. The text had "he" satisfied by the nuns' hearing. Fixed.
- **Third person "you" for God in quotes (notes 46, 101).** Now "You", as in the other quotes.
- **Way of Perfection (note 49).** "Her nuns asked" and "very probably end of 1565" had no saved source. Lewis and Zimmerman say it was written at Báñez's order, for the nuns of Saint Joseph's. The doubt word and the date are gone.
- **Andrada (note 56).** "Came back" had nothing before it. Teresa sent for him, told him the secret, and asked him to find a house; next morning he came again.
- **Nuns at Toledo (note 58).** Spanish: "¿Qué hemos de haber, madre? que ya no parece somos pobres" (their answer to her question "qué habían"). Now "What should be wrong, Mother?" The earlier English read as the nuns asking her the same question.
- **Travel (note 60).** "She never once refused a foundation" was absolute. Source: "so far as I remember, I never refrained... through fear of trouble". Softened to match.
- **Anne of Jesus (note 62).** Named in full with her role ("one of the nuns of that house") so she is not taken for Blessed Ana de San Bartolomé. The sky "full of stars" is in Ana de Jesús's sworn account (Obras vol.5 ch.19 n.1).
- **Princess paragraph (notes 64 to 65).** The Princess's husband was named in the first paragraph, and Ana de Mendoza, King Philip II and Juan de la Miseria's portrait too (four new names). Now two new names. The servants-and-Inquisition link rests on a Carmelite chronicle (Lewis note); Ribera's editor says the Princess herself denounced the book. Now "Carmelite histories say", and the Note on the Sources gives the other view.
- **Prioress's line at Pastrana (note 66).** Source (Zimmerman's note): "the monastery is lost". The text had "I give the house up for lost" in quotation marks. Fixed. The order of events (friars, hermitage) now follows the source.
- **Pastrana, Holy Week (note 67).** Holy Week 1574 is the arrival at Segovia, not the day of the receipt. Fixed.
- **Inquisition (note 68).** "All she knew was that it was being examined": Ana de Jesús says she knew nothing more about the book; the Grand Inquisitor's words came only in 1580. Now "she could only wait". The bishop of Ávila is "the bishop who had protected Saint Joseph's" (Ana de Jesús calls him bishop of Palencia, his later see).
- **Ferry (note 73).** "a carriage" is "the carriages" (Teresa: "los carros").
- **Córdoba quote (note 75).** Spanish: "fué menester aserrarlos, u no sé qué". Now "or I do not know what".
- **Seville opening (note 76).** WRONG PERSON: the text said the archbishop knelt and asked her blessing. Stanbrook (vol.1 pp.269 to 270) and Reforma: Saint Teresa knelt for his blessing and, to her great confusion, he asked for hers.
- **Lorenzo (note 78).** "after thirty-four years" is "more than thirty-four" (Teresa). Added Letter LXXXV for the children.
- **Sega (note 79).** WRONG DATE: the paragraph is dated 1575, but Ormaneto died in June 1577 and Sega came 30 August 1577. Now "In 1577 Felipe Sega came to Spain as the Pope's new envoy". The "restless, roving woman" line is Yepes's report, so it says "One biographer records".
- **Letter CCI (note 82).** The block quote began "He holds the two fathers" with no antecedent. The prose now names Hernando Maldonado, the prior of Ávila (Stanbrook heading).
- **Pedro de Castro (note 89).** "to-night" is now "tonight".
- **Valladolid sleeve (note 92).** Whose sleeve: Ana's own words are "me tiró de la manga" (Stanbrook prefatory note, from her autobiography). Now "Blessed Ana's sleeve".
- **Psalm verses (notes 101, 103).** Ribera and both nuns: she repeated several verses (Cor mundum..., Ne projicias..., Cor contritum...), above all "Cor contritum". Now "verses", "above all this one".
- **Foundress at Alba (note 107).** She is Teresa de Laíz ("pidiéndolo así Teresa de Láiz, fundadora de este convento", Procesos I p.84). Named. "kissed her feet" became "kissed her body" (Mariana de Jesús: "a besarle, y al hábito").
- **Luis de León and the witnesses (note 112).** The note named Lovat p.610, which was not saved and not checked. Rebuilt from Silverio's preliminary pages LXXXII to LXXXIII (1588) and Procesos I intro p.XXV (more than 400 witnesses).
- **Paris (note 113).** WRONG DATE AND NUMBER: "July 1604, seven nuns". Stanbrook: "one of the six nuns who in 1604 went to France"; Silverio: Anne of Jesus reached Paris on 15 October. Fixed. The note no longer cites Zimmerman's *Catholic Encyclopedia*, which does not give it.
- **Isabel de Montoya (note 114).** The Te Deum came after she told the prioress, not while she walked to Communion. Fixed.
- **Doctor vote (note 116).** "approved the vote": no vote had been named. Paul VI's letter: the cardinals of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints all agreed on 15 July 1969, and he approved on 21 July.
- **Homily line (note 117).** Italian: "Noi abbiamo conferito, o meglio: Noi abbiamo riconosciuto il titolo di Dottore della Chiesa a Santa Teresa di Gesù." The title goes with both verbs, so the line now reads "To Saint Teresa of Jesus we have conferred, or rather we have recognized, the title of Doctor of the Church."
- **Letter, Life 8:5 (note 118).** "It quoted her own words" is "It pointed to what she taught": the letter gives a reference and a paraphrase, not a quotation.
- **Homily, Spanish part (note 119).** "went back to her last night" is "spoke of her last moments" ("antes de su último suspiro").
- **Absolute claims.** "She never lost it" (the wish) and "never forgot what that walk cost her" and "fear of deception never left her" softened.
- **Titles.** "He was then called John of Saint Matthias" now begins "Saint John of the Cross was then called".
- **Doctor of the Church (note 5).** Glossed at its first use in the opening: the title honors saints of holiness and teaching (Paul VI letter), and she was the first woman to receive it.
- **Sources list and count.** Removed three entries no note uses any more (Camino, Lovat, Zimmerman). The count in "How This Life Was Researched" is now nineteen and matches the list.

**Seventeen or sixteen.** Both are right and they count differently. The witnesses in Procesos I say seventeen monasteries of nuns, fifteen founded in person and two by sending nuns (p.23, Ana de la Encarnación). Santi e Beati says the first foundation was followed by sixteen others. So seventeen houses, Saint Joseph's included, by 1582. The text says "By then there were seventeen houses of her nuns" and the opening no longer gives a count. `facts.md` now says so. Note 100 names Procesos I p.23 first, then Santi e Beati.

**Ten passages from sources the notes did not hold: all confirmed, with these fixes.**

- *Veni Creator* (n.38): all details are in Vida ch.24 pp.188 to 189 (first rapture, the words, the confessor who did not dare to order her). No change.

- The return to the Incarnation in 1571 (n.67 to 68): every detail is in Zimmerman's introduction pp.XIX to XXIII. No change.
- Return to Saint Joseph's, 1574 (n.73): the source says the nuns, "though not all", wished to re-elect her. The text said "many of the nuns who had once cried out against her". Now "Many of the nuns wanted to elect her again, though not all of them did."
