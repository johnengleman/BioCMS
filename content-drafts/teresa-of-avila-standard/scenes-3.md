## SC-11 — The ferry and the saw, May 1575
- Status: full (the Spanish of the obedience reply is not reached; see "Words spoken")
- When and where: Wednesday 18 May to Thursday 26 May 1575, the road from Veas to Seville. The fever came "the day before Pentecost"; the ferry was "a little before, I do not know if it was two days"; Córdoba was the last day of the Pentecost feast [O-5 ch.24; F-E1 ch.24 §3-8 and notes].
- Who was there: six nuns (Mary of St Joseph, Isabel of St Francis, Mary of the Holy Ghost, Isabel of St Jerome, Leonor of St Gabriel, Anne of St Albert); the priest Julián de Ávila; Antonio Gaytán; a barefoot friar. Gracián was not with them: the nuncio had called him away [F-E1 ch.24 §3 and note]. A ferryman and his son of about ten or eleven; a nobleman in a castle; a man from the castle who guided them [F-E1 ch.24 §6].
- What happened, in order:
  1. At Veas, Gracián (her superior) told her to ask God whether the next house should be Madrid or Seville. She said the answer was Madrid. He ordered Seville anyway [F-E1 ch.24 n.9, citing Yepes i.28].
  2. Two or three days later he asked why she obeyed him and not the revelation. She said she could not be as sure of any revelation as of his order, and that she must obey her superior. He told her to pray again. The answer was then Seville (paraphrase of Zimmerman's English summary of Yepes) [F-E1 ch.24 n.9].
  3. She wrote that she gave in at once, though she had meant to found elsewhere and had "some very grave reasons" against Seville [O-5 ch.24 §2].
  4. Covered carriages in the heat; she says going into them was like purgatory [O-5 ch.24 §3].
  5. A violent fever; she was near unconscious; warm water on her face; a windowless room; she got up and went on. The fever did not last more than a day [O-5 ch.24 §4-5].
  6. On the Guadalquivir the ferry lost the rope and drifted without oars; the ferryman's boy cried; it grounded on a sandbank; they went on in the dark with a guide from the castle [O-5 ch.24 §6].
  7. At Córdoba they waited over two hours for the governor's permission, the carriages did not fit the bridge gate and were sawn, and the church was full for a feast and sermon. She wanted to leave; Julián overruled her [O-5 ch.24 §7-8].
- Details: a room "like a shed", no window, sun when the door opened; a bed uneven "as if of sharp stones"; large veils, white coarse mantles, hemp sandals; they heard Mass unseen in intention; they ate at noon "under a bridge" [F-E1 ch.24 §4-5, 8, notes]. Julián de Ávila says the saw cut the axle ends (editor's note, O-5 p.208-210, paraphrase).
- Words spoken, with the plain English:
  - Teresa on the carriages: "que era entrar en ellos como en un purgatorio" — "going into them was like going into purgatory" [O-5 ch.24 §3].
  - Teresa on obedience: "luego me rendí; que esta merced me hace Nuestro Señor, de parecerme que en todo aciertan" — "I gave in at once; for Our Lord gives me this grace, that my superiors always seem to me to be right" [O-5 ch.24 §2].
  - Teresa on the saw: "no cabían los carros por la puerta de la puente; fué menester aserrarlos, y no sé qué" — "the carriages did not fit through the bridge gate; they had to saw them, or something, I do not know what" [O-5 ch.24 §7].
  - Teresa after Córdoba: "Aquel sobresalto me debía quitar la calentura del todo" — "That shock must have taken my fever away completely" [O-5 ch.24 §8].
  - The Spanish of her reply to Gracián (Yepes): not reached. Yepes' book is not on archive.org in the searches. Use the paraphrase in step 2 only.
- Inner life: she thought of hell when the heat was worst, and of "doing and suffering something for God" [O-5 ch.24 §3]. She wrote that she cared more for the ferryman's distress than for the danger [O-5 ch.24 §6].
- Testimony: Teresa, *Fundaciones* 24, §4 and §6, Spanish. Rights: public-domain.
  > Fué de tal suerte, que parecía tenía modorra, según iba enajenada. Ellas a echarme agua en el rostro, tan caliente del sol, que daba poco refrigerio. [...] la barca iba sin maroma ni remos con el carro. El barquero me hacía mucha más lástima verle tan fatigado, que no el peligro; nosotras a rezar; todos voces grandes.
  > Plain: "It was so bad that I seemed to be in a stupor, I was so far out of myself. The sisters threw water on my face, but it was so hot from the sun that it gave little relief. [...] the boat went on with the carriage, with no rope and no oars. I felt much more pity for the ferryman, he was so distressed, than fear of the danger. We began to pray, and everyone shouted."
- What changed: she obeyed an order that went against her own revelation, and she reached Seville on 26 May to find the archbishop had not given leave for the house [F-E1 ch.24 §9-12].
- Not in the sources: any dialogue between Teresa and the nuns on the road; the names of the ferryman and his son; the saw's size; the date of Córdoba beyond "last day of Pentecost" (Teresa says Tuesday; Mary of St Joseph says Whit-Monday; Julián says Sunday) [F-E1 ch.24 n.].
- Sources: O-5 (Spanish, ch.24, pp.206-211 of the edition); F-E1 ch.24 and notes 8-10.

## SC-12 — John of the Cross is seized, 3-4 December 1577
- Status: full for the letter and the seizure; the King's answer is not in the sources read
- When and where: night of 3 Dec 1577 at the Incarnation, Ávila (seizure); Teresa wrote the same letter on 4 Dec 1577 from St Joseph's, Ávila [S-3 Letter CCI].
- Who was there: John of the Cross and Fray Germán, seized by Calced friars; Hernando Maldonado, prior of the Calced house at Ávila (the Stanbrook note blames him); Philip II, the reader of the letter [S-3 p.3-6 and prefatory note].
- What happened, in order:
  1. The nuns of the Incarnation had wanted Teresa near them. She had put John and a companion in a cottage near the convent as confessors [S-3 CCI].
  2. The Discalced were seized "on the night of Dec. 3, 1577". John went to the Calced priory at Toledo; Germán to Moraleja. Stanbrook: John was held nine months in a tiny cell [S-3 prefatory note].
  3. On 4 Dec Teresa wrote to Philip II to ask for their release [S-3 CCI].
  4. On 7 Dec she wrote to Gaspar de Salazar: the nuns had elected her prioress, the Calced friars had confirmed the nun with fewer votes, and the opposing nuns, "over fifty", were excommunicated [S-3 CCII]. The count "55" comes from a Stanbrook note on Letter CXCV (vol. 2 p.311) in the notes file [L-XL2 CXCV].
- Words spoken: Teresa to the King, English (Stanbrook): "I would rather have seen our fathers in the power of the Moors who might be more merciful." Original Spanish: not reached (not in Silverio vol. 6, which is the Constitutions).
- Inner life: she calls the Discalced "the poor Discalced" who "do nothing but endure in silence, gaining great merit". She says "we have no other earthly aid" [S-3 CCI]. On 7 Dec she writes: "What a life to witness all this!" and that "until we are a separate province I believe we shall never be free from disturbances" [S-3 CCII]. The Stanbrook note puts *Interior Castle* finished 24 Nov 1577, a week before [S-3 CCII note].
- Testimony: Teresa to Philip II, 4 Dec 1577, Letter CCI (part, 140 words), Stanbrook 1922. Rights: public-domain. OCR slips corrected: "actions", "inflicted", "Commissary".
  > He keeps the two fathers imprisoned in his priory, having forced open their cells and seized their papers. The neighbours are scandalized and astonished at his daring, for he is not a superior and shows no authority for his actions; the confessors are subjects of the Apostolic Commissary and your Majesty is very near the place. These Calced friars seem to fear neither justice nor God. As they have long desired, our confessors have fallen into their hands — I am deeply grieved, for I would rather have seen our fathers in the power of the Moors who might be more merciful. This friar, who serves God very fervently, is so weak from all he has suffered that I fear for his life. For love of our Lord, I implore your Majesty to command that the confessors should be set free at once and that the sufferings inflicted on the poor Discalced by those of the cloth should be stopped.
  > Plain: "He holds the two fathers prisoner in his priory. He broke open their cells and took their papers. The neighbours are shocked at his boldness: he is not a superior and shows no authority for what he does. These Calced friars seem to fear neither justice nor God. They have long wanted our confessors in their hands, and now they have them. It grieves me deeply. I would rather have seen our fathers held by the Moors, who might show more mercy. This friar, who serves God so fervently, is so weak from all he has suffered that I fear for his life. For the love of our Lord I beg your Majesty to order that the confessors be freed at once and that the Calced friars stop harming the poor Discalced."
- What changed: John's prison began and the reform faced its worst year; the separate province came with the brief of 22 June 1580 (see FN-1).
- Not in the sources: the King's reply (none found); how the letter was carried; John's cell (the Stanbrook note gives it, the letter does not); the Escorial interview of Dec 1577 (the letter to Inés Nieto is unverified; do not use).
- Sources: S-3 Letters CCI and CCII with prefatory notes (archive:lettersofstteres0003unse); L-XL2 CXCV p.311 note (from notes file).

## SC-13 — The hardest order, September 1582
- Status: full
- When and where: mid-September 1582. She left Valladolid on 15 Sept, reached Medina del Campo, and was sent on to Alba de Tormes. At a village near Peñaranda she fainted. She reached Alba at night on "the eve of St Matthew", and went to bed [S-4 Letter CCCCLXII note; R-1 p.333-334].
- Who was there: Ana de San Bartolomé (her companion); Fray Antonio de Jesús, vicar provincial, who waited at Medina; María Enríquez, Duchess of Alba, who had asked for her; Juana del Espíritu Santo, prioress at Alba [R-1 p.333-334; P-1 p.169-170].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She had meant to go straight to Ávila, where she was prioress, and to give the veil to her niece Teresa [R-1 p.333].
  2. At Medina, Fray Antonio ordered her to Alba because the Duchess asked. Ribera says it was a great contradiction to her. She obeyed [R-1 p.333].
  3. She went in a carriage, unwell. Near Peñaranda she fainted with pain and weakness [R-1 p.333].
  4. They had only figs to give her, and the village had no egg. She comforted Ana [R-1 p.333-334].
  5. Next day they ate boiled greens with onion in another village, though it was bad for her illness. At night she reached Alba, tired, and the nuns begged her to go to bed [R-1 p.334].
- Details: carriage ("carroza"); figs; "berzas cocidas con cebolla". The carriage and the greens are in Ribera only.
- Words spoken, Spanish with plain English:
  - Ana de San Bartolomé, sworn at Ávila, 19 Oct 1595, reporting Teresa: "ninguna cosa en toda su vida se le había mandado más grave que el mandarla fuese a Alba, hallándose como se hallaba, y siendo su camino para Avila, pero que ella había de obedecer como siempre lo había hecho" — "nothing in all her life had been commanded of her that was harder than being told to go to Alba, in the state she was in, when her road was to Ávila; but she had to obey, as she always had" [P-1 p.169-170] (reported speech).
  - Teresa at Alba: "Válame Dios, qué cansada me siento; más ha de veinte años que nunca me acosté temprano, sino ahora." — "God help me, how tired I feel. It is more than twenty years since I went to bed early; now I have." [R-1 p.334]
- Inner life: Ribera: she obeyed "por parecerse al que fué obediente hasta la muerte y muerte de cruz", to be like Him who was "obedient to death, even death on a cross" [R-1 p.333].
- Testimony: Ribera, *Vida*, ch. XV, printed p.333-334, Spanish. Rights: public-domain.
  > Pusiéronla en una carroza en que fué harto trabajada é indispuesta, y llegando á un lugar cerca de Peñaranda, iba con tantos dolores y flaqueza, que la dió allí un desmayo, que á todos hizo gran lástima verla. Y con estar así no traían otra cosa para darla, sino unos higos, ni en el lugar se pudo hallar un huevo. La hermana Ana de San Bartolomé congojábase de verla en tanta necesidad, y no tener con qué regalarla; mas la Madre la consolaba, diciendo: «No tengas pena por mí, hija, que muy buenos son estos higos; muchos pobres no tendrán tanto regalo.»
  > Plain: "They put her in a carriage, where she suffered and was unwell. Near Peñaranda she was in so much pain and weakness that she fainted, and everyone felt great pity to see her. In that state they had nothing to give her but some figs, and no egg could be found in the village. Sister Ana de San Bartolomé was distressed to see her in such need and to have nothing to ease it, but the Mother comforted her: 'Do not worry about me, child; these figs are very good. Many poor people will not have such a treat.'"
- What changed: she arrived at Alba and went to bed; within days the illness that killed her began.
- Not in the sources: the date of the faint; the village name beyond "near Peñaranda"; the road's weather; Teresa's face. Caution: Ana's own account (Ribera's footnote, same book) tells a different episode of dried figs and "no llores, hija; esto quiere Dios ahora", on the way out of Burgos. Do not merge them.
- Sources: R-1 ch.XV pp.333-334; P-1 pp.169-170; S-4 CCCCLXII (p.368-369, B.Z. note, from notes file).

## SC-14 — "A daughter of the Church", 3-4 October 1582
- Status: full
- When and where: Alba de Tormes, Discalced convent. 3 Oct (eve of St Francis): the Sacrament came at 5 pm; the last anointing at 9 pm. 4 Oct: she lay on her side at 7 am and died at 9 pm, "jueves, día de San Francisco, que es á cuatro de octubre, año de 1582". The next day was counted as 15 Oct [R-1 p.335-337].
- Who was there: the nuns of Alba; Fray Antonio de Jesús; Ana de San Bartolomé, who held her in her arms; María de San Francisco; Mariana (Gaytán, then a young pupil at Alba), who testified on 1 Apr 1592 [R-1 p.335-337; P-1 p.83, 170].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Three days before, she spent nearly a whole night in prayer and asked for Fray Antonio; the nuns heard him beg God not to take her yet [R-1 p.334].
  2. On the eve of St Francis, at 5 pm, she asked for the Sacrament. She was so ill she could not turn without help [R-1 p.335].
  3. While it was brought she asked the nuns to keep the Rule and Constitutions and to forgive her bad example [R-1 p.335].
  4. When she saw the Sacrament enter the cell she sat up in bed without help [R-1 p.335; P-1 p.83].
  5. She repeated the verses "Cor contritum et humiliatum, Deus, non despicies" and asked for the last anointing, received at 9 pm [R-1 p.335-336].
  6. Asked whether her body should go to Ávila or stay, she answered with a look of pain at the question [R-1 p.336].
  7. Next morning at 7 she lay on her side with a crucifix in her hand; she died at 9 pm [R-1 p.336].
- Details: crucifix in hand; she lay "as they paint the Magdalene" (Ribera's image); the Duchess of Alba and Ana nursed her (Ribera's footnote, Reforma de los Descalzos).
- Words spoken, Spanish with plain English [R-1 p.335-336]:
  - "Hijas mías y señoras mías, por amor de Dios las pido tengan gran cuenta con la guarda de la regla y constituciones, y no miren el mal ejemplo que esta mala monja las ha dado, y perdónenmele." — "My daughters and my ladies, for the love of God I ask you to take great care to keep the Rule and the Constitutions, and not to look at the bad example this bad nun has given you, and forgive me for it."
  - "¿Tengo yo de tener cosa propia? ¿aquí no me darán un poco de tierra?" — "Am I to have anything of my own? Will they not give me a little earth here?"
  - Mariana, 1592 [P-1 p.83, from notes file]: "al entrar por la celda se sentó en la cama con gran ligereza ella sola" — "as [the Sacrament] came into the cell she sat up in bed very lightly, by herself".
- Inner life: Ribera says she thanked God many times that He had made her a daughter of the Church, and that she hoped to be saved "por los merecimientos de Jesucristo" (through the merits of Jesus Christ) [R-1 p.335].
- Testimony: Ribera, *Vida*, p.335, Spanish. Rights: public-domain.
  > Dábale muchas gracias porque la había hecho hija de la Iglesia, y porque moría en ella, y muchas veces repetía esto: «En fin, Señor, soy hija de la Iglesia.» Pedía con mucha devoción perdón á nuestro Señor de sus pecados, y decía que por los merecimientos de Jesucristo nuestro Señor esperaba ser salva, y á las hermanas las pedía rogasen esto á nuestro Señor, y con mucha humildad las pedía perdón.
  > Plain: "She thanked Him many times because He had made her a daughter of the Church and because she was dying in it, and she kept repeating: 'In the end, Lord, I am a daughter of the Church.' With great devotion she asked our Lord to forgive her sins. She said she hoped to be saved through the merits of Jesus Christ, and she asked the sisters to pray for this, and with great humility she asked their pardon."
- What changed: she died; the nuns' sworn accounts later spoke of a sweet smell in the room [P-1 p.170], which belongs to the miracles section.
- Not in the sources: any word of Teresa to the Duchess at the end; a hymn or "Nada te turbe" in her hand (not in Ribera's account); the nuns' faces or weeping; the weather. Do not add dove, star, or other late reports.
- Sources: R-1 pp.334-337 (Ribera 1590, with Yepes and Mariana's process text in footnotes); P-1 pp.83, 170 (Mariana de la Encarnación, Ana de San Bartolomé); V-1 gives her words in Latin-letter English: "I thank You, Lord, because I die a daughter of the Church".

## SC-15 — The declaration, 27 September 1970
- Status: full
- When and where: Sunday 27 Sept 1970, St Peter's Basilica, Rome, during Mass [V-1; V-2].
- Who was there: Paul VI; "legions of faithful from all nations and above all from Spain"; many cardinals and prelates of the Roman Curia (V-1). Spanish pilgrims were present; the homily has a Spanish part.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Paul VI had said on 15 Oct 1967 that he meant to name her a Doctor. The request of the Discalced Carmelite general was among the causes; on 21 July 1969 he approved the vote of the Congregation [V-1].
  2. At Mass, in St Peter's, he said the words of the declaration (below). After that he gave a homily on her holiness and her teaching, and then he offered the sacrifice at the high altar [V-1].
  3. In the homily he called her the first woman to receive the title, said it carries no office of teaching in the hierarchy, and said it does not mean less esteem for women [V-2].
  4. He quoted her last words and asked everyone to repeat them: "siamo figli della Chiesa" [V-2].
- Words spoken (our English; Holy See texts; short quotations):
  - Declaration, English tr. of the Latin letter: "WITH TRUE KNOWLEDGE AND THOUGHTFUL DECISION AND FOR THE FULLNESS OF OUR APOSTOLIC AUTHORITY WE DECLARE SAINT TERESA OF JESUS, VIRGIN OF AVILA, DOCTOR OF THE UNIVERSAL CHURCH" [V-1]. Plain: "With full knowledge, after careful thought, and with the fullness of our apostolic authority, we declare Saint Teresa of Jesus, virgin of Ávila, a Doctor of the Universal Church." The Latin text was not read.
  - Homily opening: "Noi abbiamo conferito, o meglio: Noi abbiamo riconosciuto il titolo di Dottore della Chiesa a Santa Teresa di Gesù." Plain: "We have conferred, or rather We have recognized, the title of Doctor of the Church on Saint Teresa of Jesus." [V-2]
  - Homily: "Prima del suo ultimo respiro, ella poté ben dire, come riepilogo della sua vita: «Finalmente, sono figlia della Chiesa!»" Plain: "Before her last breath she could well say, as a summary of her life: 'At last, I am a daughter of the Church!'" The Spanish part reads "En fin, soy hija de la Iglesia" [V-2].
- Inner life: the letter says that in the Libro de su vida (8:5) "prayer is that necessity of friendship, for which we speak at length alone with God by whom we know we are loved". It says she valued "faithful and humble obedience to God's ministers" more than "visions, revelations, and ecstasies" [V-1]. The homily quotes her *Vida* 8, 4-5 on prayer as friendly dealing [V-2].
- Testimony: none.
- What changed: the Church named a woman "a Doctor of the Church, first among women, especially for her knowledge and doctrine of divine things" [V-1]. The letter also calls her "a woman of simple customs and ignorant of literary culture", which answers the 1570s verdict of a "restless, roving woman" (see the opening).
- Not in the sources: the hour of the Mass; crowd numbers; weather; what Paul VI wore; who read; any statue unveiling. The homily mentions a statue in St Peter's with the title "Mater Spiritualium" (already in the churches notes); it does not say it was blessed that day.
- Sources: V-1 (https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/apost_letters/documents/hf_p-vi_apl_19700927_multiformis-sapientia.html); V-2 (https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/it/homilies/1970/documents/hf_p-vi_hom_19700927.html).

## FN-1 — When the Discalced became a separate province
Pope Gregory XIII's brief *Pia consideratione*, dated 22 June 1580, ordered a separate province for the Discalced. The Discalced chapter at Alcalá on 3 March 1581 put it into effect and elected Jerónimo Gracián first provincial. Sources: Stanbrook, *Letters* vol. 4, Letter CCCXXXV prefatory note (p.55-56; cites Bull and *Acta cap. gen.* I p.562; read this session); Teresian Carmel site karmel.katolsk.no/the-teresian-carmel/foundations/ (names the brief, date, and Gracián; read this session). The brief's own text was not reached.

## FN-2 — Date and purpose of *The Way of Perfection*
Silverio's introduction (*Obras* vol. 3, 1916, pp. XIII-XV; facts only) says the nuns of St Joseph's, Ávila, asked for it, and that Báñez ordered her under obedience to write it, because her *Life* held personal news not fit for them. He dates it to the end of 1565, after the *Life* was finished, and calls this "muy probable", not certain. A Salamanca copy says 1562; Silverio rejects that because the book speaks of thirteen nuns, and the house did not have them before late 1563. She wrote a second, revised copy later (now at Valladolid). Source: archive:obrasdesantatere03tere, introduction.

## FN-3 — How the Discalced spread beyond Spain
The Catholic Encyclopedia (1908), "Carmelite Order" (https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/03354a.htm), says Nicolò Doria was commissioned in 1584 to found a convent at Genoa, followed by Santa Maria della Scala in Rome and one at Naples. It says seven Spanish nuns, among them Anne of Jesus and Anne of St Bartholomew, reached Paris in July 1604 at the wish of Madame Acarie and Bérulle. It says Spanish nuns reached South America early in the 1600s. It does not say Antwerp. The only Antwerp item in the notes is Gracián writing there on 14 March 1610 [P-1 p.XXVI-XXVII]; it is not a foundation date. Sources read this session.
