# Check — Joan of Arc (standard), fact check of biography.html and summary.txt

Originals kept as biography.html.before-check and summary.txt.before-check. I checked quotations against raw OCR of Quicherat vols. 1-4 and Murray 1902 from archive.org, and the Catholic Encyclopedia. Lint is clean (no MUST FIX, no CHECK lines).

## Fixes

Opening
- P1-P2: Joan did not ride "beside the king". Dunois (Q3 p.14) says she rode between the archbishop of Reims and Dunois, and the crowd shouted "Noël". "North of Reims" and "the towns opened their gates" were also not in the source. Rewrote. "At the head of the army" became "with the army, carrying its banner".
- P4: "wished for a grave at home" is wrong (she said "in this land"). "The Catholic Church keeps her feast on 30 May" is too absolute (the notes say the 1969 reform moved the day off the general calendar). Both softened here, in the closing paragraph, and in summary.txt.

Ch. 1-3
- Drapier: "never swore" became "did not swear" (the Latin gives a description, not "never").
- "Herded the animals": now attributed to neighbors. Joan told her judges she did not go to the fields with the sheep (Murray p.10). Added to the Note on Sources.
- Waterin: "the other children laughed" became "others"; his words at Greux are only "To God!", not the same words as Guillemette's.
- Fairies: "none of the children ever saw one" became "no witness had ever seen one".
- "Prayed more than other girls" and "told no one for years" removed or narrowed (not in the sources).
- Father's dream: now told as Joan's report of what her mother said. The father told her brothers to drown her, and said he would do it himself.
- "Herding" cut from the years of silence (Joan denies it).
- Ch. 3 reordered. The farewell scene (Guillemette, Waterin, Hauviette) is not dated to Lent in the sources, and Laxart fetched her from her father's house, so it now comes first, undated.
- "Baudricourt sent her home all the same" removed. Laxart says only that he refused. Poulengy says she left with her uncle.
- "Heard the farmer out" and "wool" (Royer says she spun) removed.
- Horse: Laxart says he and Jacques Alain bought it, Royer says the townspeople did. Told in the passive.
- "Refused a second time, third visit" became Joan's own words: twice refused, third time received.
- "Go, and let come what may" is Joan's report of Baudricourt, so it is now attributed to her.
- Fierbois: three Masses "in one day", not "one morning" (Q1 p.75, Murray p.28).

Ch. 4
- Chinon recognition: Joan's own words (Murray p.13) are that she knew the king "among many others" by the counsel of her Voice. Removed "he stood among his courtiers" and "walked to him". The "secret" now rests on the chaplain's report of what the king said (Murray, Pasquerel). Removed "she never said in public".
- Cagny: knights and squires "who were always at it", not "all their lives".
- Alençon: removed the invented "one of them the duke never forgot". "Rightful state" became "former state" (Latin: pristinum statum).
- Coutes: "set to wait on her" removed. The Latin says he stayed with her by day. His memory of her kneeling is now marked as his memory, years later.
- Examination: Joan says three weeks at Chinon and Poitiers. "One of the churchmen said" the "nothing but a good Christian" line (d'Aulon p.209).
- Banner: "into every fight" became "when she attacked". "Loved ... forty times" became "cared for" (Murray p.31). "She never killed anyone" became "in her own words".
- Letter to the English: Joan named three mistakes, not one ("Maid" for "King", plus "chieftain of war" and "body for body").
- "By my martin" is now credited to a chronicler of her side. "Favorite oath puzzled even the scholar" became a plain sentence.

Ch. 5-6
- Removed "Joan rode up to him", "the wind blew hard" (the Latin says contrary), "the oath she used all her life", and "remembered every word" (the Latin says nothing of "every word").
- Removed the chapter ending "the captains still made their plans without her" (not in the sources). Replaced with Dunois's own line, that she was greatly wanted.
- Coutes and d'Aulon kept apart. The wounded Frenchman at the gate is d'Aulon's, so it is now under his name. Coutes has the other wounded men, the churchmen, and the bread.
- Coutes: "an English captain" became "a man called the Bastard of Granville"; "sent him running" became "told him"; "when he came back with the horse she was ready" became "when he came to ready the horse she was already armed"; "churchmen came out of the fort" became "came to meet her". Removed "her first attack outside the walls" (not in the sources).
- 7 May: "the French were worn out" became "almost no hope of winning" (Latin); "Dunois decided" became "wanted". Removed "what she did next he never forgot". Removed "up" from d'Aulon shouting. "Let them go, she said" became indirect speech (Latin: they must be allowed to leave).
- Vineyard prayer: the Latin "dimidii quarti horae" is not a clear "quarter of an hour". Murray prints "half an hour". Now "a short while" in the block quote, with a line in the Note.
- 8 May feast: "has kept ... ever since" conflicts with the Revolution gap. Now "came to keep". Later lines fixed in the same way ("went on keeping", the bull of 1920 "notes that Orléans kept ..."; "still keeps" removed).
- One-year prophecy: "soon after this" removed. d'Alençon p.99 gives no time. The four tasks are now in the order of the Latin.

Ch. 7-9
- Jargeau: "charged ahead and the men followed" is not in the Latin (she went in, urging the men to take heart). "Good reason" removed. The banner was struck, and the stone hit her helmet (it did not strike the banner). "Only a light helmet" became "a light helmet".
- "Took no pleasure in killing" became "showed pity".
- Reims road: "country north of Reims" removed.
- "The autumn was quiet" removed (contradicted Saint-Pierre in the next paragraph). Bourges and Saint-Pierre are no longer ordered.
- Gaucourt "carried" her out became "took".
- Paris: "put in her mouth" became "reported".
- Melun: she was "in the trenches", not on the walls (Murray p.58).
- Compiègne: removed Cagny's "9 a.m." from the narrative (Joan said evening). Added both to the Note.
- Flavy rumor: "It is a rumor, because the record says only ..." became "It was only a rumor. Cagny ... says only ...".
- Price of 10,000 francs now "a price of", with Cagny's different figure in the Note.

Ch. 10-12
- 21 Feb: Joan did not refuse to swear. She knelt and swore, but would not reveal her revelations (Murray p.6). 22 Feb is the "burden me" exchange. "Came in wearing chains" became "complained that she was kept in chains".
- Chapel: it was the prosecutor d'Estivet who objected, not the bishop. Massieu let her pray (Murray p.171-172). Fixed.
- "Came in with something to tell them" and "a question meant to catch her" removed (not in the sources).
- "Smelled good": "It is well to know, they smelled good" was misread. Now "the court should know that they did". "Again and again" about the clothes became "also".
- "Paris went back to the French king in November 1437" became "The English lost Paris in 1436" (Murray's note; CE gives 12 Nov 1437 as the royal entry).
- "Courage failed for once" became "that day". Ladvenu's "heard her confession" became "said she told him".
- Isambart on her face: "marked by blows" over-reads Murray. The French is "defigurée et oultragée". Now "disfigured". "That day" became "after she went back to men's clothes".
- Maurice's question reaches us through Ricquier, not Maurice. Fixed in ch. 12 and in the closing paragraph. "Hope in the Lord" became "in God" (Murray p.302).
- "The secular hand meant the English" removed; "both friars said" no civil sentence was passed.
- Isambart block quote: dropped "begging the saints for help" without an ellipsis. Restored.
- Executioner: "in terror" was wrong. Isambart says despair and fear that God would not pardon him. Now attributed to Isambart and Ladvenu.

Ch. 13-14
- "For eighteen years she carried a daughter's name" (invented feeling) removed. Isabelle is "also known as Romée" (the record says d'Arc).
- 1456 sentence: the sermon and the cross are both at the Old Market the day after the Saint-Ouen procession. Fixed.
- Pius II "a few years later" became "later". "Four centuries" became "more than four".
- Thérèse: removed "for her sisters to act" and "prayed for Joan's cause" (not verified). Titles only.
- Heroic virtue is no longer called the "first great step". The "so the bull named her a virgin" cause was removed (one-sided; the notes only say what the bull did).
- Closing: "left four short letters" (there are more; the notes read four) became "a handful". "Church has read those answers for five hundred years" removed. "The hardest question a judge could ask" removed. "Orléans still keeps" and "no grave anywhere" fixed. "There is no known grave."

Notes and counts
- "Twenty-five years after" became "twenty years or more". "More than twenty details" became "about twenty" (the notes flag about that many).
- "Translated every quotation afresh" was wrong. Murray-based lines were rendered into modern words, so it now says "most".
- Ladvenu and Toutmouillé both give the words to the bishop. March 1450 is explained.

summary.txt
- "A church court burned her" became "condemned her ... the English burned her". "At the head" became "with". The two wishes (grave and sheep) no longer read as "one thing". The feast question uses "honor".

## Checked and left as is
Quotations match the originals on the cards, or Murray, for: Dunois (Loire, vineyard, ride from Reims), d'Aulon, Coutes, d'Alençon (Jargeau), Joan on 22 and 24 Feb, 10 and 14 March, 28 May, Isambart on the cross, the 1455 record, Cagny, the Tournai and Burgundy letters, Touroulde, Midi, and the plan's Murray passages (the plain wording matches Murray's meaning). Settled points all hold: slaps, Ascension 1428, no date on the wind, Saturday 7 May, no fasting, Coutes and d'Aulon apart, light helmet, Paris and the denial, capture in her own words, "I die through you" as memory, Isabelle as reported speech, 7 July 1456, 2 March 1922, Thérèse by title.

## Open
- Order of the Alençon scene against Poitiers is not settled in the sources. The text does not claim an order.
- "Cheerful" and the poor-people bed are from Guérin and Isabellette's deposition. They are kept as told.

Verdict: ready.
