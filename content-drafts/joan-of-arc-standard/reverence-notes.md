# Reverence pass — Saint Joan of Arc (standard), 2026-10-05

Files changed: biography.html, miracles.html, summary.txt. Originals: *.before-reverence. entry.json was not touched.

Sources checked this pass: Quicherat vols. 1–3 (archive.org OCR, `procsdecondam01/02/03joanuoft`), the bull *Divina disponente* (vatican.va), the three notes files, and the scene cards. Page numbers are Quicherat's running heads.

## Changes of substance in biography.html

Each line gives the change and where the fact comes from. Nothing below adds a fact that is not in the notes, the cards, or the source text named.

1. **Opening, par. 4.** "a voice she said came from God" became "a Voice from God". Trial record, 22 Feb 1431: "habuit vocem a Deo" (Q1 p.52; SC-02). The Church, in the bull, treats the Voices as from God.
2. **"Voice" and "Voices" capitalized** wherever the narrator means her heavenly Voices (about twenty places, chapters 2, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14). Words inside quotation marks and block quotes were not changed. "The voices of farmers and soldiers and priests" (ch. 13) stays lowercase; it is not the same thing.
3. **Ch. 1.** "draw apart and speak to God, or so it seemed to him" became "went off alone to speak with God, as it seemed to him". Waterin, Q2 p.420 ("ut sibi videbatur"). Same fact, without the skeptical "or so".
4. **Ch. 4, Chinon.** "Nobody knows for sure what they were" (the king's secrets) became "At her trial she refused to tell her judges what they were." Trial, Q1 p.75: "Vos non habebitis eas adhuc a me de isto anno" (notes-church-miracles, quotation 1; notes-own-words, quotation 14).
5. **Ch. 4, the banner.** Added "The names Jesus Maria were written on it." Trial, 27 Feb 1431, Q1 p.78: "erantque scripta ibi ista nomina Jhesus Maria" (confirmed in the OCR at two places, the Latin minute and the summary). Dunois gives the image of Our Lord with the lily (Q3 p.7, SC-05).
6. **Ch. 4, Blois.** "receive Communion" became "receive Holy Communion" (Coutes, Q3 p.67). The two sentences on what "martin" meant ("Nobody is sure… The scholar who printed the chronicle…") moved to "A Note on the Sources". In their place: "That was her own oath, and the chronicler gives it more than once." Cagny gives it four times (Q4 pp.5, 8, 27, 33; notes-witnesses quotations 14–16).
7. **Ch. 5, the wind.** Added: "Dunois judged from that wind, and from the food that came into the city against a stronger English army, that her deeds were from God and not from man." Dunois, Q3 pp.6–7: "potius erant a Deo quam ab homine, attentis mutatione venti subito facta… et introductione victualium, invitis Anglicis, qui longe fortiores erant exercitu regio" (confirmed in the OCR; also notes-church-miracles, miracle A.8, and miracles.html m13).
8. **Ch. 8, Bourges.** Added: "she said that she had been sent for the comfort of the poor and needy." Touroulde, Q3 p.88: "libentissime subveniebat indigentibus et pauperibus, dicens quod erat missa pro consolatione pauperum et indigentium" (confirmed in the OCR). Note for the checker: notes-witnesses section 4 renders this "said the Mass was for the comfort of the poor"; "missa" here is "sent", not "Mass".
9. **Ch. 8, Melun.** Added: "They told her not to be dismayed but to accept it willingly, because God would help her." Trial, 10 March 1431, Q1 p.115 (miracles.html m22; notes-own-words, Murray pp.57–58).
10. **Ch. 9, the Compiègne rumor.** Cut "and old men were still telling it there nearly seventy years after. It was only a rumor." The rumor is still named and answered by Cagny in the body, and the 1498 detail stays in "A Note on the Sources". Framing only; no fact lost from the page.
11. **Ch. 10.** "Her answer is in the Latin record" became "A clerk wrote her answer down." Framing only.
12. **Ch. 12, the death.** "received Communion" became "received Holy Communion" (Massieu, Murray p.175; SC-14). The executioner "feared that God would never forgive what he had done to Saint Joan" now ends with the witness's own words, "to this holy woman": Isambart, Q2 p.7, "de ce qu'il avoit faict à ceste saincte femme" (confirmed in the OCR). The words are inside quotation marks.
13. **Ch. 12, the ashes.** "Then the English gave orders about what was left. The ashes and the heart went into the Seine" became "Then the English had her ashes and her heart thrown into the Seine". Bull of 1920: "Angli vero voluerunt ut illud cum Ioannae cineribus in Sequanam flumen proiiceretur" (confirmed); notes-church-miracles timeline, "by English order". Real actor as subject.
14. **Ch. 12, the chapter ending.** Cut the closing two sentences from the hostile Paris Journal ("A Paris writer who hated her side summed it up in a phrase. Whatever bad or good she had done, she had been burned that day."). The chapter now ends on the Seine, as the outline planned. Tone only.
15. **Ch. 13, indulgence.** "a grant of pardon from the Church" became "a gift of the Church that frees a person from some or all of the penalty still owed for sins already forgiven". A plainer and more exact description of the Church's own term; no new fact about the 1452 grant.
16. **Ch. 13.** "The record keeps no words of her own, only what she asked" became "The clerk did not write down her own words, only what she asked." Framing only (SC-15).
17. **Ch. 13, the sentence of 1456.** "The trial of 1431… were annulled. It ordered… The next day it ordered… It also ordered…" became "They declared the trial of 1431, the signed paper, and the two judgments against her null and void. They ordered… A fitting cross was to stand there for perpetual memory." Same facts (Q3 pp.361–362; FN-3); the judges are the actors.
18. **Ch. 14.** "miracles worked through her prayers" became "miracles that God had worked through her prayers". Bull: "miracula, quae ad intercessionem Aurelianensis Puellae patrata" (confirmed). "Two more cures were accepted in 1918" became "The Church accepted two more cures in 1918" (same bull). One long sentence split.
19. **Ch. 14, what she left.** Removed the opening "She could not write a book, and she founded no order" (it opened the paragraph with what is missing). The rest of the paragraph keeps the same four things she left: the letters, the trial answers, the Orléans feast (bull of 1920), the Rouen cross (1456), and her answer on grace.
20. **Closing paragraph.** "The Seine took what the fire left" became "She has no known grave, because the English threw what the fire left into the Seine." (Sources as in item 13.) The last sentences now follow Ricquier's report of Pierre Maurice in order: he asked whether she had good hope in God; she said yes; God willing, she would be in Paradise that evening (Murray p.302; notes-own-words reserve line).
21. **A Note on the Sources.** Added the two sentences on "by my martin" and Quicherat's guess (Q4 p.4 note; notes-witnesses quotation 14), moved from ch. 4.

Small wording changes with no change of fact: ch. 2, one "Saint Joan" became "She" to avoid a double; ch. 2 and ch. 13, two sentences reworded to break a run of three sentences starting with the same word (lint).

## Changes of substance in miracles.html

1. **Opening paragraph.** Rewritten to present the list as the Church's memory of God's works through Saint Joan, gathered from her trial answers, the sworn witnesses of 1450–1456, the bull of 1920, and the old Lives. Removed "Every other entry is a report". Same sources named.
2. **Approved healings, intro.** Added that the bull calls them miracles worked at Saint Joan's intercession ("ad intercessionem Aurelianensis Puellae patrata", confirmed). "did without the fourth" became "set the fourth aside" (bull: "a quarto miraculo iam dispensasset").
3. **m01–m05.** Each account now opens "Through the prayers of Saint Joan, God healed [name] instantly and perfectly. The bull of 1920 records the healing." Then the illness as before. Source: the bull, same passages; the intercession wording is the bull's own (item 2).
4. **After m05.** "many other graces of soul and body, and wonderful healings" now adds "granted by God through her help". Bull: "plures gratiae… eius ope a Deo concessae et prodigiosae sanationes" (confirmed). "It does not list them" became "It does not name them".
5. **m06 status.** "reported by" became "told by". Wording only.
6. **m08 status.** "The scene itself is a traditional account, which the Catholic Encyclopedia also tells" became "with the scene as Guérin's Life and the Catholic Encyclopedia tell it." Same tellers, no label.
7. **m11 status.** "a documented prophecy" became "a prophecy written down before the events", with the Brussels registration kept (Catholic Encyclopedia).
8. **m12 status.** Dropped "nineteenth-century" and "Compare"; now "told in Guérin's Life. See also the Lyon letter above."
9. **m14.** "after a vision of two saints comforted her" became "after her saints came to her and comforted her" (Guérin p.398, as in the notes).
10. **Status lines that said only "sworn testimony at the retrial of 1456"** now name the teller: Lord Gaucourt (siege promised as sign; wind foretold), Dunois (m13), the duke of Alençon (m16; Patay; Orléans credited to God). The tellers are those named in each account's own text and Source line.
11. **Orléans credited to God, status.** "The soldiers' words are told at second hand" became "who gives the soldiers' words as he heard them" (Q3 p.94; check-miracles.md confirms he was not present).
12. **Glasdale foretold, status.** "told at second hand in the Journal" became "told in the Journal of a Paris citizen for 1429, as a thing people were saying" (Q4 p.463, "disoit on").
13. **m28 status.** Now names the tellers with trust: Brother Isambart (1450) and Maugier Leparmentier (1456) for her cries; the bull, Guérin, and the priest Thomas Marie (1452) for the name in the flames. Replaces "told at second hand". Same sources as the account's text.
14. **m29 status.** "told by a witness at the inquiry of 1452" now names Brother Isambart de la Pierre and gives "the bull of 1920" and "Guérin's Life" in full.
15. **m30 status.** Names Brother Isambart; dropped "years after the event".
16. **m31.** Added: "The man was in despair, and he feared that God would never pardon what he had done 'to this holy woman.'" Isambart, Q2 p.7: "comme tout désespéré, craingnant de non savoir jamais impétrer pardon et indulgence envers Dieu, de ce qu'il avoit faict à ceste saincte femme" (confirmed). The quoted words are inside quotation marks.
17. **m23 and m26.** "her voices" became "her Voices" in the narrator's text.

## Not changed on purpose

- Every quotation and block quote, including lowercase "voice" inside them.
- "Some doubted even her purity" (ch. 14): the 1902 cardinals' objections are a fact of the cause, told plainly.
- The hostile Paris Journal is still named as hostile where it is a source for a scene (ch. 8, Paris), because a reader would otherwise be misled.
- The "A Note on the Sources" sections of both files keep the date and wording differences; that is where they belong.
- "Charlemagne" is left without a title, as in the titles pass.
