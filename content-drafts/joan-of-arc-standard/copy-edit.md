8. "… she begged him 'with joined hands' to make no more war against them." It is not clear who "them" is: the French, the king's people, or the holy kingdom. Check the letter (note 46) and name them.
9. "She also had a white banner made, with the image of Our Lord holding a lily." Check the image against her trial answers (note 27).
10. "Over the next two days, more English forts fell on the far bank." Check this against the dates of 5 and 6 May (note 34).
11. "The friars' first statements are dated 1449 …" (A Note on the Sources): "the friars" is not clear here. Check which witnesses are meant, and name them if the sources allow.
12. The glosses I added for "mortal sin" (item 39) and "relapsed heretic" (item 50) are explanations, not facts about Saint Joan. Confirm that they are right and fit the reverence rules.


## Resolved by the checker (2026-10-07)

1. Laxart and the house (note 15): Laxart came to Domrémy to fetch her (Lacloppe, Q2 p. 411; Guillemette, Q2 p. 416). Guillemette saw her pass her father's house with him and heard "Ad Deum, ego vado ad Vallis-Colorem". Laxart says he fetched her "ad domum patris" and took her to his own house at Burey (Q2 p. 444). The text now says he came to Domrémy and that she passed her father's house with him. "A cousin" became "a relative" (Laxart: "de parentela"). "She begged him" became "asked him" (Laxart: "dixit ... quod iret").
2. Who asked "who her lord was" (note 18): Baudricourt asked (Poulengy, Q2 p. 456: "Qui Robertus ab ea petiit quis esset ejus Dominus"). The text now says "The captain asked her".
3. "Them" in the letter to Burgundy (note 46): "contre nous" in the letter means her side. The text says "the king's side".
4. Banner image (note 27): Saint Joan told her judges: lilies scattered over the white cloth, the world painted on it, an angel on each side (Q1 p. 78; Murray p. 31). Dunois remembers Our Lord holding a lily. The text follows her; the Note on the Sources gives both.
5. "Over the next two days" (note 34): Coutes and d'Aulon compress the days; the Journal puts 4 May on the eve of Ascension. The text now says "In the days that followed". The dates 5 and 6 May are not stated in the sources we cite.
6. The friars (Note on the Sources): the first statements of the 1450 inquiry, among them Toutmouillé, Isambart, and Ladvenu, all dated 5 March 1449 (old style). The Note now names them.
7. Quotations: "the Maid" is introduced where Dunois first hears of her ("vulgariter dicta Puella", Q3 p. 3). "The secular power" became "the civil authorities". The first "from fear of the fire" (block quote of 28 May) was cut; her own words in the next paragraph stay. "Bloody boy" became "wretched boy". Originals are in `plan.md`.
8. The four Church-word glosses (items 5, 39, 50, 57 above): "sexton who rang the church bell" is right (matricularius; Drapier rang the compline bell). "A sin grave enough to cut her off from God" is the Church's meaning of mortal sin. "Relapsed heretic" after "gone back on the paper she signed" is right (the judges' charge). "Bull" is the right name for the formal papal letter. All four stay.
9. Kept on purpose, now settled: "Then she asked for the cross from the church" moved to its place in Massieu's order (after the little cross, before the soldiers' "What, priest!"), so it no longer repeats Isambart's block quote out of order.
