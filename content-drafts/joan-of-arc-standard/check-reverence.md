# Check of the reverence pass, Saint Joan of Arc (standard), 2026-10-05

Copies before this check: biography.html.before-check-rev, miracles.html.before-check-rev, summary.txt.before-check-rev. entry.json and the teachings files were not touched. Lint: biography (with summary) and miracles show no MUST FIX and no CHECK lines.

Sources read raw: Quicherat vols. 1-4 (archive.org OCR text), the bull Divina disponente (vatican.va Latin text).

## Confirmed in the raw source (no change needed)

- Dunois, Q3 pp. 6-7: "potius erant a Deo quam ab homine, attentis mutatione venti subito facta ... et introductione victualium, invitis Anglicis, qui longe fortiores erant exercitu regio".
- Touroulde, Q3 p. 88: "dicens quod erat missa pro consolatione pauperum et indigentium". "Missa" is "sent"; she is the one speaking.
- Isambart, Q2 p. 7 (inquiry of 1450): the executioner, "comme tout desespere, craingnant de non savoir jamais impetrer pardon et indulgence envers Dieu, de ce qu'il avoit faict a ceste saincte femme". The m31 "also feared he was damned" from 1452 is Q2 (Isambart 1452), so no clash.
- Banner, Q1 p. 78 (27 Feb 1431): "erantque scripta ibi ista nomina Jhesus Maria, sicut ei videtur", on the side.
- Bull of 1920: "illa miracula, quae ad intercessionem Aurelianensis Puellae patrata" (opening, with the miracles named as a sign of God's justice and mercy); "plures gratiae ... eius ope a Deo concessae et prodigiosae sanationes"; "a quarto miraculo iam dispensasset"; "Angli vero voluerunt ut illud [cor] cum Ioannae cineribus in Sequanam flumen proiiceretur". Each of the five healings is "instantaneae perfectaeque sanationis".
- Melun, Q1 p. 115: her Voices (Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret) told her she would be taken before Saint John's Day, "qu'elle ne s'eshahist, et print tout en gre, et que Dieu lui aideroit".
- Last morning, Q3 p. 191 (Ricquier, from Pierre Maurice): she asked "Where shall I be this evening?"; Maurice asked whether she had good hope in the Lord; she said yes and that, "Deo favente", she would be in Paradise.
- Quicherat's guess on "par mon martin" (Q4 p. 4 note, stick or Saint Martin) is in the Note on the Sources correctly.
- The Waterin passage (Q2, "se trahebat ad partem et loquebatur Deo, ut sibi videbatur") supports the ch. 1 sentence.

## Fixes made

biography.html
1. Ch. 1, Waterin: "went off alone" became "went off to one side". The Latin says "ad partem", not "alone".
2. Ch. 4, Chinon: "At her trial she refused to tell her judges what they were" claimed too much. At the hearing she was asked what revelations the king had, and answered "You will not have them from me yet this year"; on 13 March she later told the sign of the angel and the crown. Now: "When her judges asked at her trial what the king had learned, she said they would not have it from her yet that year."
3. Ch. 4, banner: "The names Jesus Maria were written on it" now says "She told her judges that...". The source is her own answer, with "as it seems to me".
4. Ch. 5, Dunois: "were from God and not from man" became "came from God rather than from man", the Latin "potius ... quam". It matches m13.
5. Ch. 8, Melun: "because God would help her" became "and that God would help her". The Voices' words are a list, not a reason.
6. Ch. 14, what she left: the pass had made Saint Joan the actor of "she left the feast" and "she left the cross". Orléans kept the feast and the court ordered the cross. Restored the literal form: "The bull of 1920 notes that Orléans kept her feast every 8 May, and the court of 1456 ordered a cross for her in the Old Market at Rouen." The removed opener stays removed.
7. Closing paragraph: the report of the last morning had lost its teller and now read as the narrator's own fact. Added: "The priest Pierre Maurice later told it." The question "where shall I be this evening" stays in ch. 12 with its attribution.

miracles.html
8. m22: "because God would help her" became "and God would help her" (same reason as 5).
9. Opening paragraph: "Each status line names who told the account" is not true of m01-m05 ("approved by the Church"). Now "says where the account comes from".
10. m32 status: dropped "nineteenth-century" and "as a tradition" (labels the pass removes elsewhere; m12 already dropped it).

## Checked and kept

- m01-m05, "Through the prayers of Saint Joan, God healed...": supported. The bull says all these miracles were worked "ad intercessionem" of the Maid and are a sign of God's mercy; each healing is "instantaneae perfectaeque". The line "The bull of 1920 records the healing" keeps the source named. No detail was added beyond that.
- m31: "in despair... feared that God would never pardon... 'to this holy woman'" is in Isambart (1450). The quoted words stay inside quotation marks.
- Orléans and wind accounts (m13, wind foretold): unchanged facts; teller names added in status lines match each account's text.
- m28-m30 status lines: tellers match the account texts.
- Cut of the Paris Journal's closing line ("Whatever bad or good she had done, she had been burned that day"): no fact lost. The burning is told in ch. 12, and the hostile Journal is still named where it is a source for a scene.
- Cut of "old men were still telling it ... seventy years after": the 1498 fact stays in the Note on the Sources.
- "Voice" and "Voices" capitalized in narrator text, consistent in biography, miracles, and summary. Quotation blocks keep the source's lowercase. "the voice of an angel" (generic, ch. 2) and "the voices of farmers and soldiers" (ch. 13) stay lowercase, correctly.
- "Saint" before Saint Michael, Saint Catherine, Saint Margaret and every other saint's name in the narrator's text: checked. Pope Calixtus III and Pope Pius II are not canonized and carry no title.
- Real actor: the new ashes sentence ("the English had her ashes and her heart thrown into the Seine") names the English as the actor, as the bull does ("Angli voluerunt"). The 1456 sentence now has the judges as actors.
- Tone: no clinical or legal framing, no doubt in the body; scholars' questions are only in the Note on the Sources.
