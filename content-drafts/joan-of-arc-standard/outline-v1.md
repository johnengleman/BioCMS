# Joan of Arc — scene outline (standard 1b)

Tier A. Catholic. Slug: joan-of-arc-standard.

**Key prefixes.** The three notes files number their sources separately, so every key has a file prefix:

- **W-** = `notes-witnesses.md` (W-S1–S5 = Quicherat vols. 1–5; W-S3 = rehabilitation witnesses; W-S4 = Cagny and the Bourgeois de Paris).
- **O-** = `notes-own-words.md` (O-S1 = Murray 1902, English trial and depositions; O-S3 = Quicherat, the letters).
- **C-** = `notes-church-miracles.md` (C-S1 = 1920 bull; C-S2 = Guérin; C-S3 = Catholic Encyclopedia; C-S5, C-S10 = facts-only web pages). C-S4 vol.3 is the same book as W-S3.

## 1. The driving question

Joan wanted to obey the Voice that sent a farm girl to raise a siege and crown a king, though she "knew nothing of riding or fighting" [O-S1 p.11]. Captains doubted her, a church court condemned her, and at the end her own fear of the fire made her sign a recantation [O-S1 p.137]. She took it back, kept faith with her Voices, and died calling on Jesus with a cross before her eyes [W-S2 p.6-7; C-S3].

She is famous as the peasant girl who freed Orléans, brought the king to his crowning at Reims, and was burned at about nineteen [C-S3; O-S1 p.6]. She could not read, yet she talked of war like old knights and led soldiers [W-S4 p.3; W-S3 p.100; C-S2 p.391]. She carried a banner, said she had never killed anyone, made soldiers confess, and held a dying English prisoner's head [O-S1 p.31; W-S3 p.67, 71-72].

## 2. Portrait

Joan was cheerful and liked to see a happy face [C-S2 p.390]. Her village teased her for going to church too often [W-S2 p.418, 420]. She had a will of her own from childhood: she scolded the sexton for skipping the evening bell and bribed him with wool [W-S2 p.412-413]. Under pressure she was quick and dry ("You burden me over-much!"), and proud of a plain skill: "in sewing and spinning I fear no woman in Rouen" [O-S1 p.9]. She swore "by my martin" [W-S4 p.5]. Her temper was real: she broke her sword chasing a camp woman, raged at delays, and threatened to pull a notary's ears [W-S3 p.68, 99; O-S1 p.305]. She ate little [W-S3 p.69]. The sight of French blood made her hair stand on end [W-S3 p.213]. She laughed at women who brought rosaries for her to touch [W-S3 p.87]. Her faults: impatience, the leap from Beaurevoir, which she called her gravest fault [O-S1 pp.74-75], and the recantation signed from fear.

Her life with God: she knelt in the fields at the bell [W-S2 p.420]. Her page saw her kneel in prayer, sometimes weeping [W-S3 p.66]. She asked her Voice for nothing "but the salvation of my soul" [O-S1 p.13]. Asked if she was in God's grace: "If I am not, may God place me there; if I am, may God so keep me" [O-S1 p.18].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** a hook, the broken picture: a girl wishes for a grave at home and gets none.

**Draft:** In August 1429, a few weeks after the crowning at Reims, Joan rode with the king through country where the people were glad to see him. "Here is a good people," she said to the archbishop of Reims. She wished that when her days were done she could be buried in that land. Then she said she wished God would let her put down her arms and go home, to serve her father and mother and keep their sheep [C-S4 vol.3 p.14-15]. She never went home. Less than two years later the English threw her ashes and her heart into the Seine, so that nothing of her would be left [C-S1; C-S2].

**Need:** the place on the road and who heard her (Dunois, W-S3 p.14-15).

**Thesis:** Joan knew only the Our Father, the Hail Mary, and the Creed [C-S2 p.391]. In 1429 she freed Orléans and stood at the altar at Reims with her banner [C-S3]. A church court burned her in 1431; a papal court cleared her in 1456; Benedict XV canonized her in 1920 [C-S1]. Chapters 13–14 answer it and close the grave loop.

## 4. Chapters

1. **Joan Grows Up in a Farm Village.** SC-01. Ends: a summer noon in her father's garden.
2. **Joan Hears a Voice in Her Father's Garden.** SC-02. Ends: "Go, raise the siege," and her answer that she is a poor girl.
3. **Joan Leaves Home to Find the King.** SC-03. Ends: eleven days across enemy ground [C-S1; C-S2 p.394].
4. **Joan Meets the King and Is Questioned.** SC-04. Bridge: the tower prayer; Poitiers ("I am not come to Poitiers to shew signs", O-S1 p.307). Ends: armor, the army at Blois.
5. **Joan Brings Help to Besieged Orléans.** SC-05. Ends: inside the city, the captains plan without her.
6. **Joan Is Wounded and Orléans Is Freed.** SC-06, SC-07. Ends: "I shall last a year, and little more" [W-S3 p.99].
7. **Joan Brings the King to Reims.** SC-08. Bridge: Patay, the English prisoner, Reims and the banner [C-S3], the road of the hook. Ends: the turn toward Paris.
8. **Joan Fails to Take Paris.** SC-09. Bridge: Bourges [W-S3 p.85-88]; at Melun her Voices say she will be taken before St John's Day [C-S2 p.402]. Ends: she rides on to her friends at Compiègne.
9. **Joan Is Captured and Sold to the English.** SC-10, SC-11. Ends: taken to Rouen.
10. **Joan Faces Church Judges in Rouen.** SC-12. Ends: led to a scaffold on 24 May.
11. **Joan Signs a Recantation and Takes It Back.** SC-13. Ends: on 30 May a friar comes to her cell with the news.
12. **The Burning of Joan in Rouen.** SC-14. Ends: the ashes go into the river.
13. **Joan's Mother Asks the Pope to Clear Her.** SC-15. Ends: a cross at Rouen, a feast at Orléans, but no saint for four centuries.
14. **How Joan Became a Saint of the Church.** No scene. Ends: the girl with no grave has a feast on the day she died [C-S1].

## 5. The scenes

- What happens: Joan scolds the sexton for skipping the evening bell and promises him wool.
- Why it is here: her will and her love of prayer, early.

### SC-02 — The Voice in the garden (Domrémy, summer, age 13)
- What happens: at noon a Voice comes from the church side with a light; she is very frightened. Later it sends her to Orléans.
- Why it is here: the call that drives the life.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Writings:** dictated letters: to the English, 22 Mar 1429 [O-S3 pp.95-97]; to Tournai, 25 Jun 1429 [O-S3 p.125]; to Burgundy on coronation day [O-S3 pp.126-127]; to Reims, 1430 [O-S3 p.160]. Trial answers, 1431 [O-S1]. Ch. 4, 7, 8, 10.
- **What she changed:** Orléans kept 8 May every year from 1429 [C-S1]; an indulgence for it, 1452 [C-S5]; the Rouen cross, 1456 [C-S2]; Thérèse of Lisieux wrote verses and prayers for her cause [C-other]; secondary patron of France, 2 Mar 1922 [C-S5]. Ch. 13–14.
- **Why famous:** Pius II's praise [C-S1]; Dupanloup's sermons at Orléans and a bishops' petition, 1869 [C-S5; C-S3]; 60,000 at the canonization with 140 of her family [C-S5]. Ch. 14.
- **Path:** cause opened 27 Jan 1894 [C-S1]; 1902 delay over capture and abjuration [C-S5]; heroic virtue 6 Jan 1904 [C-S1]; beatified 18 Apr 1909 [C-S5]; canonized 16 May 1920 as a virgin, not a martyr [C-S1; C-S5]; feast 30 May [C-S1]. Ch. 14.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth: 6 Jan or 6 Feb 1412; write "about 1412" [C-S1; C-S3].
- Age at Chinon: 16 or 18 [W-S3 p.209; W-S4 p.3].
- Dunois's "27 May" is the 7 May wound; the wound site differs [C-S2 p.398; C-S3].
- Capture 23 or 24 May 1430; price 10,000 francs [C-S1; C-S2], Cagny differs [W-S4 p.35].
- Old-style 1428 in Cagny and Murray means 1429.
- Vaucouleurs: left 13 Feb [C-S2 p.394] versus a prophecy there on 17 Feb [C-S3].
- The prison assault is her report to Ladvenu: attribute it [W-S2 p.8].
- Guérin's dialogue is 19th-century narrative: prefer the depositions.
- Murray's English needs modern rendering; not compared with Quicherat.
- Names: Dunois, "the Bastard of Orléans"; her mother, Isabelle Romée.
