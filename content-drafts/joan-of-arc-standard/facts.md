# Facts, Saint Joan of Arc (standard, heart draft), approved 2026-10-07

Use these exactly. The numbers in brackets are the notes of `biography.html`. A later writer who finds a source that disagrees lists the conflict for the checker.

## Names and spellings
- Saint Joan of Arc (the biography says "Saint Joan" after the first mention). At home: Jeannette [5]. Her side called her "the Maid" (Dunois, "Puella"); never "Maid of Orléans" in our text.
- Father Jacques d'Arc; mother Isabelle (not "Romée" in the text); brother Pierre d'Arc (taken with her at Compiègne) [61, 97]; friend Hauviette [6]; uncle Durand Laxart of Little Burey, married to a relative of hers [15]; captain Robert de Baudricourt of Vaucouleurs [13]; Bertrand de Poulengy [18].
- King Charles (the Dauphin until the coronation); Dunois, "the Bastard of Orléans"; the duke of Alençon; Jean d'Aulon (steward); Louis de Coutes (her page; the text says "her page"); the Basque; La Hire; Talbot; Glasdale (the English captain who drowned); the duke of Burgundy; Jean de Luxembourg; Perceval de Cagny.
- Bishop Pierre Cauchon of Beauvais; Guillaume Érard (preacher at Saint-Ouen); Jean Massieu (court usher); Brother Isambart de la Pierre; Brother Martin Ladvenu; Brother Jean Toutmouillé; Nicolas Midi (preacher at the Old Market); Pierre Maurice; Jean Ricquier; Pope Calixtus III; Pope Benedict XV.
- Places: Domrémy, Vaucouleurs, Chinon, Poitiers, Orléans, Saint-Loup, the Tourelles, Jargeau, Patay, Reims, Saint-Denis, Saint-Pierre-le-Moûtier, Melun, Compiègne, Beaulieu, Beaurevoir, Rouen, Saint-Ouen, the Old Market (Vieux-Marché).
- Her words for the saints: "Voice" (first), then Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret; the first Voice was Saint Michael.

## Dates
- Thursday 22 February 1431: first public hearing at Rouen's castle, with 48 assessors [1]. Saturday 24 February: the hearing on the Voices [71].
- Ascension 1428: first visit to Vaucouleurs (Poulengy) [18]. Near the start of Lent 1429: she returns and stays [19]. Early March 1429: Chinon [21]. 22 March 1429: letter to the English [27].
- Wednesday 4 May 1429 (eve of Ascension): Saint-Loup taken [33]. Saturday 7 May 1429: the Tourelles; wound and vineyard [34, 35]. Sunday 8 May: the English leave [38]. (The text does not date 5 and 6 May.)
- June 1429: Jargeau (Cagny: the siege began Saturday 11 June, the assault was Sunday 12 June) [40]. 18 June: Patay [44]. Sunday 17 July 1429: coronation at Reims; letter to Burgundy that day [45, 46]. August: the road from Reims [47, 48].
- Thursday 8 September 1429: Paris, the feast of Our Lady's birth [50]. 13 September: the king leaves Saint-Denis [53]. December 1429: her family is ennobled [56].
- Easter week 1430: Melun, the Voices' warning [57]. Monday 23 May 1430: Compiègne, her capture (Cagny; the bull says 24 May) [59].
- Thursday 24 May 1431: Saint-Ouen [76, 77]. Monday 28 May: the judges in her cell [83]. 29 May: "relapsed" [86]. Wednesday 30 May 1431: death [87-95].
- 11 June 1455: the papal letter [96]. 7 November 1455: Isabelle at Notre-Dame, Paris [97]. 7 July 1456: the sentence [99]. 16 May 1920: canonization [100].
- The 1450 inquiry statements are dated 5 March 1449 in the record; the text gives March 1450.

## Numbers and counts
- 48 assessors on 22 February [1]. The ride to the king: 11 days [21]. A page of 14 or 15 [25]. Three weeks of questioning at Chinon and Poitiers [25]. A horse for 12 francs [20]. Two of Baudricourt's men [20].
- The arrow: half a foot deep [34]. The assault at the Tourelles lasted until about eight in the evening [34]. She ate four or five pieces of bread dipped in watered wine [37].
- Four tasks, in this order: drive out the English; have the king crowned at Reims; free the duke of Orléans; raise the siege of Orléans [39].
- Three weeks at Bourges [55]. "Fifty thousand of her people" is her own claim; d'Aulon saw four or five men [56].
- Compiègne: 300 to 400 men [58]. Price: ten thousand gold francs (the bull); fifteen or sixteen thousand coins (Cagny) [69; Note on the Sources].
- In the cell: five English guards, three inside and two at the door; two pairs of irons [81]. Old Market: "more than eight hundred soldiers" (Massieu; the bull: about eight hundred) [89, 90]. She prayed about half an hour [91].
- Canonization: over sixty thousand people, a hundred and forty of her family's descendants [100]. The text gives no count of hearings.

## Quotations (one approved wording each; the note is the source)
- Voice, 22 February [9]: "when she was thirteen ... a voice from God to help her guide herself ... very much afraid ... about noon, in summer, in her father's garden ... on her right side, toward the church ... seldom ... without a light."
- Saint Michael [10]: "I saw them with my bodily eyes as well as I see you. When they went from me, I wept. I would have liked them to take me with them."
- Hundred fathers [16]: "If she had had a hundred fathers and a hundred mothers ... she would still have gone."
- Baudricourt [20]: "Go, and let come what may."
- Dunois on the vineyard [35]: "Then the Maid came to him and asked him to wait a little longer ... At that moment the English shuddered and were afraid."
- Dunois on the wind [30, 31]: "the counsel of God our Lord is safer and wiser than yours"; the wind turned and blew for them.
- Her four tasks and the one year [39]: "She would last a year, and little more."
- Jargeau [41, 42]: "Do not doubt. The hour is ready when it pleases God. Act, and God will act."; "Friends, friends, up, up! Our Lord has condemned the English. At this hour they are ours. Take heart!"
- Burgundy [46]: "Forgive one another with a good heart, fully, as loyal Christians ought to do ... And if you want to make war, go against the Saracens."
- Road from Reims [48]: "Where it pleases God, for I am no more sure of the time or the place than you are. And I wish it pleased God my Creator that I might now go away, lay down my arms, and go to serve my father and mother, keeping their sheep with my sister and my brothers, who would be so glad to see me!"
- Melun [57]: "You will be taken before Saint John's Day ... It must be so. Do not be troubled. Accept it, and God will help you."
- Her oath [51, 58]: "By my martin" (Cagny; a stick or Saint Martin, Quicherat).
- Compiègne [63]: "None of the places that the King of Heaven has brought back to the gentle King Charles through me will be taken back by his enemies, as long as he takes care to guard them."
- Beaurevoir [66]: Saint Catherine: "You must accept it gladly, without fail. You will not be set free until you have seen the king of England."
- On the leap [67]: "not out of despair, but in the hope of saving her body and going to help the many good people who were in need."
- Grace of God, 24 February [74]: "If I am not, may God put me there; and if I am, may God keep me in it."
- Saint-Ouen [78, 79]: "he is the most noble Christian of all Christians, and the one who best loves the faith and the Church"; "If there is any fault in them, it is mine and no one else's."
- 28 May [83, 85]: "I would rather die than be in irons!"; "If I said that God had not sent me, I would damn myself, for it is true that God has sent me."; "What I said, I said from fear of the fire." The clerk's margin note: "A deadly answer."
- Last morning [87, 88, 89]: "I would rather have my head cut off seven times over than be burned like this!"; "Bishop, I die through you."; "Master Pierre, where shall I be this evening?" (through Ricquier, from Maurice).
- Old Market [91, 93, 94]: "Ah, Rouen! I am very much afraid that you will suffer for my death!" (one man heard it); Isambart on the end (block quote); she cried "Jesus!" so loudly everyone could hear (Leparmentier).
- The executioner [95]: he feared God would never forgive him for what he had done "to this holy woman" (Isambart).
- The bull [100, 101]: she "left her family to carry out the commands of God"; the Voices: "Bear it all willingly. Do not be anxious or troubled about your martyrdom. You will enter the kingdom of Paradise."

## Points where sources differ (told once, in the Note on the Sources)
Cagny gives her age at Chinon as about eighteen; she said nineteen in 1431. Laxart: six weeks at Burey; she: eight days. Page and steward: 4 May in different orders. Dunois: "27th of May" for the wound (it was 7 May). Dunois remembers a banner with Our Lord holding a lily; she described the world and an angel on each side. Cagny: four months at Beaulieu; she: about four months at Beaurevoir. Price: ten thousand gold francs (bull) or fifteen or sixteen thousand coins (Cagny). Capture: 23 May (Cagny) or 24 May (bull). The short paper: six or seven lines (one witness) or about eight (bull).
