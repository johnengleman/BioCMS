# Plan — Saint Joan of Arc (standard, "heart" rewrite, 2026-10-07)

Follows outline.md (version 2). Old plan kept as plan.md.before-heart. Coordinator's three new write-slim lines applied: scenes end on their image; after her death only Notre-Dame and the 1456 verdict, then the closing image (no canonization history beyond "Nearly five centuries passed"); the later life keeps her question (obedience, home, the king, friends) alive.

## Departures from the outline

- Opening thesis: no lines about the trial record and the 1456 witnesses (write-slim: do not prove the story inside it). Moved to "A Note on the Sources".
- Ch. 1: herding left out (neighbors and Joan disagree). Raid to Neufchâteau and fairy talk left out.
- SC-08 Reims: no source read says her father came (Q1, Q4 searched). Left out.
- SC-09: parting from d'Alençon found in Cagny, Q4 pp. 29–30 (Gien, Sept 1429). Armor left at Saint-Denis, Q4 p. 29. Both added.
- SC-10: Charles VII after the capture: only Cagny's judgment (Q4 pp. 36–37), told plainly, attributed in the Note. Pierre d'Arc taken with her: Quicherat's note, Q2 p. 74 n. 2 (added; closes at Notre-Dame).
- SC-11: no source gives when d'Aulon was parted from her (Q3 p. 219 says only "a whole year"). Written less. Beaurevoir "four months" dropped (conflicts with Cagny's four months at Beaulieu).
- SC-12/13: Isambart does not date the Basel exchange ("une fois", Q2 p. 4), so it sits in ch. 10, not on the Saint-Ouen scaffold. Saint-Ouen adds Massieu (Q2 pp. 16–19): her defense of the king, "abjure or be burned", the pen, the chains, Trinity Sunday.
- SC-14: heart left out (miracles). Leparmentier's "more than six times" dropped to avoid retelling after Isambart's block quote.
- Ch. 13: Isabelle's death (1458) left out so the chapter ends on the cross. Whether the cross was raised: not in Q3 or Guérin (only ordered).
- Ch. 14: path, feast, 1922 patronage, Thérèse, Pius II cut (coordinator's rule 2).
- Fact needs 6.2 and 6.3 (patronages, Thérèse play dates): not used.

## Our translations (new in this draft; originals)

- Dunois, Q3 pp. 14–15: "O Johanna, in quo loco habetis vos spem moriendi?" — "Ubi placebit Deo, quia ego non sum secura, neque de tempore, neque de loco, amplius quam vos scitis; et utinam placeret Deo, creatori meo, quod ego nunc recederem, dimittendo arma, et irem ad serviendum patri et matri in custodiendo oves ipsorum, cum sorore et fratribus meis, qui multum gauderent videre me."
- Banner, Q1 p. 187: "Ipsum vexillum suum fuerat in poena: bene rationis erat quod haberet honorem."
- Burgundy letter, Q5 pp. 126–127: "Pardonnez l'un à l'autre de bon cuer, entièrement, ainsi que doivent faire loyaulx chrestians; et s'il vous plaist à guerroier, si alez sur les Sarrazins." / "à jointes mains".
- Cagny, Q4 p. 34: "Taisez tous! il ne tendra que à vous que ilz ne soient desconfiz. Ne pencez que de férir sur eulx."
- Cagny, Q4 p. 35: "Ceste poure ville de Compiengne que vous avez moult amée, à ceste foiz sera remise es mains et en la subjection des anemis de France." — "Non sera, car toutes les places que le roy du ciel a réduit et remises en la main et obéissance du gentil roy Charles par mon moien, ne seront point reprinses par ses anemis, en tant qu'il fera dilligence de les garder."
- Cagny, Q4 pp. 29–30: "moult ennuyée du département et par espécial du duc d'Alençon que elle amoit très fort"; "à très grant regret".
- Massieu, Q2 p. 16: "Par ma foy, sire, révérence gardée, car je vous ose bien dire et jurer, sur peine de ma vie, que c'est le plus noble crestien de tous les crestiens, et qui mieulx aime la foy et l'église, et n'est point tel que vous dictes." — "Faiz-la taire."
- Massieu, Q2 pp. 17–18: "Je me rapporte à l'Eglise universelle, se je les doy abjurer ou non." — "Tu les abjureras présentement, ou tu seras arse." — "Menez-la au chasteau dont elle est venue." — "Defferrez-moi, si me lèverai." — "Liève-toi." — "Messieurs, vous savez qu'il m'est deffendu: sans faulte, je ne le prendray point."
- Isambart, Q2 pp. 4–5: "O! puisqu'en ce lieu sont aucuns de nostre parti, je veuil bien me rendre et submettre au concile de Basle." — "Taisez-vous, de par le dyable!" — "Si vous, Messeigneurs de l'Eglise, m'eussiez menée et gardée en vos prisons, par advanture ne me fust-il pas ainsi."
- Guillaume de la Chambre, Q3 p. 53: "Ha! Rouen! fay [j'ay] grant paour que tu ne ayes à souffrir de ma mort!"
- Leparmentier, Q3 p. 186: "in ultimo flatu clamavit magna voce Jhesus! adeo quod ab omnibus adstantibus potuit audiri."
- Bull, closing: "quae ad exsequenda Dei mandata familiam dereliquit"; "confertissimam et exsultantem multitudinem"; "Omnia libenter sustine: ne sollicita sis et turberis erga martyrium: regnum Paradisi ingredieris."
- Murray's English, modernized (pages in the notes): 22 Feb oath and warning (pp. 8–9); 24 Feb hearing (pp. 15–18); Melun (pp. 57–58, 73); hour of capture (p. 58); Beaurevoir dialogue (pp. 54, 74–75); Saint-Ouen answer (pp. 128–129); 28 May (pp. 136–138); Toutmouillé (pp. 158–159); Maurice (p. 302); Massieu at the Old Market (pp. 174–176); Seguin and Thibaut (pp. 265, 306–307).

## Checker's retranslations (2026-10-07; originals)

- Coutes, Q3 p. 68 (note 33): "Ha, sanglant garson, vous ne me disiez pas que le sanc de France feust répandu!" — "Ah, you wretched boy! You did not tell me that French blood was being spilled!" ("sanglant" is a mild curse here, not a swear word in our sense; the earlier "bloody boy" read as a modern oath.)
- Saint Catherine at Beaurevoir, Q1 p. 151 (note 66): Latin "Sine defectu oportet quod accipiatis gratanter; et non eritis expedita quousque videritis regem Anglorum." French "Sans faulte, il fault que prenés en gré, et ne sériés point délivre, tant que aies veu le roy des Anglois." — "You must accept it gladly, without fail. You will not be set free until you have seen the king of England." (Murray's "do not falter" and our earlier "do not lose heart" rendered "sine defectu" wrongly: it means "without fail".)
- Nicolas Midi, Q3 p. 159 (note 90): "Johanna vade in pace, Ecclesia non potest plus te defendere, et te dimittit in manu saeculari." — "...it leaves you in the hands of the civil authorities." ("manus saecularis", the lay arm; "the secular power" needed a gloss.)
- Cauchon to Saint Joan, Q2 p. 4 (note 88): "Ha! Jehanne, prenez en patience. Vous mourez pour ce que vous n'avez tenu ce que vous nous aviez promis, et que vous estes retournée à voslre premier malefice." — the second clause was dropped without an ellipsis; restored as "and because you went back to your first offense."
- Burgundy letter, Q5 p. 127 (note 46): "Et vous prie et requiers à jointes mains, que ne faictes nulle bataille ne ne guerroiez contre nous, vous, voz gens ou subgiez" — "us" is her own side (the loyal French and the king); the text now says "the king's side", not "them".
- 28 May record, Q1 pp. 456–457 (note 84): the French minute's last line ("Item, dit que de paour du feu, elle a dit ce qu'elle a dit") was cut from the block quote, because the next paragraph quotes her own words "from fear of the fire" (Murray pp. 137–138). The cut is at the end, so no ellipsis is needed.
- 24 February, Q1 p. 62 (note 72): an ellipsis now marks the two sentences of her answer that the modern text skipped ("I asked advice as to how I should answer…").
- Banner, Q1 p. 78 (note 27): "habebat vexillum cujus campus erat seminatus liliis; et erat ibi mundus figuratus et duo Angeli a lateribus; ... coloris albi" — the text now follows her own words (lilies, the world, an angel on each side). Dunois (Q3 p. 7) says "figura Domini nostri tenens florem lilii"; the Note on the Sources gives the difference.
- Opening quotation, Q1 p. 52 (note 9): "dum esset aetatis xiii annorum" — the quotation says "thirteen" (the "about" belongs to Q1 p. 128, "xiii annorum vel circiter", which stays in the narrator's text).
