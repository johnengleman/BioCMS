## SC-11 — The leap from the tower (Beaurevoir, October 1430)
- Status: full
- When and where: Beaurevoir castle (tower), autumn 1430. Joan puts the leap "towards the end" of her stay (M p.75 note, Murray's inference: late October, before the relief of Compiègne about 25 Oct). Joan's own date: she was told she would be helped "before Saint Martin's Day in winter" (11 Nov) [M p.75]. She says she was in the tower "about four months" [M p.54].
- Who was there: no one is named in the passages. Her saints speak: Saint Catherine "almost every day" [M p.74-75; Q1 p.151].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She hears that the people of Compiègne, "to the age of seven years", are to be put to fire and blood, and that she herself is sold to the English [Q1 p.150, 14 Mar 1431].
  2. Her Voices forbid her to jump "many times"; Saint Catherine says God will help her and Compiègne [M p.54, 74; Q1 p.151].
  3. She jumps, "for fear of the English", commending herself to God and Our Lady [M p.54].
  4. She is injured, and for two or three days she will not or cannot eat or drink [Q1 p.151-152].
  5. Saint Catherine tells her to confess and ask God's pardon. She confesses, does penance, and recovers [Q1 p.152, 160-161].
- Details: she "was wounded" [M p.54]; she says she had the idea that the leap might let her escape [Q1 p.152]. The Burgundians said "I was dead", then reproached her when they saw her alive [M p.54].
- Words spoken (French minute, Q1 p.160-161; Murray p.77 puts it in the mid-March hearings): "Je le faisoye non pas en espérance de moy déespérer , mais en espérance de sauver mon corps , et de aler secourir plusieurs bonnes gens qui estoient en nécessité."
  plain: "I did it not hoping to despair, but hoping to save my body and to go and help many good people who were in need."
  Latin of her answer on her aim (Q1 p.152): "sed sallando commendavit se Deo" — plain: "but in leaping she commended herself to God."
- Inner life: asked on 17 March whether she had angered her saints, she says the most she ever angered them was the leap, "ce fut du sault de Beaurevoir, et dont elle leur a crié mercy" [Q1 p.172-173; M p.85]. plain: "the most I ever angered them was the leap from Beaurevoir, and I cried them mercy for it." Asked if the leap was a mortal sin: "Je n'en sçay rien, mais m'en actend à nostre Seigneur" — plain: "I know nothing of that, but I rely on Our Lord" [Q1 p.162].
- Testimony (Joan, Q1 p.150, French minute, public domain):
  "Interroguée premièrement quelle fut la cause pour quoy elle saillit de la tour de Beaurevoir : respond qu'elle avoit ouy dire que ceulx de Compiegne, tous jusques à l'aage de vu ans, dévoient estre mis à feu et à sanc , et qu'elle aymoit mieulx mourir que vivre après une telle destruction de bonnes gens ; et fut l'une des causes. L'autre qu'elle sceust qu'elle estoit vendue aux Angloys, et eust eu plus cher mourir que d'estre en la main des Angloys, ses adversaires."
  plain: "Asked first why she leaped from the tower of Beaurevoir: she answers that she had heard that the people of Compiègne, all of them down to the age of seven, were to be put to fire and blood, and that she would rather die than live after such a destruction of good people. That was one of the reasons. The other was that she knew she was sold to the English, and she would rather die than be in the hands of the English, her enemies."
- What changed: she names the leap as her gravest fault, confesses, and is forgiven by her own account.
- Not in the sources: how high the tower was; the words of any person who found her; who nursed her; the ladies of Luxembourg (I did not read that passage); a date for the jump; that she wept at the top. The question whether she cursed God after the fall was put to her by the judges; she denied it [M p.54, 75].
- Sources: Q1 pp.150-152, 160-162, 172-173; M pp.54, 74-75, 85.

## SC-12 — "Answer boldly" (Rouen castle, 24 February 1431)
- Status: full
- When and where: public hearing of Saturday 24 February 1431 [Q1 p.262-263 gives "sabbati, xxiiii. februarii" for the grace answer]. In the castle at Rouen; Joan speaks of the Voice "in the Castle" [M p.16].
- Who was there: Bishop Pierre Cauchon of Beauvais (judge), Maître Jean Beaupère (questioner), the assessors [M pp.16-18]. 52 assessors at the third public hearing [M timeline].
- What happened, in order:
  1. She says the Voice came three times the day before: morning, at Vespers, and at the evening Ave Maria [M p.16].
  2. The previous morning she was asleep and the Voice woke her without touching her; it was not in her room "so far as I know, but in the Castle". She sat on her bed, joined her hands, and asked its help [M p.16; Q1 p.62-63].
  3. It said "Answer boldly; God will help thee." She says she heard it again "this night" [M p.16; Q1 p.63].
  4. She turns to the bishop: "You say you are my judge. Take care what you are doing; for in truth I am sent by God, and you place yourself in great danger" [Q1 p.63; M p.16].
  5. Asked whether she is in God's grace, she gives the answer below [Q1 p.65; M p.18].
- Details: the Latin gives the English-sounding French words "engluant dangier" for "great danger" [Q1 p.63].
- Words spoken (Latin record, Q1 p.63): "Vox autem illa dixit eidem Johannae quod responderet audacter." — plain: "That Voice told Joan to answer boldly."
  "Vos dicitis quod estis judex meus ; advertatis de hoc quod facitis, quia in veritate ego sum missa ex parte Dei, et ponitis vos ipsum in magno periculo." — plain: "You say you are my judge. Take care what you do, because in truth I am sent by God, and you put yourself in great danger."
- Inner life: "I would that every one could hear the Voice as I hear it" [M p.18; the Latin says "vellet quod quilibet intelligeret aeque bene sicut ipsa", Q1 p.65].
- Testimony (Joan, Q1 p.65, Latin record, public domain): "Interrogata an sciat quod ipsa sit in gratia Dei : respondit : « Si ego non sim, Deus ponat me; et si ego sim, Deus me teneat in illa. Ego essem magis dolens de toto mundo, si ego scirem me non esse in gratia Dei. » Dixit ultra, si esset in peccato, credit quod vox non veniret ad eam ; et vellet quod quilibet intelligeret aeque bene sicut ipsa."
  plain: "Asked if she knows that she is in the grace of God, she answered: 'If I am not, may God put me there; and if I am, may God keep me in it. I would be sadder than for the whole world if I knew I was not in the grace of God.' She said further that if she were in sin, she believed the Voice would not come to her; and she wished everyone could understand it as well as she does."
- What changed: she does not give way to the court, and the trap question fails.
- Not in the sources: the French minute of the grace answer (I did not reach it in three tries; the Latin above is the record I could read). Her tone, the room's look, and the judges' faces are not given in the trial text. Later witnesses add that the questioners were "confounded" and broke off [M p.205, a deponent whose name I did not confirm; memory of 1455-6, not the 1431 record].
- Note: the outline says the Voice "wakes her" on this day. In the record, the waking is the morning before; the Voice also speaks "this night" [M p.16].
- Sources: Q1 pp.62-65, 262-263; M pp.16-18.

## SC-13 — The recantation and the return (Rouen, 24 to 28 May 1431)
- Status: full
- When and where: Thursday 24 May 1431, scaffold in the cemetery of the abbey of Saint-Ouen; then her prison cell in Rouen castle, Sunday 27 to Monday 28 May [M pp.127-137].
- Who was there: Bishop Cauchon and judges; Cardinal of England (Henry Beaufort) and bishops of Thérouanne, Noyon, Norwich; abbots; preacher Guillaume Érard; a great crowd [M p.127-128]. Isambart de la Pierre, Martin Ladvenu, Nicolas Loyseleur (urged her to wear the woman's dress), Jean Massieu (usher) [M pp.174-175, 186].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Érard preaches on John 15. She answers him: let her words and deeds go to the Pope "to whom after God I refer me"; "I charge no one with them, neither my King nor any one else" [M p.128].
  2. The bishop begins reading the sentence. Midway, she says she will hold all that the Church ordains [M p.130].
  3. She is read a short French formula and signs it. Witnesses say she "made a cross" and "from fear" [Q3 p.164] and that she smiled [M p.186]. The text in the record is longer than the one she signed (see notes-church-miracles).
  4. Isambart: she asked to go to the Pope; he advised the council of Basel; she cried she would submit; Cauchon cried "Hold your tongue, in the devil's name" [Q2 p.4-5; M p.159-160].
  5. After the abjuration, her woman's dress is taken and the man's dress thrown at her; she resists until midday, then wears it (Massieu, 1449) [M p.174].
  6. On 28 May she says her Voices said she "damned" herself "to save her life" and that all she said, she said "for fear of the fire" [Q1 p.456-457, 467-468].
- Details: Isambart saw her weeping, "her face covered with tears, disfigured" [M p.160; Q2 p.5]. The prison assault comes from her report to Ladvenu: attribute it [Q2 p.8]. Notary Manchon: English soldiers drove the notaries back with axes and swords on the Sunday [M p.175, 186].
- Words spoken (Q1 p.456-457, French minute, 28 May): "ses voix luy ont dit que Dieu luy a mandé par sainctes Katherine et Marguerite, la grande pitié de la trayson que elle consenty en faisant l'abjuracion et révocacion , pour sauver sa vie ; et que elle se dampnoit pour sauver sa vie."
  plain: "her Voices told her that God had sent word through Saints Catherine and Margaret of the great pity of the betrayal she agreed to in making the abjuration and recantation to save her life; and that she was damning herself to save her life."
  Also, same pages: "Item, dit que de paour du feu, elle a dit ce qu'elle a dit." plain: "She also said that she said what she said from fear of the fire."
- Inner life: "ses voix luy ont dit depuis, que avoit fait grande mauvestié" [Q1 p.467] plain: "her voices told her since that she had done a great wrong." She did not intend to deny the Voices: "elle ne l'entendoit point ainsi faire ou dire" [Q1 p.467-468].
- Testimony (Isambart, Q2 p.5 via Murray, public-domain translation; French original in Q2 p.4-5 was only partly captured, so I give Murray's English exactly): "In truth, I saw her weeping, her face covered with tears, disfigured and outraged in such sort that I was full of pity and compassion." [M p.160]
  plain: "I saw her weeping, her face wet with tears and marked by blows, and I was full of pity."
  (Isambart's "outraged" is Murray's rendering; the French says only that she was "defigurée et oultragée". Not checked beyond the notes.)
- What changed: the fear that made her sign, then her return to her Voices and to the dress that made her a relapsed heretic.
- Not in the sources: what the paper said as she heard it (she says she did not understand it); the words she spoke when she "smiled"; her feelings on the scaffold beyond the above.
- Sources: M pp.127-137, 159-160, 174-175, 186; Q1 pp.456-457, 467-468; Q2 pp.4-5, 8; Q3 p.164.

## SC-14 — The cross in the market (Rouen, 30 May 1431)
- Status: full
- When and where: 30 May 1431, morning in the cell, then the Vieux-Marché (Old Market) at Rouen. Notes give "about 9 a.m." [C-S1, C-S2; not re-checked].
- Who was there: Martin Ladvenu (confessor), Jean Toutmouillé, Isambart de la Pierre, Jean Massieu, preacher Nicolas Midi, Bishop Cauchon, the executioner, "more than 800 soldiers" with axes and swords [M p.175], English captains, the Cardinal of England.
- What happened, in order:
  1. In the cell, Ladvenu announces the death. She cries out, tearing her hair, "Alas! am I to be so horribly and cruelly treated?" and appeals to God (Toutmouillé) [M p.158].
  2. She receives communion, then goes to the Old Market [M p.175].
  3. Midi says: "Jeanne, go in peace, the Church can no more defend you and leaves you in the secular hand" [Q3 p.160].
  4. She kneels and prays. She asks for a cross. An Englishman makes a small cross from a stick; she kisses it and puts it in her bosom [M p.175-176; Q3 p.159-160].
  5. She asks for the Church cross. The clerk of Saint-Sauveur brings it; she holds it till she is fastened to the stake [M p.176].
  6. Cauchon comes; she says to him: "Bishop, I die through you" [M p.158-159, 163].
  7. English soldiers hurry the priests: "What, Priest! will you have us dine here?" The executioner is told "Do thy duty" [M p.161, 176].
  8. In the flames she keeps calling "Jesus"; at the end she bows her head and says it once more [Q2 p.6-7].
  9. After: the executioner tells the friars he cannot burn the heart and entrails to ash [Q2 p.7]. Ladvenu: he said he had never feared an execution so [Q2 p.9; M p.164]. The ashes go into the Seine [Q3 p.160].
- Details: "the cardinal of England and several other English were forced to weep" [Q2 p.6]. Lofty plaster scaffold, so the executioner could not reach her quickly [M p.163-164].
- Words spoken: "Evesque, je meurs par vous" is not in a French text I read. Latin (Q3 p.169): "ipse erat causa suae mortis, et quod sibi promiserat quod eam poneret in manibus Ecclesiae, et ipse eam dimiserat in manibus suorum inimicorum capitalium" — plain: "he was the cause of her death; he had promised to put her in the hands of the Church, and he had left her in the hands of her chief enemies."
- Inner life: she asks the cross so that "the Cross on which God was hanging" is "continually before her eyes" [Q2 p.6-7].
- Testimony (Isambart, Q2 pp.6-7, French, public domain; OCR errors kept):
  "que ladicte Jehanne eut en la fin si grande contricion et si belle repentance , que c'estoit une chose admirable , en disant parolles si dévotes , piteuses et catholiques , que tous ceulx qui la regardoient , en grant multitude, pleuroient à chaudes larmes , tellement que le cardinal d'Angleterre et plu- sieurs autres Anglois furent contraincts plourer et en avoir com- pacîon. Dit oultre plus , que la piteuse femme lui demanda , requist et supplia humblement , ainsi qu'il estoit près d'elle en sa fin , qu'il allast en l'église prouchaine. et qu'il lui apportas! la croix, pour la tenir eslevée tout droit devant ses yeux jusques au pas de la mort, afin que la croix où Dieu pendist , fust en sa vie continuellement devant sa vue. Dit oultre, qu'elle estant dedans la flambe, oncques ne cessa jusques en la fin de résonner et confesser à haulle voix le saint nom de Jhesus"
  plain: "Joan had at the end such great contrition and such fine repentance that it was wonderful. She spoke words so devout, piteous, and Catholic that everyone who watched, a great crowd, wept hot tears, so that the Cardinal of England and several other English were forced to weep and to feel pity. The pitiful woman asked and begged him humbly, as he stood near her at the end, to go to the nearest church and bring her the cross, to hold it high before her eyes up to the moment of death, so that the cross where God hung would be always before her sight. In the flames, she never stopped, to the end, calling aloud the holy name of Jesus."
- What changed: she dies with the cross in view and the name of Jesus on her lips; the executioner fears for his own soul.
- Not in the sources: weather, her clothes, the height of the crowd beyond "more than 800 soldiers", her age at the stake in any witness's words, the time of the flames. The ashes: the sources do not say who threw them or how many people saw.
- Sources: M pp.158-164, 175-176; Q2 pp.6-9; Q3 pp.159-160, 169.

## SC-15 — A mother at Notre-Dame (Paris, 7 November 1455)
- Status: full
- When and where: 7 November 1455, Notre-Dame cathedral, Paris, in the morning ("de mane") [Q2 p.84-85]. A footnote warns that some manuscripts give 17 November; the 7th is the day later fixed for the solemn presentation.
- Who was there: Isabelle d'Arc, widow of Jacques d'Arc and mother of Joan; her son Pierre d'Arc; Jean, archbishop of Reims; Guillaume, bishop of Paris; Jean Bréhal, Inquisitor of the faith. Richard, bishop of Coutances, was absent [Q2 p.84-85]. Notes add: Calixtus III named the judges on 11 June 1455 [C-S1].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Isabelle comes in "with great groans and sighs" and falls at the judges' feet. She holds out the papal letter [Q2 p.85].
  2. The record says she and her family go to the Holy See, "to which Joan had often submitted herself and had often asked to be sent" [Q2 p.85].
  3. She asks, again and urgently, that the judges carry out the Pope's letter "without delay", and give justice to the widow, her sons, and her kin [Q2 pp.85-86].
  4. Old neighbors and soldiers then testify (Dunois on 22 Feb 1456) [Q3 p.2]. Rehabilitation sentence on 7 July 1456 at the archbishop's palace at Rouen [Q3 p.361-362].
- Details: footnote in Quicherat: "Isabelle d'Arc, alias Romée"; in local speech "Zabillet" [Q2 p.74]. She left Domrémy soon after her daughter (Pasquerel, per footnote) [Q2 p.74].
- Words spoken: the record gives only reported speech, not her own words. Latin (Q2 p.85): "Ysabellis, vidua antedicta, cum praedicto filio, tam suo quam consanguineorum suorum nomine, humiliter ac instantissime requisivit quatenus praefati domini Delegati ... ad dicti rescripti eisdem praesentati visitationem vel executionem celerem, absque ulteriori dilatione vel mora, procedere dignarentur; ac dictae viduae et suis filiis et consanguineis justitiam ministrare"
  plain: "Isabelle, the widow named above, with her son, in her own name and that of her kin, humbly and most urgently asked that the said delegates deign to proceed to the quick examination and carrying out of the said letter presented to them, without further delay, and to do justice to the widow, her sons, and her kin."
- Testimony (the record, Q2 p.84-85, Latin, public domain): "cum magnis gemitibus atque suspiriis eorum pedibus se prosternens, certumque Sanctae Sedis apostolicae mandatum et rescriptum offerens et praesentans, honesta vidua Ysabellis d'Arc, defuncti quondam Johannis d'Arc relicta, mater quondam Johannae vulgariter dictae la Pucelle, una cum filio suo Petro d'Arc"
  plain: "falling at their feet with great groans and sighs, and offering and presenting a sure order and rescript of the Holy Apostolic See: the honest widow Isabelle d'Arc, widow of the late Jean [sic] d'Arc, mother of the late Joan, commonly called the Maid, together with her son Pierre d'Arc."
  (The Latin gives the father as "Johannis"; Quicherat's footnote on p.74 gives "Jacob d'Arc", and the notes give Jacques. Do not name the father from this line.)
- What changed: the Church starts the case of 1455-56; the rehabilitation ends it on 7 July 1456 with the trial of 1431 annulled.
- Not in the sources: what Isabelle said in her own voice; her age; her dress; who stood beside her beyond the names above; the weather; the crowd.
- Sources: Q2 pp.74, 84-86; Q3 pp.2, 361-362; C-S1 (notes).

## FN-1 — What people ask of Joan, and other patronages
- Verified: Pius XI declared Joan secondary patron of France on 2 March 1922 (apostolic letter, per Wikipedia "Joan of Arc", facts-only; Latin phrase in the notes, C-S5). Pius XII named Thérèse of Lisieux equal secondary patron of France on 3 May 1944 (Aleteia FR page, fetched 4 Oct 2026).
- Not verified: lists of other patronages (soldiers, prisoners, captives, martyrs, people mocked for piety, WAVES and Women's Army Corps) appear only on devotional sites in a search summary. I found no official Church source. Wikipedia's infobox lists "France" only. The Catholic Encyclopedia (1910) gives no patronages. What people ask of her: no source read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_of_Arc (facts-only); https://fr.aleteia.org/2023/05/02/comment-prier-therese-patronne-secondaire-de-la-france/; https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/08409c.htm.

## FN-2 — Thérèse's poems and plays for Joan: titles and dates
- Status: thin. The local library has no hit ("Therese Joan of Arc" returned none from find.py).
- Titles: two pious recreations, "La Mission de Jeanne d'Arc" and "Jeanne d'Arc accomplissant sa mission" (English: "The Mission of Joan of Arc"; "Joan of Arc Accomplishes Her Mission"), listed in the contents of S. Conroy's English edition of Thérèse's eight plays (publisher blurb at https://susanconroy.com/plays.shtml, fetched 4 Oct 2026). Both were written for Mother Agnes (her sister Pauline) as prioress.
- Dates: a search summary (not a page I read) says 1894 for the first and 21 Jan 1894 for the performance; treat as unverified. The outline's "poems" are not found. Need de Teil or Taylor, or the Carmel archive, for exact titles and dates.

## FN-3 — The 1456 sentence on the cross at Rouen
- Fact: the sentence of 7 July 1456, "Acta fuerunt haec in palatio archiepiscopali, anno Domini mcccclvi, die vu. mensis julii", orders a solemn sermon and "affixione crucis honestae ad memoriam perpetuam" in the Vieux-Marché "where Joan was suffocated by a cruel and horrible burning" [Q3 p.361-362]. The first reading is to be at Saint-Ouen with a general procession, the cross the next day.
- Names: judges Jean (archbishop of Reims), Guillaume (bishop of Paris), Richard (bishop of Coutances) [Q3 p.258, session of 2 July]; Jean Bréhal inquisitor; Martin Ladvenu was present at the reading [Q3 p.362]. Plaintiffs: Pierre d'Arc (Jean d'Arc), promoter Simon Chapitault.
- Source: Quicherat vol. III, `procsdecondam03joanuoft`, pp.361-362 (public domain).
