# Check — Joan of Arc (standard), fact check of miracles.html

Original kept as miracles.html.before-check. I checked each account against the notes, the scene cards, check.md, and the raw OCR of Quicherat vols. 1-5 and Murray (1902) from archive.org, the Vatican text of the bull Divina disponente (16 May 1920), the Catholic Encyclopedia, and Guérin vol. 15 (local library). Lint is clean (no MUST FIX, no CHECK lines). All 38 accounts are kept.

## The six accounts the reviser added

All six are in the text and are kept.
- Siege promised as a sign (Gaucourt, Q3 pp. 16-18): confirmed. Three weeks "et amplius" at Poitiers and Chinon; the sign was "de levatione obsidionis".
- Wind foretold (Gaucourt, Q3 p. 18): confirmed. She "expresse praedixit" that weather and wind would soon change, and that food would enter freely.
- Bridge fort promised: Cagny (Q4 pp. 8-9) gives the "month, and half as many men again" remark and her words "je la prendray demain et retourneray en la ville par sus les pons". D'Aulon (Q3 p. 217) gives "Ou nom Dé, on entrera ennuyt en la ville par le pont". Confirmed. Page range widened to 8-9.
- Glasdale's death foretold (Q4 p. 463): confirmed as "disoit on", second hand. Quicherat's note names William Glasdall. Dunois (Q3 p. 9) says Glasdale drowned.
- Orléans credited to God (Alençon, Q3 p. 94): confirmed. He was not there ("non fuit praesens"). The soldiers' words are second hand. Status line now says so.
- Patay (Alençon, Q3 pp. 98-99): confirmed, including the "hanging from the clouds" words and the French "le gentil roy ara au jour duy la plus grant victoire". The Latin adds that she was sure of victory (added). The date 18 June is from the Catholic Encyclopedia, not Alençon. "Their commander Talbot" became "the English captain Talbot" (Alençon says only that Talbot was among them).

## The reviser's corrections, checked

- Steward at Compiègne: Cagny (Q4 p. 35) says "celui qui estoit son maistre d'ostel avant sa prinse". Correct as written ("her former steward").
- Trenches at Melun: Murray p. 57 says "in the trenches of Melun"; the Latin and French have "fossata / fossés" (ditches). Kept "trenches" as in the checked biography.
- Executioner, not Isambart, astonished: correct (Q2 p. 7). Isambart's 1452 line about the executioner fearing he was damned is in Murray p. 191. Correct.
- Dove: Isambart, 1452 (Murray p. 191; Q2 p. 63). The bull and Guérin repeat it. Correct.
- Horse: the Laval letter dated Wednesday 8 June 1429 (Q5 pp. 107-108), the event on the Monday evening at Selles. Correct. Status line was "a popular account"; now it names the letter and its writer.
- Beatification decree December 1908 (third Sunday of Advent, per the bull), canonization decree 6 April 1918, canonization bull 16 May 1920: all correct.

## Fixes

Facts
- m09 secret sign: the old text said Joan later described the sign "as a story about an angel". On 13 March 1431 she said the sign was an angel bringing the king a crown and the whole kingdom through God's help and her work (Q1 pp. 139-140; Murray pp. 69-70). Rewritten as her account, without "story". "Unresolved" removed. Pages added to the Source line.
- m22 Melun: the quotation marks held a modern rendering of reported speech. The Latin and French are indirect speech (Q1 p. 115). Changed to indirect speech and named the Voices (Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret, as the source does). The capture date is now given as Guérin's 23 May, with the bull's 24 May in the closing note.
- m26: named her Voices (Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret, Q1 p. 456).
- m31: "The bull of 1920 and Guérin say her heart stayed unharmed and full of blood" was too strong for Guérin. Only the bull says "illaesum et sanguine plenum". Guérin says the fire could not consume heart and entrails. Split.
- m15: the status said Guérin "reads it as a sign that Joan's words came true". Guérin does not say that about 8 May. Replaced with plain status. Guérin's statement that she entered the town by the bridge "as she had announced" (p. 398) now supports the bridge-fort account.
- m13: "the English fort at Saint-Loup" became "the church of Saint-Loup, held by the English" (Dunois). "Her vision of Saint Louis and Charlemagne" now reads as her account of a vision, which is what Dunois reports. Split a bent sentence.
- m10: "armorer of Tours" became "arms dealer" (mercator armorum).
- m06: removed "no news of it had come yet" (the Encyclopedia does not say so).
- m14: added Cagny's page for the date 7 May.
- m32: named Le Maistre and Loyseleur (Guérin p. 414), which the old text left as "one" and "another".

Trust the tradition (owner rule 1)
- Intro: removed "Only the five healings ... were examined" and "does not dismiss any account". The paragraph now says the Church approved five healings and the rest are reports whose status line names the teller.
- m27 birds: status was "a legend, doubted by the writer who records it". Now it names the teller (her supporters, recorded in the Journal of a Paris citizen). The Latin note moved to the closing note. Removed "is hostile to Joan".
- m32 judges: status said "to be treated as legend". Now "told in Guérin's nineteenth-century Life as a tradition".
- m13: removed "The Church did not examine it as a miracle".
- m09: removed "most people now think it settled a doubt Charles had about his birth" (also "now" was not in the source). It is in the closing note as the Encyclopedia's view.
- m06, m14, m22: removed the date and wound-place disagreements from the story. They are in a new closing note, "A Note on the Sources" (two short paragraphs).
- Glasdale entry: "hearsay from a hostile writer" became "told at second hand in the Journal of a Paris citizen".
- Group headings contain no "doubted" or "legends".

Titles (owner rule 2)
- "Saint" added before Joan in the text, the status lines, the group headings, and the h3 titles. Voices named as Saint Catherine and Saint Margaret. "Pius X" is now "Pope Saint Pius X" (three places and the intro to the decrees). Benedict XV is left as "Pope Benedict XV".
- Left plain because I was not sure: Charlemagne (the Church honors him locally as Blessed; Dunois calls him "sanctus"). Names inside titles of works and in the Source lines are unchanged. "Sister Thérèse of Saint Augustine" and "Sister Julie Gauthier of Saint Norbert" are religious names.
- h3 titles stay at 8 words or fewer. To make room for "Saint", five titles lost a date: bridge fort (1429), m20 (1429), m25 (1431), m22 (April), and the soldier who repents (Rouen 1431). The date of each is still in the text or the status line, except m20, where Alençon gives no time.

## Checked and left as is
Accounts m01-m05 (approved healings, with names, diagnoses, and decree dates as the bull gives them), m07, m08, m11, m12, m16, m17, m18, m19, m20, m21, m23, m24, m25, m28, m29, m30. Quotations match the Latin or French. "You will not have them from me yet this year", "Take him to the cross", "I did not ask about it", and the Jargeau and Patay words are plain modern English with the same meaning. The m25 dates (Paris lost 1436 per Murray's note, 12 November 1437 per the Encyclopedia) both fall within seven years of 1 March 1431.

## Open
- The year 1429 in the m20 heading rests on Guérin (9 May 1429, Loches). Alençon gives no time. The heading no longer shows a year.
- The Journal of a Paris citizen gives the Glasdale drowning "the day before the killing", which matches Dunois only loosely. The text says only that the Journal says it came true.

Verdict: ready.
