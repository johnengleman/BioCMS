## 1. Identity (church-miracles part only)
- Names: Joan of Arc; Jeanne d'Arc; "la Pucelle (the Maid)"; "la Pucelle d'Orléans"; Latin in the bull: Ioanna de Arc [S1][S3]
- Born Domremy; Catholic Encyclopedia: "probably on 6 January, 1412"; died Rouen 30 May 1431 [S3]. The 1920 bull gives 6 Feb 1412 [S1]. Conflict: see section 8.
- Tradition: Catholic. Saint, canonized as a Virgin, not as a martyr [S5]. Orthodox view: not checked.
- Beatified 18 April 1909 by Pius X [S5][S6]. The bull says "dominica in Albis" 1909 [S1].
- Canonized 16 May 1920 by Benedict XV, bull "Divina disponente" [S1][S6]
- Feast: 30 May. The bull orders her memory "quotannis die trigesima maii" in the Roman Martyrology [S1]. Anglican commemoration 30 May [S5].
- Patronage: secondary patron of France, declared by Pius XI 2 March 1922 [S5]. The Latin text: "Sanctam Ioannam Virginem Arcensem, uti Patronam minus principalem Galliae" [S5 footnote, quoting Pius XI 1922].

## 4. Character (what Church sources say)
- Pious from childhood: she gave her bed to the poor and slept on the floor; one witness says so [S2 p.390; S1].
- Cheerful: Guérin says she was "gaie" and "aimait à voir un visage joyeux" [S2 p.390]. Her companions teased her for her devotion, her only fault in their eyes [S2].
- Illiterate: knew only the Our Father, Hail Mary and Creed [S2 p.391][S3 says she never learned to read or write].
- She did not kill: "n'a jamais tué un seul ennemi"; she carried the banner and "wanted to use it herself" instead of the sword [S2 p.401]. The bull, paraphrase: asked why she carried the banner, she said she wished to use it, not the sword, and wished to kill nobody [S1].
- Stern on soldiers: drove away camp women and rebuked blasphemers [S1].
- Slept dressed; after sunset she spoke to no man [S2 p.402][S3].
- Doubts: CE says the Poitiers examiners did not rule on the reality of her mission [S3]. The 1902 consistory worried about her capture and abjuration [S5].
- Weakness: at the stake of Saint-Ouen "her courage for once failed her" [S3]. In her cell she wept at the thought of burning; Guérin gives her cry "j'aimerais mieux être décapitée sept fois" [S2 p.410].

## 7. Miracles — every distinct event
Format: date, place, person, what was reported, reporter, status, source.

### A. Wonders in her life
1. Feb 1429, Vaucouleurs: Joan announces a French defeat near Orléans (Battle of the Herrings, 12 Feb) on 17 Feb, before news arrives; confirmed days later. Reporter: CE (Thurston). Status: historical claim, prophecy. [S3]
2. Feb 1429, journey: crosses many rivers and enemy-held ground to Chinon; the king hears she passed "prodigiose" and grants audience. Reporter: bull (the king learned from Baudricourt). Status: papal bull text. [S1] Whole journey of 11 days among enemies: [S2 p.394]
3. March 1429, Chinon: recognises Charles among the courtiers; "Je ne suis point le Dauphin" — "c'est vous". Reporters: S2, S3. Status: traditional narrative, accepted in CE; the bull says only that she revealed secrets known only to God and the king [S1].
4. March 1429, Chinon: the secret sign to the king. CE says what it was is unknown and the "most commonly believed" explanation is a doubt about his birth [S3]. Status: unresolved.
5. April 1429, Fierbois: the sword with five crosses found rusty behind the altar where her voices said. Reporters: Joan herself at trial [S4 vol.1 p.75–76], the bull [S1], CE [S3], Guérin [S2 p.396]. Status: stated in the trial, in the bull; investigators in the Church found it as she said. Guérin adds that she told the king of it only after much urging, as a secret.
6. 22 April 1429, Lyon letter: predicts the lifting of Orléans, her own wound by a shaft without death, and the coronation of the king at Reims in the summer; letter registered at Brussels before the events. Reporter: CE [S3]. Status: documented prophecy (original manuscript cited).
7. Before Orléans: tells Charles that she will be wounded at Orléans and not hindered [S2 p.396].
8. 29/30 April 1429, Loire: wind turns "statim, et quasi in momento" as Joan speaks to Dunois; sails raised; supply boats pass Saint-Loup despite the English. Reporter: Dunois, sworn deposition 22 Feb 1456. Status: sworn testimony at the rehabilitation; Dunois judged her acts "potius erant a Deo quam ab homine" because of the wind, the food and the vision about Louis and Charlemagne [S4 vol.3 p.5–7].
9. 7 May 1429, Orléans: arrow wound between neck and shoulder; half a foot out the back (Guérin); she is consoled by a vision of two saints, pulls out the arrow, returns [S2 p.398]. CE says wounded in the breast on 7 May [S3]. Status: historical fact plus a reported vision. Conflict on the wound location.
10. 7 May 1429: prophecy that she will be wounded "that day"; Guérin says she announced it that morning [S2 p.398].
11. 8 May 1429: English retreat without a fight at her word. Reporter: S2 [S2 p.398]. Status: narrative.
12. 12 June 1429, Jargeau: stone on her helmet, she falls and rises: "Sus, sus, amis..." [S2 p.400][S5].
13. Prophecy at Orléans: "Je ne durerai qu'un an" — attested by Alençon; Guérin ties it to her capture a year later [S2 p.399, 402].
14. Prophecy to the English (1 March 1431): "within seven years' space the English would have to forfeit a bigger prize than Orléans." Paris lost 12 Nov 1437 [S3].
15. April 1430, Melun: voices tell her of capture before St John's; the bull and Guérin agree [S1][S2][S3].
16. After the coronation, Lagny: the bull says she raised a dead newborn so he could be baptized. Trial text (Joan's own account): the baby was three days old, brought before the Virgin's statue at Lagny; Joan and the girls of the town prayed; the child gave three gasps, was baptized, then died and was buried in holy ground; he had been black like her tunic and the colour began to return. Reporter: Joan at trial [S4 vol.1 p.105 area]; bull [S1]. Status: told by Joan; the bull calls it "mortuum infantem ... resuscitavit". NEW to most popular pages.
17. 24 May 1430, Compiègne: capture after the voices' warning; the bull says her voices had said Compiègne would be freed before Martin's day and it was [S1]. Status: prophecy fulfilled (bull).
18. Rouen, 1431: Joan's voices and her saints visited her in prison and comforted her (Guérin) [S2 p.404].
19. Abjuration scene: the voices rebuked her for yielding; she answered that she did not understand the abjuration [S1].
20. 30 May 1431: white dove from the flames, seen by an English soldier who fainted [S1][S2]. Status: reported by witnesses; no source for the soldier's name in my notes.
21. 30 May 1431: name of Jesus seen in the flames by many [S1][S2].
22. 30 May 1431: heart unburnt and full of blood; executioner's sworn testimony per Guérin [S1][S2]. Status: bull and Guérin state it. The ashes and heart were thrown into the Seine [S1][S2].
23. Retribution tradition: Guérin lists deaths of Cauchon (sudden, in a barber's hands), Le Maistre (vanished), Estivet (found dead in a ditch), L'Oiseleur (sudden death at Basel), Midi (leprosy), Bedford, Henry VI [S2 p.413–414]. Status: popular tradition in a pious source; the bull only says the guilty died shamefully [S1]. Treat as legend.

### B. Miracles used in the cause (approved)
Beatification: four proposed, three approved by Pius X (decree of third Sunday of Advent 1908). The fourth was dispensed [S1].
24. Sister Thérèse of St Augustine: instant, perfect cure of chronic stomach ulceration ("chronica ulceratione stomachi"). Approved. [S1]
25. Sister Julie Gauthier of St Norbert: instant perfect cure of a fungoid, painful ulcer of the left breast ("ulcere fungoso eretistico in sinu sinistro"). Approved. [S1]
26. Sister Jeanne Marie Sagnier: instant perfect cure of chronic tubercular osteoperiostitis ("osteoperiostite chronica tuberculari"). Approved. [S1]
Canonization: two more, found by decree 6 April 1918 [S1].
27. Maria Antonia Mirandelle (Latin "Mariae Antoniae Mirandelle"): instant perfect cure of a perforating disease of the sole of the foot ("morbo plantari perforante"). Approved. [S1]
28. Thérèse Belin: instant perfect cure of peritoneal and pulmonary tuberculosis and of an organic lesion of the mitral valve ("tuberculosi peritoneali ac pulmonali necnon laesione organica orificii mitralici"). Approved. [S1]
Note: S10 (a fan site) says "three nuns ... Sister Therese leg ulcers; Sister Julie cancerous ulcer breast; Sister Marie stomach cancer". It disagrees with S1 on the diagnoses. Use S1, the bull. The bull gives no dates or places for the cures and no saying which Congregation of Rites found them. "Aliquot novi miracula" were added later; the bull also says "plures gratiae, tum spirituales, tum corporales... et prodigiosae sanationes" are described in the processes [S1].

### C. Cult and relics
29. Orléans keeps 8 May each year with a Mass, panegyric and procession by the bishop, chapter, clergy of twelve parishes, mayor and army chiefs; bull says this from 1429 to 1920 [S1]. S5 says the city marked her death yearly from 1432 and a religious play from 1435 [S5]. Conflict on starting point.
30. 1452: Cardinal d'Estouteville grants an indulgence for the 8 May ceremonies [S5].
31. Revolution: the Orléans festival suspended in 1793; relics, sword and banner destroyed; statue melted into a cannon [S5]. Napoleon allowed the festival to return in 1803 [S5 on Joan]. Statement about the sword and banner as relics: facts from S5 only.
32. Chinon vessel ("Bottle of Chinon"): discovered 1867 in a jar in the attic of a Paris pharmacy, labelled "Restes trouvés sous le bûcher de Jeanne d'Arc, Pucelle d'Orléans" [S7][S9]. Contents: a charred-looking human rib, pieces of carbonised wood, 15 cm of linen, a cat femur [S7]. Kept in the museum at Chinon of the Archdiocese of Tours; the Archbishop of Tours is curator [S7][S9].
33. 2007 analysis: Philippe Charlier (Raymond Poincaré Hospital, Garches) with 14 specialists; mass, infrared, atomic-emission spectrometry, electron microscopy, pollen analysis, and two perfumers (Sylvaine Delacourte of Guerlain and Jean-Michel Duriez of Jean Patou) who smelled "burnt plaster" and "vanilla" [S7][S9][S8]. Result: Egyptian mummy remains, not burnt; resin, bitumen, malachite in the black crust; pine pollen; Nature says pine resin was widely used in Egyptian embalming [S7]. S8 says "mummified Egyptian remains from the 3rd century" (no "BC" in S8). A search snippet gave 3rd to 6th century BC, but I did not read that in a full source. State no date until checked.
34. Church response: the Diocese of Tours (spokesman Bertrand Vincent) said the remains were "never considered relics by the Church" and "were never the object of devotion"; Charlier acted on his own initiative [S8]. Nature says "They were recognized by the Church" [S7] — conflict between S7 and S8.
35. Other relics claimed: none found. The bull and Guérin say the English threw ashes and heart into the Seine so there would be no relics [S1][S2]. Guérin lists the house at Domremy, the beam hacked by allied sabres, and a statue given by Louis XI [S2 p.415, monuments] (house, not body relics).
36. Rouen: cross at the execution place ordered by the 1456 sentence [S2].

## 8. Other

- Conflict with CE: the CE says "she asked that search might be made"; the trial text says she "scripsit" to the churchmen of that place [S3][S4].
- Dunois' evidence also says he sent two envoys to the king and the king made her wait two days [S4 vol.3 p.3–4]. The bull says "paucis elapsis diebus". Guérin says three days of consultation [S2 p.394].
- Dunois' second wonder-evidence: when Suffolk was a prisoner at Jargeau, a verse paper reached him that "a Maid shall come from the Bois-Chenu and ride on the backs of the archers" [S4 vol.3 p.15]. Source: Dunois, "una schedula papyrea ... quatuor versus". NEW; mark as testimony.
- Prophecy of the "Maid from the Bois-Chenu" popular in the countryside: S3 mentions a prophecy of a maiden from the bois chesnu [S3]; S5 mentions Marie Robine and a Merlin prophecy [S5].
- Canonization as Virgin, not martyr: S5 says she was condemned by a canonically constituted court, for private revelation [S5]. Bull: Joan in the end asked for "martyrium", and the voices told her "ne sollicita sis et turberis erga martyrium: regnum Paradisi ingredieris" [S1]. Good detail for the writer.
