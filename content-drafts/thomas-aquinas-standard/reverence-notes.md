## biography.html — changes of substance

1. **Opening.** "a large and heavy friar … The friar was Saint Thomas Aquinas" → Saint Thomas is named in the first sentence. No fact lost: his size stays at Cologne ("a tall, heavy young man") and at the tomb ("large and fat").
2. **"Three months later he was dead"** → "he died". Tone only.
3. **Storm at Roccasecca.** Cut the aside "That is all Tocco records. The dead girl has no name in it, and Theodora's own words are not given." Tocco's lesson stays, now "Tocco saw God's hand in it". Source: Tocco c. 2 (scenes-1.md, SC-01).
4. **"A later legend says … Legend or not"** → "An old story tells … It was the question he spent his life on." Source unchanged: Baring-Gould vol. 3 (notes-own-words.md line 94). No fact changed; the label "legend" is gone from the body.
5. **"The sources do not agree on the year"** cut from the body. The Note on the Sources already says it.
6. **"It is said that he also wrote a short treatise"** → "Tocco also heard that he wrote". Tocco p. 74 "ut dicitur" (scenes-1.md line 63; notes-witnesses.md line 31).
7. **Length of the captivity.** Body: "The captivity lasted almost two years" (Tocco). Cantimpré's "two or three" moved to the Note on the Sources. Source: scenes-1.md line 63.
8. **Saint Albert's prophecy.** "It is said that" → "Tocco tells that". Cut "The story was first written down about seventy years after it happened, and Tocco himself says only that it was told." The Note keeps that the story is in the Lives only.
9. **"Obedience forced him"** → "The prior held him to the order, and he obeyed." Real-actor rule; the prior's order is in the sentence before (scenes-1.md, mastership card).
10. **"Whichever words he used, the verse was the same"** → "In both tellings the verse is the same."
11. **Corpus Christi.** The feast is now named "Corpus Christi, the feast of the Body of Christ". "Scholars today think the authorship is probable" moved to the Note on the Sources (notes-own-words.md F8).
12. **New paragraph on prayer before study** (chapter "Saint Thomas Writes and Preaches in Naples"). Replaces "Reginald said that he prayed before every piece of study, and often in tears." Source: Tocco c. XXX, pp. 104–105 (fontes1 lines 5080–5106): Reginald, back from Fossanova and lecturing at Naples, "cum ingenti fletu dixit: Fratres, ego fui a Magistro meo in vita sua prohibitus, ne miranda, quae de ipso videram revelarem"; Saint Thomas gained his knowledge "non humano ingenio, sed orationis merito"; "quotiens studere voluit, disputare, legere, scribere vel dictare, prius ad orationis secretum accedens, profusus orabat lacrymis, pro divinis inveniendis in veritate secretis … accedebat dubius, sic postmodum redibat edoctus." Also notes-witnesses.md line 62.
13. **Crucifix at Naples.** Cut "No sworn witness tells it." The Note keeps it.
14. **The Mass of December 1273.** "Those words come to us from Reginald through Bartholomew of Capua. No source says what Saint Thomas saw." → "Those are Reginald's words, and Bartholomew of Capua swore to them. Saint Thomas never described what he had seen." (Process no. 79; scenes-2.md line 92: what he saw is not in the sources.) "He wrote nothing in his own hand" → "He wrote nothing himself" (the check script's meta-commentary line).
15. **Reginald's oath and San Severino.** Cut "Bartholomew swore to it sixteen years later", "Tocco tells the visit to the sister differently", and "The two accounts agree on what matters." Tocco's account now "adds". "fell into a trance" → "was rapt out of himself". "Saint Thomas had seen something" → "God had shown Saint Thomas something": Tocco p. 120 "talia sunt mihi revelata" (notes-church-miracles.md Q6).
16. **John of Caiazzo.** Sentence re-ordered so the person is the subject. "The road to Lyon tested the second prayer at once" → "On the road to Lyon Reginald soon put the second prayer to the test" (real-actor rule; Reginald's words follow).
17. **Cardinal's hat.** Cut "No source says anyone had offered Saint Thomas a cardinal's hat." Kept "Reginald was speaking of what might come."
18. **Entry to Fossanova.** "honored the altar" → "prayed before the altar": Gui p. 204 "premissa oratione coram altari" (fontes1 line 10161); Tocco p. 130 "debita reverentia sacro altari exhibita" (line 6400). Added "Tocco writes that there the hand of the Lord came upon him": Tocco "facta est super eum manus Domini et tactus spiritu prophetiae" (line 6401); Gui "facta est manus domini super ipsum" (line 10162). Cut "The sworn witnesses report them without calling them a prophecy."
19. **Last Communion.** "received the Host" → "received the Body of Christ": Tocco "sacrosanctum Christi Corporis Sacramentum" (line 6456). Cite line: "last Communion" → "last Holy Communion".
20. **Death.** "Peter said he lived three days after that Communion. Tocco says that on the next day…" → "He lived three days after that Holy Communion" (Peter, process no. 49; Tocco's "next day" stays in the Note). The last anointing is explained in its own sentence. "soon gave back his spirit. He died at the morning hour" → "gave back his spirit to God. It was the morning hour of 7 March 1274" (Tocco c. LXV p. 138; reverence point 7, used once).
21. **The hidden body.** "Saint Thomas did not stay quiet about it" → "The monks soon learned that Saint Thomas wanted his first place back." Process no. 8 (Abbot Nicholas): the dream "Bring me back to my first place."
22. **"the world outside had begun to ask for him"** → "others had begun to ask for him" (real-actor rule; the masters of Paris follow).
23. **Storm at sea.** Added: the friars "prepared for death", "prayed to God with loud weeping", the Virgin Mary "Star of the Sea", "the record of the miracles God had worked through him", "shouted that God had worked a miracle"; "away from being smashed" → "away from the rocks". Tocco p. 147 (lines 7291–7295): "per mutuam confessionem se ad transitum pararent … coeperunt fletibus magnis, et vocibus Reginam Virginum, maris stellam, B. Dominicum, B. Petrum Martyrem, et ultimo B. Thomam … invocare". Gui no. 102 (lines 12864–12875): "deumque orant … ne illa que per illum deus fecit miracula … submergantur … omnes clamare ceperunt, miraculum a deo factum".
24. **Toulouse.** Cut "We found no record from the time that confirms the number, and the sources disagree about which church received him. What is sure is…". Attributions kept in a few words ("A Dominican Life of 1911 tells", "Alban Butler tells"). "got him back" → "received their brother back". "relics" → "holy relics" (twice). The doubts stay in the Note on the Sources.
25. **"poems" → "hymns"** (twice) for the Pange lingua and Adoro te devote. Pope Saint John Paul II's homily of 1997 names both (scenes-3.md line 122). The cite "a hymn attributed to Saint Thomas Aquinas" is kept, as notes-own-words.md Q21 asks.
26. **Note on the Sources.** "both are told as hearsay" → "both come from stories the friars handed on". Added: the captivity length (Tocco almost two years; Cantimpré two or three); Ptolemy on the Corpus Christi office and the scholars' view of it and of the Adoro te devote.

Tone-only changes not listed one by one: none beyond the above; every edit is in this list.

## miracles.html — changes of substance

1. **Intro.** Rewritten to open with the Church's memory of God's works through Saint Thomas, gathered from the oldest witnesses. Added the psalm the pope quotes in the bull: "the Lord has brought his saint to light" (bull §8).
2. **m01 (body whole).** Added the procession in vestments, the Mass of a confessor, and the pope's reading of the smell as a sign of the purity of his flesh, pleasing to God (bull §9).
3. **m02 (gout).** Added "He praised God, who had healed him … through the merits of his saint" (bull §10 "praising God for having been cured … through the saint's merits").
4. **m04 (the mocker).** Added the bull's frame: he felt the power of God twice through the saint (bull §12).
5. **m05 (angina).** "turned to Saint Thomas with devotion", "begged Saint Thomas for the favor with all his heart" (bull §13 "having learned devotion to the saint … affectionately asked").
6. **m06 (fever).** "gave himself devoutly"; "God had freed him of all his sickness" (bull §14 "consecrated himself devoutly … entirely delivered"; God as the actor, reverence point 1).
7. **m07 (deaf mother).** "gave herself with complete trust to the saint of God", the vow that he take the deafness away, "healed through the merits of the saint, as she had hoped" (bull §15).
8. **m08 (girl's throat).** "commended herself devoutly to Saint Thomas" (bull §16).
9. **m09 (lay brother's arm).** "humbly made a vow … for his healing"; "He told the other monks and hid the miracle from no one" (bull §17).
10. **m10 (child with tumor).** "with devotion"; the bull's sentence that when nature fails, help is found in God, who works his wonders through his saints (bull §18).
11. **m16 status.** "says it is said that the verse was given" → "also tells it: the verse was given to him by a revelation after he had prayed" (bull §2).
12. **m20 and the sister's vision, status lines.** Dropped the label "as 'it is said'"; now "told by Tocco and by Gui".
13. **Prayer about Rainald.** "He received the answer … He was also told" → "God answered him that …" (process no. 78; the biography already says God answered).
14. **m30 (blind subprior).** Added: he knelt at the feet of the body with reverence, laid his face on the face of Saint Thomas, prayed to God for his sight through the saint's merits, and cried "Blessed be God, for by the merits of this saint my sight is perfectly restored." Tocco c. LXI pp. 134–135 (lines 6000–6008: "oravit Deum, ut ipsius dicti Doctoris meritis … fieret sibi restitutio luminis … clamavit: Benedictus Deus quia meritis huius Sancti mihi visus est perfectissime restitutus"); Calo p. 49 (lines 2065–2071).
15. **m33 (leper).** Added: he came "with devotion, trusting in the saint's merits"; "woke with joy and found himself healed of all his sickness. He gave thanks to God and praised the merits of Saint Thomas with all devotion." Tocco catalogue no. 7 p. 146 (lines 7227–7235: "cum devotione ad sepulcrum S. Thomae, eius meritis confisus accessit … Qui cum gaudio evigilasset, perfecte se invenit ab omni infirmitate curatum, et reddidit Deo gratias, merita dicti Sancti cum omni devotione collaudans"). Calo (lines 2513–2520) tells it the same way with "gratias agens deo et sancto thome".
16. **m34 (demon as an ape).** "carried him with devotion"; "on their knees they begged God to heal him through the saint's merits". Tocco catalogue no. 3 (lines 7177–7186: "cum devotione portarunt: ubi cum genuflexione Deum Sancti meritis implorassent").
17. **m35 (lame from birth).** Added "poor in goods but rich in devotion"; "God worked many miracles … through the saint's merits"; "begged God for the favor"; "He gave thanks to God, who heals the brokenhearted through the merits of his saints." Tocco catalogue no. 9 (lines 7258–7266: "pauper temporali substantia, dives tamen devotionis gratia … a Deo eius meritis multa fieri miracula … implorans a Deo beneficium … agens gratias Deo qui Sanctorum meritis sanat contritos corde").
18. **m36 (Bartholomew of Ferentino).** Added the abbot's urging and "commend himself to God through the saint's merits"; the monks "called on the name of God and on the merits of Saint Thomas". Tocco catalogue no. 8 (lines 7237–7246: "inductus ab Abbate suo … se Deo eius meritis commendaret … nomine invocato divino, per merita dicti Sancti").
19. **m38 status (mill).** Added "a lay brother of the abbey named Brother Manuel came up as it happened" (Tocco catalogue no. 36, line 7719).
20. **Margaret of Piperno.** Added "with great devotion"; she and her mother "begged a monk for God's sake" for a relic and "hoped that through his merits she would be healed"; "She prayed and begged the help of Saint Thomas." Process no. 56 (fontes4 lines 15869–15878: "cum devotione magna … rogaverunt … ut pro Deo faceret eis apportari aliquid de reliquiis … sperantes ex hoc, meritis dicti fratris Thome"); Gui no. 15 (fontes1 lines 10331, 10343–10344: "facta oracione et implorato suffragio sancti thome"). "She knelt" dropped; the Latin has prayer, not kneeling.
21. **Doubting blacksmith, status.** Added "Then he gave thanks to God, who through the saint's merits had freed his soul from the sin of unbelief." Tocco catalogue no. 22 (lines 7524–7528: "agens Deo gratias, qui meritis dicti Sancti animum eius ab infidelitatis absolvit scelere").
22. **Nicholas of Maximo, status.** Added "he rose in the morning giving thanks to God" (Tocco catalogue no. 25, lines 7566–7567 "surrexit diluculo perfecte sanatus, agens Deo gratias"). Sentence split.
23. **Paralyzed girl.** "or heal her in his mercy" (Gui no. 98, line 12762 "de dei misericordia sanaretur"); the abbot and monks "prayed that God would hear the father's prayer through the merits of Saint Thomas" (lines 12779–12780 "orant exaudiri sancti thome meritis preces patris").
24. **m40 (wind off Monte Argentario).** Same restorations as biography item 23, from Tocco p. 147 and Gui no. 102. "Mary" → "the Virgin Mary, Star of the Sea".
25. **m41 (Archbishop Humbert).** "humbly and devoutly"; "for the glory of God and of the saint"; "As soon as he had said this with devotion". Tocco catalogue no. 53 (lines 7923–7926).
26. **Lady Mary.** Added "He spoke to her with sweet words and a gentle voice" (Gui no. 89, line 12586 "dulci eloquio, voce miti").
27. **m42 (stillborn child).** "prayed devoutly with tears … so that he could be reborn in baptism and live for ever … He was reborn in baptism, and soon he went to live for ever" (Gui no. 89: "sacro renatus baptismate non periret, sed viveret in eternum … sacro regeneratus lavachro mox migravit in eternum victurus"). Replaces "soon died again, baptized".
28. **m44 (Tagulano).** "begged God with tears"; added "Their weeping turned to laughter and their grief to joy" (Gui no. 100, lines 12814–12818).
29. **m48.** "A saying claims" → "A saying holds".
30. **Closing note.** Cut "modern scholars read them with care" and "so that readers can weigh it"; now "this page gives each telling with the name of its teller".
