# Facts — Saint Thomas Aquinas (standard, heart text), checked 2026-10-06; updated 2026-10-07 for the middle revision (notes 1–109)

The miracle list and the teachings essay must use these names, dates, numbers, and wordings exactly. Note numbers are the notes of `biography.html` (now 1–109). Facts and wordings that the middle revision cut from the text are marked "cut"; do not use them in the other texts without a new check. Raw text read: Prümmer, Fontes 1 (Calo, Tocco, Gui) and Laurent, Fontes 4 (Naples process, Documenta), both in `sources/`; the bull in `sources/bull-fsspx-english.txt`; Corpus Thomisticum, Aeterni Patris, and Butler for the rest.

## Names and spellings
- Saint Thomas Aquinas; born at Roccasecca (castle of the counts of Aquino). Father Count Landulf; mother Theodora (Calo p. 17, Tocco p. 66; note 4).
- Brothers Landulf and Rainald (Calo p. 17; Gui ch. 20). Rainald: killed by Emperor Frederick II; year not given (Prümmer thinks probably 1248; Gui ch. 21 n.).
- Sister who became abbess of Santa Maria at Capua (Calo p. 23). Sister Theodora, Countess of San Severino, mother of Thomasius; she received the right hand (Calo p. 39, Tocco p. 111, Gui; notes 70, 104). Laurent's notes call the countess Mary: listed in the Note on the Sources.
- Niece Francesca, wife of Annibaldo of Ceccano, lady of Maenza (Naples no. 8 n.; Calo p. 47).
- Companion and secretary Reginald of Piperno (Latin "Raynaldus"; Ptolemy writes Raymund for the sick companion at Molara).
- Teachers: Martin (grammar, logic) and Peter of Ireland (nature) at Naples; Saint Albert at Cologne; John the Teuton, Master of the Order; Cardinal Hugh.
- Friar who urged him to enter the order: John of San Giuliano (Tocco p. 71; Gui adds that Thomas Agni of Lentini gave the robe; the sources disagree).
- Naples inquiry witnesses used: Bartholomew of Capua (nos. 76–80), Abbot Nicholas of Fossanova (no. 8), Peter of Monte San Giovanni (nos. 49, 52; a monk, later abbot, the same man as the abbot of Tocco p. 141), William of Tocco (no. 61), John Blasi, judge (no. 70), John Coppa, notary (no. 87), James of Caiazzo (no. 42).
- Sacristan of the crucifix story: Dominic of Caserta (Tocco, Gui); James of Caserta (Calo).
- Fossanova: Cistercian abbey; prior James (Naples nos. 8, 49). Franciscan bishop of Terracina Francis at the funeral (Tocco p. 135).
- The hermit who foretold Thomas's birth: unnamed in the text ("a hermit from the mountain near Roccasecca"). Calo p. 18 gives no name; Tocco p. 66 calls him Brother Bonus (notes 12, 24).
- The new master who contradicted Saint Thomas at Paris (ch. 7, notes 49–51): unnamed in the text, as in Tocco p. 99 ("quidam Religiosus", "novus Magister"). Only note 49 says that Prümmer (p. 99 n. 1) thinks he was John Peckham. Peckham is named in the text nowhere. Bartholomew (note 52, pp. 373–374) names "John of Pisa", a Franciscan, in the story of Saint Thomas's gentle answers in debate; Laurent's note identifies him as John Peckham, later archbishop of Canterbury. Do not call the new master a Franciscan or Peckham.

## Dates and numbers
- Born about 1225 (Tocco: died in his 49th year). Monte Cassino at five. Entered the order as a teenager ("still in his teens"); year disputed.
- Captivity "almost two years" (Tocco p. 76, Calo p. 24, Gui ch. 8). Cantimpré: two or three; Gerard de Frachet: about one; Bartholomew: more than a year, and his father held him.
- Bachelor at Paris about 1252; master about 1256, "barely past thirty" (Gui ch. 12: "quasi annorum triginta").
- About 1259 recalled to Italy; 1261 sent to teach at Orvieto (14 Sept 1261); 1264 Corpus Christi (Urban IV); 1265 sent to run the school at Santa Sabina, Rome; 1268 second Paris term; 1272 Naples house of studies. (The 1260 preacher general and the Anagni chapter are cut from the text.)
- 15 Oct 1272: Charles of Anjou orders one ounce of gold a month (Laurent Documenta pp. 579–580; note 55).
- Lent 1273: sermons on the Our Father at Naples (no. 87, p. 391; note 57). The judge's Lent on the Hail Mary (no. 70) is cut.
- The Isaiah night (notes 58–62; Tocco pp. 105–106, Naples no. 59): no date and no place in the sources. The text says only "Once" and does not name a city. The debate with the new master (notes 49–51): no date in Tocco or Calo; Prümmer, following Denifle, puts it in the second Paris stay (note 49 only).
- Clement IV (notes 43–44; Tocco pp. 115–116): he offered "dignities and incomes"; Saint Thomas refused both, though his relatives were exiles in Campania for the Church's sake and the pope could have let some of the income go to them. A papal bull named him archbishop of Naples "with the addition of the revenues of the monastery of San Pietro ad Aram" (the text: "added to the office the income of the abbey"); he refused the office and begged the pope never to promote him to anything else. Neither offer has a date in Tocco.
- Writing stopped after Mass around the feast of Saint Nicholas (6 December) 1273; the Summa stops in the third part, q. 90 a. 4, on Penance (no. 79 and Laurent's note, p. 376).
- Died early morning of 7 March 1274, in his 49th year, at Fossanova (Tocco p. 138). Lent 1274 (Abbot Nicholas). Communion three days before death (Peter, no. 49, p. 332); Tocco has the anointing the next day.
- First opening of the tomb about seven months after death (Abbot Nicholas p. 278; Peter no. 52). About a hundred monks and lay brothers in the abbey (no. 51, p. 336).
- Paris arts masters' letter, 2 May 1274 (Laurent Documenta pp. 583–586).
- Right hand cut off in the fourteenth year after his death (Tocco p. 141; Butler gives 1288).
- 1303: Reginald's secret reaches Bartholomew of Capua (at Anagni, shortly before Boniface VIII was seized), then Benedict XI (no. 79, p. 378; note 105). About 1317 William of Tocco goes to the pope's court (Wikipedia).
- Canonized 18 July 1323 at Avignon by John XXII; feast 7 March; pardon of one year and forty days (bull §§19–21). The text says only "after inquiries at Naples and Fossanova" (note 106); the 1319 and 1321 dates and the pardon are cut.
- 1324 ranking next to Saint Dominic: cut.
- 1368 Urban V grants his body to the Dominicans; 28 Jan 1369 the relics enter Toulouse; Louis, Duke of Anjou, heads the crowd (Butler vol. 3 p. 59). Feast moved to 28 January in 1969 (note 107).
- 1567 Pius V: Doctor of the Church (note 107). The April day, the feast's rank, the patron of Naples, and Clement VIII (1603) are cut.
- 4 Aug 1879 Leo XIII, Aeterni Patris; 4 Aug 1880, Cum hoc sit.

## Places
- Santa Sabina, Rome (where the friars took him from his mother, Calo p. 21; later the school). Monte San Giovanni and Roccasecca (where he was held, Tocco p. 73). Acquapendente (brothers' camp). Maenza (castle of Francesca); Fossanova about six miles away (Abbot Nicholas p. 277). Molara, castle of Cardinal Richard (Ptolemy). San Severino (the countess's castle). Chapel of Saint Nicholas, Dominican house at Naples. Chapel of Saint Stephen at Fossanova.

## Approved wordings of quotations (our translations from the Latin unless marked)
- Chrysostom (note 1; Bartholomew p. 376): "Father, what a beautiful city Paris is!" ... "In truth, I would rather have Chrysostom on Matthew."
- Mother's call (note 14; Calo p. 21): "Your brother Thomas has been carried off by the Preaching Friars, and they are taking him to Paris. Alas, I will never see him again! The friars would not show him to me even for a moment, the joy of my heart. Help, sons. Take my son and bring him to his mother."
- Brothers (note 15; Tocco p. 73): they rushed at him "not as brothers but as enemies." (note 17; Calo p. 22): "his tears were his bread day and night."
- Prayer in the locked room (note 22; Calo p. 23): "Lord, I cannot be pure unless you, God, give it to me. I beg your mercy, which no words can tell, to grant me purity of mind and body, so that I may serve you with a clean heart and a clean body."
- Angels (note 23; Tocco p. 75, Naples no. 61): "On God's behalf we gird you with the belt of chastity, which no temptation can undo."
- Albert (note 26): a master "from whom he could eagerly drink what he thirsted for" (Tocco p. 77). Albert to Thomas (note 30; Tocco p. 79): "Brother Thomas, you do not seem to be answering. You seem to be deciding the question." / "Master, I do not see how I can answer the question any other way." / "We call him the dumb ox, but he will one day give such a bellow in his teaching that it will sound through the whole world."
- De ente, prologue: cut from the text (old note 32).
- The dream before the mastership (note 36; Tocco p. 85): "Brother Thomas, why do you weep as you pray to God?" / "Because the burden of the mastership has been laid on me, and my knowledge is not enough for it. And I cannot think what to put forward for my first lecture." / "See, you are heard. Take up the burden of the mastership, because God is with you. For your first lecture, put forward nothing but this: 'You water the hills from your high rooms; the earth will be filled with the fruit of your works.'" Saint Thomas woke and thanked God, who had heard him so quickly. Peter's shorter telling ("What are you thinking, Brother Thomas?"; Naples no. 49 p. 331) is in note 36 only. The crowded school after the mastership: Tocco p. 86 (note 37).
- The debate with the new master (notes 50–51; Tocco pp. 99–100, Calo pp. 34–35): the students: "Master, we feel this as a deep insult to you. That master should not have spoken against your opinion, and you should not have let the truth be wronged like this in front of all the masters of Paris." Saint Thomas: "Sons, I thought a new master should be spared at his first lecture, so that he would not be shamed in front of all the masters." ... "if the brothers think otherwise, tomorrow I can make up for what I left out." Next day: "Master, this opinion of yours cannot be held without harm to the truth, because it goes against a council of the Church. You must say it another way, if you do not want to disagree with the council." Then: "Now you speak well."
- The Isaiah night (notes 59–61; Tocco pp. 105–106, Naples no. 59): "Reginald, son, get up. Light a candle and take the notebook where you were writing on Isaiah. Get ready to write again." / "Go and rest, son. There is still a long time left to rest." / Reginald: "I will not get up from this place until you tell me who you were talking with so long tonight." / "Son, you do not need to know this." / "Son, you have seen how I have suffered these days over my doubt about this text, which I have just explained. With many tears I asked God to let me understand it. Tonight God took pity on me. He sent me the blessed apostles Peter and Paul, whom I had asked to pray for me, and they taught me everything fully. But on God's behalf I command you: do not dare to tell this to anyone while I live."
- Summa against the Gentiles (notes 11, 38): "Of all the studies of men ... the study of wisdom is the most perfect, the highest, the most useful, and the most joyful." "Her company has no bitterness, and living with her brings no weariness, but gladness and joy." "My mouth will ponder truth." ("truth must be the last end of the whole universe" is cut.)
- Summa of Theology (notes 3, 11, 39): "Grace does not destroy nature but perfects it." "As it is greater to give light than only to shine, so it is greater to hand on to others what one has contemplated than only to contemplate." "the least that can be known of the highest things is more to be desired than the most certain knowledge of the smallest things." "about God we cannot know what he is, only what he is not."  (Charity "is a kind of friendship of man with God" is cut.)
- Hymns (notes 38, 40): "Dare as much as you can, for he is greater than all praise, and you cannot praise him enough." (The Pange lingua and Sacris solemniis lines are cut.) Adoro te: "Jesus, whom I now see veiled, when will that come which I so thirst for?"
- Sister's words (note 41; Tocco p. 118): "You, brother, are in a good state, and you will soon come to us. But a greater glory is prepared for you than for us. Only hold on to what you have."
- King Louis's dinner (note 48; Calo p. 42, Gui pp. 191–192): "Now the argument is settled against the Manichaean heresy." / "Get up, scribe." / "Take note, Master. You are at the table of the king of France." / "Forgive me, my lord king. I thought I was in my study."
- Cardinal scene (Tocco p. 117): cut from the text.
- Clement IV (note 43; Tocco p. 116): "As he bore his own needs for the love of God, so he could bear theirs." (the narrator's rendering of "Qui sicut amore Dei necessitates sustinuit proprias, sic sustinere potuit alienas").
- The hermit to Theodora (note 12; Calo p. 18, Tocco pp. 66–67): her son would join the Preaching Friars (her answer is not in the text). Her grief and gladness: Calo p. 21 ("Dolebat namque... consolabatur"). Her test of the promise: Tocco p. 73.
- Molara (note 54; Ptolemy via Prümmer p. 45 n. 5): Saint Thomas's fever "every second day" (tertian) and the companion's "heavy fever that did not break"; the relics of Saint Agnes; the block quote is cut.
- Creed sermon (note 57): "because human sight can be deceived, but God's knowledge never fails."
- Crucifix (note 64; Calo p. 38): "Thomas, you have written well of me. What reward will you take from me for your work?" / "Lord, nothing but you."
- Writing stops (notes 67–74): he "hung up the tools of his writing." "Reginald, I cannot." The countess: "What is this? Brother Thomas is all dazed, and he has hardly spoken to me." Reginald: "The master is often caught up in spirit when he is contemplating something, but I have never seen him out of his senses for so long." Straw (Bartholomew p. 377): "I charge you by the living, almighty God, by the faith you owe our order, and by the love that binds you to me. Tell no one in my lifetime what I am going to tell you. Everything I have written seems to me like straw, compared with what I have seen and what has been shown to me." (Tocco p. 120): "From this I hope in God that, as my teaching has come to an end, so the end of my life will come soon."
- Teano road (notes 78–80; Bartholomew pp. 375–376): "Are you hurt?" / "A little." ... "Reginald, in no state can I be as useful to our order as in this one." ... "Reginald, be sure of this. I will never change my state."
- Fossanova: "This is my rest for ever. Here I will stay, for I have chosen it." (notes 82–83). "Why is it given to me that holy men should bring me wood?" (note 84; Abbot Nicholas p. 277). Consolation of Reginald (note 87; Tocco pp. 136–137), the Communion questions and answers (notes 89, 91; Tocco pp. 131–132), the confessor's words (note 95), Albert at Cologne (note 93; Gui ch. 43): "Brother Thomas of Aquino, my son in Christ, who was the light of the Church, has passed from this light." — all as printed in the biography.
- Peter's account of the tomb (note 99; Naples no. 52 p. 337): "When the tomb was opened such a sweet smell came out that it filled the whole chapel and the cloister. His clothes were still sound and whole, and the whole body was whole and unharmed, except that a small crack showed at the tip of the nose." The dream (note 97): "Bring me back to my first place."
- Paris masters (note 101): "the morning star, outstanding in the world."
- John XXII (note 106; Tocco p. 148): "We believe that Brother Thomas is a glorious saint in heaven, because his life was holy and his teaching could not exist without a miracle." (The bull's line that his life witnessed to his miracles is cut.)
- Leo XIII (note 108): "Thomas reigned supreme"; "the minds of all, of teachers as well as of taught, rested in wonderful harmony."

## Conflicts between sources (kept in the Note on the Sources)
Who held him (mother, Tocco; father, Bartholomew); length of captivity; who arranged the escape (mother, Tocco; Thomas, Ptolemy); who gave him the robe and in what year; sacristan's name; Raymund or Reginald at Molara; the countess's name (Theodora or Mary); days between last Communion and death; who preached at the funeral (Tocco: Reginald; Peter: a Dominican confessor); who had the dream about the first place (abbot, Tocco; prior, Abbot Nicholas); how he came to Fossanova (carried, invited, or riding).
