## Listed for the checker (needs a fact; not fixed)

1. m05, main paragraph: “He turned to Saint Thomas with devotion and had himself carried to the tomb, and he wrote down what he could not say.” The Status says “So he was not carried.” and that he prayed in his heart. The main text also says “His wife could not go in with him”, which implies he went. Check the bull §13 wording and make the two agree, or say that the tellings differ.
2. m06: the main paragraph says “On Saturday afternoon he gave himself devoutly to Saint Thomas”; the Status says “On Saturday evening his wife Mabilia urged him”. Check which time the bull and the witness give.
3. m57: “He thought himself unworthy to ask such a favor from so great a man.” Who is “so great a man”: Saint Thomas or Tocco? Name him.
4. m60, Status: “In Tocco the vow comes on the fifth day of the attacks. On the third day he gets up and serves the bishop of the inquiry faithfully to the end.” The third day of what? Probably the third day after the vow; confirm in Tocco and say so.
5. m85: “He was a doctor of Pandulph’s household.” Pandulph is not introduced anywhere. Say who he is (probably one of the inquisitors).
6. m108, Status: “Brother Leo led him to the tomb. Leo was then in Sicily.” “Then” is unclear. If it means Leo was in Sicily at the time of the oaths (so he did not swear), say so.
7. m73 heading: “A farmer’s hip pain ends after a barefoot vow.” The text says only that he was alone in a field. Confirm that the source calls him a farmer, or use “A man’s hip pain…”.
8. m50: “He held the candle without moving his fingers until it burned out. Saint Thomas had told his scribe never to call him, whatever the scribe saw.” The earlier text joined these with “because”, which made the scribe’s silence the cause of the holding. I split it; check whether Gui says the scribe saw and did not call him, and add that if so.
9. m09 and m37: both name a “James of Piperno”, once as assistant sacristan and once as cellarer. Confirm whether they are the same monk; if so, the two roles may need one line.
10. tomb-opened-again-fourteenth-year: I moved Peter’s sworn details of the seven-year opening next to the sentence that introduces that opening, so the stone tomb and thumb belong clearly to it and not to Theodora’s opening. Confirm that this is the right reading of Naples no. 52.
11. Inquisitors: from m06 on, the Status lines say “the inquisitors and notaries”. A modern reader may think of the Inquisition. The intro does not explain the word; the checker may want one short gloss (“the judges of the inquiry”) the first time.
12. m53: “Many people saw him suddenly taken up by the depth of the sacrament.” The phrase is close to the Latin and slightly odd in English; I left it because a plainer form might change the meaning. The checker may compare it with Gui.

6. m108: Peter says in his oath that Brother Leo "est in Sicilia", at the time of the oath. The Status now says so. The sources do not say that Leo did not swear.
7. m73: no source calls Nicholas of Leone a farmer (Naples 64, Tocco 45, Gui 18 say only that he was alone in a field). The heading is now "A man's hip pain ends after a barefoot vow".
