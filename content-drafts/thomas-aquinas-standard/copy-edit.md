## Resolved by the checker (2026-10-06)

Each item below is checked against the Latin in sources/ (Prümmer fasc. 1; Laurent fasc. 4).

1. "It is said that" at notes 30 and 57: both removals stand. Tocco, Gui, and Calo give Saint Albert's words with "fertur", so the Note on the Sources now says so. Calo p. 48 gives the elevation prayer without a hedge ("consuevit dicere"); Calo is now in note 57.
2. "Get up, scribe": Gui p. 191 has "vocansque socium suum ex nomine ait: Surge, scribe". No source gives the name. "By name" cut from the text; the quotation stays.
3. "Two cubits": Tocco p. 108 and Calo p. 38 have "duobus cubitis elevatum in aere". Kept, with "nearly a yard" so the reader has the length.
4. "I adjure you": our translation of Bartholomew p. 377 "adiuro te per Deum vivum". Now "I charge you", same meaning.
5. "The morning star standing out in the world": Latin "stellam matutinam preeminentem in mundo" (Laurent Documenta p. 584). Now "outstanding in the world".
6. The two Peters are one man. Naples no. 52 (Laurent p. 337) has Peter of Monte San Giovanni, a monk, made abbot; Tocco p. 141 has him abbot in the fourteenth year. Text now says "that same Peter"; note 101 gains no. 52.
7. The Gospel servant: Tocco p. 71 has "sub terra velut sudario", both the cloth and the burying. No change.
8. Carried or invited to Fossanova: both are in the sources. Abbot Nicholas p. 277 and Bartholomew p. 378: he asked to be carried. Tocco p. 130: the abbot and monks invited him, and he meant to rest a few days. Peter p. 332 has him riding there. Text keeps the first two; the Note on the Sources lists the three.
9. The fever at Molara: Ptolemy has "praefatus Doctor infirmatus est de tertiana" and then the companion's continuous fever. The text is right. The lead-in now says Ptolemy wrote down what happened to "both sick men".
10. Whose lectures: Calo p. 37 and Tocco p. 105 say Reginald, then lector at Naples, resumed his own. Text now says so.
11. "Rode over": Peter p. 331 says the prior "ivit ad visitandum", no horse. Now "came to visit him".
12. "Escort": Latin is "honorabili comitatu" / "comitiva honesta" (Gui "decenti comitatu"). Now "retinue".
13. "Pretended not to see": Tocco p. 77 has "dissimulata prudenter custodia, permisit ipsum ... per funem deponi". Now "relaxed the guard and let Saint Thomas be lowered", which ties her act to the escape.
14. Theodora: Calo p. 39, Tocco p. 111, and Gui all make the sister Theodora the Countess of San Severino, the mother of Thomasius. Text now says "Saint Thomas's sister Theodora, the Countess of San Severino".
15. The 31-word sentence with the quotation: left, as the copy editor advised.
