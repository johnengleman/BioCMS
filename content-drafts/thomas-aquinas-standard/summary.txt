Saint Thomas Aquinas's fellow students called him the dumb ox: a huge young friar who hardly said a word. Years before, his own brothers had dragged him off a road and locked him in a castle, and he had wrapped himself in the shreds of his torn robe rather than give it up. He wanted one thing: to know God as far as a human mind can, and to hand on what he knew. He prayed in tears before he studied, and he turned down the honors a pope offered him. Where would that desire lead him, and what would it cost?
