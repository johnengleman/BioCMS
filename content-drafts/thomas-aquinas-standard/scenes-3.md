## SC-11 — Arrival at Fossanova, February–March 1274
- Status: full
- When and where: Lent 1274 [N-1 n.8 p.278, Abbot Nicholas: "tempore quadragesimali"]. Cistercian abbey of Fossanova, about six Roman miles from Maenza, the castle of his niece's husband [N-1 n.8 p.277]. Church, then cloister (Tocco, Calo) or the parlour (Peter); then the abbot's room.
- Who was there: Thomas; his companion Reginald of Piperno; Prior James; monks Peter of Monte San Giovanni, John of Pedemonte, Fidelis [N-1 n.49 p.330-331]; Abbot Nicholas (later a witness) [N-1 n.8 p.278]; "many monks".
- What happened, in order:
  1. Ill at Maenza on the way to the council at Lyons. Abbot Nicholas heard from "many people" that Thomas said: "if the Lord is willing to visit me, it is better that I be found in a house of religious than in the houses of seculars". He had himself carried to Fossanova, about six miles, and lay ill about a month [N-1 n.8 p.277].
  2. Tocco: he was trying to go on "toward Rome"; the abbot and monks invited him; he wished to rest some days because no Dominican house was near [N-2 Tocco p.130]. Peter: Thomas rode there with the prior and monks, four days after they reached Maenza [N-1 n.49 p.331-332].
  3. He entered the church, honoured the altar, and entered the cloister (Tocco, Calo p.47). There he said to Reginald and the monks: "This is my rest for ever, here I will dwell, for I have chosen it" (Psalm 131:14) [N-2 Tocco p.130; N-1 n.8 p.277, n.49 p.332].
  4. He was put in the abbot's room. Monks carried wood from the forest for his fire on their own shoulders (Tocco, Calo) or hands (Abbot). Thomas said: "Why is this granted me, that servants of God serve a man?" [N-2 Tocco p.131; N-1 n.8 p.277]
  5. Some monks asked for a memorial of his learning; weak as he was, he briefly explained the Song of Songs [N-2 Tocco p.131; Calo p.47-48].
- Details: the abbot's room; the wood was for the fire; the monks said it was not fitting that "brute animals" bring wood for such a man [N-1 n.8 p.277].
- Words spoken: see steps 1, 3, 4. Latin of step 4 (Tocco): "Unde hoc mihi, ut servi Dei serviant homini, et onus tantum de longe deferant, quo laborant?" Plain: "Why is this given to me, that God's servants wait on a man and carry such a load from far off, and work so hard?"
- Testimony (Abbot Nicholas, sworn at Naples, 1319; N-1 n.8 p.277; public-domain; he saw the wood and heard the rest):
  > "Si Dominus voluerit me visitare, melius est quod reperiar in domo religiosorum quam in domibus secularium." Et ex tunc causa devotionis fecit se portari ad monasterium Fosse-Nove ... ubi iacuit infirmus quasi per mensem. ... "Hec requies mea in seculum seculi, hic habitabo quoniam elegi eam." Et dum idem frater Thomas iaceret infirmus, monachi dicti monasterii ob magnam devotionem ... propriis manibus portabant ligna de silva ad ipsum pro igne faciendo, sibi non arbitrantes congruum, quod ad usum tanti viri animalia bruta portarent ligna. Et ipse quoties videbat ipsos monachos portantes ipsa ligna ad cameram, ubi ipse iacebat, assurgebat cum magna devotione et humiliter dicens: "Unde hoc michi ut sancti viri deferant michi ligna?"
  
  plain: "If the Lord wants to visit me, it is better that I am found in a house of religious than in the houses of lay people." ... He lay ill there about a month. ... "This is my rest for ever; here I will stay, for I have chosen it." While he lay ill, the monks, out of deep respect, carried wood from the forest to his room with their own hands for his fire, because they thought it wrong that animals should carry wood for such a man. Each time he saw the monks bringing the wood to his room, he rose, with great reverence and humility, and said: "Why is it given to me that holy men bring me wood?"
- Inner life: Tocco says he believed, as he had foretold, that he would pass from this life [N-2 Tocco p.131]. Peter calls him one who prayed with tears at Mass [N-1 n.49 p.330].
- What changed: the man who refused bishoprics and castles takes a monastery bed and the service of monks, and does not go on to Lyons.
- Not in the sources: the weather; the road or its sights; what the cloister looked like; the number of monks at the door; any words of the monks; how Thomas was carried (the Abbot says "carried"; Peter says he rode); whether he said "this is my rest" aloud as a prophecy (Tocco) or only on entering (the Abbot heard it from others).
- Conflicts: place of "hec requies": cloister (Tocco, Calo), parlour (Peter), entrance (Abbot, hearsay). Wood: shoulders (Tocco, Calo) or hands (Abbot). Mind: Tocco hears prophecy in "requies"; the sworn men do not.
- Sources: N-1, N-2; W-q19 to q21 and C-S1 p.136 (not rechecked).

## SC-12 — Last Communion and death, Fossanova, 7 March 1274
- Status: full
- When and where: Lent 1274 [N-1 n.8 p.278]; died "septimo die Martii, hora matutinali" [W-S1 Tocco p.138, from notes; the bull fixes his feast at "nonis martii", the 7th, N-1 p.530]. In the abbot's room, Fossanova.
- Who was there: Thomas; the abbot and monks who carried the Host; Reginald; Peter of Monte San Giovanni, who served him; "many" Dominicans and Franciscans; the whole community [N-1 n.49 p.332; N-2 Tocco p.131-132].
- What happened, in order:
  1. Very weak, Thomas asked for the Sacrament as his viaticum. The abbot and monks brought it with respect [N-2 Tocco p.131].
  2. He threw himself on the ground, "weak in body, strong in mind", and met the Lord with tears [N-2 Tocco p.131; Calo p.48].
  3. Asked, as was the custom, whether he believed this Host was the true Son of God, he answered with a long profession (Tocco): "If a greater knowledge than faith can be had of this sacrament in this life, I answer in that knowledge that I truly believe, and know for certain, that this is true God and true man." Paraphrase [N-2 Tocco p.132].
  4. As he took the Host he said the words below; he left all he had written to the correction of the Roman Church [N-2 Tocco p.132; N-1 n.49 p.332].
  5. Peter's sworn account: he lived three days after receiving the Body of Christ and died on the third day. Tocco: the next day he asked for the holy oil, answered every prayer of the anointing, and soon gave back his spirit [N-1 n.49 p.332; N-2 Tocco p.132].
  6. At the funeral his long-time confessor preached: he had heard his general confession.
- Details: Tocco says Thomas also used to say at the elevation: "Tu Rex gloriae Christe, Tu Patris sempiternus es Filius" [N-2 Tocco p.132] (as report: "dicitur").
- Words spoken (Tocco, p.132): "Sumo te pretium redemptionis animae meae, sumo te viaticum peregrinationis meae, pro cuius amore studui, vigilavi, et laboravi, te praedicavi et docui, nihil contra te dixi unquam, sed si quid dixi, ignorans dixi nec sum pertinax in sensu meo, sed si quid male dixi de hoc Sacramento et aliis, totum relinquo correctioni Sanctae Romanae Ecclesiae, in cuius oboedientia nunc transeo ex hac vita."
  plain: "I receive you, the price of my soul's redemption. I receive you, the food for my journey. For love of you I studied, kept watch, and worked; I preached you and taught you. I never said anything against you. If I said anything wrong, I said it in ignorance, and I do not hold stubbornly to my own opinion. If I said anything wrong about this sacrament or about other things, I leave it all to the correction of the holy Roman Church, in whose obedience I now pass from this life."
- Testimony (Peter of Monte San Giovanni, sworn eyewitness at Naples, 1319; N-1 n.49 p.332; public-domain):
  > "Ego de isto santissimo Corpore et aliis sacramentis multa docui et scripsi, in fide Christi et sancte romane Ecclesie cuius correctioni omnia subicio et expono." Et recepto ipso Corpore, vixit per tres dies et ipso tertio die obdormivit in Domino. In cuius funere quidam frater Predicator qui fuerat longo tempore ipsius fratris Thome confessor, predicavit publice ... "Ego confessionem generalem istius sancti viri audivi de quo testificor quia ita inveni eum purum sicut puerum V annorum quia nunquam sue carnis sentiit corruptelam."

  plain: "I have taught and written much about this most holy Body and the other sacraments, in the faith of Christ and of the holy Roman Church, and I submit and give everything to its correction." After receiving the Body, he lived three days, and on the third day he fell asleep in the Lord. At his funeral a Dominican who had long been his confessor preached in public: "I heard this holy man's general confession, and I bear witness that I found him as pure as a child of five, because he never felt corruption of his flesh."
- Inner life: his own words above: study and watching were "for love of" Christ.
- What changed: the study ends in the Host received; the last act is submission of his work to the Church.
- Not in the sources: the room's look or light; names of those who wept; a sound or bell at the moment of death in a sworn account (Tocco alone has a monk's dream of a falling star and the tolling bell, p.133, legend); the exact hour inside the dawn.
- Conflicts: three days (Peter) against "next day" for the unction (Tocco). Funeral preacher: Reginald (Tocco p.136; Laurent's note, n.49 p.332) or Peter of Sezze (a note in the notes file; not re-read). Tocco's "last words" are a late literary account; Peter's words are shorter.
- Sources: N-1, N-2; W-q23 to q25 (checked against the Latin here).

## SC-13 — The hidden body, Fossanova, 1274
- Status: full
- When and where: burial just after 7 March 1274 before the high altar; secret move to the chapel of St Stephen, near the cloister; about seven months later the tomb is opened [N-1 n.8 p.278; n.52 p.336-337; N-2 Tocco p.138-140].
- Who was there: the abbot (Tocco: "Abbas"; Gui: Prior James); Abbot Nicholas and Peter (witnesses, both later); two outside monks and some oblates at the opening [N-2 Tocco p.139]; the whole community; the cantor.
- What happened, in order:
  1. Burial before the high altar. Fearing his Dominican brothers would take the body by order of the pope, "since it was entrusted to the monastery as a deposit", the abbot had it moved secretly to St Stephen's chapel [N-2 Tocco p.138-139; N-1 n.8 p.278].
  2. Thomas appeared to the abbot in a dream, scolded him for lacking reverence, and threatened him unless he soon brought the body back to the former place (Tocco). Abbot Nicholas: he appeared to Prior James and said "Reduc me ad locum pristinum" [N-1 n.8 p.278].
  3. After about seven months the abbot, with the chapel keys, two outside monks, and some oblates, opened the tomb with iron tools. Such a smell rose that the place seemed filled with many spices; monks came without being called [N-2 Tocco p.139].
  4. The body was whole, in habit, cloak and hood, "though the body had been large and fat and the grave wet and deep". Peter adds: a small crack at the tip of the nose [N-2 Tocco p.139; N-1 n.52 p.337].
  5. They carried him to the church. Without planning, the cantor began "Iste Sanctus digne in memoriam vertitur hominum, qui ad gaudium transiit Angelorum"; the community sang the Mass of a confessor, not the Mass for the dead; the cantor began "Os iusti meditabitur sapientiam" [N-2 Tocco p.139-140; N-1 n.8 p.278, n.52 p.337].
- Words spoken: "Reduc me ad locum pristinum" — plain: "Bring me back to my first place" (Abbot Nicholas, hearsay: "sicut publice dicebatur", N-1 n.8 p.278).
- Testimony (Peter, sworn eyewitness, N-1 n.52 p.336-337; public-domain):
  > post septem menses postquam fuit sepultus in capella sancti Stephani ... fuit exhumatum corpus ipsius ... et in apertura ipsius sepulcri tanta manavit inde fragrantia odoris quod replevit totam ipsam cappellam et etiam claustrum; et vestimenta ipsius ... adhuc erant sana et integra, et etiam totum corpus ipsius erat integrum et inllesum excepto quod modica ruptura apparebat in puncta nasi eius. ... missa de uno confessore ... nec reputabant congruum ... si cantarent missam de "Requiem eternam".

  plain: Seven months after he was buried in St Stephen's chapel the body was dug up. When the tomb was opened such a sweet smell came out that it filled the whole chapel and the cloister. His clothes were still sound and whole, and the whole body was whole and unharmed, except that a small crack showed at the tip of the nose. ... The monks sang the Mass of a confessor, because they thought it wrong to sing the "Requiem" as for an ordinary dead man.
- Paris letter: the Paris masters of arts wrote to the Dominican chapter at Lyons (the date 2 May 1274 is from the notes; the printed letter has no date in the passage read). They called him "stellam matutinam preeminentem in mundo" ("the morning star standing out in the world") and asked for his bones "pro maximo munere" ("as the greatest gift"), and for unfinished works: Simplicius on De caelo, Expositio of Plato's Timaeus, "De aquarum conductibus" [N-1 p.584-585]. The letter assumes he was already buried; it does not know where.
- What changed: the community treats him as a saint at once; the Dominicans and Paris both start to claim him.
- Not in the sources: the hour or night of the first move; who carried the body; the abbot's name in Tocco; what the two outside monks said; whether Thomas's dream was seen by the abbot or the prior (sources differ).
- Conflicts: abbot (Tocco) or Prior James (Abbot Nicholas, Gui); interval "seven months" (Abbot, Peter, Tocco's note) against "seven years" (some in Gui, notes C-§8). Peter places the first burial in the chapel then a move to the altar; Abbot Nicholas and Tocco say a move to the chapel then back.
- Sources: N-1, N-2; W-S2 p.583-586 in notes.

## SC-14 — The pope hears the case, 1317–23
- Status: full (Tocco's own words on the storm found)
- When and where: 1317–18 the Dominican provincial chapter at Gaeta sends Tocco and Robert of Benevento to the papal court (Tocco: "ad Romanam Curiam"; at Avignon per the bull); inquiries 1319 and 1321; canonization 18 July 1323 [N-2 Tocco p.147-149; bull N-1 p.530: "Datum Avinione xv kal. augusti, pontificatus nostri anno septimo"; Gui "XV kal. Augusti" 1323, N-1 p.531].
- Who was there: John XXII; Cardinals (Dominican cardinals "heard" his first answer); Tocco and his companions (named in Cod. Flor.: William of Tocco, prior of Benevento, and Robert of Benevento); sailors on a galley with "several Provincials".
- What happened, in order:
  1. The friars sail from Naples to the papal court with the written miracles. At night, in rain, with two sails full, the wind drives the galley toward Monte Argentario. Sailors strip to swim. A man shouts: "Pray to your saints, brothers, for the galley is about to break on the mountain." [N-2 Tocco p.147]
  2. The friars confess to each other, then call on the Virgin, Dominic, Peter Martyr, and last Thomas, asking that his miracles not be lost in the sea. A new wind blows from the side of the mountain, turns the galley, and puts it in open water. All shout "a miracle" [N-2 Tocco p.147-148].
  3. Before the pope, the friars ask that an inquiry into miracles be committed to the Church's authority. John XXII answers (see Testimony). He assigns the first consistory. On the third day the friar who had spoken is sent out, and the pope speaks to the cardinals [N-2 Tocco p.148-149].
  4. He names three prelates for the inquiry: the Archbishop of Naples, the Bishop of Viterbo, and his notary Pandulf of Sabello [N-2 Tocco p.149].
  5. After two inquiries, the bull "Redemptionem misit" declares him a saint: "eum sanctorum confessorum cathalogo duximus ascribendum"; plain: "we have decided to add him to the list of holy confessors." It orders his feast on the nones of March, and gives one year and forty days of pardon at his tomb [N-1 p.529-530].
- Words spoken, Testimony (John XXII, as Tocco reports; N-2 Tocco p.148-149; public-domain):
  > "Nos credimus quod Fr. Thomas est gloriosus sanctus in caelo, quia vita sua fuit sancta, et doctrina eius non potuit esse sine miraculo." ... "Fratres, nos reputamus nobis ad magnam gloriam et nostrae Ecclesiae, si istum Sanctum possimus catalogo Sanctorum adscribere, dummodo aliqua miracula possint de eo inveniri: quia ipse plus illuminavit Ecclesiam, quam omnes alii Doctores, in cujus libris plus proficit homo uno anno, quam in aliorum doctrina toto tempore vitae suae."
  
  plain: "We believe that Brother Thomas is a glorious saint in heaven, because his life was holy and his teaching could not exist without a miracle." ... "Brothers, we would count it a great glory for us and for our Church if we can enter this saint in the list of saints, provided some miracles can be found for him. For he lit up the Church more than all the other doctors, and in his books a man gains more in one year than in the teaching of others in his whole life."
  Storm (Tocco, p.148): "novus ventus a latere montis flavit, et galeam, quae impetu ferebatur ad montem, ab excidio percussionis avertit" — plain: "a new wind blew from the side of the mountain and turned the galley, which was rushing toward the mountain, away from being smashed."
- What changed: the Church opens the inquiry; Thomas is declared a saint.
- Not in the sources: the pope's look beyond Tocco's "like the sun sending a ray"; the hall; the number of cardinals present on the third day; the day the friars sailed; who steered. The Franciscan who would "rather die" than see it is in Wikipedia only (legend). "As many miracles as articles" is not in Tocco (notes: Conway, no source).
- Conflicts: Tocco tells it of "the Fathers" and the friars in third person; the Cod. Flor. adds the names. Maritain's list dates a papal word on 1 March 1318 ("He alone enlightened the Church more than all the other doctors") [iapweb.org JMC_STA_6, secondary]; Tocco gives no date.
- Sources: N-2, N-1; Maritain list (JMC_STA_6, secondary, not copied).

## SC-15 — The relics reach Toulouse, 28 January 1369
- Status: thin (no primary source for the 150,000 or the arrival; Conway only)
- When and where: Urban V at Viterbo/Montefiascone, 1368; relics laid in the papal chapel on 4 August 1368; arrival at the Dominican church, Toulouse, 28 January 1369 (Conway; Catholic Encyclopedia gives the date and Urban V's order).
- Who was there: Urban V; Elias of Toulouse, Master General; Louis, Duke of Anjou; "many prelates"; "a concourse of 150,000 persons" (Conway, no source given).
- What happened, in order (all Conway, ch. X, PD, popular and unsourced):
  1. The Cistercians complain to Urban V. Elias goes to the pope. Urban says: "it is you who stole the body of St. Thomas." Elias: "Holy Father ... he is our brother and our flesh."
  2. At Montefiascone, on Corpus Christi, Elias reminds the pope that Thomas wrote the Office of the Blessed Sacrament for Pope Urban IV. Urban gives the body "to you, the Master General, and to the Order of Friars Preachers ... to be placed at Toulouse, or Paris", and next day chooses Toulouse. He adds the head.
  3. Head and body laid in the papal chapel, 4 Aug 1368; a month at Prouille; 28 Jan 1369 carried into the Dominican church by Louis of Anjou, prelates, and the crowd.
- Words spoken: "he is our brother and our flesh" (Elias to Urban V; Conway ch.X). plain: "He is one of us; he is our own flesh." Source is a 1911 popular life, not a document.
- Official support: Leo XIII quotes Urban V's order to the University of Toulouse: "Volumus et tenore praesentium vobis iniungimus, ut b. Thomae doctrinam tamquam veridicam et catholicam sectemini, eamdemque studeatis totis viribus ampliare." Plain: "It is our will, and by this letter we order you, to follow the teaching of Blessed Thomas as true and Catholic and to work with all your strength to spread it." (Aeterni Patris, Latin text at vatican.va; the footnote dates it 3 Aug 1368.)
- What changed: the Dominicans hold the body; the 1969 reform moved the feast from 7 March to 28 January, the day of this arrival (Wikipedia "Feast of Saint Thomas Aquinas", facts-only).
- Not in the sources: the weather, the route of the procession, the sound of bells, hymns sung, words of Louis of Anjou, what the crowd did.
- Conflicts: Wikipedia names the receiving church as Saint-Sernin; Conway and Catholic Encyclopedia say the Dominican church. The body later lay at Saint-Sernin (Conway; CathEnc). Not resolved.
- Tries: Conway, CathEnc, Wikipedia, a search for a contemporary chronicle. Not reached: a 14th-century text.
- Sources: Conway (https://books.thomistica.net/Conway/conway04.htm); CathEnc (https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14663b.htm); Aeterni Patris Latin (https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/la/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_04081879_aeterni-patris.html); Wikipedia Feast of Saint Thomas Aquinas.

## FN-1 — What his teaching formed
Leo XIII, Aeterni Patris, 4 Aug 1879, sections 20-22 (read at vatican.va). It names the universities Paris, Salamanca, Alcalá, Douai, Toulouse, Louvain, Padua, Bologna, Naples, Coimbra, where "Thomas reigned supreme". It says Urban V ordered the University of Toulouse to follow his teaching (letter of 3 Aug 1368 per its note). It says the Fathers of Trent laid the Summa on the altar with Scripture and papal decrees. It names the Councils of Lyons, Vienna, Florence, and the Vatican. It lists the Benedictines, Carmelites, Augustinians, and Jesuits as bound by statute to follow him. English: https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_04081879_aeterni-patris.html

## FN-2 — Date of Mirabilis Deus
11 April 1567: Pius V proclaims Thomas a Doctor in the constitution "Mirabilis Deus". Source: Jacques Maritain Center list "Testimonies of the Popes" (https://iapweb.org/web-archive/JMC_STA_6.html), secondary, read. The bull text was not found in four tries. Catholic Encyclopedia gives only "1567". Aeterni Patris names "the bull Mirabilis" of Pius V without a date. Use "April 1567" if a primary text is needed.

## FN-3 — His Corpus Christi hymns still used
Not found stated as "still sung today". Found: John Paul II, homily for Corpus Domini, 29 May 1997 (vatican.va), names "Pange, lingua" and "Adoro te, devote" as Thomas's poems, and quotes lines from them ("gloriosi Corporis mysterium"; "Latens Deitas"). Catholic Encyclopedia (1912) says the Office of Corpus Christi has "Sacris solemniis", "Pange Lingua" (ending in "Tantum ergo"), "Verbum supernum", and the sequence "Lauda Sion". Vatican liturgy booklets exist but the PDF could not be read. https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/homilies/1997/documents/hf_jp-ii_hom_29051997.html
