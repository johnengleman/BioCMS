## Listed, not fixed

Each needs the source or a decision by the owner.

### biography.html

1. "Tocco says the message came to them under the cover of a mother's blessing." The figure is unclear (was the blessing a disguise for the call to arms?). A plain version needs Tocco's Latin.
2. "the Mass of a confessor" (narration after Peter's block quote). Readers may take "confessor" as the priest who hears confessions, and the same page says "a Dominican who had long been his confessor". Suggested added sentence: "In the Church's language a confessor is a saint who was not a martyr." Not added, because it is a new statement. The same applies to m01 in the miracles.
3. "It was the morning hour of 7 March 1274". "The morning hour" is odd English. The source may mean early morning or the night hour of Matins; the checker should confirm before it changes.
4. "With many tears he prayed to find the secrets of God in truth." Awkward order, but it follows Reginald's words as Tocco gives them; left as is.
5. "The letter does not say where he lay." (Paris masters' letter.) The point of this sentence is unclear to a reader. Left because a fix would need to know what the writer meant.

### miracles.html

6. "The Mother of God also told him not to change the state of his Order, as he had often asked in his prayers." This reads as if she refused his prayer. Elsewhere he prayed never to change his own state. Check Tocco ch. 32 for the exact sense.
7. Sulmona vision: "He added that Saint Peter of Morrone had been canonized only after he died, and that Saint Thomas was still alive." The logic of the monk's remark is unclear. Also, "The monk woke and could not remember the invitatory. He seemed to see Saint Thomas putting on his vestments for Mass, and he woke up rejoicing." He wakes twice. Needs Tocco no. 14.
8. m29: "the knowledge he had on the way". "On the way" is the old term for this earthly life; a reader may not follow. A gloss ("on the way, in this life") would be safe if the checker agrees.
9. m42: "She prayed devoutly with tears that Saint Thomas would bring him back". Unclear if "she" is Lady Mary or her mother. Needs Gui no. 89.
10. m21 status: "told under oath in 1319 by Tocco, who heard that Francis of Alatri had often heard it from Reginald." Clumsy, but the chain of tellers is not clear enough to rewrite safely.
11. For the fact checker, not a wording issue: the second-opening status says the bull counts witnesses to the scent "some after seven years", while the first opening was about seven months after the burial. It may be right (the bull may mean another opening); flagged only.

### biography.html

1. "under the cover of a mother's blessing". Tocco: "mandans eis sub maternae benedictionis obtentu" (p. 72), and Gui: "precipitque eis sub obtentu materne benedictionis" (p. 172). It means she commanded them in the name of her blessing. It does not mean a disguise. Now: "Tocco says she commanded them in the name of a mother's blessing to take Saint Thomas and send him to her under guard." (Tocco: take him in their hands and send him to her "sub bona custodia".)
2. "Mass of a confessor". The term stays, because Peter's oath, the bull ("missam veluti de uno confessore"), and Tocco all use it. Added one plain sentence before "Tocco adds what Peter left out": "In the Church's calendar, a confessor is a saint who was not a martyr."
3. "the morning hour". Calo, Tocco, and Gui all say "hora matutinali". Tocco links it to "diluculum" (first light). No source gives a clock time. Now: "early in the morning of 7 March 1274."
4. "With many tears he prayed to find the secrets of God in truth". Tocco: "profusus orabat lacrymis, pro divinis inveniendis in veritate secretis". Now: "With many tears he prayed that God would show him his secrets in truth." (The word "truly" and "holy" trip the glow-word check, so the plain form is kept.)
5. "The letter does not say where he lay". Cut. The Paris letter (Laurent, Documenta xxxi, pp. 585-586) says that no other land than Paris should keep his bones, because Paris reared and nourished him. Now: "They wrote that no other city should keep his bones, because Paris had raised him and had been fed by his teaching."
6. "About seven months after the burial". Peter of Monte San Giovanni (Naples process no. 52, p. 337) and Nicholas of Piperno (pp. 291-292) both say "quasi septem mensibus" after his death. Another witness counts from the burial in the chapel of Saint Stephen. Changed to "About seven months after his death".

### miracles.html

7. m01 and "Mass of a confessor": same one-sentence explanation added after the Mass sentence. "About seven months later" is now "About seven months after his death".
8. First opening against second opening ("some after seven years"). Both are right. The bull (Latin: "post septem annorum spatium, quidam ex testibus, quidam vero quasi post quatuordecim annorum spatium") speaks of later inspections, not of the first opening. Peter of Monte San Giovanni swore that the first opening was about seven months after the death and that a second came seven years after the first (when he was abbot). Nicholas of Piperno swore to the opening for Theodora almost fourteen years after the death. The status line of "The tomb is opened again" now says this.
9. Mother of God. Calo (p. 37): "quod non mutaret statum sui ordinis, sicut frequenter in oracione peciverat, impetravit" (she assured him that he would not change the state of his Order, which he had often asked in prayer, and he obtained it). Tocco (p. 107) has the same words. In his own words to Reginald (Gui, ch. 22, and the Naples process, no. 78) he asked never to be lifted out of his humble state by a high office. So the Mother of God gave him what he asked. Now: "She also gave him what he had often asked in his prayers: he would not change his state in his Order."
10. Sulmona vision (Tocco, catalogue no. 14, p. 150). The monk says: "It is true that you ought to be canonized, but Brother Peter of Morrone was canonized only after he was dead, and you are still alive." Saint Thomas answers that no one is canonized unless alive, and Peter lives. Now told with the monk's words in quotation marks. The printed text has two waking words; the Florence manuscript (Prümmer's note) has one waking: "evigilans postmodum non fuit recordatus" (afterward on waking he did not remember). The entry follows that one waking: Saint Thomas speaks the invitatory in the dream, the monk sees him vesting for Mass, wakes rejoicing "quia Deus dignatus fuerat de suo Sancto mira revelare", and afterward cannot remember the invitatory.
11. m29: "the knowledge he had on the way". Tocco: "dum essem in via"; Calo: "dum essem in vita". Now: "the knowledge he had while he lived on earth".
12. m42, the stillborn baby. "She" is Lady Mary's mother (Gui no. 89: "credens autem mater puerpere ... oravit devote cum lacrimis"). The text now says "Lady Mary's mother", adds that Saint Thomas had already freed her daughter twice ("bina vice"), and says the child lay half born.
13. m21 status. Naples process no. 59 (pp. 346-347): Tocco often heard it from Francis de Amore of Alatri, vicar ("vicarius") of the bishop of Nola, and Francis often heard it from Reginald. Rewritten as a plain chain; "official" changed to "vicar".
