# Saint Thomas Aquinas — scene outline (standard 1b, v2)

**Keys** as in `outline-v1.md` (W-, O-, C- notes files); **card** = `scenes-1/2/3.md` by old number.

## 1. The driving question

Saint Thomas wanted God himself: to know him as far as a mind can, and to hand on what he knew. His family's force, a pope's honors, and the limit he taught ("about God we cannot know what he is, only what he is not" [O-Q5]) stood in the way. He held to the friars' robe, refused every rank [W-S1 Tocco p.116; W-S2 p.375-376], and studied on his knees; at the end grace gave him more than he could write, and he died receiving Christ, "for love of whom I studied" [O-Q34].

**Why it was beautiful to him** (his later words, given at the first great decision, ch. 2): "Of all the studies of men, the study of wisdom is the most perfect, the highest, the most useful, and the most joyful" [O-Q23]; its company has "no bitterness … but gladness and joy" [O-Q24]. He chose preaching friars over monks because "it is greater to give light than only to shine" [O-Q17]. The notes hold no words from the day itself (Fact need 1).

**Why famous:** the Church's great teacher: canonized 1323, Doctor 1567, patron of every Catholic school 1880 [C-S4; C-S2 p.60; C-S7].

**Set apart:** a count's son, he chose a begging order and held to it against his family [card SC-02]. He put Aristotle to work for the faith ("grace does not destroy nature but perfects it" [O-Q3, O-Q4]), let the other side speak first, and met taunts with mildness [W-S2 p.374].

## 2. Portrait

A huge, quiet man: tall, heavy, bald at the front [W-S2 n.42 p.319]. Students called him the dumb ox [W-S1 Tocco p.78]. A dish could be set before him and taken away unnoticed [W-S2 p.374]. Dry wit: offered Paris, he would rather have a book [W-S2 n.78 p.376]. Mild in debate, face "cheerful, gentle, pleasant" [W-S2 p.374]. Strangely sensitive to pain [W-S1 Tocco p.121]. He feared his own unfitness [card SC-05]; monks carrying his firewood embarrassed him [card SC-11]. Faults are few: absent-mindedness, and anger quick enough to seize a burning stick [card SC-03]. No hostile witness survives [W §4].

**Life with God.** He prayed before every study, often in tears, and came back taught [W-S1 Tocco p.104-105]; at Naples he said Mass at dawn and heard a second [W-S2 n.77 p.374]; he preached with eyes closed [W-S2 n.70 p.362]. Charity is "a kind of friendship of man with God" [O-Q13].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** hook, a scene at the edge (SC-01). It shows the desire and spends nothing.

> One day Saint Thomas Aquinas was walking back to Paris with some friars, and they stopped to look at the city. "Father, what a beautiful city Paris is!" one of them said. "It certainly is," he answered. "I wish it were yours," said the friar. "What would I do with it?" he asked. "You could sell it to the king of France and build every house of the Preaching Friars with the money." "In truth," said Saint Thomas, "I would rather have Chrysostom on Matthew": an old book of sermons on a Gospel. His family once tried to take what he wanted by force, and a pope tried to draw him from it with honors.

[W-S2 n.78 p.376; check.md ch. 7]

**Never in the opening or summary.txt:** the December Mass, "I cannot", straw, the death, the last Communion, Trent.

**Thesis** (2–4 sentences after the opening): canonized 1323, Doctor 1567, Leo XIII's guide for Catholic schools [C-S4; C-S2 p.60; C-S6]; his mark is faith and reason together [O-Q3] and teaching as handing on what one has seen [O-Q17]. Answered in ch. 5–7 and 13.

**Ending:** ch. 13 gives canonization, Toulouse, 1567 and 1880 briefly, then Leo XIII's 1879 letter. Closing image: at the Council of Trent his unfinished Summa lay on the altar beside the Bible [C-S6; card FN-1]. One quiet sentence may follow: the book still stops in the pages on Penance [W-S2 p.376 n.a].

## 4. Chapters

1. **Saint Thomas Goes to the Monks at Five** — SC-02. Ends on its own image: at Naples he meets friars who own nothing and preach.
2. **Saint Thomas Joins the Friars and Is Seized** — the choice, the desire in his words; SC-03. **Pull:** carried home a prisoner, still in the robe.
3. **Saint Thomas's Family Locks Him in a Castle** — SC-04. **Pull:** the friars send him north to a great teacher.
4. **Saint Thomas Studies Under Saint Albert** — SC-05. Ends: Saint Albert asks that he go to Paris.
5. **Saint Thomas Becomes a Master at Paris** — SC-06. **Pull:** he takes the old friar's verse as his task.
6. **Saint Thomas Teaches in Italy and Refuses Honors** — bridges: the two Summas, Corpus Christi hymns, Clement IV's offer refused, reason held back [W-S2 p.582-583; O-S5; W-S1 Tocco p.116]. Ends: back to Paris with the unfinished Summa.
7. **Saint Thomas Dines With Saint Louis of France** — SC-07; Peckham. Ends: 1272, sent to Naples.
8. **Saint Thomas Teaches and Preaches in Naples** — SC-08, Lent 1273, SC-09. Do not end on the crucifix. Ends on its own image: the book has reached Penance.
9. **Saint Thomas Stops Writing After a Mass** — SC-10, climax. **Pull:** a pope summons him to a council.
10. **Saint Thomas Falls Ill on the Road** — SC-11. **Pull:** he asks to be taken to a monastery.
11. **Saint Thomas Dies Among the Monks** — SC-12, SC-13. Ends on its own image: "pure as a child of five".
12. **The Monks Find Saint Thomas's Body Whole** — SC-14, SC-15; Paris masters' letter; his sister. **Pull:** his order asks Rome to name him a saint.
13. **The Church Makes Saint Thomas Its Teacher** — bridges and closing image.

## 5. The scenes

**Turning points (slow, from inside him):** TP1 SC-04, TP2 SC-06, TP3 SC-09, TP4 SC-10 (climax), TP5 SC-13 (resolution).

**Climax.** SC-10 is the fullest scene, in story words; the chain of witnesses goes to the note. A short bridge (the book reaches Penance) stands between SC-09 and SC-10.

**Relationships to their end**
- Theodora: parting at the rope (SC-04). Brothers: Rainald later killed by Frederick II [W §2]. Saint Albert: last seen in the 1252 letter [W-S1 Tocco p.80-81]. See Fact needs 2–3.
- The Countess of San Severino: he hardly speaks to her in December 1273; about fourteen years later she receives his right hand "with tears" [C-S1 Tocco p.141-142]: one sentence in ch. 12.
- Reginald: ends in SC-14. Niece Francesca: mourns at the gate [W-S1 Tocco p.135]. Saint Louis: died 1270 at Tunis [card SC-07], one line.

**Losses at their dates:** sister, c.1225; home at five; captivity c.1243–45 (concrete day: the burning stick); Rainald; fever, 1272; silence, December 1273; the month of dying, 1274.

### SC-01 — Outside Paris, undated — OPENING
- What happens: a friar wishes Paris were his to sell; he would rather have Saint John Chrysostom on Matthew.
- Why: the desire as the hook; ch. 7 does not retell it.
- Have: W-S2 n.78 p.376; check.md ch. 7. Need: nothing.

### SC-02 — Storm at Roccasecca, c.1225; Monte Cassino at five
- What happens: lightning kills his baby sister; Theodora runs trembling to his bed; at five he goes to the abbey; his father means him for abbot.
- Why: the first loss and the family plan.
- Have: card SC-01; W-S2 n.76 p.371; "What is God?" as old story only [O-S10 p.119-120]. Need: nothing.

### SC-03 — By a spring on the road, c.1243
- What happens: resting with four friars, he is seized by his brothers "not as brothers but as enemies"; shouting and weeping, he holds the robe.
- Why: the first great decision, held under force.
- Have: card SC-02; W-S3 XXII.20. Need: Fact need 1.

### SC-04 — The locked room, c.1244–45 (Monte San Giovanni) — TP1
- What happens: robe torn, he reads the Bible; the girl, the burning stick, the cross on the wall, tears, prayer, the angels' belt, the cry he never explains. Ends at the rope, his mother looking away.
- Why: the call at its highest cost; inside him, desire felt, then prayer.
- Have: card SC-03; W-q4, W-q5. Testimony: Tocco, W-S2 n.61 p.349. Need: nothing.

### SC-05 — Saint Albert's classroom, Cologne
- What happens: the dumb ox repeats a lecture; Saint Albert tests him; "his bellow will sound through the whole world." Told as "it is said".
- Why: the hidden gift and the teacher who frees it.
- Have: card SC-04. Need: nothing.

### SC-06 — Night before the mastership, Paris, c.1256 — TP2
- What happens: ordered to become master, he thinks himself unfit and too young; prays at an altar in tears; an old friar gives him "You water the hills from your high rooms."
- Why: fear, prayer, and God's answer, in his own telling.
- Have: card SC-05. Testimony: Peter, W-S2 n.49 p.331. Need: nothing.

### SC-07 — Saint Louis's table, Paris, c.1269–70
- What happens: he strikes the table against the Manichaean heresy; the prior pulls his cape; the king calls a scribe.
- Why: the mind at work; humor; a fault shown kindly.
- Have: card SC-07; W-S2 p.374. Need: nothing.

### SC-08 — Molara, late 1272
- What happens: he and his companion fall ill; he sends the man Saint Agnes's relics; the man recovers; he keeps a feast for the friars.
- Why: illness and care for a friend, told by an eyewitness.
- Have: card SC-06. Testimony: Ptolemy, W-S3 XXIII.10. Need: nothing.

### SC-09 — The crucifix, chapel of Saint Nicholas, Naples, 1273 — TP3
- What happens: before night prayer he prays in tears; "Thomas, you have written well of me. What reward will you take?" "Lord, nothing but you."
- Why: the desire in his own answer. The friars' tradition; sacristan unnamed.
- Have: card SC-08. Need: nothing.

### SC-10 — After Mass, chapel of Saint Nicholas, about 6 December 1273 — TP4, CLIMAX
- What happens: moved by a wonderful change, he stops in the pages on Penance; "Reginald, I cannot"; Reginald fears for his mind; at his sister's he hardly speaks; the oath; "Everything I have written seems to me like straw, compared with what I have seen."
- Why: the answer to his desire, beyond writing. Slowest scene.
- Have: card SC-09. Testimony: Bartholomew, art. LXXIX, W-S2 p.376-377. Need: nothing.

### SC-11 — The road near Teano, early 1274
- What happens: he hits his head on a fallen tree; Reginald talks of cardinals; "Reginald, be sure of this. I will never change my state." Then illness at Maenza.
- Why: the last refusal of rank, by a changed man.
- Have: card SC-10; W-S2 p.374-375. Need: nothing.

### SC-12 — Arrival at Fossanova, Lent 1274
- What happens: "This is my rest for ever"; monks carry wood and he rises each time; the Song of Songs; "Do not be troubled, son. I asked it of God among my other desires."
- Why: the reason for every refusal, held back since ch. 6.
- Have: card SC-11; C §6 q9. Testimony: Abbot Nicholas, W-S2 n.8 p.277. Need: nothing.

### SC-13 — Last Communion and death, 7 March 1274 — TP5
- What happens: on the floor in tears; "I receive you … For love of you I studied, kept watch, and worked"; all he wrote given to the Church's correction; death at dawn; Francesca at the gate; "pure as a child of five."
- Why: the resolution: the study ends in Christ received.
- Have: card SC-12; O-Q34. Testimony: Peter, W-S2 n.49 p.332. Need: nothing.

### SC-14 — Reginald's secret, Naples, 1274
- What happens: back at Naples, Reginald tells the friars, weeping: "Brothers, my master forbade me in his lifetime to reveal the wonders I saw in him." He keeps the straw oath until his own last illness.
- Why: closes the closest friendship; his prayer, told by the one who knew it.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.104-105; W-S2 p.377-378. Need: nothing.

### SC-15 — The opened tomb, Fossanova, autumn 1274 — CASE AFTER DEATH
- What happens: the monks hide the body; "Bring me back to my first place"; after about seven months fragrance fills chapel and cloister; the body is whole; unplanned, they sing: "The mouth of the just man will speak wisdom."
- Why: a community, a year, the first veneration; the silent man "speaks wisdom".
- Have: card SC-13. Testimony: Peter, W-S2 n.52 p.336-337. Need: nothing.

## 6. The saint in the Church

- **Works:** Summa against the Gentiles 1259–65 [O-Q23], ch. 6. Corpus Christi office 1264, "probable" [O-F8], ch. 6; named by Saint John Paul II, 1997 [card FN-3], ch. 13. Summa of Theology 1265–73, stopped at III q.90 [W-S2 p.376], ch. 6, 9. Lent sermons 1273 [W-S2 n.87 p.391], ch. 8. Paris masters' letter 1274 [W-S2 p.583-586], ch. 12.
- **What he changed:** Dominican chapters 1324–28 rank him after Saint Dominic [C-S8]; Blessed Urban V orders Toulouse University to follow him, 1368 [card SC-15]; Doctor, Saint Pius V, April 1567 [card FN-2]; Aeterni Patris 1879: universities, four orders, councils, Trent [C-S6]; patron of schools 1880 [C-S7]. All ch. 13.
- **Why famous:** tomb healings, ten in the bull [C-S4 §9-18]; Toulouse 1369, feast moved 1969 [C-S8]; patron of Naples [C-S2 p.60]. Ch. 12–13.
- **Path:** first veneration 1274, ch. 12; inquiries 1319, 1321; canonized at Avignon 18 July 1323 [card SC-14], ch. 13.

## 8. What we leave out

- Hermit's prophecy, Hail Mary paper, light from his face, stars, comet, mule: legends or Tocco only.
- Levitations, tooth, Isaiah vision, demon, herrings, candle, the 1318 sea storm: miracles section.
- Head, 1585 skull, Fondi, Toulouse details: contested or thin.
- Bow and rest, Summa articles: teachings section.
- False lines (O-F1 to O-F7), "as many miracles as articles", Saint Albert's 1276 remark: no primary source.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth c.1225 or 1227; entry year disputed.
- Captor: mother (Tocco) or father (Bartholomew); almost two years, or two or three. No "night" at the rope.
- Molara: late 1272. Saint Louis's dinner: no year.
- Teano: no riding, no hat offered; "cardinals" is Reginald's word.
- Do not write "largest theology of his age" or "Neapolitan dialect".
- "Palee": chaff or straw; the day is "about the feast of Saint Nicholas".
- Sacristan: Dominic or James of Caserta. Funeral preacher: Reginald or Peter of Sezze.
- The hand: "fourteenth year" (Tocco). Toulouse crowd: Conway and Butler only.
