# Thomas Aquinas — scene outline (standard 1b)

- **O-** = `notes-own-words.md`: O-Q3 = its quotation Q3; O-S5 Corpus Christi office note; O-S9 Fontes (same edition as W-S1/W-S2); O-S11 Wikipedia; O-F = its "wrongly credited" flags.

## 1. The driving question

From boyhood Thomas wanted to know God and to hand on what he knew. A family that wanted a lord abbot used force against him, popes later offered high office, and his own teaching set a harder limit: "about God we cannot know what he is, only what he is not" [O-Q5]. He refused every rank to stay a teaching friar [W-S2 p.375-376] and wrote the largest theology of his age; then after one Mass all of it seemed like straw beside what he had seen [W-S2 p.377], and he died receiving Christ, "for love of whom I studied" [O-Q34].

**Why famous:** he is the Catholic Church's great teacher: canonized 1323, Doctor of the Church 1567, guide of Catholic thought under Leo XIII in 1879, patron of Catholic schools since 1880 [C-S4; O-S11; C-S6; C-S7].

**What set him apart:** a count's son, he chose a begging order of preachers and held to it against his family's force [W-S1 Tocco p.72-73]. He put the pagan philosopher Aristotle to work for the faith ("grace does not destroy nature but perfects it") and met taunts with mildness, never with grand words [O-Q3, O-Q4; W-S2 p.374].

## 2. Portrait

A huge, quiet man: tall, heavy, bald at the front [W-S2 n.42 p.319; W-S1 Calo p.40]. At Cologne his fellows called him the dumb ox [W-S1 Tocco p.78]. A dish could be set before him and taken away several times without his noticing [W-S2 p.374]; at a king's table he struck the board when an argument came to him [W-S1 Tocco p.116-117]. In debate he used "no boastful or swollen words" and met Peckham's taunts "with sweetness and kindness"; his face was cheerful and gentle [W-S2 p.374]. One meal a day; no care for food or clothes [W-S2 p.346, 362]. Strangely sensitive to pain [W-S1 Tocco p.121]. Dry wit: offered Paris, he would rather have Chrysostom on Matthew [W-S2 n.78 p.376]. Embarrassed when monks carried his firewood [W-S2 p.277]. He feared honour: he pleaded youth against the mastership and refused the see of Naples [W-S1 Tocco p.84-85, 116]. He taught that the mind breaks like a bow never unstrung [O-Q16]. Faults in the notes are few: absent-mindedness, and a fierceness that drove out a tempter with a burning brand. No hostile witness survives [W §4].

**Life with God.** He prayed before every study, often in tears [W-S1 Tocco p.105]; at Naples he said Mass at dawn and heard a second [W-S2 n.77 p.374]; he preached with eyes closed [W-S2 n.70 p.362]. Charity is "a kind of friendship of man with God" [O-Q13]. In the old story he asks Christ for no reward "except yourself" [O-Q33].

## 3. The opening

**Choice:** hook, the scene at the edge.

> In December 1273, in a chapel of the friars' house at Naples, a large, heavy friar said Mass, and something in him changed. For years he had written or dictated every day, and he was deep in the third part of his great work, on the sacrament of Penance. After that Mass he never wrote or dictated another line. His friend Reginald begged him to go on. "Reginald, I cannot," he said. "Everything I have written seems to me like straw, compared with what I have seen."

[W-S2 n.77 p.374; n.79 p.376-377; O-S9 p.376-377]

**Thesis plan:** the Church made this silent friar its chief teacher: canonized 1323, Doctor 1567, guide of Catholic schools under Leo XIII [C-S4; O-S11; C-S6; C-S7]. His own mark: faith and reason working together, philosophy as a servant [O-Q3, O-Q4], and teaching as handing on to others what one has contemplated [O-Q17]. The straw is not defeat: ch. 9 shows it, ch. 11 resolves it, ch. 13–14 answer the rest.

## 4. Chapters

1. **Thomas Is Sent to a Great Abbey as a Boy** — SC-01. Ends: in his teens at Naples he meets the preaching friars.
2. **Thomas Becomes a Friar, and His Brothers Seize Him** — SC-02. Ends: carried home a prisoner, still in his torn habit.
3. **Thomas's Family Locks Him in a Castle** — SC-03; the escape by rope as bridge. Ends: friars wait below the wall at night.
4. **Thomas Studies Under Albert the Great** — SC-04. Ends: Albert writes to send him to Paris.
5. **Thomas Becomes a Master Teacher at Paris** — SC-05. Ends: he starts to teach, and the writing begins.
6. **Thomas Teaches in Italy and Refuses a Bishopric** — SC-06; the Corpus Christi office as bridge. Ends: he is sent back to Paris.
7. **Thomas Dines With King Louis of France** — SC-07; Chrysostom joke, Peckham. Ends: the order calls him home to Naples. (If SC-07 dates to 1256–59, move it to ch. 5 and rename this "Thomas Teaches in Paris a Second Time".)
8. **Thomas Writes and Preaches in Naples** — SC-08; routine, Lent sermons. Ends: a Mass in the same chapel in December.
9. **Thomas Stops Writing After a Mass** — SC-09. Ends: the pope summons him to a council.
10. **Thomas Falls Ill on the Way to a Council** — SC-10. Ends: he asks to be taken to a monastery.
11. **Thomas Dies Among Cistercian Monks** — SC-11, SC-12. Ends: the monks fear someone will take the body.
12. **The Monks Hide Thomas's Body and Find It Whole** — SC-13; Paris masters' letter. Ends: his order begins to ask Rome to name him a saint.
13. **A Pope Declares Thomas a Saint** — SC-14. Ends: the body still lies far from his own order.
14. **How Thomas Became the Church's Great Teacher** — SC-15; 1567, 1879, 1880, hymns. Ends: back to the straw and to "nothing but yourself".

## 5. The scenes

### SC-01 — A storm over the family castle, c.1225 (Roccasecca)
- What happens: lightning kills his baby sister in the tower and the horses below; Theodora runs trembling to the bed and finds nurse and baby safe.
- Why: opens the family whose plans for him make the first storms; his mother told it.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.67 (LEG); Theodora as Tocco's source [W-S1 Tocco p.69]; at five at Monte Cassino, "not talkative" [W-S1 Tocco p.69]; father meant him for abbot [W-S2 n.76 p.371].
- Need: Tocco's full Latin; any words of Theodora's.
- Where to look: W-S1 Tocco p.67-69.

### SC-02 — By a spring on the road to Rome, c.1240–44
- What happens: the new friar rests by a spring with four friars; his brothers, with the emperor's leave, attack "not as brothers but as enemies"; he holds his habit fast.
- Why: the first cost of the call.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.72-73; W-S1 Gui p.172; a well in Calo [W-S1 Calo p.21]; Rainald and Peter della Vigna [W-S3 XXII.20].
- Need: the year; the exact Latin of the attack.
- Where to look: W-S1 Tocco p.72-73.

### SC-03 — The locked room, c.1244–45 (Monte San Giovanni)
- What happens: his brothers tear his habit; a girl is shut in; he drives her out with a burning brand, burns a cross on the wall, prays, sleeps; two angels gird him; he cries out and tells no one until near death.
- Why: the hardest trial of the call; prayer under pressure.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.74-76; angels' words [W-q5]; "light came to him in the dark" [W-q4]; Cantimpré's plainer early version [W-S1 Calo p.22 n.1]; a sister converted [W-S1 Tocco p.74]; escape [W-S1 Tocco p.77; W-S3 XXII.21].
- Need: Tocco's full Latin; length of captivity.
- Testimony: Tocco's 1319 deposition, W-S2 n.61 p.349.

### SC-04 — Albert's classroom, Cologne, c.1248
- What happens: the silent "dumb ox" explains a lecture a classmate cannot; Albert tests him and says his bellow will sound through the world.
- Why: the hidden gift comes out.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.77-79; W-q6. Written about 70 years later, "it is said".
- Need: Latin of the notebook page and Albert's test.
- Where to look: W-S1 Tocco p.78-79.

### SC-05 — The night before the mastership, Paris c.1256
- What happens: ordered to become master, he pleads youth, prays Psalm 11 in tears, sleeps; an old friar gives him his text, "Rigans montes."
- Why: life with God; he takes up teaching in fear and prayer.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.84-85; Gerard before 1260 [W-S1 Tocco p.85 n.1]; Thomas told it at Maenza in 1274 [W-S2 n.49 p.331]; W-q7.
- Need: the prior's sworn account in full.
- Testimony: W-S2 n.49 p.331.

### SC-06 — A cardinal's castle at Molara, 1260s
- What happens: on the road from Rome Thomas and his companion fall ill with fever; Thomas sends him the relics of St Agnes he wears; the man recovers.
- Why: Thomas as friend in the Italian years, told by a man who was there.
- Have: W-S1 Calo p.45 n.5 quoting W-S3 XXIII.10; Orvieto 1261, Rome 1265 [W-S2 p.582-583]; refuses Naples [W-S1 Tocco p.116; W-S3 XXII.39]; Corpus Christi office, 1264 [O-S5].
- Need: the companion's name; the year.
- Testimony: Ptolemy, W-S3 XXIII.10.

### SC-07 — King Louis's table, Paris
- What happens: silent at dinner, he strikes the table: "Now it is settled against the Manichaean heresy." The prior tugs his cape; the king calls a scribe.
- Why: the mind at work, the comedy, a saint-king's respect.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.116-117; W-q9, W-q10; Chrysostom [W-S2 n.78 p.376]; Peckham [W-S2 p.374].
- Need: the year; Tocco's full text.
- Where to look: W-S1 Tocco p.116-117.

### SC-08 — The crucifix, St Nicholas chapel, Naples, 1272–73
- What happens: the sacristan sees him raised in prayer and hears the image: "Thomas, you have written well of me. What reward will you take?" "Lord, nothing but yourself."
- Why: states the answer before the end. Tell as tradition.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.108; Calo p.38; Gui p.189; no 1319 witness [W §3]; routine [W-S2 n.77 p.374]; Lent sermons [W-S2 n.87 p.391; n.70 p.362].
- Need: the sacristan's name as Tocco gives it.
- Where to look: W-S1 Tocco p.108.

### SC-09 — After Mass, St Nicholas chapel, Naples, about 6 December 1273
- What happens: changed by the Mass, he stops in the treatise on Penance; "Reginald, I cannot"; he swears Reginald to silence; everything seems like straw. At his sister's house he hardly speaks.
- Why: the turn of the whole life.
- Have: O "STRAW – exact check"; W-S2 n.79 p.376-377; W-q16, W-q17; sister [W-S2 p.377]; San Severino version [W-S1 Tocco p.120].
- Need: nothing; copy the Latin and check the chain from Reginald [W-S2 p.377-378].
- Testimony: Bartholomew of Capua, art. LXXIX, W-S2 p.376-377, in full.

### SC-10 — The road near Teano, early 1274
- What happens: riding to the council he strikes his head on a fallen tree; Reginald says he and Bonaventure will be cardinals; "Reginald, in no state can I be so useful to our order as in this one."
- Why: the last refusal of rank, made while hurt.
- Have: W-S2 n.78 p.375-376; W-q14, W-q15; summons, treatise against the Greeks [W-S1 Tocco p.129]; illness at his niece's castle, Maenza [W-S2 n.8 p.276; n.49 p.330].
- Need: Reginald's exact words.
- Where to look: W-S2 p.375-376.

### SC-11 — Arrival at Fossanova, February–March 1274
- What happens: "better that I be found in a house of religious"; at the door, "This is my rest for ever"; monks carry logs on their shoulders and he asks why holy men serve him; he explains the Song of Songs to them.
- Why: the man who refused castles chooses a monastery to die in.
- Have: W-S2 n.8 p.277, 332; W-S1 Tocco p.130-131; W-q19 to W-q21; "do not be troubled, son" [C-S1 p.136].
- Need: the full line on the wood (W-q21 and C §6 q4 differ).
- Testimony: Abbot Nicholas, W-S2 n.8 p.277.

### SC-12 — Last Communion and death, Fossanova, 7 March 1274
- What happens: before monks and friars he receives Communion, "I receive you … for love of whom I studied, kept watch, and worked"; gives his writing to the Church's correction; dies at dawn. His confessor calls him pure "as a child of five."
- Why: the resolution: all the study ends in Christ received.
- Have: W-S1 Tocco p.131-133; O-Q34; W-S2 n.49 p.332; W-q24, W-q25; subprior's sight [W-S2 n.51 p.335-336].
- Need: Peter's full account; days between Communion and death.
- Testimony: Peter of Monte San Giovanni, sworn eyewitness, W-S2 n.49 p.332.

### SC-13 — The hidden body, Fossanova, 1274
- What happens: fearing the friars will take him, the monks move the body by night; Thomas appears to the prior, "Bring me back to my first place"; after about seven months the tomb is opened, fragrance fills the cloister, the body is whole; they sing "Os iusti."
- Why: the first veneration; a community's claim to him.
- Have: W-S2 n.8 p.278; n.52 p.336-337; W-S1 Tocco p.138-140; C-S4 §9; Paris masters' letter, 2 May 1274 [W-S2 p.583-586].
- Need: the sworn wording of Nicholas and Peter.
- Testimony: W-S2 n.52 p.336-337.

### SC-14 — The pope hears the case, 1318–23
- What happens: Tocco carries the petition; a storm nearly drowns him and the miracle records; John XXII says Thomas's life was holy and his teaching "could not be without miracle"; on 18 July 1323 he declares him a saint.
- Why: the path to sainthood as a scene.
- Have: C-S1 p.147-149; W-S1 Tocco p.57-58; C-S4 §19-21; C-S8.
- Need: Tocco's own words on the storm.
- Testimony: C-S1 p.147-148.

### SC-15 — The relics reach Toulouse, 28 January 1369
- What happens: Urban V gives the body to the Dominicans ("He is our brother and our flesh"); Louis of Anjou and a huge crowd meet it; since 1969 this day is his feast.
- Why: the real case after death; ties the feast day to a crowd.
- Have: C-S3 ch.X; C-S2 p.59; C-S5; C-S8.
- Need: a primary source for the 150,000.
- Where to look: C-S3 ch.X; C-S5.

## 6. The saint in the Church

**Writings and works**
- Summa contra Gentiles, 1259–65 [O-S11; O-Q23]: ch. 6.
- Corpus Christi office for Urban IV, 1264: Pange lingua, Lauda Sion; authorship "probable" [O-Q19, O-Q20, O-F8]: ch. 6, 14.
- Summa Theologiae, unfinished at III, on Penance, Dec 1273 [W-S2 p.376-377]: ch. 9.
- Naples Lent sermons 1273; "almost the whole city" came [W-S2 n.87 p.391]: ch. 8.
- Reception: Paris arts masters ask for his bones and unfinished writings, 2 May 1274 [W-S2 p.583-586]: ch. 12. John XXII: his books lit the Church more than all other doctors [C-S1 p.149]: ch. 13.

**What he changed**
- Dominican chapters 1324–28 rank him after Dominic [C-S8]: ch. 13.
- Doctor of the Church, Pius V, 1567 [O-S11; C-S2 p.60]: ch. 14.
- Aeterni Patris, Leo XIII, 4 Aug 1879 [C-S6]: ch. 14.
- Patron of Catholic universities and schools, 4 Aug 1880 [C-S7]: ch. 14.

**Why famous**
- Fragrance at the tomb; healings there, ten printed in the bull [C-S4 §9-18]: ch. 12–13.
- Toulouse 1369; feast moved to 28 January in 1969 [C-S8]: ch. 14.
- Patron of Naples, 1567 and 1603 [C-S2 p.60]: ch. 14.

**Path to sainthood**
- First veneration: the Mass at the opened tomb, 1274 [W-S1 Tocco p.140]: ch. 12.
- Bartholomew's petition c.1303 [C-S8]; inquiries Naples 1319, Fossanova 1321 [W-S1 Tocco p.57]: ch. 12–13.
- Canonized by John XXII at Avignon, 18 July 1323 [C-S4; W-S1 Tocco p.58]: ch. 13.

**Fact needs**
1. A later saint, council, or school formed by his teaching. Look in C-S5; C-S3 Epilogue.
2. The date of Mirabilis Deus (11 or 15 April 1567). Look in C-S2 p.60; C-S5.
3. A source that his Corpus Christi hymns are still sung. Look in C-S5; O-S5.

## 8. What we leave out

- Hermit's prophecy before birth: legend, sworn only as hearsay.
- Hail Mary paper, light from his face: legends.
- Herrings at Maenza: sources disagree; miracles section.
- Levitations, lost tooth, Peter and Paul on Isaiah, demon on the terrace: miracles section.
- Stars, comet, mule at the bier: Tocco only, legend.
- Tomb miracles: miracles section.
- Head, hand, 1585 skull, Fondi chest: contested; relics section.
- Lines falsely credited to him (O-F1 to O-F7): not his.
- Albert's 1276 remark: no primary source.
- Summa articles beyond one line: teachings section.

## 9. Accuracy notes

- Birth c.1225 (Tocco: died in his 49th year) or 1227.
- Entry to the order: c.1240 at 14 (Gui), at 16 (Ptolemy), or 1244 (O-S11).
- Captor: mother (Tocco) or father (Bartholomew); one to three years. Who arranged the escape is disputed.
- Dumb ox and crucifix are late: "it was told".
- Straw rests on Bartholomew's sworn hearsay from Reginald; "palee" = chaff or straw; the day is "about the feast of St Nicholas".
- Sacristan: Dominic (Tocco, Gui) or James (Calo) of Caserta. Funeral preacher: Reginald or Peter of Sezze.
- "As many miracles as articles": no primary source; omit or call it tradition. The bull prints ten miracles, not 96.
- Names: Reginald of Piperno, William of Tocco, Bartholomew of Capua, the Cistercian abbey of Fossanova, Maenza, his mother Theodora.
